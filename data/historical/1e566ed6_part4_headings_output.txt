Line 100: '##### 1.	The prostrations we make at the three first troparia, according to the Typikon - are not great, but small, although due to ignorance of the rubric, or due to excessive piety, great ones are made among us. For here this prostration is called simply metanoia, and with the addition megale [great] it is given only at the prayer of St. Ephrem and never at the end of Vespers, after the litany "Have mercy on us, O God". Likewise at the Great Canon the rubric does not prescribe great prostrations, but simply metanoias.'
Line 101: '##### 2.	Neither Greek nor Slavonic rubrics give the form of the great or small prostration. However, according to custom it will be thus: he who makes a great prostration falls on his knees and touches the ground with his forehead; he who makes a small prostration does not fall on his knees, nor touches the ground with his forehead, but only crossing himself reaches the ground with his right hand, or only to the knees as the Synod of Lviv prescribes in Title V "On the Holy Liturgy", in the prayers before the iconostasis before the Proskomedia[^481]. We reach the ground with the right hand when we bow to the Holy Mysteries; only to the knees when we bow to holy icons, or at various supplications. However, our people in these parts do not have the custom, when bowing, to extend hands to the ground, or to the knees, but only crossing themselves to bow low before the Holy Mysteries, and higher - in other circumstances. We suppose, however, that at small Lenten prostrations we should reach the knees with the hand, for throughout the whole year at the Trisagion and at "O come, let us worship" we make prostrations, not reaching the knees with the hand[^482].'
