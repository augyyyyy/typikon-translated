Line 1: ## 6.1 Appendix (Rubrics of the Divine Services)
Line 5: ## I. Introductory Remarks
Line 6: ##### 1. The Sanctuary and the Holy Table
Line 8: ##### 1. The sanctuary should be at least one step higher than the temple of the faithful. Within the sanctuary itself, however, there should be no steps leading to the holy table.
Line 9: ##### 2. The holy table is square, separated from the wall, situated in the middle of the sanctuary so that it can be walked around. Under the northern wall of the sanctuary is placed the table of proskomedia (oblation), and under the southern - another table, on which the sacred vestments are prepared.
Line 10: ##### 3. The Artophorion, or tabernacle [kyvot], which is contained in the middle of the holy table (throne), must be constructed in the form of a small temple or tomb. It should not be too high and wide, so that by its dimensions it does not hinder the liturgical actions of the celebrant and concelebrants nor render them impossible.
Line 11: ##### 4. Candlesticks standing on the holy table should not obscure it, but it is proper to place one candlestick with one, two or three candles on both sides of the cross or tabernacle. It is also permitted to use a seven-branched candlestick with oil lamps, which is placed behind the holy table.
Line 12: ##### 5. It is not permitted to place on the holy table any reliquaries, nor flowers, natural or artificial, nor anything that is unnecessary for divine service. The book for divine service (*Liturgicon*) should be placed on a cushion, on the holy table, and not on a stand, unless it lies on an analogion on the left side of the throne.
Line 13: ##### 6. The iconostasis must have the icon of the Savior to the right of the holy doors[^754] and of the Theotokos - to the left, and other icons placed, according to custom, in their places.
Line 15: ##### 7. No one dares to have entry into the sanctuary except priests, deacons, clerics and servers. For the faithful who do not cooperate in the divine service, there is no place in the sanctuary, and even less so for women, regardless of origin and reason.
Line 16: ##### 8. The diskos should be sufficiently deep and provided with a rim; it may have a foot.
Line 17: ##### 9. The censer must have short chains so that it can be set in motion with a light swing.
Line 18: ##### 10. One should not use other liturgical vestments nor other liturgical cloths except those indicated in this Rubric for each divine service; the use of small bells, organs, large or small, i. e., harmoniums, is excluded.
Line 19: ##### 2. General Rules
Line 20: ##### 11. A small bow, - during which, signing ourselves with the sign of the cross, we incline the head and shoulders slightly, however we do not extend the hands to the knees or even to the ground, before or after the sign of the cross, - we make:
Line 37: ##### 12. A great bow [prostration], during which, signing ourselves with the sign of the cross, we fall to the ground with our whole body, we make only during the Forty Days [Lent] and at the Liturgy of Presanctified Gifts.
Line 38: ##### 13. Prayers with extended and uplifted hands are recited only when this is explicitly prescribed, that is: at the prayer "No one is worthy" (at the Cherubic Hymn), at "Let us lift up our hearts" and at the Lord's Prayer. When extending the hands, the elbows should not rest on the sides, but the hands themselves should be freely lifted upwards.
Line 39: ##### 14. The hands, if nothing is prescribed about them, can be held in various ways: placed on the holy table or holding the *Liturgicon* with them, lowered down under the phelonion or folded on the breast, but not interlocking finger to finger.
Line 40: ##### 15. When the Deacon exclaims the litanies, he raises the orarion with three fingers of the right hand upwards, even if it is not always explicitly prescribed by the rubrics. Already at the beginning of the litanies, the Priest may begin to recite its prayer quietly.
Line 41: ##### 16. When the Deacon exclaims: "Bow your heads unto the Lord," all bow their head and shoulders.
Line 42: ##### 17. Servers in the sanctuary during the Divine Liturgy do not bend their knees, but the people, if they wish, may kneel.
Line 43: ##### 18. When the Priest blesses, he places the thumb of the right hand crosswise on the ring finger, keeps the index finger straight, and slightly bends the middle and little fingers. To receive the blessing, all bow their heads and shoulders and sign themselves with the sign of the cross.
Line 44: ##### 19. Regarding the opening and closing of doors and the drawing and undrawing of the curtain of the iconostasis, one must observe the following general rules:
Line 53: ##### 20. Not the Priest alone places incense into the censer, this may also be done by a server or the Deacon and then presented to the Priest for blessing.
Line 55: ##### 21. When the Priest at the divine service blesses the censer for the first time, he recites the prayer "Incense we offer to Thee," and subsequently - only the words: "Blessed is our God."
Line 56: ##### 22. When putting on and kissing vestments, it is necessary to follow the prescriptions given at the beginning of the Proskomedia (see nos. 103-106); at other divine services, having left aside the corresponding prayers, one must observe the same.
Line 57: ##### 23. If a Priest, having to celebrate a divine service, does not put on the sticharion, he must put on over his long garment also an outer one with wide sleeves, which is called in Greek rason, and in Slavonic - ryasa.
Line 58: ##### 24. No one is permitted to put on vestments proper to a rank higher than the one to which he belongs.
Line 59: ##### 25. The Deacon, while serving the Priest, stands to his right, unless explicitly prescribed otherwise. If there are two deacons, the first stands to the right, and the second - to the left of the Priest.
Line 60: ##### 26. Clerics (seminarians) who do not yet have diaconal ordination are not permitted to perform the diaconal service.
Line 61: ##### 27. What the Priest or Deacon exclaims, other concelebrants should not recite quietly, except for the exclamations that conclude prayers, unless prescribed otherwise.
Line 62: ##### 28. The full ceremonies are explained in that part which speaks about the celebration in concelebration with one deacon. In other parts, which speak about the service with two deacons, without a Deacon, with Priest-concelebrants, only that which is proper to these parts is explained fully.

Line 348: ##### 97. According to the ancient custom of the Eastern Church, only one Holy Liturgy may be celebrated at the same holy table. However, one may depart from this rule if the Divine Liturgy must be repeated for the benefit of the faithful. If there is one or several secondary churches in the parish, then the more solemn Liturgy is celebrated in the main sanctuary, and other liturgies should be celebrated rather in the side sanctuaries.
Line 349: ##### 98. The Eucharistic bread does not absolutely have to be baked on the day the Holy Sacrifice is offered. The same bread can be kept for several days and used at several liturgies, provided that all the ceremonies of the Proskomedia are observed over it or over a part of it, cutting the bread and removing the Lamb and particles according to the prescription. Also, it is not necessary to always use five prosphora, if the Lamb and all particles can be taken from one and the same prosphora. The custom of using five prosphora may be kept, if one pleases, when celebrating more solemn liturgies (see no. 179).

Line 1088: ##### 228. During the singing of "Let my prayer be set forth" the server walks with a lighted candle before the Priest.
Line 1089: ##### 224. At the Great Entrance candle-bearers, if there be such, turned to the northern doors and arranged in order, accompany the Presanctified Lamb. After they arrive before the holy doors they stand together on both sides, and then return to their places.
