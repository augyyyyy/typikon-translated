[^191]: This sessional hymn refers to the Life of St. Boniface of Tarsus, whom the noble Roman matron Aglaida sent to Cilicia to purchase and bring back relics of holy martyrs. Instead, Boniface boldly confessed Christ in the amphitheater, was tortured and beheaded, and his own holy relics ("the right marvelous burden") were reverently carried back to Rome to Aglaida by his companions.
[^192]: Codicological Note: In this physical copy of the Pochaiv 1761 Menaion, a 4-folio gathering from the October volume (Folios 104–107, physical leaves p204–p210) was erroneously bound into the December volume between physical leaves p203 and p211. Leaves p204–p208 contain the services for October 19 (the Holy Prophet Joel and the Holy Martyr Varus), followed on leaf p208 by October 20 (the Holy Great-Martyr Artemius), continuing through leaf p210, whereupon leaf p211 resumes the December service (December 20, Forefeast of the Nativity of Christ / St. Ignatius the God-bearer, Folio 105). In accordance with Master Translation Standard MTS-1 (Recensional & Epigraphic Fidelity), the printed source text is transcribed and translated exactly as preserved on the physical leaves.
[^193]: Cf. Joel 3:18 (LXX 4:18): "And a fountain shall come forth out of the house of the Lord, and shall water the torrent of shrubs [the valley of Shittim]."
[^194]: St. Cleopatra of Mount Tabor in Palestine (commemorated Oct. 19 with St. Varus). A devout Christian widow from Cilicia, Cleopatra witnessed the martyrdom of St. Varus under Maximian (ca. 307), secretly took his body, buried it in her home, and later built a church in his honor in her native village of Dara near Mount Tabor.
[^195]: This troparion refers to the miracle of St. Varus regarding Cleopatra's son, John. Cleopatra prayed that Varus would obtain for her son that which was most pleasing to God and profitable for him. Soon after, the youth died and appeared to his mother in a vision alongside St. Varus in glorious heavenly military armor, having been received into the celestial army of Christ the King.
[^196]: St. Artemius of Antioch (martyred ca. 362), military commander (*dux Aegypti*) under Constantine the Great and Constantius, who denounced Emperor Julian the Apostate for his pagan persecutions, was subjected to gruesome tortures (hung and scraped, crushed beneath a gigantic stone, pierced with spits), and beheaded. His relics were translated to Constantinople and laid in the Church of St. John the Baptist in Oxeia, where he became renowned as a healer of hernias and bodily ailments.
