[^198]: St. Philogonius, Bishop of Antioch (commemorated Dec. 20). Originally a lawyer and magistrate renowned for defending the poor and oppressed, he was chosen from the laity to become Bishop of Antioch (319–324) during the rise of the Arian heresy. In 386, St. John Chrysostom delivered a famous encomium in Antioch celebrating his memory.
[^199]: Typikon Rubric: From December 20 through the Apodosis (Apodosis) of Theophany, the recitation of the weekday Biblical Canticles with the refrain "Господеви поем" ("Cantemus Domino") is omitted at Matins, and the festive poetic canons of the Forefeasts and Feasts take precedence.
[^200]: According to early Christian tradition preserved in the Synaxarion and Greek hagiography, St. Ignatius was the little child whom the Lord Jesus Christ took into His arms in Capernaum when teaching His disciples humility: "Except ye be converted, and become as little children, ye shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven" (Matt. 18:2–3). Hence he was called "Theophoros" (God-bearer), both as one borne in the arms of God and as one who carried God in his heart.
[^201]: An exact liturgical quotation of the celebrated passage from St. Ignatius of Antioch's *Epistle to the Romans* (chapter 4), written while on his journey to martyrdom under Trajan (ca. 107): "I am the wheat of God, and let me be ground by the teeth of the wild beasts, that I may be found the pure bread of Christ."
