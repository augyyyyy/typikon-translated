[^172]: On the prophetic vision of the uncut stone cut from the mountain without hands, see Dan. 2:34–45.
[^173]: Church Slavonic *Мала́хово* represents the Greek ascription *Μαлаξοῦ* ("by Malaxos"), referring to Manuel Malaxos (16th c.), author of several stichera in the Menaion.
[^174]: St. Zoe of Rome (d. ca. 286), wife of the court official Nicostratus, was healed of muteness by St. Sebastian, converted, and thereafter martyred by suffocation over smoke.
[^175]: The acrostic in Greek reads Θεῖόν σοι μάρτυς ἐμφρόνως τιμὴν νέμω ("Divinely do I wisely give honor unto thee, O martyr"), authored by St. Joseph the Hymnographer (ca. 816–886).
[^176]: St. Castulus was an officer of the Roman palace who sheltered Christians; he was tortured and buried alive in a pit on the Via Labicana ca. 286.
[^177]: According to the Passio of St. Sebastian, the twin deacons Sts. Mark and Marcellinus were nailed by their feet to a wooden post and pierced with lances after enduring long imprisonment.
[^178]: The troparion plays on the martyr's name Zoe (Greek *Ζωή*, meaning "life"): "Thou didst attain the blessed life, even as thou wast namesake thereof."
[^179]: December 20 marks the beginning of the Forefeast of the Nativity. Therefore, if the Sunday of the Holy Fathers falls on December 18 or 19 (prior to December 20), the service combines only the Sunday Octoechos with the Holy Fathers, without the Forefeast hymns.
