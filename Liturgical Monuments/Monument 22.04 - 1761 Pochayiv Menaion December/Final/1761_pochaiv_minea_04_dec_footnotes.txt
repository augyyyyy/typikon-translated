[^10]: Slavonic: *краегране́сїе* (acrostic). The canon bears the acrostic: "Тя̀ проро́че Авваку́ме пою̀ блаже́нне" (I sing of thee, O blessed Prophet Habakkuk), composed by Saint Theophanes.

[^13]: The Prophet Zephaniah (Sophonias), ninth of the Twelve Minor Prophets, who prophesied during the reign of King Josiah of Judah (cf. Zephaniah 1:1) concerning the Day of the Lord.

[^16]: Saint Barbara the Great-Martyr of Heliopolis (beheaded c. 306 under Emperor Maximian) and Saint John of Damascus (John Damascene, c. 676–749), Monk of the Lavra of Saint Sabbas the Sanctified, Father and Doctor of the Church, celebrated together on December 4.

[^17]: The Matins canon pairs the Canon of Saint Barbara (composed by Saint John of Damascus, Tone 2, Heirmos: *In the deep of old*) with the Canon of Saint John Damascene (composed by Saint Theophanes the Branded, same Tone and Heirmos).

[^18]: The Church Slavonic reads *ѡ҆фїе́на*, transliterating/adapting the Greek participle *ὀφθεῖσα* (*having appeared* / *seen*).

[^19]: The printed text has *оу҆́є*, an epigraphic misprint for *ѻ҆́ч҃е* (*O father*).

[^20]: Slavonic *є҆диноко́льное пребезꙋ́мїе* refers to Monothelitism (the 'one-will' heresy), while *вѣ́рꙋ же є҆динодѣ́йственнꙋ* refers to the single divine-human operation or Orthodox confession against Monenergism/Monothelitism.

[^21]: The Pochaiv Menaion places the complete All-Night Vigil service for the Holy Great-Martyr Barbara at the end of the volume (following December 31), providing here only the standard Polyeleos/Doxology rank service.

[^22]: Slavonic *зерца́лѡмъ разрѣ́шшымся* (*the mirrors being resolved/abolished*) alludes to 1 Cor. 13:12 (*"For now we see in a mirror, dimly; but then face to face"*), expressing direct beatific contemplation of the Holy Trinity after the dissolution of earthly mortal mirrors.

[^23]: The printed Slavonic has *сообра́въ*, an epigraphic/orthographic variant for *собра́въ* (*having gathered* or *assembled*).

[^24]: Slavonic: *ꙋ҆ꙗзвлѧ́лъ є҆сѝ ꙋ҆́глїе* (literally *thou didst wound/quench the coals*), referring to extinguishing the scorching coals of demonic temptations in the wilderness.

[^25]: Slavonic: *И҆з̾ ꙋ҆лтро́бы ѡ҆ст҃и́лсѧ є҆сѝ*, where *ꙋ҆лтро́бы* is an epigraphic misprint for *ꙋ҆тро́бы* (*from the womb*).

[^26]: Slavonic: *Неꙋдержи́мыми на на́съ сме́рть стремле́ньми и҆тїа̀*, where *и҆тїа̀* is an archaic/epigraphic aorist form from *и҆тѝ* (*came* / *advanced*); the text expresses: *Death came with unrestrainable assaults upon us*.

[^27]: The printed text has *чꙋлодѣ́йствꙋѧй*, an epigraphic misprint for *чꙋдодѣ́йствꙋѧй* (*wonderworking* / *working wonders*).

[^28]: The Slavonic text has *ѡ҆шла́нїемъ слезе́й*, an epigraphic/orthographic variant for *ѡ҆лиѧ́нїемъ* / *ѡ҆мыва́нїемъ* (*by the washing / pouring forth of tears*).

[^29]: The printed text has *Ѡ҆ложи́лъ є҆сѝ*, an epigraphic misprint for *Ѿложи́лъ є҆сѝ* (*thou didst put off* or *renounce*).

[^30]: The printed Slavonic has *ꙋ҆ѻбло́вленнїи*, an epigraphic dittography/misprint for *ꙋ҆ловле́ннїи* (*caught* or *enamored*).

[^31]: Slavonic: *бре́меннꙋю бра́нь* (burdensome warfare/strife); Greek collates to τὴν πάλαι μάχην (the ancient strife/enmity).

[^32]: Slavonic: *Оу҆тро́бъ неѡпа́льнѡ ѡ҆бразꙋ́ютъ*, incipit of the Katavasia of Ode 8 of the Nativity Canon: *The furnace moist with dew typified the image of a wonder surpassing nature; for it burnt not the youths whom it received...* (Ὦδε δὲ τῆς καμίνου δρόσος ἐξεικόνιζε).

[^33]: Slavonic: *ꙋ҆подо́бился сегѡ̀ живоно́снѣй ꙗ҆́кѡ мо́щно бѣ̀ ста́ти*, translating the Greek: κατὰ τὸ δυνατὸν τῇ ζωηφόρῳ τούτου στάσει ὡμοιώθης (thou didst become conformed, as far as possible, unto His life-bearing manner of life/standing).

[^34]: Slavonic: *Люби́ти ꙋ҆́бѡ на́мъ*, incipit of the Katavasia of Ode 9 of the Nativity Canon: *It were easier for us to love silence, which is free from danger...* (Στέργειν μὲν ἡμᾶς ὡς ἀκίνδυνον φόβῳ).

[^35]: Slavonic: *сладодохнове́нными* (with sweet-breathing/sweet-blowing). The printed edition has the epigraphic dittography *сладодохонове́нными*.

[^37]: Slavonic: *невѣ́рное* (literally *faithless*), an epigraphic corruption/misprint for *невѣ́тренное* (calm, windless, storm-free), Greek: ἀχείμαστον λιμένα (a haven free from storms).

[^38]: The liturgical paroemia for Saint Nicholas is a classic composite lection combining Proverbs 10:7, 3:13–16, and 8:4–21.

[^40]: Pericope 36 of the Gospel according to John comprises John 10:9–16, the classical liturgical Gospel lection for holy hierarchs.

[^43]: Slavonic: *стла́мнꙋ* (epigraphic variant for *ста́мнꙋ*, urn/pot), translating the Greek στάμνον, the golden urn holding the manna (Heb. 9:4), a standard Old Testament type of the Mother of God.

[^44]: Slavonic: *побѣ́ды на врагѝ тезоимени́тъ лю́демъ вѣ́рнымъ* (namesake of victory over enemies unto the faithful people), reflecting the Greek hymnographic play on the etymology of Saint Nicholas' name: Νικόλαος, from νίκη (victory) and λαός (people).

[^45]: Slavonic: *бл҃гочести́вомꙋ королю̀ на́шемꙋ* ("our pious King"), referring to King Augustus III of Poland (reigned 1734–1763), under whose royal privilege this 1761 Pochaiv edition was published under the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth. [^154]: *The Wisdom and Word* (*Премꙋ́дрости* / *Τὴν Σοφίαν καὶ Λόγον*): The printed text abbreviates the Greek and Slavonic incipit to its opening noun in the genitive; the full incipit references the divine Wisdom and Word of the Father.

[^165]: *By Andrew of Pyrgos* (*А҆ндре́а пѷ́рскагѡ* / *Ἀνδρέου Πυροῦ*): Andrew of Pyrgos (or Jerusalem), a Byzantine hymnographer.
