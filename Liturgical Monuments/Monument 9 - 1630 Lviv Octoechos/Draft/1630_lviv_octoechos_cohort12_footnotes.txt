[^170]: Monday Matins, Tone 4: Sessional Hymns after the 3rd Kathisma (or Ode/Poetic Kathisma) to the Bodiless Powers (Holy Angels and Archangels), invoking their constant standing before the Supradivine Trinity and their intercession for peace and remission of sins.

[^171]: Monday Matins, Tone 4: Penitential Canon and Canon to the Bodiless Powers, Ode 1, with the Heirmos "The three mighty captains..." (based on the drowning of Pharaoh's commanders in Exod. 15:4), invoking Christ's mercy shown unto the prodigal, the harlot, and the publican, followed by the contemplation of the angelic intellects before the Primal Mind.

[^172]: Monday Matins Canons, Tone 4, Odes 3 and 4: Extolling deliverance from the night of worldly passions by the Unwaning Light, the steadfastness of the Holy Martyrs in the shedding of their blood, and the nine angelic ranks praising the single, beginningless Godhead.

[^173]: Monday Matins Canons, Tone 4, Ode 7: Sessional themes and troparia to the Bodiless Powers based on the Song of the Three Holy Children in the Babylonian furnace (Dan. 3), and Isaiah's vision of the Seraphim before the divine throne (Isa. 6:1–3).

[^174]: Monday Matins Canons, Tone 4, Ode 8: Based on the Benedicite (Dan. 3:57–88), celebrating the bedewing of the faithful in the furnace, the deliverance of the soul from irrational passions and the wild boar of evils (Ps. 79:14 LXX), and the unceasing doxology of the Archangels.

[^175]: Monday Matins Canons, Tone 4, Ode 9, Aposticha, and Beatitudes: The eschatological call to trim lamps with the oil of virtues before the Bridechamber (Matt. 25:1–13), followed by the Monday Beatitudes (Makarismoi) in Tone 4, commemorating the Bodiless Powers and the Holy Martyrs.

[^176]: Monday Evening, Vespers for Tuesday, Tone 4: Dedicated to repentance and the Holy Forerunner and Baptist John, the greatest born of women (Matt. 11:11), opening with the Penitential Stichera on "Lord, I have cried" to the melody "With tears I desired," followed by Stichera to the Forerunner.

[^177]: Tuesday Matins, Tone 4: Sessional Hymns after the Kathismata, meditating on the impending judgment, the vigilance of the Wise Virgins, the endurance of the Holy Martyrs, and the sweet-singing Turtledove (the Forerunner) calling all to repentance.

[^178]: Tuesday Matins, Tone 4: Penitential Canon composed by St. Joseph the Hymnographer (with acrostic) and Canon to the Holy Forerunner John, Odes 1 and 3, beseeching deliverance from the sleep of slothfulness and spiritual barrenness through the prayers of the Baptist.

[^179]: Tuesday Matins Canons, Tone 4, Odes 4, 5, and 6: Praising the Forerunner as the spiritual morning star, the sweet-singing turtledove and swallow announcing the spiritual spring of Christ, and the baptizer of the Lord in the Jordan.

[^180]: Tuesday Matins Canons, Tone 4, Odes 7, 8, and 9: Based on the Three Children in Babylon (Dan. 3), praising the Forerunner who leaped in his mother's womb, calling upon the soul to bear fruits worthy of repentance before the axe strikes the barren fig tree (Luke 3:9).

[^181]: Tuesday Divine Liturgy, The Beatitudes (Makarismoi) in Tone 4: Intercalating the Beatitudes with troparia of repentance invoking the tears of Peter and the harlot, the intercession of the Holy Forerunner, and the steadfast confession of the Holy Martyrs.

[^182]: Tuesday Evening, Vespers for Wednesday, Tone 4: Dedicated to the Precious and Life-giving Cross of the Lord, with Cruciform Stichera (Stavropegial) at "Lord, I have cried" to the melody "As one noble," and Stavrotheotokia meditating on the maternal lamentations of the Virgin Mother at the foot of the Cross.

[^183]: Wednesday Matins, Tone 4: Sessional Hymns of the Precious Cross and the Holy Martyrs after the Kathismata, celebrating the wood of the Cross which abolished the ancient curse of the tree of knowledge and redeemed mankind by the Blood of Christ.

[^184]: Wednesday Matins, Tone 4: Canon of the Precious and Life-giving Cross of the Lord, composed by St. Joseph the Hymnographer, Ode 1, with the Heirmos "Traversing of old the deep of the Red Sea with dry feet..." (Exod. 14–15), prefiguring the Cross through Moses' hands stretched out crosswise over Amalek.
