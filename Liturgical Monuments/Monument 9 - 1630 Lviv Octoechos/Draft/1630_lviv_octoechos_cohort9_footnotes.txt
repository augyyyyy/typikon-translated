[^109]: Tone 3 Tuesday Matins, Canon to the Forerunner, Ode 6: Drawing upon Jonah 2:1–3 (LXX) and Psalm 68:2–3 (LXX) ("The lowest depth of sins hath surrounded me..."), while celebrating John the Baptist baptizing Christ in the streams of the Jordan.

[^110]: Tone 3 Tuesday Matins, Penitential Canon, Ode 7 Heirmos: Meditating upon the Three Holy Children in the Babylonian furnace (Daniel 3:23–26 LXX), who were preserved unconsumed by the cooling angel of God.

[^111]: Tone 3 Tuesday Matins, Penitential Canon, Ode 9 Heirmos: Quoting the Canticle of Zechariah (Luke 1:68–79): "Blessed be the Lord God of Israel; for He hath visited and redeemed His people, and hath raised up an horn of salvation for us in the house of David His servant..."

[^112]: Tone 3 Tuesday Divine Liturgy, The Beatitudes (Makarismoi): Troparia chanted with the Beatitudes on Tuesday, alternating the confession of the Good Thief (Luke 23:42) with petitions through the intercession of the Baptist, the holy martyrs, and the angelic orders.

[^113]: Tuesday Evening (Vespers for Wednesday), Tone 3: Stichera on "Lord, I have cried" dedicated to the Precious and Life-Giving Cross, inaugurating the liturgical observance of Wednesday commemorating Christ's betrayal and crucifixion.

[^114]: Tuesday Evening (Vespers for Wednesday), Aposticha Stichera of the Cross: Typological meditation on Moses lifting up his hands upon the mountain against Amalek (Exodus 17:11–12) as a prefiguration of Christ stretching out His hands upon the Cross.

[^115]: Tone 3 Wednesday Matins, Sessional Hymns of the Cross (after the First Kathisma): Sessional hymns celebrating the wood of the Cross touching the earth and reaching unto the heavens, prefigured in the cypress, pine, and cedar (Isaiah 60:13).

[^116]: Tone 3 Wednesday Matins, Canon of the Precious Cross: Composed by St. Joseph the Hymnographer, Tone 3. The Heirmos of Ode 1 celebrates God gathering the waters into one gathering and dividing the Red Sea (Genesis 1:9; Exodus 14:21).

[^117]: Tone 3 Wednesday Matins, Canon to the Most Holy Theotokos: Companion canon to the Canon of the Cross for Wednesday Matins, meditating on the Mother of God standing beside the Cross and shedding maternal tears.

[^118]: Tone 3 Wednesday Matins, Canon of the Cross, Ode 4 Heirmos: "Thou hast placed toward us steadfast love, O Lord, for Thou didst give Thine Only-begotten Son unto death for us..." Reflecting John 3:16 and Romans 5:8.

[^119]: Tone 3 Wednesday Matins, Canon of the Cross, Ode 9 Heirmos: The Burning Bush upon Mount Sinai (Exodus 3:2) as a prophetic type of the Virgin Theotokos conceiving the unquenchable divine fire without being consumed.

[^120]: Tone 3 Wednesday Divine Liturgy, The Beatitudes: Troparia chanted with the Beatitudes on Wednesday, commemorating the piercing of Christ's side with a lance (John 19:34) and the fountain of immortality poured out for the life of the world.

[^121]: Wednesday Evening (Vespers for Thursday), Tone 3: Stichera on "Lord, I have cried" celebrating the Holy Apostles as witnesses of the Word, and St. Nicholas the Wonderworker of Myra in Lycia as a swift protector and deliverer.

[^122]: Tone 3 Thursday Matins, Sessional Hymns to the Apostles (after the First Kathisma): Hymning the Holy Apostles who like wise fishermen cast their nets into the deep and drew all nations to faith in the undivided Trinity.

[^123]: Tone 3 Thursday Matins, Sessional Hymns (after the Third Kathisma): Sessional hymn to St. Nicholas of Myra, celebrating his miraculous deliverance of the three innocent men condemned to death by an unjust judge (an event famously recorded in his Vita).

[^124]: Tone 3 Thursday Matins, Canon to the Holy Apostles: Composed by St. Theophanes the Branded (Graptos, 9th century), Tone 3, celebrating the Apostles as immovable pillars of the Church and heralds of the divine illumination of the Gospel.

[^125]: Tone 3 Thursday Matins, Canon to Saint Nicholas: Composed by St. Joseph the Hymnographer, Tone 3. The Heirmos of Ode 1 refers to the crossing of the Red Sea (Exodus 15:1–2), and praises Nicholas as an unwaning beacon and pillar of piety.
