[^220]: Tone 5 Sunday Matins Canons, Odes 3–5 (physical leaves p141–p142): The Sunday Resurrection Canon, Cross-Resurrection Canon, and Canon to the Most-holy Theotokos, meditating upon Christ Who suspended the uncontainable weight of the earth upon nothing, the bitter gall offered in place of manna by ungrateful Israel, and the Virgin Mother prefigured in the unburned bush of Sinai.

[^221]: Tone 5 Sunday Matins Ode 6 and Resurrectional Kontakion and Ikos (physical leaf p142): Celebrating Christ's descent into hades, shattering its eternal gates as Almighty, breaking the sting of death, and delivering Adam from the ancient curse; followed by the Ikos recounting the Myrrh-bearing women hearing the angelic proclamation and the Risen Lord appearing to send them unto Galilee.

[^222]: Tone 5 Sunday Matins Canons, Odes 7 and 8 (physical leaves p142–p143): Contemplating Christ Who assumed human flesh as bait upon a fishhook to draw down the spiritual serpent, the two natural wills and two essences of the Incarnate Word in His voluntary agony in Gethsemane, and the mocking of hades at His life-giving descent.

[^223]: Tone 5 Sunday Matins Ode 9 and Praises (Ainoi) (physical leaves p143–p144): The concluding ninth ode taking as its biblical model Isaiah's prophecy of Emmanuel, followed by the Resurrectional Praises Stichera in Tone 5, meditating upon the guards and the unbroken seals of the sepulchre, and the Anatolian (Eastern) Stichera proclaiming the overthrow of death.

[^224]: Tone 5 Praises Stichera (concluding) and Sunday Divine Liturgy Beatitudes (Makarismoi) (physical leaf p144): Concluding stichera depicting the soldiers' terror and the angel rolling away the stone, followed by the Sunday Liturgy Beatitudes praising the good thief who confessed Christ upon the cross and received paradise.

[^225]: Tone 5 Sunday Evening (Vespers for Monday) Penitential Stichera and Stichera to the Bodiless Hosts (physical leaves p144–p145): Opening the liturgical week with profound compunction, mourning the squandering of baptismal grace before the unquenchable fire and the sickle of death, followed by supplications unto the holy Archangels and the heavenly angelic hierarchies.

[^226]: Tone 5 Monday Matins Sessional Hymns and Penitential/Bodiless Canons, Odes 1–3 (physical leaves p145–p146): Sessional Hymns after the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd Kathismata on the parable of the ten virgins and the impending judgment, followed by the Penitential Canon of Saint Joseph the Hymnographer and the Canon to the Bodiless Hosts.

[^227]: Tone 5 Monday Matins Canons (Odes 4–9), Aposticha, and Monday Divine Liturgy Beatitudes (physical leaves p146–p148): The completion of the Monday canons, celebrating the Archangels Michael and Gabriel as heralds and guardians, the Penitential Aposticha meditating upon the barren fig tree, and the Monday Liturgy Beatitudes.

[^228]: Tone 5 Monday Evening (Vespers for Tuesday) Penitential Stichera and Stichera to Saint John the Forerunner (physical leaves p148–p149): Stichera on "Lord, I have cried" to the melody "Rejoice, O container of ascetics," lamenting the violation of the second baptismal covenant and entreating the holy Forerunner and Baptist John as the radiant beacon of the spiritual Sun.

[^229]: Tone 5 Tuesday Matins Sessional Hymns and Penitential/Forerunner Canons, Odes 1–6 (physical leaves p149–p151): Sessional Hymns on the dread judgment seat and the holy Martyrs, followed by the Penitential Canon of Saint Joseph and the Canon to Saint John the Baptist, contemplating his immaculate and ascetic life in the wilderness as a mediator between the Old and New Covenants.
