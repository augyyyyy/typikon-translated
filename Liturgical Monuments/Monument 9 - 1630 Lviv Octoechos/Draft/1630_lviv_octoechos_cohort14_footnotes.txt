[^206]: Friday Matins Canons, Tone 4: Odes 8 and 9 of the Canon of the Precious and Life-giving Cross and the Canon to the Most-holy Theotokos (physical leaf p131), meditating on Christ Who was bound by His own will to loose mankind from the temporal bonds of mortality, the holy Martyrs mounted upon their blood as upon a chariot to attain supramundane dwellings, and the maternal lamentations of the Virgin Mother at the foot of the Cross.

[^207]: Friday Matins Cruciform Aposticha and Friday Divine Liturgy Beatitudes (Makarismoi), Tone 4 (physical leaf p132): Stichera praising the Cross as an invincible rampart and standard of victory, followed by the Beatitudes meditating upon Adam's expulsion from paradise through the tree and the wise thief's entrance into Eden through the wood of the Cross.

[^208]: Friday Evening (Vespers for Saturday), Tone 4 (physical leaf p132): Stichera on "Lord, I have cried" to the holy Martyrs, Hierarchs, and Venerables to the melody "Thou hast given a sign," honoring the holy Shepherds who laid down their lives for their spiritual flocks against fierce wolves, and the holy Martyrs as rational whole-burnt offerings.

[^209]: Friday Evening Aposticha and Saturday Matins Kathismata 1 and 2, Tone 4 (physical leaves p132–p133): Sessional Hymns and Stichera for the departed, invoking Christ to grant repose to the souls of His servants in the company of the righteous, followed by Saturday Matins Sessional Hymns celebrating the angelic hosts rejoicing at the memory of the Passion-bearers.

[^210]: Saturday Matins Canons, Tone 4 (physical leaves p133–p134): Odes 1, 3, and 4 of the Canon to All Saints (Holy Martyrs, Hierarchs, and Venerables) and the Canon for the Departed, meditating upon the crossing of the Red Sea, the triumph of female martyrs, and intercessions for the faithful departed to attain the heavenly dwellings of the righteous.

[^211]: Saturday Matins Canons, Tone 4 (physical leaves p134–p135): Odes 5, 6, 7, and 8 to All Saints and for the Departed, invoking Christ who preserved the Three Children unconsumed in the Babylonian furnace, the holy Hierarchs and Venerables illumined by ascetic struggles, and supplications for the departed to be granted peaceful repose in the light of the Saints.

[^212]: Saturday Matins Ode 9 and Praises (Ainoi), Tone 4 (physical leaves p135–p136): Ode 9 contrasting Eve who brought in the curse through disobedience with the Virgin who blossomed forth divine blessing unto the world, followed by the Praises Stichera honoring the martyrs who armed themselves with the Cross to conquer the incorporeal enemy, and concluding with the Aposticha for the departed.

[^213]: Codicological Note on Foliation Omission in the 1630 Lviv Edition: In the original printed foliation of the 1630 Lviv Octoechos, folio number 136 (рл҃ѕ) is omitted in the printed sequence. Tone 4 concludes on Folio 135v (physical leaf p136 Left Page), and Tone 5 begins immediately on Folio 137r (physical leaf p136 Right Page), with complete textual and liturgical continuity. Physical leaf indexing (p136) strictly preserves the continuous leaf count of the physical codex.

[^214]: Tone 5 Little Vespers (physical leaves p136–p137): Beginning of Tone 5 on Saturday afternoon. Opening with Stichera on "Lord, I have cried" celebrating the Lord's Resurrection that shattered hades' gates and blunted the sting of sin, followed by the Resurrectional Aposticha and Stichera to the Most-holy Theotokos to the melody "Rejoice, life-bearing [Cross]".

[^215]: Tone 5 Saturday Great Vespers, "Lord, I have cried" (physical leaves p137–p138): The classical Sunday Resurrectional Stichera attributed to Saint John of Damascus in Tone 5, meditating on the shame of the devil and liberation from death, followed by the Anatolian (Eastern) Stichera contemplating the unwaning Light and the confession of the Holy Trinity in three Hypostases.

[^216]: Tone 5 Saturday Great Vespers Dogmatikon and Aposticha (physical leaf p138): The renowned Dogmatikon Theotokion in Tone 5 ("In the Red Sea of old an image of the unwedded Bride was prefigured..."), followed by the Resurrectional Aposticha arranged according to the Greek acrostic/alphabet, and the Resurrection Troparion in Tone 5 ("Let us, the faithful, hymn and worship the Word...").

[^217]: Tone 5 Sunday Midnight Office (Mesonyktikon) Canon to the Holy Trinity (physical leaves p138–p139): Composed by Saint Metrophanes of Smyrna, contemplating the tri-hypostatic, co-unoriginate, and undivided Godhead, the three-sunned illumination of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, and the vision of Daniel and the Thrice-Holy hymn of the Cherubim and Seraphim.

[^218]: Tone 5 Sunday Matins Resurrectional Sessional Hymns and Hypakoe (physical leaf p140): Sessional Hymns after the 1st and 2nd Kathismata contemplating the holy Myrrh-bearing women at the tomb, the remorse of Peter, the theological confession of the repentant thief upon the cross, and the Angel's proclamation to the Myrrh-bearers.

[^219]: Tone 5 Sunday Matins Anavathmoi and Sunday Canons, Ode 1 (physical leaf p140): Antiphons 1, 2, and 3 in Tone 5, based on the Gradual Psalms (Psalms 119–133 LXX), followed by the Prokeimenon (Psalm 9:33 LXX), and Ode 1 of the Sunday Resurrection Canon ("The steed and rider into the Red Sea..."), the Cross-Resurrection Canon, and the Canon to the Theotokos.
