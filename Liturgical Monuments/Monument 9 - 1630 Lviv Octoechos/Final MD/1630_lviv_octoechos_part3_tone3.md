# TONE 3: SATURDAY VESPERS, SUNDAY MATINS, AND WEEKDAY OFFICES

## ON SATURDAY EVENING, AT GREAT VESPERS

[f. 69rA] *At "Lord, I call:" Stichera of the Resurrection, Tone 3:*[^85]

[f. 69rA] "By Thy Cross, O Christ Savior, the dominion of death was destroyed, and the deceit of the devil was brought to naught; and the human race, being saved through faith, ever brings a hymn unto Thee."

"All things were enlightened by Thy Resurrection, O Lord, and Paradise was opened again; and all creation, praising Thee, ever brings a hymn unto Thee."

"I glorify the power of the Father and the Son, and hymn the authority of the Holy Spirit: the undivided, uncreated Godhead, the consubstantial Trinity, reigning unto ages of ages."

*Other Stichera, Anatolian (Eastern), Tone the same:*

"Before Thy precious Cross we bow down, O Christ, and Thy Resurrection do we sing and glorify; for by Thy wound were we all healed."

"We hymn the Savior Who was incarnate of the Virgin; for for our sake was He crucified, and on the third day He arose:

[f. 69rB] granting unto us great mercy."

"Having descended unto them that were in Hades, Christ proclaimed good tidings, saying: 'Be of good cheer, now have I conquered! I am the Resurrection, I will lead you up, having destroyed the gates of death!' "

"Standing unworthily in Thine all-pure house, we send up an evening hymn, crying from the depths, O Christ God, Who didst enlighten the world by Thy three-day Resurrection: deliver Thy people from the hand of Thine enemies, O Lover of mankind."

*Glory: Both now: Theotokion (Dogmatikon):*

"How should we not marvel at thy God-manbearing childbearing, O all-honored one? For not having accepted the trial of man, O all-immaculate one, thou gavest birth in the flesh without a father unto a Son Who was begotten of the Father without a mother before the ages, having suffered neither change, nor mingling, nor division; but preserving intact the property of each nature."

[f. 69vA] Therefore, O Virgin Mother and Lady, pray Him to save the souls of them that orthodoxly confess thee to be the Mother of God."

### AT THE APOSTICHA, STICHERA OF THE RESURRECTION

*Tone 3:*[^86]

"By Thy passion, O Christ, Thou didst darken the sun, and by the light of Thy Resurrection Thou didst enlighten all things: accept our evening hymn, O Lover of mankind."

*Other Stichera, Alphabetical:*

"Thy life-giving arising, O Lord, enlightened the whole inhabited world, and recalled Thine own corrupt creation. Therefore, loosed from the curse of Adam, we cry aloud: 'All-powerful Lord, glory to Thee!' "

"Being God immutable, Thou didst change in suffering flesh, which creation unable to bear to see, bent down in fear; and groaning, it hymned Thy long-suffering; and having descended into Hades, Thou didst arise on the third day, granting life unto the world and great mercy."

"That Thou mightest deliver our race from death, O Christ, Thou didst endure death, and having arisen as the firstborn from the dead, Thou didst raise up with Thyself them that acknowledged Thee as God; and Thou didst enlighten the world, O Lord, glory to Thee!"

*Glory: Both now: Theotokion:*

"Without seed, from the Holy Spirit, by the Father's good pleasure, thou didst conceive the Son of God, Who is from the Father without mother before all ages; and for our sake, without father from thee, thou didst bear Him in the flesh, and didst nourish the Infant with milk: therefore cease not [f. 69vB] to pray, that our souls may be delivered from tribulations."

*Then: "Now lettest Thou Thy servant depart..." Trisagion. After "Our Father...":*

*Resurrection Troparion, Tone 3:*

"Let the heavenly things rejoice and the earthly things be glad, for the Lord hath wrought dominion with His arm: He hath trampled down death by death, He hath become the firstborn from the dead; He hath delivered us from the belly of Hades, and hath granted unto the world great mercy."

*Glory: Both now: Theotokion:*

"Thee who didst mediate the salvation of our race do we hymn, O Theotokos Virgin: for in the flesh assumed from thee, Thy Son and our God, having accepted suffering through the Cross, hath delivered us from corruption, as He is the Lover of mankind."

# ON SUNDAY, AT THE MIDNIGHT OFFICE
*Triadical Canon. The Composition of Metrophanes [of Smyrna]. Tone 3.*[^87]

*Ode 1. Heirmos:*

*"He Who of old by His nod gathered the waters into one gathering: and O one Trinity, source of Godhead, vouchsafe me the light of Thy thrice-radiant light, that I may sing of Thee in unceasing hymns. Triadikon:"*

"The immaterial ranks sacredly sing, standing with trembling before the primary Light of the Trinity, as written in the book of creation, and we earthborn with one mouth faithfully glorify Thee with sacred sacrifices."

"Of the Mind, and the Word, and the Spirit, [f. 70rA] so great as they call the unoriginate and eternal generation unto Thy Word, from the unbegotten Father: O Son, signifying God, Son of the sole beginning, together with the procession of the Holy Spirit."

"The supreme Light of the one Godhead, having most gloriously shown forth unto all, O pure Virgin who gavest Him birth."

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*

*"He Who brought all things out of nothing: to divide the waters of old, He commanded, watering the earth; prefiguring unto all the Trinitarian hypostasis of the sole Godhead and Dominion."*

"The nature of earthborn minds sings of Thee, the one and thrice-radiant immutable Creator, and we cry unto Thee: 'Deliver me from every assault, and save me!' *Glory:*"

"As in the utterances of prophetic words, and of the all-wise Apostles, preachers of the faith, we glorify Thee as the equal-acting Trinity in one God of all. *Both now:*"

"From the exalted throne descended the Word, lifting up man, as He is the Lover of mankind, through thee, O all-pure one, and the thrice-sunny light shone forth upon all."

*Sessional Hymn (Sessional Hymn), Tone 3.*

*Podoben: "Beholding the beauty of thy virginity" (Красотѣ̀ дѣ́вства твоегѡ̀):*

"O undivided and inseparable Trinity, remember us who bow down before Thee, and who piously hymn and unceasingly call upon Thee: save them whom Thou [f. 70rB] hast fashioned for the sake of the abundance of Thy tender compassion, in Thy holy Church, which Thou didst acquire by Thy power; for Thou knowest, O Merciful One, the weakness of our heart."

*Glory: Both now: Theotokion:*

"I am wholly changed through my lack of self-control, and through my senseless fearlessness I have become wretched; I cannot bear the day whereat all visible and invisible things tremble; O Lady, help me, and dispel the perplexity of my heart; for thou thyself art the hope and protectress of thy servants."

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*

*"Thou hast set firm love toward us: the Father indeed toward Thee, O Father, as a shoot from the root, the Son and the right Spirit, natural shoots and God-planted flowers without beginning, so as to be luminaries of the Godhead in spiritual essences; unceasingly they hymn Thee, the unfathomable God, with whom we also glorify, saying: 'O transcendent Trinity, preserve Thy servants, as Thou art merciful!' "*

*Both now:*

"Thou didst save us from great peril, O abundantly merciful Word of God, Who wast incarnate for our sake, and mystically revealed the thrice-sunny Godhead, Whom we glorify."

*Ode 5. Heirmos:*

*"As Isaiah saw:"*

"The image of the one Sovereign Ruler, as Isaiah saw God: the ranks of three Angels..." [f. 70vA] Persons. Join my voices thereto: a Seraph was sent to proclaim the thrice-radiant Essence and the thrice-sunny Unity.[^88]

*Glory:*

"O Thrice-sunny Unity, Who of old didst bring into being out of non-existence the nature of things visible and invisible: deliver from every assault them that cry unto Thee in faith as the one God, to Whose glory Thou art like."

*Both now:*

"Having become a light-bearing lantern and vessel of honor unto God, O Virgin, we sing of thee with love and bless thee: for from thee was Christ born, in two natures and two wills, Who is One of the Trinity; and the Lord is glorified with Him."

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*

*"The lowest depth of sins..."*

"Through the figures of the most glorious Powers, in a manner resembling an image, to receive an image of the Lord in three Persons, material in human forms."

*Glory:*

"Being the purifier of the hearts of Thy servants, unto the unapproachable light, O Thrice-sunny Lord, and the radiance of Thy glory, vouchsafe unto our souls that which is desirable by Thine unutterable divine beauty."

*Both now:*

"Open unto me the gates of the light born of thy womb, O all-pure one: that I may behold the thrice-radiant light of the Godhead, and glorify thee as Lady of all."

[f. 70vB] *Sessional Hymn, Tone 3.*

*Podoben: "Beholding the beauty of thy virginity" (Красотѣ дѣвства):*

"Having cleansed him as Thou art merciful, deliver Thy righteous servant from those ages, and cast him not into the fire of Gehenna: for Thou alone, O Christ, art abundantly merciful."

*Glory: Both now: Theotokion:*

"Grant me teaching of groaning, as unto the Publican, O Virgin, and tears of the harlot: that bringing forth Thy judgment, I may cry: 'Have mercy on me, O all-merciful one, and lead me forth from my perplexity by this thy grace, granting me a stream of compunction; for unto thee have I fled, O all-pure one!' "

*Ode 7. Heirmos:*

*"As of old the pious..."*

"Show forth the God-originating, divine Abraham of threefold Person, whose good is higher than grievous sinful darkening and passions; by which grace-bestowing mysteries: 'O God of our fathers, blessed art Thou!' "

"Let us alone desire the harvest of Thy life for me, in three Hypostases and distinct properties, of Father and Son and Spirit, crying together: 'O God of our fathers!' "

*Glory:*

"Abraham of old, seeing God in three Hypostases near the oak of Mamre, received Isaac as the reward of hospitality for mercy, Whom we also now glorify as the God of our fathers."

*Both now:*

"Having been born on earth, a man, the Creator: in a God-befitting manner and in virginity, O all-pure one, of thy womb, and He deified us, O blessed, all-immaculate, and all-pure Theotokos."

*Ode 8. Heirmos:*

*"In the unenduring fire..."*

"The unoriginate Father, as root, the Word, and the Spirit, being co-unoriginate [f. 71rA] by nature, as the clarity of the super-essential Godhead, showed forth the one glory and power of the Trinity, Whom we all sing of, strengthen unto the ages."

*Glory:*

"Thou dost direct by Thy bright ranks the heavenly orders to sing unceasingly with thrice-holy divine hymns: O Father, Word of like form, and Spirit, the thrice-radiant and equal-in-power dominion; therefore we sing of Thee unto all ages."

*Both now:*

"The prophetic utterances, having seen thy childbearing from afar, O Theotokos, praised it as unutterable, and the God born above nature: and in harmony with this we sing of Him as the Lord, and we honor thee who gavest Him birth, O Virgin, unto all ages."

*Ode 9. Heirmos:*

*"A new wonder and God-befitting..."*

"The God-beholding ranks of the incorporeal ones, wishing to ascend on high as with material wings to behold the glory of the Thrice-radiant One: yet being greatly unable to comprehend that Godhead by reason of the light, they unceasingly cry out a hymn, with whom in harmony, O unique Trinity, we glorify Thee."

*Glory:*

"With love, the nature upon earth, having received Thee with an intelligent soul in speech, we hymn Thee, O Master, God Almighty, the one nature in truth and the Trinitarian Persons, from our whole heart; therefore, O Bountiful One, as Thou art abundantly merciful, have compassion on us."

*Both now:*

"Show me forth as a temple of Thy monarchical and all-bright Godhead, O Knower of all things: teach me to serve Thee, the Creator [f. 71rB] of the ages, and spiritually to behold Thine unutterable glory, through the intercessions of the only Theotokos, whom worthily, as most glorious, we magnify."

### AT MATINS

*After the Six Psalms:*

*"God is the Lord, and hath appeared unto us..." in Tone 3. And we say the Resurrection Troparion:*

"Let the heavenly things rejoice..." *Glory: Both now: Theotokion:* "Thee who didst mediate..." *Then the customary reading of the Kathismata of the Psalter.*

*After the First Kathisma, Resurrection Sessional Hymns, Tone 3:*[^89]

"Christ is risen from the dead, the firstfruits of them that slept, the firstborn of creation, and Maker of all things that have come to be; the corrupted nature of our race hath He renewed in Himself. No longer dost thou have dominion, O death; for the Master of all hath destroyed thy dominion."

*Glory:*

"At Thine immutable Godhead and voluntary passion, O Lord, Hades was dismayed and wailed aloud, saying: 'I tremble at the flesh of an incorruptible hypostasis; I behold the Invisible One secretly warring against me; therefore also those whom I hold cry out: Glory, O Christ, to Thy Resurrection!' "

*Both now: Theotokion:*

"With great love the heavenly ranks were refreshed, and the earth was dismayed with trembling, when the all-pure voice came upon thee, O Theotokos; for it became a single festival unto both, when the bodiless joy was brought unto thee; therefore with the Angel we cry unto thee: 'Rejoice, O pure dwelling-place of God!' "

*After the Second Kathisma, Resurrection Sessional Hymns:*

[f. 71vA] *Resurrectional, Tone 3:*[^90]

"Having tasted death in the flesh, O Lord, Thou didst cut off the bitterness of death by Thy rising, and didst strengthen man against it, calling back the overthrow of the ancient curse, O Defender of our life: O Christ God, glory to Thee!"

*Glory:*

"Thy comprehension-surpassing Crucifixion and unutterable Resurrection do we faithful glorify, an ineffable mystery; for today death and Hades have been despoiled, and the human race hath put on incorruption; therefore also with thanksgiving we cry aloud: Glory, O Christ, to Thy Resurrection!"

*Both now: Theotokion:*

"Incomprehensible and uncircumscribed, consubstantial with the invisible Father, thou didst secretly contain in thy womb, O Theotokos; one and unconfused Godhead of the Trinity, we have recognized how to glorify thy childbearing in the world; therefore also with thanksgiving we cry unto thee: 'Rejoice, full of grace!' "

Understand the Transformer of corruption, and say unto God: How awesome are Thy works, for Thou hast saved the human race!' "

Cleanse, O Good One, through the lance of Thy side, tearing up the handwriting of those translated unto Thee, and the partition-wall of their transgressions; deign that they may softly hymn Thee, O Savior: Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord, sing and exalt Him unto the ages.

Theotokion:

Thou hast appeared as a mystical cloud, O pure Virgin, unto those who are parched with the thirst of mortification, pouring forth living water of forgiveness and immortality for the dead, unto all who sing with faith, crying: Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord, sing and exalt Him unto the ages.

Ode 9. Heirmos.

Moses saw Thee upon Mount Sinai in the Bush, who didst unburnt conceive the fire of the Godhead in Thy womb; and Daniel saw Thee as the unhewn mountain; and Isaiah cried aloud, calling Thee the rod that sprouted from the root of David.

Like sheep were ye brought unto Him Who was slaughtered for our sake, and ye filled the divine choirs of Angels with joy, O passion-bearers of Christ; therefore, by your prayers on high, establish all men, and deliver them from the harmful deception of the enemy.

[f. 100vA] As luminaries possessing the living Word, enlighten our souls, O most-sacred High Priests of Christ, having received the fire of the Spirit; ye consumed the passions of deception and destroyed the generation of idols.[^146]

With the holy members of the Church let us honor the multitude of the Venerable, who cried aloud before the Law and under the Law through pure life; and let us praise the choir of holy women, crying aloud: O Lord, by their prayers save us all.

*Hymn for the Departed (Поко́инъ):*

Thy burial, O Christ, and Thy Resurrection became life unto all. Therefore, taking courage, we cry aloud unto Thee: together with Thine elect servants, give rest unto the faithful servants whom Thou hast received, and forgive all their transgressions, as the all-good God.

Theotokion:

Dreadful is this place! cried Jacob aloud when he beheld Thy prefiguration in the Ladder, O Mother of God, un-deceiving glory of the martyrs, praise of the venerable, proclamation of all the Prophets, and salvation of the faithful.

Another [Canon]. Heirmos.

Her who was prefigured by the bush and by fire on Sinai unto Moses the lawgiver, and who without being burned conceived the divine Fire in her womb: the all-radiant and unquenchable lamp, the true Mother of God, we honor in hymns, magnifying her.

Troparion:

Now as the only good and mankind-loving God, by the supplications of the martyrs, give forgiveness of transgressions unto those who have been translated unto Thee, O Merciful One, in the land of the meek, the land of gladness, [f. 100vB] unto those who unceasingly magnify Thee with hymns.

In the holy tabernacles, in the bosom of Abraham, number those whom Thou hast received, O Christ, with the righteous, where the light of Thy countenance shines, the ineffable and divine, and true everlasting joy that abides unto the ages.

Make Thy servants whom Thou hast translated by Thy will worthy of Thy blessed life, eternal good things, unceasing delight, and true gladness, O Giver of life, in a place of pasture, beside the waters of rest.

Theotokion:

As the holy Ark and candlestick of the Tabernacle of the Lawgiver, O all-immaculate Lady, Thou didst receive in Thy womb Thy Creator, Who by His death overthrew the ancient weapon found against us and the law of death.

AT THE PRAISES: STICHERA

Martyric. Tone 3.[^147]

Come, all ye peoples, let us honor the memory of the holy passion-bearers; for they became a spectacle unto Angels and men, and received crowns of victory from Christ; and they pray for our souls.

The soldiers of Christ cast away the fear of kings and tyrants, and with holy boldness and courage confessed Him, the Lord God of all and our King; and they pray for our souls.

The powers of the holy Angels were astonished at the contests of the martyrs, how being clothed in flesh they disregarded tortures, having become imitators of the passions of Christ the Savior; and they pray [f. 101rA] for our souls.

Ye shine as luminaries in the world, and even after death, O holy martyrs, having fought the good fight and possessing boldness, pray unto Christ that our souls may find mercy.

Glory... Both now... Theotokion:

Without seed Thou didst conceive of the Holy Spirit; and glorifying Thee, we sing: Rejoice, O most-holy Virgin!

AT THE APOSTICHA, STICHERA

*For the Departed. Tone 3.*

*Podoben: "Great is the power of Thy Cross, O Lord" (Ве́ліа Кр́та твое́го Гди):* [^148]

I glorify Thy precious Cross, by which life was bestowed and the enjoyment of delight unto those who sing of Thee in faith and love, O only abundant in mercy; therefore we cry unto Thee, O Christ God: Give rest unto those translated from among us, where there is a dwelling of all who rejoice in Thee.

*Verse: Blessed are they whom Thou hast chosen and taken to Thyself, O Lord.*

O only Merciful and Tender-compassionate One, Who possessest an unfathomable abyss of goodness, and knowest human nature which Thou didst fashion: we pray Thee, O Christ God, give rest unto those translated from among us, where there is a dwelling of all who rejoice in Thee.

*Verse: Their souls shall dwell among good things.*

Thou didst abide in the tomb as a man; but by invincible power as God Thou didst raise up those who sleep in the tombs, who offer unceasing hymns unto Thee; therefore we pray Thee, O Christ God:

[f. 101rB] give rest unto those translated from among us, where there is a dwelling of all who rejoice in Thee.

Glory... Both now... Theotokion:

We all know Thee, O Mother of God, as an intelligible lamp bearing the light of the Godhead united unto the coarseness of human nature; pray Thou unto Thy Son and God to give rest unto those translated from among us, where there is gladness for all who rejoice.

ON SATURDAY, THE BEATITUDES

Tone 3.[^149]

Thou didst cast out our forefather Adam from Paradise when he set aside Thy commandment, O Christ; but the thief who confessed Thee upon the cross Thou didst bring into it, O Compassionate One, as he cried: Remember me, O Savior, in Thy kingdom.

Mingling yourselves with fiery wounds, O passion-bearers of Christ, ye found heavenly dew which refreshed and strengthened you to endure the bitter bodily pains; therefore ye ease every pain continually for our souls.

O sacred hierarchs, glorious Prophets, multitude of the venerable, and choir of divine women, through fasting ye received heavenly glory, trampling down the deception of the enemy; by their prayers, O Savior, have mercy upon Thy servants.

*Hymn for the Departed (Поко́инъ):*

Where Thy light shines in the mansions of Thine elect, O God, where delight flows evermore, establish those who have been translated from among us in faith, O Jesus, overlooking their transgressions, O Savior, that we may diligently glorify Thee.

Glory...

[f. 101vA] Glory... Triadicon:

To the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, the divine Trinity: we the faithful bow down before the uncreated Trinity, Who art in three Persons and in one Godhead, ever faithfully glorified by the bodiless powers in thrice-holy hymns.

Both now... Theotokion:

Enlightenment and cleansing, and deliverance for all through Him Who was revealed from Thee, [f. 101vB] O Lady full of the grace of God, fervently beseech Him to deliver us from all condemnation at the dread future judgment, O Most-pure One, who ever faithfully hymn Thee.

[Woodcut: The Golgotha Crucifixion scene, depicting the Cross of Christ on Mount Golgotha flanked by the spear on the left and the reed with sponge on the right, bearing the titulus inscribed "Jesus Christ, King of Glory" (*ІС ХС ЦРЬ СЛВЫ*), with the walled city of Jerusalem in the background and the skull of Adam in the cavern below.][^150]

[f. 102rA] ON SATURDAY EVENING, AT LITTLE VESPERS:

Resurrection Stichera, Tone 4.[^151]

Unceasingly bowing down before Thy life-creating Cross, O Christ, we glorify Thy three-day Resurrection; for thereby Thou hast renewed corrupted human nature, O Almighty One, and hast renewed for us the ascent into heaven, as the only good and mankind-loving God.

Thou didst loose the condemnation of the tree of disobedience, O Savior, by willingly being nailed to the wood of the Cross; and descending into hades, O Mighty One, Thou didst tear apart the bonds of death as God; therefore we bow down before Thy Resurrection from the dead, crying aloud with joy: O Almighty Lord, glory to Thee!

Thou didst shatter the gates of hades, O Lord, and by Thy death didst destroy the kingdom of death; and Thou didst set free the human race from corruption, granting life, incorruption, and great mercy unto the world.

Glory... Both now... Theotokion:

Great and most-glorious is the miracle wrought in Thee! And who is able to speak of the mighty acts of the Lord? For the wonders wrought in Thee, [f. 102rB] O Mother of God, surpass our understanding, being worthy of great astonishment; for being unable to speak of Thy mystery which cannot be comprehended, who hath ever heard from the ages of a Virgin nursing, and a Mother appearing on earth without a man, and carrying her Creator as an infant upon her arms? Therefore, O Lady, all generations unceasingly bless Thee.

AT THE APOSTICHA, STICHERON

Resurrectional. Tone 4.[^152]

Ascending the Cross, O Lord, Thou didst wipe away our ancestral curse; and descending into hades, Thou didst set free the eternal prisoners, granting incorruption unto the human race. Therefore, singing we glorify Thy life-creating and saving Resurrection.

Other Stichera to the Most-holy Mother of God,

*Same Tone.*

*Podoben: "Being called from on high" (Зва́нный бы́вше):*

*Verse: Virgins shall be brought unto the King after her, her companions shall be brought unto Thee.*

Save everyone who piously bows down down before Thy birth-giving, [f. 102vA] save from the assault of the enemy, O all-immaculate Mother of God, as the Protectress of our race; having now obtained Thee as our helper, we all possess a refuge and confirmation, and an advocate before the Master, Christ God; whom pray Thou to grant peace unto the world, we beseech Thee, and forgiveness of transgressions unto those who flee unto Thy protection.

*Verse: Hear, O daughter, and see, and incline thine ear.*

Behold, I lay bare unto Thee the sores of my soul, and show Thee the wounded afflictions which robbers have inflicted upon me through wicked thoughts and demonic agitations, O all-immaculate Mother of God, who gavest birth to the Physician of souls and bodies; grant me healing of these by Thy supplication, pouring forth the oil of Thy compassion and goodness according to the dignity of salvation and deliverance.

*Verse: I will remember thy name in every generation and generation.*

I, wretched one, do not hide my ways, for I have acquired all things that God hates; I have defiled flesh [f. 102vB] and my spirit, and the intellectual thoughts, with unfitting deeds and vile words, with tongue and deeds, senses, and most-grievous acts. O Mother of God, grant me amendment of all these, that I may not be put to shame by my evil customs; I fall down and weep for the wretched deeds I have committed walking through this life.

Glory... Both now... Theotokion:

All the sins I have committed and my iniquities which I have practiced, therefore I fall down before Thee: have mercy upon me, O Mother of God, all-immaculate Lady; cast me not away, nor put me to shame, O Helper of sinners; enlighten my humble and darkened heart; cleanse it with tears, weeping, sighing, and thoughts of compunction, that I may escape the unendurable fire and the worm; before the end, O all-wretched soul, hasten and repent fervently, and crying aloud with tears, say: O Mother of God, have compassion upon me!

Then: Now lettest Thou Thy servant depart: Trisagion. Troparion: The radiant proclamation of the Resurrection: Theotokion: The mystery hidden from the ages: and Dismissal.

### Notes

[^85]: Folio 69r — Hymnographic Structure: Tone 3 Saturday Great Vespers at *"Lord, I call"*. Folio 69r opens with a woodcut headpiece and decorative title vyaz introducing Tone 3 (*"Гла́съ Тре́тій"*), featuring the Resurrection Stichera (*"Твои́мъ Крⷭто́мъ Хе҃ Сп҃се..."*), Anatolian (Eastern) Stichera, and the Dogmatikon Theotokion (*"Ка́кѡ недиви́мся, Бгомꙋ́жному рождествꙋ̀ ти́..."* / *"How should we not marvel..."*).
[^86]: Folio 69v — Typikon Rubrics: Tone 3 Great Vespers Aposticha and Sunday Resurrection Troparion. Great Vespers concludes with the Resurrection Aposticha (*"Стрⷭтїю твое́ю Хе҃..."*), the alphabetical stichera, and the Troparion of Tone 3 (*"Да веселя́тся нб҃наѧ..."* / *"Let the heavenly things rejoice..."*).
[^87]: Folio 69v — Hymnographic Realia: Tone 3 Sunday Midnight Office (Mesonyktikon) Triadical Canon by Saint Metrophanes of Smyrna. Folio 69v introduces the Sunday Midnight Office Canon to the Holy and Consubstantial Trinity (*"Творе́нїе Митрофа́ново"*, Heirmos *"И́же во́ды дре́вле мані́емъ..."*), an exalted patristic contemplation of the triune Godhead, praising the Father unbegotten, the Son begotten, and the Holy Spirit proceeding.
[^88]: Tone 3 Sunday Midnight Office, Triadical Canon: Metrophanes of Smyrna (9th century) composed the triadical canons for the Sunday Midnight Office across the eight tones of the Octoechos. In Greek hymnography, the acrostic forms Ἐν τῇ Τριάδι τὸν τρίτον πλέκω λόγον ("Unto the Trinity I weave this third discourse").
[^89]: Tone 3 Sunday Matins, Resurrection Sessional Hymns (after the First Kathisma): "Christ is risen from the dead, the firstfruits of them that slept..." quotes 1 Corinthians 15:20 verbatim. The Slavonic text reads: "Хс ѡ мертвыхъ воста, начатокъ оумершимъ, первенецъ твари, и содѣтель всѣмъ бывшимъ..."
[^90]: Tone 3 Sunday Matins, Resurrection Sessional Hymns (after the Second Kathisma): Meditating on the harrowing of Hades and Christ's voluntary passion in the flesh, cutting off the bitterness of death and destroying the ancient curse.
[^91]: Tone 3 Sunday Matins, Hypakoe: The Hypakoe (Greek: Ὑпакоή, "attentive listening" or "response") recounts the dazzling appearance of the Angel at the empty tomb and his announcement of the Resurrection unto the Myrrh-bearing women.
[^92]: Tone 3 Sunday Matins, Anabathmoi (Antiphons of the Steps) and Prokeimenon: The Anabathmoi are based on the Gradual Psalms (LXX 119–133). Antiphon 1 is rooted in Psalm 125 (LXX): "When the Lord turned again the captivity of Sion..." The Prokeimenon quotes Psalm 95:10 (LXX): "Say among the heathen that the Lord reigns: He hath also established the world so that it cannot be moved."
[^93]: Tone 3 Sunday Matins, Canon of the Resurrection: Composed by St. John of Damascus. In Greek hymnography, the acrostic is Ὁ τρίτος οὗτος ὕμνος ἐντὸς τῆς тριάδος ("This third hymn is within the Trinity").
[^94]: Tone 3 Sunday Matins, Canon of the Cross and Resurrection: A classic Byzantine liturgical composition meditating upon the mystery of Christ's life-giving death upon the Cross and His victorious descent into Hades.
[^95]: Tone 3 Sunday Matins, Resurrection Kontakion: Composed by St. Romanos the Melodist (Tone 3, Podoben: "The works of the day"): "Thou didst arise today from the tomb, O Compassionate One..."
[^96]: Tone 3 Sunday Matins, Resurrection Ikos: The Ikos follows the Kontakion after Ode 6, meditating upon the cosmic jubilation of heaven and earth at the shattering of the gates of Hades.
[^97]: Tone 3 Sunday Divine Liturgy, The Beatitudes (Makarismoi): In Byzantine and Ruthenian practice, these troparia are chanted during the Divine Liturgy with the Beatitudes (Matthew 5:3–12), alternating between the confession of the Good Thief and the redemption of Adam.
[^98]: Sunday Evening (Vespers for Monday), Tone 3: Penitential Stichera on "Lord, I have cried" (*Kyrie Ekekraxa*), marking the transition into the weekday liturgical cycle dedicated to personal repentance and the heavenly angelic hierarchy.
[^99]: Monday Matins, Tone 3 Sessional Hymns (after the First Kathisma): Penitential sessional hymns emphasizing personal mortality ("the dust in the grave sings not") and supplication before the dread judgment seat of Christ.
[^100]: Monday Matins, Penitential Canon: Composed by St. Joseph the Hymnographer (9th century), Tone 3, characterized by intimate compunction, tears of repentance, and the examples of the harlot and the publican.
[^101]: Monday Matins, Canon to the Holy Bodiless Powers (Angels): Composed by St. Theophanes the Branded (Graptos, 9th century), Tone 3, celebrating the nine celestial orders who stand before the throne of the Holy Trinity.
[^102]: Monday Matins, The Beatitudes: Troparia chanted with the Beatitudes on Monday, invoking the intercession of the heavenly angelic hosts and the holy martyrs before the second coming.
[^103]: Monday Evening (Vespers for Tuesday), Tone 3: Stichera on "Lord, I have cried" dedicated to the Holy Prophet, Forerunner, and Baptist John, inaugurating Tuesday's commemoration of the prophets and the Baptist.
[^104]: Tone 3 Tuesday Matins, Sessional Hymns (after the Third Kathisma): Tone 3, Podoben "Of the divine faith" (*Божественныя вѣры*), dedicated to the Holy Forerunner, beseeching him to quiet the tempest of afflictions and illnesses.
[^105]: Tone 3 Tuesday Matins, Penitential Canon: Composed by St. Joseph the Hymnographer (9th century), Tone 3. The Heirmos of Ode 1 refers to God drying the abyss of the Red Sea and drowning Pharaoh's chariots (Exodus 14–15).
[^106]: Tone 3 Tuesday Matins, Canon to the Holy Forerunner: Tone 3, Ode 1 Heirmos: "He Who of old by His divine nod gathered the waters into one gathering..." (referencing Genesis 1:9 and Exodus 14:21–22).
[^107]: Tone 3 Tuesday Matins, Penitential Canon, Ode 3 Heirmos: "O barren and childless soul, acquire blessed fruit..." Echoing the Canticle of Hannah in 1 Kingdoms 2:1–2 (LXX), "My heart is established in the Lord... there is none holy as the Lord."
[^108]: Tone 3 Tuesday Matins, Penitential Canon, Ode 4 Heirmos: "Foreseeing the overshadowed mountain, even thy pure womb, O pure one..." Drawing upon the Canticle of the Prophet Habakkuk (Habakkuk 3:3 LXX: "God shall come from Teman, and the Holy One from the thick and shadowy mountain of Paran").
[^109]: Tone 3 Tuesday Matins, Canon to the Forerunner, Ode 6: Drawing upon Jonah 2:1–3 (LXX) and Psalm 68:2–3 (LXX) ("The lowest depth of sins hath surrounded me..."), while celebrating John the Baptist baptizing Christ in the streams of the Jordan.
[^110]: Tone 3 Tuesday Matins, Penitential Canon, Ode 7 Heirmos: Meditating upon the Three Holy Children in the Babylonian furnace (Daniel 3:23–26 LXX), who were preserved unconsumed by the cooling angel of God.
[^111]: Tone 3 Tuesday Matins, Penitential Canon, Ode 9 Heirmos: Quoting the Canticle of Zechariah (Luke 1:68–79): "Blessed be the Lord God of Israel; for He hath visited and redeemed His people, and hath raised up an horn of salvation for us in the house of David His servant..."
[^112]: Tone 3 Tuesday Divine Liturgy, The Beatitudes (Makarismoi): Troparia chanted with the Beatitudes on Tuesday, alternating the confession of the Good Thief (Luke 23:42) with petitions through the intercession of the Baptist, the holy martyrs, and the angelic orders.
[^113]: Tuesday Evening (Vespers for Wednesday), Tone 3: Stichera on "Lord, I have cried" dedicated to the Precious and Life-Giving Cross, inaugurating the liturgical observance of Wednesday commemorating Christ's betrayal and crucifixion.
[^114]: Tuesday Evening (Vespers for Wednesday), Aposticha Stichera of the Cross: Typological meditation on Moses lifting up his hands upon the mountain against Amalek (Exodus 17:11–12) as a prefiguration of Christ stretching out His hands upon the Cross.
[^115]: Tone 3 Wednesday Matins, Sessional Hymns of the Cross (after the First Kathisma): Sessional hymns celebrating the wood of the Cross touching the earth and reaching unto the heavens, prefigured in the cypress, pine, and cedar (Isaiah 60:13).
[^116]: Tone 3 Wednesday Matins, Canon of the Precious Cross: Composed by St. Joseph the Hymnographer, Tone 3. The Heirmos of Ode 1 celebrates God gathering the waters into one gathering and dividing the Red Sea (Genesis 1:9; Exodus 14:21).
[^117]: Tone 3 Wednesday Matins, Canon to the Most Holy Theotokos: Companion canon to the Canon of the Cross for Wednesday Matins, meditating on the Mother of God standing beside the Cross and shedding maternal tears.
[^118]: Tone 3 Wednesday Matins, Canon of the Cross, Ode 4 Heirmos: "Thou hast placed toward us steadfast love, O Lord, for Thou didst give Thine Only-begotten Son unto death for us..." Reflecting John 3:16 and Romans 5:8.
[^119]: Tone 3 Wednesday Matins, Canon of the Cross, Ode 9 Heirmos: The Burning Bush upon Mount Sinai (Exodus 3:2) as a prophetic type of the Virgin Theotokos conceiving the unquenchable divine fire without being consumed.
[^120]: Tone 3 Wednesday Divine Liturgy, The Beatitudes: Troparia chanted with the Beatitudes on Wednesday, commemorating the piercing of Christ's side with a lance (John 19:34) and the fountain of immortality poured out for the life of the world.
[^121]: Wednesday Evening (Vespers for Thursday), Tone 3: Stichera on "Lord, I have cried" celebrating the Holy Apostles as witnesses of the Word, and St. Nicholas the Wonderworker of Myra in Lycia as a swift protector and deliverer.
[^122]: Tone 3 Thursday Matins, Sessional Hymns to the Apostles (after the First Kathisma): Hymning the Holy Apostles who like wise fishermen cast their nets into the deep and drew all nations to faith in the undivided Trinity.
[^123]: Tone 3 Thursday Matins, Sessional Hymns (after the Third Kathisma): Sessional hymn to St. Nicholas of Myra, celebrating his miraculous deliverance of the three innocent men condemned to death by an unjust judge (an event famously recorded in his Vita).
[^124]: Tone 3 Thursday Matins, Canon to the Holy Apostles: Composed by St. Theophanes the Branded (Graptos, 9th century), Tone 3, celebrating the Apostles as immovable pillars of the Church and heralds of the divine illumination of the Gospel.
[^125]: Tone 3 Thursday Matins, Canon to Saint Nicholas: Composed by St. Joseph the Hymnographer, Tone 3. The Heirmos of Ode 1 refers to the crossing of the Red Sea (Exodus 15:1–2), and praises Nicholas as an unwaning beacon and pillar of piety.
[^126]: Tone 3 Thursday Matins, Canons to the Apostles and St. Nicholas, Odes 5–6: Meditations on the Apostles as heralds of the unwaning Light and Nicholas as the wise pilot guiding souls tossed upon the tempestuous waves of life.
[^127]: Tone 3 Thursday Matins, Canons, Ode 7: Allusion to the Three Holy Children in the Persian furnace (Daniel 3:23–26 LXX), contrasted with Arius whose impious heresy Nicholas strangled with the sinews of his orthodox theological words.
[^128]: Tone 3 Thursday Matins, Ode 9 Heirmos: "In the shadow and letter of the Law, we the faithful perceive a figure..." Based on Exodus 13:2 and Luke 2:23 ("Every male that opens the womb shall be called holy to the Lord").

[^130]: Tone 3 Thursday Divine Liturgy, The Beatitudes (Makarismoi): Troparia chanted with the Beatitudes in Tone 3, specifically naming the Twelve Apostles: Peter, Paul, Mark, Luke, Matthew, Simon, James, Andrew, John, Bartholomew, Philip, and Thomas.
[^131]: Thursday Evening (Vespers for Friday), Tone 3: Stichera on "Lord, I have cried" dedicated to the Precious Cross, sung to the automelon "They weighed thirty pieces of silver" (*Поставиша тридесяте*), commemorating the price of betrayal (Zechariah 11:12; Matthew 26:15) and Christ's voluntary Passion.
[^132]: Thursday Evening Vespers, Aposticha of the Cross: Typological meditation on Moses stretching forth his hands upon the mountain against Amalek (Exodus 17:11–12) as a primordial prophetic type of Christ stretching out His hands upon the Cross.
[^133]: Friday Matins, Sessional Hymns of the Cross (after the First Kathisma): Celebrating the cosmic height of the Cross touching heaven and earth, prefigured in the wood of the cypress, pine, and cedar (Isaiah 60:13).
[^134]: Friday Matins, Canon of the Precious Cross: Composed by St. Joseph the Hymnographer, Tone 3. The Heirmos of Ode 1 refers to Israel passing dry-shod through the Red Sea (Exodus 14:21–22).

[^137]: Friday Matins, Canon of the Cross, Ode 7 Heirmos: The Three Holy Children in the Babylonian furnace (Daniel 3:23–26 LXX) preserved unburnt by the divine dew, prefiguring the unconsumed womb of the Virgin Mother.
[^138]: Friday Matins, Canon of the Cross, Ode 9: The cosmic convulsions at the Crucifixion (Matthew 27:45, 51–52: the sun darkened, rocks rent, and the temple veil torn in twain), and Christ drawing all things to Himself upon the Cross.
