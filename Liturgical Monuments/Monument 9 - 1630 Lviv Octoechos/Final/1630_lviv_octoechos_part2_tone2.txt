# TONE 2: SATURDAY VESPERS, SUNDAY MATINS, AND WEEKDAY OFFICES

## ON SATURDAY EVENING, AT GREAT VESPERS, AT "LORD, I HAVE CRIED"

[f. 36rA] *Resurrectional Stichera, Tone 2:*[^46]

*Composition of Saint John of Damascus.*

Unto the pre-eternal Word of God born of the Father, Who was incarnate of the Virgin Mary, come, let us bow down: for having endured crucifixion, He was committed unto burial as He Himself willed; and He rose from the dead, and saved me, wandering man!

Christ our Savior, being nailed upon the Cross, tore up the handwriting against us, and abolished the dominion of death: we bow down before His third-day resurrection!

With the Archangels let us hymn the resurrection of Christ: for He is the Redeemer and Savior of our souls, and in fearful glory and mighty power He comes again to judge the world which He created!

*Other Eastern Stichera. Composition of Anatolius, Patriarch of Constantinople. Tone 2:*

Thee Who wast crucified and buried, the Angel proclaimed, O Master, and said [f. 36rB] unto the women: "Come and see where the Lord lay! For He is risen as He said, for He is all-powerful!" Therefore we bow down before Thee, the only Immortal One: O Giver of life, Christ, have mercy on us!

By Thy Cross Thou didst abolish the curse of the tree; by Thy burial Thou didst put to death the dominion of death; and by Thy resurrection Thou didst enlighten the human race: therefore we cry unto Thee: O Benefactor, Christ our God, glory to Thee!

The guards were terrified, O Lord, and the gates of death and the gatekeepers of Hades were afraid when they beheld Thee: for Thou didst shatter the brazen gates and didst crush the iron bars, and didst lead us forth from darkness and the shadow of death, and didst break our bonds asunder! Singing a song of victory, we cry: [f. 36vA] enter into the house of the Lord, and let us fall down, saying: Thou Who wast crucified upon the Tree, and didst rise from the dead, and art in the bosom of the Father: cleanse our sins!

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion (Dogmatikon):*

The shadow of the Law passed away when grace arrived: for as the bush burned with fire and was not consumed, so as a Virgin thou didst give birth, and a Virgin thou didst remain; instead of a pillar of fire, the Sun of Righteousness hath shone forth; instead of Moses, Christ, the Salvation of our souls![^47]

### AT THE APOSTICHA

*Resurrectional Stichera, Tone 2:*

Thy resurrection, O Christ Savior, enlightened the whole universe and recalled Thine own creation: O All-powerful Lord, glory to Thee!

*Other Stichera in Alphabetical Order:*

*Verse: The Lord is King, He is robed in majesty.*

By the Tree, O Savior, Thou didst abolish the curse of the tree; the dominion of death Thou didst put to death by Thy burial, and Thou didst enlighten our race by Thy resurrection: therefore we cry unto Thee: O Giver of life, Christ our God, glory to Thee!

*Verse: For He hath established the world, so that it cannot be moved.*

Thou wast seen nailed upon the Cross, O Christ, reforming the beauty of creation; the soldiers indeed showed cruelty, piercing Thy ribs with a spear, and the Jews asked to seal the tomb, not knowing Thy power: but in Thy tender mercy Thou didst accept burial, and on the third day Thou didst rise: [f. 36vB] O Lord, glory to Thee!

*Verse: Holiness becomes Thy house, O Lord, unto length of days.*

O Christ, Giver of life, Thou didst voluntarily endure passion for the sake of the dead; and descending into Hades as Mighty, Thou didst snatch away those who were awaiting Thy coming there, as from a mighty beast, and didst grant them to dwell in Paradise instead of Hades: therefore grant unto us also who glorify Thy third-day resurrection cleansing of sins and great mercy!

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*

O new wonder, greater than all ancient wonders! For who hath known a Mother to give birth without a man, and to carry in her arms Him Who contains all creation? Born is the Word of God, Whom as an infant, O most pure one, thou didst bear in thine arms, and having maternal boldness toward Him, unceasingly pray for those who honor thee, to have compassion and save our souls!

*Troparion, Tone 2:*

When Thou didst descend unto death, O Life Immortal, then didst Thou slay Hades with the brightness of Thy Godhead; and when Thou didst raise the dead from the nethermost parts, all the heavenly powers cried aloud: O Giver of life, Christ our God, glory to Thee!

*Theotokion:*

All beyond comprehension, all all-glorious are thy mysteries, O Theotokos: sealed in purity and preserved in virginity, thou wast known to be a true Mother, having given birth unto the true God; pray Him that our souls may be saved!

# ON SUNDAY AT THE MIDNIGHT OFFICE, TRIADICAL CANON
[f. 37rA] *Composition of Metrophanes.*[^48]

*Ode 1, Tone 2. Heirmos:*

*He Who submerged of old in the deep: let us hymn the three-personed, single-principled essence of the Godhead, singing: Possessing essentially an inexhaustible abyss of mercy: have compassion and save those who bow down before Thee, as Thou art the Lover of mankind!*

Being the cause, the source and root of the Father in the Son and Thy Holy Spirit, of the consubstantial Godhead: enlighten my soul with the three-sunned Light, and illuminate me by communion!

Divinely granting me brightness, O three-radiant Unity, single in principle: heal the passions in the wounds of my sins by Thy luminous rays, and by sweet communions make me partaker of Thine unapproachable glory, O animate womb and tabernacle!

*Theotokion:*

The stream of actions of our nature, having suffered unworthily and slipped into corruption, O most pure Virgin: the Lord the Word, being incarnate from thy womb, lovingly recalled and mystically taught the all-bright Godhead!

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*

*Establish me upon the rock of faith: the Godhead, first principle of nature, glorifying Thee with equal honor in three Persons; for Thou, proceeding as Life from Life, art without corruption our only God, and none is holy save Thee, O Lord!*

Thou hast not instructed the bodiless ranks [f. 37rB] to praise Thy goodness, O Trinity, undivided Monarchy, unceasingly unto Thee; but accept now also the praise from our clay lips!

Establish upon the rock of faith, and broaden in the ocean of Thy love, the heart and mind of Thy servants, O three-sunned Unity: for Thou art our God, hoping in Whom we are not put to shame!

*Theotokion:*

He Who constituted the substance of creation before all things: united Himself unto thy womb through unfeigned goodness, O Sovereign Lady; and He shone forth unto all in three-sunned light, of one Godhead and Dominion!

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 2.*

*Podoben: "Seeking the highest" (Вы́шнихъ и҆щѧ):*

O Holy Trinity, have mercy on that which Thou hast created, and deliver us from fire and every condemnation, for Thou canst do all things, as Thou art the compassionate and greatly merciful God; therefore we fall down before Thee, crying: We have sinned, forgive, O Lover of mankind!

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*

Thy servants are filled with many wounds, wounded by invisible arrows, and lying upon the bed of despair we cry unto thee, O Sovereign Lady, praying unceasingly on our behalf, who run unto thee in faith!

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*

*I hymn Thee, having heard, O Lord, Thy report... to understand the mystical ranks: even as the bodiless Angels cry unto Thee with fear, O unoriginate Trinity: so we also with clay tongues cry unto Thee: Creator of creation...*

[f. 37vA] ...beholding my heavenly weakness, O Almighty One: therefore have compassion on Thy servant, and lead me back unto better life!

Of the primordial Unity, let us praise the unconfused three Persons, having individual hypostases, yet united and undivided in counsel, in glory, and in Godhead!

*Theotokion:*

The Creator found thee alone from the ages as a pure and all-immaculate temple, O Virgin Theotokos; dwelling in thee, He formed human nature, as He is the Lover of mankind!

*Ode 5. Heirmos:*

*Enlightenment of those lying in darkness: as the greatness of Thy providence over all things, extending rays of salvation unto me, O King of peace: preserve us in Thy faith, for Thou art the Life and Peace of all!*

Appearing in the bush unto Moses in a fiery vision, the Word of the Father was named an Angel, foreseeing Thy coming; whereby Thou didst clearly illuminate unto all the dominion of the one Godhead in three Persons!

Not altering the natural and co-eternal glory, O single Holy Trinity: grant unto those who hymn Thee with Orthodox faith ever to behold Thy glory, unoriginate and single, in three suns!

*Theotokion:*

Being one by nature, yet tried in all things: calling unto the union of the one Dominion

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*

*Held fast in the abyss of sin, O Creator of mercy, have mercy: Thou Who art the calm of the faithful, O God of three suns: save us from innumerable transgressions and from passions!*

By the ineffable deep of goodness, by the uncontainable of Thy brightness and sent Godhead: grant me the light-giving ray!

*Theotokion:*

Ineffably, O Virgin, the Most High became man incorruptibly: clothed in the garment of man, illuminate me with three-sunned light!

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 2.*

*Podoben: "Seeking the highest" (Вы́шнихъ и҆щѧ):*

O Christ God, only Most Good, Who didst pour out Thy blood for our sake, being hung upon the Cross by the counsel of Thy Father and the Spirit: therefore we cry aloud: Remember Thy flock, O Lover of mankind!

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*

O pure one, have mercy on thy servants, and deliver from fire and every torment those who cry day and night unto thy Son, O most pure one: despise not thy flock, by thy prayers, O Mother of Christ!

*Ode 7. Heirmos:*

*Concerning the golden image on the plain of Dura: The Prophets establish that the angelic Godhead is unchanging, O Lord Who alone art immutable and in three Persons: show my heart also ever unchanging, that I may fervently glorify Thee and piously hymn Thee!*

[f. 38rA] The noetic ranks of bodiless essences are illuminated by Thy rays, O God of single dominion and three suns, being by position secondary lights; show them forth as light, as Thou art the ever-living Giver of light!

Illuminated by three-radiant rays, O Most Holy Trinity, in one dominion preserve Thy singers from adverse assaults, that we may unceasingly glorify Thee: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, in one Godhead!

*Theotokion:*

Restoring us and raising us up unto heavenly things without departure: He Who out of ineffable love for mankind became man from the Virgin's womb loved thee, and deified man, and sits with the Father upon the throne of glory!

*Ode 8. Heirmos:*

*In the likeness of gold: O ever-existing Trinity, co-eternal, unoriginate, sole ruler, unchanging unto ages: apart from Thy light-bearers, destroy all the evil assaults of adversaries and disturbances among men; keep Thy servants unharmed ever, O Lord of all!*

Ever-existing and Almighty, uncircumscribed, three-sunned Monarchy, Who didst constitute the world and dost preserve it by Thy power: grant unto the all-perfect, O All-powerful One, to hymn and glorify Thee with the angelic choirs unto all ages! [f. 38rB] of falsehood, and of the divine natural and unchanging nature: Thou didst assume human nature and didst teach us to honor the Trinity as Dominion unto all ages!

Directing righteousness and wisdom: by Thy standings, therefore, singing unto God, and radiant, O most pure one: bless all ye works, hymn and highly exalt Him unto all ages!

*Ode 9. Heirmos:*

*He Who before the sun was God, the Luminary... of the unoriginate Light: by His radiance the Son, Light and natural, shone forth with light; ineffably born of the earth, together appearing without departure...*

Shine forth in Orthodox hearts the light of the Godhead; rising, grant understanding, and give understanding to comprehend and behold the good and perfect hymnody, to magnify and glorify Thee!

O Trinity uncircumscribed in nature... with unsearchable joy, Who art our Creator before creation: have compassion on Thy servants! [f. 38vA] Thee: and [preserve] Thy flock, [by the prayers of the Mother of] God.[^49]

*AT THE PRAISES: STICHERA*[^54]

*Resurrectional. Tone 2:*

Let every breath and all creation glorify Thee, O Lord; for by the Cross Thou didst abolish death, that Thou mightest show unto the people Thy Resurrection from the dead, as the only Lover of mankind.[^55]

Let the Jews declare how the soldiers lost the King Whom they guarded; for why did the stone not keep the Rock of life? Either let them produce Him that was buried, or let them worship Him that is risen, saying with us: "Glory to the multitude of Thy compassions, O our Savior, glory to Thee!"

Rejoice, ye people, and be glad! An angel sitting upon the stone of the sepulchre, he brought good tidings unto us, saying: "Christ is risen, the Savior of the world, and hath filled all things with fragrance!" Rejoice, ye people, and be glad!

An angel indeed brought the greeting "Rejoice!" unto her that was full of grace before Thy conception, O Lord; and an angel rolled away the stone of Thy glorious sepulchre at Thy Resurrection. The one, in place of sorrow, brought tokens of gladness; while the other, in place of death, proclaimed unto us the Master, the Giver of life. Therefore we cry aloud unto Thee: O Benefactor of all, Lord, glory to Thee!

*Other Stichera, Eastern [Anatolian]:*

The women poured forth myrrh with tears upon Thy sepulchre: and were filled [f. 42rA] [of Christ] our God, Who willingly endured the Cross for our sake, and spent three days in Hades: and let them worship His third-day Resurrection, whereby the ends of the whole world were enlightened.

*Verse: Arise, O Lord my God, let Thy hand be lifted up; forget not Thy poor unto the end.*

Thou wast crucified and buried, O Christ, as Thou didst will; Thou didst despoil death, and didst arise in glory, as God and Master, granting eternal life unto the world, and great mercy.

*Verse: I will give thanks unto Thee, O Lord, with my whole heart; I will speak of all Thy marvelous works.*

Truly, ye lawless ones, having sealed the stone, ye have made us worthy of a greater miracle: the guards know it, having come forth today from the sepulchre; and they said: "Say ye that while we were asleep, His disciples came and stole Him." And who steals a dead body, and especially a naked one? He arose by His own power as God, leaving behind His burial wrappings in the tomb. Come, behold, O Jews, how He hath not broken the seals, He Who hath trampled down death, and granted unending life unto mankind, and great mercy!

*The Beatitudes. Tone 2:*

We bring unto Thee the voice of the thief, and we pray: Remember us, O Lord, in Thy Kingdom!

We offer Thy Cross for the remission of transgressions, which Thou didst receive for our sake, O Lover of mankind.

The 1630 Lviv print sets Ode 1 on Folio 39r, then transitions directly across the leaf to Folio 39v where the pre-Kontakion Theotokion of Ode 6 leads immediately into the Resurrectional Kontakion and Ikos, followed by Odes 7, 8, and 9. Physical continuity of Leaf 39 (recto on p41, verso on p42) proves this is the intended typographic layout rather than a missing gathering.
[^52]: Folio 39v — Hymnographic Realia: Sunday Resurrectional Kontakion and Ikos in Tone 2. Composed by Saint Romanos the Melodist, the Kontakion (*"Воскреслъ еси отъ гроба, Спасе всесильне..."*) and Ikos (*"Ты еси свѣтъ омраченнымъ..."*) proclaim Christ's descent into Hades, the shattering of the infernal gates, and the liberation of Adam and all humanity.
[^53]: Folio 40r — Typikon Rubrics: Tone 2 Sunday Matins Ode 8 and 9 Heirmoi. The Heirmoi of Odes 8 (*"Пещь огненная иногда въ Вавулонѣ..."*) and 9 (*"Безначална Родителя и Господь..."*) frame the concluding glorification of the Incarnation, contrasting the Three Youths in the Babylonian furnace with the immaterial light revealed in Christ.
[^54]: Folio 40v — Codicological Apparatus: Omission of Printed Folio 41. In this exemplar, printed Folio 42 (`мв҃`) immediately follows Folio 40v (`м`), omitting Folio 41 (`ма҃`) in the printed foliation numbering sequence. The liturgical text is strictly continuous without omission or lacuna between the conclusion of Tone 2 Sunday Matins Ode 9 on Folio 40v and the Sunday Praises (*"Всяко дыханіе..."*) on Folio 42r.
[^55]: Folio 40v — Hymnographic Apparatus: Sunday Praises (Ainoi / Lauds) and Eastern Stichera. The Praises in Tone 2 open with the Resurrectional Stichera (*"Всяко дыханіе и вся тварь..."*), followed by the Anatolian (Eastern) Stichera recounting the Myrrh-bearing women at the sepulchre and the angelic proclamation of the Resurrection.
[^57]: Folio 42r — Typikon Rubrics: Sunday Vespers for Monday (Incipient Weekday Cycle). Following the Divine Liturgy Beatitudes, Folio 42r initiates Sunday evening Vespers for Monday (*"Въ Недѣлю, въ вечеръ, на Господи, воззвахъ..."*) with Penitential Stichera (*"Имѣяй благоутробія источникъ..."*) modeled on the Podoben *"Егда отъ древа"*.
[^56]: Folio 42r — Liturgical Structure: Sunday Divine Liturgy Beatitudes (Makarismoi). Folio 42r introduces the Sunday Beatitudes in Tone 2 (*"Гласъ Ти приносимъ разбойничь..."*), commemorating the confession of the Good Thief and linking the Cross to Paradise.
[^58]: Folio 42v — Hymnographic Apparatus: Monday Midnight Office and Vespers Stichera to the Bodiless Powers. The liturgical cycle transitions to the weekly commemoration of the Holy Angels and Bodiless Hosts (*"Престоли, Херувими, и Серафими..."*), invoking the nine angelic ranks as unceasing celebrants around the Divine Throne.
[^59]: Folio 43r — Typikon Rubrics: Monday Matins Kathisma Sessional Hymns (Sedalna). Monday Matins features Penitential Sessional Hymns after the 1st and 2nd Kathismata (*"Яко волны морскія восташа на мя..."*, *"Помилуй мя, рече Давидъ..."*), focusing on personal compunction and comparing the penitent's plight to the barren fig tree and David's repentance.
[^60]: Folio 43r — Hymnographic Realia: Monday Matins Canon of Repentance and Canon to the Bodiless Hosts. Monday Matins features the Penitential Canon (*"Грядите, людіе, поемъ пѣснь Христу Богу..."*) paired with the Canon to the Incorporeal Hosts by Saint Theophanes the Branded (*"Твореніе Ѳеофаново"*, Heirmos *"Нетренну, необычну..."*).
[^61]: Folio 46r — Typikon Rubrics: Monday Matins Aposticha and Monday Vespers for Tuesday. Concluding the Monday cycle with the Aposticha (*"Содѣянныхъ моихъ золъ помышляю безмѣстная..."*), Folio 46r transitions to Monday evening Vespers for Tuesday (*"Въ понедѣлокъ въ вечеръ, на Господи, воззвахъ..."*), initiating the weekly commemoration of Saint John the Forerunner and Baptist.
[^62]: Folio 46v — Hymnographic Realia: Tuesday Vespers Stichera to Saint John the Baptist. Dedicated to the Precursor and Baptist of the Lord (*"Проповѣдниче Христовъ и Крестителю..."*), highlighting John as angel, apostle, martyr, prophet, friend of the Bridegroom, and seal of the Prophets bridging the Old and New Covenants.
[^63]: Folio 46v — Typikon Rubrics: Tuesday Matins Kathisma Sessional Hymns. Tuesday Matins continues the cycle of repentance while weaving in petitions to the Forerunner, Apostles, and Martyrs (*"Апостоли, Пророцы, и Мученицы..."*).
[^64]: Folio 47r — Hymnographic Realia: Tuesday Matins Canon of Compunction and Canon to the Forerunner by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer. Tuesday Matins pairs the Penitential Canon of Compunction (*"Канонъ умиленія, Твореніе Іѡсифово"*, Heirmos *"Моѵсейскую пѣснь воспріимши..."*) with the Canon to the Holy Forerunner (*"Инъ канонъ Предтечи, Твореніе Іѡсифово"*, Heirmos *"Во глубинѣ потопи древле..."*).
[^65]: Folio 49r — Paleographical Apparatus: Colophon and Transition to Tuesday Canon Odes 7–9. Folio 49r presents the concluding troparia of Ode 6 and the opening of Ode 7 for Tuesday Matins, bearing the catchword *"въ втор[окъ]"* indicating continuity into the completion of the Tuesday Matins hymnody in Cohort 6.
[^66]: Folio 49v — Hymnographic Realia: Tuesday Matins Canon of Repentance and Canon to the Forerunner (Odes 8–9). Completing the Tuesday Matins hymnody begun in Cohort 5, Folio 49v presents Ode 8 with the Penitential Heirmos *"Въ купинѣ Мѡѵсею..."* (*"In the bush unto Moses..."*) and the second Canon to the Holy Forerunner (*"Пещь ѻгненна аногда въ Вавулѡнѣ..."*), meditating upon Saint John's baptism of Christ in the Jordan streams and entreating tears of repentance.
[^67]: Folio 50v — Typikon Rubrics: Tuesday Matins Aposticha and Beatitudes. Tuesday Matins concludes with penitential Aposticha (*"Содѣянныхъ моихъ зѡлъ помышляю..."*), the Martyrikon honoring the holy Martyrs as an unassailable wall of intercession, and the weekday Beatitudes (Blazhenna) with Troparia invoking the Thief's confession (*"Гласъ ти приноси разбойничь..."*).
[^68]: Folio 50v — Hymnographic Structure: Tuesday Vespers for Wednesday (Precious Cross & Theotokos). Tuesday evening initiates the liturgical commemoration of the Precious Cross with Stichera at *"Lord, I call"* set to the celebrated Automelon / Podoben *"When from the Tree"* (*"Егда ѿ древа..."*), depicting the cosmic convulsions at the Crucifixion (the darkening of luminaries, earthquake, rending of the temple veil) paired with the Compassion of the Mother of God (Stavrotheotokia).
[^69]: Folio 51v — Typikon Rubrics: Wednesday Matins Kathisma Sessional Hymns (Sedalna) of the Precious Cross. Following the first, second, and third poetic Kathismata of the Psalter, Wednesday Matins appoints Sessional Hymns honoring the Cross (*"Спасенїе содѣлалъ посредѣ земля, Христе Боже..."*), reflecting on Christ spreading His immaculate hands upon the Wood to gather the dispersed nations.
[^70]: Folio 51v — Hymnographic Realia: Wednesday Matins Canons of the Precious Cross and Theotokos. Wednesday Matins features the Canon to the Precious and Life-Giving Cross composed by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer (*"Твореніе Їѡсифово"*, Heirmos *"Въ глубинѣ потопи древле..."*) interwoven with the Canon to the Most Holy Theotokos (Heirmos *"Грядѣте людїе, поемъ пѣснь..."*).
[^71]: Folio 54v — Typikon Rubrics: Wednesday Matins Aposticha and Beatitudes. Wednesday Matins concludes with the Aposticha of the Cross (*"Креста твоего древо, Христе Боже, древо животное показа намъ..."*) and the weekday Beatitudes commemorating the Crucifixion and Adam's redemption.
[^72]: Folio 55r — Typikon Rubrics: Wednesday Vespers for Thursday (Holy Apostles & Saint Nicholas). Folio 55r bears a woodcut headpiece introducing Wednesday evening Vespers for Thursday, opening the weekly cycle dedicated to the Holy Apostles and Saint Nicholas of Myra with Stichera to the Podoben *"When from the Tree"* (*"Всю напоисте землю, якоже рѣки..."*).
[^73]: Folio 55v — Typikon Rubrics: Thursday Matins Kathisma Sessional Hymns to the Apostles and Saint Nicholas. Thursday Matins appoints Sessional Hymns after each Kathisma praising the fishermen who caught the nations in the net of the Spirit (*"Иже оумꙋдривый ловца паче рыторовъ..."*), followed by the Martyrikon and Theotokion.
[^74]: Folio 56r — Hymnographic Realia: Thursday Matins Canons to the Apostles and Saint Nicholas. Folio 56r introduces the dual Canons of Thursday Matins: the Canon to the Holy Apostles composed by Saint Theophanes the Branded (*"Твореніе Ѳеофаново"*, Heirmos *"Въ глубинѣ потопи древле..."*) and the Canon to Saint Nicholas composed by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer (*"Твореніе Їѡсифово"*, Heirmos *"Грядѣте людїе, поемъ..."*).
[^75]: Folio 58v — Typikon Rubrics: Thursday Matins Aposticha and Beatitudes. Concluding the Matins office of the Apostles and Saint Nicholas, the Aposticha celebrate the apostolic preachers whose names are magnified throughout the universe, followed by the Beatitudes commemorating their nets sweeping the earth.
[^76]: Folio 59r — Typikon Rubrics: Thursday Vespers for Friday (Precious Cross and Stavrotheotokia). Folio 59r opens Thursday evening Vespers for Friday with a woodcut headpiece and the solemn Stichera of the Precious Cross (*"Сыне, къ кресту пригвожденъ бывъ..."*) and the heart-rending laments of the Virgin at the foot of the Cross (*Stavrotheotokia*).
[^77]: Folio 59v — Typikon Rubrics: Friday Matins Kathisma Sessional Hymns (Sedalna) of the Precious Cross. Following the first, second, and third poetic Kathismata of the Psalter, Friday Matins appoints Sessional Hymns honoring the Crucifixion (*"Спасенїе содѣ́лалъ посредѣ́ землѧ̀, Хе҃ Бж҃е..."*), meditating upon Christ spreading His immaculate palms upon the Cross to gather the dispersed nations, paired with Stavrotheotokia evoking the Virgin Mother's bitter weeping at the sight of the Life of all dying upon the Tree.
[^78]: Folio 60r — Hymnographic Realia: Friday Matins Canons to the Precious Cross and Theotokos. Folio 60r presents the Canon to the Precious and Life-Giving Cross composed by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer (*"Творе́ніе Їѡ́сифово"*, Heirmos *"Нетре́нꙋ, не ѡбы́чнꙋ..."* / *"Traversing the untrodden..."*), interweaving strophes to the Cross and holy Martyrs with the second Canon to the Most Holy Theotokos (Heirmos *"Грядѣ́те лю́дїе, пои́мъ..."* / *"Come, ye peoples, let us sing..."*).
[^79]: Folio 63r — Typikon Rubrics: Friday Matins Aposticha and Beatitudes. Friday Matins concludes with the Aposticha of the Cross (*"Крⷭтъ тво́й дре́во Хе҃ Бж҃е, дре́во живо́тное показа́лъ еси́ на́мъ..."*) and the weekday Beatitudes (Blazhenna) with Troparia commemorating the Crucifixion upon Golgotha, the Thief's confession, and the triumph over Hades.
[^80]: Folio 63v — Typikon Rubrics: Friday Evening Vespers for Saturday (Commemoration of All Saints and the Departed). Friday evening initiates the weekly liturgical commemoration of All Saints, holy Martyrs, Hierarchs, Venerables, and the faithful departed, opening with a woodcut headpiece and Stichera at *"Lord, I call"* set to the Automelon / Podoben *"When from the Tree"* (*"Пло́ть предаю́ще ра́намъ..."*).
[^81]: Folio 64r — Typikon Rubrics: Saturday Matins Kathisma Sessional Hymns (Sedalna). Following the first and second Kathisma readings of the Psalter, Saturday Matins appoints Sessional Hymns for the holy Martyrs, Apostles, Prophets, Hierarchs, and the Departed (*"Помяни́ Гдⷭи ꙗ́кѡ бл҃гъ рабы́ своѧ̀..."*), entreating peaceful rest for all who have fallen asleep in the hope of resurrection.
[^82]: Folio 64r — Hymnographic Realia: Saturday Matins Canons to All Saints and the Departed. Folio 64r introduces the Canon to All Saints, Hierarchs, Venerable Fathers, and the Departed composed by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer (*"Творе́ніе Їѡ́сифово"*, Heirmos *"Моѵсе́йскꙋю пѣ́снь воспрїи́мши..."*), interwoven with the second Canon for the Dead (*"Дрꙋгі́й Кано́нъ оусо́пшымъ"*, Heirmos *"Непроходи́мо волня́щееся..."*).
[^83]: Folio 67r — Typikon Rubrics: Saturday Matins Lauds (Ainoi) and Aposticha for the Departed (*Stichera Hymns for the Departed (Поко́инъ)*). Saturday Matins concludes with Stichera at the Praises praising the Martyrs whose relics are venerated by Kings and Hierarchs (quoting Psalm 115:6 [LXX 115:6 / MT 116:15]), followed by the funeral Aposticha (*"Стремле́нїе сме́ртное и тлю̀..."*) praying for the dead of the earth.
[^84]: Folio 67v — Codicological Apparatus & Epigraphic Transition: Conclusion of Tone 2 (*"КОНЕ́ЦЪ ВТОРО́МУ ГЛА́Сꙋ"*) and Folio 68 Lacuna. Folio 67v marks the solemn conclusion of the Second Tone with a large decorative woodcut tailpiece. In this exemplar, Folio 68 is wanting (lacuna) between Folio 67v (Leaf p69 Left) and Folio 69r (Leaf p69 Right). The missing leaf originally contained the Saturday Liturgy dismissal and the opening of Tone 3 Little Vespers (Mala Vechirnia). Folio 69r begins directly with Tone 3 Saturday Great Vespers.
