# FOOTNOTES AND SCHOLARLY COMMENTARY



[^1]: Physical description of the binding of the 1630 Lviv Octoechos: blind-tooled brown calfskin over bevelled wooden boards with concentric ornamental rolls, rosettes, and geometric strapwork. In the central oval mandorla medallion is embossed the Crucifixion of Christ with the Most Holy Mother of God and Saint John the Theologian standing at the foot of the Cross. Three brass clasps on leather straps are preserved along the fore-edge.

[^2]: Library classification and accession shelfmarks: "LNBU 2124, SV-97", representing the holdings of the Lviv National Scientific Library of Ukraine named after V. Stefanyk (Department of Manuscripts and Rare Books), where this copy is preserved.

[^3]: Mounted fragments of an early Cyrillic printed liturgical edition (containing Resurrectional stichera from an Octoechos), affixed to the front flyleaf during a historical binding repair.

[^4]: 17th/18th-century liturgical annotations in semi-uncial and cursive by a parish cantor or reader, citing folio 171 verso, the Tone 6 Gradual Antiphon 1 ("Unto heaven I lift up mine eyes"), the Tone 6 Prokeimenon ("O Lord, stir up Thy might..."), and Paschal cycle calculations.

[^5]: Mounted printed fragments on the flyleaf verso preserving Church Slavonic Gradual Antiphons (Anabathmoi) based on Psalm 126 (LXX): "Unless the Lord build the house..." (Аще не Господь созиждетъ домъ...).

[^6]: Parish ownership inscription dated 1838: "Oktoich Cerkwi Borchowskiey 1838", attributing the book to the Church of the Dormition in the village of Borkhiv near Lviv.

[^7]: A humorous cantor's note in Polish orthography recording a vernacular Ruthenian ditty: "Oy pope popoyku pryichaly władyki / chot' nam kony wziaty nie / wim meje semoho klasa wspinjuty" ("O priest, dear little priest, the hierarchs have arrived! Even if they want to take our horses, I do not know how to sing my seventh tone!"), signed by the cantor Kaniwczyk *manu propria*.

[^8]: A reader's chronological calculation in pencil inscribed in 1964: "1964 - 1630 = 334", calculating the 334th anniversary of the monument's publication.

[^9]: Engraved architectural title page of the 1630 Lviv Octoechos (*Октоихъ, сирѣчь Осмогласникъ*), printed in red and black cinnabar/carbon ink by the Lviv Dormition Stauropegion Brotherhood under the blessing of the Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople. Imprint dated December 15, 7138 / 1630. The printer's device displays the coat of arms of Lviv (a three-turreted gate with a passant lion) and the Brotherhood's initials (G-A-V-L).

[^10]: Heraldic woodcut of the coat of arms of the Mohyla family, Voivodes of Moldavia, surrounded by the initials P-B-M-M-K-P, accompanied by a six-line panegyric epigram in Ruthenian verse celebrating their valor and eternal heavenly renown (*"Снѣжныи знаки за Клейнотъ маютъ..."*).

[^11]: A two-column manuscript replacement leaf in 17th/18th-century semi-uncial supplying the missing opening of the Weekday Midnight Office (*Полунощница повседневная*), replacing lost or damaged printed folios of the 1630 edition.

[^12]: Manuscript continuation of the Weekday Midnight Office, containing the prayers of Saint Eustratius and Saint Mardarios (*Владыко Боже, Отче Вседержителю...*), concluding with the mutual forgiveness formula.

[^13]: Dedicatory preface (*Премова*) by the Lviv Dormition Brotherhood addressed to Archimandrite Petro Mohyla of the Kyiv Caves Monastery (later Metropolitan of Kyiv), honoring his noble Moldavian lineage and lavish benefactions to the Lviv Brotherhood.

[^14]: Conclusion and date of the dedicatory preface to Petro Mohyla: Lviv, December 25, 1631 (from the Incarnation, 7139 / 1631), signed by the Stauropegion Brotherhood of Lviv.

[^15]: Tone 1 begins on Folio 1r with Great Vespers for Saturday evening. The text is supplied by a calligraphic 17th/18th-century manuscript replacement leaf imitating early Cyrillic liturgical type.

[^16]: Saturday Great Vespers Stichera and the Dogmatic Theotokion of Tone 1 (*"Всемирную славу..."*), extolling the Virgin Mary as the heavenly gate and temple of the Godhead.

[^17]: Saturday Great Vespers dismissal, Resurrectional Troparion of Tone 1 (*"Камени запечатанну..."*), Theotokion (*"Гаврїилу провѣщавшу..."*), and rubrics for the beginning of Sunday Matins.

[^18]: Sunday Matins Sessional Hymns (Kathisma 1 and 2) in Tone 1, commemorating the angel at the tomb and the overthrow of Hades.

[^19]: Sunday Matins Hypakoe and Songs of Ascents (Anabathmoi, Antiphons 1–3) in Tone 1, meditating upon divine longing in the wilderness and the heavenly Jerusalem.

[^20]: Sunday Matins Prokeimenon, Gospel order, and the Resurrectional Canon of Tone 1 by Saint John Damascene, containing the Heirmoi of Odes 1 through 6.

[^21]: Sunday Matins Resurrectional Kontakion and Ikos in Tone 1 (*"Воскреслъ еси яко Богъ из гроба..."*), followed by the Heirmoi of Odes 7 and 8.

[^22]: The 17th/18th-century calligraphic manuscript replacement concludes on Folio 5r with the Sunday Matins Praises (Lauds) and dismissal rubric, supplying the text missing from the lost opening folios of the 1630 print.

[^23]: The original 1630 printed Cyrillic edition resumes on Folio 13r (`г̃і`) with the conclusion of the Sunday Beatitudes and Sunday Vespers for Monday. The bottom-right corner of Folio 13r has suffered physical damage and was restored with blank laid paper during historical rebinding, causing partial lacunae in the second and third Stichera to the Bodiless Powers. The damaged and missing text is reconstructed in brackets according to the standard Ruthenian Octoechos tradition.

[^24]: Monday Matins Sessional Hymns in Tone 1, following the first, second, and third Kathismata of the Psalter, meditating upon compunction, the parable of the Prodigal Son, and the intercession of the All-Holy Mother of God.

[^25]: The Monday Matins Penitential Canon by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer bears the Cyrillic alphabetical acrostic: *"Согрѣшеніи мои скверну ѡмыи Слове"* ("Wash away the defilement of my transgressions, O Word"). It is paired with the Canon to the Bodiless Powers by Saint Theophanes the Branded (*"Твореніе Ѳеофаново"*).

[^26]: Saint Theophanes' Canon to the Bodiless Powers in Tone 1 systematically invokes the celestial orders of Dionysian angelology: Angels, Archangels, Principalities, Authorities, Powers, Dominions, Thrones, Cherubim, and Seraphim.

[^27]: Odes 7 and 8 of the Monday Matins canons, interweaving the confession of the Holy Trinity with the endurance of the holy martyrs under torture and their victorious entry into the heavenly Kingdom.

[^28]: Monday Beatitudes (Typika) in Tone 1, combining the troparion of the Good Thief with penitential petitions, angelic intercessions, commemoration of the holy martyrs, and doxological stanzas to the Holy Trinity and the Mother of God.

[^29]: Monday Vespers Stichera at *"Lord, I Have Cried"* dedicated to the Holy Prophet, Forerunner, and Baptist John, invoking him as the preacher of repentance and baptizer of the Lamb of God.

[^30]: Tuesday Matins Penitential Canon by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer, alternating with the Canon to the Holy Forerunner and Baptist John, commemorating his ascetic dwelling in the wilderness and intercession before Christ.

[^31]: Odes 3 and 4 of Tuesday Matins, depicting the Baptist's witness to the Lamb of God, his immersion of the Torrent of Delight in the Jordan, and his preaching of repentance.

[^32]: Tuesday Matins Aposticha and Beatitudes in Tone 1, concluding the liturgical services of Tuesday in the Octoechos cycle before the opening of Tuesday Vespers.

[^33]: Folio 21v concludes the Tuesday cycle in Tone 1 with the final stanzas of the Beatitudes (martyric, triadikon, and theotokion), transitioning immediately into Tuesday Vespers for Wednesday. The service introduces the weekday commemoration of the Precious and Life-Creating Cross and the Stavrotheotokia (lamentations of the Mother of God at the Foot of the Cross) in Tone 1.

[^34]: Folio 22v opens the Wednesday Matins cycle in Tone 1 with the Canon to the Precious Cross by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer (*"Твореніе Іѡси́фово"*), paired with a second Canon to the Most Holy Theotokos. The hymnody frames the wood of the Cross as the cosmic antidote to the tree of Eden, restoring fallen mankind and granting immortality.

[^35]: Folios 23v–24r contain Odes 5 and 6 of the Wednesday Matins canons, developing typological imagery: Moses extending his hands upon the mountain as a figure of the Crucifixion, the piercing of Christ's side with the spear, and the tearing up of the handwriting of human debt through the outpouring of blood and water.

[^36]: Folio 25v contains the Wednesday Beatitudes and the evening transition to Thursday, initiating the liturgical commemoration of the Holy Apostles and Saint Nicholas of Myra. The Stichera at *"Lord, I Have Cried"* honor the Apostles as the closest friends of Christ and fishers of the universe.

[^37]: Folios 26v–27r open Thursday Matins with the Canon to the Holy Apostles by Saint Theophanes the Branded (*"Твореніе Ѳеофа́ново"*), paired with the Canon to Saint Nicholas of Myra by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer (*"Твореніе Іѡси́фово"*). The hymnody enumerates the Holy Apostles: Peter, Paul, James, Andrew, Philip, Simon, Bartholomew, Thomas, Matthew, John, Mark, Luke, and the broader choir of the Seventy Disciples.

[^38]: Folios 27v–28r present Odes 4 through 6 of Thursday Matins, celebrating Saint Nicholas as a fruitful olive tree in the house of God, the myrrh-scented chief hierarch of Myra, and a fervent protector of Christians in trials and perils.

[^39]: Folios 28v–29r contain the conclusion of Thursday Matins and the Beatitudes, followed on Folio 29v by the opening of Thursday Vespers for Friday, returning to the commemoration of the Precious Cross. Later archivists inscribed modern Arabic pencil numbers (`26`, `27`, `28`) on Folios 29r, 30r, and 31r, diverging from the original 1630 printed Cyrillic foliation (`кѳ҃` = 29, `л҃` = 30, `ла҃` = 31).

[^40]: Folios 30v–31r open Friday Matins with the Cruciform Canon by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer and a Canon to the Mother of God, meditating upon the cosmic trembling of creation at the death of the Word, the darkening of the sun, the opening of Paradise to the Thief, and the maternal lamentations of the Virgin at the Foot of the Cross.

[^41]: Folio 27v presents Friday Matins Odes 5 and 6, opening with the Heirmos of Ode 5 (*"Make the bright and ever-existing Light to shine forth upon us who rise early at dawn unto the judgments of Thy commandments..."*). The hymnody combines Cruciform troparia with petitions to the Theotokos as the spiritual ladder, animate ark, and unburnt bush.

[^42]: Folio 30r contains the Sessional Hymns after the 1st and 2nd Kathismata of Saturday Matins, including the celebrated Theotokion of Gabriel's greeting (*"Гавріи́лъ провѣща́вшꙋ ти́ Дв̃о: Ра́дꙋйся..."*), highlighting the Virgin as more spacious than the heavens for containing the Creator.

[^43]: Folio 30v opens the Saturday Matins Canon to the Holy Martyrs, Hierarchs, Venerable Fathers, and the Departed, bearing the traditional liturgical attribution to Saint Joseph the Hymnographer (*"Творе́ніе Іѡ́сифово"*), paired with an additional Canon for the Departed (*"Дрꙋгіи Кано́нъ Оу҆со́пшимъ"*).

[^44]: Codicological Apparatus: Quire 5 Misbinding. In this exemplar of the 1630 Lviv Octoechos, printed Folio 35 (`лє҃`), containing the opening of Tone 2 Saturday Little Vespers (*"В СУББОТУ ВЕЧЕРЪ НА МАЛОЙ ВЕЧЕРНИ..."*), is bound immediately after Folio 32v (`лв҃`), ahead of Folio 33 (`лг҃`). Furthermore, printed Folio 34 (`лд҃`) is wanting entirely in this copy. Physical facsimile leaf order (p36 -> p37 -> p38) is strictly preserved in transcription and translation in accordance with Master Translation Standard codicological protocols.

[^45]: Codicological Apparatus: Tone 2 Saturday Little Vespers concludes here at the bottom of Folio 35v with the dismissory troparion and Theotokion. Following this leaf, the physical codex returns on Folio 33r (`лг҃`) to the conclusion of Tone 1 Saturday Matins (Ode 9, Praises, and Aposticha).

[^46]: Codicological Apparatus: On Folio 36r (`лѕ҃`), following the conclusion of Tone 1 Saturday Matins Aposticha on Folio 33v, the text initiates Tone 2 Saturday Great Vespers (*"В СУББОТУ ВЕЧЕРЪ НА ВЕЛИЦѢЙ ВЕЧЕРНИ НА ГД̃И КО ЗВАХЪ: Стіхи́ры воскр(ес)ны, гласъ, в҃. Творе́ніе Іѡ́анна Дамаски́на"*).

[^47]: Folio 36v presents the conclusion of the Great Vespers Stichera at *"Lord, I Have Cried"*, including the iconic Dogmatikon Theotokion in Tone 2 (*"Пре́йде сѣ́нь зако́ннаѧ, бл҃гти прише́дши..."* — *"The shadow of the Law passed away when grace arrived..."*), composed by Saint John of Damascus, celebrating the Virgin as the unburnt bush and the mystery of the Incarnation.

[^48]: Folio 37r opens the Sunday Midnight Office in Tone 2 with the Triadical Canon (*"В НЕДѢЛЮ, НА ПОЛꙊНОЩНИЦI, КАНОНЪ ТР(ОИ)ЧЕНЪ"*), bearing the attribution to Metrophanes, Metropolitan of Smyrna (*"Творе́ніе Митрофа́ново"*), meditating on the undivided Trinity of equal majesty and the co-eternal Godhead.

[^49]: Folio 38v — Paleographical Apparatus: Paper Damage and Physical Reconstruction. On Leaf p41 (Folios 38v and 39r), the lower inner and outer margins have suffered physical loss, attrition, and rodent or moisture damage. Standard liturgical restorations for the Tone 2 Sunday Midnight Office conclusion, Sessional Hymns (Sedalna), and Anabathmoi Antiphons are supplied in editorial square brackets `[...]` adhering to epigraphic paleographical standard MTS-1.

[^50]: Folio 39r — Liturgical Apparatus: Tone 2 Sunday Matins Anabathmoi and Resurrectional Prokeimenon. The Gradual Antiphons of Tone 2 (*"На небо очи пущаю моего сердца..."*, LXX Psalm 122:1; *"Аще не Господь бы былъ въ насъ..."*, LXX Psalm 123:1; *"Надѣющіися на Господа..."*, LXX Psalm 124:1) culminate in the Sunday Matins Prokeimenon from LXX Psalm 9:33 (*"Воскресни, Господи Боже мой..."*).

[^51]: Folio 39r — Codicological and Typographical Structure: Canon Selection and Abridgement. Tone 2 Sunday Matins features the Resurrectional Canon by Saint John of Damascus (*"Во глубинѣ потопи древле..."*) paired with the Cross-Resurrectional Canon (*"Нетренну не обычну..."*). The 1630 Lviv print sets Ode 1 on Folio 39r, then transitions directly across the leaf to Folio 39v where the pre-Kontakion Theotokion of Ode 6 leads immediately into the Resurrectional Kontakion and Ikos, followed by Odes 7, 8, and 9. Physical continuity of Leaf 39 (recto on p41, verso on p42) proves this is the intended typographic layout rather than a missing gathering.

[^52]: Folio 39v — Hymnographic Realia: Sunday Resurrectional Kontakion and Ikos in Tone 2. Composed by Saint Romanos the Melodist, the Kontakion (*"Воскреслъ еси отъ гроба, Спасе всесильне..."*) and Ikos (*"Ты еси свѣтъ омраченнымъ..."*) proclaim Christ's descent into Hades, the shattering of the infernal gates, and the liberation of Adam and all humanity.

[^53]: Folio 40r — Typikon Rubrics: Tone 2 Sunday Matins Ode 8 and 9 Heirmoi. The Heirmoi of Odes 8 (*"Пещь огненная иногда въ Вавулонѣ..."*) and 9 (*"Безначална Родителя и Господь..."*) frame the concluding glorification of the Incarnation, contrasting the Three Youths in the Babylonian furnace with the immaterial light revealed in Christ.

[^54]: Folio 40v — Codicological Apparatus: Omission of Printed Folio 41. In this exemplar, printed Folio 42 (`мв҃`) immediately follows Folio 40v (`м`), omitting Folio 41 (`ма҃`) in the printed foliation numbering sequence. The liturgical text is strictly continuous without omission or lacuna between the conclusion of Tone 2 Sunday Matins Ode 9 on Folio 40v and the Sunday Praises (*"Всяко дыханіе..."*) on Folio 42r.

[^55]: Folio 40v — Hymnographic Apparatus: Sunday Praises (Ainoi / Lauds) and Eastern Stichera. The Praises in Tone 2 open with the Resurrectional Stichera (*"Всяко дыханіе и вся тварь..."*), followed by the Anatolian (Eastern) Stichera recounting the Myrrh-bearing women at the sepulchre and the angelic proclamation of the Resurrection.

[^56]: Folio 42r — Liturgical Structure: Sunday Divine Liturgy Beatitudes (Makarismoi). Folio 42r introduces the Sunday Beatitudes in Tone 2 (*"Гласъ Ти приносимъ разбойничь..."*), commemorating the confession of the Good Thief and linking the Cross to Paradise.

[^57]: Folio 42r — Typikon Rubrics: Sunday Vespers for Monday (Incipient Weekday Cycle). Following the Divine Liturgy Beatitudes, Folio 42r initiates Sunday evening Vespers for Monday (*"Въ Недѣлю, въ вечеръ, на Господи, воззвахъ..."*) with Penitential Stichera (*"Имѣяй благоутробія источникъ..."*) modeled on the Podoben *"Егда отъ древа"*.

[^58]: Folio 42v — Hymnographic Apparatus: Monday Midnight Office and Vespers Stichera to the Bodiless Powers. The liturgical cycle transitions to the weekly commemoration of the Holy Angels and Bodiless Hosts (*"Престоли, Херувими, и Серафими..."*), invoking the nine angelic ranks as unceasing celebrants around the Divine Throne.

[^59]: Folio 43r — Typikon Rubrics: Monday Matins Kathisma Sessional Hymns (Sedalna). Monday Matins features Penitential Sessional Hymns after the 1st and 2nd Kathismata (*"Яко волны морскія восташа на мя..."*, *"Помилуй мя, рече Давидъ..."*), focusing on personal compunction and comparing the penitent's plight to the barren fig tree and David's repentance.

[^60]: Folio 43r — Hymnographic Realia: Monday Matins Canon of Repentance and Canon to the Bodiless Hosts. Monday Matins features the Penitential Canon (*"Грядите, людіе, поемъ пѣснь Христу Богу..."*) paired with the Canon to the Incorporeal Hosts by Saint Theophanes the Branded (*"Твореніе Ѳеофаново"*, Heirmos *"Нетренну, необычну..."*).

[^61]: Folio 46r — Typikon Rubrics: Monday Matins Aposticha and Monday Vespers for Tuesday. Concluding the Monday cycle with the Aposticha (*"Содѣянныхъ моихъ золъ помышляю безмѣстная..."*), Folio 46r transitions to Monday evening Vespers for Tuesday (*"Въ понедѣлокъ въ вечеръ, на Господи, воззвахъ..."*), initiating the weekly commemoration of Saint John the Forerunner and Baptist.

[^62]: Folio 46v — Hymnographic Realia: Tuesday Vespers Stichera to Saint John the Baptist. Dedicated to the Precursor and Baptist of the Lord (*"Проповѣдниче Христовъ и Крестителю..."*), highlighting John as angel, apostle, martyr, prophet, friend of the Bridegroom, and seal of the Prophets bridging the Old and New Covenants.

[^63]: Folio 46v — Typikon Rubrics: Tuesday Matins Kathisma Sessional Hymns. Tuesday Matins continues the cycle of repentance while weaving in petitions to the Forerunner, Apostles, and Martyrs (*"Апостоли, Пророцы, и Мученицы..."*).

[^64]: Folio 47r — Hymnographic Realia: Tuesday Matins Canon of Compunction and Canon to the Forerunner by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer. Tuesday Matins pairs the Penitential Canon of Compunction (*"Канонъ умиленія, Твореніе Іѡсифово"*, Heirmos *"Моѵсейскую пѣснь воспріимши..."*) with the Canon to the Holy Forerunner (*"Инъ канонъ Предтечи, Твореніе Іѡсифово"*, Heirmos *"Во глубинѣ потопи древле..."*).

[^65]: Folio 49r — Paleographical Apparatus: Colophon and Transition to Tuesday Canon Odes 7–9. Folio 49r presents the concluding troparia of Ode 6 and the opening of Ode 7 for Tuesday Matins, bearing the catchword *"въ втор[окъ]"* indicating continuity into the completion of the Tuesday Matins hymnody in Cohort 6.

[^66]: Folio 49v — Hymnographic Realia: Tuesday Matins Canon of Repentance and Canon to the Forerunner (Odes 8–9). Completing the Tuesday Matins hymnody begun in Cohort 5, Folio 49v presents Ode 8 with the Penitential Heirmos *"Въ купинѣ Мѡѵсею..."* (*"In the bush unto Moses..."*) and the second Canon to the Holy Forerunner (*"Пещь ѻгненна аногда въ Вавулѡнѣ..."*), meditating upon Saint John's baptism of Christ in the Jordan streams and entreating tears of repentance.

[^67]: Folio 50v — Typikon Rubrics: Tuesday Matins Aposticha and Beatitudes. Tuesday Matins concludes with penitential Aposticha (*"Содѣянныхъ моихъ зѡлъ помышляю..."*), the Martyrikon honoring the holy Martyrs as an unassailable wall of intercession, and the weekday Beatitudes (Blazhenna) with Troparia invoking the Thief's confession (*"Гласъ ти приноси разбойничь..."*).

[^68]: Folio 50v — Hymnographic Structure: Tuesday Vespers for Wednesday (Precious Cross & Theotokos). Tuesday evening initiates the liturgical commemoration of the Precious Cross with Stichera at *"Lord, I call"* set to the celebrated Automelon / Podoben *"When from the Tree"* (*"Егда ѿ древа..."*), depicting the cosmic convulsions at the Crucifixion (the darkening of luminaries, earthquake, rending of the temple veil) paired with the Compassion of the Mother of God (Stavrotheotokia).

[^69]: Folio 51v — Typikon Rubrics: Wednesday Matins Kathisma Sessional Hymns (Sedalna) of the Precious Cross. Following the first, second, and third poetic Kathismata of the Psalter, Wednesday Matins appoints Sessional Hymns honoring the Cross (*"Спасенїе содѣлалъ посредѣ земля, Христе Боже..."*), reflecting on Christ spreading His immaculate hands upon the Wood to gather the dispersed nations.

[^70]: Folio 51v — Hymnographic Realia: Wednesday Matins Canons of the Precious Cross and Theotokos. Wednesday Matins features the Canon to the Precious and Life-Giving Cross composed by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer (*"Твореніе Їѡсифово"*, Heirmos *"Въ глубинѣ потопи древле..."*) interwoven with the Canon to the Most Holy Theotokos (Heirmos *"Грядѣте людїе, поемъ пѣснь..."*).

[^71]: Folio 54v — Typikon Rubrics: Wednesday Matins Aposticha and Beatitudes. Wednesday Matins concludes with the Aposticha of the Cross (*"Креста твоего древо, Христе Боже, древо животное показа намъ..."*) and the weekday Beatitudes commemorating the Crucifixion and Adam's redemption.

[^72]: Folio 55r — Typikon Rubrics: Wednesday Vespers for Thursday (Holy Apostles & Saint Nicholas). Folio 55r bears a woodcut headpiece introducing Wednesday evening Vespers for Thursday, opening the weekly cycle dedicated to the Holy Apostles and Saint Nicholas of Myra with Stichera to the Podoben *"When from the Tree"* (*"Всю напоисте землю, якоже рѣки..."*).

[^73]: Folio 55v — Typikon Rubrics: Thursday Matins Kathisma Sessional Hymns to the Apostles and Saint Nicholas. Thursday Matins appoints Sessional Hymns after each Kathisma praising the fishermen who caught the nations in the net of the Spirit (*"Иже оумꙋдривый ловца паче рыторовъ..."*), followed by the Martyrikon and Theotokion.

[^74]: Folio 56r — Hymnographic Realia: Thursday Matins Canons to the Apostles and Saint Nicholas. Folio 56r introduces the dual Canons of Thursday Matins: the Canon to the Holy Apostles composed by Saint Theophanes the Branded (*"Твореніе Ѳеофаново"*, Heirmos *"Въ глубинѣ потопи древле..."*) and the Canon to Saint Nicholas composed by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer (*"Твореніе Їѡсифово"*, Heirmos *"Грядѣте людїе, поемъ..."*).

[^75]: Folio 58v — Typikon Rubrics: Thursday Matins Aposticha and Beatitudes. Concluding the Matins office of the Apostles and Saint Nicholas, the Aposticha celebrate the apostolic preachers whose names are magnified throughout the universe, followed by the Beatitudes commemorating their nets sweeping the earth.

[^76]: Folio 59r — Typikon Rubrics: Thursday Vespers for Friday (Precious Cross and Stavrotheotokia). Folio 59r opens Thursday evening Vespers for Friday with a woodcut headpiece and the solemn Stichera of the Precious Cross (*"Сыне, къ кресту пригвожденъ бывъ..."*) and the heart-rending laments of the Virgin at the foot of the Cross (*Stavrotheotokia*).

[^77]: Folio 59v — Typikon Rubrics: Friday Matins Kathisma Sessional Hymns (Sedalna) of the Precious Cross. Following the first, second, and third poetic Kathismata of the Psalter, Friday Matins appoints Sessional Hymns honoring the Crucifixion (*"Спасенїе содѣ́лалъ посредѣ́ землѧ̀, Хе҃ Бж҃е..."*), meditating upon Christ spreading His immaculate palms upon the Cross to gather the dispersed nations, paired with Stavrotheotokia evoking the Virgin Mother's bitter weeping at the sight of the Life of all dying upon the Tree.

[^78]: Folio 60r — Hymnographic Realia: Friday Matins Canons to the Precious Cross and Theotokos. Folio 60r presents the Canon to the Precious and Life-Giving Cross composed by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer (*"Творе́ніе Їѡ́сифово"*, Heirmos *"Нетре́нꙋ, не ѡбы́чнꙋ..."* / *"Traversing the untrodden..."*), interweaving strophes to the Cross and holy Martyrs with the second Canon to the Most Holy Theotokos (Heirmos *"Грядѣ́те лю́дїе, пои́мъ..."* / *"Come, ye peoples, let us sing..."*).

[^79]: Folio 63r — Typikon Rubrics: Friday Matins Aposticha and Beatitudes. Friday Matins concludes with the Aposticha of the Cross (*"Крⷭтъ тво́й дре́во Хе҃ Бж҃е, дре́во живо́тное показа́лъ еси́ на́мъ..."*) and the weekday Beatitudes (Blazhenna) with Troparia commemorating the Crucifixion upon Golgotha, the Thief's confession, and the triumph over Hades.

[^80]: Folio 63v — Typikon Rubrics: Friday Evening Vespers for Saturday (Commemoration of All Saints and the Departed). Friday evening initiates the weekly liturgical commemoration of All Saints, holy Martyrs, Hierarchs, Venerables, and the faithful departed, opening with a woodcut headpiece and Stichera at *"Lord, I call"* set to the Automelon / Podoben *"When from the Tree"* (*"Пло́ть предаю́ще ра́намъ..."*).

[^81]: Folio 64r — Typikon Rubrics: Saturday Matins Kathisma Sessional Hymns (Sedalna). Following the first and second Kathisma readings of the Psalter, Saturday Matins appoints Sessional Hymns for the holy Martyrs, Apostles, Prophets, Hierarchs, and the Departed (*"Помяни́ Гдⷭи ꙗ́кѡ бл҃гъ рабы́ своѧ̀..."*), entreating peaceful rest for all who have fallen asleep in the hope of resurrection.

[^82]: Folio 64r — Hymnographic Realia: Saturday Matins Canons to All Saints and the Departed.
