**PETER MOHYLA, VOIVODE’S SON**

**OF THE MOLDAVIAN LANDS.**

Great Archimandrite of the Holy, Great, and Wonder-Working

**LAVRA OF THE CAVES OF KYIV.**

Unto our gracious Lord and Benefactor.

[f. p6RB] A true proverb it is, most illustrious and gracious Lord, our Father and Pastor, that beneficence is a golden chain and fetter whereby the human soul is secretly bound and drawn: this we ourselves have manifestly experienced. For it is evident unto the whole world what generous and illustrious benefits our Lviv Brotherhood Church hath received from Their Mercies, the illustrious rulers of Moldo-Wallachia, the renowned Mohylas, ancestors of Your Mercy—in particular, of pious memory, His Mercy Symeon, your father, and His Mercy Jeremiah, uncle of Your Illustriousness. For by their renowned and lavish expenditure our church was exalted on high, remaining unto their eternal glory.

Likewise thee also, O renowned Mohyla, our Father and Pastor, by thine outstanding noble virtues, our Brotherhood hath ever possessed as a particular benefactor, experiencing special proofs of confirmed benevolence: which have so bound, strengthened, and drawn us unto thee, that in renewing the memory of those of holy remembrance, we strive that we, well-wishers in God, might in some small part repay Their Mercies, the ancestors of Your Grace, and you their renowned descendants. Especially since virtue itself and gratitude, ready to spring forth from our hearts into open sight, demand this of us. With the memory therefore of light and grateful confession of the ancestors of Your Illustriousness, while offering yearly prayer for their souls and for the long-lasting life and health of Your Grace in this world, with fitting declarations:

[f. p7LA] our desire is ardently to honor and exalt the renowned family of Your Illustriousness with immortal renown. Striving therefore with all our strength, behold, following the renewal of our Brotherhood Printing-House, we issue under the illustrious name of Your Grace this very first book, filled with God-inspired consolations, and offer it unto thee, as a pious man unto a pious patron. For who is such a witness thereof, as that thou, being born of a most renowned family, having deservedly gained the royal favor of the Crown of Poland and the goodwill of His Royal Majesty our gracious King, and of the whole Commonwealth, and having formed bonds of friendship with the noble assembly of Senators of the Crown of Poland, didst renounce all these things out of piety, making an offering unto God the Creator, voluntarily embracing poverty and virginal chastity, lovingly receiving the angelic monastic habit? And immediately, by the providence of God and the favor of His Royal Majesty, by the common consent of both spiritual and lay fathers, thou wast crowned with high ecclesiastical dignity. [^14]

[f. p7LB] Witness thereto is thy fervent prayer unto God the Creator, unto His most pure and most blessed Mother, and unto the beloved Eastern Church. Witness also is thy zealous and pious care for the multiplication of the praise of God, for the elevation of schools, for good order in the Monastery, and for the internal and external adornment of churches. By the rule of thy piety, not only Kyiv, but the whole Rus’ land rejoiceth and giveth thanks unto God, that unto that holy place, for the profit and consolation of all Christendom, after holy and pious men, He hath granted so holy, pious, and watchful a Pastor.

Unto thee therefore, a pious father, we offer, present, and dedicate this pious Book, and place it beneath the broad wings of our Father and Pastor. Let it fly forth under thy patronage; let it pay thee this tribute; under thy name let it go forth securely into the world. Receive therefore, most illustrious, most venerable, and gracious Father and our Pastor, this work kindly, graciously, and favorably; and deign ever to keep us, thy servants and perpetual petitioners, grateful well-wishers of Your Illustriousness, in thy gracious, fatherly love and holy prayers.

Written in Lviv. In the year from the Incarnation of the Son of God, 1631, in the month of December, the 25th day.

Unto Your Venerable Illustriousness,

our Gracious Lord,

Perpetual intercessors in God

and humble servants,

[The Stauropegion Brotherhood of the Dormition of the Most Holy Mother of God in Lviv]

### Notes

[^1]: Physical description of the binding of the 1630 Lviv Octoechos: blind-tooled brown calfskin over bevelled wooden boards with concentric ornamental rolls, rosettes, and geometric strapwork. In the central oval mandorla medallion is embossed the Crucifixion of Christ with the Most Holy Mother of God and Saint John the Theologian standing at the foot of the Cross. Three brass clasps on leather straps are preserved along the fore-edge.
[^2]: Library classification and accession shelfmarks: "LNBU 2124, SV-97", representing the holdings of the Lviv National Scientific Library of Ukraine named after V. Stefanyk (Department of Manuscripts and Rare Books), where this copy is preserved.
[^3]: Mounted fragments of an early Cyrillic printed liturgical edition (containing Resurrectional stichera from an Octoechos), affixed to the front flyleaf during a historical binding repair.
[^4]: 17th/18th-century liturgical annotations in semi-uncial and cursive by a parish cantor or reader, citing folio 171 verso, the Tone 6 Gradual Antiphon 1 ("Unto heaven I lift up mine eyes"), the Tone 6 Prokeimenon ("O Lord, stir up Thy might..."), and Paschal cycle calculations.
[^5]: Mounted printed fragments on the flyleaf verso preserving Church Slavonic Gradual Antiphons (Anabathmoi) based on Psalm 126 (LXX): "Unless the Lord build the house..." (Аще не Господь созиждетъ домъ...).
[^6]: Parish ownership inscription dated 1838: "Oktoich Cerkwi Borchowskiey 1838", attributing the book to the Church of the Dormition in the village of Borkhiv near Lviv.
[^7]: A humorous cantor's note in Polish orthography recording a vernacular Ruthenian ditty: "Oy pope popoyku pryichaly władyki / chot' nam kony wziaty nie / wim meje semoho klasa wspinjuty" ("O priest, dear little priest, the hierarchs have arrived! Even if they want to take our horses, I do not know how to sing my seventh tone!"), signed by the cantor Kaniwczyk *manu propria*.
[^8]: A reader's chronological calculation in pencil inscribed in 1964: "1964 - 1630 = 334", calculating the 334th anniversary of the monument's publication.
[^9]: Engraved architectural title page of the 1630 Lviv Octoechos (*Октоихъ, сирѣчь Осмогласникъ*), printed in red and black cinnabar/carbon ink by the Lviv Dormition Stauropegion Brotherhood under the blessing of the Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople. Imprint dated December 15, 7138 / 1630. The printer's device displays the coat of arms of Lviv (a three-turreted gate with a passant lion) and the Brotherhood's initials (G-A-V-L).
[^10]: Heraldic woodcut of the coat of arms of the Mohyla family, Voivodes of Moldavia, surrounded by the initials P-B-M-M-K-P, accompanied by a six-line panegyric epigram in Ruthenian verse celebrating their valor and eternal heavenly renown (*"Снѣжныи знаки за Клейнотъ маютъ..."*).
[^11]: A two-column manuscript replacement leaf in 17th/18th-century semi-uncial supplying the missing opening of the Weekday Midnight Office (*Полунощница повседневная*), replacing lost or damaged printed folios of the 1630 edition.
[^12]: Manuscript continuation of the Weekday Midnight Office, containing the prayers of Saint Eustratius and Saint Mardarios (*Владыко Боже, Отче Вседержителю...*), concluding with the mutual forgiveness formula.
[^13]: Dedicatory preface (*Премова*) by the Lviv Dormition Brotherhood addressed to Archimandrite Petro Mohyla of the Kyiv Caves Monastery (later Metropolitan of Kyiv), honoring his noble Moldavian lineage and lavish benefactions to the Lviv Brotherhood.
[^14]: Conclusion and date of the dedicatory preface to Petro Mohyla: Lviv, December 25, 1631 (from the Incarnation, 7139 / 1631), signed by the Stauropegion Brotherhood of Lviv.
[^26]: Saint Theophanes' Canon to the Bodiless Powers in Tone 1 systematically invokes the celestial orders of Dionysian angelology: Angels, Archangels, Principalities, Authorities, Powers, Dominions, Thrones, Cherubim, and Seraphim.
[^30]: Tuesday Matins Penitential Canon by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer, alternating with the Canon to the Holy Forerunner and Baptist John, commemorating his ascetic dwelling in the wilderness and intercession before Christ.
[^213]: Codicological Note on Foliation Omission in the 1630 Lviv Edition: In the original printed foliation of the 1630 Lviv Octoechos, folio number 136 (рл҃ѕ) is omitted in the printed sequence. Tone 4 concludes on Folio 135v (physical leaf p136 Left Page), and Tone 5 begins immediately on Folio 137r (physical leaf p136 Right Page), with complete textual and liturgical continuity. Physical leaf indexing (p136) strictly preserves the continuous leaf count of the physical codex.
