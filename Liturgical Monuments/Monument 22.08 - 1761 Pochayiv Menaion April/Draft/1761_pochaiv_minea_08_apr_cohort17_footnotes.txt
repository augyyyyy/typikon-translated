[^188]: The printed Pochaiv text reads *стра́ство* (*strastvo*), an evident typographical conflation or dialectal form for *страда́льчество* (the state of suffering/martyrdom) or *ста́рчество* (venerable old age, referring to St. Symeon’s martyrdom at age 120).
[^189]: The printed text reads *стрѣ́пе* (*strepe*), a regional Ruthenian variant or misprint for *тре́пете* (*trepete*, "terror/trembling"), hence "terror of demons."
[^190]: The hymnographer plays upon the dual apostolic naming of the saint as both Simon and Symeon (*Сїмѡ́нъ сѷмеѡ́нъ*), mirroring his orthodox confession of the two natures (divine and human) of the incarnate Word against the early heretics.
[^191]: The Slavonic text reads *то́чы* (*tochy*), an archaic/Ruthenian nominal form corresponding to streams, gouts, or copious outflows (*то́ки*).
[^192]: Slavonic *блѧде́нїѧ* (*blyadeniya*), deriving from the Patristic root denoting senseless prating, ravings, delirium, or blasphemous pagan taunts.
[^193]: The Pochaiv printed text has the typographical error *по дꙋшелоби́тельномꙋ* (*po dushelobitel'nomu*) for *по дꙋшегꙋби́тельномꙋ* (*po dushegubitel'nomu*, “upon the soul-destroying path”).
[^194]: Slavonic *ѻ҆све́тней* (*osvetney*) in the printed text is an epigraphic defect/variant, likely intended for *всесвѣ́тней* (“all-worldly”) or *свѣ́тней* (“worldly/secular”) delusion.
[^195]: The printed Pochaiv text reads *Глы̑* (*Gly*) with circumflex, an evident printer’s defect or broken type for *Хꙋлы̑* (*Khuly*, “blasphemies/insults”) or *Злы̑* (*Zly*, “evil things”), referring to the martyrs despising the blasphemous insults or evils of the impious tormentors.
