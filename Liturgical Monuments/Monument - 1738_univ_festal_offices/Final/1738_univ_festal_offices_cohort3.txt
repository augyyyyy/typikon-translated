...and a mirror of every virtue, thou didst fix thy mind upon heavenly things, to behold the ineffable beauty of Christ the King and God, Whom having loved with all thy heart, and strengthened by His power, thou didst endure many afflictions and torments: Whom do thou with boldness entreat for our souls.

*Sticheron 2:*  
Sitting upon the hierarchal throne, O all-blessed one, thou didst adorn thyself with great virtues, and by teaching and deeds didst reveal us unto repentance and salvation. Therefore, armed with purity and fervent prayers, with fasting and all-night vigil, and like good Christ gently drawing near unto passion and death, prostrated cruciformly upon the earth in prayer,[^28] thou didst prepare thyself for suffering, O Hieromartyr Josaphat; therefore the wicked enemies, coming upon thee like unmerciful beasts, unjustly tore thee in pieces, tenderhearted one, like a guileless lamb, who suffered valiantly and with boldness dost pray for our souls.

*Sticheron 3:*  
Thy fierce and wicked enemies, as Solomon of the Jews said in the Psalms,[^29] they that hate thee hated thee, and lifted up their head against thee, O blessed Josaphat; they dealt craftily of their own will, and having taken wicked counsel, said: "Come, and let us destroy him from among the nations, and his name shall be remembered no more"; as they took counsel with one mind, the mighty fell upon thee, and severed thy precious head, striking thee unmercifully and dragging thee into a ditch; they poured out thy blood like water throughout the whole city; likewise having tied stones to thy neck and to thy holy feet, from the hill they hurled and cast thee into the river's torrent; by reason of thy courage and long-suffering, for whose sake thou didst receive, O much-suffering Josaphat, an unfading crown.

*AT THE DIVINE LITURGY*  
*Prokeimenon, Tone 7:*  
Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of His saint and His venerable one.  
*Verse:* What shall I render unto the Lord for all that He has rendered unto me?  

*A Reading from the Epistle of the Holy Apostle Paul to the Hebrews (Hebrews 4:14–5:10, Pericope 334):*[^30]  
Brethren: Having a great Priest Who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our confession. For we have not an High Priest Who cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities, but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin. Let us therefore come with boldness unto the Throne of His grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need. For every High Priest taken from among men is ordained for men in things pertaining to God, that he may offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins: who can have compassion on the ignorant, and on them that are out of the way; for that he himself also is compassed with infirmity. And by reason hereof he ought, as for the people, so also for himself, to offer for sins. And no man takes this honor unto himself, but he that is called of God, as was Aaron. So also Christ glorified not Himself to be made an High Priest, but He that said unto Him: "Thou art My Son, today have I begotten Thee." As He says also in another place: "Thou art a Priest forever after the order of Melchizedek." Who in the days of His flesh, having offered up prayers and supplications with strong crying and tears unto Him that was able to save Him from death, and was heard in that He feared; though

*Alleluia, Tone 4:*  
Thy priests shall be clothed with righteousness, and Thy venerable ones shall rejoice with joy.  
*Verse:* The righteous man shall flourish like the palm tree, and shall be multiplied like the cedar in Lebanon.  

*A Reading from the Holy Gospel according to John (John 10:9–16, Pericope 36):*  
The Lord said unto the Jews who came unto Him: "I am the door: by Me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture. The thief comes not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy: I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly. I am the good Shepherd: the good Shepherd lays down His life for the sheep. But he that is an hireling, and not the shepherd, whose own the sheep are not, sees the wolf coming, and leaves the sheep, and flees; and the wolf catches them, and scatters the sheep. And the hireling flees, because he is an hireling, and cares not for the sheep. I am the good Shepherd, and know Mine own, and am known of Mine. As the Father knows Me, even so know I the Father: and I lay down My life for the sheep. And other sheep I have, which are not of this fold: them also I must bring, and they shall hear My voice; and there shall be one flock, and one Shepherd."
