To the Very Reverend Fathers Protopresbyters, Reverend Presbyters, and to the entire Clergy of the Church abiding within our Eparchies: greeting in the Lord.  

Since the Holy Synod of Zamość, in the General Table of Feasts of the whole year, has designated for us also these present ones, that is to say,[^4] of the MOST HOLY MYSTERY OF THE EUCHARIST, of the COMPASSION OF THE MOST HOLY THEOTOKOS, and of the BLESSED HIEROMARTYR JOSAPHAT, and commanded that these be celebrated everywhere: Therefore, unto the Very Reverend Fathers Protopresbyters and Reverend Presbyters, We pastorally command that they announce all these Feasts in their churches unto the people belonging to their flock, and command them to keep them holy in every way, and themselves strictly follow these Feasts in the manner prescribed without alteration.  

Athanasius, Archbishop-Metropolitan of all Rus', with his own hand.[^5]

# PART I: OFFICE OF THE FEAST OF THE MOST HOLY MYSTERY OF THE EUCHARIST

*The Office of the Feast of the Most Holy Mystery of the Eucharist.*[^6]  
*Celebrated on the Thursday after the Sunday of All Saints.*[^7]  
*At Vespers, on "Lord, I have cried", Stichera, Tone 5, Podoben: "On the Third Day" (Tridnevno):*  

*Sticheron 1:*  
Revealing the great mystery of Thy becoming man unto the initiates as Thou didst recline at supper, O Lover of mankind, Thou didst say: "Eat the living Bread; this is My Body, and the Blood of incorruptible life."  

*Sticheron 2:*  
The Upper Room was shown forth as a heavenly tabernacle, wherein Christ accomplished the Passover and the Supper as an unbloody service for mankind; and the Table of the Mysteries accomplished there, a noetic altar.  

*Sticheron 3:*  
Having blessed the Bread—being Himself the Heavenly Bread—having given thanks unto the Father Who begat Him, He gave also the Cup to be drunk unto the Disciples, crying aloud: "Take, eat, this is My Body; and drink the Blood of incorruptible life."  

*Glory... Both now... Tone 4:*  
Come, ye faithful lovers of the feasts, let us clap our hands today at the saving solemnity for the world of the Living Bread and the Lamb of God Who takes away the sins and transgressions of the world; not in the old leaven of malice, but in the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth; having cleansed ourselves, therefore, let us worthily partake of Him, that we may obtain His eternal Kingdom.  

*Entrance, and the Prokeimenon of the day, then the Lections.*  

*A Reading from Exodus (Exodus 19:10–19 LXX):*[^8]  
The Lord said unto Moses: "Go unto this people, and testify unto them, and sanctify them today and tomorrow, and let them wash their garments. And let them be ready against the third day; for on the third day the Lord will come down upon Mount Sinai before all the people. And thou shalt set bounds unto the people round about, saying: 'Take heed to yourselves, that ye go not up into the mount, or touch the border of it; whosoever touches the mount shall surely die. There shall not a hand touch it, for he shall surely be stoned, or shot through with an arrow; whether it be beast or man, it shall not live.' When the voice of the trumpet and the cloud depart from the mount, they shall go up upon the mountain." And Moses went down from the mount unto the people, and sanctified them; and they washed their garments. And he said unto the people: "Be ready against the third day; come not near a woman." And it came to pass on the third day in the morning, that there were voices and lightnings, and a thick cloud upon Mount Sinai; and the voice of the trumpet sounded exceedingly loud, so that all the people that were in the camp trembled. And Moses brought forth the people out of the camp to meet God; and they stood beneath the mount. And Mount Sinai was altogether on a smoke, because the Lord descended upon it in fire; and the smoke thereof ascended as the smoke of a furnace, and the whole people were greatly amazed. And the voices of the trumpet sounded forth, waxing exceedingly loud. Moses spoke, and God answered him with a voice.

*A Reading from the First Book of Kingdoms (1 Kingdoms 21:1–6 LXX):*[^9]  
In those days, David came to Nob unto Ahimelech the priest; and Ahimelech was astonished at meeting him, and said unto him: "Why art thou alone, and there is no man with thee?" And David said unto the priest: "The King hath commanded me a business today, and said unto me: 'Let no man know anything of the business whereabout I send thee, and what I have commanded thee'; and I have appointed the young men to the place called Emonim. And now, if there be under thine hand five loaves of bread, give me whatsoever thou hast." And the priest answered David, and said: "There is no common bread under my hand, but there is holy bread; if the young men have kept themselves at least from women, then they may eat them." And David answered the priest, and said: "Yea, unto this day since we set forth on the way; and all the young men are clean, and this journey is clean, seeing that today there remains vessels for my sake." And Ahimelech the priest gave him showbread, for there was no bread there but only one loaf brought, which was before the face of the Lord, and that was given on that day.  

*A Reading from the Third Book of Kingdoms (3 Kingdoms 19:3–8 LXX):*[^10]  
The word of the Lord came unto Elijah, saying: "Go hence eastward, and hide thyself by the brook Cherith, that is before Jordan. And it shall be, that thou shalt drink water of the brook; and I have commanded the ravens to feed thee there." And Elijah went and did according to the word of the Lord, and sat by the brook Cherith, that is before Jordan. And the ravens brought him bread in the morning, and flesh in the evening; and he drank water of the brook. And it came to pass after a while, that the brook dried up, because there had been no rain upon the earth. And the word of the Lord came unto Elijah, saying: "Arise, and go to Zarephath of Sidon, and dwell there; behold, I have commanded a widow woman there to sustain thee." And he arose and went to Zarephath to the gate of the city; and behold, the widow woman was there gathering sticks; and Elijah called to her, and said unto her: "Fetch me, I pray thee, a little water in a vessel, that I may drink." And she went to fetch it; and Elijah cried after her, and said: "Bring me, I pray thee, a morsel of bread in thine hand." And the woman said: "As the Lord my God lives, if I have a cake, but only a handful of meal in a barrel, and a little oil in a cruse; and behold, I am gathering two sticks, that I may go in and dress it for me and my children, that we may eat it, and die." And Elijah said unto her: "Fear not; go and do according to thy word; but make me thereof a little cake first, and bring it unto me, and after make for thee and for thy children. For thus says the Lord God of Israel: 'The barrel of meal shall not fail, neither shall the cruse of oil diminish, until the day that the Lord sends rain upon the face of the earth.'" And the woman went and did according to the word of Elijah, and gave unto him, and she and he and her children did eat. And from that day the barrel of meal wasted not, neither did the cruse of oil fail, according to the word of the Lord, which He spoke by the hand of Elijah.  

*At the Litiya, Stichera, Tone 5:*  

*Sticheron 1:*  
David the ancestor of God danced, leaping before the shadow of the Ark; and we today worship Thee as God. Which Ark prefigured Thee manifestly: instead of manna, the incarnate Godhead; instead of the tables of the lawgiver, Christ the Lord hidden from sight, saving our souls.  

*Sticheron 2:*  
Though the Old Testament possessed an ark overlaid on every side with gold, which was taken by the aliens, it endured not to abide in the temple with the idol; for it fell down upon the earth, and crushed the head of Dagon[^11] *Sticheron 3:*
Yet Thou, O Lover of mankind, Christ our God, how wilt Thou enter into our foul, wretched souls? Thou Who alone hast immortality, and dwellest in light unapproachable, how dost Thou unite Thyself with the mire of the darkness of sin? And O marvelous wonder! Illuminating every human being, condemn us not, nor cast into outer darkness our dark and sinful souls; but illuminate us, we pray Thee, by Thy grace, O supernatural Bread, cleansing for the world.  

*Sticheron 4:*  
O innocent Lamb, Who takest away the sins of the whole world, for the sake of the salvation of wretched man, Thou didst transform the bread into Thy Body; and in the bread Thou hast concealed the Godhead, Flesh, and Spirit by divine power. Therefore all we faithful today keep feast, bowing down with tenderness and faith.  

*Glory... Both now... Tone 2:*  
Today we the faithful approach with fear the mystical supper and the divine table, desiring to behold the fountain of immortality; but trembling and fear seize us by reason of our many sins, from which we desire to be cleansed by Thee through the prayers of our Lady, the Theotokos.  

*At the Aposticha, Stichera, Tone 4, Podoben: "O Ye Heavenly Ranks" (Nebesnykh chinov):*  

*Sticheron 1:*  
Let us hymn the true living Bread that unceasingly comes down from heaven, ineffably saying with one mind unto Christ Who has redeemed us all, and into the divine and holy things which He has now prepared.  

*Sticheron 2:*  
At the Supper Thou didst give Thyself unto Thy Disciples, a marvel unto angels, for food: "Eat the bread, for this is My Body"; and having taken the cup, Thou didst say: "Drink of it, all of you, this is My Blood."  

*Verse:* And He rained down manna upon them to eat, and gave them the bread of heaven.  

*Sticheron 3:*  
O divine and most precious and most dearly-bought Body of God the Word made man: save Thy servants from many and incurable plagues, and grant the great promises, the heavenly kingdom and life, for the sake of Thy mercy.  

*Glory... Both now... Tone 8:*  
O surpassing wonder! The living and divine Bread having come down from heaven: how great a joy was bestowed at the mystical supper! For having taken the bread and blessed it, He gave it unto the Disciples: "Take, eat, this is My Body"; likewise also the cup. Therefore let every man examine himself, and so let him eat and drink, unto salvation and eternal life.

*And now: Dismissal, etc. Troparion, Tone 6:*  

*Troparion:*  
In the supernatural things, He Whom the Angels tremble to behold is seen today festively upon the altar, and is given unto men for food; wherein, proclaiming Thy most rich mercy, O Christ our God, and asking Thy grace as we behold Thee, we fall down and confess the hidden mystery, crying aloud: Make us worthy to be partakers, that we may delight in Thine eternal gifts.  

*Dismissal:*  
May Christ our true God, Who for His exceeding great mercy gave us at His mystical supper His Flesh to eat and His true Blood to drink for our salvation, through the prayers of His most pure Mother, of the holy twelve Apostles, and of all the Saints, have mercy upon us and save us, for He is good and the Lover of mankind. Amen.  

*At Matins, "God is the Lord": Troparion: "The supernatural Bread...", thrice.*  

*After the First Kathisma, Sessional Hymn, Tone 7:*  

*Sessional Hymn:*  
Christ is our great and all-honorable Passover; for when He was slain as a sheep in His Passion, He was offered for us as a sacrifice well-pleasing unto God; Whose Body and most precious Blood we devoutly venerating, cry with tenderness: Dwell Thou Thyself ever with us, for no one shall prevail against us.  

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*  
Behold, O ever-virgin Mary, behold the Flesh of thy Savior Christ our God, which having received in thy womb by the operation of the Holy Spirit, He gave unto His Disciples at the mystical supper, saying: "Take, eat, this is My Body; and drink, this is My Blood"; Whom do thou with maternal boldness implore, that He consume us not who partake, but save our souls.  

*After the Second Kathisma, Sessional Hymn, Tone 3:*  

*Sessional Hymn:*  
Having taken the heavenly Bread and the Cup, Christ blessed them, showed them unto God the Father Who begat Him, and giving them unto the wondering Disciples, said: "He that eats My Flesh and drinks My Blood has eternal life; and I will raise him up at the last day." Therefore we the unworthy, keeping feast today, bow down before the most pure Mysteries of Thy Flesh and Blood, boldly believing that Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God, Who didst come into the world to save sinners.  

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*  
Without seed of the Holy Spirit thou didst conceive and bear the Son, Christ our God; Whose most pure Flesh, as God and our Redeemer, we worship falling prostrate to the ground, desiring to be partakers thereof; and unto thee as Mother do we flee, O all-pure Theotokos, and praying with tenderness we implore thee: Receive our prayers, and carry them unto thy Son and our God, that He may save our souls.  

*Polyeleos: Megalynarion:*[^12]  
We magnify Thee, O Christ the Giver of life, Who for our sake didst transform the bread into Flesh, and the wine into Thy Blood. Therefore today we worship Thee as God incarnate.  

*Verse 1:* The Lord is good and greatly to be praised in the city of our God, in His holy mountain.  
*Verse 2:* What shall I render unto the Lord for all that He has rendered unto me? I will take the cup of salvation.  
*Verse 3:* He has made a remembrance of His marvelous works; merciful and compassionate is the Lord; He has given food unto them that fear Him.  
*Verse 4:* And He rained down manna upon them to eat, and gave them the bread of heaven; man did eat the bread of angels; He sent them food to the full.  

*Glory... Both now... Alleluia, thrice.*  

*After the Polyeleos, Sessional Hymn, Tone 3, Podoben: "Awed by the Beauty" (Krasote):*  

*Sessional Hymn:*  
The pre-eternal and unoriginate God, Who assumed human nature, came into the prepared Upper Room with His Disciples, where Thou didst finish the old Passover, O Christ our Savior, and didst make a new, mystical Passover; for taking bread, Thou didst change it into Thy Flesh, and the cup into Thy Blood, and didst give them unto us for food and drink. Beholding this Mystery, O God, we say with tenderness: Glory to Thy compassion, O Lover of mankind!  

*Glory... Both now... The same.*  

*Gradual Antiphon 1 of Tone 4.*  

*Prokeimenon, Tone 4:* He that eats My Flesh and drinks My Blood has eternal life, and I will raise him up at the last day.  

*The Holy Gospel according to John (pericope 24 / John 6:55–58):*  
The Lord said unto the Jews who came unto Him: "My Flesh is meat indeed, and My Blood is drink indeed..." The end: "shall live forever."  

*Sticheron after the Gospel, Tone 6:*  
Today the Angels are struck with awe, beholding Thee, the incarnate God, concealed within the Mysteries, and they worship with fear in wonder as Thou art given for food. Together with them, we also that are of the earth sing unto the good One and Lover of mankind, saying: Make us worthy to partake of Thy most pure Flesh and Blood, O compassionate Lord!

*Troparion 2:*

*Both now... Theotokion:*

*Troparion 1:*

*Troparion 2:*  
May Thy most pure Body and divine Blood be unto me for the remission of sins, and communion of the Holy Spirit, and unto life eternal, O Lover of mankind, and estrangement from passions.  

*Glory...*  
Grant me to partake uncondemned of Thy life-creating Body and precious Blood, and to glorify Thy goodness.  

*Both now... Theotokion:*  
O all-pure living table of Him Who came down from on high for the sake of mercy to give life unto the world: make me also now worthy, unworthy though I be, to taste thereof with fear, and to be made alive.

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 1, Podoben: "Though the Stone Was Sealed" (Kameni znamenanu):*  

*Sessional Hymn:*  
Even as Thou, O Master, didst eat the lamb with Thy Disciples according to the commandment of Moses, so Thou didst mystically give Thyself, the spotless Lamb, unto us for food, unto the remission of sins and sanctification of our souls; fed wherewith we pray: Let not the communion of Thy holy Mysteries be unto judgment or condemnation because of our sins and iniquities.  

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*  
O Virgin Theotokos, Mother of the Lamb and Shepherd, implore the Son whom thou hast borne, that He may save and enlighten our souls.  

*Ode 4. Heirmos: Thou didst come forth from the Virgin:*  

*Troparion 1:*  
Having become incarnate for our sake, O most merciful One, Thou didst will to be slain as a sheep for the sins of men; therefore I pray Thee, cleanse also my transgressions.  

*Troparion 2:*  
Heal the wounds of my soul, O Lord, and wholly sanctify me, and make me wholly worthy, O Master, that I, wretched though I be, may become a partaker of the mystery of Thy divine supper.  

*Glory...*  
Standing and weeping with fear and trembling, I lift up the eyes of my heart on high unto Thee, O Savior, Who didst stretch out Thine all-pure hands upon the Cross; through them give me the food of Thy good pleasure, confirming me and strengthening me in Thy fear.  

*Both now... Theotokion:*  
Make Thy compassionate Son merciful also unto me, O Lady, and preserve me thy servant without spot and without blemish, that receiving within me the spiritual Pearl,[^14] I may be sanctified.

*Troparion 2:*

*Glory...*  
Having an exceedingly defiled spirit and an impure mind, I dare not now approach unto Thee, O Christ, and receive Thy Body; but do Thou make me worthy.  

*Both now... Theotokion:*

*Ikos:*  
Falling down with fear at the mystical table, O ye faithful, let us receive with pure souls Christ the true God, looking for salvation; having washed the feet of our conscience, let us enjoy eternal life through the prayers of the Mother of God.  

*Ode 7. Heirmos: The golden image:*  

*Troparion 1:*  
May the communion of Thine immortal Mysteries, O Christ, be unto me, I pray Thee, a fountain of good things, light, life, and dispassion, mediating unto the advancement and increase of divine virtue, O Thou Who alone art good, that I may glorify Thee.  

*Troparion 2:*  
That I may be delivered from passions, enemies, want, and every affliction, as I now approach Thy immortal and divine Mysteries with trembling, love, and reverence, O Lover of mankind: make me worthy, and grant that I may sing: Blessed art Thou, O Lord God of our fathers!  

*Glory...*  
O my wretched soul, O passionate soul, be struck with awe as thou beholdest the most glorious Mysteries; sighing, weep and beat thy breast, crying aloud and saying: O God, cleanse me even as the prodigal!  

*Both now... Theotokion:*  
O Mother of God, who beyond understanding didst bear Christ the Savior, full of God's grace: I thy defiled servant earnestly implore thee, the pure one: as I now desire to approach the most pure Mysteries, cleanse me wholly from the defilement of flesh and spirit.  

*Katavasia: The Children of God did not worship the creature...*  

*Ode 8. Heirmos: Heedless of the golden image:*  

*Troparion 1:*  
Make me also, O God my Savior, to be a partaker of Thy heavenly, dread, and holy Mysteries, and of Thy divine and mystical supper, O Christ.  

*Troparion 2:*  
Having fled unto Thy tender compassion, O good One, with fear I cry unto Thee: Abide in us, O Savior, as Thou hast said, for unto Thee I cry; for behold, trusting in Thy mercy, I eat Thy Body and drink Thy Blood.  

*Glory...*  
As I receive fire, may I not be consumed like wax and like hay! But, O the tender mercy of God! O the dread Mystery! How do I, being clay, partake of the divine Body and Blood, and am made incorruptible?  

*Both now... Theotokion:*  
The divine Bread of life was truly baked within thy womb, O Mother of God, preserving thine immaculate virginity unharmed; therefore, as the Nourisher of our life, we hymn thee unto the ages.  

*Katavasia: The godly children in the furnace...*  

*Ode 9. Before the Heirmos, Refrain:*[^15]  
Magnify, O my soul, the Lord, Who in a Mystery has given His Flesh for food.  

*Another Refrain:*  
Magnify, O my soul, Christ our God, Who has given His Flesh for the salvation of men.  

*Heirmos: God is desire...*  

*Troparion 1:*  
Christ is seen and slain in a marvelous manner, Who was of old; having once offered Himself for our sake as a sacrifice unto His Father, He is ever slain, sanctifying them that partake.  

*Troparion 2:*  
May I be sanctified in soul and body, O Master, may I be enlightened, may I be saved, may I become Thy dwelling-place through the communion of the sacred Mysteries; having Thee dwelling within me together with the Father and the Spirit, by grace, O most merciful One.  

*Glory...*  
Like fire and like light may Thy Body and Blood be unto me, O my all-pure Savior: burning up the matter of sin, consuming the thorns of passions, and wholly enlightening me who worship Thy Godhead.  

*Both now... Theotokion:*  
God was incarnate of thy pure blood; therefore every generation hymns thee, O Lady, and the noetic hosts glorify thee, for through thee they have manifestly seen the Master of all holding fellowship with mankind.

*Exapostilarion (Photagogicon):*  
When the Disciples sat at the Mystical Supper, Thou, O Lord, having chosen them, didst take Bread into Thy holy Hands, and lifting up Thine eyes to heaven unto God the Father, didst give thanks, bless, and break it, and didst give it unto Thy holy Disciples: Grant it also unto us, Thy sinful servants!  

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*  
Behold, O all-pure Virgin Theotokos, He Who is worshipped in all the Mysteries, Whom thou didst bear before in thy maternal womb, is hymned by the people; do thou implore Him, that He may save our souls.  

*At the Praises, Stichera (4), Tone 1, Podoben: "A Marvelous Wonder" (Divnoe chudo):*  

*Sticheron 1:*  
O marvelous wonder! The Fountain of life is seen upon the altar, and is given unto men for food! Rejoice, O divine Church, adorned with the Bread of the Flesh of Christ; let us the faithful cry aloud, having John the Theologian as our leader: "And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us; and we beheld His glory, the glory as of the Only-begotten of the Father!"  

*Sticheron 2:*  
O marvelous wonder! The true God, Who was prefigured in the manna, born of true God, having assumed the nature of human flesh! Rejoice today, O human race, seeing God in the flesh giving Himself unto thee; shouting for joy with gladness and earnestly giving thanks unto God: "And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us; and we beheld His glory, the glory as of the Only-begotten of the Father!"  

*Sticheron 3:*  
O marvelous wonder! Wisdom has slain Her sacrificial offerings, and mingled Her wine in Her cup, and prepared Her table, calling all unto Herself: "Come, eat of My bread, and drink of the wine which I have mingled for you!" Rejoice, O churches, give thanks unto the Savior: "And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us; and we beheld His glory, the glory as of the Only-begotten of the Father!"  

*Sticheron 4:*  
O marvelous wonder! He Who spoke with Moses on Mount Sinai in the unburnt bush, and wrote the tables of the law, Whose voice of thunder, trumpets, and lightnings terrified the sons of Israel; He Who destroyed my former transgressions, the same today is hidden divinely in the bread, and is given unto all for food: the Lamb, God, and the Savior!  

*Glory... Both now... Tone 6:*

# OFFICE OF THE FEAST OF THE BLESSED HIEROMARTYR JOSAPHAT

*Celebrated on the 16th Day of the Month of September.*[^25]  

*If it occur on a Sunday, on Saturday at Vespers we recite the Kathisma "Blessed is the man", and we sing at "Lord, I have cried": Resurrection Stichera of the Tone, 4; and of the Saint, 6. But if on another day, we sing the Stichera of the Feast of the Exaltation, and of the Saint:*  

*Stichera unto the Saint, Tone 1, Podoben: "O All-Praised Martyrs" (Prekhvalnii muchenitsy):*  

*Sticheron 1:*  
Stained with martyr's blood upon thy sacred vestment, thou didst enter into the Holy of Holies, O crowned head; where are the voices of them that feast, from Whom for thy labors thou dost receive the victories: an unfading crown of eternal beauty, and paradise-dwelling, O Hieromartyr Josaphat; and now pray that there be granted unto the Churches union and peace, and unto our souls great mercy.  

*Sticheron 2:*  
First thou didst offer unto God the unbloody sacrifice, as a divine Hierarch; as a fruitful, acceptable sacrifice, by thine own blood thou didst bring thyself unto Christ as a true Hieromartyr, O divinely-speaking, all-honorable one; and now pray that there be granted unto the Churches union and peace, and unto our souls great mercy.  

*Sticheron 3:*  
Having cast down the schismatic eating by thy hierarchal word, and having strengthened by faith the hearts of the unconfirmed, thou didst pass unto life eternal, having suffered for union: a witness of the Lord thou wast, O Hieromartyr Josaphat; and now pray that there be granted unto the Churches union and peace, and unto our souls great mercy.  

*Sticheron 4:*  
Having dyed thy sacred vesture with the blood of thy sacred flesh, O holy father Hierarch; a double crown from Christ thou hast received, all-rich one, and with the Angels thou dancest in the heavens, standing before the Holy Trinity; Whom do thou now pray that there be granted unto the Churches union and peace, and unto our souls great mercy.  

*Sticheron 5:*  
O Hieromartyr Josaphat, what an honorable life hast thou desired! And unto union thou hast guided the people by thine honorable words, and by grace thou hast passed unto eternal things, having washed thyself with suffering blood having washed thyself with suffering blood, O blessed one; and now pray that there be granted unto the Churches union and peace, and unto our souls great mercy.

*Sticheron 6:*  
A goodly guiding cloud, entering noetically into the unutterable light, as a Hieromartyr and Shepherd, thou wast taught the ineffable mysteries of Christ; as a sun enlightened by God, as a Shepherd mystically instructed, therefore also promised double crowns of glory, O favorite of God; and now pray that there be granted unto the Churches union and peace, and unto our souls great mercy.  

*Glory... Tone 6:*  
A lawful priest, even unto thy departure wast thou, O Hieromartyr Josaphat: for thou didst celebrate the divine ineffable mysteries, and didst pour out thy blood for Christ God, offering an acceptable sacrifice; therefore having boldness unto Him, pray fervently for them that with faith keep thy most honorable memory and honor it, that thy flock and the whole world may be delivered from assaults, from all foreign nations, and from all tribulations and distresses.  

*Both now... Of the Feast of the Exaltation, the same Tone. (If it be on Saturday: the Dogmatic Theotokion of the Tone; but if on another day: Both now... Of the Feast of the Exaltation).*  

*After the Entrance, the Prokeimenon of the day, then the Lections.*  

*A Reading from the Proverbs of Solomon (Proverbs 10:7, 3:13–16, 8:6, 34, 4, 12, 14, 17, 5–9 LXX):*  
The memory of the righteous is celebrated with praises, and the blessing of the Lord is upon his head. Blessed is the man that finds wisdom, and mortal man who understands prudence. For the merchandise thereof is better than the merchandise of silver, and the gain thereof than fine gold. She is more precious than costly stones; and all honorable things are not worthy of her. Length of days and years of life are in her right hand, and in her left hand riches and glory. Out of her mouth proceeds righteousness, and law and mercy she carries upon her tongue. Give ear therefore unto me, O children, for I speak honorable things; and blessed is the man that keeps my ways. For my outgoings are the outgoings of life, and will shall be prepared from the Lord. For this cause I entreat you, and put forth my voice unto the sons of men: for I, wisdom, have established counsel and understanding, and knowledge I have called upon. Counsel is mine, and sound wisdom; mine is understanding, and strength is mine. I love them that love me; and those that seek me shall find grace. Understand therefore, O simple ones, subtlety, and ye uninstructed, apply your hearts. Give ear unto me again, for I speak honorable things; and I will open from my lips right things. For my throat shall meditate truth, and lying lips are an abomination before me. All the words of my mouth are with righteousness; there is nothing crooked or perverse in them. They are all plain to him that understands, and right to them that find knowledge. For I teach you truth, that your hope may be in the Lord, and ye may be filled with the Spirit.  

*A Reading from the Wisdom of Solomon (Wisdom 4:7–15 LXX):*  
When the righteous man is praised, the peoples will praise him; for his memory is immortality, because he is known both by the Lord and by men, and his soul was well-pleasing unto God. Love wisdom therefore, O men, and live; desire her, and ye shall be instructed. For the beginning of her is love, and the keeping of laws. Honor wisdom, that ye may reign forever; I will tell you, and will not hide from you the mysteries of God. For He is the guide of wisdom, and the directer of the wise; and in every understanding and work an artificer, and with all understanding wisdom will teach: for in her is an understanding and holy spirit, the brightness of the everlasting light, and the unspotted mirror of the power of God. She makes men friends of God and prophets; she is more beautiful than the sun, and above all order of stars: being compared with the light, she is found before it. She delivers them that please her from afflictions, and directs them into straight paths. She gives them to have holy understanding, and preserves them from them that lie in wait, and gives them a sore conflict. That they may understand all, that godliness is stronger than all, and malice shall never overcome wisdom, neither shall judgment passing by let the wicked go unrebuked. For they said within themselves, reasoning not rightly: "Let us oppress the righteous man, and let us not spare his holiness, neither let us respect the ancient gray hairs of the aged. Let our strength be the law. Let us lie in wait for the righteous, because he is displeasing unto us, and is contrary to our doings; he upbraids us with our offending the law, and objects to our infamy the transgressions of our education. He professes to have the knowledge of God, and he calls himself the servant of the Lord. He was made to reprove our thoughts, and he is grievous unto us: we see that his life is not like other men's, his ways are of another fashion. We are esteemed of him as counterfeits; he abstains from our ways as from filthiness; he pronounces the end of the just to be blessed. Let us see if his words be true, and let us prove what shall happen in the end of him. With despitefulness and torture let us examine him, that we may know his meekness, and prove his patience. Let us condemn him with a shameful death; for by his own saying he shall be respected." Such things did they imagine, and were deceived; for their own malice blinded them. As for the mysteries of God, they knew them not, and considered not that Thou art the only God, having power of life and death, and saving in time of affliction, and delivering from all evil: compassionate and merciful, and giving grace unto His holy ones, and with His arm resisting the proud.

*A Reading from the Wisdom of Solomon (Wisdom 4:16–5:7 LXX):*  
Though the righteous be prevented with death, yet shall he be in rest. Thus the righteous that dies shall condemn the ungodly which are living. For they shall see the end of the wise, and shall not understand what God in His counsel has decreed of him: that the Lord shall cast down the ungodly speechless, and shall shake them from the foundation; and they shall be utterly laid waste in sorrow, and their memorial shall perish. For they shall come with fear unto the reckoning of their sins, and their own iniquities shall convince them to their face. Then shall the righteous man stand in great boldness before the face of such as have afflicted him, and made no account of his labors. When they see it, they shall be troubled with terrible fear, and shall be amazed at the strangeness of his salvation. And they repenting and groaning for anguish of spirit shall say within themselves: "This was he whom we had sometimes in derision, and a proverb of reproach, fools that we were! We accounted his life madness, and his end to be without honor: how is he numbered among the children of God, and his lot is among the saints! Therefore have we erred from the way of truth, and the light of righteousness has not shined unto us, and the sun of understanding rose not upon us. We wearied ourselves in the way of wickedness and destruction; yea, we have gone through deserts, where there lay no way, but as for the way of the Lord, we have not known it."  

*AT THE LITIYA, Stichera of the Feast of the Exaltation, Tone 1:*  
Now truly the prophetic word of David has received its fulfillment: for behold, manifestly we worship the footstool of Thine all-pure feet, and trusting under the shadow of Thy wings, O all-compassionate Son, we cry aloud unto Thee: let the light of Thy countenance be marked upon us; exalt the horn of the Orthodox people, for the sake of the Exaltation of Thy Precious Cross, O Christ abundant in mercy.

*Stichera unto the Saint, Tone 1:*  

*Sticheron 1:*  
With divine sacred hymns, come, all ye people, let us spiritually keep festival in honor of the Hieromartyr Josaphat, who cut down the thorns of schism, and planted for souls saving teachings, bearing fruit unto the blossom of holiness through virtues.  

*Sticheron 2:*  
Having celebrated the divine Gospel of God, having sealed the divine teachings with thy blood shed by schismatic sheep, O Hieromartyr Josaphat, thou didst escape their snares, and as a victor didst enter into heaven, receiving the crown of victory.  

*Sticheron 3:*  
A step of ascents the Church acquired through thy sacred struggles, O Hieromartyr Josaphat, which thou also keepest unharmed and unassailed by mighty wolves, who proclaim thy valiant deeds and magnify thee, O blessed one.  

*Glory... Tone 5:*  
Radiant is thy solemnity; the Church makes an assembly for thee, rejoicing; O Hieromartyr Josaphat, mystically honoring thee: do thou therefore as a Shepherd cease not to pray for us all.

*Sticheron 1:*  
As a many-splendored sun, spiritually thou hast shone forth upon the firmament of the Church, enlightening souls darkened by the malice of the enemy, through the radiance of words and God-wise teaching, and by a life equal to the Angels; and having guided the wandering into the way of truth, and drawn them unto the union of the Orthodox Catholic Church, therefore today with a loud voice we celebrate thy radiant and holy memory.  

*Verse:* Thy priests shall be clothed with righteousness, and Thy venerable ones shall rejoice.  

*Sticheron 2:*  
Equal in honor unto the Apostles wast thou, O Hieromartyr Josaphat, having received a mind of equal zeal, not fearing the cruelty of wicked tormentors, but manfully preaching union with boldness, Thou didst endure manifold wounds and tortures, and didst receive great reward for thy long-suffering from the one Umpire of the contest, Christ our God; Whom do Thou fervently entreat, on behalf of all that with faith honor thy holy memory.

*Katavasia: "The senseless decree of the wicked tyrant..."*  

*Ode 8. Heirmos:* The fiery furnace of old...  
*Refrain:* Blessed Hieromartyr Josaphat, pray unto God for us.  

*Troparion 1:*  
With praises is thy memory celebrated, O speaker of divine words; for from divine inspiration thou wast taught knowledge of future things, victory in suffering, and thy departure unto the highest; and Angels escorted thy soul unto heaven, joyfully singing with the heavenly beings: Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord, and exalt Him unto ages!  

*Troparion 2:*  
Now hast thou stood with the theologians before the throne of the Almighty, as like unto them, of one mind, O all-blessed Josaphat: primate of Polotsk, unconquered champion of the united Church; therefore with righteous testimony and peace thou hast sealed it unto ages.  

*Troparion 3:*  
Now is the assembly of the schismatics overcome and distressed by thy deeds and words of peace, O Hieromartyr Josaphat; and like smoke their rotten folly vanished away, through thy thunder-voiced and theological tongue.

*Theotokion:*  
Supernaturally thou didst bear the Creator and Savior, God the Word, O Virgin, Whom we exalt unto all ages.  
*Katavasia: "Bless the Lord, ye children equal in number to the Trinity..."*  

*Ode 9. Heirmos:* God the Word, Who is from God...  
*Refrain:* Blessed Hieromartyr Josaphat, pray unto God for us.  

*Troparion 1:*  
Wholly filled with the higher wisdom, O all-glorious one, thou didst shine the light of union upon the world; thou didst conceive divine fear, and didst bring forth words of the Spirit, therefore thou wast the most radiant dwelling of the Holy Trinity; therefore we call thee blessed.  

*Troparion 2:*  
Armed with love and hope, and hedged round with faith, thou didst tear down the crafts and deceits of the enemy; and having become a victor, now thou dwellest in the highest with the divine choirs of Angels and Venerable Ones, around the throne of the King of all in heaven.  

*Troparion 3:*  
As thou hast boldness unto God, O Hieromartyr, remember fervently, O Josaphat, them that joyfully celebrate this thy divine festival, and with faith and praise honor thy precious and sacred suffering; and deliver us from tribulations and distresses.  

*Theotokion:*  
By Thine arm, O Christ, crush the insolent who oppose Thy Church, and establish our King,[^27] that we may unceasingly magnify Thee.  
*Katavasia: "A mystery art thou, O Theotokos, Paradise..."*  

*EXAPOSTILARION*  
Unmercifully the enemy severed thy head, O blessed Hierarch, whom Christ God crowned in the highest with a crown of victory for eternity, O much-suffering one, great lamp, adornment of Hierarchs, glory of Martyrs, and praise of all the Venerable Ones.  
*Glory... Both now... Of the Feast of the Exaltation: "The Cross is the preserver of the whole universe..."*  

*AT THE PRAISES*  
*Stichera, Tone 6:*  

*Sticheron 1:*  
Wholly from youth dedicated unto good Christ, having become separate from the flesh and from worldly love: chosen for the monastic life, O most sacred father Josaphat, and a receptacle of the Holy Spirit, thou didst preserve purity of soul and body, having lived on earth like an Angel among monks, in obedience and poverty, in love toward God and neighbor, in prayers and all-night vigils, being an example and mirror for all monks and a mirror of every virtue, thou didst fix thy mind upon heavenly things, to behold the ineffable beauty of Christ the King and God, Whom having loved with all thy heart, and strengthened by His power, thou didst endure many afflictions and torments: Whom do thou with boldness entreat for our souls.

*Sticheron 2:*  
Sitting upon the hierarchal throne, O all-blessed one, thou didst adorn thyself with great virtues, and by teaching and deeds didst reveal us unto repentance and salvation. Therefore, armed with purity and fervent prayers, with fasting and all-night vigil, and like good Christ gently drawing near unto passion and death, prostrated cruciformly upon the earth in prayer,[^28] thou didst prepare thyself for suffering, O Hieromartyr Josaphat; therefore the wicked enemies, coming upon thee like unmerciful beasts, unjustly tore thee in pieces, tenderhearted one, like a guileless lamb, who suffered valiantly and with boldness dost pray for our souls.

*Sticheron 3:*  
Thy fierce and wicked enemies, as Solomon of the Jews said in the Psalms,[^29] they that hate thee hated thee, and lifted up their head against thee, O blessed Josaphat; they dealt craftily of their own will, and having taken wicked counsel, said: "Come, and let us destroy him from among the nations, and his name shall be remembered no more"; as they took counsel with one mind, the mighty fell upon thee, and severed thy precious head, striking thee unmercifully and dragging thee into a ditch; they poured out thy blood like water throughout the whole city; likewise having tied stones to thy neck and to thy holy feet, from the hill they hurled and cast thee into the river's torrent; by reason of thy courage and long-suffering, for whose sake thou didst receive, O much-suffering Josaphat, an unfading crown.

*Glory... Tone 6:*  
Having loved Christ God from infancy, O all-blessed Josaphat, and for the sake of His love forsaken the world and all the beautiful things that are in the world: in every obedience, purity, and monastic poverty thou didst follow Him all the days of thy life, and didst truly please Christ God by deeds that cleanse us; and straightway as a good Shepherd thou didst prepare thyself manfully for suffering, and for the union of the Church of God thou didst endure many afflictions patiently from wicked men; reviled and beaten, and wounded with many stripes every day, struck upon the head, thou wast vouchsafed a crown from Christ God: entreat Him, O all-blessed one, for us who with love celebrate thine ever-honored memory, that we may be delivered from enemies visible and invisible, from assaults and attacks of the evil one, and from future judgment, and that our souls may be saved.

*Both now... Of the Feast of the Exaltation, the same Tone: "Today the Cross of the Lord goes forth..."*  
*Great Doxology, and Dismissal.*

*AT THE DIVINE LITURGY*  
*Prokeimenon, Tone 7:*  
Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of His saint and His venerable one.  
*Verse:* What shall I render unto the Lord for all that He has rendered unto me?  

*A Reading from the Epistle of the Holy Apostle Paul to the Hebrews (Hebrews 4:14–5:10, Pericope 334):*[^30]  
Brethren: Having a great Priest Who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our confession. For we have not an High Priest Who cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities, but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin. Let us therefore come with boldness unto the Throne of His grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need. For every High Priest taken from among men is ordained for men in things pertaining to God, that he may offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins: who can have compassion on the ignorant, and on them that are out of the way; for that he himself also is compassed with infirmity. And by reason hereof he ought, as for the people, so also for himself, to offer for sins. And no man takes this honor unto himself, but he that is called of God, as was Aaron. So also Christ glorified not Himself to be made an High Priest, but He that said unto Him: "Thou art My Son, today have I begotten Thee." As He says also in another place: "Thou art a Priest forever after the order of Melchizedek." Who in the days of His flesh, having offered up prayers and supplications with strong crying and tears unto Him that was able to save Him from death, and was heard in that He feared; though He were a Son, yet learned He obedience by the things which He suffered; and being made perfect, He became the author of eternal salvation unto all them that obey Him: called of God an High Priest after the order of Melchizedek.

*Alleluia, Tone 4:*  
Thy priests shall be clothed with righteousness, and Thy venerable ones shall rejoice with joy.  
*Verse:* The righteous man shall flourish like the palm tree, and shall be multiplied like the cedar in Lebanon.  

*A Reading from the Holy Gospel according to John (John 10:9–16, Pericope 36):*  
The Lord said unto the Jews who came unto Him: "I am the door: by Me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out, and find pasture. The thief comes not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy: I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly. I am the good Shepherd: the good Shepherd lays down His life for the sheep. But he that is an hireling, and not the shepherd, whose own the sheep are not, sees the wolf coming, and leaves the sheep, and flees; and the wolf catches them, and scatters the sheep. And the hireling flees, because he is an hireling, and cares not for the sheep. I am the good Shepherd, and know Mine own, and am known of Mine. As the Father knows Me, even so know I the Father: and I lay down My life for the sheep. And other sheep I have, which are not of this fold: them also I must bring, and they shall hear My voice; and there shall be one flock, and one Shepherd."

*Communion Hymn:*  
The righteous man shall be in everlasting remembrance. Alleluia, thrice.

[^4]: Church Slavonic: *настоящїа тїесть* ('these present ones, that is to say', calquing Polish *to jest* / Latin *id est*), introducing the three feasts promulgated by Title III (*De Cultu Sanctorum et Festis*) of the 1720 Synod of Zamość: the Most Holy Mystery of the Eucharist, the Compassion of the Most Holy Theotokos, and the Blessed Hieromartyr Josaphat.

[^7]: The Feast of the Most Holy Mystery of the Eucharist (the Byzantine-Ruthenian observance of Corpus Christi) was formally promulgated for the Ruthenian Church at the Synod of Zamość (1720, Title III), assigned to the Thursday after the Sunday of All Saints (that is, the second Thursday after Pentecost, precisely matching the Western Thursday after Trinity Sunday).

[^8]: Septuagint Exodus 19:10–19 (in the Slavonic text rubricated as Exodus, chapter 24 or an opening lection), recording the theophany on Mount Sinai and the ritual sanctification of the people before the descent of God.

[^10]: Septuagint 3 Kingdoms 19:3–8 (1 Kings 19:3–8 Masoretic), Elijah sustained by the hearth cake and cruse of water provided by the angel.

[^11]: The text refers to the Philistine captivity of the Ark of the Covenant and the falling of Dagon in 1 Kingdoms 5 (1 Samuel 5).

[^12]: The Festal Polyeleos Megalynarion for the Feast of the Most Holy Eucharist, composed specifically for the Ruthenian festal anthologia following the Synod of Zamość.

[^13]: Church Slavonic: *Класъ вз̾расти́вшаѧ не ѡ҆ра́нный* ('who didst grow the unplowed ear of corn/wheat'), a classic Byzantine patristic and hymnographic epithet of the Theotokos as the untilled soil producing the Eucharistic Wheat (Christ).

[^14]: Church Slavonic: *ꙋ҆мный Би́серъ* ('the spiritual/noetic Pearl'), patristic Eucharistic terminology designating the consecrated particle of the Holy Body.

[^15]: The Slavonic text provides two alternative Refrains (*Припѣвы*) to be sung before the Heirmos and Troparia of Ode 9 of the Festal Canon of the Eucharist.

[^16]: 1 Corinthians 11:23–32, the standard Pauline lection for Holy Thursday and the Feast of Corpus Christi, expounding the institution of the Holy Eucharist and the requirement of reverent self-examination before receiving the Holy Mysteries.

[^20]: Fol. 11 refers to the Saturday Vespers service, physical leaf p13.

[^21]: Fol. 4 refers to the Litiya of the Feast of the Eucharist, physical leaf p6.
