# 1761 Pochaiv Menaion — Vol. 5: January (Pochaiv Lavra, 1761) <a id="1761-pochaiv-menaion-vol-5-january-pochaiv-lavra-1761"></a>

[Title Page with typographical ornament border printed in cinnabar and black ink]

THE BOOK OF THE MENAION
Month of January

To the glory of the Holy, Consubstantial, Life-Creating, and Undivided Trinity: of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit; under the reign of the Great King Augustus the Third;[^1] by the blessing of His Grace, Lord Sylvester Lubieniecki-Rudnytskyi,[^2] Exarch of the Metropolitanate of Kyiv, Halych, and all Rus', Bishop of Lutsk and Ostroh; by the volition of the Most Reverend Lord Father Hieromonk Hypatius Bilynskyi,[^3] Protoarchimandrite of the Order of Saint Basil the Great; through the zeal and at the expense of the monks of the same Order; in the Holy Wonderworking Lavra of Pochaiv, printed with types: Published. In the year from the Creation of the world according to the Greek chronographs 7269,[^4] and of the Nativity of Christ 1761.

*(Vacat — Blank page)* [Fol. 3r][^5]
*Day 1*

...unrighteousness shall fall. The righteousness of upright men shall deliver them, but the lawless are taken by their lack of counsel. When a righteous man dies, his hope does not perish, but the boast of the ungodly perishes. A righteous man escapes from the snare, and in his stead the ungodly is delivered up. In the mouth of the ungodly is a snare to citizens, but the perception of the righteous is prosperous. In the good prosperity of the righteous a city shall be set aright, and at the destruction of the ungodly there is rejoicing. In the blessing of the upright a city shall be exalted, but by the mouths of the ungodly it is overthrown. He who lacks understanding mocks citizens, but a prudent man keeps silence.[^6]

*At the Litiya, the Sticheron of the Temple:*
*Then of Saint Basil, Tone 3:*
*By Germanus:*
Having welcomed Christ into thy soul through thy pure life, the fountain of life, O sacredly blessed Basil, thou didst pour forth rivers of pious teachings upon the inhabited world; watered by them, the faithful people of the Church, offering the fruit of lips confessing Him, bring thanksgiving unto Him Who glorifies thy memory unto ages of ages.

*Another Sticheron:*
The chrism of grace poured out upon thee anointed thee to minister sacredly the Gospel of the heavenly Kingdom, O Basil revealed by God; having become the sweet savor of Christ, with the fragrance of His knowledge thou didst fill the inhabited world. Therefore, favorably receiving the voices of thy servants, entreat great mercy for us who honor thee.

*By Byzantios:*
Clothed in sacerdotal vestments, O Basil, champion of the Trinity, thou didst stand before the judgment seat, enduring danger for the faith; and having displayed steadfast suffering, thou didst put to shame the fury of the prefects raging with the power of impiety. And when he threatened thee with unmerciful dismemberment, having courageously resolved upon this, O father, and become a martyr by volition, thou didst receive the crown of victory from Christ, Who has great mercy.

*Glory, Tone 6:*
Grace was poured out upon thy lips, O venerable father, and thou wast a shepherd of the Church of Christ, teaching the reasonable sheep to believe in the Consubstantial Trinity in one Godhead.

*Both now, Tone 8:*
*He Who descended, the Savior:*[^7]

*At the Aposticha, Stichera Idiomela, Tone 1:*
*By Byzantios:*
O divine and sacred bee of the Church of Christ, all-blessed Basil: for thou [Fol. 3v]
...with the stings of thy wise words thou didst wound the blasphemies of god-abhorred heresies, and for the souls of the faithful thou didst treasure up the sweetness of piety. And now, walking through the incorruptible pastures of the divine Paradise, remember also us as thou standest before the Consubstantial Trinity.

*Verse: My mouth shall speak wisdom, and the meditation of my heart understanding.*[^8]
*By Basil the Monk:*
Thou didst gather the virtues of all the saints, O our father Basil: the meekness of Moses, and the zeal of Elijah, the confession of Peter, the theology of John. Like Paul, thou didst not cease crying out: Who is weak, and I am not weak? Who is offended, and I burn not? Therefore, dwelling with them, pray that our souls may be saved.

*Verse, Tone 2:*
*The mouth of the righteous shall meditate wisdom, and his tongue shall speak judgment.*[^9]
*By John the Monk:*
Having learned the nature of things that exist, and having perceived the instability of them all, thou didst find One immutable, the super-essential Creator of all; and cleaving all the more unto Him, thou didst cast away desire for things that are not. Pray that we also may obtain divine desire, O Basil revealed by God.

*Another Sticheron:*
Having received the grace of wonders from heaven, and having refuted the deceit of idols by thy teachings, thou art the glory and confirmation of hierarchs, O all-blessed Basil, and the pattern of teaching for all the venerable; having boldness toward God, pray Him that our souls may be saved.

*Both now, Tone 6:*
The all-good God was not ashamed to be circumcised with bodily circumcision; but He gave Himself as an example and pattern unto all for salvation: for the Creator of the Law fulfills the requirements of the Law, and the prophecies of the prophets concerning Him. O Thou Who holdest all things in Thy palm, and wast wrapped in swaddling clothes: Lord, glory to Thee!

*Troparion of the Saint, Tone 4:*
Into all the earth went forth thy proclamation, for it received thy word, whereby thou didst teach in a manner befitting God: thou didst make clear the nature of existing things, thou didst adorn the customs of men, a royal priesthood, O venerable father; pray Christ God that our souls may be saved.

*Glory, and both now, of the Feast, the same Tone:*
Sitting on a throne of fiery appearance in the highest with the unoriginate Father and Thy Divine Spirit, Thou didst deign to be born on earth of a maiden that knew not man, Thy Mother, O Jesus: therefore also circumcised [Fol. 1r][^10]

### Notes

[^1]: King Augustus III of Poland (Frederick Augustus II of Saxony, reigned 1734–1763).
[^2]: Sylvester Lubieniecki-Rudnytskyi (Sylwester Rudnycki-Lubieniecki, c. 1708–1777), Basilian Bishop of Lutsk and Ostroh (1750–1777), Exarch of the Metropolitanate of Kyiv, Halych, and all Rus'.
[^3]: Hypatius Bilynskyi (Ipatij Bilyns'kyj, 1704–1771), Protoarchimandrite of the Basilian Order of Saint Basil the Great (OSBM, 1751–1771).
[^4]: The Byzantine Anno Mundi year 7269 corresponds to 1760/1761 AD (September 1 era).
[^5]: In this physical copy of the 1761 Pochaiv Menaion, Folio 3 (leaves p3–p4) was misbound immediately following the blank verso of the Title Page (leaf p2), preceding Folio 1 (leaf p5) and Folio 2 (leaves p7–p8). Leaf p3 opens with the conclusion of the second Old Testament Paremia for the feast of Saint Basil the Great (Proverbs 11:5–12 LXX), continuing directly from the catchword (*па́даетъ*) at the foot of Folio 2v (leaf p8).
[^6]: Proverbs 11:5–12 (LXX).
[^7]: Incipit of the idiomelon of the Circumcision, printed in full at Great Vespers on Folio 1v (leaf p6): *He Who descended, the Savior, unto the human race...* (*Сходя́й сп[а́]съ къ ро́ду человѣ́ческому...*).
[^8]: Psalm 48:4 [LXX 48:4; MT 49:3].
[^9]: Psalm 36:30 [LXX 36:30; MT 37:30].
[^10]: Here begins the true textual sequence of the Menaion for January, designated in the lower margin by gathering signature *A* (*а*). In the printed pagination, this leaf is Folio 1 recto.
