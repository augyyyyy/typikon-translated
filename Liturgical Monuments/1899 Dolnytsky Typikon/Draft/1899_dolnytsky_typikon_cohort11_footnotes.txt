[^342]: The Greek Menaia and Typika provide a Typikon only for two cases of Saturdays and Sundays between the Nativity and Theophany, that is: 1: for two Saturdays and two Sundays; 2: for one Saturday and one Sunday, and for that single Saturday and Sunday they provide the following Typikon: *Εἰ δὲ τύχῃ ἓν Σάββατον καὶ μία Κυριακὴ, ἀναγινώσκεται τῷ μὲν Σαββάτῳ, Σαββάτῳ μετὰ τὴν Χριστοῦ γέννησιν, τῇ δὲ Κυριακῇ, Κυριακῇ πρὸ τῶν Φώτων* [If however there happen to be one Saturday and one Sunday, there is read on the Saturday, that of the Saturday after the Nativity of Christ, and on the Sunday, that of the Sunday before the Lights (Theophany)]. And this Typikon indeed suffices for the case of a single Saturday with a single Sunday, because then this Saturday occurs within the limit of December (the 31st), and the Sunday within the limit of January (the 1st); nevertheless it does not suffice for a single Saturday with two Sundays, nor for a single Sunday with two Saturdays, because then this Saturday occurs within the limit of January (the 1st), as in the 6th row, while the Sunday within the limit of December (the 30th), as in the 3rd row; and just as it is not customary, according to the Greek Typikon itself, to read the Apostle and Gospel of the Sunday after the Nativity beyond the limit of the Feast of the Nativity, in January, but it must be transferred back to December 26, *διὰ τὸ ἀρχὴν λαμβάνειν τὸν Ἰαννουάριον μῆνα, καὶ τὴν τῶν Χριστοῦ Γεννῶν ἑορτὴν ἀποδίδοσθαι* [because the month of January takes its beginning, and the feast of the Nativity of Christ is concluded (undergoes Apodosis)]: so it is not customary to read the Apostle and Gospel of the Sunday before Theophany within the limit of the Feast of the Nativity, or the Apostle and Gospel of the Saturday after the Nativity within the limit of January. On this account it is no wonder if the Slavs supplemented the missing Typika for themselves. Nevertheless the Typika of our Menaion and the Russian ones set forth on December 26 for the Saturdays and Sundays between the Nativity and Theophany, probably through the negligence of copyists, are in certain points poorly drafted, and require correction, in our opinion thus: The Typikon of the first case: *If the Nativity occurs on Sunday*, ought to conclude thus: “as appears in the present Chapter in the order, when the Circumcision and Basil occur on Sunday” (as in the following case). The Typikon of the third case: *If the Nativity is on Tuesday*, from the words: “And for the Sunday” even unto the end, ought to be: “And for the Sunday which is before Theophany, we read the Prokimenon, Apostle, Alleluia, and Gospel on January 1, as will be indicated in the order, when the Circumcision and Basil occur on Sunday.” The Typikon of the fifth case from the words: “and on the morrow, we read the Prokimenon, Apostle, Alleluia, and Gospel first of the Sunday before Theophany, then of the Saint” and the rest as there. For that which the Typikon commands to read in this case at Matins two Gospels, one of the Sunday, and the other regular, we do not know whence it has this, because except for a single regular Sunday Gospel at Matins we know none, for the Sunday before Theophany has nothing at Matins.
