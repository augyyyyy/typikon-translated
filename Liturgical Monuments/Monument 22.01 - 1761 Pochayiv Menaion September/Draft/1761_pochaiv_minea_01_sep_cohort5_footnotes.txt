[^33]: *Establish our faithful king (вѣ́рнагѡ королѧ̀ на́шего оу҆твердѝ)*: In the 1761 Pochaiv Menaion, published under the jurisdiction of the Basilian Order within the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, this petition in the festal Theotokion explicitly names the Catholic monarch of Poland (Augustus III of Poland, Elector of Saxony, r. 1734–1763) rather than the Russian Tsar or Emperor found in Moscow Synodal printings (*благовѣ́рныхъ царе́й на́шихъ* or *вѣ́рнагѡ импера́тора на́шего*).

[^34]: *O divine and God-wise and sacred pair of fathers, most radiant (Бж҃твеннаѧ и҆ бг҃омꙋ́драѧ и҆ сщ҃еннаѧ дво́ице ѻ҆тє́цъ пресвѣ́тлаѧ)*: The closing troparion of Ode 9 invokes the monastic dyad of the Judean wilderness: Saint Euthymius the Great and his disciple and co-founder Saint Theoctistus, whose coenobium in the wadi served as the foundational nursery for wilderness ascetics.

[^35]: *Idiomelon. By Ephraim of Caria (Самогла́сенъ. Є҆́фрема карі́йскагѡ)*: Ephraim of Caria (Greek: *Ἐφραὶμ ὁ Καρίας*), an 8th/9th-century Byzantine hymnographer whose idiomelic stichera for prominent martyrs and hierarchs are preserved in the Studite and Constantinopolitan liturgical traditions.

[^36]: *With those who suffered with thee (съ пострада́вшими съ тобо́ю)*: The 1761 Pochaiv edition prints the typographical defect *съ токо́ю* for *съ тобо́ю*. The hymn addresses Saint Anthimus together with the multitude of Christians of Nicomedia who suffered martyrdom alongside him under Diocletian and Maximian.

[^37]: *The Holy Hieromartyr Babylas... and the Holy Prophet and God-Seer Moses (Ст҃а́гѡ сщ҃енномꙋ́ченика вавѵ́лы... И҆ ст҃а́гѡ проро́ка и҆ бг҃ови́дца мѡѷсе́а)*: Saint Babylas was Bishop of Antioch (d. 250/251) during the Decian persecution; according to Saint John Chrysostom's renowned panegyric (*De Sancto Babyla contra Iulianum et Gentiles*), Babylas barred the pagan Roman Emperor (traditionally Philip the Arab or Decius) from entering the Christian assembly until he did penance for a murder. He was martyred together with three young Christian brothers whom he had catechized: Urban, Prilidian, and Epolonius (along with their mother Christodoule). The same day commemorates the Holy Prophet Moses, who beheld the divine glory on Mount Sinai and mediated the Old Covenant.

[^38]: *With innocent children (съ неѕло́бивыми ѻ҆́троки)*: An explicit reference to the three young boys—Urban (twelve years old), Prilidian (nine), and Epolonius (seven)—who were interrogated, tortured, and beheaded with Saint Babylas.

[^39]: *To be made a god over Pharaoh (є҆́же ѡ҆божи́тисѧ над̾ фараѡ́номъ, ꙗ҆́кѡ бг҃ъ)*: An explicit hymnographic allusion to Exodus 7:1: "And the Lord said unto Moses, See, I have made thee a god to Pharaoh: and Aaron thy brother shall be thy prophet."


[^40]: *Thou veilest evil-believing blindness, revealing a revelation unto the faithful (спокрыва́еши ѕловѣ́рное ѡ҆слѣпле́нїе, ꙗ҆влѧ́ѧ вѣ́рнымъ ѡ҆крове́нїе)*: An explicit allusion to Exodus 34:29–35 and 2 Corinthians 3:13–16, where Moses put a veil over his shining face when speaking to the people of Israel who could not gaze upon the divine glory.

[^41]: *Having seen His back parts... and through the cleft (ви́дѣвши за́днаѧ... и҆ чрез̾ скважню̀)*: A direct liturgical citation of Exodus 33:22–23, where God places Moses in a cleft of the rock and permits him to see His 'back parts' (*ὀπίσω μου* / *за́дняя*), patristically interpreted by the Church Fathers (Gregory of Nyssa, John Chrysostom) as the foreshadowing of God's revelation through the human nature of Christ in the Incarnation.

[^42]: *Behold, I and the children whom God hath given me (се́ а҆́зъ и҆ дѣ́ти ꙗ҆̀же мнѣ̀ дадѐ бг҃ъ)*: Citing Isaiah 8:18 (quoted also in Hebrews 2:13), applied liturgically to Saint Babylas and the three spiritual sons catechized and martyred with him.

[^43]: *Even as Simeon foretold (ꙗ҆́коже сѷмеѡ́нъ проречѐ)*: An allusion to Luke 2:35 ('Yea, a sword shall pierce through thy own soul also'), fulfilling Simeon's prophecy at the Crucifixion.

[^44]: *By John the Monk (Їѡа́нна мона́ха)*: Saint John of Damascus (ca. 676–749), monk of Saint Sabbas the Sanctified Lavra, author of the Tone 6 canon honoring Saint Babylas.

[^45]: *Moses, first of the prophets... and in the Theotokia, by Clement (Мѡѷсе́й проро́кѡвъ пе́рвый... Въ бг҃оро́дичныхъ же Кли́ментово)*: The Greek Menaion preserves this exact acrostic: *Μωσεὺς προφητῶν πρῶτος αἰνείσθω λόγοις*, with the Theotokia signed by Clement the Hymnographer (9th century).

[^46]: *Hidden by the knowledge of God as in an ark... therefore the queen and divine Providence reared thee (сокрове́но зна́нїемъ бж҃їимъ, ꙗ҆́коже въ ковче́зѣ... тѣ́мъ тѧ̀ воспита́ше цр҃ца)*: Allusion to Exodus 2:2–10, where the infant Moses was placed in an ark of bulrushes by the river's brink, discovered by Pharaoh's daughter (called 'queen' or 'princess' in Byzantine hymnography), and nurtured under divine Providence.

[^47]: *The Holy Spirit through an angel clearly taught thee the knowledge of ancient things (дх҃ъ ст҃ы́й чрез̾ а҆́гг҃ла, дре́внихъ навꙋчѝ тѧ̀ зна́нїю ꙗ҆́снѡ)*: Reflecting the patristic and Jewish tradition (attested in the Book of Jubilees and Patristic commentaries on Genesis) that an angel of the Lord revealed the primeval account of the six days of Creation and the patriarchal generations unto Moses during the forty days upon Mount Sinai.


[^48]: *When the slowness of thy tongue and thy voice was made clear (Оу҆гна́нїю ѧ҆зы́ка и҆ гла́са твоегѡ̀, оу҆ѧсни́вшꙋсѧ)*: An allusion to Exodus 4:10, where Moses professed: 'O my Lord, I am not eloquent... but I am slow of speech, and of a slow tongue', which God removed, inspiring him to set down the words of the book of Genesis (*бытїѧ̀ та́инъ бж҃їихъ*).

[^49]: *For God appeared unto thee as a Son (ꙗ҆ви́сѧ бо сы́нъ тебѣ̀ бг҃ъ)*: In Patristic Christological typology (e.g. Justin Martyr, Irenaeus, John Chrysostom), all theophanic visions of the Old Testament—including the burning bush and Sinai—were manifestations of the pre-incarnate Son and Word of God.

[^50]: *If Thou hast known me above all... manifest Thyself intelligently unto me (а҆́ще позна́лъ є҆сѝ мѧ̀ па́че всѣ́хъ... разꙋ́мнѡ мнѣ̀ ꙗ҆ви́сѧ)*: A liturgical paraphrase of Exodus 33:12–13: 'Yet Thou hast said, I know thee by name, and thou hast also found grace in My sight. Now therefore, I pray Thee, if I have found grace in Thy sight, shew me now Thy way, that I may know Thee.'

[^51]: *Thou didst destroy the seven nations of Canaanite malice (се́дмь оу҆́бѡ ѕлѡ́бы ханане́йскїѧ ѧ҆зы́ки погꙋби́лъ є҆сѝ)*: Referring to Deuteronomy 7:1: 'seven nations greater and mightier than thou; the Hittites, and the Girgashites, and the Amorites, and the Canaanites, and the Perizzites, and the Hivites, and the Jebusites.'

[^52]: *Tablets of the Law written with His finger (Пи́санныѧ скрижа̑ли пе́рстомъ є҆гѡ̀ зако́нныѧ)*: Citing Exodus 31:18: 'tables of stone, written with the finger of God.'

[^53]: *As an ark, covered everywhere with the rays of the Spirit as with gold (ковче́гъ бцⷣе, зарѧ́ми дх҃а всюдꙋ̀, ꙗ҆́кѡ зла́томъ покрове́нъ)*: Typological interpretation of the Ark of the Covenant overlaid with pure gold within and without (Exodus 25:11), prefiguring the all-pure Virgin sanctified by the Holy Spirit.


[^54]: *Korah was shown descending alive into Hades (коре́й... ꙗ҆́висѧ низше́дъ во а҆́дъ жи́въ)*: An explicit citation of the rebellion of Korah in Numbers 16:31–33, where the earth opened her mouth and swallowed up Korah, Dathan, and Abiram alive into the pit (*во а҆́дъ*), demonstrating God's vindication of Moses and Aaron's legitimate authority.

[^55]: *Michael the leader of the heavenly hosts appeared as the divine guardian of thy body (ꙗ҆ви́сѧ михаи́лъ нача́лникъ нбⷭныхъ, и҆ тѣ́ла твоегѡ̀ бж҃е́ственный храни́тель)*: A direct liturgical invocation of Jude 1:9: 'Yet Michael the archangel, when contending with the devil he disputed about the body of Moses, durst not bring against him a railing accusation, but said, The Lord rebuke thee.'

[^56]: *Aaron's divine rod showed thee forth budded (А҆арѡ́новъ тѧ̀ бж҃е́ственный же́злъ прозѧбнꙋ́ти показа̀)*: An allusion to Numbers 17:8, where Aaron's dry rod miraculously budded, blossomed, and yielded ripe almonds, an ancient patristic type of the Virgin Mary conceiving and giving birth without human seed.
