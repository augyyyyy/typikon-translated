[^26]: *Month of September: Of the Holy Hieromartyr Anthimus... (Мцⷭъ Септе́мврїй ст҃а́гѡ сщ҃нному́ченика а҆нѳі́ма...)*: The right margin of fol. 15v (leaf p32) displays a vertical cinnabar rubric serving as an epigraphic running indicator announcing the commemoration for the following day (September 3).

[^27]: *The Holy Hieromartyr Anthimus... and our Venerable Father Theoctistus (Ст҃а́гѡ сщ҃енному́ченика а҆нѳі́ма... И҆ преподо́бнагѡ ѻ҆ц҃а̀ на́шегѡ ѳеокти́ста)*: Saint Anthimus, Bishop of Nicomedia, was beheaded ca. 303–304 during the Great Persecution under Diocletian and Maximian, following the burning of the 20,000 martyrs in the church of Nicomedia. Saint Theoctistus (d. 451/467) was the companion and co-laborer of Saint Euthymius the Great in the Judean wilderness, establishing the renowned coenobitic lavra in the wadi known as the Monastery of Saint Theoctistus (Deir Mukallik).

[^28]: *Podoben: "On the Third Day" (Тридне́венъ)*: Byzantine model melody (*automelon*) *Τριήμερος ἀνέστης*, characteristic of Tone 6 Stavrotheotokia appointed on Wednesday and Friday cycles in the Octoechos and Menaion.

[^29]: *Canon of the Hieromartyr, whose Acrostic is (Кано́нъ сщ҃енному́ченика, є҆гѡ́же краегране́сїе)*: The acrostic (Slavonic: *краегранесіе*) is an alphabetical/thematic phrase woven through the opening letters of the troparia across the odes. Here the Slavonic reads: *Цвѣ́тъ тѧ̀ пою̀ па́стырей и҆ му́ченикѡвъ* ("The bloom of pastors and martyrs I sing"), reflecting the Greek hymnographic original *Ἀνθῶ σε τὸν ἔξαρχον ποιμένων, ἄθλων*, wordplay on the name of the Hieromartyr *Anthimus* (from Greek *ἄνθος*, bloom, flower).

[^30]: *Thy struggles, O Theoctistus, I recount. By Theophanes (Твоѧ̑ по́двиги ѽ ѳеокті́сте сказу́ю. Деофа́ново)*: The acrostic of the second canon explicitly names its author, Saint Theophanes the Branded (Graptos, ca. 778–845). In the Greek Menaion, the acrostic reads: *Τὰ σὰ παλαίσματα, Θεόκτιστε, φράζω. Θεοφάνους*.

[^31]: *Ode 4. Heirmos: Thou didst mount upon horses... (Сѣ́лъ є҆сѝ на ко́ни...)*: An allusive liturgical citation of the Prophet Habakkuk's canticle: 'Thou didst mount upon Thine horses, and Thy chariots were salvation' (Habakkuk 3:8).

[^32]: *In the tomb a saint buried thy body, one who was bright namesake of good cheer (Во гро́бѣ тѣ́ло твоѐ ст҃ъ погребѐ, и҆ бл҃году́шїѧ свѣ́тлый тезоимени́тъ)*: An explicit allusion to Saint Euthymius the Great, whose Greek name *Εὐθύμιος* derives from *εὔθυμος* ('of good cheer', 'encouraging', Slavonic *бл҃годꙋ́шїе*). When Saint Theoctistus reposed in extreme old age (ca. 451/467), Saint Euthymius came to the monastery and presided over his burial, as recorded in Cyril of Scythopolis's *Life of Saint Euthymius*.
