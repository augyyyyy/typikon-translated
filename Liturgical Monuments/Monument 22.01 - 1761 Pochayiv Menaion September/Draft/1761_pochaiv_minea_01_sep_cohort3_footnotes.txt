[^17]: *preserve in peace the king (королѧ̀)*: The Pochaiv Menaion, printed in 1761 within the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth under royal patronage (King Augustus III, r. 1734–1763, named on the title leaf p1), explicitly petitions for the reigning Polish monarch under the vernacular East Slavic/Polish title *король*. This recensional reading contrasts sharply with contemporary Moscow synodal editions, which petition for the Russian Emperor (*цр҃ѧ́* / *царя*), and traditional Byzantine Greek editions, which preserve the imperial plural (*τοὺς βασιλεῖς*).

[^18]: *By Andrew of Pyrgos (А҆ндре́а пѵ́рскагѡ)*: St. Andrew of Crete (ca. 660–740), Archbishop of Gortyna, who is frequently designated in Greek hymnographic manuscripts and Slavonic liturgical codices by his monastic origin or epithet (variously *τοῦ Πυρροῦ*, *Andrew of Pyrgos*, or *Andrew of Jerusalem*).

[^19]: *One should know (Подоба́етъ вѣ́дати)*: This rubrical chapter sets forth the liturgical order when September 1 (the Indiction) coincides with a Sunday, prescribing the omission of the office of the forty holy ascetic women and detailing the combined Resurrectional and festal sequence at Vespers, Matins, and Divine Liturgy.

[^20]: *Memory of the Holy Martyr Mamas (Ст҃а́гѡ мч҃ника ма́манта)*: Saint Mamas of Caesarea in Cappadocia (ca. 260–275), child martyr under Emperor Aurelian, born in prison to martyred Christian parents Theodotus and Rufina, renowned as an ascetic hermit of Mount Argaios.

[^21]: *By Byzantios (Вѷзанті́ево)*: Ascribed in Byzantine liturgical sources to Leo the Despot or other Constantinopolitan hymnographers bearing the appellation *Βυζάντιος* (*the Byzantine*).

[^22]: *The composition of Theophanes (Творе́нїе ѳеофа́ново)*: Saint Theophanes the Branded (Graptos, ca. 778–845), monk of Saint Sabas Monastery, Metropolitan of Nicaea, prominent confessor during the second Iconoclast persecution, and prolific Melodist.

[^23]: *raise the irrational brute creation unto rational hymnody*: An allusion to the hagiographical tradition of Saint Mamas living in ascetic solitude upon Mount Argaios, where wild animals assembled meekly around him, fed from his hand, and were milked by him to provide nourishment for the poor.

[^24]: *a voice from heaven... lightening the pain of thy sufferings*: According to the traditional *Martyrium* and Byzantine Synaxarion of Saint Mamas, while the young martyr was being scraped with iron claws, a celestial voice resounded: *"Be strong and of good courage, Mamas!"*, which miraculously alleviated the physical pain of his wounds.

[^25]: *a most fair dove bearing the food of incorruption*: An allusion to the hagiographical account wherein a white dove visited the imprisoned martyr in his dungeon cell, bearing heavenly nourishment to sustain him during his confinement.
