*Sticheron 2:*
At first thou didst offer unto God an unbloody sacrifice as a divine hierarch; and as an acceptable whole-burnt offering thou didst bring thyself through blood unto Christ as a true hieromartyr, O divinely-speaking and most honorable father; and now pray that He grant unto the Churches union and peace, and unto our souls great mercy.
*Sticheron 3:*
Casting down the net of dissension by thy hierarchic word, thou didst confirm with faith the hearts of the unstable, and hast passed over into eternal life, having suffered for the Union; thou wast a witness of the Lord, O hieromartyr Josaphat; and now pray that He grant unto the Churches union and peace, and unto our souls great mercy.
*Sticheron 4:*
Staining thy sacred vestment with the blood of thy holy flesh, O father and hierarch, thou didst receive a twofold crown from Christ, O most wealthy one; and thou dost dance with the angels in Heaven, standing before the Holy Trinity: pray that He grant unto the Churches union and peace, and unto our souls great mercy.
*Sticheron 5:*
O hieromartyr Josaphat, thou didst accomplish an honorable life in purity, and didst unite the people in concord by thine honorable words and by grace; thou hast passed over unto the day that knows no evening, having washed thyself in the blood of martyrdom, O divinely-blessed one; and now pray that He grant unto the Churches union and peace, and unto our souls great mercy.
*Sticheron 6:*
Having entered understandingly into the cloud of unsetting light, the ineffable light, as hieromartyr and shepherd, he learned the unutterable Mysteries of Christ; for being illuminated as a martyr and secretly instructed as a shepherd, he was adorned with customary double crowns of glory; and now pray that He grant unto the Churches that same union, and peace, and unto our souls great mercy.

*AT THE PRAISES, Stichera, Tone 6:*
*Sticheron 1:*
Wholly dedicated from youth unto the Master Christ, being apart from flesh and worldly love, an excellent monk wast thou, O most sacred father Josaphat, and a receptacle of the Holy Spirit; thou didst preserve purity of soul and body, living upon earth like an angel among monastics, in obedience and poverty, in love toward God and neighbor, in prayers and all-night vigils, becoming an example unto all monastics, and a mirror of every virtue; thou didst lift up thy mind unto heavenly things, to behold the ineffable beauty of Christ the King and God, Whom thou didst love with all thy heart; and being strengthened by His power, thou didst endure many afflictions and torments: pray with boldness unto Him for our souls.
*Sticheron 2:*
Seated upon the hierarchic throne, O all-blessed one, thou didst adorn thyself with great virtues, directing souls unto repentance and salvation through teaching and deeds; therefore arming thyself with purity and fervent prayers, with fasting and all-night vigil, as the Master Christ drew near unto His Passion and death upon the Cross, so also didst thou prepare thyself for suffering, O Hieromartyr Josaphat; therefore the ungodly enemies, rushing upon thee like unmerciful beasts, dragging thee like a gentle lamb, rent thee unjustly, while thou didst suffer valiantly and with boldness pray for our souls.
*Sticheron 3:*
The ungodly people, thine enemies, as David spake in the Psalms: They that hate thee have roared (Ps. 73:4); they maliciously consulted together against thy head, O blessed Josaphat, and taking evil counsel they said: Come, and let us destroy him from among the nations, that his name may be remembered no more (Ps. 82:5); and as they took counsel with one consent, they attacked thee fiercely, and cleaved thine honorable head, dragging thee unmercifully through the streets, and poured out thy blood like water in the town; then tying stones unto thy neck and holy feet, they mockingly cast thee from the hill into the rushing river: O thy courage and longsuffering! *Sticheron 1:*
