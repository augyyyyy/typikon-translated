# PART I: FESTAL OFFICES DECREED BY THE SYNOD OF ZAMOŚĆ

*OFFICE OF THE FEAST OF THE MOST HOLY MYSTERY OF THE EUCHARIST:*

*Celebrated on the Thursday after the Sunday of All Saints.*[^8]

*On Wednesday evening at "Lord, I have cried," we set 8 stichoi. Tone 2:*

*Tone 2. Sticheron 1:*
The great mystery of Thy becoming man,[^9] while reclining at supper, Thou didst reveal unto Thy initiates, O Lover of mankind: Eat the Living Bread; this is My Body, and the Blood of incorruptible life. *(Twice)*

*Sticheron 2:*
The upper room appeared greater than the shadow of old, where Christ celebrated the Passover, both the unbloody supper and the reasonable service; and the table of the perfected Mysteries therein is a spiritual altar.

*Sticheron 3:*
Taking the blessed Bread, being Himself the heavenly Bread, giving thanks unto God the Father Who begat Him, and taking also the cup, He gave unto the disciples, crying aloud: Take, eat, this is My Body, and drink the Blood of incorruptible life.

*Sticheron 4:*
The Lord nourished His disciples with heavenly food; before the Passion He gives them the angelic Bread rather than the marvelous manna. Your fathers, said the Lord, ate manna in the wilderness and died; this is the Bread which comes down from heaven: if any man eat of this Bread, he shall not die, but shall live unto ages. Whereof do Thou also make us worthy now to partake, by the prayers of Her that bare Thee, the Mother of God.

*Glory... Now and ever... Tone 4:*
Come, ye faithful lovers of the feasts, let us clap our hands today at the world-saving solemnity of the Living Bread, and the Lamb of God Who takes away the sins of the world and transgressions; not in the leaven of old malice, but in the unleavened bread of purity and truth; having therefore cleansed ourselves, let us worthily partake of Him, that we may obtain His eternal Kingdom.

*The Service. Entrance, and the Prokeimenon of the day. Then the Paremias:*

*A Reading from Exodus (Exodus 19:10–19):*
The Lord said unto Moses: Go down and testify unto this people, and sanctify them today and tomorrow, and let them wash their garments. And let them be ready against the third day; for on the third day the Lord will come down upon Mount Sinai before all the people. And thou shalt set bounds unto the people round about, saying: Take heed to yourselves, that ye go not up into the mount, or touch the border of it; whosoever touches the mount shall surely die. There shall not a hand touch it, for he shall surely be stoned, or shot through with an arrow; whether it be beast or man, it shall not live. When the voice of the trumpet and the cloud depart from the mount, they shall come up to the mount. And Moses went down from the mount unto the people, and sanctified his people; and they washed their garments. And he said unto the people: Be ready, for three days approach not a woman. And it came to pass on the third day in the morning, that there were voices and lightnings, and a thick cloud upon Mount Sinai, and the voice of the trumpet exceeding loud; and all the people that were in the camp trembled. And Moses brought forth the people out of the camp to meet with God; and they stood at the nether part of Mount Sinai. And Mount Sinai was altogether on a smoke, because God descended upon it in fire; and the smoke thereof ascended as the smoke of a furnace, and all the people were greatly amazed. And the voices of the trumpet sounded long, and waxed exceeding louder; Moses spake, and God answered him by a voice.

*A Reading from the First Book of Kingdoms (1 Samuel 21:1–6):*
In those days, David came to Nob to Ahimelech the priest; and Ahimelech was astonished at his meeting. And he said unto him: Why art thou alone, and no man with thee? And David said unto the priest: The king hath commanded me a business today, and hath said unto me: Let no man know anything of the business whereabout I send thee. And as for what I commanded thee, I have appointed the young men to such and such a place called Emmonim. Now therefore if thou hast here under thine hand five loaves of bread, give them me, whatsoever is found. And the priest answered David, and said: There is no common bread under mine hand, but there is hallowed bread under my hand, kept for them who are clean from women, and I will give it them to eat. And David answered the priest, and said: Yea, we are clean from women about these three days, and since I came out on the way, all the young men are clean; and this way is clean, for today it will be sanctified because of my vessels. And Ahimelech the priest gave him the shewbread; for there was no other bread there except the bread of the presence that was taken from before the face of the Lord, and he gave it him on that day.

*A Reading from the Third Book of Kingdoms (1 Kings 17:1–16):*
The Word of the Lord came unto Elijah, saying: Get thee hence, and turn thee eastward, and hide thyself by the brook Cherith, that is before the face of the Jordan. And it shall be, that thou shalt drink of the brook; and I have commanded the ravens to feed thee there. And He went and did according unto the word of the Lord; and He sat by the brook Cherith, that is before the face of the Jordan. And the ravens brought him bread in the morning, and flesh in the evening; and He drank of the brook. And it came to pass after some days, that the brook dried up, because there had been no rain in the land. And the word of the Lord came unto Elijah, saying: Arise, go to Zarephath, which belongs to Zidon, and dwell there; behold, I have commanded a widow woman there to sustain thee. And He arose and went to Zarephath, to the gate of the city; and behold, the widow woman was there gathering sticks. And Elijah called to her from behind, and said unto her: Fetch me, I pray thee, a little water in a vessel, that I may drink. And as she was going to fetch it, Elijah called after her, and said: Bring me, I pray thee, a morsel of bread in thine hand. And the woman said: As the Lord my God lives, I have not a cake in secret, but an handful of meal in a barrel, and a little oil in a cruse; and behold, I am gathering two sticks, that I may go in and dress it for me and my children, that we may eat it, and die. And Elijah said unto her: Fear not, go and do according to thy word; but make me thereof a little unleavened cake first, and bring it unto me, and after make for thee and for thy children. For thus said the Lord God of Israel: The barrel of meal shall not fail, neither shall the cruse of oil diminish, until the day that the Lord sends rain upon the face of all the earth. And the woman went and did according to the word of Elijah; and she gave it him, and He did eat, and she, and her children. And from that day the barrel of meal wasted not, neither did the cruse of oil fail, according to the word of the Lord, which He spake by the hand of Elijah.

*At the Litiya, Stichera. Tone 6:*

*Tone 6. Sticheron 1:*
When the Ark was brought low, David the ancestor of God leaped and danced; but we today bow down unto Him as God, Whom the Ark prefigured in His humanity; for instead of manna, the Godhead incarnate, and instead of the tablets of the Law, Christ the Lawgiver is seen hidden, saving our souls.

*Sticheron 2:*
The Old Covenant possessed the Ark overlaid round about with gold, which was captured by the Philistines; it endured not to abide in the temple with the idol, but cast it down unto the earth, and broke its head and both palms of its hands. But Thou, O Christ our God, Lover of mankind, how wilt Thou enter into the temple of our wretched and defiled souls?

*Sticheron 3:*
Thou Who alone hast immortality, and dwellest in light unapproachable, how hast Thou joined Thyself with the idol of the darkness of sins? But O Light That enlightenest every man, shatter not nor cast forth into outer darkness our dark and sinful souls; but enlighten them by Thy goodness, we beseech Thee, O Christ beyond nature, Thou Abyss of tender mercy.

*The Service.*

*Tone 6. Sticheron 4:*
O gentle Lamb, That takest away the sins of the whole world, for the sake of man's salvation Thou didst transform bread into Thy Flesh; and in the bread Thou didst conceal the Godhead, Flesh, and soul by Divine power, Whom all we the faithful today festively worship with compunction and faith.

*Glory... Now and ever... Tone 2:*
Unto the mystical supper today and the Divine table, we the faithful approach with fear, desiring to taste of the immortal fountain; but trembling and fear seize us because of our many sins, wherefrom we desire to be cleansed by the tears of repentance, by the prayers of the Mother of God our Sovereign Lady.

*At the Aposticha, Stichera. Tone 1, Podoben: "Joy of the Heavenly Ranks" (Небным чином):*

*Tone 1. Sticheron 1:*
Let us praise the heavenly Living Bread, Who comes down unceasingly from heaven ineffably, saying with one mind: The Body of Christ, whereby we all are delivered, let us rejoice, and the Divine and holy Blood, wherewith we have been redeemed.

*Stichos 1: Man ate the bread of angels; He sent them food to the full (Ps. 77:25).*

*Sticheron 2:*
At supper Thou didst give Thine own self, O marvelous wonder, unto which disciples and friends as food: Eat bread, for this is My Body; and taking the cup Thou didst say: Drink ye all, this is My Blood.

*Stichos 2: And He rained down manna upon them to eat, and gave them the bread of heaven (Ps. 77:24).*

*Sticheron 3:*
O Divine, most honorable, and dearest Body of the God-man Word, save Thy servants from the affliction of many temptations and great distresses, granting the Heavenly Kingdom and life, for the sake of Thy mercy.

*Glory... Now and ever... Tone 8:*
O most glorious wonder! The Living and Divine Bread, having descended from heaven, was freely given for food at the mystical supper; for taking bread He blessed and gave it, saying unto the disciples: Take and eat, this is My Body; likewise also the cup. Therefore let each man examine himself, and so let him eat and drink unto forgiveness and eternal life.

*To the Most Holy Mystery of the Eucharist.*
*Now lettest Thou Thy servant depart... and the rest. Troparion, Tone 7:*
The supernatural Bread, Whom the angels and archangels with trembling desire to behold, is seen today festively upon the Altar, and is given unto men for food; wherein we, beholding Thy most rich mercy, O Christ our God, confess Thee with the Flesh to be God hidden beneath the appearance of the species, saying with invincible voice: Make us worthy to be partakers, that we may enjoy eternal life.

*Dismissal:*
Christ our true God, Who for the sake of His exceeding great mercy gave us His Flesh to eat and His true Blood to drink unto salvation at His mystical supper: by the prayers of His most pure Mother, of the holy twelve Apostles, and of all the saints, have mercy on us and save us, as He is a good God and Lover of mankind.

*At Matins, at "God is the Lord," the Troparion: "The supernatural Bread..."*

*After the first Kathisma, Sessional Hymn, Tone 7:*
Our great and most venerable Passover is Christ; having been eaten as bread, having been slain as a Lamb, He was offered for us an acceptable sacrifice unto God; Whose most pure Body and precious Blood piously worshiping, we say with compunction: Dwell Thou ever with us Thyself, and no one can do anything against us.

*Glory... Now and ever... Theotokion:*
O Birthgiver of God, Ever-Virgin Mary, behold the Flesh of thy Son, Christ our God, which He received from thy womb by the operation of the Most Holy Spirit, and at the mystical supper gave unto His disciples, saying: Take, eat, this is My Body, and drink, this is My Blood. Him do thou beseech with maternal boldness, that He consume not us who partake, but save our souls.

*After the second Kathisma, Sessional Hymn, Tone 4:*
Taking the heavenly Bread and the cup, Christ blessed them, showed them unto God the Father Who begat Him, and giving them unto the wondering disciples, said: Eat My Flesh, and he that drinks My Blood hath eternal life, and I will raise him up at the last day. Therefore we the unworthy, today festively bow down unto the most pure Mysteries of Thy Flesh and Blood, boldly believing [continues to next leaf] [continued from preceding leaf] that Thou art the Christ, the Son of the Living God, Who didst come into the world to save sinners.

*Glory... Now and ever:*
Seedlessly from the Holy Spirit thou didst conceive and bare the Son, Christ our God, Whose most pure Flesh, as God and our Redeemer, we bow down unto the face of the earth, and desire to be partakers; and unto thee as Mother we flee, O Most Pure Birthgiver of God, saying with compunction we beseech thee: Receive our prayers, and bear them unto thy Son and our God, that He may save our souls.

*Polyeleos. Megalynarion:*
We magnify Thee, O Christ Giver of life, Who for our sake didst transform bread into Flesh and wine into Thy Blood, Whom today as God incarnate we bow down and worship.

*Stichos 1: Great is the Lord, and greatly to be praised in the city of our God, in His holy mountain (Ps. 47:2).*
*Stichos 2: What shall I render unto the Lord for all that He hath rendered unto me? I will take the cup of salvation (Ps. 115:3–4).*
*Stichos 3: He hath made a memorial of His wonders; merciful and gracious is the Lord, He hath given food unto them that fear Him (Ps. 110:4–5).*
*Stichos 4: And He rained down manna upon them to eat, and gave them the bread of heaven; man ate the bread of angels, He sent them food to the full (Ps. 77:24–25).*

*Glory... Now and ever... Alleluia. (Thrice).*

*After the Polyeleos, Sessional Hymn, Tone 3, Podoben: "Beholding the beauty of thy virginity" (Красотѣ):*
The pre-eternal and unoriginate God, Who assumed human nature, came into the prepared upper room with His disciples, where Thou didst bring to an end the old Passover, O our Savior, and didst make a new mystical Passover; for taking bread He transformed it into His Flesh, and the cup into Blood, and gave them unto us for food and drink. Beholding today this Divine mystery, we say with compunction: Glory to Thy tender mercy, O Lover of mankind!

*Glory... Now and ever... The same.*

*The Gradual Hymns. 1st Antiphon of Tone 4.*

*Prokeimenon, Tone 4: He that eats My Flesh and drinks My Blood hath eternal life, and I will raise him up at the last day (John 6:54).*

*The Gospel according to John, Pericope 24 (John 6:55–58):*
The Lord said unto the Jews who came to Him: My Flesh is meat indeed... The end: He shall live unto ages.

*Sticheron after the Gospel, Tone 6:*
Today the angels are astonished, beholding Thee, the incarnate God, hidden in the Mysteries, worshiped with fear by men, and given for food; with them also we earthly ones praise Thee, the Master and Lover of mankind, saying: Make us worthy to partake of the most pure Flesh and Blood, O merciful Lord!

*Canon, Tone 2.*
*Ode 1. Heirmos: Come, ye people, let us sing a song unto Christ God...*
*Refrain before each troparion: Glory, O Christ, to Thy Flesh and Blood.*

*Troparion 1:*
The Bread of eternal life: may Thy holy Body be unto me, O compassionate Lord, and Thy precious Blood the healing of diverse diseases.

*Troparion 2:*
Thy most pure Blood, O Master, and Thy most holy Body, make me worthy to eat and drink with desire and faith.

*Glory...*
Defiled by unfitting deeds, I the wretched one am unworthy to partake of Thy most pure Body and Divine Blood, O Christ: whereof do Thou make me worthy.

*Now and ever... Theotokion:*
O good land, blessed bride, that didst bud forth the unplowed and world-saving ear of grain: make me worthy, eating this, to be saved.

*Katavasia. Heirmos: I shall open my mouth...*

*Ode 3. Heirmos: Establishing me upon the rock of faith, Thou hast enlarged my mouth...*

*Troparion 1:*
Grant me, O Christ, teardrops that cleanse the defilement of my heart; that cleansed with a good conscience, I may approach with faith and fear, O Master, unto the communion of Thy gifts.

*Troparion 2:*
Unto the remission of transgressions may Thy most pure Body be unto me, and Thy Divine Blood, and the communion of the Holy Spirit, and unto eternal life, O Lover of mankind, and alienation from passions.

*Glory...*
Make me worthy, O Lord, to partake uncondemned of Thy Body, and of Thy precious Blood, and to glorify Thy goodness.

*Now and ever... Theotokion:*
Most pure table of the Living Bread, Who for the sake of mercy from on high descended to give life unto the world: make me also now worthy, unworthy though I be, to taste thereof with fear and to live.[^10]

*The Service.*

*Katavasia: O Birthgiver of God, fountain of living water...*

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 1, Podoben: "When the stone had been sealed" (Камени знаменану):*
Eating the Lamb with Thy disciples according to the commandment of Moses, Thou, the spotless Lamb, didst mystically give Thyself unto us for food; unto the remission of sins, unto sanctification of souls, whereon feeding we pray: Let not the communion of Thy holy Mysteries become unto judgment or condemnation, because of our sins and transgressions.

*Glory... Now and ever:*
O Virgin Birthgiver of God, Mother of the Lamb and Shepherd, beseech the Son Whom thou didst bear, that He may save and enlighten our souls.

*Ode 4. Heirmos: Thou hast come forth from the Virgin...*

*Troparion 1:*
Thou didst will to become incarnate for our sake, O most merciful One, to be slain as a sheep for the sake of human sins: therefore I beseech Thee, cleanse also my transgressions.

*Troparion 2:*
Heal the wounds of my soul, O Lord, and sanctify me wholly; and make me worthy, O Master, that I the wretched one may become a partaker of Thy mystical Divine supper.

*Glory...*
Standing and groaning with fear and trembling, I lift up the eyes of my right hand on high unto Thee, O Savior, Who didst stretch forth both Thy hands upon the Cross; wherewith grant me the food of Thy good pleasure, establishing me and strengthening me in Thy fear.

*Now and ever... Theotokion:*
Make Thy tender-hearted Son merciful also unto me, O Sovereign Lady, and keep me Thy servant without defilement and without blemish: that receiving inwardly the spiritual pearl, I may be sanctified.

*Katavasia: He Who sits in glory...*

*Ode 5. Heirmos: O Christ, illumination of them that lie in darkness...*

*Troparion 1:*
As Thou didst foretell, O Christ, let it be unto Thy humble servant; and abide in me, as Thou didst promise: for behold, I eat Thy Divine Body and drink Thy Blood.

*Troparion 2:*
O Word of God and God, let the live coal of Thy Body be unto the illumination of me the darkened one, and Thy Blood unto the cleansing of my defiled soul.

*Glory...*
Having an exceedingly defiled soul and unclean lips, I dare not approach unto Thee, O Christ, and receive Thy Body: but do Thou make me worthy.

*Now and ever... Theotokion:*
O Mary, Mother of God, honorable dwelling of sweet fragrance, by thy prayers make me a chosen vessel: that I may partake of the Holy Mysteries of thy Son.

*Katavasia: All creation was astonished...*

*Ode 6. Heirmos: In the abyss of sins...*

*Troparion 1:*
Sanctify my mind, soul, and heart, O Savior, and my body; and make me worthy without condemnation, O Master, to approach Thy dread Mysteries.

*Troparion 2:*
May I be alienated from passions, being accounted worthy to have an increase of Thy grace, and the confirmation of life, through the communion of Thy holy Mysteries, O Christ.

*Glory...*
Grant me with fear and trembling to approach Thy Divine Mysteries, O Christ, and to receive Thy true and holy Body, and Thy true and holy and precious Blood.

*Now and ever... Theotokion:*
O Holy Word of God, O God, sanctify me wholly, who now come unto Thy Divine Mysteries, by the entreaties of Thy Holy Mother.

*Katavasia: This Divine and most honorable feast...*

*Kontakion, Tone 4:*
Behold, Christ lies before us as food for all: come, and falling down let us worship Christ God hidden in these Mysteries, crying aloud with compunction: O Master, consume us not the unworthy by communion, but be Thou the consumer of sins and the cleanser of our souls.

*Ikos:*
Having all drawn near unto the mystical table with fear, with pure souls let us receive the Divine Bread and the cup of salvation, having washed the feet of our conscience: that we may enjoy eternal life by the prayers of the Mother of God.

*Ode 7. Heirmos: Concerning the golden image...*

*Troparion 1:*
May the communion of Thine immortal Mysteries now be unto me, I pray, O Christ, a fountain of good things, light, life, and dispassion: conducing unto the advancement and increase of most divine virtue, O Thou only Good: that I may glorify Thee.

*The Service.*

*Troparion 2:*
That I may be delivered from passions, from enemies, necessities, and every affliction, with trembling, love, and piety, O Lover of mankind: approaching now unto the venerable, immortal, and Divine Mysteries, and make me worthy that I may sing unto Thee: Blessed art Thou, O Lord God of our fathers!

*Glory...*
O my wretched soul, O passionate soul, be astonished beholding the most glorious Mysteries! Sighing, weep, beating thy breast, calling out and saying: Cleanse me even as the prodigal!

*Now and ever... Theotokion:*
Having borne Christ the Savior beyond understanding, O thou who art full of God's grace: I thy defiled servant earnestly beseech thee, O pure one: desiring now to approach unto the most pure Mysteries, cleanse me wholly from defilement of flesh and spirit.

*Katavasia: The children did not serve creation...*

*Ode 8. Heirmos: They disregarded the golden image...*

*Troparion 1:*
Of Thy heavenly, dread, and holy Mysteries now, O Christ, and of Thy Divine and mystical supper, make me worthy to be a partaker, O God my Savior.

*Troparion 2:*
Fleeing unto Thy tender mercy, O Good One, with fear I cry: Abide in me, O Savior, and I, as Thou hast said, in Thee; for behold, trusting in Thy mercy, I eat Thy Body and drink Thy Blood.

*Glory...*
I shudder receiving the Fire, lest I be burned as wax and as grass; O the tender mercy of God! O the dread Mystery! How do I, who am clay, partake of the Divine Body and Blood, and am made incorruptible!

*Now and ever... Theotokion:*
The Divine Bread of life was incorruptibly baked within thy womb, O Mother of God, preserving thine immaculate womb uninjured: therefore as our Nourisher we praise thee unto all ages.

*Katavasia: The pious youths in the furnace...*

*To the Most Holy Mystery of the Eucharist.*
*Ode 9. Heirmos: Conception without seed...*

*Troparion 1:*
Taste Christ God, and see that the Lord of old, having become for our sake, once offered Himself as a sacrifice unto His Father, and is ever slain, sanctifying them that partake.

*Troparion 2:*
May I be sanctified in soul and body, O Master, may I be enlightened, may I be saved, may I become Thy dwelling through the communion of the Sacred Mysteries: having Thee dwelling within myself with the Father and the Spirit, by grace, O most merciful One.

*Glory...*
Like fire may Thy Body be unto me, and like light, and Thy most pure Blood, O my Savior: burning up the matter of sin, consuming the thorns of passions, and wholly enlightening me who worship Thy Godhead.

*Now and ever... Theotokion:*
God became incarnate of thy pure blood, therefore every generation praises thee, O Sovereign Lady; and the spiritual hosts glorify thee, for through thee they clearly beheld Him Who rules over all, substantiated in human nature.

*Glory... Now and ever... Theotokion:*
This is He, O most pure Virgin Birthgiver of God, worshiped in these Mysteries, Whom thou as Mother didst bear formerly in thy womb, holding Him in thine arms; Him do thou beseech for us, that He may save our souls.

*ORDER OF THE FEAST OF THE COMPASSION OF THE MOST HOLY THEOTOKOS*[^27]
*Celebrated on the Friday of the Second Week after All Saints Sunday.*
*On Thursday evening, at "Lord, I have cried," Stichera of the Mother of God on 6, Tone 2, Podoben: "When from the Tree" (Е҆гда̀ ѿ дре́ва):*
Unto all men a protectress, unto all a great refuge, unto all Christians a defender did He reveal thee, Who out of thee clothed Himself in our lowliness according to His tender mercy, and endured crucifixion and death for our sake: Him therefore beseech unceasingly, O Bride of God, that He send down forgiveness of transgressions unto all.
Of old the Virgin and Ewe-lamb, beholding Him Who sprang from her womb hanging upon the Tree as one condemned between two condemned men, crying aloud said: O my Son and my God, strange sight, strange Mystery! Unsearchable is the abyss of Thy wisdom, which no man can fathom, nor yet Thy longsuffering!
Where is the goodly beauty of Thy countenance? Where hath the fairness of Thy goodness hidden itself, O my Son? How is He Who created all things with ineffable beauty, God comely in fairness, made dishonored for the sake of all men, hanging upon the Tree without glory, now having no form nor beauty, O my Child! the all-good Virgin cried aloud, groaning and weeping.
Beholding Thee, O Jesus, lifted up upon the wood of the Cross, the unwedded Maiden wept and said: O sweet Child, why hast Thou left me alone who bare Thee? O unapproachable Light of the Pre-eternal Father, make haste and be glorified, that they who glorify Thy Divine Passions may receive Divine glory.
When the Virgin beheld Life dying upon the Tree, and [continues to next leaf] [continued from preceding leaf] pierced painfully with a spear, weeping cried aloud: O my Son and my God, what hath the ungrateful assembly repaid Thee? Woe is me, unable to endure the pain, consumed in my inward parts seeing Thee suffering, O Master!

When the Maiden who knew not man beheld Thee taken down from the Tree, stretched out and laid upon the earth without breath like any mortal man: taking Thee into her bosom, and kissing Thy lips and eyes, she cried aloud unto Him in wonder: How do I now behold Thee, Who givest life unto all, speechless and motionless? Truly a great wonder!

*Glory... Now and ever... Stavrotheotokion:*
Having endured many sorrows at the Crucifixion of her Son and God, the Most Pure one groaned with tears and cried aloud: Woe is me, O sweet Child! *ORDER OF THE FEAST OF THE BLESSED HIEROMARTYR JOSAPHAT,*
*Celebrated on the 16th day of the Month of September.*[^38]
*If it occur on Sunday, on Saturday at Vespers we recite: "Blessed is the man," the entire Kathisma. At "Lord, I have cried," Resurrection Stichera of the Tone on 4, and to the Saint on 6. But if on another day, we sing the Stichera of the Feast of the Elevation, and to the Saint.*
*Stichera to the Saint, Tone 4, Podoben: "Being called from on high" (Призва́нїи мꙋ́чницы):*
*Sticheron 1:*
Having stained thy sacred vesture with the blood of martyrdom, thou didst enter into the Holy of Holies, O praise-worthy one, where are the voices of them that feast, from Whom thou dost receive the victories of thy labors: the unfading crown, eternal beauty, and the life of paradise, O Josaphat, hieromartyr; and now pray that He grant unto the Churches union and peace, and unto our souls great mercy.
*Sticheron 2:*
At first thou didst offer unto God an unbloody sacrifice as a divine hierarch; and as an acceptable whole-burnt offering thou didst bring thyself through blood unto Christ as a true hieromartyr, O divinely-speaking and most honorable father; and now pray that He grant unto the Churches union and peace, and unto our souls great mercy.
*Sticheron 3:*
Casting down the net of dissension by thy hierarchic word, thou didst confirm with faith the hearts of the unstable, and hast passed over into eternal life, having suffered for the Union; thou wast a witness of the Lord, O hieromartyr Josaphat; and now pray that He grant unto the Churches union and peace, and unto our souls great mercy.
*Sticheron 4:*
Staining thy sacred vestment with the blood of thy holy flesh, O father and hierarch, thou didst receive a twofold crown from Christ, O most wealthy one; and thou dost dance with the angels in Heaven, standing before the Holy Trinity: pray that He grant unto the Churches union and peace, and unto our souls great mercy.
*Sticheron 5:*
O hieromartyr Josaphat, thou didst accomplish an honorable life in purity, and didst unite the people in concord by thine honorable words and by grace; thou hast passed over unto the day that knows no evening, having washed thyself in the blood of martyrdom, O divinely-blessed one; and now pray that He grant unto the Churches union and peace, and unto our souls great mercy.
*Sticheron 6:*
Having entered understandingly into the cloud of unsetting light, the ineffable light, as hieromartyr and shepherd, he learned the unutterable Mysteries of Christ; for being illuminated as a martyr and secretly instructed as a shepherd, he was adorned with customary double crowns of glory; and now pray that He grant unto the Churches that same union, and peace, and unto our souls great mercy.

For they said within themselves, reasoning unrighteously: Let us oppress the righteous man, and let us not spare his holiness, neither let us reverence the ancient gray hairs of the aged. Let our strength be the law. Let us lie in wait for the righteous, because he is not for our turn, and he is contrary to our doings: he upbraids us with our offending the law, and objects to our infamy the transgressions of our education. He professes to have the knowledge of God, and calls himself the child of the Lord. He was made to reprove our thoughts, and is grievous unto us even to behold: for his life is not like other men's, his ways of another fashion. We are esteemed of him as counterfeits; he abstains from our ways as from filthiness; he pronounces the end of the just to be blessed. Let us see if his words be true, and let us examine what shall happen in the end of him. Let us examine him with despitefulness and torture, that we may know his meekness, and prove his patience. Let us condemn him with a shameful death, for by his own words shall he be visited. Such things did they imagine, and were deceived; for their own wickedness blinded them. And they knew not the Mysteries of God, neither judged they that Thou art the only God, Who hast the power of life and death, Who savest in time of tribulation, and deliverest from every evil; Who art compassionate and merciful, giving grace unto Thy venerable ones, and with Thine arm resisting the proud.

*A Reading from the Wisdom of Solomon (Wisdom 4:7, 16–17, 19; 5:1–7 LXX):*[^41]
Though the righteous be prevented with death, yet shall he be in rest. The living ungodly shall see the righteous that is dead; for they shall see the end of the wise, and shall not understand what He in His counsel hath decreed of him: that the Lord shall cast down the ungodly speechless prone, and shall shake them from the foundations; and they shall be utterly laid waste in grief, and their memorial shall perish. For they shall come with fear when they reflect upon their sins, and their own iniquities shall convince them to their face. Then shall the righteous man stand in great boldness before the face of such as have afflicted him, and made no account of his labors. When they see it, they shall be troubled with terrible fear, and shall be amazed at the strangeness of his salvation. Repenting and groaning for anguish of spirit, they shall say within themselves: This was he whom we had sometimes in derision, and a proverb of reproach, fools as we were! We accounted his life madness, and his end to be without honor: how is he numbered among the children of God, and his lot is among the saints! Therefore have we erred from the way of truth and righteousness, the light hath not shined unto us, and the Sun rose not upon us. We wearied ourselves in the way of wickedness and destruction; we have gone through deserts where there lay no way, but as for the way of the Lord, we have not known it.

[^2]: *Compassion of the Most Holy Theotokos* (*Состраданіе Пресвятыя Богородицы* / *Compassio Beatae Mariae Virginis*, also known as Our Lady of Sorrows), instituted for the Ruthenian Church by the Synod of Zamość (1720, Title III, §4).

[^3]: Saint Josaphat Kuntsevych, Archbishop of Polotsk, martyred in Vitebsk on November 12, 1623; beatified by Pope Urban VIII in 1643.

[^4]: The Provincial Synod of Zamość (*Synodus Provincialis Zamoscena*), held in 1720 under Metropolitan Leo Kishka, standardized Ruthenian liturgical usage and decreed the universal celebration of these festal offices throughout the Kyivan Metropolia.

[^5]: The printing house of the Holy Dormition Lavra in Pochaiv was transferred to the Basilian Order (OSBM) in the 1730s and became the preeminent publisher of liturgical books in Western Ukraine.

[^6]: Church Slavonic alphanumeric dating `҂аѱма` corresponds to 1741 (`҂а` = 1000, `ѱ` = 700, `м` = 40, `а` = 1).

[^7]: 18th-century Polish manuscript provenance inscription indicating the book's contents and origin: *"This is the Book of Services... issued [by the authority of] the Synod of Zamość..."*

[^8]: Feast of the Most Holy Mystery of the Eucharist (Corpus Christi / *Боже Тѣло*), observed on the Thursday following All Saints Sunday (the octave of Pentecost) in the Ruthenian liturgical calendar, as mandated by the Synod of Zamość.

[^9]: The festal hymnography for Corpus Christi was newly composed for the Ruthenian Church in the 18th century, drawing upon the Latin office of St. Thomas Aquinas while adapting it to Byzantine hymnographic meters and the Eight Tones.

[^10]: The hymnography of the festal canon for the Most Holy Mystery of the Eucharist was composed following the Nine Odes structure of Byzantine hymnody, using the Heirmoi of Tone 2 from the Octoechos with the Katavasia of the Theotokos ("I shall open my mouth").

[^11]: Folio 5r (physical leaf p8 of the codex).

[^12]: The festal pericope from 1 Corinthians 11 is the classic Pauline account of the institution of the Holy Eucharist at the Mystical Supper, read at the Divine Liturgy on Holy Thursday and on the Feast of Corpus Christi.

[^13]: The evening service on the day of the feast continues the festal cycle of Corpus Christi, featuring newly composed idiomela celebrating Christ as the High Priest after the order of Melchizedek (Hebrews 7:17; Psalm 109:4).

[^14]: Allusion to Philippians 2:10 in the post-festal hymnography of the Holy Eucharist.

[^15]: The Church Slavonic printed witness reads `неневи́тно` (an orthographic corruption/hapax for `неиспы́тно` / *ineffably* or `несли́тно` / *unconfusedly*).

[^16]: Folio 2v (physical leaf p4 of the codex, containing the Stichera at "Lord, I have cried" for Wednesday evening).

[^17]: Allusion to Genesis 27:28–37, where Isaac's patriarchal blessing of Jacob with corn and wine is interpreted typologically as a mystical figure of the Eucharist.

[^18]: The vision of the Heavenly Liturgy in Revelation 4:4 and 5:6–14 applied to the Divine Liturgy and the presence of the Lamb upon the Holy Table.

[^19]: Folio 2v (physical leaf p4).

[^20]: Folio 2v–3r (physical leaves p4–p5).

[^21]: Folio 6v (physical leaf p9).

[^22]: The hymnographic formulation reflects the classical Ruthenian Eucharistic synthesis after the Synod of Zamość, confessing the whole Christ (*totus Christus*—Divinity, Flesh, and Soul) present under each Eucharistic species and particle.

[^23]: Folio 11r (physical leaf p14).

[^24]: Folio 5r (physical leaf p8).

[^25]: Folio 2v (physical leaf p4).

[^26]: Rubrical instruction for the Octave / Apodosis (*Apodosis* / *Отданіе*) of the Feast of the Most Holy Mystery of the Eucharist (Corpus Christi), observed on the octave day (the following Thursday).

[^27]: Feast of the Compassion of the Most Holy Theotokos (*Состраданіе Пресвятыя Богородицы* / *Compassio Beatae Mariae Virginis*, Our Lady of Sorrows), decreed by the Synod of Zamość (1720) to be celebrated universally across the Ruthenian Church on the Friday after the Octave of Corpus Christi (Friday of the second week after Pentecost / All Saints).

[^28]: Lamentations 1:1–2, 1:9, 1:12–13, 1:16–17, 1:20 (LXX versification), the classical festal Paremia prophesying the desolation of Zion, applied typologically to the sorrow of the Mother of God at the Cross.

[^29]: Lamentations 2:13, 2:15, 2:17–19; 3:1, 3:7, 3:12–13, 3:19–21, 3:22–26 (LXX versification).

[^30]: Lamentations 3:40–41, 3:55–59, 3:64; 4:1–2, 4:7; 4:20; 5:19, 5:21 (LXX versification). The phrase "the breath of our nostrils, the anointed of the Lord, was taken in their pits" (Lamentations 4:20) is a famous Christological and Patristic prophecy of the Passion.

[^31]: Festal Troparion of the Compassion of the Most Holy Theotokos, meditating on the scene at the Cross with the Virgin Mother and John the Beloved Disciple (John 19:25–27).

[^32]: The festal Matins Gospel for the Compassion of the Mother of God is the Johannine account of the Mother of God at the foot of the Cross (John 19:25–27).

[^33]: Sessional hymn alluding to Psalm 82:17 (LXX: "Fill their faces with dishonor; and they shall seek Thy Name, O Lord").

[^34]: The Ikos of the festal canon of the Compassion of the Most Holy Theotokos directly incorporates the dramatic poetic dialogue from the ancient Kontakion of St. Romanos the Melodist (*On Mary at the Cross*), drawing a striking contrast between Christ's first miracle at Cana in Galilee (turning water into wine) and His swift course to the Cross.

[^35]: Ode 9 concludes the body of the festal canon with double heirmoi and refrains, anticipating the concluding Exapostilarion (*Свѣтиленъ*), Praises, and Divine Liturgy of the Compassion of the Mother of God.

[^36]: Folio 14r (physical leaf p16 of the codex), containing the festal Troparion of the Compassion of the Most Holy Theotokos (*Со оученико́мъ Дѣ́вственникомъ* / *With the virgin disciple*).

[^37]: Judith 13:17–20 (LXX / Ruthenian Vulgate), appointed as the solemn Festal Epistle Reading (*Вмѣсто Апостола*) for the Feast of the Compassion of the Most Holy Theotokos, typologically applying Judith's deliverance of Bethulia to the Virgin Mother crushing the power of the adversary.

[^38]: Feast of the Blessed Hieromartyr Josaphat Kuntsevych (1580–1623), Archbishop of Polotsk, Vitebsk, and Mstislav, beatified by Pope Urban VIII on May 16, 1643. The Provincial Synod of Zamość (1720) instituted his universal festal commemoration across the Ruthenian Uniate Church on September 16 (the post-festal octave of the Elevation of the Holy Cross).

[^39]: Proverbs 10:7; 3:13–18; 8:1–10 (LXX versification), composite Wisdom reading traditionally appointed in Ruthenian and Byzantine Menaia for Holy Hierarchs and Martyrs.

[^40]: Wisdom 4:7; 6:12–16; 7:25–8:1; 2:12–24 (LXX composite Paremia), read at Great Vespers for Hieromartyrs, prophesying the persecution, trial, and martyrdom of the righteous by the ungodly.

[^41]: Wisdom 4:7, 16–19; 5:1–7 (LXX), the classic festal Paremia contrasting the ultimate boldness and triumph of the martyred righteous with the terror and repentance of their persecutors.

[^42]: Allusion to the schismatic factions and anti-Union violence centered in Kyiv and the Dnieper region during the early 17th century, which Josaphat resisted through his preaching and pastoral ministry.

[^43]: Festal Troparion of Blessed Hieromartyr Josaphat, Tone 4 (*Свѣти́льникъ свѣ́тлый*), summarizing his martyrdom at Vitebsk (November 12, 1623) for the unity of the Church and his entrance into the heavenly Holy of Holies.

[^44]: The Church Slavonic reads `ѡско́рдомъ` (*oskordom*), referring to the broadaxe / halberd with which Archbishop Josaphat was struck in the head during the riot at Vitebsk on November 12, 1623.

[^45]: Historical witness to the events following the martyrdom: Josaphat's body was tied to a heavy stone and cast into the Western Dvina River (Dzvina), but according to contemporary accounts a miraculous column of light revealed its location beneath the water, allowing the Uniate faithful to recover his incorrupt relics.

[^46]: Festal Kontakion of St. Josaphat, Tone 4 (*Зѧре́ю ѻ́гненною*), commemorating the miraculous vision of his youth when a fiery ember from the crucifix in the Church of St. Sophia in Volodymyr entered into his heart, inspiring his monastic and hierarchic vocation.

[^47]: St. Josaphat is compared to St. John the Theologian (*с Бгослово́мъ*), whose defense of Catholic communion and primacy was sealed by Josaphat's pastoral letters, canonical synodal decrees, and martyric death.

[^48]: The phrase "tri-natural solemnity" (*трїесте́ственное сѐ торжество̀*) is an epigraphic witness reflecting the threefold festal synthesis decreed by the Synod of Zamość: the Holy Eucharist, the Compassion of the Mother of God, and the Hieromartyr Josaphat.

[^49]: In the 1741 Pochaiv edition, the petition "establish our King" (*Королѧ̀ на́шего оу҆твердѝ*) invokes the reigning Polish-Lithuanian monarch (King Augustus III, r. 1734–1763), under whose royal privilege and protection the Basilian Printing House operated.

[^50]: A vivid colometric and hymnographic narrative of Josaphat's martyrdom in Vitebsk on November 12, 1623, drawing on Psalm 73:4 and 82:4–5, describing how his murderers cleaved his skull with an axe, dragged his mutilated body through the streets, weighted it with stones, and threw it from the high bank into the Dvina River.

[^51]: The official woodcut emblem of the Basilian Order of Saint Basil the Great (OSBM), prominently displaying the fiery pillar (*столпъ огненный*), symbol of Saint Basil the Great, within a Baroque wreath supported by putti. This device marks the official colophon-terminus of the festal liturgical collection promulgated by the Synod of Zamość.

[^52]: St. Peter, Metropolitan of Kyiv and All Rus (c. 1260–1326), born in Volhynia, who transferred the metropolitan see from Vladimir to Moscow in 1325. This single leaf (bearing the printed folio number `рѕ҃і` = 116) represents an independent supplementary liturgical printing bound into the codex by the Pochaiv Lavra binder.

[^53]: Internal folio cross-reference in the original printed book: `И҆щѝ Ли́стъ, ст` (*seek at fol. 200 / fol. 100*), directing the reader to the common readings for Hierarchs in the complete Menaion / Trebnik corpus.
