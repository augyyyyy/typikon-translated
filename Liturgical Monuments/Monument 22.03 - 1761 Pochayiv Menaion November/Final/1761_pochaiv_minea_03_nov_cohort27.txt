*Stichera of Saint Amphilochius, Tone 4. Podoben: "Thou hast given a sign" (Да́лъ є҆сѝ зна́менїе):*
Having inherited a self-governing character and a sovereign mind over passions, thou hast sovereignly given supremacy unto that which is better over the worse, O all-blessed one, having weighed them by the most righteous measure, O father. Therefore wast thou fit for the vision of God and the knowledge of God, O divinely-inspired hierarch!

Thou hast shattered the snares and destroyed the subtleties of heresies, O venerable one, by the straightness of dogmas and by the most certain theology; having avoided division and the confused constraint [of blending], O father.[^991] Remaining within the bounds of our pious faith, thou didst proclaim the Trinity in the unity of the Godhead.

Having skillfully made wise the ruler on earth, thou didst pass by his son without bowing unto him;[^992] wisely teaching that when the Son is despised without worship, the Father is moved to the warmest indignation—He Who begot Him before the ages beyond understanding and dispassionately, as incorporeal, as He alone knows, O venerable one!

*Other Stichera, of Saint Gregory, Tone 8. Podoben: "O most marvelous wonder" (Ѽ пресла́внагѡ чудесѐ):*
O most marvelous wonder! From swaddling bands dedicated diligently unto God, Who by His unfailing nods created all things; and already illumined by Him, thou didst pass through the night of passions. And thou didst shine forth as a light of healings and of the grace of miracles, driving away chronic afflictions and deceitful spirits!

O marvelous Gregory! Shining purely with the most radiant beams of the mind, thou didst put to sleep the surge-tossed sea of passions; and flying by dispassion upon honorable wings, thou didst reach that place where there is ineffable and incomprehensible beauty; ever praying for us who praise thee with hymns!

O Gregory [glory] of fathers, rule of the priesthood, image of chastity, strength of monastics, establishment of the Church...
