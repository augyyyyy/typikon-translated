[^1076]: An allusion to the traditional attribution to Saint Clement of Rome of the Apostolic Constitutions and Apostolic Canons, setting forth ecclesiastical order and canons. The printed text reads "вчинѧ́еши и҆сполне́нїе" (or "оу҆чинѧ́еши"), whereas standard Synodal editions read "вчиня́я исполне́ніе" (ordering the fullness [of the Church]).
[^1077]: The printed Slavonic text has the typographical misprint "нѣ́сныхъ" in place of "небе́сныхъ" (heavenly).
[^1078]: Typographical variant/misprint in the bottom catchword on fol. 152v (`Page_0302.jpg`), printed as "Рѣ́кностїю" for "Ре́вностїю" (Zeal), the opening word of leaf 303.
[^1079]: Quotation from Psalm 44:15 [LXX]: "Virgins shall be brought after her; her companions shall be brought unto thee." The printed text reads "глаго́ла па́хѡмъ" (or "па́кѡмъ", an irregular orthography for "па́ки глаго́ля", Greek: λέγων πάλιν).
[^1080]: Reference to the conversion of the Roman patrician Sisinnius (Sisinnes), who according to the hagiographical tradition of Saint Clement lost his sight and hearing while spying on the Christian assembly, and was subsequently healed and baptized by the saint.
[^1081]: Allusion to the celebrated vision of Hieromartyr Peter of Alexandria, who beheld Christ as a youth in a torn tunic. When Peter asked: "Who hath torn Thy tunic, O Savior?", the Lord replied: "Arius hath torn it; beware lest thou receive him into communion."
[^1082]: The printed Slavonic text reads "соꙋ́тлалъ є҆сѝ" (or "сосꙋ́тлалъ"), an archaic/Ruthenian formation corresponding to standard Church Slavonic "оу҆толи́лъ є҆сѝ" or "сокруши́лъ є҆сѝ" (thou didst suppress/destroy).
[^1083]: Allusion to the traditional miracle associated with the underwater shrine of Saint Clement in Chersonesus: a child was accidentally left behind when the tide returned, but was found alive and unharmed when the waters miraculously receded the following year on the saint's feast day.
[^1084]: Biblical allusion to Exodus 4:25, where Zipporah cut off the foreskin of her son with a sharp stone (flint) to avert the lethal wrath of the Lord. Here, Saint Peter of Alexandria's martyrdom is hailed as the final sealing sacrifice that ended the Diocletianic persecutions.
[^1085]: In the printed rubric for the Liturgy, Ode 4 is specified ("пѣ́снь д҃, на и҃"), but standard Menaia prescribe Ode 9 of both feast and saints.
[^1086]: Allusion to the burning bush of Moses (Exodus 3:2) as a type of the virginal conception without corruption.
[^1087]: Acrostic in Greek: Γηθόσυνος μέλπω σε πόνοις, Ἀλύπιε. Ἰωσήφ (Rejoicing I praise thee in thy labors, O Alypius. By Joseph), translated into Slavonic as "Ра́дꙋѧсѧ похвалѧ́ю а҆лѵ́пїевы болѣ́зны. І҆ѡ́сифъ".
[^1088]: Allusion to 1 Samuel (1 Kings LXX) 1:28, comparing Saint Alypius's early consecration to that of the Prophet Samuel.
[^1089]: According to the Vita of Saint Alypius, before his birth his mother had a vision wherein the whole chamber was filled with heavenly light and angels chanting.
[^1090]: Heirmos of Ode 3, Tone 5 (Greek: Ὁ πήξας ἐπ' οὐδενὸς τὴν γῆν τῷ προστάγματί σου). The archaic Slavonic verb "Ѡ҆дробзи́вый" translates the Greek "having founded/made firm".
