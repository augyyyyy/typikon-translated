[^1061]: Tone 3 Podoben "Дꙋхо́мъ во ст҃или́щи" (In the Spirit in the sanctuary) from the Sunday Matins Exapostilaria.
[^1062]: The printed text has "Возжиза́ема" (or "Возгнуша́ема") "мꙋдрова́нїемъ женъ, ѿ [= Ѽ] дв҃о єкатерї́но". Other recensions read: "Возгнуша́вшися мудрова́нія женъ" (having spurned the [frail] mind of women).
[^1063]: Authorship rubric: Babylas the Monk (Вавѵ́ла мона́ха), 8th/9th-century hymnographer.
[^1064]: Authorship rubric: Sergius the Hagiopolite (Се́ргїй а́гїополї́тъ), 9th-century Jerusalem hymnographer.
[^1065]: Typographical corruption in printed text: "не́ною" in place of "небе́сною" or "не́бною" (heavenly).
[^1066]: The printed text gives "пѣ́снь з҃-ѧ, и н҃-ѧ", where "н҃-ѧ" is an error for "и҃-ѧ" (Ode 8).
[^1067]: The Apodosis (ѿда́нїе, apodosis) of the Entry of the Most Holy Theotokos falls on November 25.
[^1068]: Doxasticon at "Lord, I have cried" of the Entry of the Theotokos.
[^1069]: The rubric references leaf 115 (fol. 115) where the Troparion of the Entry ("Дне́сь благоволе́нїя") is printed in full.
[^1070]: Liturgical rubric outlining the rubrical displacement and transfer of the office of Hieromartyr Peter of Alexandria when the Apodosis of the Entry coincides with a Sunday.
[^1071]: Allusion to the historical miracle of the annual receding of the Black Sea at Chersonesus (Crimea) on the feast day of Hieromartyr Clement, revealing his submerged undersea shrine and relics.
[^1072]: The melody follows the Podoben "Твердыя и боговещанныя" (The steadfast and divinely-speaking preachers), originally composed for the Holy Apostles Peter and Paul (Tone 2).
[^1073]: Acrostic in Greek: Τὸν τρισόλβιον αἰνέσω Πέτρον (I shall praise thrice-blessed Peter), rendered in Slavonic as "Восхвалю̀ трибога́таго петра̀".
[^1074]: The printed rubric reads "Подо́быхъ то́йже" (an apparent typesetter misprint for "Подо́бенъ то́йже"), directing the hymn to be sung to the melody of Tone 8, or specifically "Премудрости" (Of Wisdom) as given in the following troparion.
[^1075]: Slavonic "дорѷно́сѧтъ" (literally: bear spears as a royal bodyguard, from Greek δορυφοροῦσιν), denoting an honorific escort by angelic hosts.
