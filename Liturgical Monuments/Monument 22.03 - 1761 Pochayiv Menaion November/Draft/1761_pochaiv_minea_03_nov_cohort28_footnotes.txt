[^1034]: A reference to the miraculous trial before the Roman council, in which St. Gregory held burning incense coals in his vestments without harm, vindicating his sanctity.
[^1035]: An explicit liturgical witness to icon veneration (*obrazu svyatago tvoego zraka*) embedded within the canon of the Afterfeast.
[^1036]: *Bog polozheniem byv* — translating Greek θεὸς θέσει γενόμενος, referring to the classic patristic doctrine of deification (*theosis*) by adoption and grace, in contrast to Christ Who is God by nature (*physei*).
[^1037]: Referring to the ancient tradition of the translation of St. Catherine's bodily relics to Mount Sinai by angels, where her monastery stands to this day.
[^1038]: "Spiritual Ethiopians" (*razumnykh murinov* / νοητῶν Αἰθιόπων) — a standard Byzantine patristic metaphor for invisible demonic powers and spiritual forces of darkness.
[^1039]: *Zemnym blagonasledie i nazdanie* — translating Greek εἰς ἀνάκτισιν καὶ θέωσιν τῶν γηγενῶν / structural restoration and rebuilding of earthly mortals.
[^1040]: *Obgotvoriv vse chelovechestvo* — another explicit witness to the doctrine of deification (*theosis*), affirming that by the Incarnation Christ deified human nature.
[^1041]: The Slavonic acrostic reflects the Greek acrostic: Αἰκατερίναν τὴν ἀείμνηστον μέλπω. Θεοφάνους.

[^1043]: Slavonic acrostic representing the Greek: Μερκουρίου με καρтеρία σκέποι. Ἰωσήφ ("May the steadfastness/strength of Mercurius shelter me. By Joseph [the Hymnographer]").
[^1044]: Cf. Ps. 44:10 (LXX) / Ps. 45:9: "The queen stood at thy right hand in vesture of gold".
[^1045]: A typical patristic antitype contrasting Eve, through whom transgression entered humanity, with Mary the Theotokos, through whom restoration and incorruption blossom.
