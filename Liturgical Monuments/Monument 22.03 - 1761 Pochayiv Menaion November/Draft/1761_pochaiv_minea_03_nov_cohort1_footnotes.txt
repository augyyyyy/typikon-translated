[^2]: Augustus III (1696–1763), King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania, and Elector of Saxony (as Frederick Augustus II), reigned 1734–1763.
[^3]: Sylvester Lubieniecki-Rudnytsky (Sylwester Lubieniecki-Rudnicki, d. 1777), Greek Catholic (Uniate) Bishop of Lutsk and Ostroh (1752–1777) and Exarch of the Metropolitanate of Kyiv, Halych, and all Rus'.
[^4]: Hypatius Bilynsky (Ipatii Bilynskyi, OSBM, 1704–1771), Protoarchimandrite of the Order of Saint Basil the Great (1751–1771), under whose leadership the Pochaiv press flourished.
[^5]: The Byzantine Creation Era date (Anno Mundi 7269), reckoned from September 1, 5509 BC, corresponds to 1760/1761 AD. The accompanying Christian Era date is explicitly given as 1761 AD.
[^6]: Saints Cosmas and Damian of Mesopotamia (Asia), sons of Saint Theodota, unmercenary physicians who healed both men and beasts without charge. In the Byzantine calendar, their commemoration on November 1 is distinguished from the Roman martyrs Cosmas and Damian (July 1) and the Arabian martyrs Cosmas and Damian (October 17).

[^7]: This Pochaiv rubric provides the full festal order (Polyeleos rank with Great Doxology) for parishes or monasteries where the temple is dedicated to Saints Cosmas and Damian, or whenever the superior chooses to celebrate them with greater solemnity.
[^8]: An allusion to the Pool of Bethesda in Jerusalem (John 5:2–4), where an angel moved the water once a year to heal the first person stepping in, contrasted with the perpetual healing stream flowing from the unmercenaries.
[^9]: Psalm 15:3 (LXX): "To the saints that are in His earth, the Lord has made all His desires marvelous in them."
[^10]: Matthew 10:8: "Freely you have received, freely give." This scriptural mandate forms the definitive hymnographic identity of the holy unmercenary physicians (Anargyroi).
[^11]: Mark 6:7–13 (pericope 23 in the Slavic reckoning): Christ sending forth the Twelve two by two with authority over unclean spirits to heal the sick and preach repentance.
[^12]: Psalm 132:1 (LXX).

[^15]: The acrostic of the second Canon, composed by Saint John of Damascus: ᾌσμασι γεραίρω τοὺς σοφοὺς ἀναργύρους ("With hymns I honor the wise unmercenaries").
