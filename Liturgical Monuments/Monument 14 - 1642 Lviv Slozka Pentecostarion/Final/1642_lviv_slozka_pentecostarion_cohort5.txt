=== LEAF p41 ===

hidden in the digging of a pit; and his father, deceived by the children with the blood-stained garment, grieved, thinking that he had been devoured by a beast. Then he was sold for thirty pieces of silver unto the Ishmaelites, who in turn sold him unto Potiphar, the chief of the eunuchs of Pharaoh, King of Egypt. And when his mistress raged against the chastity of the youth—because he refused to commit iniquity—he left his garment and fled; and she slandered him unto his master, and bonds and a bitter prison received him. Then, on account of the interpretation of dreams, he was brought forth, and shown unto the King, and appointed lord of all the land of Egypt. And again, on account of the distribution of grain, he was revealed unto his brethren. And having well spent the whole course of his life, he died in Egypt, renowned for his great chastity alongside his other virtues. This is a type of Christ: for Christ also was envied by His fellow Jews, and sold by His Disciple for thirty pieces of silver, and shut up in the dark and gloomy pit of the Tomb; and bursting forth thence by His own sovereign power, He reigns over Egypt—that is, over every sin—and utterly overcomes its dominion. And He rules over all the world, and in His love for mankind redeems us, the mystical Giver of grain, since He gave Himself for us, and nourishes us with heavenly Bread, His own Life-bearing Flesh. For this reason, then, the all-comely Joseph is commemorated today.[^185]

On this day also we commemorate the withered fig tree: because the divine Evangelists, namely Matthew and Mark, record after the account of the Palms: on the morrow, as they went out from Bethany, He hungered. And the other says: returning in the morning into the city, He hungered; and seeing a fig tree having leaves only—for it was not the season of figs—He came to it, and when He found no fruit upon it, He said: Let no fruit grow on thee henceforward unto the age! And immediately the fig tree withered away.[^186] Now the fig tree signifies the Jewish synagogue, upon which the Savior found not the fitting fruit, but only the shadow of the Law; and this He took away from them, leaving it entirely destitute. And if anyone should say: Why did the soulless tree, which had not sinned, become dry after receiving a curse? Let him learn that the Jews, seeing Christ always doing good unto all, and having never brought anything painful or grievous upon anyone, supposed that He had power only to do good, but not to work harm. But the Master and Son, the Lover of mankind, would not show this upon a man, that the ungrateful people might indeed be convinced that He possesses sufficient power unto punishment also; yet since as God He desires not to punish man, He worked this chastisement upon a soulless and insensible nature. Together with this, there is also a certain ineffable teaching handed down to us from the ancient wise elders, as Saint Isidore of Pelusium says: that this was the tree of transgression, whose leaves the transgressors needed for a covering; therefore

=== LEAF p42 ===

it was cursed by Christ in His love for mankind, since it had not suffered this then, that it might no longer bear fruit that was the cause of sin. And that sin is likened unto a fig is manifest: for it possesses the sweetness of sensual pleasure, the stickiness of guilt, and afterwards the hardening and prickling of the conscience. Nevertheless, by the Fathers, the narrative of the fig tree is placed here for the sake of compunction, just as that of Joseph is placed here because he bears the type of Christ. For the fig tree is every soul destitute of all spiritual fruit; wherein in the morning, after this present life, the Lord finding no rest in her, withers her with a curse and sends her into the eternal fire. And she stands as a withered pillar, striking fear into those who do not bring forth the fitting fruit of virtues. Through the prayers of the all-comely Joseph, O Christ our God, have mercy on us, now and ever and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^187]
