# PART V: GREAT FEAST OF HOLY PENTECOST, KNEELING PRAYERS, SUNDAY OF ALL SAINTS, AND REAR FLYLEAVES

## Memorial Saturday of the Seventh Week after Pascha (Saturday of Souls)[^912]

*On which we make commemoration of all the departed from the ages, our Orthodox fathers and brethren, and all who have fallen asleep in the faith of Christ.*

*On Friday evening, at Vespers: We set 6 Stichera. Stichera for the Martyrs, Tone 6:*
Thy martyrs, O Lord, not having denied Thee, nor departed from Thy commandments: by their prayers have mercy on us!

The passion-bearing martyrs and citizens of heaven, who suffered on earth, endured many torments, and received the perfect crown in heaven: let them pray for our souls!

Having suffered for Thy sake, O Christ, they endured many torments: by their prayers and intercessions, O Lord, preserve us all!

*Other Stichera for the Departed, Tone 8, Podoben: "In Eden of old" (И҆́же въ є҆де́мѣ):*[^913]
Today, O faithful, making commemoration of all the dead from the ages who lived piously in the faith: let us hymn the Savior and Lord, earnestly beseeching that in the hour of judgment they may give a good defense unto Him, our very God Who judges all the earth, that having obtained a place at His right hand in joy, in the portion of the Righteous and in the radiant lot of the Saints, they may be accounted worthy of His heavenly Kingdom!

O Savior, Who by Thy blood hast redeemed mankind, and by Thy death hast delivered us from bitter death, and by Thy Resurrection hast granted us life everlasting: give rest, O Lord, unto all that have fallen asleep in piety, whether in deserts, or in cities, or in the sea, or on land, or in any place: emperors, priests, high priests, monks, and laymen, in every age of the whole human race; and account them worthy of Thy Heavenly Kingdom!

By Thy Resurrection from the dead, O Christ, death no longer holds dominion over those that have died in piety: therefore we earnestly pray, give rest unto Thy servants in Thy courts and in the bosom of Abraham, from Adam even unto this day, who have served Thee purely: our fathers and brethren, friends together and kinsmen, every person who in this life has served faithfully and has passed away unto Thee in divers manners and forms, O God; and account them worthy to obtain Thy Heavenly Kingdom!

*Glory... Tone 8:*
I weep and lament when I contemplate death, and behold our beauty created according to the image of God lying in the graves, formless, inglorious, having no shape! O wonder! What is this mystery that has befallen us? How have we been surrendered unto corruption? How have we been yoked unto death? Truly by the command of God, as it is written, Who grants rest unto the departed!

*Both now... Theotokion, Same Tone [Tone 8]:*
"Who will not call thee blessed, O all-pure Virgin..." (see in the Fourth Saturday, fol. 196)[^914].

*Then, instead of the Prokeimenon, we sing Alleluia, Tone 8.*
*Verse: Blessed are they whom Thou hast chosen and taken, O Lord [LXX Ps. 64:5].*[^915]
*Verse: And their soul shall dwell among good things [LXX Ps. 24:13].*[^916]

*At the Aposticha, Stichera for the Martyrs, Tone 6:*
The army of the holy martyrs became an invincible weapon: for seeing death lying before them, and looking beyond unto the life to come, they were strengthened by Thine hope: by their prayers, have mercy on us!

*Verse: Blessed are they whom Thou hast chosen and taken, O Lord [LXX Ps. 64:5].*
Thy creative command was my origin and substance: for wishing to fashion a living being out of the invisible and the visible nature, Thou didst form my body from the earth, and gavest me a soul by Thy divine and life-creating breathing: therefore, O Savior, give rest unto Thy servants in the land of the living, and in the tabernacles of the righteous!

*Verse: And their soul shall dwell among good things [LXX Ps. 24:13].*
The tasting of the tree of old in Eden became grief unto Adam, when the serpent vomited forth his poison: for through it entered universal death, devouring man. But the Master having come overthrew the serpent, and granted us rest: let us therefore cry aloud unto Him: Spare, O Savior, and give rest with Thine elect unto those whom Thou hast taken!

*Glory... Tone 6:*
Like a blossom that withers, and like a shadow that passes away, every man is dissolved; but again at the sounding trumpet, all the dead, as in an earthquake, shall rise up to meet Thee, O Christ God: then, O Master, do Thou rank in the tabernacles of Thy Saints those whom Thou hast translated from us, mercifully overlooking their transgressions!

*Both now... Theotokion, Same Tone [Tone 6]:*
All my hope do I place in thee, O Mother of God: preserve me under thy protection!

*Then: "Now lettest Thou Thy servant depart in peace, O Master..." Troparion, Tone 8:*
O Thou Who by the depths of Thy wisdom in love for mankind dost govern all things, and dispensest what is profitable unto all, O only Creator: give rest, O Lord, unto the souls of Thy servants; for they have placed their hope in Thee, our Maker and Fashioner and God! *Glory... (the end of the Troparion).*

*Both now... Theotokion:*
In thee we have a wall and a haven, and an acceptable intercessor before the God Whom thou didst bear, O unwedded Mother of God, salvation of the faithful!

*And the remaining order of the Vigil: that is, the entire service for the departed, from beginning unto end, which is performed in the evening and in the morning:*

*and observe strictly as it was ordained for Meatfare Saturday (see the Lenten Triodion, fol. 37 through 51)[^917]. And concerning the Canon at Panikhidas: see Marko, Chapter 141; and in the manuscript: After the dismissal in church, we do the customary service in the narthex, at the Great Panikhida for the departed. And we sing the Canon for the dead of the current Tone of the Octoechos. But if the commemoration of one of the Great Saints falls on this day, then the service of the Saturday is sung in the crypt-chapel of rest; and in church the service of the Menaion is sung, that is, of the celebrated Saint, and of the patron of the temple—these two canons. For the rest, see above on folio 55[^918].*

## On the Seventh Saturday after Pascha: Matins

*After the Six Psalms, Alleluia in Tone 8, and its verses. Troparion: "O Thou Who by the depths of Thy wisdom..." (twice). Glory... Both now... Theotokion: "In thee we have a wall and a haven..."*

*After the First Kathisma, Sessional Hymn, Tone 6:*
By the resistance of their passion-bearing contest, the wounds of the tormentors were turned unto torments for the tormentors, and like a pillar of angelic faces holding the honors of victory, they extinguished the boasts of the tormentors and tyrants, and overthrew Belial by confessing Christ: O Lord Who didst strengthen them, glory to Thee!

*Verse: Marvelous is God in His saints, the God of Israel [LXX Ps. 67:36].*[^919]
Having endured the contest of suffering, the holy ones, receiving the honors of victory from Thee, brought to nought the schemings of the lawless and received unfading crowns: for their sake, O God, save our souls!

*Verse: The righteous cried, and the Lord heard them [LXX Ps. 33:18].*[^920]
Thou hast shown the memory of Thy saints, O Lord, like the paradise of Eden: for in it all creation rejoices; grant us therefore by their prayers peace and great mercy!

*Glory... Sessional Hymn for the Dead:*
Truly all things are vanity, and life is but a shadow and a dream; for in vain does every mortal disquiet himself, as the Scripture says: when we have gained the world, then we take up our dwelling in the grave, where emperors and beggars are together! Therefore, O Christ God, give rest unto the departed, as Lover of mankind!

*Both now... Theotokion:*
Calling Thy Mother blessed, Thou didst come unto voluntary Passion, and wast lifted up upon the Cross, seeking to search out Adam, saying unto the angels: "Rejoice with Me, for the lost drachma is found!" O God Who hast ordered all things wisely, glory to Thee!

*The Homily of John Chrysostom is read: Consolation concerning the Departed, and Instruction concerning Thanksgiving and Praise of God in Afflictions and in Immoderate Weeping. Its incipit: "God created the first man with all creation..."*[^921]

*Then after the Amomos [Psalm 118]: "Blessed art Thou, O Lord, teach me Thy statutes." "The choir of saints has found the fountain of life..." and all the Troparia in order. And then, Sessional Hymn for the Dead, Tone 5:*
Give rest, O our Savior, with the righteous unto Thy servants, and settle them in Thy courts, as it is written: overlooking as good their transgressions, voluntary and involuntary, known and unknown, O Lover of mankind!

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*
O Christ God, Who didst shine forth unto the world from the Virgin, and through her hast shown us to be sons of light, have mercy on us!

*Then the Reading from the same homily. Psalm 50 [LXX Ps. 50]. Then we sing the Stichology... Canon to the Saint of the Temple with its Heirmos on 6; and the present Canon on 8, the composition of our venerable father Sir Theodore the Studite, Tone 8.*

*Ode 1. Heirmos: Let us send up a song, ye people, unto our marvelous God, Who freed Israel from bondage, singing a song of victory and crying: Let us sing unto Thee, the only Master!*[^922]
*Refrain: Give rest, O Lord, to the souls of Thy departed servants.*

*Troparia:*
Let us all pray unto Christ, making commemoration today of the dead from the ages, that He may deliver from eternal fire those who have fallen asleep in faith and in the hope of everlasting life!

In the depth of Thy judgments, O Christ, Thou hast all-wisely foreordained for each the end of life, the place and manner: therefore, those whom the grave has covered in every land, save at the judgment, O Thou Who didst descend!

O Thou Who gavest the boundaries of our life, do Thou, O Lord, show those who have departed from the night of this life to be sons of the never-setting Day: Orthodox priests, emperors, and all people!

Those whom the water covered, and war mowed down, an earthquake encompassed, and murderers slew, and fire burned up: rank the faithful, O merciful One, in the portion of the Righteous!

Overlooking all our debts in the flesh, O our Savior, of every age of the whole human race, set them uncondemned before Thy judgment seat, as they make answer unto Thee, the Creator!

*Glory... Triadicon:*
I hymn the three Persons of one Essence above all things that exist: the unbegotten Father, the begotten Son, and the Holy Spirit: one unoriginate Kingdom, Dominion, and Godhead!

*Both now... Theotokion:*
Truly thou hast been shown to be a heaven upon earth, greater than the heaven on high, O all-immaculate Virgin: for from thee the Sun shone forth upon the world, purifying it with righteousness!

*Katavasia: "Let us send up a song..."*

*Ode 2. With this Heirmos on 8. If in the Psalter we do not sing this ode, we say the rest. Heirmos: See, see that I am your God, born before the ages of the Father, and in these latter days incarnate without man of the Virgin, and having destroyed the sin of our forefather Adam, as Lover of mankind!*[^923]

*Troparia:*
"See, see that I am your God," Who by righteous judgment of old set the boundaries of life, receiving into incorruption from corruption all who have fallen asleep in the hope of everlasting resurrection!

O Lord, Who from the four corners of the earth receivest those who have fallen asleep in faith: in the sea, or on land, or in rivers, springs, or lakes, or in pits, or those who have become food for beasts, birds, and reptiles: give rest unto all!

In Thine hand, O Lord, Thou hast fore-judged all things, dissolved into four elements: at Thy Coming, having gathered them, raise them all up, forgiving their transgressions done in ignorance and in knowledge!

O fearful second Coming of Thine, O Lord! For when Thou comest as lightning upon earth, Thou shalt raise up all Thy creation to judge: account those who then lived in faith in Thee, who went out to meet Thee, worthy to be with Thee!

*Glory... Triadicon:*
All-perfect, supersubstantial, unoriginate Trinity: unbegotten Father, and only-begotten Son, Spirit proceeding from the Father and made manifest through the Son: one Essence and Nature, Lordship and Kingdom, save us all!

*Both now... Theotokion:*
O ineffable wonder of thy childbearing, O Virgin Mother! How dost thou give birth and yet remain pure? How dost thou bear a child and knowest not man? O wonders, as He knows, Who was born of thee beyond nature in new splendor, God the Word!

*Katavasia: "See, see..."*

*Ode 3. Heirmos: O Word of God, Who with Thine hand didst establish the heavens in the light of Thy true knowledge: establish also the hearts of us that place our hope in Thee!*[^924]

*Troparia:*
Account those who have completed the course of life in pious glory worthy, O God, to be clothed with the crown of the righteous, and to enjoy heavenly blessings!

Those suddenly snatched away, consumed by lightning, frozen by frost, and struck by any wound: give rest, O God, when Thou shalt try all things with fire!

Those who have sailed over the ever-troubled sea of this life, O Christ, account worthy to run unto the incorruptible haven of Thy life, those whose lives were steered in Orthodoxy!

Those whom every creature of the sea, and birds of the air, received as food, O Christ: by Thy judgments raise them up, O God, in the last day with glory!

*Glory... Triadicon:*
Intellectually joining the divine Unity in three Persons simply, together co-eternal and unmingled: for like the speed of lightning, so the Fashioner is seen in Unity!

*Theotokion:*
Thy wonder is incomprehensible: for without man thou givest birth, and keepest thy virginity pure after bearing. Therefore the host of angels and the race of mankind hymn thee unto the ages!

*Katavasia: "O Word of God, Who with Thine hand..." Kontakion of the Temple.*

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 5, Podoben: "The Word co-unoriginate" (Собезнача́льное Сло́во):*[^925]
O Thou Who upon the Cross didst endure the Cross and death, and didst put to death the dominion of Hades, and didst raise the dead: give rest, O Savior, unto the departed, as the God Who loves mankind; and at Thy fearful and Dread Coming, O Life-giver, as Thou possessest a multitude of mercy, account them worthy of Thy Kingdom! *Glory... the same.*

*Both now... Theotokion:*
Show thy speedy protection, help, and mercy upon thy servants, and calm the waves of vain thoughts, O pure one, and raise up my fallen soul, O Mother of God; for I know, yea, I know, O Virgin, that thou canst do whatsoever thou desirest!

*And the Reading.*

*Ode 4. Heirmos: Looking from the mountain overshadowed, O Word, the Prophet foresaw in divine vision that from the alone Mother of God Thou wouldst become incarnate, and with trembling he glorified Thy power!*[^926]

*Troparia:*
Fathers and forefathers, grandfathers and great-grandfathers, from the first even unto the last who died in the lawful covenant and in the right faith: remember them all, O our Savior!

Those who left this life in faith in the mountains, on the path, in desolate places and in deserts—monks and laymen, youths and elders: settle them with the saints, O Christ!

Those who passed away unexpectedly from sorrow and joy, who suffered in prosperity or in adversity: give rest unto them all, O Savior of us all!

Those whom the sword slew, and a horse dashed down, whom hail, snow, and torrential rain overwhelmed, whom a falling brick choked, or earth buried: O Christ our Savior, give them rest!

*Triadicon:*
Wondrously as one and three-radiant, in each Person indivisibly: for the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are worshipped as one God!

*Theotokion:*
Direct us by thy prayers, steering those assailed by the tempest of sin into saving havens, O Mother of God, delivering us from every distress!

*Ode 5. Heirmos: Early in the morning my spirit seeks Thee, O God, for Thy commandments are light at Thy Coming: in them therefore enlighten our mind, O Master, and guide us upon the paths of life!*[^927]

*Troparia:*
Making commemoration today of each of the dead who have lived piously from the ages, O Lord, we earnestly cry unto Thee: Give rest unto all with Thy saints!

Deliver from eternal torment, O Bountiful One, those whom Thou hast received of every generation and race, among emperors and princes, or those who have lived monastic lives in Orthodoxy!

Knowing what is profitable for all whom Thou hast fashioned, O Lord, those whom Thou hast permitted to die unexpectedly by sudden falls: deliver our God from every torment!

From the fire that ever burns, and from dark gloom, from the gnashing of teeth, and the worm that torments without end, and from every punishment: deliver all who have died in faith, O our Savior!

*Glory... Triadicon:*
Co-enthroned, unoriginate Unity, unique division, possessing one nature in three Persons, united in one will for the keeping of the commandments!

*Theotokion:*
More honorable than the fiery-visaged Seraphim hast thou been shown to be, O pure one, having borne the inaccessible Jesus the Savior, Who by incarnation deified the commingling of earthborn mortals!

*Ode 6. Heirmos: Held fast by many transgressions and falling down before Thy compassions, receive me as the Prophet, O Lord, and save me, O Lover of mankind!*[^928]

*Troparia:*
Having loosed the pains of death by Thy suffering, O Prince of Life, our God: give rest unto Thy servants who have fallen asleep from the ages!

By Thine ineffable judgments, those whom magical potions, poisons, and sudden stranglings have slain: give rest with the saints, O Lord!

When Thou shalt judge all things, as they stand naked and uncovered before Thy face: then, O Bountiful One, spare those who have served Thee faithfully, O God!

When Thine Archangel shall sound the last trumpet for the resurrection of life of all: then, O Christ, give rest unto Thy servants!

Those whom Thou hast received in faith from the ages, O God, account worthy of the whole mortal race to glorify Thee unto the ages with those that glorify Thee!

*Glory... Triadicon:*
O Godhead in Trinity, co-enthroned: Father, Son, with the Holy Spirit: Thou art my God, holding all things in Thy sovereign power!

*Both now... Theotokion:*
O Forefather Jesse, leap for joy! From thy root has sprung the flower of life, Christ God, saving the world from the pure Maiden!

*Kontakion, Tone 8. Automelon:*[^929]
Settle in the tabernacles of the elect those who have been translated from among us from temporal things, and give rest with the righteous, O immortal Savior! For if as mortals they have sinned upon earth, yet do Thou, as the sinless Lord, forgive them their voluntary and involuntary transgressions, through the mediation of the Mother of God who bore Thee, that with one accord we may cry aloud over them: Alleluia!

*Ikos:*
Thou alone art immortal, Who didst fashion and make man; for we mortals were formed from the earth, and unto the same earth shall we return, as Thou didst command, Who didst fashion me and say unto me: "Dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return," whither all we mortals go, making our funeral weeping a song: Alleluia!

*Ode 7. Heirmos: In the beginning Who didst lay the foundations of the earth, and didst establish the heavens by Thy word: blessed art Thou unto the ages, O Lord God of our fathers!*[^930]

*Troparia:*
Making commemoration of those who have departed from the ages in piety, we cry aloud: Blessed art Thou unto the ages, O Lord God of our fathers!

Those who have died suddenly in piety, fallen from every precipice, from tree, iron, or any stone: give rest, O God, unto those who have fallen asleep in faith!

At Thy fearful Coming, O Bountiful One, set at the right hand of Thy sheep those who in this life served Thee in Orthodoxy, O Christ, and have passed away unto Thee!

Rank Thy servants in the choir of Thine elect, O Christ, to cry aloud unto Thee: Blessed art Thou unto the ages, O Lord God of our fathers!

O bountiful Savior, Who from the dust of the earth didst fashion flesh and vivify it with the spirit: give rest, O God, in ageless life unto those whom Thou hast taken!

*Glory... Triadicon:*
As by three suns let the Godhead of the one Light in three Hypostases be hymned: Father, and Son, and Holy Spirit: one nature, but three Persons!

*Both now... Theotokion:*
With one accord we sing unto thee the song of David, calling thee the Mountain of God, O Virgin, in which the Word dwelt in the flesh, deifying us spiritually within Himself!

*Ode 8. Heirmos: Him Who was glorified on the holy mountain, and in the bush revealed the mystery of the Ever-Virgin by fire unto Moses: praise the Lord, and supremely exalt Him unto all the ages!*[^931]

*Troparia:*
Having shattered first the shadow of death, shining forth like the Sun from the tomb: make all who have died in faith sons of Thy resurrection, O Lord of glory, unto the ages!

O Witness of secret and hidden things, when Thou shalt reveal the works of darkness and expose our hearts: enter not into judgment with all who have fallen asleep in faith!

When Thou shalt sit upon the Throne, and command Thine elect to appear for judgment from the ends of the earth: then spare all, O Christ, as Thou art merciful!

Unto those who died suddenly by accident, from violent death and swift torrent, suffocation, strangling, and the stomping of horses, O Lord of glory: grant relief unto the ages unto those who have fallen asleep in faith!

*Bless the Father and the Son... Triadicon:*
I hymn Thee as Unity in essence, and I honor Thee as three Persons: Father, Son, and All-Holy Spirit; the unoriginate dominion of Thy Kingdom I glorify unto the ages!

*Theotokion:*
A stream of living water and a sealed fountain hast thou been shown to be, O Virgin Mother of God: for having given birth unto the Lord without man, thou waterest the faithful with the water of immortality unto all the ages!

*We praise, we bless... Heirmos: "Him Who was glorified on the holy mountain..."*

*Ode 9. Heirmos: The childbearing of the Ever-Virgin, manifested of old on the mountain unto the Lawgiver by fire and the bush, for the salvation of us the faithful, we magnify with unceasing hymns!*[^932]

*Troparia:*
Where the dwelling of those that rejoice is, of Thy saints, O Lord: account worthy all who have fallen asleep from the ages in faith and in hope to rejoice!

Those who died by God's rebuke, by deadly thunders of all kinds poured out from heaven, swallowed by the gaping earth, or engulfed by the sea: give rest, O Christ, unto all the faithful!

From poisonous bites of serpents, from swallowing by beasts, from trampling by horses, and from strangling and hanging by one's neighbor: give rest unto those who in faith have served Thee!

Every age: the elder and the infant, the youth and the child, nursing babes, male and female: give rest, O God, unto the faithful whom Thou hast received!

Those who have died in faith from the ages and generation of generations, each by name: account them worthy to stand uncondemned before Thee, O Christ, at Thy Coming!

*Glory...*
O God one in Trinity, glory to Thee unceasingly: for though each is God, yet the Father, the Son, and the Spirit are one in essence by three-radiant properties!

*Both now... Theotokion:*
Thy childbearing is beyond comprehension: for thou didst bear Him Who was before, and ineffably feddest with milk the Nourisher of the world, and heldest Him Who holds all things, Christ our only Redeemer, O all-immaculate one!

*Then: "It is truly meet..."*

*Exapostilarion, Tone 3, Podoben: "He that adorned the heavens with stars" (Не́бо ѕвѣ́здами):*[^933]
Making commemoration of all the departed in Christ, let us, the faithful, remember also that final day, ever beseeching Christ on our own behalf and for them!

*Glory... Another Exapostilarion for the Dead:*
O Thou Who rulest over the living and the dead as God: give rest unto Thy servants, settle Thine elect; for though they sinned, O Word, yet they departed not from Thee!

*Both now... Theotokion:*
Sweetness of angels, joy of the sorrowing, protectress of Christians, O Virgin Mother of the Lord: having interceded for us, deliver us from eternal torment!

*At the Praises, we set 4 Stichera, Tone 8, Podoben: "In Eden of old" (И҆́же въ є҆де́мѣ):*[^934]
O Savior, Who by Thine own hand didst fashion Adam and set the bounds between incorruption and mortality, and by grace didst exchange corruption for life, restoring him unto the first life: do Thou Thyself, O Master, give rest with the righteous in the choir of Thine elect unto Thy servants whom Thou hast translated from us, and having inscribed their names in the Book of Life, raise them up at the voice of the archangel, at the sound of the trumpet, and account them worthy of Thy Heavenly Kingdom! *(Twice).*

O Savior, Who by Thy blood hast redeemed mankind, and by Thy death hast delivered us from bitter death, and by Thy Resurrection hast granted us life everlasting: give rest, O Lord, unto all that have fallen asleep in piety, whether in deserts, or in cities, or in the sea, or on land, or in any place: emperors, priests, hierarchs, monks, and laymen, in every age of the whole human race; and account them worthy of Thy Heavenly Kingdom!

Christ, having risen, loosed the bonds of Adam the first-formed, and destroyed the might of Hades: be of good cheer, all ye dead! Death is put to death, and Hades is led captive with it, and Christ is King, Who was crucified and is risen! He has bestowed upon us incorruption of the flesh; He raises us up, and grants us resurrection, and will account all worthy of that glorious joy who with faith and love have warmly trusted in Him!

*Glory... Tone 6:*
The tasting of the tree of old in Eden became grief unto Adam, when the serpent vomited forth his poison: for through it entered universal death, devouring man. But the Master having come overthrew the serpent, and granted us resurrection: let us therefore cry aloud unto Him: Spare, O Savior, and give rest with the saints unto those whom Thou hast taken, as Lover of mankind!

*Both now... Theotokion:*
By the prayers of her that bore Thee, O Christ, of Thy martyrs and apostles, prophets, hierarchs, venerable and righteous ones, and all the saints: give rest unto Thy departed servants!

*At the Aposticha, from the Octoechos: Stichera by Theophanes, Tone 6, Podoben: "Having laid aside all earthly hope" (Всѐ ᲊпова́нїе):*[^935]
Possessing incomprehensible tender mercy toward us, and an inexhaustible wellspring of divine goodness, O greatly merciful One: settle in the land of the living those who have passed away unto Thee, O Master! In beloved and longed-for dwellings, granting an inheritance that abides forever! For Thou didst shed Thy blood for all, O Christ, and didst redeem the world with a life-bearing price!

*Verse: Blessed are they whom Thou hast chosen and taken, O Lord [LXX Ps. 64:5].*
Having willingly endured life-creating death, Thou didst pour forth life and gavest everlasting sweetness unto the faithful, and divine loving-kindness, O Master, Who alone art all-bountiful and sinless: therein we pray Thee, rank those who have fallen asleep in the hope of the resurrection, granting remission of sins, that Thy Name may be praised above all things, O Christ, and being saved we may glorify Thy love for mankind!

*Verse: And their soul shall dwell among good things [LXX Ps. 24:13].*
Ruling over the living by Thy divine authority, and exercising mastery over the dead, we pray Thee with all our soul, O Christ: do Thou Thyself, O Lover of mankind, give rest unto Thy faithful servants who have departed unto Thee, the only Benefactor, with Thine elect, in places of refreshment, in the splendors of the saints! For Thou art the Desirer of mercy, and savest as God those whom Thou didst create according to Thine image, O Thou Who alone art abundant in mercy!

*Glory... Tone 6:*
Thy creative command was my origin and substance: for wishing to fashion a living being out of the invisible and visible nature, Thou didst form my body from the earth and gavest me a soul by Thy divine and life-creating breathing: therefore, O Savior, give rest unto Thy servants in the land of the living, in the tabernacles of the righteous!

*Both now... Theotokion, Same Tone [Tone 6]:*
Thou art our God, Who in wisdom createst and accomplishest all things: Thou didst send prophets, O Christ, to foretell Thy Coming, and apostles to proclaim Thy marvelous works! And the former indeed foretold Thy Coming, while the latter enlightened the nations through Baptism; and the martyrs suffering received what they longed for; and the choir of both prays unto Thee, together with her that bore Thee: give rest, as God, unto the souls whom Thou hast received, and account us worthy of Thy Kingdom, O our Redeemer and God, Who didst endure the Cross for our condemned works!

*Then: "It is good to give thanks unto the Lord..." The Trisagion. Then the Troparion: "O Thou Who by the depths of Thy wisdom..." Glory... Both now... "In thee we have a wall and a haven..." (see fol. 379)[^936]. And the customary Dismissal.*

*At the Liturgy: The Beatitudes from the Canon, Ode 3 and Ode 6. Prokeimenon, Tone 6: Marvelous is God in His saints, the God of Israel [LXX Ps. 67:36]. Verse: In the congregations bless ye God, the Lord from the springs of Israel [LXX Ps. 67:27]. Another for the Departed: Their soul shall dwell among good things [LXX Ps. 24:13]. The Epistle, in Acts, Pericope 51 [Acts 28:1–31]; and another, Pericope 163 [1 Thess. 4:13–17]. Alleluia, Tone 6: The righteous cried, and the Lord heard them [LXX Ps. 33:18]. *Prologue.*

*Synaxarion for the Sunday of Pentecost:*[^970]

*Verse: By a rushing breath Christ imparts the Spirit;*  
*By a flame and fiery tongues He distributes Him.*  
*Verse: The Holy Spirit appeared in these seven great days;*  
*And was richly given unto the fishermen disciples.*  

On this day, the eighth Sunday after Pascha, we celebrate the Holy Pentecost. This feast also we have received from the Hebrew books; for they celebrate Pentecost among them, honoring the number seven, because fifty days after Passover having passed, they received the Law. So also we, celebrating fifty days after Pascha, receive the all-holy Spirit, Who lays down the law and guides into all truth, and commands what is well-pleasing unto God.

Be it known that among the Hebrews there were three feasts: Passover, Pentecost, and Tabernacles (that is, the honoring of booths). Passover indeed they kept in remembrance of the passage through the Red Sea; for *Pascha* is interpreted *Passage*. Such a feast was a type of our passage from the darkness of sin back unto Paradise, and our restoration. And Pentecost they celebrated in remembrance of their affliction in the wilderness, and how through many sorrows they were brought into the Promised Land; for then they enjoyed the fruit of wheat and wine: and this feast showed our affliction from unbelief and our entrance into the Church, for then we also partake of the Master's Body and Blood. Some indeed say that for this cause Pentecost was celebrated among the Hebrews; but others say it was because of the fifty days wherein Moses, having fasted, received the God-written Law, together commemorating the sacrifice of the calf and other things which Moses did upon going up and coming down the mountain. Unto others it was thought that Pentecost was devised among the Hebrews for the honor of the number seven, as was said; for that number, being multiplied by itself, makes fifty, wanting one day. And the honor of the sevenfold number among the Hebrews is not only in days, but also reaches unto years, whereof is the Jubilee, which they celebrate, interpreted *Remission*; for this happens in the forty-ninth year, when they leave the land unsown, and grant rest unto animals, and command purchased slaves to go free. And the third feast, Tabernacles (that is, the honoring of booths), was celebrated after the gathering of fruits, which is five months after the feast of Passover: this was celebrated in memory of the day wherein Moses first pitched the booth seen on Mount Sinai, prepared by the cloud, and fashioned by Bezaleel. And making booths, they kept this feast, abiding in the fields, thanking God as they gathered the fruits of their labors.

David also seems to have written concerning the winepresses in the Psalms: and that feast was a type of our resurrection from the dead, when our bodily tabernacles having been dissolved and fashioned anew, we shall receive the fruits of our labors, celebrating in everlasting tabernacles! And it is meet to know that on that day when Pentecost was fulfilled, the Holy Spirit came upon the disciples: and the holy Fathers themselves determined to separate the feasts, because of the majesty of the all-holy and life-creating Spirit, seeing that He is One of the holy and life-originating Trinity; therefore we also shall declare on the morrow how the Holy Spirit came. Through the prayers of the holy apostles, O Christ God, have mercy upon us, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

There is no death, O Lord, for Thy servants when we depart from the body and come unto Thee our God, but a translation from things most sorrowful unto things most blessed and joyful, and rest and joy: and if we have sinned in anything against Thee, be merciful unto us and unto them: for none is pure from stain before Thee, even though his life be but one day, save Thou alone, our Lord Jesus Christ, Who didst appear sinless upon the earth: through Whom we all hope to obtain mercy and the remission of sins: therefore unto us and unto them, as Thou art good and the Lover of mankind, remit, pardon, and forgive our offenses, voluntary and involuntary,

*Synaxarion on Monday after Pentecost, of the Holy Spirit:*[^1019]

*Verses:*  
*{ For every breath praises Christ;*  
*{ The Spirit, by Whom every hostile spirit is expelled.*  

On this day, Monday of Pentecost, we celebrate the All-Holy, Life-Creating, and All-Powerful Spirit Himself, one of the Trinity, God, equal in honor, consubstantial, and equal in glory with the Father and the Son. For on this fiftieth day He descended substantially upon the holy Apostles, having appeared in the form of fiery tongues, and sat upon each one of them in the Upper Room where they were abiding. And out of reverence unto the All-Holy Spirit, the holy and divine Fathers, having well ordered all things, divided the feast and decreed that this day also should be celebrated as Pentecost: for the Savior, before His holy Passion, promised the coming of the Comforter, saying:[^1020] *It is expedient for you that I go away; for if I go not away, the Comforter will not come.* And again: *When He, the Spirit of truth, is come, He will teach you and guide you into all truth.* And again: *I will pray the Father, and He will send you another Comforter, the Spirit of truth, Who proceeds from the Father.* And after the Passion again, when He was ascending into the heavens, He said: *Tarry ye in Jerusalem, until ye be endued with power from on high.*

He sends Him, when the days of Pentecost were being fulfilled, as they were abiding together. At the third hour of the day in the Upper Room, suddenly there was a sound from heaven as of thunder, so that the multitude of the inhabited earth ran together; and the Holy Spirit appeared in the likeness of fiery tongues upon each one of them, not only unto the Twelve, but also unto the Seventy; and they spoke in strange tongues, that is to say, each apostle spoke the tongues of all tribes—not that the apostle spoke his own tongue and the foreigner understood, but the apostle heard and spoke the tongue of each nation. And some among the gathered peoples thought they were drunk; for each did not comprehend how an apostle spoke especially unto all, and they thought this was drunkenness. But others wondered, saying: *What does this mean?* For these were gathered from all the earth because of the feast: Parthians, Medes, and Elamites, who had a short time before been taken captive by Antiochus.

Ten days after the Ascension having passed, the All-Holy Spirit comes, and not immediately then after the Ascension, in order that He might make the disciples who awaited Him even more fervent.[^1021] Some also say this: that on each day, each one of the nine angelic ranks bowed down before the coming of that deified Flesh: nine days therefore having been fulfilled, on the tenth day the Comforter came. We are transformed by sleep; and fifty days after Pascha He comes in commemoration of the Old Law: for when Israel crossed the Red Sea, fifty days having passed, they received the Ten Commandments. Behold also the signs: there a Mountain, here an Upper Room; there fire, and here fiery tongues; and instead of thunder and darkness, an exceedingly violent wind. And in the appearance of tongues the Holy Spirit descended, showing that He hath a kindred nature with the living Word; or also because the apostles were about to teach and bring the nations into the tongues; and in fiery tongues, because God is a consuming fire, and for the sake of purification; and divided, because of the gifts that were to be distributed. And as He once divided and confused into many the single tongue of those of old, so now also in those who knew one tongue He divided it into many, that He might gather together from all nations those who had been dispersed unto the ends of the inhabited earth. And it takes place at the Feast, that, a greater multitude of people being gathered, the report of the wonder might go forth through all the inhabited earth, so that even those who happened to be at Pascha and had seen the miracles wrought by Christ might now again be filled with wonder. On the fiftieth day it was fitting...

...it was fitting that in the very time when of old the Law was given, in that same time also the grace of the Holy Spirit should be poured out: as Christ also did, when at the legal Passover He accomplished His own Passover, the true Passover.

And the Holy Spirit sat not upon the mouths, but upon the heads of the apostles, embracing that which is divine, and the highest part of the body, the mind itself, from which also the tongue hath its power of speech; or as a certain image, the Spirit uttering a sound by tongues, as if to ordain the apostles to be teachers of the whole world under heaven: for ordination (that is, cheirotonia) takes place upon the heads. The sound and the fire occurred because these took place also at Sinai: that the same Spirit might be shown to be both then and now, enacting the Law and ordering all things. And the multitude of the people was troubled by the rushing sound of the wind, because they thought it was the end of the world, as Christ foreshowed unto the Judeans concerning their destruction. And He said "as of fire," lest anyone should think anything bodily concerning the Holy Spirit: for they accused the apostles of drunkenness; but Peter, standing up and speaking forth in the midst of the people, refuted them, showing that what was said by them was not adhering to the truth: and he brought forward the prophecy of Joel as witness, and brought from among them unto divine knowledge about three thousand souls.

And the Holy Spirit is called Comforter because He is able to comfort and give rest unto us: for instead of Christ we have received Him, and in Him we have Christ: for He also intercedes for us with unutterable groanings unto God, as our Lover of mankind and Advocate, as is Christ also: for He too is a Comforter. On this account the Holy Spirit is called "another Comforter": for the Apostle says: *We have an Advocate [Comforter], Jesus, with God.* And "another" was said on account of consubstantiality: for to say "another and another" (*allos kai allos*) is of identical substance and nature, whereas "another and another [thing]" (*heteron kai heteron*) we know is said of different natures. This Holy Spirit is consubstantial with the Father and the Son in all things; therefore together with Them He creates all things, and that very resurrection which is to be, and whatsoever He wills He does: He sanctifies, He divides, He fashions anew, He sends forth, He makes wise, He anoints the Prophets; and to speak simply, in all things He is self-governing, all-powerful, good, upright, sovereign: by Him all things receive life and motion, whatsoever is partaker of holiness and of any life: simply, He hath all things that the Father and the Son have, except unbegottenness and begottenness, proceeding from the Father alone.

The Holy Spirit having been poured out upon all flesh, the whole world was filled with all manner of graces; and thereby all nations were guided unto divine knowledge, and every disease and every plague was driven away. And thrice by Christ was the Holy Spirit given unto the disciples: before the Passion obscurely; after the Resurrection by breathing, more effectually; and now He hath sent Him down substantially: or rather, He Himself descended, perfectly enlightening and sanctifying them, and through them bringing the ends of the inhabited earth unto God. By the visitation of His holy coming, through the prayers of the holy Apostles, O Christ our God, have mercy on us, both now and ever and unto ages of ages. Amen.

## First Sunday after Pentecost: Sunday of All Saints

*On Saturday evening, we celebrate the customary All-Night Vigil.*  
*At Small Vespers: in the Octoechos, for 4: one sticheron, twice. Glory... Both now... Dogmatikon, Tone 8:*[^1041]  
How shall we bless thee, O Mother of God? How shall we praise, O all-blessed one, the incomprehensible mystery of thy childbearing? For every Maker and Fashioner of our nature, having had compassion on His own image, brought Himself down into ineffable emptying: the Son, Who is in the immaterial bosom of the Father, took up His abode in thy womb, O pure one, and unchangeably became flesh from thee who knewest not wedlock, remaining what He was by nature, God. Therefore, we worship Him as perfect God and perfect man, the same in both forms; for both natures truly exist in Him, and we proclaim all His natural properties according to His double essence, honoring two operations and two wills: for being of one essence with God the Father, He sovereignly wills and operates as God; and being also of one essence with us, He sovereignly wills and operates as man. Entreat Him, O pure and all-blessed one, that our souls may be saved!

*At the Aposticha: according to custom, to the Mother of God, with their verses.*

*AT GREAT VESPERS.*  
*After the customary opening: Psalm 103. Kathisma 1: Blessed is the man. At "Lord, I have cried," we set 10 verses, and we sing: 3 Resurrection Stichera, 3 Anatolian Stichera, and 4 Stichera of the Saints.*[^1042]

*Resurrection Stichera, Tone 8:*  
Evening hymn and spiritual service we offer unto Thee, O Christ, for Thou didst deign to have mercy upon us through Thy Resurrection!

O Lord, O Lord, cast us not away from Thy presence, but deign to have mercy upon us through Thy Resurrection!

Rejoice, O holy Zion, Mother of the Churches, the dwelling-place of God: for thou didst first receive remission of sins through the Resurrection!

*Anatolian Stichera:*  
The Word born of God the Father before the ages, in these latter times, by His own will took flesh from her who knew not wedlock, and endured crucifixion unto death; and through His Resurrection, O Savior, He hath raised up man who had been slain of old!

We glorify Thy Resurrection from the dead, O Christ, whereby Thou hast freed the race of Adam from the tyranny of Hades, and as God hast granted unto the world life eternal and great mercy!

Glory to Thee, O Christ Savior, only-begotten Son of God, Who wast nailed upon the Cross, and didst rise from the tomb on the third day!

*Stichera to All Saints, Tone 6, Podoben: "All the hope" (Все ᲊпова́нїе):*  
The spiritual orators, the disciples of the Savior, having become instruments through faith, scattered throughout the ends of the earth the venerable preaching, sowing right belief; and by divine husbandry and grace they sprouted the nations, forming hosts of martyrs wondrously depicting Christ's passions on account of many kinds of diverse wounds and fire; and with boldness they pray for our souls! *(Twice)*

Kindled by the fire of the love of the Lord, they despised fire; and as divine coals ignited in Christ, the ranks of the martyrs burned up the audacious insolence of deceit; and they stopped the mouths of wild beasts by divine invocations; and being beheaded, they struck down all the armies of the enemy, and patiently pouring forth streams of blood, they watered the Church that is enlightened through faith!

Throughout all the ends of the earth, let us worthily praise with sacred hymns those who suffered faithfully: the apostles, the martyrs, the God-wise hierarchs, and the sacred assembly of holy women; for earthly beings were united unto heavenly ones, and through sufferings they received freedom from passion through the grace of Christ; and now, shining as bright stars upon us, with boldness they pray for our souls!

*Glory... Tone 6:*  
O divine choir of martyrs, foundation of the Church, fulfillment of the Gospel: ye have in deed fulfilled the sayings of the Savior! For in you the gates of Hades that were opened against the Church have been shut; the shedding of your blood hath dried up the sacrifices of idols; your slaughter hath brought forth the full assembly of the faithful! Ye have astonished the bodiless powers, standing before God with divine manifestations: entreat Him unceasingly for our souls!

*Both now... Theotokion, Tone 8:*  
The King of heaven through His love for mankind... (see in the Octoechos, Saturday, on fol. 198).

*Then, the Entrance. O Gladsome Light. The Prokeimenon of the day.*

*Reading from the Prophecy of Isaiah (Chapter 43:9–14):*[^1043]  
Thus saith the Lord: All the nations were gathered together, and princes shall be gathered out of them. Who will announce these things, or who will declare to us the things from the beginning? Let them bring forth their witnesses, and let them be justified, and let them hear, and let them speak truth. Be ye My witnesses, and I too am a witness, saith the Lord God, and the servant whom I have chosen, that ye may know and understand that I am He: before Me there was no other God, neither shall there be any after Me. I am God, and beside Me there is no savior. I have declared, and have saved, and have reproached, and there was no strange god among us. Ye are My witnesses, and I am the Lord God. Even from the beginning, and there is none that can deliver out of My hands: I will work, and who shall turn it back? Thus saith the Lord God, the Holy One of Israel Who delivers you.

*Reading from the Wisdom of Solomon (Chapter 3:1–9):*[^1044]  
The souls of the righteous are in the hand of God, and no torment shall touch them. In the eyes of the foolish they seemed to die, and their departure was taken for misery, and their going from us to be utter destruction; but they are in peace. For though they be punished in the sight of men, yet is their hope full of immortality. And having been a little chastised, they shall be greatly rewarded; for God proved them, and found them worthy of Himself. As gold in the furnace hath He tried them, and received them as a burnt offering of sacrifice. And in the time of their visitation they shall shine, and run to and fro like sparks among the stubble. They shall judge nations, and have dominion over peoples, and the Lord shall reign over them unto the ages. They that put their trust in Him shall understand the truth, and such as be faithful in love shall abide with Him; for grace and mercy are in His saints, and visitation among His elect.

*Reading from the Wisdom of Solomon (Chapter 5:15–6:3):*[^1045]  
The righteous live unto the ages, and their reward is in the Lord, and the care of them is with the Most High. Therefore shall they receive a kingdom of glory, and a crown of beauty from the hand of the Lord; for with His right hand shall He cover them, and with His arm shall He protect them. He shall take to Him His jealousy for complete armor, and make the creation His weapon for the vengeance of His enemies. He shall put on righteousness as a breastplate, and true judgment instead of a helmet. He shall take holiness for an invincible shield. His severe wrath shall He sharpen for a sword, and the world shall fight with Him against the unwise. Then shall the right-aiming thunderbolts go abroad, and from the clouds, as from a well-drawn bow, shall they fly to the mark. And hailstones full of wrath shall be cast as out of a stone bow, and the water of the sea shall rage against them, and the floods shall cruelly drown them. A mighty wind shall stand up against them, and like a storm shall blow them away: thus iniquity shall lay waste the whole earth, and ill dealing shall overthrow the thrones of the mighty. Hear therefore, O ye kings, and understand; learn, ye that be judges of the ends of the earth. Give ear, ye that rule the multitude, and glory in the nations! For power is given you of the Lord, and sovereignty from the Most High!

*At the Litiya: the Sticheron of the saint of the temple, and the Idiomela of All Saints.*  
*If, however, for some very grave and unavoidable reason the All-Night Vigil is not celebrated, there are sung at "Lord, I have cried": 3 Resurrection Stichera, 4 Anatolian Stichera, and 6 to All Saints, Podoben: "All the hope": 4 stichera; "The spiritual orators": 2 stichera; "Kindled by the fire": 1 sticheron; "Throughout all the ends": and after this, those written for the Litiya: Tone 4, Idiomelon: "In the harmony of faith": 2 stichera; "Come, all ye faithful": 3 stichera; Tone 2: "Come, let us all spiritually rejoice": and the rest is sung at the Litiya.*[^1046]

*At the Litiya, Idiomelon Stichera, Tone 4:*  
In the harmony of faith, let us spiritually celebrate the earthly feast of those who have pleased God from the beginning: the venerable choir of the Patriarchs and Prophets, the adornment of the Apostles, the assembly of the Martyrs, the boast of the Ascetics, the memory of all the Saints; for they pray unceasingly that peace may be granted unto the world, and unto our souls great mercy!

Come, all ye faithful, in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs let us praise the all-glorious memory of all the saints: the Forerunner of the Savior, the Apostles, Prophets, and Martyrs, Hierarchs, Teachers, the Venerable, Ascetics, and Righteous, and the godly assembly of holy women; worthily blessing them, with one accord let us cry aloud: O all-good Christ our God, by their prayers grant peace unto Thy churches, victories over enemies unto our Christ-loving rulers, and unto our souls great mercy!

*Tone 2:*  
Come, all ye people, let us spiritually rejoice in the memory of the Saints: for behold, it hath arrived, bringing unto us wealth-producing gifts! Therefore, with the voice of gladness and a pure conscience, let us cry out, saying: Rejoice, choir of the Prophets, who didst announce the coming of Christ unto the world, and didst make present from afar things that were to come as though near at hand! Rejoice, choir of the Apostles, fishers of the nations and catchers of men! Rejoice, assembly of the Martyrs, gathered from the ends of the earth into one faith, who didst endure the torment of tortures, and didst perfectly receive the crown of suffering! Rejoice, leaders of the Fathers, who didst waste away your bodies through fasting, and having mortified the passions of the flesh, didst wing your minds with divine desire and soar up to the heavens, and dwelling with the angels, do receive eternal good things! But O Prophets, Apostles, and Martyrs with the Ascetics, earnestly pray unto Him Who hath crowned you all, that they who faithfully and lovingly celebrate your honorable memory may be delivered from visible and invisible enemies!

*Glory... Tone 6:*  
Let us assemble today, ye faithful, for a spiritual table is set before us, and a mystical cup filled with sweet meats, the gladness of the virtues of the Martyrs! For these patient ones from the ends of the earth, having surrendered their bodies to barbarian wounds, offered unto God a rational sacrifice: some being beheaded, others having their backs scraped, others having their hands and all their members cut off, all together became partakers of Christ's suffering; but O Lord Who didst grant them crowns in recompense for their wounds, account us worthy to live according to their footsteps, as Thou art the Lover of mankind!

*Both now... Theotokion, the same Tone:*

Thou art the temple and the gate, the palace and the throne... (see on fol. 134).

*At the Aposticha, Resurrection Stichera, Tone 8.*  
*Other Anatolian Stichera.*  
*Alphabetical. Verse: The Lord is King, He is robed in majesty.*  
Thou didst ascend the Cross, O Jesus, Who camest down from heaven; Thou camest unto death, O immortal Life, the true Light unto those sitting in darkness, the Resurrection unto all that had fallen: our Illumination and Savior, glory to Thee!

*Verse: For He hath established the world, which shall not be moved.*  
We glorify Christ Who rose from the dead: for having assumed soul and body, He separated one from the other by His passion; while His all-pure soul descended into Hades, which He despoiled, His holy body saw not corruption in the tomb: the Redeemer of our souls!

*Verse: Holiness becomes Thine house, O Lord, unto length of days.*  
In psalms and hymns we glorify Thy Resurrection from the dead, O Christ, whereby Thou hast delivered us from the tyranny of Hades, and as God hast granted life eternal and great mercy!

O incomprehensible Master of all, Creator of heaven and earth, through the passion of the Cross Thou hast poured forth freedom from passion upon me; and having received burial and risen in glory, Thou didst raise up Adam with Thine all-powerful hand: Glory to Thy third-day rising, whereby Thou hast granted eternal life and cleansing of sins, as Thou alone art merciful!

*Glory... Tone 6:*  
Come, ye faithful, having assembled a choir today, let us celebrate piously; and the all-glorious and honorable memory of all the saints let us gloriously honor, saying: Rejoice, glorious apostles, prophets, martyrs, and hierarchs! Rejoice, assembly of the venerable and righteous! Rejoice, choir of holy women, and pray to Christ for the world, that He grant victories over barbarians unto our sovereign, and unto our souls great mercy!

*Both now... Theotokion:*  
My Maker and Redeemer... (see on fol. 25).

*Then, after the Trisagion: Rejoice, O Virgin Mother of God... (twice), and of the Saints (once): In all the world... Blessing of the loaves. Reading from the General Epistle of James.*

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*  
O Good One Who for our sake wast born of a Virgin, and didst endure crucifixion, despoiling death by death, and revealing the Resurrection as God: despise not those whom Thou hast fashioned with Thine own hand; manifest Thy love for mankind, O Merciful One; accept the Mother of God who bare Thee, interceding for us; and save, O our Savior, a despairing people!

*And a reading in the Commentary Gospel. After the Evlogitaria, the Troparia: The angelic assembly... and after them, the Ypakoi, Tone 8:*

*Gradual Antiphons, Tone 8. Antiphon 1:*  
From my youth the enemy tempts me and burns me with pleasures; but trusting in Thee, O Lord, I vanquish him!

*Antiphon 3:*

Through the Holy Spirit every divine man sees and foretells; he works lofty wonders, singing one God in three Persons; for though He shines in three lights, yet the Godhead holds supreme rule!

*Antiphon 4:*

*Synaxarion for the Sunday of All Saints.*[^1053]
*Verses:*
All the friends of my Lord do I extol;
In heaven with them do I desire to be.

*On the same day, the First Sunday after Pentecost, we celebrate the Feast of All Saints who are everywhere throughout the whole inhabited world, in Asia, Libya, and Europe, in the North and in the South. For after the coming of the All-Holy Spirit, the divine Fathers established that we should celebrate this feast, as if displaying an image: that the coming of the All-Holy Spirit accomplished such great things through the Apostles, sanctifying and making wise from among our clay, to fill up that fallen Angelic Rank, establishing them and bringing them unto God through Christ—some indeed by martyrdom and blood, and others by a virtuous life and conversation—and performing deeds surpassing nature. For the Holy Spirit descends in the appearance of fire, having an upward-tending nature; while our clay, which by nature tends downward, ascends unto the heights—our human clay which was previously assumed and deified by God the Word, the flesh which was taken up and seated at the right hand of the Father of Glory. And now He draws all who are willing, as the promise of God the Word shows the accomplishment of the transformation and a certain intended goal: that His bodily coming and dispensation toward us draws into unity and divine love those who of old were alienated, removing godless peoples, and offering unto God the first-fruits of nature as it were, through those who in various ways were well-pleasing unto Him. In this first manner, therefore, we celebrate the feast of all the saints. And in the second manner: because many were well-pleasing unto God through extreme virtue, and yet remained nameless and unknown among men, hidden from human knowledge, but nevertheless possess great glory before God; or because many lived according to Christ in the Indies, among the Egyptians, and the Arabs, in Mesopotamia, and Phrygia, and those beyond the Great Sea, and furthermore in the whole West, even unto the Black Sea and the British Isles themselves—and simply to say, in the East and in the West—and it was not possible to honor them properly one by one because of their vast number, as ecclesiastical custom hath received. That we may therefore receive help from all of them, wherever anyone was well-pleasing unto God on earth, the divine Fathers established that the Feast of All Saints be celebrated—both of all those who were before and of those who were after, both revealed and unrevealed—since the one Holy Spirit dwelt in them, honoring them all collectively. Or in the third manner: it was fitting to gather the saints celebrated on each day...*

*...and to gather the saints celebrated on each day into one day, that it might be made manifest that they all strove for the sake of the one Christ, and all ran the race of the same virtue; and thus all, as servants of the one God, were worthily crowned, establishing this Church, filling the heavenly world, and urging us also to engage in an equal contest with them, diverse and manifold though it be, striving with all zeal according to the power each possesses. In honor of all these saints from the ages, the ever-memorable Emperor Leo the Wise built a great and most magnificent church, near the Church of the Holy Apostles within the city of Constantine.[^1054] At first, as they say, he was building it for Empress Theophano his wife, who had been completely well-pleasing unto God; and it was a most glorious church in the midst of tiles and within the churches. But the Church did not allow the fulfillment of this intention, submitting rather to common counsel: for they deemed it inappropriate that a church should immediately receive such an honor, and be dedicated as a church of such surpassing beauty and distinction, for one who only yesterday and before three days had reposed, even though she had been well-pleasing unto God. The wise Emperor therefore acquiesced in this, and dedicated the church erected for the whole Church unto all the saints throughout all the earth, saying: 'If Theophano also be holy, let her be numbered together with all these!' And I deem that from that time above all, the celebration of the present feast had its origin, having not existed previously. For this reason it is placed at the end of the Triodion, as if enclosing all the feasts with a fence: for ecclesiastical good order and ritual, though begun from above, was gradually established in greater perfection, as was fitting; but in the days of this Emperor it was brought unto perfection and assumed the form that its orders and ritual now possess. The Triodion, to speak in brief, carefully contains within itself a continuous account of all that God hath done for us in ineffable words: the devil's fall from on high, the first parable of Adam's expulsion and transgression, the entire dispensation of God the Word for our sake, and how by the Holy Spirit we were brought back up unto heaven and filled up that fallen rank, which is made known through all the saints. And let it be known that today we celebrate all those whom the Holy Spirit hath sanctified by grace: I mean the surpassing divine minds, the nine manifest angelic orders—namely, Cherubim, Seraphim, Thrones, Dominions, Powers, Authorities, Principalities, Archangels, Angels—Forefathers and Patriarchs, Prophets, Holy Apostles, Hieromartyrs and Venerable Martyrs, the Venerable and the Righteous, and the choirs of all holy Women, and all other nameless saints. And with them, above all and in all and with all the saints, let our Most Pure Lady and Sovereign, incomparably higher than all the Angelic Ranks, the Mother of God, Mary Ever-Virgin, be commemorated! By whose prayers, O Christ God, and those of all Thy saints who have been from the ages, have mercy on us and save us, as Thou alone art good and the Lover of mankind, now and ever and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

Having descended unto Thy servants as the Most High, having become man by nature, Thou didst call the Father Thy God according to divine essence; and having risen from the tomb by grace, having made the Father of the earthborn His Father by nature, with Him do we all magnify Thee as God and Master!

*Both now... Theotokion:*
Thou hast been revealed, O Virgin Mother of God, having borne in the flesh beyond nature the good Word Whom the Father uttered from His heart before all ages, as He is good; Whom we now understand to be higher than bodily forms, even though He clothed Himself in a body!

*Second Canon, Ode 9. Heirmos: Be struck with awe:*
*Troparion:*
Beholding the Son of God by nature, Who was conceived in thy womb, O Mother of God, and became man for our sake, suffering upon the Cross according to human nature, yet remaining impassible as God, we magnify Thee!

*Troparion:*
Grievous darkness was destroyed from Hades; for Thou, O Christ, Sun of Righteousness, didst shine forth, enlightening all the ends of the earth with the rays of Thy Godhead: heavenly Man, earthly God, Whom we magnify in two natures!

*Third Canon, Ode 9. Heirmos: Truly Mother of God:*
*Troparion:*
Full of joy and gladness is thy memory, pouring forth healings for those who come, who piously proclaim thee as Mother of God!

*Troparion:*
With hymns do we extol thee, O full of grace, and unceasingly do we cry aloud unto thee the 'Rejoice': for thou hast poured forth joy unto all!

*Fourth Canon (All Saints), Ode 9. Heirmos: Heaven was amazed at this:*

*Troparion:*
Armed with hope and love and faithfully fenced about is the company of the elect; for rejoicing they endured all the assaults of the tyrants, the torments and the wounds, gloriously enriched by Christ, triumphing in their struggles as true champions!

*Troparion:*
Let the haven of salvation, the Cross, Apostles, Prophets, and Martyrs with Ascetics, divine Teachers, and Priests, the company of Patriarchs, and glorious Hieromartyrs, God-adorned women, the Venerable and the Righteous, be worthily praised today! *(Twice)*

*Glory...*
*Troparion:*
Breathing of immaterial fire and ceaselessly rejoicing in sweetness, the glorious assemblies of the saints in heaven, radiant, stand before the Throne eternally; for mirrors have been done away, and shadows have passed away through true revelations!

*Both now... Theotokion:*
Thou hast appeared as she that units those that were afar off; for through thee men have truly become fellow-citizens of the angels in heaven, and the ranks of all the saints bear witness, singing now with them thy childbearing, O Virgin Mother of God, in eternal songs!

*Katavasia: Let every earthborn mortal:*
*Holy is the Lord our God: Chant according to the tone.*
*Then, Resurrectional Exapostilarion (Eothinon 1):*
Let us go up with the disciples into the mountain of Galilee, in faith to behold Christ, Who hath received authority over things above and things below; let us learn how He teaches to baptize all nations in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, and to abide with His disciples, as He promised, unto the end of the age![^1058]

*Exapostilarion of All Saints. Tone 2, Podoben: "Give ear, ye women" (Жены̀ ᲊслы́шите):*
*Glory...*
Let the many-named multitude of the saints be praised today: Patriarchs and Apostles, Righteous Ones with Prophets, Martyrs who suffered in the Law and in Grace, and assemblies of Women: that through their prayers we may obtain remission of our sins!

*Both now... Theotokion:*
O Mary most pure, Golden Censer, who didst become the receptacle of the indivisible Trinity: in whom the Father was well pleased, the Son took up His abode, and the Holy Spirit overshadowed thee, O Maiden, and revealed thee as Mother of God!

*At the Praises, "Let every breath praise the Lord." We set 8 stichoi, and chant the Resurrection stichera in Tone 8:*[^1059]
*Tone 8. Sticheron 1:*
O Lord, though Thou didst stand before the tribunal of Pilate to be judged, yet Thou didst not depart from Thy throne, seated together with the Father; and having risen from the dead, Thou didst deliver the world from bondage to the alien one, as Thou art compassionate and the Lover of mankind!

*Sticheron 2:*
O Lord, Thou hast given us Thy Cross as a weapon against the devil; for he trembles and shakes, unable to look upon its power, because it raises the dead and hath abolished death: therefore do we worship Thy burial and Thy Resurrection!

*Sticheron 3:*
O Lord, though the Jews laid Thee in the tomb as one dead, yet soldiers guarded Thee as a sleeping King, and sealed Thee with a seal as a treasury of life; but Thou didst rise and hast granted incorruption unto our souls!

*Sticheron 4:*
Thine Angel, O Lord, who proclaimed the Resurrection, struck fear into the watch, but called aloud unto the women, saying: Why seek ye the Living among the dead? Being God, He hath risen, and hath granted life unto the universe!

*Another Sticheron, Anatolian, the same Tone:*
Thou didst suffer through the Cross, impassible in Thy Godhead; Thou didst accept three days' burial, that Thou mightest free us from the bondage of the enemy, and having made us immortal, mightest quicken us, O Christ God, for the sake of Thy Resurrection, O Lover of mankind!

*Other Stichera for All Saints. Tone 4, Podoben: "As one valiant among martyrs" (Я҆́кѡ до́бля):*
*Sticheron 1:*
The Lord hath made His saints marvelous on the earth; for they received His wounds and sufferings in the flesh, adorning themselves with them and visibly embellished with divine beauties: whom let us hymn as unfading flowers, as unerring stars of the Church, as voluntary sacrifices!

*Stichos: The righteous cried, and the Lord gave ear unto them. [Ps. 33:18 LXX]*
*Sticheron 2:*
With the Prophets, Apostles, and Venerable Teachers, with the Hieromartyrs, let all the Righteous and the suffering Women who attained through love, the multitude of the saints and the ranks of the Venerable, be praised with sacred songs, as inheritors of the kingdom on high, as holy dwellers in Paradise!

*Stichos: God is marvelous in His saints. [Ps. 67:36 LXX]*
*Sticheron 3:*
Those who adorned the earth with the brightness of virtues, who imitated the death of Christ, who walked the mediating path of immortality, who through the guidance of the Spirit cleanse the passions of men, who suffered valiantly with one soul throughout all the world: let the Martyrs be praised!

*Glory...*
*First Gospel Sticheron (Eothinon 1), Tone 1:*[^1060]
When the disciples were going up into the mountain for the earthly ascension, the Lord stood before them; and having worshiped Him, and being instructed concerning the authority given Him everywhere, they were sent under heaven to proclaim the Resurrection from the dead and the Ascension into heaven: with whom Christ God, the Savior of our souls, promised without deceit to abide!

*Both now... Most blessed art thou, O Virgin Mother of God:*
*The Great Doxology. After the Trisagion, the Resurrection Troparion: Today salvation is come unto the world. Litanies and Dismissal. And the First Hour in the Narthex, and the Catechesis of St. Theodore the Studite, and the final Dismissal.*

*At the Divine Liturgy:*
*The Beatitudes, Resurrectional, Tone 8, on 6:*
Remember us, O Christ, Savior of the world, even as Thou didst remember the thief upon the tree; and count all worthy, O only compassionate One, of Thy heavenly Kingdom!

Hear, O Adam, and rejoice with Eve; for he that of old stripped both of you naked and took you captive through deceit hath been abolished by the Cross of Christ!

Nailed upon the tree of Thine own will, O our Savior, Thou hast delivered Adam from the curse through the tree, restoring, as Thou art compassionate, the divine image and the dwelling in Paradise!

Even as the multitude of angels in heaven, so also the races of men on earth celebrate the most holy Resurrection of Thy goodness, O Lord!

*Triadikon:*
O unoriginate Trinity, undivided Essence, co-enthroned Unity, co-equal Glory, pre-eternal Nature and Kingdom: save those who hymn Thee in faith!

*Theotokion:*
Rejoice for our sake, O holy Mother of God; rejoice, thou that didst bear joy for the world! Rejoice, only protectress of mankind and salvation of our souls!

*And to the Saints, from the Canon, Ode 6, on 4.*
*Prokeimenon of the Epistle, Tone 4: God is marvelous in His saints, the God of Israel. [Ps. 67:36 LXX]*
*Stichos: In congregations bless ye God the Lord. [Ps. 67:27 LXX]*

*Stichos: Many are the afflictions of the righteous, and the Lord will deliver them out of them all. [Ps. 33:20 LXX]*

*At the Trapeza: Consolation for the brethren as they prepare for the course of the Fast.*
*The End, and unto God be glory. Amen.*[^1062]

## From the Typikon of the Holy Mountain of Athos: Beginning of the Holy Fast of the Holy Apostles[^1063]

*On the Monday after the Memory of All Saints. Likewise also the Fast of the Nativity of Christ, and the Dormition of the Most Holy Mother of God, on whichever day they happen to occur:*
If there be no saint celebrated with a feast, we chant *Alleluia* in the Tone of the Oktoechos, and the Triadica in the Tone, making prostrations according to the Order of the Great Fast; on Wednesday and Friday we likewise make prostrations and fast, and eat once a day; and on Mondays we fast until the Ninth Hour, and eat once. On Tuesdays and Thursdays we do not eat fish, but only wine and olive oil. On Friday, Wednesday, and Friday, neither oil nor wine, but we eat once on those days. But if a saint fall on a Tuesday or on a Thursday having a Polyeleos, we eat fish. If it be on a Monday, we eat oil and drink wine. If it be on a Wednesday or on a Friday, we eat twice and drink wine. But if there be a Vigil for the saint on a Wednesday or on a Friday, we eat oil and drink wine; but if on a Monday, we eat fish and drink wine. If these feasts happen to fall: the Nativity of the Most Holy Mother of God, the Entry, the Meeting, and the Dormition, or the Transfiguration of the Lord; likewise the two feasts of the Forerunner, the Nativity and the Beheading, and the Holy Apostles Peter and Paul, and the Falling Asleep of John the Theologian, or the memory of the saint to whom the temple and monastery are dedicated: on Wednesday or on Friday we eat fish. But on the Exaltation of the Precious Cross let none dare touch fish to eat, nor oil, but we drink wine; nevertheless we eat oil and drink wine in glory of Christ Who was crucified. During the Afterfeasts of Master Feasts, we eat twice and drink wine. But if a saint occur having a Polyeleos on Wednesday or Friday outside the Fast, we eat twice and drink wine. If the saint be chanted on 8, we eat with oil, that is, olive oil; but if on Monday, we eat fish. If we chant the saint on 6, on those days we drink blessed wine, which is the fourth part of a ladle; nevertheless, just as the divine Fathers placed all things in the holy Typikon under the discernment of the Superior, so let this Typikon likewise remain in the midst.
