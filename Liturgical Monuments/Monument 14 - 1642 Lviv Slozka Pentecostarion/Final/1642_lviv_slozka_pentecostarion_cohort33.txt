*Synaxarion on the Sunday of the Blind Man:*
*Verses:*
{ Bestower of light, Who art Light from Light:
{ From birth a blind man Thou dost make to see today, O Word! }

On this same day, the Sixth Sunday after Pascha, we celebrate the miracle of our Lord God and Savior Jesus Christ wrought upon the man blind from his birth; for this also is an illumination, even as was that concerning the Samaritan woman and the Paralytic. It came to pass on this wise: As Christ was conversing with the Jews, showing Himself equal to the Father and saying, "Before Abraham was, I am" [John 8:58], they cast stones at Him; and going forth from thence, He finds a blind man stumbling. Now he was so from his birth, having only the form and hollows of eyes. The Savior having found him in this state, is asked by His disciples, who had heard concerning the Paralytic: "Behold, thou art made whole; sin no more!" [John 5:14], and that the sin of parents falls upon the children: "Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?" [John 9:2]. In another way they also held a certain Epicurean opinion: that souls pre-exist, and having sinned bodilessly, are brought into bodies.[^711] Removing all these notions, Christ said: "Not for these reasons, but that the works of God might be made manifest" [John 9:3]—that is, Mine. For the word was not concerning the Father, and "that" is of result, not of cause. And having said this, Christ spat upon the ground, and made clay, and anointed the place of his eyes, and commands him to go wash in the pool of Siloam, that He might show that He is the One Who in the beginning took dust from the earth and fashioned man; and since He creates this which is the most lordly part in the body among the ignorant, showing that He is the One Who gives motion also to the soul's power. And He did not anoint with water, but with spittle, that it might be known that all grace was from the spittle of His mouth, and that He wished to send him to Siloam. And He commands him to wash, lest anyone attribute the healing to the earth and clay of that place. He sends him to Siloam that there might be many witnesses of the healing; for many met him as he went, having his eyes anointed with clay. Some say that having washed, he did not wash away the mud that was from the spittle, but that very mud, by the application of the moist matter, was transformed into the structure of the eyes.

=== LEAF p330 ===
Now Siloam is interpreted "Sent" [John 9:7]: for that pool was outside the city of Jerusalem. In the days of Hezekiah, when enemies besieged the city and occupied Siloam, the water was suddenly diverted from thence; previously, they had dug a cistern and a conduit within to receive the waters. And he who was sent thither by the command of the Prophet Isaiah, the water again went forth and was drawn; but if any of the enemies wished to draw, the springs of waters were withheld; and so it was from that time. Therefore, that Christ might show that He also is from God, on this account He sends the blind man thither, and the blind man immediately obeys. Some indeed thought that "Siloam" is interpreted "Sent" because of this blind man who was sent by Christ. The blind man, having washed, receives sight by a certain ineffable power, without even the sufferer himself knowing the mystery. And his neighbors and those otherwise acquainted with him, seeing him seeing well, were unbelieving; but he confessed that he was formerly blind, and being questioned concerning the cause of his receiving sight, he preached Christ the Healer of passions. The Pharisees, hearing of this most glorious sign, again blaspheme the Savior, saying that He keeps not the Sabbath. For it was on the Sabbath that what was done unto the blind man was wrought: therefore a division arises among them, some saying that Jesus is from God because of the wonders performed, and others that He is not from God because He keeps not the Sabbath. Those who held a good opinion of Him question the blind man: "What sayest thou of Him?" And he proclaims Him a Prophet, which was considered more honorable by some. But they again disbelieve that Christ granted healing unto one who was blind; therefore they summon his parents, not believing the witnesses, wishing rather to conceal it; but they make it yet more manifest, the parents confessing all, although they were afraid of being put out of the synagogue, referring the deeds unto his age. And again they say unto the blind man: "Give glory to God, for the healing is from Him, and not from Christ; for He is a sinner, being a breaker of the Sabbath!" But he, wishing to show by deeds that He is God, said: "I know not this; one thing I know, that whereas I was blind, now I see!" [John 9:25]. Again they said unto him: "How did He open thine eyes?" But he, being weary, speaks not in detail, but summarily: that if He were not from God, He could not have done such a sign; and first he is reviled by them as being an examiner and having become His disciple, and because he said that no one has ever opened the eyes of one born blind: for others indeed enlightened the blind, but one born blind not a single one! Having mocked him, they cast him far out of the synagogue. After these things Jesus finds him, and says unto him: "Dost thou believe in the Son of God?" And he, having learned Who He is, bowed down before Him and becomes His disciple, confessing His beneficence [John 9:35-38]. This can also be interpreted anagogically, that is, mystically: The blind man is the people from the Gentiles, whom Christ, passing by, found in their dwelling on earth instead of heaven; or because He came for the Hebrew people, but passing by, He came also unto the Gentiles. And having spat upon the ground and made clay, He anointed: instead of teaching, and before that, because He came down as a drop upon earth and was incarnate of the Holy Virgin. Then also He delivered Divine Baptism, which is Siloam.[^712] Thereafter the Christian people from the Gentiles dare all things for Christ, being persecuted, and bear witness, and afterwards were preached and glorified by Him. By Thine unsearchable mercy, O Giver of light, Christ our God, have mercy on us, now and ever and unto ages of ages. Amen.
