# PART II: GREAT AND HOLY PASCHA AND BRIGHT WEEK

## The Holy and Great Sunday of Pascha

**Superior:** GLORY TO THE HOLY, CONSUBSTANTIAL, LIFE-CREATING, AND UNDIVIDED TRINITY, ALWAYS, NOW AND EVER, AND UNTO AGES OF AGES!  
*And when we have answered:* Amen.  
*The Superior begins authentically with a loud voice together with the Deacon:[^482]*  
*Troparion, Tone 5:*  
Christ is risen from the dead, trampling down death by death, and unto those in the tombs bestowing life!  
*The Superior sings this thrice, and the brethren thrice. And immediately the Superior proclaims:*  
*Verse 1:* Let God arise, and let His enemies be scattered, and let them that hate Him flee from before His face!  
*And to each verse the brethren sing the entire Troparion, Christ is risen, thrice.*  
*Verse 2:* As smoke vanishes, so let them vanish away; as wax melts before the fire.  
*Verse 3:* So let the sinners perish from the face of God, but let the righteous be glad!
*Verse 4:* This is the day which the Lord has made; let us rejoice and be glad in it!  
*Glory: Christ is risen... thrice. Both now: Christ is risen... thrice.*  
*Then with an elevated voice the Superior sings:*  
**Superior:** Christ is risen from the dead, trampling down death by death!  
*And the doors being opened by the precious Cross, the Superior enters, the lamp preceding; and the brethren singing:*  
**Choir:** And unto those in the tombs bestowing life!  
*Then they strike forcefully all the wooden and iron clappers and likewise the bells, and toll them sufficiently for three hours.*  
*And when the Superior has entered with the priests into the holy sanctuary, the Deacon says the Great Ektenia:* In peace let us pray to the Lord. *And after the exclamation:* For unto Thee is due all glory... *the Superior begins the Canon, the Heirmos: "The day of Resurrection, let us be radiant..." with a loud voice.[^483]*  
*Then we place the Icon of the Feast in the midst of the church. And immediately the Superior, having gone out, censes the Holy Icons and both choirs, a deacon preceding him with a lamp; and he censes the brethren also according to their order. After which, returning to the holy church doors, he censes there according to order; and departing, he stands in the midst of the church opposite the church doors and censes the deacon, and gives the censer to the deacon. Then the Superior makes according to custom three prostrations before the Icon of the Feast in the midst, and bows slightly to both choirs, to the right side and to the left. And immediately the deacon censes him, and gives the censer to another priest, having censed him.*  
*And in the same manner the censing is performed by all the priests, as many as there may be. After each one the deacons cense. And this takes place on this single day alone.*  
*We sing the CANON OF PASCHA, a Work of Lord John of Damascus. The Heirmos, to 4; and the Troparia, to 12. After each ode, both choirs sing the Heirmos together. At both choirs the Superior gives the intonation for each choir to begin. And at each ode there is a Little Ektenia by the deacons outside the sanctuary, and the exclamations by the priests according to their order inside the holy sanctuary.*

*Refrain:* Christ is risen from the dead!  
*Troparion:* Beholding Thy boundless compassion, O Christ, they who were held in the bonds of Hades pressed forward unto the light with joyful feet, praising the eternal Pascha!

Though Thou didst descend into the grave, O Immortal One, yet didst Thou destroy the power of Hades, and didst arise as Victor, O Christ God, proclaiming "Rejoice!" unto the Myrrh-bearing Women, and granting peace unto Thine Apostles, and bestowing resurrection upon the fallen!  
*Ikos:*  
The Myrrh-bearing maidens anticipated the dawn, seeking as day the Sun that set before the sun in the tomb, and cried out one to another: "O companion women, come let us anoint with spices the life-bearing and buried Body, the flesh that raises fallen Adam lying in the tomb! Let us go, let us make haste like the Magi, and let us worship, and let us bring myrrh as gifts unto Him Who is wrapped not in swaddling clothes, but in a winding-sheet; and let us weep and cry out: 'O Master, arise, Who bestowest resurrection upon the fallen!'"  
*Synaxarion for the Holy and Great Sunday of Pascha:[^487]*  
*Bless, father, to read.*  
*Verses:*  
Christ, having gone down alone to battle with Hades,  
Came forth bearing many spoils of victory.  
On the Holy and Great Sunday of Pascha, we celebrate the very Life-bearing Resurrection of our Lord God and Savior Jesus Christ: which we also call Pascha, which in the Hebrew tongue is interpreted as "passage" or "crossing over". For this is the day on which God in the beginning brought the world out of non-existence; on this day He led the Israelite people through the Red Sea, snatching them from the hands of Pharaoh; on this day again, having descended from the heavens, He took up His abode in the womb of the Virgin; and now, having snatched all human nature from the depths of Hades, He has led it up into heaven and brought it unto the ancient dignity of incorruption. Yet having descended into Hades, He did not raise up all, but as many as chose to believe in Him; and the souls of the saints held by force from ages past He freed from Hades, and granted unto all to ascend into heaven. Therefore, rejoicing beyond nature, we radiantly celebrate the Resurrection, portraying the joy wherewith our nature has been enriched through the tenderheartedness and mercy of God; and likewise showing forth the destruction of enmity, our union with God and with the very Angels, we perform the customary kiss of peace.  
And the Resurrection took place in this manner: While the soldiers were guarding the tomb, at midnight an earthquake occurred; for an Angel descended and rolled away the stone from the doors of the tomb, and the guards, terrified from fear of him, fled. And the arrival of the women took place on Saturday late, as it were about the middle of Saturday night. And first of all, the Resurrection of the Lord became known unto the Mother of God sitting right opposite the tomb with Magdalene, as Matthew says; but lest the Resurrection be disbelieved on account of maternal kinship, the Evangelists say that He appears first to Mary Magdalene, who also saw the Angel upon the stone, and stooping down again, saw those within, who indeed proclaimed the Lord's Resurrection: "For He is risen," they said, "He is not here; behold the place where they laid Him." Therefore she runs, having heard this, and comes unto the most fervent of the disciples, unto Peter and John, and proclaims unto them the Resurrection. And as she was returning with Mary, Christ met them, saying: "Rejoice!" For it was fitting that the sex which first heard: "In sorrow shalt thou bring forth children", should first hear of joy. And overcome by love, she approaches and touches His most pure feet, desiring to perceive more carefully. And both disciples ran to the tomb; and Peter, merely stooping down into the tomb, departed; but John enters within and inspects more thoroughly, and touches the winding-sheet and the napkin.[^488]
Again Magdalene comes to the tomb in the morning with other women, desiring to be assured more carefully of what had been seen. And standing outside she wept, and stooping down into the tomb, she sees two Angels shining with radiance, who also addressed her and said: "Woman, why weepest thou, and whom seekest thou? Ye seek Jesus of Nazareth Who was crucified; He is risen, He is not here." And immediately they arose in fear, having seen the Lord. Therefore she, turning back, sees Christ standing, and thinking Him to be the gardener, for the tomb was in a garden, says unto Him: "Sir, if thou hast taken Him away, tell me where thou hast laid Him, and I will take Him away." Then the Savior, looking slightly toward the Angels, says to Mary Magdalene: "Mary!" And she, recognizing the sweet and customary voice of Christ, wished to touch Him; but He said: "Touch Me not, for I am not yet ascended unto My Father, as thou supposest, thinking Me still to be a mere mortal; but go unto My brethren, and say unto them what things thou hast seen and heard." And this Magdalene does.  
And as day was dawning, she is again at the tomb with the others; and those with Joanna and Salome came when the sun was risen; and to put it simply, the coming of the women to the tomb took place at various times, among whom was also the Mother of God; for she is the one whom the Gospel calls Mary the mother of Joses, and this Joses was the son of Joseph.  
And it is uncertain in what hour the Lord arose: for some say at the first cockcrow, others when the earthquake occurred, and others variously. While these things were thus taking place, behold, some of the watch went and reported unto the chief priests what had happened; and they, having bribed them with pieces of silver, persuaded them to say: "His disciples came by night and stole Him away while we slept."

And on the evening of the same day, the disciples being assembled together for fear of the Jews, and the doors being firmly shut, Jesus enters unto them, since He was in an incorruptible Body, and according to His custom proclaims peace unto them; and seeing Him, they rejoiced exceedingly, and through the inbreathing received the most perfect operation of the All-Holy Spirit.  
And how the three-day Resurrection of the Lord is reckoned, know thus:[^489] Thursday evening and Friday day: for thus the Hebrews count a single night-day. Again, Friday night and Saturday: behold the second night-day, this is the second day. Again, Saturday night and Sunday day: from a part of the beginning the whole is taken, the third night-day; behold the third day.  
Or thus: At the third hour of Friday Christ was crucified; then from the sixth hour until the ninth hour there was darkness: understand this as night for me; behold from the third until the ninth hour is one night-day. Then again after the darkness, the day and night of Friday: behold two night-days. The day of Saturday and its night again: behold three night-days, according to the Evangelist who says: "As it began to dawn toward the first day of the week." If He who promised in the third day bestows grace, He is the only God blessed by the Father and highly exalted: and thus is it good, and glory be unto Him, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*And at the conclusion of the kiss of peace of all, the Priest takes the Holy Gospel from the hands of the Superior, and places it upon the Holy Table. And while all the brethren stand and listen attentively, the Superior reads the homily of Chrysostom from the sanctuary in the Royal Doors. And turning his face to the West, he says:[^495]* "Paul's mouth is Christ's mouth; and Christ's mouth is Paul's mouth; the mouth of Chrysostom is the mouth of Christ and Paul." *Then he says this:*  
*The Catechetical Homily of our father among the saints, John Chrysostom, Archbishop of Constantinople, for the Holy and Light-Bearing Day of the all-glorious and saving Resurrection of Christ our God, and concerning those that tarried until late in the ascetic contest of the fast:[^496]*

If any man be devout and a lover of God, let him enjoy this fair and radiant triumphal festival! If any man be a prudent servant, let him enter rejoicing into the joy of his Lord! If any have labored in fasting, let him now receive his reward! If any have wrought from the first hour, let him today receive his just reward! If any have come after the third hour, let him celebrate with thanksgiving! If any have arrived after the sixth hour, let him have no misgivings, for he is not penalized in any way! If any have tarried even until the ninth hour, let him draw near without doubting, fearing nothing! If any have arrived even at the eleventh hour, let him not fear because of his delay: for the Master is generous in lovingkindness, He receives the last even as the first; He gives rest unto him that came at the eleventh hour, even as unto him that wrought from the first hour; and He shows mercy unto the last, and He comforts the first; and unto this one He gives, and upon that one He bestows; and He honors the deed, and He praises the intention, and He loves him that was delayed! Therefore enter ye all into the joy of your Lord: and first and second and last, receive your reward! Rich and poor, dance together! Ascetics and slothful, honor the day! Ye that have fasted and ye that have not fasted, be glad today! The table is full-laden, delight ye all! The calf is fatted and abundant, let no one go forth hungry!  
Delight ye all in the banquet of faith; receive ye all the riches of goodness! Let no one lament his poverty, for the universal Kingdom has been revealed! Let no one mourn for his transgressions, for forgiveness and remission have shone forth together from the tomb! Let no one fear death, for the death of the Savior has set us free: He that was held by it extinguished it; He that descended into Hades tormented Hades; it was embittered when it tasted of His flesh! And anticipating this, Isaiah cried out: "Hades," he said, "was embittered, encountering Thee below!" It was embittered, for it was abolished; it was embittered, for it was mocked; it was embittered, for it was slain; it was embittered, for it was cast down; it was embittered, for it was bound! It seized a body, and encountered God; it seized earth, and met heaven; it seized that which it saw, and fell into that which it saw not! Where, O death, is thy sting? Where, O Hades, is thy victory? Christ is risen, and thou art cast down! Christ is risen, and the demons are fallen! Christ is risen, and the Angels rejoice! Christ is risen, and life abides in all the world! Christ is risen, and not one dead is left in the tomb! For Christ, being raised from the dead, became the Firstfruits of them that slept! Unto Him be glory and dominion, unto ages of ages. Amen.

## Paschal Hours for Holy Pascha and Throughout All Bright Week[^498]
*When the Priest has begun:* "Blessed is our God:", *or if there is no Priest, the elder says:* "Through the prayers of our holy fathers...", *we say:* "Amen." *Then we say, not to a chant, but simply reciting:* "Christ is risen from the dead...", *the entire Troparion. Then we say thrice:*  
Having beheld the Resurrection of Christ, let us worship the holy Lord Jesus, the only sinless One. We venerate Thy Cross, O Christ, and Thy holy Resurrection we hymn and glorify: for Thou art our God, apart from Thee we know no other, we call upon Thy Name. Come, all ye faithful, let us worship Christ's holy Resurrection, for behold, through the Cross joy hath come into all the world! Always blessing the Lord, let us praise His Resurrection: for having endured the Cross, He destroyed death by death!  
*Then the Ypakoë, Tone 4:*  
Anticipating the dawn, they that were with Mary, and finding the stone rolled away from the tomb, heard from the Angel: "Why seek ye with the dead as mortal Him Who abides in unapproachable light? Behold the winding-sheets of the tomb! Run and proclaim unto the world that the Lord is risen, slaying death: for He is the Son of God, Who saves the human race!"

Though Thou didst descend into the tomb, O Immortal, yet didst Thou destroy the power of Hades, and didst rise as Victor, O Christ God, proclaiming joy unto the Myrrh-bearing Women, and granting peace unto Thine Apostles, O Thou Who bestowest resurrection upon the fallen!

In the tomb bodily, in Hades with the soul as God, in Paradise with the thief, and on the throne wast Thou, O Christ, with the Father and the Spirit, filling all things, Thou Who art uncircumscribed!  
*Glory:*

*Then:* Lord, have mercy *(40 times).* *Glory: Both now:* More honorable than the Cherubim... *In the Name of the Lord, bless, father!*  
*Priest's verse:* Through the prayers of our holy fathers...  
*And we say:* Amen. *Then we say:* Christ is risen... *(Thrice).* *Glory: Both now:* Lord, have mercy *(Thrice).* *Bless! Then the Priest gives the dismissal, and we conclude the Hour. But if there is no Priest, the senior says:* Through the prayers of our holy fathers... *And we say:* Amen. *Then we say:* Memory eternal unto you, O worthy of beatitude, ever-memorable Founders!  
*In this manner it is chanted for the Third Hour, for the Sixth, and for the Ninth Hour, and for Compline once, likewise also for the Midnight Office, even until Thomas Sunday.*  
*And the Third Hour, the Sixth, and the Ninth we chant together in the narthex. When the senior directs, we strike the clapper as is customary for the resurrection. And we enter into the narthex. And we chant, having said:* Through the prayers... *and* Amen. *At the conclusion of the Hour for the 3rd, we strive likewise a second time, and say:* Christ is risen... *(Thrice), and the rest of the Hour for the 6th Hour. Then also the third time, for the 9th.*

*We chant:* Anticipating the dawn... *Glory: Kontakion:* Though Thou didst descend into the tomb, O Immortal... *Both now:* Only-begotten Son... *We chant the Beatitudes, from the Canon, Odes 3 and 6, once. Priest:* Wisdom! *Reader, the Title of the Apostle and the reading itself. Then the Priest reads the Gospel.[^499] Then:* Remember us, O Lord, when Thou comest... *Then the choir sings, and we chant:* I believe in one God... Remit, pardon... *And after the* Our Father: *Kontakion:* Though Thou didst descend into the tomb, O Immortal... *Glory: Both now: Kontakion:* Rejoice, O hallowed divine dwelling... Lord, have mercy *(40 times).* *Then:* One is Holy, One is Lord, Jesus Christ, to the glory of God the Father, Amen. *And we sing in place of* "Blessed be the Name of the Lord": Christ is risen... *(Thrice).* *Then Psalm 33:* I will bless the Lord at all times... *The conclusion, unto* "shall not want any good thing". *And we chant the dismissal, and depart to the refectory.*

But only after chanting the third in the narthex, when he has said:* Lord, have mercy *(40 times).* *Then we go from the narthex into the church, saying:* More honorable than the Cherubim... *and* In the Name of the Lord, bless, father! *And the Priest desiring to sing the Holy Liturgy, standing before the Holy Doors, says:* God be gracious unto us and bless us, cause His face to shine upon us, and have mercy on us! *Glory: Both now:* Lord, have mercy *(Thrice).* *Bless! The Priest gives the dismissal and forgiveness, and begins the Divine Liturgy.*

*First Antiphon, Psalm 65, Tone 2. Verse 1:*  
Shout with jubilation unto the Lord, all the earth!  
*Refrain:* Through the intercessions of the Theotokos, O Savior, save us!

*Refrain:* Through the intercessions of the Theotokos, O Savior, save us!

*Refrain:* Through the intercessions of the Theotokos, O Savior, save us!

*Refrain:* Through the intercessions of the Theotokos, O Savior, save us!

Let God arise, and let His enemies be scattered.

Christ is risen... *(Thrice).* *Glory: Both now: Kontakion:* Though Thou didst descend into the tomb... *In place of the Trisagion:* As many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ, Alleluia!  
*It is fitting to know that these Antiphons and "As many as have been baptized into Christ" we sing throughout the entire Bright Week, even until New Sunday; likewise also the Communion verses.*

*Verse:* Give thanks unto the Lord, for He is good, for His mercy endures unto the ages.  
*Verse:* Let the house of Israel now say: For He is good, for His mercy endures unto the ages.  
*The Epistle, from the Acts of the Apostles, Pericope 1:* The former treatise have I made...  
The former treatise have I made...  
*Alleluia, Tone 4:* Thou, having risen, shalt have mercy upon Zion.  
*Verse:* The Lord looked down from heaven upon the earth.  
*The Gospel according to John, Pericope 1.*  
*And when the time for the reading of the Holy Gospel hath arrived, the Superior stands in the sanctuary at the High Place, on the eastern side of the Altar-Table facing west, and the Holy Gospel is held before him by two clerics. The Senior Deacon, desiring to read the Gospel, proclaims with a loud voice:*  
**Deacon:** Bless, master, the evangelist of the holy, glorious, and all-praised universal herald of God, the Apostle and Evangelist John the Theologian!  
*Having taken the Holy Gospel, he makes a bow unto the Superior. Then the Superior says:*  
**Superior:** God, through the intercessions of the holy, glorious, and all-praised universal apostle... *and the rest.*  
*And the Deacon goes forth and stands at the customary place directly facing the royal gates, elevating his face. And the Superior having first proclaimed:* "Wisdom, stand upright! Let us listen to the Holy Gospel!" *The Deacon says the same:* "Wisdom, stand upright!" *Superior:* "The reading of the Holy Gospel according to John." *Likewise also the Deacon. Superior:* "Let us attend!" *Deacon, the same.*  
*And the other deacons do not proclaim, as is customary. Then the Superior reads:* "In the beginning was the Word..." *facing west, while the Deacon faces east. And at each exclamation or verse of the Gospel they strike the church bell once: and the Paraecclesiarch outside the church strikes the great wooden clapper and the great bell. And at the final exclamation, they strike all the bells and the great clapper a little.[^502]*  
*In Little Russia there is a custom that all concelebrating PRIESTS, as many as there are, and likewise the Deacons, read after the Superior, having taken their stand in various places, the Gospel by strophes as the Superior arranges, and breaks off; concerning which see above in order.*

*And in order the Divine Liturgy of Chrysostom proceeds according to custom. Communion verse:* Receive ye the Body of Christ; taste ye of the fountain of immortality, Alleluia! *And when the Priest says:* With the fear of God and with faith... *we, in place of "Blessed is He that comes in the Name of the Lord", sing:* Christ is risen... *(Thrice).* *And in place of "Let our mouths be filled":* Christ is risen... *(Thrice).* *Likewise in place of "Blessed be the Name of the Lord":* Christ is risen... *(Thrice).* *And in place of the Psalm "I will bless the Lord": we sing:* Christ is risen... *(12 times), and continuously until the antidoron has been distributed by the Superior. Then the Deacon:* Wisdom! *We:* Christ is risen... *(Thrice).* *After this the Priest says the dismissal, according to custom as indicated at Matins.*  
*Let it also be known that the Doors of the Church and of the Sanctuary, both the Great and the small side doors, across all churches throughout the entire Bright Week, are not shut, except during the time of Communion.*  
*Then Prayers at the Offering of Meats in the Narthex:*  
*When the Priest has begun:* "Blessed is our God:", *and after we have chanted:* "Christ is risen..." *(Thrice).* *The Priest again:* "Let us pray to the Lord."  
*And this First Prayer:*  
Look down, O Lord Jesus Christ, upon these meats of flesh, and sanctify them, as Thou didst sanctify the ram which the faithful Abraham offered unto Thee, and the ram which Abel offered unto Thee as a whole burnt offering; likewise also the fatted calf which Thou didst command to be slain for Thy prodigal son when he returned again unto Thee; that even as he was accounted worthy to delight in Thy goodness, so we also may receive as sanctified and blessed by Thee these foods for the nourishment of us all. For Thou art the true Food and the Giver...  
...and the Giver of good things, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.  
*Second Prayer, for the blessing of Cheese and Eggs:[^503]*  
**Priest:** Let us pray to the Lord.  
O Sovereign Lord our God, Maker and Creator of all things, bless the curdled milk and with it also the eggs, and preserve us in Thy goodness; that partaking of these Thine ungrudgingly bestowed gifts, we may be filled with Thine ineffable goodness. For Thine is the majesty, and Thine is the Kingdom, and the Power, and the Glory, of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen. *And the dismissal.*  
*And he sprinkles with the lesser sanctified water. And the poor and needy distribute from these offerings, and take a little for themselves home for a blessing.*  
*Be it known that swine's flesh and the offering of pascha is not the Pascha, nor the Lamb, as some say and receive, and partake thereof with all reverence as if of some holy thing:[^504] but it is a simple offering: for it is not to be offered as a Sacrifice unto God. But only that for them that begin to eat meat and cheese after the Fast, it may be blessed for food by the prayers appointed by the Priest. For Christ Himself is the Pascha and the Lamb Who takes away the sins of the world, offered by the Priest unto God and the Father upon the Altar in the unbloody Sacrifice in the All-Pure Mysteries of His Precious Body and Life-Giving Blood; and they that worthily partake thereof eat the True Pascha. And for this reason it is not proper to bring those offerings into the Sanctuary or into the Church, seeing they are not the Pascha, but to bless them with prayer outside the Church or in the Narthex, according to the Third Canon of the Holy Apostles, which says:* If...

"If any Bishop or Presbyter, contrary to the Lord's ordinance concerning the Sacrifice, shall bring any other things to the Altar, honey or milk, or coffee, or instead of wine, strong drink, that is to say, beer, or mead, or fowl, or any animals, or pulse, except fresh ears of corn or grapes in their season, and oil for the lamp, and incense at the time of the Liturgy, let him be deposed." *And according to the Sixth Council in Trullo, in Canon 99, which says:* "We have learned that this also takes place in the land of the Armenians: that some, boiling parts of meats within the Holy Sanctuaries, offer portions to the Priests, dividing them in the Jewish manner. Therefore, keeping the Church blameless, we decree that it is not lawful for any Priest to receive divided portions of meat from them that bring them, but let them be content with whatever the bringers shall see fit to give them, such offering being made outside the Church. And if anyone do not this, let him be excommunicated."  
*After the dismissal of the Divine Liturgy:[^505]*  
*The Priest having taken the festal icon of Christ's Resurrection, and the Deacon having received from the Priest the Artos through the Royal Doors, if both are in the sanctuary; but in monasteries it is placed on the iconostasion on the left side before the patronal icon of the All-Pure Mother of God: and at the antidoron where the first kiss the icon of the Feast, and then also the Artos; and the Deacon in sticharion takes it from the place, and carries it in his hands, one or two lamps preceding. And the Superior and all the brethren according to their rank go forth with the Artos, that is, the Bread, a whole Prosphora prepared with the Cross inscribed upon it, from the church into the Refectory, chanting in place of the Psalm "I will exalt Thee":* Christ is risen... *while the Paraecclesiarch and other brethren ring all the bells and clappers. And having entered into the Refectory, they place the Feast icon and the Artos in their places. And the Superior having said:* Christ is risen... *(Thrice), and the* Our Father: *Glory: Both now:* Lord, have mercy *(Thrice).* *Bless! Superior:* O Christ God, bless the food... *and the rest. Then:* Through the prayers of Thine all-pure Mother and of all the saints, O Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on us! *And we say:* Amen. *We sit down, each in his own place, with all silence and reverence. And in the Refectory there is great consolation for all.*  
*If, however, by reason of some great necessity there was no Liturgy, then after the Hours he takes the Antidoron brought from the Prothesis where the Liturgy was served, or left for that purpose from a previously celebrated Liturgy. We go to the Refectory, as said above.*  
*We ought to know this also, brethren: NOTE: That our consolation should not be carnal, but spiritual, remembering the divine saying of the Savior:* "Let not your hearts be weighed down with surfeiting and drunkenness." *And the Apostle saying:* "Whether ye eat, or drink, or whatsoever ye do, do all to the glory of God." *Rather let our consolation be in listening to the Holy Scriptures and in being filled with them, especially on such great Feasts, and not in filling the belly beyond the measure handed down by rule to monks from the holy and God-bearing Fathers.*  
*We begin to read in the Refectory, from this light-bearing day, Chrysostom's Commentaries on the Acts of the Holy Apostles: which we complete unto the Sunday of All Saints.*  
*The Order of the Panagia performed in Monasteries at the customary Refectory on Holy Pascha, and throughout all Bright Week even until Saturday of New Week:*  
*The Artos, which is the Prosphora, and around the Cross which is on the Prosphora we write this:* "Christ is risen! Truly He is risen!" *(Thrice).* "We worship His third-day Resurrection!" *And he places it covered in the Panagiarion, and the prayer over the Artos:* "With joy and gladness..." *and the rest. After this, rising from the table, the brethren place it upon the table, singing:* Christ is risen... *(Thrice), and:* Lord, have mercy *(Twice).* *Lord, bless! Then the Cellarer, having received the customary forgiveness from the Superior and the brethren:* "Bow down, holy fathers, and forgive me a sinner!" *We say:* "May God forgive and bless!" *Then taking off his kamilavka, he proclaims in place of the great Name of the Holy Trinity, saying:* "Christ is risen!" *(Thrice).* *And we answer:* "Truly He is risen!" *The same, having signed the Artos cruciformly, says:* "We worship His third-day Resurrection!" *and places it upon the Panagiarion. Then the Cellarer brings the Artos upon the Panagiarion unto the Elder, and the Superior kisses the Artos, and all the brethren after him, chanting Ode 9 Heirmos:* Shine, shine... *and the other two troparia of that Ode, and again the Heirmos:* Shine, shine... *And after the kissing of the Prosphora by all, the Cellarer returning sets it before the Superior, and says:*

*And he says the verse:* Through the prayers of our holy fathers, O Lord Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us! *And we say:* Amen. *And we sing:* Christ is risen... *(Thrice).* *Glory: The Ypakoë, we say not to chant:* Anticipating the dawn... *Both now: Kontakion, likewise not to chant:* Though Thou didst descend into the tomb... Lord, have mercy *(Twice).* *Lord, bless! The Priest, or if there is no Superior:* Blessed is God Who shows mercy unto us and nourishes us from His great and rich gifts, by His grace and love toward mankind, always, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. *And we say:* Amen. *Then the Superior:* May God forgive and have mercy on them that have served us. *And immediately he goes forth from the Refectory into the Church; and all the brethren according to their rank, the Priest preceding with the Icon of Christ's Resurrection, and the Deacon with the Artos, and the clerics singing:* Christ is risen... *Then the Paraecclesiarch and the other brethren strike all the bells and clappers. And when the Superior and brethren have gone out into the Church, the Priest and Deacon place the Icon and the Artos in their places. And we chant:* And unto us He hath given eternal life! *And we go unto our cells.*  
*In this manner takes place the kissing of the Prosphora, which is the Artos, throughout all the week of the holy Bright Sunday, even unto Saturday.*  
*Note: It is fitting to know this also: That if it happen that any brother fall asleep and depart unto the Lord during this holy Week of Pascha:[^506]*  
*We do not sing over him the customary Order for the Departed, but the entire Resurrectional Order; that is to say: After the Priest says:* Blessed is our God! *We sing:* Christ is risen... *(Thrice).* *And:* Having beheld the Resurrection of Christ, let us worship the holy Lord... *The Canon, the Exapostilarion of Sunday, the Epistle and the Gospel of the Feast, on the Praises, the Kiss of Peace, and the Dismissal.*  
*This office was translated from the Greek dialect into the Slavonic tongue by Gabriel the Hieromonk, Protos of the Holy Lavra of Athos.*  
## On the Holy and Great Sunday of Pascha: At the Service of Vespers[^507]
*The Paraecclesiarch, having received a blessing from the Superior, goes into the Church, and lights all the tapers and the church lamps. He rings all the bells abundantly. *Be it known that concerning how the order of the Annunciation is to be sung on the days of Holy Forty Days, during the weeks of Great Week, and on Holy Pascha, we have followed not any private opinion of our own, but the Typikon of the Holy Great Church. It is also well certified by having examined the Synaxarion of the venerable Studite Monastery and other great Typika of Jerusalem.[^511] Therefore let no one marvel at the changes made in the stichera, canons, readings, prokeimena, epistles, alleluia verses, gospels, and communion verses: for so it hath been commanded.*  
*When the Annunciation falls on any fasting day of the Holy Forty Days: then the service of the Feast precedes the daily order, because the fast is relaxed. Only on the paramon of the Feast, when it falls on a fasting day, do we sing the stichera first of the day, and then of the Annunciation, and the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts, out of honor for the Holy Forty Days, because it is a fast.*  
*In the days of Great Week, if the Annunciation occurs: at Vespers the Stichera of the day precede, and then of the Annunciation, on account of the Fast, as we said before regarding the paramon of the Feast. Likewise on Bright Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday, if the Annunciation occurs, the daily service precedes the order of the Feast, on account of the transcendent glory of the bright days.*  
*Some say that on Great Friday, when the Annunciation or the commemoration of the Holy Great-Martyr George occurs, the Liturgy of Chrysostom should be celebrated, especially where the temple is dedicated to that feast: but since the Canons forbid it, and this is not customary, it ought not to be, unless there be for this the blessing of the Patriarch or Metropolitan in his own Metropolia, and of the whole synod of hierarchs, out of honor for the temple; for the patron saint of a temple in his own monastery is counted as a great feast.*

### Notes

[^482]: The Paschal Matins Opening at the Church Doors: The clergy and faithful process out of the church into the closed narthex or before the locked western portal of the church, typifying the sealed rock-hewn sepulchre of Christ. The Superior (*Igumen*) censes the closed doors three times in the form of a cross, proclaims the solemn doxology to the Holy Trinity, and intones the Paschal Troparion *Christ is risen from the dead*, followed by the Paschal verses from Psalm 67 (LXX 67:2–4) and Psalm 117 (LXX 117:24).
[^483]: The Paschal Canon of St. John of Damascus (Tone 1): The crown of Byzantine hymnography, composed of eight odes (Ode 2 being omitted in accordance with the festal canon structure). Each ode is chanted by alternating choirs, followed by the Little Ektenia proclaimed by the deacon outside the sanctuary, while the priest intones the festal exclamation from within the holy altar.
[^484]: The Ypakoë of Pascha (Tone 4): Sung after Ode 3 in place of the customary Kathisma/Sessional Hymn. The Greek term *Hypakoë* (*Ὑπακοή*, lit. "hearkening" or "reverent hearing") signifies a contemplative hymn commemorating the Holy Myrrh-bearing Women who went early to the tomb and heard the archangel proclaim that the Lord is risen.
[^485]: The Patristic Reading from St. Gregory the Theologian: Oration 45 (*On Holy Pascha*, PG 36:623–664), opening with the prophetic stance of Habakkuk: "I will stand upon my watch..." (Habakkuk 2:1), directly echoing the opening heirmos of Ode 4.
[^486]: The Paschal Kontakion and Ikos (Tone 8): Attributed to St. Romanos the Melodist, chanted after Ode 6 of the Canon, celebrating Christ's voluntary descent into the grave as the Immortal Victor, breaking the power of Hades, and bringing the joyous proclamation to the Myrrh-bearers and the Apostles.
[^487]: The Synaxarion of Holy Pascha: Authored by Nikephoros Kallistos Xanthopoulos (c. 1256–c. 1335) for the liturgical Triodion and Pentecostarion, expounding the etymological meaning of the Passover (*Pascha* as the Hebrew *Pesach*, translated as "crossing over" or "passage"), the biblical typology of the Red Sea crossing, the resurrection chronology according to the Four Evangelists, and the joy of the cosmic renewal.
[^488]: Woodcut Engraving of the Holy Myrrh-bearing Women and the Risen Christ: Folio 175 (p179) features a fine 17th-century Ruthenian woodcut engraving depicting the Myrrh-bearing Women arriving at the empty rock-hewn tomb greeted by the radiant Angel, while the adjacent scene portrays the appearance of the Risen Lord to St. Mary Magdalene in the garden, bearing the inscribed dialogue label *Раббуни!* ("Rabboni!").
[^489]: Patristic Reckoning of the Three Days and Nights: The Synaxarion presents two classical patristic solutions for calculating the "three days and three nights" of Christ's entombment (Matthew 12:40): first, by civil synecdoche (where any portion of a 24-hour night-day cycle represents the whole period); and second, by reckoning the supernatural three-hour darkness on Good Friday (from the sixth to the ninth hour) as a separate night, followed by the return of light as an extra day.
[^490]: The Hymn "Having Beheld the Resurrection of Christ": Sung thrice after the Synaxarion in the Ruthenian recension, followed by the Paschal sticheron *Jesus, having risen from the tomb, as He foretold, has given us eternal life and great mercy* (Tone 6).
[^491]: Ode 9 Heirmos of the Paschal Canon: "Shine, shine, O New Jerusalem..." (*Свѣти́ся свѣти́ся но́вый І҆ерли́ме*), composed by St. John of Damascus based on Isaiah 60:1 ("Arise, shine, for thy light is come, and the glory of the Lord is risen upon thee").
[^492]: The Paschal Exapostilarion (Photogogikon / Svitylen): "Having fallen asleep in the flesh as a mortal, O King and Lord..." (*Пло́тїю ꙋ҆снꙋ́въ ꙗ҆́кѡ ме́ртвъ*), attributed to St. John of Damascus, chanted thrice to the model melody (*Podoben*) "Thou hast visited us" (*Посѣти́лъ ны*).
[^493]: The Paschal Stichera: "A Pascha sacred has been revealed to us today..." (*Па́сха Сщ҃енная намъ дне́сь показа́ся*), opening with verses from Psalm 67 (LXX 67:2–4) and Psalm 117 (LXX 117:24).
[^494]: The Paschal Kiss of Peace (*Цѣлова́нїе*): The Superior takes the Holy Gospel and the Precious Cross, and stands outside the Holy Doors on the solea; the clergy and brethren approach one by one, venerating the Cross and Gospel and exchanging the joyous Easter greeting: *Christ is risen! Truly He is risen!*
[^495]: The ceremonial acclamation before the Catechetical Homily: "Paul's mouth is Christ's mouth; and Christ's mouth is Paul's mouth; the mouth of Chrysostom is the mouth of Christ and Paul" (*Па́влова ꙋ҆ста̀, Хв҃а ꙋ҆ста̀...*), pronounced by the Superior facing west from the Holy Doors before reading the Golden Discourse.
[^496]: The Catechetical Homily of St. John Chrysostom (*Сло́во ꙋ҆чи́телное... на Ст҃ый и҆ Свѣтоно́сный Де́нь...*), expounding the parable of the laborers in the vineyard (Matthew 20:1–16) and the universal triumph of Christ over Hades and death, citing the prophecy of Isaiah (Isaiah 14:9) and the Apostle Paul (1 Corinthians 15:54–55).
[^497]: The Troparion of St. John Chrysostom (Tone 8): "The grace shining forth from thy mouth like gold enlightened the universe..." (*Оу҆ста̀ твои́хъ ꙗ҆́кѡ зла́то возсїя́вшаѧ бл҃гть...*), sung by the entire congregation at the conclusion of the reading.
[^498]: The Paschal Hours (*Часы бываемы на ст҃ую Пасхꙋ, и чрезъ всю Свѣтлꙋю Седмицꙋ*): In place of the customary psalmody and readings of the First, Third, Sixth, and Ninth Hours, a distinctive joyful order composed entirely of Paschal hymnography is chanted without change throughout Bright Week until Thomas Sunday.
[^499]: The Typika order when the Divine Liturgy is not celebrated: A unique monastic adaptation for paschal days without a priest, chanting the Beatitudes with troparia from Odes 3 and 6 of the Paschal Canon, the Epistle, the Gospel, the Symbol of Faith, the Kontakion, and Psalm 33 (LXX 33:2–11).
[^500]: The Paschal Moleben (*Ѡ Молебнѣ на ст҃ую Пасхꙋ*): A special festal supplicatory service celebrated during Bright Week, optionally combined with the canon of a commemorated saint.
[^501]: The Paschal Antiphons at the Divine Liturgy: Psalm 65 (LXX 65:1–3), Psalm 66 (LXX 66:2–3), and Psalm 67 (LXX 67:2–4), with the festal refrains: "Through the intercessions of the Theotokos...", "Save us, O Son of God, Who didst rise from the dead...", and the Paschal Troparion *Christ is risen*.
[^502]: The Ruthenian and Slavic practice of polyglot and distributed Gospel reading: At the Paschal Liturgy, the reading of John 1:1–17 is chanted with solemn bell-ringing after each verse; the rubric specifically notes the custom in *Little Russia* (*В̾ Мало́й же Рѡссі́и*) of having all concelebrating priests and deacons read strophes of the Gospel in succession from various stations facing the four quarters of the church.
[^503]: The Prayer for the Blessing of Cheese and Eggs (*Втора́я Мл҃тва, въ є҆́же бл҃вити Сы́ръ и҆ Ꙗ҆йца*): Chanted at the conclusion of the Liturgy in the narthex over the paschal baskets, distinguishing the domestic foods from the liturgical Artos.
[^504]: The canonical treatise against calling blessed meats "the Pascha" or bringing them into the sanctuary: The Slozka Triodion includes an extensive polemical and pastoral rubric enforcing Apostolic Canon 3 and Trullan Canon 99, sternly condemning Armenian and Judaizing customs of boiling and distributing sacrificial meats in the holy altar.
[^505]: The Monastic Order of the Elevation of the Panagia and the Artos (*Чинъ ѡ Панагїи бываемый в Монастырѣхъ...*): The procession from the church to the refectory (*Trapeza*) bearing the Artos (the loaf inscribed with the Resurrection of Christ and the cross), its ceremonial elevation with the acclamation "Great is the Name of the Holy Trinity!", and its veneration by all the brethren.
[^506]: The Paschal Order for the Burial of a Departed Brother translated from Mount Athos: The 1642 Slozka imprint preserves the historical colophon certifying that this joyful funeral rite—omitting all funeral dirges and chanting exclusively the Paschal Canon—was translated directly from the Greek of the Holy Mountain of Athos by Gabriel the Hieromonk, Protos of the Great Lavra of St. Athanasios.
[^507]: The Opening of Paschal Vespers (*Къ Вечерней Слꙋжбѣ*): Chanted on the afternoon of Pascha (the "Agape Vespers"), beginning with the bells ringing across all bells and clappers (*klepalo*), the priest censing the Holy Table cruciformly, and intoning the Paschal Troparion with the verses of Psalm 67.
[^508]: The Great Prokeimenon of Paschal Vespers (Tone 7, Psalm 76:14–15, 11–12 LXX): "Who is a god so great as our God? Thou art the God Who workest wonders!" intoned four times with its verses on Sunday of Pascha and throughout the cycle of festal Paschal Vespers services.
[^509]: The Agape Vespers Gospel (John 20:19–25, Pericope 65): Describing the first appearance of the Risen Lord to the disciples in the upper room on the evening of the Resurrection and the absence of Thomas, traditionally chanted in multiple languages facing the four quarters of the world.
[^510]: Ruthenian and Byzantine Typikon rubrics for the concurrence of the Feast of the Annunciation (*Blahovishchenie*, March 25) with Bright Week (*Kyriopascha* and Bright Monday through Wednesday), referencing the Paschalion table and the historical chapters on April 23 (St. George).
[^511]: The canonical and liturgical prohibition against celebrating the Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom on Great and Holy Friday even if the Annunciation or the Feast of the Great-Martyr George coincides, unless expressly authorized by episcopal blessing for a patronal temple feast.
[^512]: The Bright Week Matins structure: In place of the Ikos after Ode 6, the Ruthenian Typikon prescribes the reading of the Prologue (Synaxarion), and after Ode 3, St. John Chrysostom's Homilies on the Gospel of John (from the Ninth Book / Homiliary).
[^513]: The solemn outdoor monastic procession (*Iskhozhdenie*) on Bright Monday: The brethren process outside the monastery perimeter bearing processional banners (*khoruhvy*), the Holy Gospel, the Precious Cross, and icons of the Resurrection, the Mother of God, and monastic wonderworkers, chanting the Paschal Canon and Moleben.
[^514]: The Great Prokeimenon of Bright Monday Vespers (Tone 7, Psalm 113:11, 1, 3, 5 LXX): "Our God is in Heaven and on earth; all things that He hath willed, He hath done!"
[^515]: The reservation of the Pre-sanctified Holy Lamb (*Ahnets*) for the communion of the sick: Consecrated during the Divine Liturgy of Great and Holy Thursday, and dried, divided, and reserved in the tabernacle (*Darokhranitelnytsia*) at Matins on Bright Tuesday according to historical Slavic practice.
[^516]: The Bright Tuesday Liturgy Gospel (Luke 24:12–35, Pericope 113): The Emmaus narrative detailing Christ's revelation in the breaking of the bread to Cleopas and his companion.
[^517]: The Great Prokeimenon of Bright Tuesday Vespers (Tone 8, Psalm 76:2–4, 14 LXX): "With my voice have I cried unto the Lord, with my voice unto God, and He gave ear unto me."
[^518]: The Bright Wednesday Liturgy Gospel (John 1:35–51, Pericope 4): The witness of John the Baptist and the calling of the first disciples—Andrew, Peter, Philip, and Nathanael.
[^519]: The Dogmatikon Theotokion of Tone 5 ("In the Red Sea an image of the unwedded Bride was inscribed of old..."): Setting forth the classical typological parallel between Moses dividing the Red Sea and the Archangel Gabriel ministering at the seedless Incarnation.
[^520]: The Great Prokeimenon of Bright Wednesday Vespers (Tone 7, Psalm 54:2–4, 17–18 LXX): "Give ear, O God, unto my prayer, and despise not my supplication."
[^521]: Liturgical lectionary variation in the 1642 Slozka imprint for Bright Thursday Liturgy: The text indicates Luke Pericope 50 ("A certain man..."), whereas standard received Byzantine and Synodal typika appoint John 3:1–15 (Nicodemus).
[^522]: Historical rubric on the feast of the *Life-Giving Spring* (*Zhyvopryiemnyi Istochnyk*): Composed by Nikephoros Kallistos Xanthopoulos (c. 1256–1335) in commemoration of the miraculous spring of the Mother of God at Baloukli near Constantinople, chanted on Bright Friday and included as an appendix at the conclusion of the 1642 Lviv Pentecostarion.
[^523]: The Great Prokeimenon of Thursday evening of Bright Week (Tone 7, Psalm 17:2–4, 7 LXX): "I will love Thee, O Lord, my strength: the Lord is my foundation," intoned with its verses at Bright Friday Vespers.
[^524]: The Great Prokeimenon of Friday evening of Bright Week (Tone 8, Psalm 60:6, 3, 5, 9 LXX): "Thou hast given an inheritance unto them that fear Thy Name, O Lord," chanted at Bright Saturday Vespers.
[^525]: Liturgical lectionary readings for Bright Saturday Liturgy: Epistle from the Acts of the Apostles, Pericope 8 (Acts 3:11–16); Gospel according to John, Pericope 7 (John 3:22–33); followed by the solemn conclusion of Bright Week in the monastic refectory.
[^526]: The ceremonial elevation and breaking of the Artos (*Voznoshenie Artosa / Panagii*): On Bright Saturday, the whole Artos loaf, which had been blessed on Pascha night and elevated throughout Bright Week in place of the Panagia, is broken and consumed by the brethren before the meal, concluding with the ritual signing of the Panagiarion.
