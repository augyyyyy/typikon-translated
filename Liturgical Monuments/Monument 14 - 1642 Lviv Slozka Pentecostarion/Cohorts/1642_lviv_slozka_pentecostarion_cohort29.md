*Then the Prologue. After it:*

*Synaxarion on Wednesday of Mid-Pentecost. Bless, Father.*[^629]

*Verses:*
Standing in the midst of the Teachers, Jesus instructs; in the midst of the Feasts, He shows Himself as Mediator.

On the Wednesday of the Paralytic, we celebrate the Feast of the Midpoint of Pentecost, in honor of the great two days of feasts—Pascha, I mean, and Pentecost—as uniting and joining them together. And it occurred in this manner: After Christ had performed the supernatural miracle upon the Paralytic, the Judeans being scandalized concerning the Paralytic because it was done on the Sabbath,

=== LEAF p285 ===
sought to kill Him. He therefore departed into Galilee, and staying in the mountains there, He satisfied five thousand men with five loaves and two fish, besides women and children. Afterwards, when the Feast of Booths arrived—and this was also a great feast among the Judeans—He went secretly up unto Jerusalem. And at the midpoint of this feast, He entered into the Sanctuary and taught. Now the Sanctuary is the dwelling place of the One God, where love and peace, faith and chaste wisdom reside. Therefore it behooves the Hierarch of God who serves God to be peaceful and gentle, not thievish, not deceitful, not covetous of silver: so that he may first settle God in his own soul, and thereafter illumine the people according to his image and struggle. It may likewise be said of every believer, if he become a purified sanctuary, where the Lord God may dwell and abide. And all marveled at His reading, and envying Him they said: "How knows this man letters, having never learned?" For being the New Adam, He was, like the first Adam, filled with all wisdom, and also as God. Again, they all murmured, and rushed together to murder Him; but Christ, using greatness against them, said unto them as they disputed concerning the Sabbath: "Why seek ye to kill Me?" And they said: "Hast thou a demon? Who seeks to kill thee?" And He extended this further unto earlier things: "If ye dispute concerning the Law," He said, "why are ye angry with Me, because I made a man entirely whole on the Sabbath? Moses also gave this ordinance, and when circumcision falls on the Sabbath, ye circumcise." Having spoken many things unto them concerning this, and shown Himself to be the Giver of the Law and equal unto the Father, especially on the last great day of the Feast, He was pelted with stones by them; but not one stone touched Him. And passing by thence, He found a man blind from birth, and opening his eyes, He granted him sight.

It should be known that there were three great feasts among the Judeans. The first was Pascha, which was kept in the first month, having the commemoration of the passage through the Red Sea. The second was Pentecost, commemorating their dwelling in the wilderness after the passage through the Red Sea; for they remained fifty days in the wilderness until they received the Law, and also in honor of the number seven, which was held in honor among them. The third venerable feast was the Tabernacles, commemorating the tabernacle of witness, which Moses saw in the cloud upon the Mountain, and which Bezaleel the chief builder framed and erected, which was celebrated for seven days, having the memory of the ingathering of fruits and the resting in the wilderness. When that feast was being celebrated, Jesus stood and cried with a loud voice: "If anyone thirst, let him come unto Me and drink!" In that very teaching Christ showed Himself to be the Messiah, having become our Mediator and Reconciler with His eternal Father. For this reason, celebrating the present feast, and naming it Mid-Pentecost, we sing of Christ the Messiah, and set forth the venerable link between both great feasts. For this reason also, as it seems, the Feast of the Samaritan Woman is celebrated after it: because there also many things were spoken concerning Christ the Messiah, and concerning water and thirst, as here in John the Evangelist.[^630] The Sunday of the Blind Man follows that of the Samaritan Woman. By Thine unsearchable mercy, O Christ our God, have mercy and save us, now and ever and unto ages of ages. Amen.

Having destroyed the dominion of death by Thy death, O Christ, Thou hast given life unto men in Thy glorious resurrection: Thou hast raised the human race with Thyself by Thy descent; therefore we sing grateful praise unto Thee, celebrating the Feast of Thy seven-day and light-bearing resurrection, wherein the midpoint
