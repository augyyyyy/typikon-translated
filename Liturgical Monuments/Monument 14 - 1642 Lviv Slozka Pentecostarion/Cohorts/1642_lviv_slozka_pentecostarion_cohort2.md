On this same day we celebrate the resurrection of the holy and righteous friend of Christ, Lazarus of the four days. He was a Hebrew by race, of the sect of the Pharisees, and the son, as is found recorded in some accounts, of Simon the Pharisee, originating from the village of Bethany.[^56] When our Lord Jesus Christ was walking upon the earth for the salvation of our race, Lazarus was thus joined unto Him in friendship. For because Christ often conversed with Simon—who also inquired earnestly of Him concerning the resurrection of the dead—and frequently visited his house, Lazarus came to be dearly loved by Him, and not he alone, but also his two sisters, Martha and Mary. Now as the saving Passion was drawing near, wherein it was needful that the mystery of the resurrection be more clearly assured, Jesus was staying beyond the Jordan, having previously raised the daughter of Jairus

=== LEAF p19 ===

and the son of the widow from the dead. But His friend Lazarus, being seized by a grievous illness, died. Jesus therefore, though not being there, said unto the Disciples: "Lazarus our friend hath fallen asleep"; and pausing a little, He said: "Lazarus is dead." Leaving the Jordan, He came unto Bethany, having been sent for by his sisters. Now Bethany is distant from Jerusalem about fifteen stadia or furlongs. And the sisters of Lazarus met Him beforehand, saying: "If Thou hadst been here, our brother had not died; but even now, if Thou willest, Thou canst raise him up, for Thou art able." Jesus then inquired of the people: "Where have ye laid him?" And straightway all went before Him unto the tomb. And when the stone was taken away, Martha said: "Lord, by this time he stinks, for he hath been dead four days." Jesus therefore, having prayed and shed tears over him that lay there, cried out with a loud voice: "Lazarus, come forth!" And straightway he that was dead came forth; and being unbound, he went away unto his house.

This strange marvel stirred up the Jewish people unto envy, and they raged against Christ. Jesus therefore again withdrew and departed. And the chief priests took counsel to put Lazarus also to death, because many seeing him were believing in Christ. But he, perceiving their thoughts, fled unto the island of Cyprus. And dwelling there, he was later consecrated by the Apostles as Bishop of the City of Kition.[^57] Having lived worthily and in a manner pleasing unto God for thirty years after his revival, he died again, and was buried there, having wrought many wonders.

It is said that after his revival he would eat nothing except what was sweet; and that the All-Pure Mother of God, having prepared an omophorion with her own hands, bestowed it upon him.[^58] His precious and holy body was subsequently translated thence by the most wise Emperor Leo, in consequence of a certain divine vision, and reverently and sumptuously deposited in Constantinople, in the church built by him in honor of this saint, on the right hand as one enters the temple, in the wall opposite the holy altar; and even unto this day his precious body remains there, his relics working a certain ineffable sweet fragrance.[^59]

Now the celebration of his resurrection was appointed for the present day because our holy and God-bearing Fathers, and more especially the holy Apostles, desiring that after the Forty-Day Fast, through purification, we should behold the holy Passion of our Lord Jesus Christ—and seeing that they found this wonder to be the primary beginning and cause of the Jewish fury against Christ—therefore placed this supernatural marvel here. This wonder the Evangelist John alone records, the other Evangelists having omitted it because Lazarus was still alive and visible.[^60] It is also said that for this very reason the rest of the Gospel was written, and because the others had mentioned nothing concerning the unoriginate generation of Christ. For this was what was sought to be assured: that Christ was the Son of God and God, that He arose, and that there shall be a resurrection of the dead; which is assured above all through Lazarus. Lazarus related nothing concerning what was in Hades, either because it was not permitted him to see

=== LEAF p20 ===

what was there, or because, having seen it, he was commanded to keep silence concerning it. Nevertheless, even now every man who dies is called a "Lazarus," and the burial garment is called a *lazaroma*, signifying the word concerning Lazarus, so that it may come in remembrance of the first Lazarus.[^61] For if he by the word of Christ arose and lived again, so also this man, even though he has died, shall arise at the last trumpet and live unto eternity.

By the prayers of Thy friend Lazarus, O Christ God, have mercy on us, always, now and ever and unto ages of ages. Amen.
