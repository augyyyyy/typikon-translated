# PART I: LAZARUS SATURDAY, PALM SUNDAY, AND GREAT AND HOLY PASSION WEEK

## Lazarus Saturday: Vespers on Friday Evening

*At Vespers, Kathisma: "Unto the Lord in my affliction" (Kathisma 18).[^3]*
*At "Lord, I have cried," we set 10 stichoi, and chant the idiomelon stichera. Tone 8. On: "Lead forth from prison:"*[^4]

*Tone 8. Idiomelon:*
Having completed the forty days that bring profit to our souls,[^5] we entreat Thee, O Lover of mankind, to let us behold the Holy Week of Thy Passion, that in it we may glorify Thy mighty acts and Thine ineffable dispensation toward us, singing with one mind: O Lord, glory to Thee. *(Twice)*

*Then, the Martyrikon:*
O Martyrs of the Lord, pray unto our God, and entreat for our souls a multitude of compassions and cleansing from our many transgressions, we beseech you.

*Then, to Saint Lazarus. 5 stichera, repeated to make 6, idiomela. The composition of the Pious Emperor Kyr Leo the Wise.[^6] Tone 6:*

*Tone 6. Idiomelon:*
Wishing to behold the tomb of Lazarus, though by Thine own will Thou wouldest be seen in the tomb, Thou didst inquire: [continued on next leaf]

=== LEAF p6 ===

"Where have ye laid him?" And having learned that which Thou knewest full well, Thou didst call unto him whom Thou lovedst: "Lazarus, come forth!" And the lifeless corpse obeyed Him Who gives breath unto him, even Thee, the Savior of our souls. *(Twice)*

*Tone 6. Idiomelon:*
O Lord, Thou didst come unto the tomb of Lazarus who was four days dead; and shedding tears over Thy friend, Thou didst raise up the four-day-dead corpse, O Ear of Life! Therefore death was bound by Thy voice, and the grave-bands were loosed by human hands; then the multitude of the disciples was filled with joy, and from all there arose one concordant cry: Blessed art Thou, O Savior, have mercy on us! *(Twice)*

*Tone 6. Idiomelon:*
O Lord, Thy voice destroyed the kingdom of Hades, and the word of Thy sovereign power raised up from the tomb him that was four days dead; and Lazarus became a saving prefiguration of the rebirth of being. All things are possible unto Thee, as King of all: grant unto Thy servants cleansing and great mercy. *(Twice)*

*Tone 6. Idiomelon:*
O Lord, wishing to confirm Thy disciples in Thine own resurrection from the dead, Thou didst come unto the tomb of Lazarus; and when Thou didst call him forth, Hades was despoiled and released the four-day-dead man, who cried aloud unto Thee: Blessed art Thou, O Lord, glory to Thee! *(Twice)*

*Tone 6. Idiomelon:*
O Lord, taking Thy disciples with Thee, Thou didst come unto Bethany to raise Lazarus; and having wept over him according to the law of human nature, yet as God Thou didst raise him up who was four days dead; and they cried unto Thee, O Savior: Blessed art Thou, O Lord, glory to Thee!

*Glory:*
Standing before the tomb of Lazarus, O our Savior, and calling out to the dead man, Thou didst raise him up as if awakening another, by the nod of Thy secret incorruptibility; and bound in grave-bands, he came forth at Thy word. All things Thou art able to do; all things serve Thee, O Lover of mankind; all things submit unto Thee: O our Savior, glory to Thee!

*Now and ever: Tone 6:*
Having completed the forty days that bring profit to our souls, let us cry aloud: Rejoice, city of Bethany, homeland of Lazarus! Rejoice, Martha and Mary, his sisters! For tomorrow Christ comes to quicken with a word your dead brother; hearing His voice, bitter and insatiable Hades shall tremble with fear and groan exceedingly, and shall release Lazarus bound in grave-bands. Marveling at His wonder, the assembly of the Hebrews shall meet Him with boughs and branches; and the children praising Him shall appear, at whom the fathers shall look with envy: Blessed is He that comes in the name of the Lord, the King of Israel!

*Entrance with the censer. "O Joyful Light."*
*Prokeimenon. Psalm 123 (LXX).[^7] Tone 6:*
Our help is in the name of the Lord, Who made heaven and earth.
*Stichos: If the Lord had not been among us.*

*Reading from the Book of Genesis (49:29–50:26 [LXX]):*[^8]
Jacob gave commandment unto his sons; and having gathered up his feet upon his bed, he expired, and was gathered unto his people. And Joseph fell upon his father's face, and wept bitterly over him, and kissed him. And Joseph commanded his servants the undertakers to bury his father; and the undertakers buried Israel. And forty days were fulfilled for him, for so are the days of burying fulfilled; and Egypt wept for him seventy days. [continued on next leaf]

And when the days of mourning were past, Joseph spoke unto the potentates of Pharaoh, saying: If I have found grace in your sight, speak into the ears of Pharaoh, saying: My father bound me by oath before his decease, saying: In the tomb which I dug for myself in the land of Canaan, there shalt thou bury me. Now therefore let me go up and bury my father, and I will return. And Pharaoh said according to the word of Joseph. And Pharaoh said unto Joseph: Go up, bury thy father, as he bound thee by oath. And Joseph went up to bury his father; and with him went up all the servants of Pharaoh, and the elders of his house, and all the nobles of the land of Egypt, and the whole house of Joseph, and his brethren, and all his father's household, and his kinsmen; only their sheep and oxen did they leave in the land of Goshen. And chariots and horsemen went up with him, and it was a very great host. And they came unto the threshing-floor of Atad, which is beyond Jordan; and there they lamented him with a great and very sore lamentation; and he made mourning for his father seven days. And when the inhabitants of the land of Canaan saw the mourning at the threshing-floor of Atad, they said: This is a great mourning unto the Egyptians; therefore the name of that place was called "Mourning of Egypt," which is beyond Jordan. And his sons did unto him even as he commanded them; for his sons carried him into the land of Canaan, and buried him in the double cave which Abraham had acquired for the possession of a burial place from Ephron the Hittite, over against Mamre.

And Joseph returned into Egypt, he and his brethren, and all that had gone up with him to bury his father. And when Joseph's brethren saw that their father was dead, they said: Lest Joseph remember the evil we did against him, and requite us with retribution for all the evils which we showed unto him! And they drew near unto Joseph, saying: Thy father bound us by oath before his decease, saying: Thus shall ye say unto Joseph: Forgive them their injustice and their sin, for they showed evil unto thee; and now receive the injustice of the servants of the God of thy father. And Joseph wept while they spoke unto him. And they came unto him and said: Behold, we are thy servants. And Joseph said unto them: Fear not, for I am God's. Ye devised evil against me, but God devised good concerning me, that it might be brought to pass as it is this day, that many people should be nourished. And he said unto them: Fear not; I will nourish you and your households. And he comforted them, and spoke unto their heart.

And Joseph dwelt in Egypt, he and his brethren, and the whole household of his father; and Joseph lived one hundred and ten years. And Joseph saw the children of Ephraim unto the third generation; and the sons of Machir the son of Manasseh were born upon Joseph's knees. And Joseph spoke unto his brethren, saying: I die; but God will surely visit you with a visitation, and bring you out of this land unto the land which He swore unto our fathers, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. And Joseph bound the sons of Israel by oath, saying: In the visitation [continued on next leaf]

*Stichos: For the Lord will not leave the rod of sinners upon the lot of the righteous.*

*Reading from the Proverbs (31:8–31 [LXX]):*[^10]
My son, open thy mouth with the word of God, and judge all things soundly. Open thy mouth, and judge righteously, and plead the cause of the poor and weak. Who can find a valiant woman? For such an one is more precious than costly stones. The heart of her husband trusts boldly in her; such an one shall not lack good spoils. For she works good for her husband all her life, and not evil. Getting wool and flax throughout the year, she has wrought what is useful with her hands. She has become like a merchant ship trading from afar, gathering her wealth. She rises while it is yet night, and gives food to her household, and appointed tasks to her maidservants. Having surveyed a field, she bought it; and from the fruit of her hands she planted a possession. Having girded her loins with strength, she strengthened her arms for work. She tasted that working is good, and her lamp goes not out the whole night. She stretches forth her arms unto profitable things, and her hands she applies unto the spindle. Again she opens her hands to the needy, and reaches forth fruit to the poor. Her husband is not anxious concerning those in his house when he lingers elsewhere, for all that are with her are clothed. Double garments has she made for her husband, and of fine purple and scarlet are her own vesture. Her husband is renowned in the gates, whenever he sits in council with the elders, the inhabitants of the land. Linen sheets has she made, and sold them to the Phoenicians, and girdles unto the Canaanites. Strength and comeliness has she put on, and rejoices in the days to come. She opens her mouth with discretion and lawfully, and order has she appointed for her tongue. Strict are the ways of her household, and the bread of idleness she has not eaten. Her children have risen up and enriched her, and her husband has praised her: "Many daughters have wrought power, many have acquired wealth; but thou hast excelled and surpassed them all!" False charms and vain beauty of women are not in thee; for a prudent woman is blessed, and let her praise the fear of the Lord. Give her of the fruit of her lips, and let her husband be praised in the gates.

*At the Trapeza: We eat cooked food with oil, and drink wine.*

*At the sixth hour of the night (around midnight), they strike the great bell (or sufficiently upon the wooden klepalo). And we assemble in church and chant Great Compline. At the second hour of the day (8:00 AM), make the procession outside the Monastery; and having returned, chant the Hours. In place of "It is truly meet," we chant the Heirmos of the Annunciation: "As the living Ark of God."*

*Troparion:*

*Troparion:*
