*Synaxarion on Great and Holy Saturday. Father, bless us to read.*[^432]  
*Verses:*  
In vain dost thou guard the Tomb, O watch;  
For no sepulchre can contain Essential Life!  
On Great and Holy Saturday, we celebrate the divine and bodily Burial of our Lord, God, and Savior Jesus Christ, and His descent into Hades, whereby our race was recalled from corruption and passed over unto eternal life. For Holy Great Lent surpasses all other days, and this Holy and Great Week is greater than Great Lent, and this Great and Holy Saturday is greater than the whole Great Week. It is called the Great Week, not because its days or hours are longer, but because great, supernatural, and extraordinary marvels and works of our Savior were wrought therein, and most especially on this day. For just as in the first creation of the world, God, having accomplished every work, and finally on the sixth

=== LEAF p145 ===

day having created man, on the seventh day rested from all His works, and sanctified it, naming it Sabbath, which is interpreted as rest; so also in the accomplishment of the intelligible world, having wrought all things excellently, and on the sixth day having remade fallen man and renewed him by His life-bearing Cross and death, on this present Seventh Day He again rested, accomplishing a complete rest by falling asleep in a life-giving and saving sleep. The Word of God descends with His flesh into the tomb, and He also descends into Hades with His incorruptible and divine soul, which was separated by death from the body, and which He commended into the hands of the Father, unto Whom He also offered His Blood, which was our ransom, without His asking for it. For the Lord's soul was not held captive in Hades as were the souls of the other saints. How could it be? For it was not subject to the ancestral curse as they were; nor did our enemy the devil take it, although he held us, for whose sake we were bought. For how could the robber devil seize Him Who was not only from God, but was God Himself? Yet bodily our Lord Jesus Christ was seen in the tomb with His Divinity, supremely united to the flesh. He was also in Paradise with the Thief, and in Hades, as has been said, with His deified soul, and supernaturally sitting with the Father and the Spirit, as uncircumscribed God. Thus the Divinity suffered nothing in the tomb, even as It suffered nothing upon the Cross; and the Lord's Body indeed underwent corruption, which is the separation of the soul from the body, but dissolution or decay—that is to say, the decomposition of the flesh and the limbs, or complete destruction—It suffered in no wise. But Joseph, having taken down the sacred Body of the Lord, buries It in a new tomb, and in a garden near the Jews rolls a very great stone against the door of the tomb, and departs. And Mary Magdalene and the other Mary were there, sitting opposite the sepulchre.  
Now the next day, which is the day after the Preparation, the Chief Priests and Pharisees gathered together unto Pilate, saying: "Sir, we remember that that deceiver said, while he was yet alive, 'After three days I will arise.' It seems good unto us, therefore, that thy authority command the guard to secure the sepulchre." Pilate answered: "If he was a deceiver, why do ye trouble yourselves about his words while he was yet alive, since as a condemned man he is dead? When did he say: 'I will arise'?" They deduced this from the parable of Jonah. "Lest, if the tomb be not secured, he be stolen." O how foolish they were! Whatever they did against themselves, they did unwittingly for us! For Pilate having commanded, they themselves with a military detachment and careful sealing secured the sepulchre, so that the guard and seals being alien, His Resurrection could not be falsely slandered. But Hades meanwhile is overturned and driven to frenzy, having touched a stronger power; and it will soon vomit forth those who were unjustly swallowed, struck by Christ the strongest Cornerstone, whom from the ages it held devoured in its belly.  
By Thine ineffable condescension, O Christ our God,
