[^591]: The opening dialogue of Great Vespers begins with the sacerdotal blessing: "Blessed is our God, always, now and ever, and unto ages of ages," followed by the choir's "Amen," the triple invitation "Come, let us worship" (*Пріидите поклонимся*), and the recitation or chanting of the Introductory Psalm (Psalm 103 / LXX 103: *Благослови, душе моя, Господа*).
[^592]: The rubric indicates that while Psalm 103 is being chanted, the priest reads the seven silent Evening (Lucernarium) Prayers (*молитвы вечерняя* / Gk. *Εὐχαὶ λυχνικαί*) before the holy table or before the Royal Doors with uncovered head, a foundational element of the Byzantine cathedral rite of the Lucernarium.
[^593]: The Great Litany (*Ектенія великая* / Gk. *Συναπτὴ μεγάλη*), traditionally chanted by the deacon from the solea, is here designated to be recited by the priest in the altar (*гл҃етсѧ є҆кте́нїа вели́каа ѿ їере́а , въ ѡ҆лтарѝ*), reflecting the primary liturgical disposition of the Striatyn Sluzhebnik for sacerdotal use, especially when serving without a deacon.
[^594]: The royal and civil petitions in the Great Litany of the 1604 Striatyn Sluzhebnik commemorate the ruling monarch of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, King Sigismund III Vasa (reigned 1587–1632), and the local Ukrainian and Polish Orthodox and Greek-rite nobility and benefactors: "For our great king, *[Name]*, and our pious lords, and for all their palace and their host..." (*Ѽ вели́комъ кра́ли на́шемъ , І҆́мк҃ · И҆ бл҃гочести́выⷯ гпⷭрен на́шихъ · и҆ ѡ҆ въсе́й пола́тѣ и҆ вѡ́ехъ и҆́хъ*).
[^595]: The doxological exclamation concluding the Great Litany: "For unto Thee is due all glory, honor, and worship: to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit..." (*Яко подобаетъ Ти всякая слава, честь и поклоненіе...*), standard in all recensions of the Byzantine Rite.
[^596]: Following the Great Litany, the rubric prescribes the chanting or reading of the Kathisma (*Та́ каⷪ҇* = *Таже каѳизма*), typically the first kathisma of the Psalter ("Blessed is the man", Pss. 1–3) on Saturday evening for Sunday Vespers. The rubric notes that after the second stasis or "Glory" (*по второ́ же сла́вѣ каді́змы*), the deacon enters the altar to vest.
[^597]: The rubric instructs the deacon to make a metania (bow) to the hegumen / superior or toward his stall, enter the altar, vest in the sticharion (*стиха́рь*), place the orarion (*ѡ҆ра́*) on his left shoulder, and exit onto the solea to chant the Little Litany (*дїа́конства мала́а* / Gk. *Μικρὰ συναπτή*).
[^598]: The special rubric provides the rubrical variant for Dominical Feasts on which the Kathisma is omitted (*А҆́ще же слꙋчи́тсѧ пра́зникъ гпⷭдьскый , въ не́мже не быва́етъ каⷪ҇ве*): while Psalm 103 is recited, the deacon vests immediately and intones the Great Litany outside the altar on the solea; then "Lord, I have cried" is begun directly.
[^599]: The Little Entrance of Vespers (*Малый входъ* / Gk. *Εἴσοδος*): during the singing of the Theotokion or Dogmatikon at "Lord, I have cried" (*на Сла̀ И҆ нн҃*), the deacon and priest proceed around the holy table and through the north deacon's door into the nave, holding the censer.
[^600]: The Entrance Prayer of Vespers (*Молитва входа*): "O Good King and Lover of mankind, Who doest good unto all things: we entreat Thee diligently, with a contrite heart and a humble spirit..." (*Благій человѣколюбче царю, благійвый всяческая: молимтися прилежно...*). This prayer blesses the entrance and exit of the clergy and commemorates the saving advent and sojourning of Christ among mankind.
[^601]: The ancient 3rd-century Lucernarium hymn *Phos Hilaron* (*Свѣ́те ти́хїи* / "O Gladsome Light"), followed by the daily or festal Prokeimenon, marking the transition from sunset to the liturgical lighting of the evening lamps.
[^602]: The rubric regulates the chanting of the Old Testament readings (Paremias / *паримі́а*), opening with the diaconal proclamation "Wisdom!" (*Премудрость*), the reader's announcement of the source book (e.g., "The Reading from Genesis" / *Ѿ бытїа̀ чьте́нїе*), and the diaconal call to attention: "Let us attend!" (*Вонмемъ*).
[^603]: The Augmented or Triple Litany (*дїа́конства сꙋгꙋ́баѧ* / *Ектенія сугубая* / Gk. *Ἐκτενὴς ἱκεσία*), beginning with the earnest exhortation: "Let us all say: from our whole soul and from our whole mind, let us say!" (*Рцемъ вси...*).
[^604]: The royal petition of the Augmented Litany commemorations in the Striatyn print includes the Polish-Lithuanian King Sigismund III Vasa and the Crown nobility (*па́нѡвъ на́шихъ*). A contemporary 17th-century scribal hand has added marginal annotations: glossing `пребыванїа` with `състоянїи`, and noting `туймо` in the margin, illustrating active liturgical use in a Ruthenian parish or monastery.
[^605]: The monastic alternative rubric: `А҆́ще є҆́стъ въ ѡ҆би́тели , гл҃и сїѐ` ("If it be in a monastery, say this:"), which inserts a specific petition for the hegumen by name (*ѻ҃цоу на́шемоу , и҆гꙋ́меноу , І҆́мк҃*) and the monastic brotherhood.
[^606]: The secular parish alternative rubric: `А҆́ще же не въ мона́стыри · рьци́ се` ("If it be not in a monastery, say this:"), substituting the monastic petition with a broader commemoration of "our spiritual fathers" (*ѡ҆ ѻ҃це́хъ на́ши дх҃ѡ́вныхъ*) and "all our brotherhood in Christ" (*въсемоу́ є҆́же ѡ҆ х҃ѣ бра́тствоу на́шемоу*), pointing directly to the urban lay brotherhoods (*братства*) that formed the backbone of Ruthenian church life under the Polish Crown.
[^607]: The evening hymn/prayer *Kataxioson* (*Сподоби, Господи, въ вечеръ сей безъ грѣха сохранитися намъ* / "Vouchsafe, O Lord, to keep us this evening without sin"), an ancient evening prayer attested in the Apostolic Constitutions (Book VII, 48), read by the superior or appointed reader.
[^608]: The Evening Litany of Supplication (*Ектенія просительная* / Gk. *Αἰτήσεις*): "Let us complete our evening prayer unto the Lord" (*Исполнимъ вечерняя молитвы наша Господеви*), requesting an angel of peace, forgiveness of sins, and a Christian ending to life.
[^609]: The petition for the completion of our life in peace and repentance: `Про́чее лѣ́то живота̀ на́шего въ смѣре́нїи , и҆ покаа́нїи сконча́ти...` The Striatyn text uniquely reads `въ смѣре́нїи` ("in humility/peace") where standard Nikonian and modern editions read `въ мирѣ и покаяніи` ("in peace and repentance").
[^610]: The Prayer at the Bowing of Heads (*Молитва главопреклоненія*): "O Lord our God, Who didst bow the heavens and come down for the salvation of the human race..." (*Господи Боже нашъ, преклонивый небеса и сошедый на спасеніе рода человѣческаго...*), recalling Psalm 143:5 (LXX 143:5: *Господи, преклони небеса и сниди*).
[^611]: The rubric for the procession to the narthex for the Litiya (*Литія* / Gk. *Λιτή*), prescribing that two candle-bearers precede the clergy (*пре́идꙋщима двѣ́ма свѣти́лникѡма*) as they exit the nave.
[^612]: The term `пра́пратъ` (from Old Church Slavonic *прапрада* / Greek *πρόναος* / *πρόπυλον*) is the historical Ruthenian and South Slavic liturgical term for the narthex or church porch (*притворъ*), where the Litiya and Artoklasia are traditionally celebrated at Great Vespers.
[^613]: The deacon's great intercessory prayer of the Litiya: "Save, O God, Thy people, and bless Thine inheritance..." (*Спаси, Боже, люди Твоя, и благослови достояніе Твое...*), said while standing in the midst of the narthex facing east.
[^614]: The phrase "exalt the horn of Christians" (*възвы́си ро́гъ хрⷭтїа́нскый*) employs the Biblical idiom of the "horn" as a symbol of divine strength, dignity, and salvation (cf. 1 Sam. 2:1, 10; Ps. 88:18, 25 [LXX 88:17, 24]; Luke 1:69). The Striatyn imprint preserves the unexpanded pre-Nikonian reading `ро́гъ хрⷭтїа́нскый` rather than the later Russian standard `ро́гъ православныхъ христіанъ`.
