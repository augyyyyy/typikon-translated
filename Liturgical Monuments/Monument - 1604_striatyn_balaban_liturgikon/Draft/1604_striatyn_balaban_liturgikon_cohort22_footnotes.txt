[^576]: The concise invitation "Draw near!" (*Приступите*) preserves the archaic pre-Nikonian form of the Greek *Μετὰ φόβου Θεοῦ... προσέλθετε* (cf. the expanded modern recension: *Со страхомъ Божіимъ и вѣрою приступите*). It is immediately followed by the communion verse from Psalm 33:2 (LXX 33:1): "I will bless the Lord at all times; His praise shall continually be in my mouth."
[^577]: The secret formula spoken by the priest as he places the diskos upon the deacon's head: "Blessed is God Who enlightens and sanctifies us" (*Благословенъ Богъ просвѣщаяй и освящаяй насъ*). The transfer of the holy vessels to the table of oblation (prothesis / proskomedia) is carried out in reverent silence, and the hymn "Let our mouths be filled" is sung only after the vessels are deposited there.
[^578]: The post-communion thanksgiving hymn "Let our mouth be filled with Thy praise, O Lord" (*Да исполнятся уста наша хваленія Твоего, Господи*), sung by the choir while the priest returns to the holy table and folds the eileton.
[^579]: The Prayer Behind the Ambo (*Заамвонная молитва*) of the Presanctified Liturgy, traditionally attributed to Saint Gregory Dialogos: "O Master Almighty, Who hast created all creation in wisdom..." (*Владыко Вседержителю, иже всю тварь мудростію содѣлавъ...*), which recalls the forty days of fasting and Moses on Mount Sinai (Exod. 24:18; 34:28).
[^580]: The Prayer at the Consumption of the Holy Gifts (*Молитва потребити святыя дары*): "O Lord our God, Who hast brought us unto these all-pure days..." (*Господи Боже нашъ, введый насъ въ пречистыя дни сія...*). The Striatyn text reads `сушныхъ твоихъ таинъ` (fol. 215r / p. 418), an epigraphic compositor form for `соущныхъ` / `сꙋ́щныхъ` (spiritual or essential mysteries; Gk. *νοερῶν μυστηρίων*).
[^581]: The distribution of antidoron (*раздаетъ андідоръ*, from Gk. *ἀντίδωρον*, "instead of the gift"), the blessed bread from the prosphora cuttings distributed to the faithful at the conclusion of the Liturgy, followed by the pastoral blessing: "The blessing of the Lord be upon you all..."
[^582]: The polychronion rubric (*ликове многолѣтствуютъ краля и архіереа аще есть*) prescribes chanting "Many Years" for King Sigismund III Vasa of Poland (reigned 1587–1632) and the local ruling bishop, Hedeon Balaban of Lviv, Galicia, and Kamianets-Podilskyi (1569–1607), illustrating the dual civil and ecclesiastical commemorations typical of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth.
[^583]: The Dismissal Troparion of Saint Gregory Dialogos, Pope of Rome, in Tone 8: "O teacher of Orthodoxy, instructor of piety and purity..." (*Православію наставниче, благочестія учителю и чистотѣ...*), which is the standard Common Troparion for a Hierarch (*Общій святителю*), adapted here for St. Gregory.
[^584]: The formal colophon marking the conclusion of the Divine Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts of Saint Gregory Dialogos, Pope of Rome (*Конецъ божественныя літургіа прежде священныя святаго Григоріа діалога папы римскаго*), terminating Part III of the 1604 Striatyn Liturgikon.
[^585]: Here begins Part IV of the Liturgikon, comprising liturgical appendices, blessings, variable dismissals, and sacerdotal orders. The first appendicular rite is the Order of Blessing Wine (*Чинъ благословити вино*), traditionally used during Great Lent or following the Presanctified Liturgy to sanctify wine for refreshment after fasting.
[^586]: The rubric `Тропарѐ · н҃е · и҆ ᲂу҆па́кои гласо́въ ѡ҆смѝ въсѣ̑мъ` refers to the Resurrectional Troparia and Hypakoe of the Eight Tones (*Тропари недѣльны иѵпакои гласовъ осми*), sung according to the current Tone of the week.
[^587]: The First Prayer of the Blessing of Wine commemorates Christ's first miracle at the wedding in Cana of Galilee (John 2:1–11), praying that the wine may be protected from any demonic operation (*безъ всякія непріязнинныя дѣтели*).
[^588]: The Second Prayer of the Blessing of Wine invokes three Biblical types of healing and sanctification: the well of Jacob at Sychar (John 4:5–14), the pool of Siloam (John 9:7), and the cup of the holy apostles at the Mystical Supper (Matt. 26:27; Luke 22:20).
[^589]: Folio 220v (p. [428v]) is unprinted in the 1604 Striatyn imprint, serving as a physical divisional break between the appendicular blessings and the sacerdotal Lucernarium orders that follow.
[^590]: The Lucernarium Order (*Послѣдованіе свѣщное*, from Gk. *Ἡ ἀκολουθία τοῦ λυχνικοῦ* / Lat. *lucernarium*), the liturgical order of the lighting of lamps at Great Vespers (*на велицѣи вечерни*) and All-Night Vigils (*на бдѣніихъ*), containing the priest's secret prayers and opening litanies.
