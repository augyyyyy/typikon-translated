[^219]: The Cherubic Hymn (Херꙋві́мскаѧ пѣ́снь: И҆́же херꙋві́мы та́йнѣ ѡ҆бразꙋ́юще...). The celebrants recite the hymn together with three profound bows before departing to the Prothesis table (та́йнѣ = mystically; ѿве́ржемъ печа́ль = put away/cast aside worldly care).
[^220]: The angelic escort (dorophoria, а҆́гг҃льскими неви́димо дариноси́ма чи́нми), drawing upon the Byzantine military-imperial imagery of spear-bearers bearing the triumphant Emperor upon shields.
[^221]: The celebrants proceed to the Prothesis (приложенїе / проскомидїа) to transfer the prepared bread and wine to the main altar. The deacon censes the gifts, and both clergy pray the humble formula of the Publican (Luke 18:13): "O God, cleanse me a sinner, and have mercy on me."
[^222]: The deacon bids the priest "Lift up, master" (Възмѝ влⷵко), whereupon the priest places the great veil (aer, а҆е́ръ) upon the deacon's left shoulder, quoting Psalm 133:2 [LXX: 133:2; MT: 134:2]: "Lift up your hands unto the holies, and bless the Lord."
[^223]: The priest places the holy diskos upon the deacon's head with all reverence, while the deacon holds the censer by one finger, symbolizing the heavenly bearing of Christ the King.
[^224]: The order of the Great Entrance procession: two taper-bearers precede, followed by the deacon carrying the holy diskos on his head and censer on one finger, and the priest carrying the holy chalice in his hands, exiting via the north deacon's door into the nave.
[^225]: The Great Entrance petition of the deacon ("May the Lord God remember all of you in His kingdom..."), followed by the solemn dialogue at the Holy Doors quoting Psalm 23:7 [LXX: 23:7; MT: 24:7]: "Lift up your gates, ye princes, and be ye lifted up, ye everlasting gates, and the King of Glory shall enter in," with the deacon replying with Psalm 117:26–27 [LXX: 117:26–27; MT: 118:26–27]: "Blessed is He that cometh in the Name of the Lord; God is the Lord and hath appeared unto us."
[^226]: Placement of the Holy Gifts upon the holy Table (antimension): the priest places the chalice, takes the diskos from the deacon's head and sets it down, and removes the small veils from both vessels, placing them at the corner of the altar table. (Note: in the physical binding of this copy, the ritual continuation—covering with the aer, reciting "The noble Joseph," and censing—is preserved on Folio 71r [p. 127], which was misbound in Leaf p66).
[^227]: Quire binding transposition in the physical facsimile: the facing right page is Folio 64r (p. 113, quire signature З), representing the outer half-sheet of Quire Zeta transposed to the interior. Textually, it contains the doxology of the First Prayer of the Faithful and the Second Litany of the Faithful (Пе́рваѧ / Втора́ѧ ектенїа̀ вѣ́рныхъ).
[^228]: Petitions of the Second Litany of the Faithful for the holy monastery (or city/countryside), temperate weather, abundance of the fruits of the earth, and deliverance from all affliction, wrath, danger, and necessity.
[^229]: Conclusion of the Second Litany of the Faithful and the opening of the Second Prayer of the Faithful (Втора́ѧ мл҃тва вѣ́рныхъ: Па́ки и҆ мно́гажды...), recited by the priest when celebrating without a deacon. (This prayer continues directly onto Folio 65r [misnumbered 125], bound in Leaf p67).
[^230]: Quire boundary transition: the facing right page is Folio 72r (p. 129, signature И / Eta 1), which directly continues the post-Entrance rubrics from Folio 71v (p. 128, misbound in Leaf p67).
[^231]: The solemn dialogue between celebrant and deacon before the closed sanctuary: the deacon inclines his head, holding his orarion with three fingers of his right hand, and asks the priest to pray for him; the priest blesses him with the words of the Annunciation (Luke 1:35): "The Holy Spirit shall come upon thee, and the power of the Highest shall overshadow thee."
[^232]: Concluding blessing of the deacon by the priest ("May the Lord God remember thee in His kingdom..."). The deacon kisses the priest's right hand and goes out to the ambo for the Litany of Completion / Supplication (Ектенїа̀ проси́тельнаѧ: И҆сплъ́нимъ мл҃твы на́ша...).
[^233]: The Petitions of the Litany of Completion over the precious gifts that have been set forth (ѡ҆ предложе́нныхъ чⷵтныхъ дарѣ́хъ), interceding for the temple, the worshipping faithful, and deliverance from all distress.
[^234]: The Prayer of the Prothesis / Prayer after the Setting Down of the Divine Gifts (Мл҃тва по поставле́нїи на ст҃ѣ́й трапе́зѣ бж҃твенныхъ дарѡ́въ: Гн҃и бж҃е вседержи́телю, є҆ди́не ст҃е...), secretly recited by the priest at the altar, imploring God to accept this spiritual sacrifice for personal sins and the people's transgressions (echoing Hebrews 5:1–3 and 9:7).
[^235]: Continuation of the Prayer of the Prothesis: asking that the good Spirit of grace may rest upon the celebrants, upon the oblation set forth, and upon all the people.
[^236]: The Petitions of Supplication chanted by the deacon (Aitarches/angelic petitions): asking for a day that is perfect, holy, peaceful, and sinless; an angel of peace; and remission of sins and transgressions.
[^237]: Epigraphic note on physical damage: the bottom outer margin of Folio 74v (p. 134) has suffered paper loss, mended with antique paper backing; the fragmentary petitions for the Christian ending of life ("painless, blameless, peaceful") and a good defense before the dread judgment seat of Christ are securely restored from standard Ruthenian recensional witnesses.
[^238]: The Commemoration of the Most Holy Theotokos and all saints, committing ourselves, one another, and our whole life unto Christ our God, followed by the priest's exclamation glorifying the compassions of the Only-begotten Son.
[^239]: Liturgical greeting of peace: the priest blesses the congregation ("Peace be unto all"), and the choir responds ("And to thy spirit"), preparing the assembly for the mutual reconciliation of the Kiss of Peace.
[^240]: The Deacon's call to love and concord: "Let us love one another, that with one mind we may confess," and the choir's confession of the consubstantial and undivided Trinity (Father, Son, and Holy Spirit).
[^241]: The celebrant's personal preparation for the Kiss of Peace: bowing three times secretly at the altar and reciting Psalm 17:2–3 [LXX: 17:2–3; MT: 18:1–2]: "I will love Thee, O Lord, my strength; the Lord is my support, and my refuge."
[^242]: The ritual Kiss of Peace among concelebrating clergy: the celebrant kisses the veiled holy gifts (diskos, chalice, and altar edge); when concelebrating, the superior gives the greeting "Christ is in our midst" (Хрⷵто́съ посредѣ̀ на́съ), and the respondent answers "He is and shall be" (И҆ є҆́сть и҆ бꙋ́детъ); deacons likewise kiss the cross embroidered on their orarions and each other.
[^243]: The deacon on the ambo likewise bows, kisses the cross on his orarion, and proclaims the ancient guard of the sanctuary doors: "The doors! The doors! In wisdom let us give heed!" (Две́ри две́ри премꙋ́дростїю вънмемъ), commanding that the church doors be closed against unbelievers and catechumens as the Creed begins.
[^244]: The Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed (Symbol of Faith, И҆сповѣ́данїе вѣ́ры / Вѣ́рꙋю), chanted by the choir and recited by all the faithful: Articles 1–4, confessing God the Father Almighty, the consubstantial Only-begotten Son, the Incarnation of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary, and the Crucifixion under Pontius Pilate.
[^245]: The Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed (Articles 5–8): confessing Christ's Burial, Resurrection on the third day according to the Scriptures, Ascension into heaven, session at the right hand of the Father, and future coming in glory to judge the living and the dead; and the Holy Spirit, Lord and Giver of Life, Who proceedeth from the Father (strictly without Filioque, conforming to the authentic Eastern Orthodox text).
[^246]: Conclusion of the Creed (Articles 9–12): confessing one holy, catholic, and apostolic Church; one baptism for the remission of sins; the resurrection of the dead; and the life of the world to come.
[^247]: The deacon's solemn call immediately following the Creed, opening the Eucharistic Anaphora: "Let us stand well, let us stand with fear; let us give heed to offer the holy oblation in peace" (Ста́нѣмъ до́брѣ, ста́нѣмъ съ стра́хомъ; вънмѣ́мъ ст҃ое възноше́нїе съ ми́ромъ приноси́ти), to which the choir responds with the sacrifice of praise: "A mercy of peace, a sacrifice of praise" (Млⷵть ми́ръ, же́ртвꙋ хвале́нїа).
