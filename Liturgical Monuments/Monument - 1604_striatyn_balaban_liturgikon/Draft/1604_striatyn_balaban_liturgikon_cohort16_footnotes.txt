[^461]: Fol. 150v (p. 288). The preface to the Triumphal Hymn (Sanctus) in the Anaphora of Saint Basil the Great, evoking the nine angelic choirs and celestial hierarchies: "angels, archangels, thrones, dominions, principalities, authorities, powers, and the many-eyed Cherubim; before Thee stand round about the Seraphim, six-winged" (*а҆́гг҃ли , а҆рха́гг҃ли , прⷵтѡ́ли , гдⷵствїа , нача̑ла , вла́сти , си́лы , и҆ мнѡ́гоѡ҆чи́таꙗ херꙋві́мъ · тебѣ̀ предсто́ѧтъ ѡ҆́крꙋгъ серафі́ми , шестокрила́тїи*), echoing Isaiah 6:2-3 and Ezekiel 1:6. Note the early printed morphology in the witness: *покрыва́ю* (active indicative without final *-тъ*).

[^462]: Fol. 151r (p. 289). The priest's exclamation introducing the Sanctus: "Singing the triumphal hymn, shouting, crying aloud, and saying" (*Побѣ́дноую пѣ́снь въспѣва́юще , въпїю́ще , въззыва́юще , и҆ глаго́люще*), followed by the choral Trisagion (*Свѧ́тъ, свѧ́тъ, свѧ́тъ Госпо́дь Саваѡ́ѳъ...*), uniting the angelic hymn of Isaiah 6:3 with the Messianic entry acclamation of Matthew 21:9.

[^463]: Fol. 151v (p. 290). The post-Sanctus prayer of the Anaphora of Saint Basil: "With these blessed powers, O Master, Lover of mankind, we sinners also cry aloud and say: Holy art Thou in truth and all-holy..." (*Съ си́ми бл҃женными си́лами влⷣко чл҃колюбче...*). The contemporary printed text reads *великоуслѣ́пїю сты́нѧ ти* ("magnificence of Thy holiness"), where the corrector supplied the genitive *твое́ѧ* in red cinnabar in the lower margin to restore the full phrase *великолѣ́пїю сты́ни твое́ѧ* (Greek: τῆς μεγαλοπρεπείας τῆς ἁγιωσύνης σου).

[^464]: Fol. 152r (p. 291). The creation and original dignity of man: fashioned from the dust of the earth and honored with the divine image (Gen. 1:26-27, 2:7), placed in the paradise of delight (Gen. 2:8, 15) with the promise of immortality, followed by the tragic transgression and disobedience of the Fall (Gen. 3:1-6).

[^465]: Fol. 152v (p. 292). The expulsion from paradise and the economy of redemption: man was led astray by the deceit of the serpent, slain by his own sins, and cast out into this world by God's righteous judgment (Gen. 3:19, 23-24). Yet God prepared salvation through the new birth in Christ. The contemporary corrector added *ти* in red in the left margin next to *сꙋ́домъ бж҃е* to emend the syntax to *сꙋ́домъ твои́мъ Бо́же ти* (or *Бо́жиимъ*).

[^466]: Fol. 153r (p. 293). The providential preparation for the Messiah in the Old Testament: God visiting mankind in diverse manners through the tender mercy of His compassions (Heb. 1:1), sending prophets, working mighty deeds through the saints, and foretelling the salvation to come. The contemporary corrector made several precise red cinnabar revisions: adding *илъ єси* in the right margin after *посѣти́* to form the past tense *посѣти́лъ є҆сѝ*; subpuncting *за млⷵди* with red deletion dots to replace it with *ми́лости твое́ѧ*; and glossing *х҃ и҆ ро́дѣ* in the margin.

[^467]: Fol. 153v (p. 294). The Incarnation of the Son in the fullness of time (Gal. 4:4; Heb. 1:1-3; Phil. 2:6). The margins contain extensive early Modern Ruthenian/Slavonic theological annotations in red ink: *сїѧ́нїе* (glossing *ѡ҆заре́нїе* / απαύγασμα), *ѡ҆́бразъ* and *начеръта́нїе сосꙋта́ва твое́го* (glossing *ѡ҆́бразъ ѵ҆поста́си твое́ѧ* / χαρακτὴρ τῆς ὑποστάσεως αὐτοῦ), documenting scholarly collation with Greek patristic terms.

[^468]: Fol. 154r (p. 295). The theological mystery of the Kenosis: Christ emptied Himself, taking the form of a servant (Phil. 2:7-8), and assumed our mortal body that He might conform us to the image of His glory (Phil. 3:21; Rom. 5:12). The right margin contains contemporary red glosses: *излїꙗ* (glossing *истощи́въ себѐ*) and *ѡ҆́бразъ* (glossing *зра́къ ра́бїи*).

[^469]: Fol. 154v (p. 296). The Nativity of the Son from the Virgin Theotokos under the Law (Gal. 4:4), condemning sin in the flesh (Rom. 8:3) so that all who died in Adam might be made alive in Christ (1 Cor. 15:22). Contemporary red marginalia supply variants: *рожсеа ѿ* (glossing *бывъ и҆з жены̀*), *и҆сꙋдити / ѡсꙋдити / ѡсꙋдивъ* (glossing *и҆сꙋди грѣ́хъ*), and *избранны* (glossing *лю́ди бога́тыꙗ*, rendering Titus 2:14, *λαὸν περιούσιον* / "a peculiar / chosen people").

[^470]: Fol. 155r (p. 297). The soteriological sequence: baptism by water and the Spirit, Christ giving Himself as a ransom unto death (1 Tim. 2:6), descending through the Cross into Hades (1 Pet. 3:19), loosing the pangs of death (Acts 2:24), and rising on the third day as the pioneer of the resurrection of all flesh.

[^471]: Fol. 155v (p. 298). Christ as the Author of life (Acts 3:15), the firstfruits of them that slept (1 Cor. 15:20), and the firstborn from the dead (Col. 1:18), ascended to the right hand of the Father on high (Heb. 1:3) and coming again to judge the world. The left margin has a red note *тлен* glossing *тлѣ́нїа*. Christ left behind the Eucharistic memorials of His saving Passion (*ѡ҆ста́ви же на́мъ въспоми́нанїе сп҃си́телныꙗ своꙗ̀ стра́сти*).

[^472]: Fol. 156r (p. 299). The Words of Institution over the Bread: Christ giving Himself up for the life of the world (John 6:51), taking bread into His holy and immaculate hands, showing it unto the Father, giving thanks, blessing, sanctifying, and breaking it. The rubric instructs the priest to bow his head.

[^473]: Fol. 156v (p. 300). The Words of Institution over the Bread uttered aloud: "Take, eat, this is My Body, which is broken for you for the remission of sins" (*Прїи́мѣ́те , ꙗ҆ди́те , сѐ є҆́стъ тѣ́ло моѐ, є҆́же за вы̀ ломи́мое въ ѡ҆ставле́нїе грѣ́хѡвъ*). The choir responds "Amen," and the rubric directs the priest and deacon to observe the same order as in the Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom.

[^474]: Fol. 157r (p. 301). The Words of Institution over the Chalice: "Drink of it, all of you, this is My Blood of the New Testament..." (*Пі́ите ѿ неꙗ̀ въсѝ, се́ є҆ крѡ́вь моꙗ̀ нова́го завѣ́та...*). The rubric specifies that the priest holds his hand elevated above the cup with fear, without making the sign of the cross (*рꙋ́коу и҆мѣ́ѧи съвы́ше съ стра́хомъ не бл҃влѧ́ꙗ глаго́летъ*), preserving an important pre-Nikonian rubrical distinction.

[^475]: Fol. 157v (p. 302). The Anamnesis of Saint Basil the Great: "Do this in remembrance of Me; for as often as ye eat this bread and drink this cup, ye proclaim My death, ye confess My resurrection" (1 Cor. 11:24-26), recalling the Passion, the life-giving Cross, and the three-day burial.

[^476]: Fol. 158r (p. 303). The Anaphora elevation: "Thine own of Thine own we offer unto Thee, in all things and for all things" (*Твоꙗ̑ ѿ твои́хъ тебѣ̀ приносѧ́ще , ѡ҆ въсѣ́хъ и҆ за въсѧ̑*), accompanied by the solemn choral hymn *Тебѐ пое́мъ, тебѐ бл҃гослови́мъ...* The priest bows his head to begin the secret prayer of the Epiklesis.

[^477]: Fol. 158v (p. 304). The Epiklesis prayer of Saint Basil the Great: confessing personal unworthiness, relying wholly upon the divine mercy and compassions, and approaching the altar to offer the antitypes (*проти́вꙋѡ҆́бразнаа* / τὰ ἀντίτυπα) of the holy Body and Blood of Christ.

[^478]: Fol. 159r (p. 305). The invocation of the Holy Spirit: praying that the Spirit may come upon the assembly and upon the gifts set forth, to bless, sanctify, and reveal them (*бл҃ви́ти ꙗ҆, и҆ ѡ҆ст҃ити, и҆ показа́ти*). The deacon sets aside the liturgical fan (rhipidion) or veil and draws near to the priest to participate in the consecratory dialogue.

[^479]: Fol. 160v (p. 308). Physical lacuna in the codex: Leaf 161 (printed pages 306–307, quire signature `т г`) is absent in this exemplar between Leaf p159 and Leaf p160. This missing folio contained the diaconal dialogue for the blessing of the Bread (*Бласлови, владыко, святый хлеб...*) and the first part of the blessing of the Cup. The text resumes mid-sentence at the top of Fol. 160v (p. 308) with the completion of the consecration of the Chalice: *...кро́вь г҃а и҆ б҃а и҆ сп҃са на́шего і҃у х҃а̀* ("...the Blood of our Lord and God and Savior Jesus Christ"). The omission is corroborated codicologically by the sequence of quire signatures (`т`, `т в`, [gap], `т д`) and the jump in modern library pencil foliation from 160 to 162.

[^480]: Fol. 161r (p. 309). The Post-Epiklesis intercession: praying for unity among all communicants in the one Holy Spirit (1 Cor. 10:17), that the mystery may not be received unto judgment or condemnation (1 Cor. 11:29), but that they may obtain mercy and grace with all the saints from the beginning of the world.
