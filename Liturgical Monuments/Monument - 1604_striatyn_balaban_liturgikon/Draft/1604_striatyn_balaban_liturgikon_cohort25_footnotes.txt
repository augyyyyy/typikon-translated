[^642]: The traditional monastic formula of mutual forgiveness (*Бл҃гослови́те мѧ̀ ѻ҃тцы ст҃і́и и҆ прости́те мѧ̀ грѣ́шнаго*), recited by the celebrant at the conclusion of Compline and the Midnight Office.
[^643]: The reciprocal mutual forgiveness exchanged quietly (*ле́гко*) among all the monastics or faithful in choir (*Та́кожде и҆ въси къ себѣ̀ то́же глю́тъ*), following the ancient Sabbaitic and Studite monastic custom.
[^644]: The opening intercessory petition for the civil authorities: *Помо́лимсѧ ѡ҆ бл҃гоꙋти́выхъ и҆ бг҃омъ храни́мыхъ цр҃е́хъ и҆ кнѧзѣ́ на́ши*. The word *бл҃гоꙋти́выхъ* is a typographical variant in the Striatyn edition for *бл҃гочести́выхъ* ("pious" or "devout").
[^645]: Throughout this intercessory litany, the choir responds to each successive invocation with the formula: "May God forgive and have mercy on them" (*Бг҃ъ да прости́тъ и҆ поми́лꙋетъ и҆́хъ* / *Бг҃ъ да прости́ и҆ поми́лꙋетъ ѧ҃*), characteristic of the Slavic post-Compline and Midnight Office intercessions.
[^646]: The term *кти́торы* (from Byzantine Greek κτήτωρ) designates the founders, benefactors, and legal patrons of the monastery or church.
[^647]: The Slavonic text reads *Ѡ҆ недꙋ́гꙋющи и҆ зажа́щи*, where *зажа́щи* represents those held in restraint, custody, or captivity (derived from *зажати* / *задержати*, parallel to *плѣне́нныѧ* / αἰχμαλώτων).
[^648]: The rubric directs that on Sundays following the intercessions, the reader recites the title/incipit of the Epistle reading (*написа́нїе а҆пл҃а*), introducing an instructional patristic or Scriptural reading before the subsequent office.
[^649]: The priest imparts the traditional blessing (*За мл҃твъ ст҃ы́хъ ѡ҃цъ на́шихъ...*) authorizing the reader to begin the appointed instructional reading.
[^650]: If it be an ordinary ferial day (*а҆́ще нѣ́сть недѣ́лѧ , нѝ нарочи́тъ ст҃ы́й*), the service proceeds immediately to the solemn commemoration of the departed (*Помѧне́мъ правосла́вныа...*).
[^651]: The solemn liturgical singing of "Eternal memory" (*Вѣ́чнаѧ и҆́мъ па́мѧ*) concluding the commemorations of departed rulers, hierarchs, benefactors, and brethren.
[^652]: A woodcut headpiece and title in ornate Cyrillic vyaz: *Послѣдова́нїе всенощное* ("The Order of the All-Night Vigil"). [^654]: Psalm 19 (LXX 19:2–10; Masoretic 20:1–9): "The Lord give ear unto thee in the day of affliction..." The first of the two Royal Psalms recited at the beginning of the introductory Royal Office of Matins.

[^658]: The Theotokion of the Royal Office (*Предстательство страшное и непостыдное...*), sung after "Both now and ever, and unto ages of ages."
[^659]: The priest proclaims the solemn initial doxology of the All-Night Vigil Matins (*Сла́ва ст҃ѣ́й є҆диносꙋ́щнѣй и҆ животворѧ́щой и҆ нераздѣли́мѣй трⷪ҇ици...*) with a loud voice (*съ гла́сомъ*), making the sign of the cross with the censer before the Holy Table.
[^660]: The Six Psalms (Hexapsalmos: Psalms 3, 37, 62, 87, 102, and 142 LXX), beginning with the Angelic Hymn from Luke 2:14: "Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will toward men."
[^661]: Psalm 87 (LXX 87:2; Masoretic 88:1): "O Lord God of my salvation, I have cried day and night before Thee." During the second half of the Hexapsalmos, beginning at this psalm, the priest recites the twelve Morning Prayers.
[^662]: The Striatyn Liturgikon specifies that the priest says the twelve Morning Prayers secretly within the altar (*гле́тъ і҆ере́й млт҃вы ᲂу҆́трнѣѧ , въ ѡ҆лтарѝ*), whereas post-Nikonian rubrics generally direct the priest to recite them bareheaded before the closed Royal Doors in the nave.
[^663]: Psalm 117:27 (LXX 117:27; Masoretic 118:27): "God is the Lord, and hath appeared unto us; blessed is he that cometh in the Name of the Lord."

[^665]: The Resurrectional Troparia (*Тропари по Непорочнах* / Evlogitaria of the Resurrection), beginning with the refrain *Благословен еси, Господи, научи мя оправданием Твоим* and the first troparion *Ангельский собор удивися* ("The assembly of the Angels was amazed...").

[^671]: The solemn prayer of intercession: *Спаси, Боже, люди Твоя, и благослови достояние Твое...* ("O God, save Thy people, and bless Thine inheritance..."), with twelve repetitions of "Lord, have mercy" (*Г҃и поми́лꙋй , в҃і*).
[^672]: The Matins Canon, consisting of nine odes (with Ode 2 traditionally omitted except during Great Lent). The deacon performs a full censing of the altar and church at Ode 1 and Ode 9.

[^674]: The Sunday Exapostilarion hymn: *Свят Господь Бог наш* ("Holy is the Lord our God," Psalm 98:5, 9 LXX), proclaimed by the deacon on Sundays before the Photagogicon / Exapostilarion.
