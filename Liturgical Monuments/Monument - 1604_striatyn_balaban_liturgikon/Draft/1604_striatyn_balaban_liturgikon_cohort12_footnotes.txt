[^356]: Rubric for the faithful approaching Holy Communion: "And all in order, even as the priest himself said beforehand: And thus they come one by one..." (*I vse poriadku, iako-zhe i sam ierei prezhde hlalal est...* / И҆ всѐ порѧ́дкꙋ, ꙗ҆́коже и҆ са́мъ і҆ере́и пре́жде гла́лалъ є҆́стъ), referring to the corporate recitation of the pre-communion prayers led by the celebrant on fol. 108r.
[^357]: Ceremonial posture for receiving the Holy Mysteries: approaching with all compunction and fear, with hands folded crosswise upon the breast (*sъgnkennê rutsi k persemъ imiashchy* / съгнкеннѣ̀ рꙋ́цѣ къ пе́рсемъ и҆мѧ́щи), prostrating to the earth, kissing the honorable cross, and receiving the Body and Blood of Christ from the chalice. Note the distinct Ruthenian form `съгнкеннѣ̀` (folded/crossed, cognate with Ukrainian *зігнені* / Church Slavonic *согбены*).
[^358]: The Administration Formula to the Laity: "The servant of God [Name] partakes of the precious and holy Body and Blood of our Lord and God and Savior Jesus Christ, unto the remission of his sins and unto life eternal" (Ра́бъ бж҃їи [і҆́мⷶ] · Причаща́етсѧ чⷵтныѧ і҆ ст҃ы́ѧ пло́ти и҆ кро́ве г҃а бг҃а і҆ сп҃са на́шего і҆ѵ҃ х҃а въ ѡ҆ставле́нїе грѣхѡ́въ є҆го̀ и҆ въ жи́знь вѣ́чнꙋю).
[^359]: Liturgical hapax legomenon and realia: *obrysuet* (ѡ҆брисоу́етъ), an early modern Ruthenian denominal verb derived from *obrus* (обрусъ / Polish *obrus*, linen cloth/purificator): "And thus he wipes his lips with the holy veil; he kisses the holy chalice, and having bowed, departs" (И҆ та́ко ѡ҆брисоу́етъ є҆мꙋ̀ ꙋ҆стна̀ ст҃ы[м] покро́вцемъ · цѣлꙋ́ет ст҃ꙋю ча́шоу и҆ покло́нсѧ ѿхо́ди[тъ]).
[^360]: Rubric for the deacon: taking the holy diskos over the holy chalice, he sweeps the remaining particles into the chalice with the holy sponge (*omêtaet st[o]iu huboiu zêlo opasno* / ѡ҆мѣта́ет ст҃о́ю гꙋ́бою ѕѣло̀ ѡ҆па́сно) with great care, attentiveness, and reverence, and covers the chalice with the veil.
[^361]: Rubric for vesting the sacred vessels: the deacon replaces the asterisk and veils upon the empty holy diskos, while the celebrant prepares to recite the post-communion thanksgiving.
[^362]: The Celebrant's Post-Communion Prayer of Thanksgiving (*Eucharistia*): "We thank Thee, Master, Lover of mankind, Benefactor of our souls, that in the present day Thou hast vouchsafed us Thy heavenly and immortal mysteries..." (Бл҃годари́мъ тѧ̀ влⷣко чл҃колю́бче, бл҃года́телю дꙋ́шъ на́шихъ...).
[^363]: Conclusion of the Prayer of Thanksgiving: entreating Christ to make straight our path, establish us in His fear, protect our life, and firm our steps through the intercessions of the Theotokos and all the saints. Note the typographical slip in the print witness: `сла́ныѧ` for `сла́вныѧ` (*slavnyia* / glorious).
[^364]: Rubric for opening the royal doors: the doors of the holy sanctuary are opened, the deacon makes a bow, takes the holy chalice with reverence, and brings it to the royal doors, elevating it before the congregation.
[^365]: The Deacon's Solemn Invitation to the faithful: "With the fear of God and with faith draw near" (Съ стра́хомъ бж҃їимъ и҆ съ вѣ́рою пристоупи́те / *Meta phobou Theou kai pisteōs proselthete*).
[^366]: The Celebrant's Solemn Blessing over the congregation with the chalice: "Save, O God, Thy people, and bless Thine inheritance" (Psalm 27:9 / LXX 27:9 / Heb. 28:9; Сп҃сѝ бж҃е лю́ди своѧ̀ и҆ бл҃ви достоѧ́нїе твоѐ).
[^367]: The Incensation of the Holy Gifts at the holy Table: the ministers return to the altar, and the priest incenses the chalice thrice, reciting Psalm 56:6, 12 (LXX 56:6, 12 / Heb. 57:5, 11): "Be Thou exalted above the heavens, O God, and Thy glory above all the earth" (Възнеси́сѧ на нб҃са бж҃е · и҆ по въсе́й землѝ сла́ва твоѧ̀), mystically commemorating the Ascension of Christ into heaven.
[^368]: Transfer of the holy diskos to the Table of Oblation (Prothesis): the priest places the diskos upon the deacon's head, and the deacon, looking straight forward toward the royal doors and remaining silent, transfers the diskos to the Prothesis.
[^369]: Final blessing and elevation toward the people: the priest takes the holy chalice, turns toward the doors, looks upon the congregation, and proclaims: "Always, now and ever, and unto ages of ages" (Въсегда̀ нн҃ѣ и҆ прⷵно и҆ въ вѣ́ки вѣко́[въ]).
[^370]: Post-communion hymn of the choir: "Let our mouth be filled with Thy praise, O Lord..." (*Da ispolniatsia usta nasha* / Да испѡ́лнасѧ ꙋ҆ста̀ на́ша). Note the printed form `испѡ́лнасѧ` (an archaic or South Slavic aorist/subjunctive spelling for *ispolniatsia*).
[^371]: Conclusion of the post-communion hymn: praying to be kept in holiness and to meditate upon God's righteousness all the day long, concluding with a threefold Alleluia.
[^372]: The Deacon's Post-Communion Thanksgiving Litany: stepping out through the northern door onto the solea, the deacon proclaims: "Arise! Having partaken of the divine, holy, immaculate, and life-creating mysteries, let us worthily give thanks unto the Lord" (Про́сти прїе́мше бж҃твенны[хъ], сты́х и҆ престы[х]...).
[^373]: Petitions of the litany: "Help us, save us, have mercy on us, and keep us, O God, by Thy grace," praying for a perfect, peaceful, and sinless day, and commending our whole life unto Christ our God.
[^374]: The Priest's Thanksgiving Exclamation: "For Thou art our sanctification, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Choir: Amen" (Ꙗ҆́ко ты є҆сѝ ст҃ы́нѧ на́ша...).
[^375]: Diaconal dismissal dialogue: "Let us go forth in peace" (Съ ми́ромъ и҆зы́демъ); the choir responds "In the name of the Lord" (Ѡ҆ и҆́мени гн҃и); the deacon bids "Let us pray to the Lord" (Г҃ꙋ помо́лимсѧ); and the choir responds "Lord, have mercy" (Г҃и поми́лꙋй).
[^376]: The Prayer Behind the Ambon (*Molitwa zaamvonnaia* / Моли́тва за а҆мво́ннаѧ): the celebrant descends from the solea and recites this corporate intercession for the whole ecclesial community in the midst of the nave.
[^377]: Opening invocation of the Ambo Prayer: "O Lord, Who dost bless those who bless Thee, and sanctify those who trust in Thee: save Thy people and bless Thine inheritance; preserve the fullness of Thy Church; sanctify those who love the beauty of Thy house; do Thou in return glorify them by Thy divine power, and forsake not us who hope in Thee" (Бл҃гословлѧ́ѧи бл҃гослвѧ́щїѧ тѧ̀ г҃и...).
[^378]: Concluding petitions and Trinitarian doxology of the Ambo Prayer: asking peace for the world, churches, priests, civil authorities (*tsarem nashym*), the army, and all people, confessing that every good giving and perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights (James 1:17).
[^379]: The post-ambo chant: the choir sings "Blessed be the name of the Lord, from this time forth and unto ages" thrice (Psalm 112:2 / LXX 112:2), followed by the reading or singing of Psalm 33: "I will bless the Lord at all times..." (*Blahoslovliu Hospoda na vsiakoe vremia*). Note the printed heading: `Три́десѧть тре́тїй` (thirty-third).
[^380]: The Celebrant's Prayer at the Consummation of the Holy Gifts: the priest enters the sanctuary through the holy doors and recites before the Table of Oblation: "The fullness of the Law and the Prophets, Thou Thyself art, O Christ our God, Who didst fulfill all the Father's dispensation; fill our hearts with joy and gladness, always, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen" (*Ispolnenie zakona i prorok* / И҆сполне́нїе зако́на и҆ пррⷵкъ).
[^381]: The Ablutions (*Consumptio* / Потребленіе): the deacon enters through the northern entrance (*sêvernoiu zapoiu* / сѣ́верною за́пою) to consume the Holy Gifts at the Prothesis with fear and all caution, ensuring that not even the smallest particle is dropped or remains on the holy diskos.
[^382]: Rubric for purifying the chalice: the deacon pours wine and water into the chalice, rinses it thrice, consumes the ablutions, and carefully dries the interior with the liturgical sponge.
[^383]: Rubric for storing the sacred vessels: the deacon dries the sponge thoroughly, binds the sacred vessels together, and places them in their appointed depository, reciting the Canticle of Simeon (Luke 2:29: "Now lettest Thou Thy servant depart in peace...") and the post-communion prayers.
[^384]: Distribution of the Antidoron: the celebrant washes his hands and distributes the blessed bread (*antidoron* / а҆нті́дѡръ) to the faithful from the solea. Note the rubric: `по сконча́нїи же и҆лѝ раздаа́нїи а҆нті́дѡры гл҃етъ` ("and after the completion or distribution of the antidoron, he says:").
[^385]: The Celebrant's final blessing over the faithful: "The blessing of the Lord be upon you, [through His grace and love for mankind,] always, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Choir: Amen" (Бл҃ве́нїе гн҃е на ва́съ...).
[^386]: Opening of the Great Dismissal (*sovershennyi otpost*): the reader recites "Glory... Both now... Lord, have mercy (thrice). Bless!" Note the typographical error in the print witness: `съвеше́нны` for `съверше́нный` (*sovershennyi* / full, complete).
[^387]: The Full Dismissal Formula of Saint John Chrysostom: "Christ our true God, through the intercessions of His all-pure Mother, and of our father among the saints, John Chrysostom, Archbishop of Constantinople, and of all the saints, will have mercy on us and save us, as [He is good and loves mankind]..." (*Khristos istinnyi Boh nash...*). Fol. 115r (p. 215) terminates abruptly on the conjunction `ꙗ҆́ко` (*iako* / as).
[^388]: Typographical lacuna in the 1604 Striatyn print: the printer omitted the conclusion of the dismissal formula (`бл҃гъ и҆ чл҃колю́бецъ. Ликъ: А҆ми́нь`) and the opening strophes of the Dismissal Kontakion of St. John Chrysostom (*Ot nebes priial yesi bozhestvennuiu blahodat... vseblazhenne*), resuming at the top of fol. 115v (p. 216) in mid-phrase: "...venerable father, worthily do we praise thee; for thou art an instructor, revealing divine things" (прпⷣбне досто́йно хва́лимъ тѧ̀ · є҆сѝ бѡ наставник ꙗ҆́ко бж҃твеннаа ꙗ҆ви́ла).
[^389]: The Dismissal Theotokion: *Zastupnytse khristiianom nepostydnaia* ("O Protection of Christians unashamed, Mediatrix before the Creator unwavering, despise not the voices of sinners in prayer, but precede, as thou art good, to our help who faithfully cry unto thee: make haste to prayer, and speed to supplication, ever protecting, O Theotokos, those who honor thee").
[^390]: Alternative short dismissal order: reciting the daily troparia, "Lord, have mercy" (12 times), "Glory... Both now...", "More honorable than the Cherubim", followed by the final dismissal, bowing, thanking God for all things, and departing.
[^391]: Concluding colophon of the Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom: "End of the Divine Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom" (Коне́цъ бж҃твенныѧ лїргі́и ста́го і҆ѡа́нна хрѷсосто́ма).
[^392]: Physical leaf p119 features a blank verso (fol. 116v / p. 218) showing through from the recto, followed by the decorative title page of Saint Basil's Liturgy on fol. 117r (p. 220, marked `с҃к` / `118`). The printer omitted page numbers 218–219 in the sequence.
[^393]: Major structural landmark: Part II of the 1604 Liturgikon opens under a floral woodcut headpiece band with the grand title set in ornate red vyaz' (ligature script): "ORDER OF THE DIVINE SERVICE OF OUR HOLY FATHER BASIL THE GREAT, ARCHBISHOP OF CAESAREA IN CAPPADOCIA" (ОУ҆СТА́ВЪ БЖ҃ТВЕННЫꙖ СЛУ́ЖБЫ И҆́же въ ст҃ы́хъ ѻ҆ц҃а на́шего васи́лїа вели́каго а҆рхїепⷵпа кесарі́а каппадокі́йскыѧ).
[^394]: Opening general rubric for the Liturgy of St. Basil: the Prothesis is performed completely according to custom, identically with the Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom, all the way to the dismissal.
[^395]: Rubric on the annual frequency of the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great: "The priest ought to know (Behold) that the Divine Liturgy of our holy and great father Basil is sung ten times throughout the year..." (*Podobaet vêdaty iako bozhestvennaia liturhiia... desiatyshchy poietsia chrez hodyshche*).
[^396]: The ten canonical days for celebrating the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great: (1–5) the first five Sundays of Great Lent; (6) Holy and Great Thursday; (7) Holy and Great Saturday; (8) the Feast of Saint Basil the Great (January 1); (9) the Eve of the Nativity of Christ (December 24); and (10) the Eve of the Holy Theophany (January 5).
[^397]: Specific typikon prescription for the Eves of the Nativity and Theophany: if the Eve of either feast falls on a Saturday or Sunday, the Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom is celebrated on the Eve in its proper time, and the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great is sung in the morning on the very day of the feast itself.
[^398]: Concluding rubrical confirmation: the Liturgy of Saint Basil follows the order of Saint John Chrysostom in every detail, with the Prothesis performed completely unchanged, all the way to the dismissal, with the celebrant substituting the distinctive prayers of Saint Basil.
