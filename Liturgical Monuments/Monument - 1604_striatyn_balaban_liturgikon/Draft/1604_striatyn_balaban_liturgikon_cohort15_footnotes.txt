[^443]: Fol. 139v (p. 266). The Second Prayer of the Faithful of Saint Basil the Great concludes with an explicit petition for the epiklesis: "to invoke the grace of Thy Holy Spirit upon the gifts that are about to be offered" (*въ є҆́же призыва́ти бл҃гⷣть ст҃го дх҃а , на хотѧ́щїѧ преложи́тисѧ да́ры*), highlighting the preparatory consecratory intention before the Great Entrance.

[^444]: Fol. 140r (p. 267). The rubric refers back to the Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom (*ꙗ҆́коже и҆ на лїтꙋргі́и златоꙋ́стаго*) for the ceremonial actions of the Great Entrance, the censing of the altar and sanctuary, and the recitation of Psalm 50. The priest then recites secretly the celebrated Cherubic Prayer attributed to St. Basil (*Никто́же досто́инъ*), identical in wording in both Byzantine liturgies.

[^445]: Fol. 141r (p. 269). The prayer confesses Christ as sovereign over all creation, borrowing the prophetic imagery of Isaiah 6 and Ezekiel 1: "borne upon the throne of the Cherubim, Who art Lord of the Seraphim and King of Israel" (*и҆́же на прⷵтолѣ хероуві́мскомъ ѣ́здѧй , и҆́же на серафі́ми г҃ь и҆ цр҃ь і҆и҃ле*).

[^446]: Fol. 142r (p. 271). Christological paradox of the Eucharistic sacrifice: "For Thou art He that offereth and is offered, that accepteth and is distributed" (*Ты́ бо є҆сѝ приносѧ́и и҆ приноси́мый , и҆ прїе́млѧй , и҆ раздава́емый*), affirming Christ as simultaneous High Priest, Victim, Recipient of sacrifice together with the Father and the Spirit, and Food given to the faithful.

[^447]: Fol. 142v (p. 272). The standard Cherubic Hymn (*Херуви́мскаѧ пѣ́снь* / *Cherubikon*) in Church Slavonic: *И҆́же херуві́мъ та́йнѣ ѡ҆бразꙋ́юще...* Note the Greek spelling with double lambda in the printed text: *А҆λληлꙋ́їа*.

[^448]: Fol. 143r (p. 273). Great and Holy Thursday proper Cherubic replacement hymn: *Ве́чери твое́й та́йнѣ дне́сь сн҃е бж҃їй , прича́стника мѧ̀ прїимѝ...* ("Of Thy Mystical Supper..."), printed in full with its rubric (*А҆ сѐ въ вели́кый четверто́къ то́чїю пое́тсѧ*).

[^449]: Fol. 143r (p. 273). Great and Holy Saturday proper Cherubic replacement hymn: *Да ᲂу҆молчи́тъ въсѧ́ка пло́ть чл҃ча...* ("Let all mortal flesh keep silence..."), taken from the ancient Liturgy of St. James.

[^450]: Fol. 143v (p. 274). The marginal rubric *хѡ́ри* marks the second strophe of the Great Saturday Cherubic hymn describing the angelic armies preceding the King of kings: *Преⷣ и҆́дꙋ́тъ же семꙋ̀ ли́ци а҆́гг҃льсті́и...*

[^451]: Fol. 144r (p. 275). Rubric directing that the ceremonial actions and words of the transfer of the Gifts, the Great Entrance, the diaconal commemorations, and the placement of the chalice and paten upon the altar be performed exactly as prescribed in the Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom (*та́ꙗже творѧ́тъ , и҆ гл҃ютъ , ꙗ҆́коже на лїтургі́и златоꙋ́ста*).

[^452]: Fol. 144r (p. 275). The diaconal petitions of supplication (*И҆спо́лнимъ мл҃твы на́ша гв҃и*) and the heading for the Prayer of the Prothesis / Offering of St. Basil (*Мл҃тва приноше́нїю*).

[^453]: Fol. 144v (p. 276). The Prayer of the Offering (*Моли́тва приноше́нїѧ*) of Saint Basil the Great, beginning *Го́споди Бо́же на́шъ, созда́вый на́съ...* The prayer expresses deep thanksgiving for creation, salvation history, and the revelation of divine mysteries.

[^454]: Fol. 145v (p. 278). Scriptural typology of sacrifice in the Prayer of the Offering: Abel (Gen. 4:4), Noah (Gen. 8:20), Abraham (Gen. 22:13), Moses and Aaron (Lev. 8-9), and Samuel (1 Sam. 7:9). The printed text reads *възни посла̀и* for *низпосли* ("send down in return").

[^455]: Fol. 146r (p. 279). Parabolic allusion to the faithful and wise steward (Luke 12:42) who receives his reward in the day of righteous recompense (*въ дн҃ь стра́шныи въздаѧ́нїа твое́го пра́ведна*).

[^456]: Fol. 147r (p. 281). The Kiss of Peace and threefold recitation of Psalm 17[18]:2-3: "I will love Thee, O Lord, my strength..." (*Възлю́блю́ тѧ г҃и крѣ́пости моѧ̀...*).

[^457]: Fol. 147v (p. 282). Diaconal exclamation guarding the sanctuary doors: "The doors! The doors! In wisdom let us give heed!" (*Две́ри две́ри премꙋ́дростїю въни́ме́мъ*), introducing the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed.

[^458]: Fol. 147v (p. 282). The diaconal warning preceding the Anaphora: *Ста́нѣмъ до́брѣ , ста́нѣмъ съ стра́хомъ...* ("Let us stand well, let us stand with fear...").

[^460]: Fol. 150r (p. 287). The Pneumatological confession in the Anaphora of St. Basil, extolling the Holy Spirit as Spirit of truth, gift of adoption (Rom. 8:15), earnest of future inheritance (Eph. 1:14), and fountain of sanctification, leading directly into the angelic Sanctus.
