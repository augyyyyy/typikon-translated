[^163]: Conclusion of the Prayer of the Third Antiphon (Мл҃тва тре́тїаго а҆нтїфѡ́на), attributed in Byzantine and Ruthenian traditions to Saint John Chrysostom. The petition asks for divine illumination ("позна́нїе твое́ѧ и҆́стинны") in the present world and life eternal in the age to come.
[^164]: The Little Entrance (Ма́лый въхо́дъ) with the Holy Gospel. The rubric explicitly directs that the clergy be preceded by two candle-bearers (двѣ́ма лампа́домъ).
[^165]: In the right margin of Fol. 49r (p. 83), a contemporary scholarly hand has penned a Greek annotation: ἡ δὲ θεία λειτουργία ("and the Divine Liturgy"), marking the entrance rubric where the deacon holds his orarion with three fingers of his right hand.
[^166]: The doxological exclamation concluding the Entrance Prayer (В̾хо́днаѧ мл҃тва). The Trinitarian doxology strictly follows the canonical standard: "now and ever, and unto ages of ages."
[^167]: The blessing of the Little Entrance by the celebrant (Бл҃венъ въхѡ́дъ сты́хъ твои́хъ...), common to both the Ruthenian and Byzantine recensions.
[^168]: Fol. 50r (p. 85), bottom right corner: a physical tear in the paper leaf has truncated the concluding rubric concerning concelebration: а҆́ще же не б[л҃гословлѧ́етъ...] ("but if he does not bless...").
[^169]: Fol. 50v (p. 86), line 3: the typesetter has printed the numeral 3 using the form 3-го rather than the traditional church Cyrillic .г҃.-го.
[^170]: The deacon's solemn elevation and proclamation at the holy doors: Премꙋ́дрость про́сти ("Wisdom! Stand upright!"), bidding the faithful to reverent attention as the Holy Gospel enters the sanctuary.
[^171]: The Entrance Hymn (Входный стих): Прїидѣ́те поклони́мсѧ... ("Come, let us worship..."). On Sundays the refrain commemorates the Resurrection (Въскресы́й и҆з̾ ме́ртвыхъ), while on feast days and weekdays it invokes Christ Who is marvelous in His saints (ди́венъ є҆сѝ въ сты́хъ).
[^172]: The liturgical petition of the deacon to the priest before the Trisagion: Бл҃ви вл҃ко врѣ́мѧ трⷵта́го ("Bless, Master, the time of the Trisagion"), initiating the liturgical exclamation of the priest.
[^173]: The deacon's exclamation from the solea before the holy doors: Г҃и сп҃си бл҃гочести́выѧ, и҆ ꙋ҆слы́ши на́съ ("O Lord, save the pious, and hear us"), preserving the ancient pre-Nikonian and Byzantine entrance solemnity before intoning the final clause И҆ въ вѣ́ки вѣ́кѡмъ.
[^174]: The Prayer of the Thrice-Holy Hymn (Мл҃тва тресвѧта́го пѣ́нїа), recited secretly by the priest while the choir chants the Trisagion (Ст҃ы́й бж҃е...).
[^175]: The Trisagion Prayer's theological exposition reflects the classical Byzantine synthesis of creation ex nihilo, the fashioning of man in the divine image, and the gift of repentance unto salvation.
[^176]: Concluding doxological exclamation of the Trisagion Prayer: Ꙗ҆́ко ст҃ъ є҆сѝ бж҃е на́шъ... ("For holy art Thou, our God...").
[^177]: Scripture witness: Psalm 117:26 [LXX: 117:26; MT: 118:26]: "Blessed is He that cometh in the Name of the Lord." Recited as the celebrant ascends toward the High Place (го́рнее сѣда́лище).
[^178]: Scriptural blessing of the High Throne from the Prayer of Azariah (Daniel 3:55 [LXX]): "Blessed art Thou upon the throne of the glory of Thy kingdom, Who sittest upon the cherubim."
[^179]: Announcement of the Prokeimenon before the Epistle. The rubric directs the reader to announce Ѱало́мъ дв҃двъ ("A Psalm of David") together with the Tone.
[^180]: The liturgical title formulas for the Epistle: Acts of the Apostles (Дѣѧ́нїа сты́хъ а҆пл҃ъ), Catholic Epistles (Ѿ събѡ́рнаго посла́нїа), or Pauline Epistles (Посла́нїе ст҃аго а҆пл҃а па́вла).
[^181]: Ruthenian rubric for censing during the singing of the Alleluia: the deacon censes the holy Table crosswise, the Prothesis (ст҃ое предложе́нїе), the entire altar space, and the celebrant.
[^182]: The Prayer before the Gospel (Мл҃тва пред є҆ѵа́глїемъ): a classic petitional prayer for spiritual illumination, asking that the incorruptible light of divine knowledge shine in the hearts of clergy and faithful.
[^183]: The interior petition of the Gospel prayer: asking for spiritual understanding of the Gospel proclamations (є҆ѵлⷵскаѧ твоѧ̀ проповѣ́данїѧ) and victory over carnal desires so as to walk according to the Spirit.
[^184]: Doxological conclusion of the Prayer before the Gospel: addressing Christ God as the illumination of souls, glorifying Him together with the Father and the Holy Spirit unto ages of ages.
[^185]: Blessing bestowed by the celebrant upon the deacon prior to the Gospel proclamation: invoking the intercessions of the specific holy Evangelist whose text is about to be proclaimed (ст҃аго сла́внаго всехва́лнаго а҆пл҃а і҆ є҆ѵа́лїста [і҆́мⷶ]).
[^186]: Rubric for the Gospel procession to the ambo, preceded by two candles. The text also provides explicit instructions for the distribution of liturgical duties when two deacons concelebrate.
[^187]: The threefold Lord, have mercy chanted by the choir in response to the petitional clauses of the Insistent Litany (Ектенїа̀ сꙋгꙋ́баѧ).
