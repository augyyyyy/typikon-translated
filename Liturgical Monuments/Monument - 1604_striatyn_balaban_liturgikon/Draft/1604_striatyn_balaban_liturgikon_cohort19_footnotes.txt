[^521]: Fol. 181v (p. 351). Concluding dismissal prayers of Saint Basil: the end of the post-liturgical Theotokion, the twelvefold *Kyrie eleison*, the Little Dismissal (*ѿпоу́стъ*), and the priest's departure from the altar after giving thanks to God. Followed by the explicit concluding colophon: *Коне́цъ бж҃тве́нныѧ лїтоургі́а ст҃го васи́лїа вели́каго* ("The End of the Divine Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great").

[^522]: Fol. 182r (p. 352). Opening of Part III (The Divine Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts) and preliminary rubric concerning the preparation and slaughter of the Presanctified Lambs (*Ѡ закла́нїи ст҃а́гѡ а҆́гнца, съ ни́мже тво́ритсѧ ст҃а́ѧ лїтоургі́а прежесвѧще́ннаѧ*). The rubric specifies that the Presanctified Liturgy is celebrated strictly during the Holy Great Forty Days (Great Lent), normally on Wednesdays and Fridays or on feast days. The text opens beneath a woodcut renaissance strapwork headpiece containing an eagle/pelican holding a phylactery, with ornamental woodcut initial `П` (*Подоба́етъ вѣ́дати*). Note signature `х` at the foot of the folio.

[^523]: Fol. 182v (p. 352b). The rubric prescribes the slaughter of multiple Lambs during the Prothesis of Cheese-fare Sunday or the preceding Sunday Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom. Each additional Lamb designated for a Presanctified Liturgy is cut, pierced, and accompanied by the exact sacrificial formulas and incipits as the first Lamb (*по ѡ҆́бразоу ꙗ҆́коже и҆ на пръвы, ꙗ҆́коже пи́шетъ въ с҃ проскомиді́и*).

[^524]: Fol. 183r (p. 353). The consecration and elevation of the Presanctified Lambs during the Sunday Liturgy. At the elevation (*Holy Things unto the holy*), the priest takes the communion spoon (*ст҃ꙋ́ю лави́дꙋ си́рѣчь ло́жицꙋ* / λαβίς) in his right hand and the consecrated Lamb in his left hand, turning the cut side upward over the chalice. Note signature `х в҃` at bottom.

[^525]: Fol. 183v (p. 354). The Ruthenian rite of intinction: the priest takes a drop of the consecrated Blood with the spoon and moistens the Lamb (*поурꙑ́пъ лави́дою бж҃твенныѧ кро́ве... и҆ и҆сплазнѧ́етъ ст҃ы́и а҆́гнецъ*), subsequently depositing each Lamb into a dedicated pyx (*въ на то̀ ᲂу҆зоенный съсоу́дъ*, arca/artophorion) kept upon the Holy Table until the appointed day of celebration. A small ornamental typographical vignette marks the conclusion of the preliminary rubric. In the printed foliation, page number 355 is omitted, jumping directly from `т҃нд` (354) to `т҃нѕ` (356).

[^526]: Fol. 184r (p. 356). Sacerdotal title and headpiece for the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts, traditionally attributed in Byzantine and Slavic euchologia to Saint Gregory the Great (*Грїго́рїа дъвоесло́ва, па́пы ри́мскаго* / Γρηγόριος ὁ Διάλογος). The text is crowned by an exquisite woodcut vignette depicting the Man of Sorrows (Christ standing in the tomb / *Imago Pietatis*, with `IC XC`). The preparatory rubrics prescribe the exit of the clergy during the Typika after the Beatitudes, while the Reader recites the hymn "The heavenly choir" (*Ли́къ небе́сный пое́тъ Тя и глаго́летъ...*). Signature `х г҃` at bottom.

[^527]: Fol. 184v (p. 357). Sacerdotal vesting and entrance ritual: the priest and deacon bow (make a metania) before the superior (hegumen) or his vicar, pray before the royal doors (*Го́споди, посли́ ми рꙋ́кꙋ тво́ю...*), make seventeen prostrations (metanias), bow to the left and right choirs, enter the sanctuary, and vest in full sacerdotal vestments according to the customary order.

[^528]: Fol. 185r (p. 358). Preliminary altar devotions: standing before the Holy Table with raised hands, the clergy make three bows, recite *Glory to God in the highest* twice (Luke 2:14), and *O Lord, open Thou my lips* once (Psalm 50:17, LXX). The deacon waits reverently without requesting the blessing of the time until the priest finishes his metanias, then exits to the solea and makes three bows before exclaiming *Благословѝ, Влады́ко*. Signature `х д҃` at bottom.

[^529]: Fol. 185v (p. 359). Opening of the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts. In Early Modern Ruthenian practice, the opening blessing is given as *Благослове́нъ Бо́гъ на́шъ* (the standard Vesperal opening) rather than *Благослове́но ца́рство*, followed immediately by the three invitatory verses (*Прїиди́те поклони́мсѧ*) and Psalm 103 (the Prooemiac Psalm of Vespers). The deacon stands before the icon of the Savior facing the people, while the priest secretly reads the Prayers of the Light (Lychikon). The initial `Б` is set in an ornamental woodcut cartouche.

[^530]: Fol. 186r (p. 360). Sacerdotal secret Prayer of the First Antiphon / Agreement (*Мл҃тва пръваго съгла́сїа* / *Го́споди ще́дрыи и҆ млⷭтивыи...* / Prayer 4 of Byzantine Vespers, Κύριε οἰκτίρμων καὶ ἐλεήμων), beginning with a woodcut initial `Г`. The prayer draws heavily from Psalm 85:15, 17, 11, and 10 (LXX), entreating the Lord for mercy, a token for good, guidance in the divine way, and reverence for His holy Name.

[^532]: Fol. 187r (p. 362). The prayer addresses Christ as the Physician and Healer of souls, praying for spiritual guidance to the harbor of God's will and enlightenment of the spiritual eyes of the heart (*ᲂу҆́чи срⷣцъ на́шихъ* / τοὺς ὀφθαλμοὺς τῶν καρδιῶν ἡμῶν).
