[^399]: Fol. 118v (p. 223). Church Slavonic: *Ѻ҆ навече́рїах̾ а҆́ще слꙋча́тсѧ въ по́стъ · и҆ ѡ҆ ст҃ѣ́мъ вели́цѣ четве́рткꙋ и҆ ѡ҆ ст҃ѣ́и вели́цѣ сꙋбо́тѣ*. This rubric governs the celebration of the Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great and St. John Chrysostom when conjoined with Vespers (the Vesperal Liturgy) on the Eves (*paramonies*) of the Nativity of Christ and Theophany during fasting seasons, as well as on Holy and Great Thursday and Holy and Great Saturday. In Byzantine-Ruthenian practice, these services begin late in the afternoon (at the "tenth hour of the day," approximately 4:00 PM) with the monastic bells/klepalo.

[^400]: Fol. 118v (p. 223). Church Slavonic: *кле́племъ въ вели́кое*. That is, striking the great *klepalo* (Greek *semantron*), the traditional sounding board (wood or iron) struck with wooden mallets to summon the brethren to solemn divine services.

[^401]: Fol. 119r (p. 224). Church Slavonic: *възнима́ютъ вре́мѧ* (literally, "they take the time"). This is the technical Slavic liturgical term translating the Greek *kairos* (*καιρός*, cf. Ps. 118:126), referring to the canonical preparatory order in which the priest and deacon seek the blessing (*blagoslovenie*) of the superior and recite the entrance prayers prior to entering the sanctuary and vesting.

[^402]: Fol. 119r (p. 224). The Prayer of the Kairos: *Го́споди, посли́ ми рꙋ́кꙋ тво́ю* ("O Lord, send forth Thy hand from Thy holy dwelling-place, and strengthen me for this Thy present service..."), based on Psalm 143:7 and Psalm 137:8, recited by the celebrants standing before the Holy Doors.

[^403]: Fol. 119v (p. 225). Troparia recited during the veneration of the holy icons of the iconostasis: the troparion to the Icon of Christ (*Пречи́стому Твоему́ о́бразу покланя́емся, Благі́й* / "We venerate Thy most pure icon, O Good One...") and the troparion to the Theotokos (*Милосе́рдія сꙋ́щи исто́чникъ* / "Being a fountain of tender compassion...").

[^404]: Fol. 120r (p. 226). The rubric cross-references the complete vesting prayers and order of the Prothesis (*proskomide*) set forth earlier in the codex at the beginning of the Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom (fols. 1r–15r / leaves p10–p25), noting that all formulas and actions are performed identically without variation.

[^405]: Fol. 120v (p. 227). The preparatory prayers recited by the priest and deacon standing together before the Holy Table: the invocation of the Holy Spirit (*Царю́ Небе́сный* / "Heavenly King, Comforter..."), the Angelic Hymn (*Сла́ва въ вы́шнихъ Бо́гу* / "Glory to God in the highest...", Luke 2:14, twice), and the versicle (*Го́споди, ꙋстнѣ̀ моѝ ѿве́рзеши* / "O Lord, Thou shalt open my lips...", Psalm 50:17 [LXX 50:17], once).

[^406]: Fol. 121r (p. 228). The dialogue of the Kairos before the Holy Table: *Вре́мя є҆́же сотвори́ти Го́сподеви* ("It is time for the Lord to act", Psalm 118:126 [LXX 118:126]), spoken by the deacon to the priest while bowing with his orarion.

[^407]: Fol. 121v (p. 229). Rubric specifying the deacon's emergence onto the ambon and the opening of the curtain (*катапета́зма*, Greek *καταπέτασμα*) of the Royal Doors before the priest proclaims the initial doxological blessing.

[^408]: Fol. 122r (p. 230). The introductory Psalm of Vespers (Psalm 103 [LXX 103], *Благослови́, душе́ моѧ̀, Го́спода* / "Bless the Lord, O my soul"), sung at the beginning of the Vesperal Liturgy, while the deacon stands before the icon of Christ awaiting its conclusion before chanting the Great Litany. Note the early modern printed notation: *ѱало сто́ з҃етїй* (the 103rd Psalm).

[^409]: Fol. 122v (p. 231). [^411]: Fol. 123v (p. 233). The Little Litany following the readings, leading to the Trisagion dialogue (*Благослови́, влады́ко, вре́мя трисвята́го* / "Bless, Master, the time of the Trisagion") and the priest's exclamation.

[^412]: Fol. 124r (p. 234). [^413]: Fol. 124r (p. 234). Rubric for the Feast of the Meeting of the Lord (Candlemas, February 2): if the feast occurs on a weekday of the Great Fast (Lent), the Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom is celebrated according to this identical vesperal order.
