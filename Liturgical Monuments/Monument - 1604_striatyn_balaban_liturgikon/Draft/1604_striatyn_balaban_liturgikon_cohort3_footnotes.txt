[^63]: Formula accompanying the incision of the Lamb, citing John 1:29: *Жре́тся а́гнецъ бо́жїй, взе́мляй грѣхѝ ми́ру, за ми́рскїй живо́тъ, и спасе́нїе* ("Sacrificed is the Lamb of God, Who takes away the sin of the world, for the life and salvation of the world"). In Ruthenian printed liturgicons, this combines the Johannine text with the eucharistic formula for the life and salvation of the world.
[^64]: The formula accompanying the piercing of the right side of the Lamb with the spear, verbatim from John 19:34–35: *И еди́нъ от во́инъ ко́пїемъ ему̀ ре́бра прободѐ, и а́бїе изы́де кро́вь, и вода̀...*
[^65]: This rubric explicitly settles the disputed orientation of the Lamb on the diskos in Early Modern Ruthenian practice: the "right side" of the Lamb corresponds physically to the side adjacent to the chalice (i.e. the priest's right hand), where both the Theotokos particle and the Nine Ranks particles are placed in Balaban's edition.
[^66]: The deacon's petition *Благословѝ влады́ко свято́е единенїе* corresponds to the Greek *Εὐλόγησον, δέσποτα, τὴν ἁγίαν ἕνωσιν*, requesting the blessing of the commingling of wine and water in the holy chalice.
[^67]: The priest blesses the chalice crosswise while the deacon pours the wine and water. A contemporary 17th-century marginal gloss in the right margin of Fol. 12r supplies the customary rubrical practice: the priest blesses the mixture, and three drops of water are added if not already mingled.

The Striatyn editors explicitly print this diagram in order to reject it.

The editors note that Patristic commentaries do not support placing the Theotokos on the left, but rather at Christ's right hand.
