# 1604 Striatyn Liturgikon of Hedeon Balaban (Служебник, Стрятин: Друкарня Балабанів, 1604) <a id="1604-striatyn-liturgikon-of-hedeon-balaban-служебник-стрятин-друкарня-балабанів-1604"></a>

Of our Fathers among the Saints,[^2]
John Chrysostom,
Basil the Great, and

IN STRIATYN,

Throne of Constantinople.[^6]

[Seventeenth-century cursive inscription in the lower margin:] This Liturgikon...

[Woodcut Headpiece: Renaissance cartouche containing the Korczak heraldic arms of the Balaban family, flanked by floral cornucopias and strapwork grotesque figures.]

*Preface: Epistle of Bishop Hedeon Balaban.*

Unto the Orthodox followers of the sacred rites of the holy Eastern Catholic and Apostolic Church,[^7] and fellow ministers of our Humility, brethren in Christ: Hedeon Balaban, humble Bishop of Lviv, Halych, and Kamianets of Podolia, Exarch of the Throne of Constantinople: Grace from on High.

Truly, according to Gregory the Theologian,[^8] what food is to the body, that the word is to the soul.

*Preface.*

Which instruction concerning good things toward God is acknowledged by all who possess understanding to be foremost; and this is multiplied through the printing of books. For this reason, I also was moved by the sacred letters of Meletios of blessed and holy memory, Pope of Alexandria,[^9] who was then governing the Ecumenical Throne,[^10] who foresaw this supreme good whereof he spoke, seeing our downfall: "It was fitting, therefore, and even now it is fitting, if ye wish not to suffer the utmost ruin, to establish schools (*gymnasia*) and printing presses (*typographia*)"; and this he said for the multiplication of books for the imparting of understanding. And having been stirred again unto this according to the desire of our lineage, *Unto this Book:*

Having been held by such thoughts, the spirit of desire has now brought forth fruit: when, by grace from on High, together with my co-worker and kinsman, the most noble Lord Theodore Balaban, son of George,[^11] who came forward with every zeal from his own ancestral estates unto this very work. And therefore, deliberating before all others upon that which pertains unto the order of the Church, we resolved to print the Divine Liturgies,[^12] as holding preeminence, for the sake of concord in the celebration of the awesome and unbloody Sacrifice, lest anyone find cause for reproach in this matter; and having obtained ancient and carefully preserved manuscript recensions,[^13] and diligently investigated them, *Preface.*

And having corrected them, we publish them unto your brotherly love. And having sweetly received this sacred book, and desiring also the remaining books, one after another; and if we are alive, to be deemed worthy without delay: books which agree with the Greek orders; and not only this, but also for the instruction of the teachers of the Church, as we strive by the grace of Christ prevailing within us, in other languages and writings also from this printing press,[^14] through the cooperation of your prayers and thanksgiving in these matters, urging us forward unto things ahead: Fare ye well.

[Woodcut Headpiece: Renaissance vase with lush floral bouquets, acanthus foliage, and scrollwork.]

*Instruction unto Priests, by our Father among the Saints, Basil the Great, Archbishop of Caesarea in Cappadocia:*[^15]

Take heed unto thyself, O priest, and unto those whom thou desirest to instruct; and watch over the ministry which thou hast received, that thou mayest fulfill it. For thou hast not been entrusted with an earthly ministry, but a heavenly one; not a human one, but an angelic one. Strive to present thyself unto the Lord as a workman who has no need to be ashamed, rightly dividing His word of truth.[^16]

*Instruction unto Priests.*

Neither stand thou ever in the assembly having enmity against anyone, lest thou drive away the Comforter.[^17] On the day of the assembly, be not quarrelsome, nor contradict at all; but pray in private and read until the hour of the service; and so with fear stand before the holy altar. Look not around, nor shorten the prayers in haste. Have no respect of persons, but behold the King Who lies before thee, lest hypocritically thou give the Holy Body where it is not fitting. Make thyself worthy of the priestly canons, and celebrate not with those whom the canons reject. Behold, therefore, how thou standest before God; how thou celebratest; how, and unto whom *Instruction unto Priests.*

thou distributest; and how thou consumest the Holy Gifts.[^18] Take heed lest thou forget the commandment of the Lord Who says: "Give not that which is holy unto the dogs," and what follows;[^19] lest thou be seized by human fear. Deliver not the Son of God into the hands of the unworthy. Be not ashamed before the glorious of the earth, nor before him who wears a diadem, lest thou be dismayed in that hour. Behold also concerning those who desire to receive the Gift in their houses, how thou impartest it; I have no part in this matter, thou shalt see to it.[^20] Behold, therefore, that thou give it not unto those whom the divine canons forbid, for they are accounted as heathens; and woe unto those who impart it unto them before their conversion! Behold also this:

*Concerning the Immolation and Preservation of the Holy Lamb.*

Lest in any way a mouse or anything else touch the Divine Mysteries; lest through thy negligence they become damp, or be blackened by smoke, or be delivered into the hands of the unordained. Keep these and things similar unto these, and thou shalt save both thyself and those who hear thee.[^21]

[Woodcut Headpiece: Foliate arabesque frieze with acanthus scrolls.]

*Concerning the Holy Lamb Immolated on Great and Holy Thursday:*[^22]

It is fitting for the priest to do thus, and to preserve the Holy Body of Christ: On Great and Holy Thursday, in the evening at the Liturgy, he prepares two Lambs, and celebrates the Liturgy with them;

*Instruction unto Priests.*

And after the exclamation: "Let us attend: The Holy Things for the holy," he celebrates with one of them at that time; but the other he moistens with the Blood of Christ upon the holy chalice, and he preserves it well with great fear and trembling.[^23] And let the prosphora not be unsuitable, or unbaked, or unsalted. And watch, O priest, with fear, and dry it so that it become not moldy, nor crumble. And on Tuesday of Bright Week, in the morning, having vested in all the priestly vestments, having washed his hands, and taken the censer, having censed the Holy Lamb upon the holy table, and having unfolded the antimension, and placed a small stone or plinth upon it, then he places a clay vessel with fire upon the stone;

*Concerning the Immolation and Preservation of the Holy Lamb.*

And having taken the Holy Lamb, he breaks it very small into minute particles upon the holy diskos prepared for this; and he sets the diskos upon the clay vessel over the fire.[^24] Then, taking the censer, having censed round about the holy table, he exclaims: "Glory to the holy, consubstantial, and life-creating [Trinity]!" Then he himself begins inside the altar: "Christ is risen," three times with chanting; and the rest of Matins according to custom; and having thus preserved the Holy Gifts with care in the vessel prepared for this, he makes the dismissal.

*Concerning how and when the Holy Lamb is consumed:*[^25]

*At the Liturgy, after the exclamation: "Let us attend: The Holy Things for the holy," when thou fillest the holy chalice, Of the Holy Lamb.*

*Here also insert a particle:* Put the first portion into the holy chalice on the feast of Saint Peter;[^26] the second portion, on the Dormition of the Most Holy Theotokos; the third portion, on the Nativity of Christ; and the fourth portion, again at Pascha.[^27]

[Woodcut Tailpiece: Renaissance strapwork cartouche with a grotesque bearded faun mask.]

[Verso of preliminary folio 10: Blank page exhibiting a faint ghost impression/offset of the woodcut mask tailpiece from folio 10r, along with a seventeenth-century cursive commemorative inscription in brown ink at the upper margin:] "Handmaid of God, the priest's wife Symvia; servant of God... Paraskovia, and Euphrosyne..."

INRI[^28]

### Notes

[^1]: The Striatyn Liturgikon (*Служебник*) of 1604 was printed at the private printing press established in Striatyn (near Rohatyn, Western Ukraine) by Bishop Hedeon Balaban of Lviv and his nephew Fedir Balaban, with typography directed by Pamvo Berynda. The woodcut architectural border features the Korczak heraldic arms of the Balaban family at the foot.
[^2]: The Church Slavonic title uses the collective titular formula *И́же въ ст҃ы́хъ ѿц҃ъ на́шихъ* ("Of our Fathers among the Saints") governing the three classical Byzantine liturgies: the Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom, the Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great, and the Divine Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts (attributed to St. Gregory the Dialogist).
[^3]: Striatyn (*Стрятин*), a town in the Ruthenian Voivodeship of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, was the seat of the Balaban press which issued two monumental liturgical editions: this 1604 Liturgikon (*Sluzhebnik*) and the 1606 Euchologion (*Trebnyk*), before its typographic equipment was acquired in 1615 by Archimandrite Yelysei Pletenetsky for the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra.
[^4]: Hedeon Balaban (c. 1530–1607) was the Orthodox Bishop of Lviv, Halych, and Kamianets-Podilskyi from 1569 until his death. Although initially participating in early discussions surrounding the Union of Brest, he formally rejected the Union in 1595–1596 and remained the primary hierarch defending the Eastern Orthodox Church in the Ruthenian lands of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth.
[^5]: The Balaban family bore the Polish-Ruthenian noble coat of arms *Korczak* (*Wręby*), which blazons three horizontal silver bars (*wręby*) on a red shield, surmounted by a helmet crest depicting a silver talbot/hound sitting within a golden bowl or floating basin.
[^6]: In 1589, Patriarch Jeremias II Tranos of Constantinople visited the Ruthenian lands and bestowed upon Bishop Hedeon Balaban the personal title of Patriarchal Exarch (*Exarch of the Ecumenical Throne*), granting him supervisory authority over Orthodox ecclesiastical affairs in the Crown lands.
[^7]: The Greek loanword *каѳоли́цкіѧ* (*katholike*) in Early Modern Ruthenian Church Slavonic signifies the universal, catholic dimension of the Eastern Orthodox Church, in accordance with the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed, rather than Roman Catholicism.
[^8]: St. Gregory of Nazianzus (the Theologian), *Oration 2: Apology for His Flight to Pontus*, §35: "As food is for the body, so is the word for the soul" (ὅπεр γὰρ σώματι τροφή, τοῦτο ψυχῇ λόγος).
[^9]: Meletios I Pegas (1549–1601), Pope and Patriarch of Alexandria (1590–1601). A prominent Greek theologian and humanist educated in Venice and Padua, he maintained active epistolary relations with the Ruthenian Orthodox hierarchy, Prince Konstantyn Ostrozky, and the Lviv Dormition Brotherhood, urging the defense of Orthodoxy through education and book printing.
[^10]: Following the death of Patriarch Matthew II of Constantinople in 1597, Patriarch Meletios Pegas of Alexandria was elected Locum Tenens (Guardian) of the Ecumenical Throne of Constantinople, administering the patriarchal see from 1597 until late 1598.
[^11]: Fedir (Theodore) Yuriiovych Balaban (d. 1606), nephew of Bishop Hedeon Balaban, elder or Starosta of Vinnytsia. Together with his uncle, he financed and established the Striatyn printing press on his Striatyn estate, importing Greek and Cyrillic typographic fonts and paper.
[^12]: The printing of the 1604 Liturgikon was undertaken to provide an authoritative, uncorrupted liturgical text for Orthodox clergy in the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, counteracting textual variations and Western Romanized influences following the 1596 Union of Brest.
[^13]: Church Slavonic *зводъ* (*zvod*) refers to a collation or recension of manuscript exemplars. The Striatyn editors, led by Pamvo Berynda, collected ancient South Slavic, East Slavic, and Venetian printed Greek Liturgika to establish their liturgical text.
[^14]: Bishop Hedeon Balaban and Pamvo Berynda intended the Striatyn press to print not only liturgical texts but also grammatical, patristic, and educational treatises in Church Slavonic, Greek, and Latin. Following the Liturgikon (1604) and Trebnyk (1606), the press also prepared materials that later culminated in Berynda's famous *Lexicon Slavenorossica* (Kyiv, 1627).
[^15]: The *Instruction to Priests* (*Поученіе к іереомъ*) attributed to St. Basil the Great was a standard prefatory text in East Slavic and South Slavic Liturgika from the 14th century onward (found in manuscript Euchologia and early imprints including the 1519 Bozhidar Vuković Liturgikon and 1583 Vilnius Liturgikon), expounding pastoral vigilance, worthiness before the altar, and reverent handling of the Holy Mysteries.
[^16]: An adaptation of 2 Timothy 2:15: "Study to show thyself approved unto God, a workman that needs not to be ashamed, rightly dividing the word of truth."
[^17]: Liturgical rubrics and patristic canons strictly require clergy to be reconciled with all persons before entering the sanctuary to celebrate the Divine Liturgy, in accordance with Matthew 5:23–24.
[^18]: Slavonic *съпрѧтоу́еши* (from *съпрѧтати*), meaning to consume the remaining Holy Mysteries and perform the ablutions of the sacred vessels after the Liturgy.
[^19]: Matthew 7:6: "Give not that which is holy unto the dogs, neither cast ye your pearls before swine."
[^20]: Alluding to the warning of Pontius Pilate in Matthew 27:24 ("See ye to it"), here invoked patristically to place the absolute burden of pastoral responsibility upon the administrating priest when taking Holy Communion to the sick in their homes.
[^21]: 1 Timothy 4:16: "Take heed unto thyself, and unto the doctrine; continue in them: for in doing this thou shalt both save thyself, and them that hear thee."
[^22]: This rubric details the traditional Byzantine rite for preparing and consecrating the Reserved Holy Sacrament (*Artos* / Holy Lamb for the sick and Viaticum) during the Vesperal Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great on Great and Holy Thursday.
[^23]: The rubric instructs the priest to intinct the second consecrated Lamb with the Precious Blood of Christ using the liturgical spoon (*spoonfuls applied to the soft crumb of the Lamb*) before carefully drying it for year-round reservation in the Tabernacle (*Artophorion* / *Darokhranitelnytsia*).
[^24]: The procedure for gently desiccating the intincted Eucharistic particles on Bright Tuesday using indirect, mild heat over an earthenware brazier (*скоудельница*) was standard in medieval and early modern Slavic Liturgika, preventing the damp particles from decaying or scorching.
[^25]: A distinctive pre-Nikonian and Southwestern Ruthenian rubrical provision: dividing the reserved Lamb into four designated portions and consuming them across the four principal annual fasting periods or great feasts of the liturgical year.
[^26]: The Feast of the Holy Preeminent Apostles Peter and Paul (June 29 / July 12), concluding the Apostles' Fast.
[^27]: The four great liturgical markers: Sts. Peter and Paul (end of Apostles' Fast), the Dormition of the Theotokos (August 15, concluding the Dormition Fast), the Nativity of Christ (December 25, concluding the Nativity Fast / Pylypivka), and Great and Holy Pascha, when the remaining reserved Lamb of the previous year is consumed before the new Lamb of Holy Thursday is consecrated.
[^28]: Cyrillic abbreviation *І҃НЦІ҃* representing the Titulus Crucis: *І҆исоу́съ Назарѧни́нъ Ца́рь І҆уде́йскїй* ("Jesus of Nazareth, King of the Jews"; Greek: ΙΝΒΙ; Latin: INRI).
