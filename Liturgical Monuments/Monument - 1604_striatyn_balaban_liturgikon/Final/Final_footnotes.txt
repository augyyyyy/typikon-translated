[^1]: The Striatyn Liturgikon (*Служебник*) of 1604 was printed at the private printing press established in Striatyn (near Rohatyn, Western Ukraine) by Bishop Hedeon Balaban of Lviv and his nephew Fedir Balaban, with typography directed by Pamvo Berynda. The woodcut architectural border features the Korczak heraldic arms of the Balaban family at the foot.
[^2]: The Church Slavonic title uses the collective titular formula *И́же въ ст҃ы́хъ ѿц҃ъ на́шихъ* ("Of our Fathers among the Saints") governing the three classical Byzantine liturgies: the Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom, the Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great, and the Divine Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts (attributed to St. Gregory the Dialogist).
[^3]: Striatyn (*Стрятин*), a town in the Ruthenian Voivodeship of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, was the seat of the Balaban press which issued two monumental liturgical editions: this 1604 Liturgikon (*Sluzhebnik*) and the 1606 Euchologion (*Trebnyk*), before its typographic equipment was acquired in 1615 by Archimandrite Yelysei Pletenetsky for the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra.
[^4]: Hedeon Balaban (c. 1530–1607) was the Orthodox Bishop of Lviv, Halych, and Kamianets-Podilskyi from 1569 until his death. Although initially participating in early discussions surrounding the Union of Brest, he formally rejected the Union in 1595–1596 and remained the primary hierarch defending the Eastern Orthodox Church in the Ruthenian lands of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth.
[^5]: The Balaban family bore the Polish-Ruthenian noble coat of arms *Korczak* (*Wręby*), which blazons three horizontal silver bars (*wręby*) on a red shield, surmounted by a helmet crest depicting a silver talbot/hound sitting within a golden bowl or floating basin.
[^6]: In 1589, Patriarch Jeremias II Tranos of Constantinople visited the Ruthenian lands and bestowed upon Bishop Hedeon Balaban the personal title of Patriarchal Exarch (*Exarch of the Ecumenical Throne*), granting him supervisory authority over Orthodox ecclesiastical affairs in the Crown lands.
[^7]: The Greek loanword *каѳоли́цкіѧ* (*katholike*) in Early Modern Ruthenian Church Slavonic signifies the universal, catholic dimension of the Eastern Orthodox Church, in accordance with the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed, rather than Roman Catholicism.
[^8]: St. Gregory of Nazianzus (the Theologian), *Oration 2: Apology for His Flight to Pontus*, §35: "As food is for the body, so is the word for the soul" (ὅπεр γὰρ σώματι τροφή, τοῦτο ψυχῇ λόγος).
[^9]: Meletios I Pegas (1549–1601), Pope and Patriarch of Alexandria (1590–1601). A prominent Greek theologian and humanist educated in Venice and Padua, he maintained active epistolary relations with the Ruthenian Orthodox hierarchy, Prince Konstantyn Ostrozky, and the Lviv Dormition Brotherhood, urging the defense of Orthodoxy through education and book printing.
[^10]: Following the death of Patriarch Matthew II of Constantinople in 1597, Patriarch Meletios Pegas of Alexandria was elected Locum Tenens (Guardian) of the Ecumenical Throne of Constantinople, administering the patriarchal see from 1597 until late 1598.
[^11]: Fedir (Theodore) Yuriiovych Balaban (d. 1606), nephew of Bishop Hedeon Balaban, elder or Starosta of Vinnytsia. Together with his uncle, he financed and established the Striatyn printing press on his Striatyn estate, importing Greek and Cyrillic typographic fonts and paper.
[^12]: The printing of the 1604 Liturgikon was undertaken to provide an authoritative, uncorrupted liturgical text for Orthodox clergy in the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, counteracting textual variations and Western Romanized influences following the 1596 Union of Brest.
[^13]: Church Slavonic *зводъ* (*zvod*) refers to a collation or recension of manuscript exemplars. The Striatyn editors, led by Pamvo Berynda, collected ancient South Slavic, East Slavic, and Venetian printed Greek Liturgika to establish their liturgical text.
[^14]: Bishop Hedeon Balaban and Pamvo Berynda intended the Striatyn press to print not only liturgical texts but also grammatical, patristic, and educational treatises in Church Slavonic, Greek, and Latin. Following the Liturgikon (1604) and Trebnyk (1606), the press also prepared materials that later culminated in Berynda's famous *Lexicon Slavenorossica* (Kyiv, 1627).

[^241]: The celebrant's personal preparation for the Kiss of Peace: bowing three times secretly at the altar and reciting Psalm 17:2–3 [LXX: 17:2–3; MT: 18:1–2]: "I will love Thee, O Lord, my strength; the Lord is my support, and my refuge."
[^242]: The ritual Kiss of Peace among concelebrating clergy: the celebrant kisses the veiled holy gifts (diskos, chalice, and altar edge); when concelebrating, the superior gives the greeting "Christ is in our midst" (Хрⷵто́съ посредѣ̀ на́съ), and the respondent answers "He is and shall be" (И҆ є҆́сть и҆ бꙋ́детъ); deacons likewise kiss the cross embroidered on their orarions and each other.
[^243]: The deacon on the ambo likewise bows, kisses the cross on his orarion, and proclaims the ancient guard of the sanctuary doors: "The doors! The doors! In wisdom let us give heed!" (Две́ри две́ри премꙋ́дростїю вънмемъ), commanding that the church doors be closed against unbelievers and catechumens as the Creed begins.
[^244]: The Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed (Symbol of Faith, И҆сповѣ́данїе вѣ́ры / Вѣ́рꙋю), chanted by the choir and recited by all the faithful: Articles 1–4, confessing God the Father Almighty, the consubstantial Only-begotten Son, the Incarnation of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary, and the Crucifixion under Pontius Pilate.
[^245]: The Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed (Articles 5–8): confessing Christ's Burial, Resurrection on the third day according to the Scriptures, Ascension into heaven, session at the right hand of the Father, and future coming in glory to judge the living and the dead; and the Holy Spirit, Lord and Giver of Life, Who proceeds from the Father (strictly without Filioque, conforming to the authentic Eastern Orthodox text).
[^246]: Conclusion of the Creed (Articles 9–12): confessing one holy, catholic, and apostolic Church; one baptism for the remission of sins; the resurrection of the dead; and the life of the world to come.
[^247]: The deacon's solemn call immediately following the Creed, opening the Eucharistic Anaphora: "Let us stand well, let us stand with fear; let us give heed to offer the holy oblation in peace" (Ста́нѣмъ до́брѣ, ста́нѣмъ съ стра́хомъ; вънмѣ́мъ ст҃ое възноше́нїе съ ми́ромъ приноси́ти), to which the choir responds with the sacrifice of praise: "A mercy of peace, a sacrifice of praise" (Млⷵть ми́ръ, же́ртвꙋ хвале́нїа).

[^248]: Rubric on the removal and folding of the aer: following the choir's completion of the response to the call to the Anaphora ("A mercy of peace, a sacrifice of praise"), the celebrant lifts the aer (large veil) from the Holy Gifts, recites the Trisagion ("Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, have mercy on us"), kisses the veil, and sets it aside; meanwhile the deacon re-enters the sanctuary and folds the aer and the two smaller chalice/diskos veils (покровцы), laying them neatly upon the holy Table.
[^249]: The solemn Pauline Trinitarian greeting (2 Cor. 13:13) opening the Eucharistic Dialogue: "The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God the Father, and the communion of the Holy Spirit, be with you all" (Бл҃года́ть гдⷵа на́шего І҆ѵ҃ Хрⷵта̀, и҆ любо́вь бг҃а и҆ ѻ҆ц҃а, и҆ прича́стїе ст҃го дх҃а, бꙋ́ди со всѣ́ми ва́ми).
[^250]: Sursum Corda ("Lift up our hearts" / "Горѣ̀ имѣ́имъ сердца̀") and the Eucharistic invitation to thanksgiving ("Let us give thanks unto the Lord" / "Бл҃годари́мъ гдⷵа"), with the congregation's confession of the undivided Trinity. In this 1604 edition, the choir response reads "Досто́йно и҆ пра́ведно є҆́, покланѧ́ти ѻ҆ц҃ꙋ сн҃ꙋ и҆ ст҃мꙋ дх҃ꙋ" (without the reflexive -ся on покланяти), reflecting Early Modern Ruthenian Church Slavonic syntax.
[^251]: The Eucharistic Preface (Prayer of Thanksgiving) recited secretly by the celebrant: confessing God as ineffable (неизрече́ненъ), incomprehensible (недовѣ́домъ), invisible (неви́димъ), beyond understanding (непостижи́мъ), existing eternally the same, together with His Only-begotten Son and Holy Spirit.
[^252]: The commemoration of divine providential economy: recalling creation ex nihilo ("from non-existence into being"), restoration after the Fall, the promise of the heavenly kingdom, and offering thanksgiving for all benefits known and unknown, manifest and unmanifest (ꙗ҆вле́нныхъ и҆ неꙗ҆вле́нныхъ бл҃годѣѧ́нїи).
[^253]: Commemoration of the celestial hierarchy surrounding the throne of God: thousands of angels and myriads of archangels, the cherubim and seraphim, six-winged, many-eyed, soaring aloft upon their wings (шестокрыла́таѧ, многоѡчи́таѧ възвыша́ющаѧсѧ перна́таѧ).
[^254]: The celebrant's Triumphal Exclamation introducing the Sanctus: "Singing the triumphal hymn, shouting, crying aloud, and saying" (Побѣ́днꙋю пѣ́снь, пою́ще, вопїю́ще, възыва́юще и҆ гл҃юще), uniting the earthly liturgy with the perpetual worship of the celestial powers (Isa. 6:3; Matt. 21:9).
[^255]: Rubrics for the asterisk and ripidion: the deacon removes the asterisk from the diskos, makes the sign of the cross over the diskos with it, wipes its feet upon the antimension/eiliton (і҆ ѡ҆ти́рае кра́и ѡ҆ илито́н), kisses it, and sets it aside with the aer; he then takes the liturgical fan (ripidion) and gently fans the Holy Gifts to guard them with reverence and prevent any insects from alighting.
[^256]: The Post-Sanctus prayer ("With these blessed powers, O Master, Lover of mankind, we also cry aloud and say..."): confessing the absolute holiness and majesty of God and His love for the world in giving His Only-begotten Son (John 3:16).
[^257]: The Anamnesis of the Mystical Supper: "Who having come and fulfilled all the dispensation for us, in the night in which He was betrayed—or rather, surrendered Himself for the life of the world..." (въ но́щь въню́же преда́нъ быва́ше, па́че же са́мъ сѧ предаѧ́ше за мі́рскїй живо́тъ).
[^258]: The Words of Institution over the Bread: taking bread into His holy, pure, and blameless hands, giving thanks, blessing, hallowing, breaking, and giving it to His holy disciples and apostles, saying: "Take, eat: this is My Body, which is broken for you for the remission of sins" (Прїими́те, ꙗ҆ди́те: се́ є҆стъ тѣ́ло моѐ, є҆́же за вы̀ ломи́мое къ ѡ҆ставле́нїе грѣхѡ́въ).
[^259]: Rubric for pointing and blessing: the deacon indicates the holy diskos with his orarion held by three fingers of his right hand; the priest secretly introduces the blessing over the cup ("Likewise also the cup after supper, saying"), elevates his right hand, and blesses the holy chalice with the exclamation.
[^260]: The Words of Institution over the Cup: "Drink of it, all of you: this is My Blood of the New Testament, which is shed for you and for many for the remission of sins" (Пі́йте ѿ неѧ̀ всѝ: се́ є҆стъ кръ́вь моѧ̀ но́ваго завѣ́та, є҆́же за вы̀ и҆ за мно́ги и҆злива́емаѧ въ ѡ҆ставле́нїе грѣхѡ́въ; Matt. 26:27–28).
[^261]: The celebrant's secret anamnetic prayer commemorating the entire saving economy: the Cross, the Tomb, the Resurrection on the third day, the Ascension into heaven, the Session at the right hand of the Father, and the second and glorious Coming again (и҆ втора́го сла́внаго па́ки прише́ствїа).
[^262]: The Solemn Oblation: "Thine own of Thine own, we offer unto Thee, on behalf of all and for all" (Твоѧ̑ ѿ твои́хъ, тебѣ̀ приносѧ́ще, ѡ҆ всѣ́хъ и҆ за всѧ̑), while the choir responds with the hymn of adoration and thanksgiving: "We sing unto Thee, we bless Thee, we give thanks unto Thee, O Lord, and we pray unto Thee, O our God."
[^263]: Rubric for the Elevation of the Gifts during the Oblation: the deacon lays aside the ripidion, crosses his right arm over his left, and elevates the diskos and chalice together, bowing with compunction. If two deacons serve, one elevates the diskos and the other the chalice from opposite sides of the altar.
[^264]: The opening petition of the Epiklesis: "Yet we offer unto Thee this rational and unbloody worship, and we ask, and pray, and suppliantly beseech Thee: send down Thy Holy Spirit upon us, and upon these gifts here spread forth" (Є҆щѐ прино́симъ ти слове́снꙋю сїю̀ и҆ безкръ́внꙋю слꙋ́жбꙋ...).
[^265]: A distinctive Ruthenian practical rubric concerning the physical state of the Holy Lamb: noting that if the prosphora was warm when pierced during the Proskomide and was turned cross-downward to prevent steam/condensation from softening it from below, it must now be turned back cross-upward prior to the invocation of the Holy Spirit.
[^266]: Preparation for the Epiklesis: the deacon approaches the celebrant, and both make three metanias (bows/prostrations) before the holy Table in preparation for the descent of the Holy Spirit.
[^267]: The Troparion of the Third Hour ("O Lord, Who didst send down Thy Most Holy Spirit at the third hour upon Thine Apostles..."), interpolated into the Anaphora with its two psalm verses (Ps. 50:12–13 [LXX: 50:12–13; MT: 51:10–11]). This interpolation was standard in Slavic liturgical practice prior to the Nikonian and 20th-century Greek liturgical reforms.
[^268]: The first blessing of the Epiklesis: the deacon bows his head, points with his orarion to the holy bread, and says "Bless, master, the holy bread"; the priest elevates his hand, signs the holy bread with the cross, and pronounces: "And make this bread the precious Body of Thy Christ."
[^269]: The second blessing of the Epiklesis: the deacon asks "Bless, master, the holy cup"; the priest blesses the chalice, pronouncing: "And that which is in this cup, the precious Blood of Thy Christ."
[^270]: The third blessing and consecratory clause of the Epiklesis: the deacon points to both the bread and the cup, requesting "Bless, master, both"; the celebrant blesses both together, pronouncing: "Changing them by Thy Holy Spirit" (Преложи́въ дх҃омъ твои́мъ ст҃ы́мъ), to which the deacon responds with the triple ratification: "Amen. Amen. Amen."
[^271]: Post-consecration mutual petition: the deacon bows to the priest, asking "Remember me, holy master, a sinner," and the celebrant blesses him, praying: "May the Lord God remember thee in His kingdom, always, now and ever, and unto ages of ages."
[^272]: Post-consecration rubrics: the deacon returns to his station at the altar and resumes fanning the Holy Gifts with the ripidion; if two deacons serve, one censes the altar while the other fans; if only one serves, he lays down the ripidion to cense.
[^273]: The fruit of communion: the celebrant's secret prayer that the Holy Mysteries may be for those who partake for vigilance of soul (въ трезве́нїе дꙋша̀), remission of sins, communion of the Holy Spirit, fulfillment of the heavenly kingdom, and boldness toward God, not for judgment or condemnation.
[^274]: Commemoration of the ranks of the righteous: offering the rational worship for the departed forefathers, fathers, patriarchs, prophets, apostles, preachers, evangelists, martyrs, confessors, ascetics, and every righteous spirit made perfect in faith.
[^275]: Exclamation in honor of the Mother of God: the priest proclaims aloud the commemoration of the Most Holy, Pure, Most Blessed, and Glorious Lady, Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary, while the deacon finishes censing the sanctuary.
[^276]: The Marian Megalynarion (*Axion Estin* / Досто́йно є҆́стъ): "It is truly proper to bless thee, O Theotokos, ever-blessed and all-blameless, and the Mother of our God," chanted by the choir in honor of the Theotokos while the celebrant secretly commemorates the living and the dead.

[^277]: The conclusion of the Marian Megalynarion (*Axion Estin* / Достойно есть): "More honorable than the cherubim, and beyond compare more glorious than the seraphim, who without corruption didst bear God the Word, very Theotokos, thee do we magnify" (Чтⷵнѣ́йшꙋю херꙋві́мъ, и҆ славнѣ́йшꙋю без̾разсꙋжде́нїа серафі́мъ...), followed by the rubrical provision: "Or of the feast" (И҆лѝ пра́зникꙋ). On Great Feasts of the Lord and of the Mother of God, as well as throughout their post-festive periods (попразднство), the ordinary hymn to the Theotokos is replaced by the proper Zadostoinyk (the ninth heirmos of the festal canon, often preceded by its proper refrain/prispiv).
[^278]: Rubric for the Diptychs (*Diptycha* / сирѣчь помѣнникъ): "The deacon commemorates also the diptychs, that is, the pomiannyk, of the living and of the departed whom he wills." Striatyn explicitly glosses the Greek-derived ecclesiastical term *diptycha* (дїпти́ха) with the living East Slavic vernacular/pastoral liturgical term *pomiannyk* / *pom'iannyk* (помѣ́нникъ), indicating the physical memorial booklet containing the baptismal and monastic names of benefactors and faithful brought to the altar.
[^279]: The Intercessory Commemoration of the Saints: the celebrant continues the secret prayer of the Anaphora, commemorating the Prophet, Forerunner, and Baptist John, the holy and all-praiseworthy apostles, the patron or saint of the day whose memory is observed ("ст҃го И҆́мк̾, є҆го́же и҆ па́мѧть съвръша́емъ"), and all the saints; he then turns to the commemoration of the dead: "And remember all those who have fallen asleep before in the hope of the resurrection unto life eternal" (И҆ поманѝ всѣ́хъ пре́жде ꙋ҆со́пшихъ ѡ҆ наде́жи въскрⷵсенїа живота̀ вѣ́чнаго).
[^280]: Rubric for the celebrant's personal commemorations: "Here the priest commemorates whom he wills of the living and the dead, and says" (Здѣ̀ і҆ере́й по́минает и҆́хже хо́щетъ живы́х и҆ ꙋ҆ме́ршихъ · и҆ гл҃етъ). The celebrant privately pauses to remember his spiritual children, family, clergy, and benefactors, using the prescribed formulas recorded on the subsequent folio.
[^281]: Liturgical formulas for personal commemoration: for the living, praying for "salvation, visitation, and remission of sins of the servant of God, [Name]" (О҆ сп҃се́нїи посѣще́нїи ѡ҆ставле́нїи грѣхѡ́въ ра́ба бж҃їа); for the departed, praying for "repose and remission of the soul of Thy servant, [having said the name]: In a place of light, whence pain, sorrow, and sighing have fled away, give them rest, O our God; and give them rest where the light of Thy countenance shines" (cf. Isa. 35:10; Ps. 4:7 [LXX: 4:7]).
[^282]: Commemoration of the Orthodox Hierarchy: "Again we pray Thee, remember, O Lord, every episcopate of the Orthodox, rightly dividing the word of Thy truth" (є҆ппⷵства̀ правосла́вны, пра́во пра́вѧщи сло́во твоеѧ̀ и҆́стинны; cf. 2 Tim. 2:15), embracing the entire collegiate episcopate faithful to Orthodox dogma, followed by the presbyterate, the diaconate in Christ, and every sacred order.
[^283]: Intercession for the Universal Church, Civil Realm, and the Sovereign: offering the rational worship for the holy Catholic and Apostolic Church, for those living in purity and honorable estate, and "for our great King, [having said the name]" (ѡ҆ вели́комъ кра́ли на́шемъ И҆́мѧ ре́къ). Striatyn and the Lviv Eparchy were situated within the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth; this printed commemoration specifically witnesses the royal titulature of King Sigismund III Vasa (Zygmunt III Waza, reigned 1587–1632).
[^284]: Manuscript marginalia and liturgical diptychs: fol. 89v–90r preserves contemporary 17th-century and slightly later cursive Ruthenian/Slavonic ink inscriptions in the margins, recording individual names for remembrance (e.g. Melaniia, Evstafii, Tatiana, Lazar, Hryhorii, Ioann), demonstrating the continuous pastoral and altar use of this physical copy of the 1604 Striatyn Liturgikon.
[^285]: Ruthenian socio-political terminology in the intercession: the celebrant prays for "our lords and rulers" (гс᷵преи пано́въ на́шихъ / господей панов наших), combining Church Slavonic *gospod'* with the vernacular Polish/Ruthenian title *panove* (the szlachta nobility and magnates of the Commonwealth), their whole court/palace (пола́та), and their armed forces (во́инство), that under their rule the faithful may lead a calm and quiet life in all godliness and purity (cf. 1 Tim. 2:2).
[^286]: The Diaconal Ratification: the deacon turns toward the holy doors holding his orarion aloft with the tips of his fingers and exclaims to the faithful: "And of all men and women" (И҆ всѣ́хъ и҆ всѧ̑, literally "And all and all"), to which the choir responds in kind, ratifying the celebrant's commemorations before the congregation.
[^287]: Exclamation for the Hierarch: the celebrant exclaims aloud: "Among the first, remember, O Lord, our Bishop, [having said the name]; whom do Thou grant unto Thy holy churches in peace, whole, honorable, healthy, in length of days, rightly dividing the word of Thy truth" (Въ пе́рвы поманѝ гн҃и є҆ппⷵа на́шего, И҆́мѧ ре́къ, є҆го́же дарꙋ́и ст҃ы̑м твои́мъ цр҃квамъ...). In this eparchy, this was Bishop Hedeon Balaban of Lviv, the publisher and patron of the Striatyn press.
[^288]: The Deacon's Solemn Soleal Commemorations: from the solea, the deacon intones aloud the name of the local bishop, the celebrating priests offering the Holy Gifts, and the salvation of the King.
[^289]: Conclusion of the diaconal commemorations: the deacon commemorates the civil lords and nobles and concludes "and of all men and women," answered by the choir; he then reads from his personal pomiannyk of the living while the priest continues the secret intercessions.
[^290]: Intercessory Prayer for the City, Travellers, the Afflicted, and Benefactors: the celebrant secretly prays for the city in which the community dwells, every city and countryside, travelers by water and land, the sick, the suffering, those who bear fruit and do good works in the holy churches, and those who remember the poor, beseeching the divine mercy upon all.
[^291]: Doxological Exclamation of Unity: "And grant us with one mouth and one heart to glorify and praise Thine all-honorable and magnificent Name, of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit..." (cf. Rom. 15:6), concluding the great intercessory section of the Anaphora.
[^292]: The Pax and Pauline Blessing: turning toward the congregation, the celebrant blesses them: "And may the mercies of the great God and our Savior Jesus Christ be with you all" (cf. Titus 2:13), marking the formal transition from the Eucharistic Anaphora to the communion preparation rites.
[^293]: The Litany before the Lord's Prayer (Aitēseis / Prosbna Litaniia): having received the liturgical blessing from the celebrant ("прїе́мъ вре́мѧ ѿ і҆ере́а"), the deacon exits the sanctuary through the northern deacon's door and stands in his customary place on the ambo, intoning the petition: "Having commemorated all the saints, again and again in peace let us pray unto the Lord."
[^294]: Petition for the spiritual acceptance of the Consecrated Gifts: praying that God the Lover of mankind, having received the precious gifts upon His holy, heavenly, and spiritual altar for an odor of spiritual sweetness, may send down upon the communicants divine grace and the gift of the Holy Spirit.
[^295]: The Prayer before the Lord's Prayer (Prayer of Commendation): the celebrant bows and secretly prays: "Unto Thee we commit our whole life and hope, O Master, Lover of mankind..." (Тебѣ̀ прелага́ем живо́тъ на́шъ ве́съ и҆ наде́ждꙋ), asking that the clergy and faithful may be deemed worthy to partake of the heavenly and sacred mysteries of this spiritual Table with a pure conscience.
[^296]: The Angel of Peace Petitions: the deacon intones the specific requests of the Aitēseis: asking for a day that is perfect, holy, peaceful, and sinless, and for "an angel of peace, a faithful guide, a guardian of our souls and bodies."
[^297]: Petitions for Repentance, a Christian End, and Unity: the litany concludes with petitions for pardon of sins, things profitable for souls, completion of life in repentance, a Christian, painless, blameless end before the dread judgment seat of Christ, and asking for the unity of faith and communion of the Holy Spirit.
[^298]: Celebrant's Exclamation introducing the Paternoster: "And vouchsafe us, O Master, with boldness, without condemnation, to dare to call upon Thee, the heavenly God and Father, and to say" (И҆ сподо́би на́съ влⷣко, съ дерзнове́нїем неосꙋжде́нно смѣ́ти призыва́ти тебѐ нб҃наго бг҃а ѻ҆ц҃а и҆ гл҃ати), introducing the chanting of the Lord's Prayer.
[^299]: The Lord's Prayer (*Otche Nash* / Matt. 6:9–13): in early modern Ruthenian and Slavic practice, the rubric explicitly assigns the recitation or singing of the prayer to the congregation ("The people" / Лю́дїе), underscoring the corporate baptismal adoption and boldness of the faithful standing before God as His children prior to Holy Communion.
[^300]: The Doxology of the Lord's Prayer and the Pax: the celebrant exclaims the Trinitarian doxology ("For Thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory..."), followed immediately by the liturgical greeting of peace ("Peace be to all") and the deacon's bidding: "Bow your heads unto the Lord."
[^301]: The Prayer of Head-Bowing (Prayer of Inklisis / Inclinatio): the celebrant bows and prays secretly: "We give thanks unto Thee, O King invisible, Who by Thine immeasurable power hast created all things, and in the multitude of Thy mercy hast brought all things from non-existence into being..." (Бл҃годари́мъ тѧ̀ цр҃ю неви́димыи · и҆́же неизче́тною твое́ю си́лою всѧ́ческаѧ съдѣ́телствовалъ є҆сѝ...).
[^302]: Pastoral Intercessions in the Prayer of Head-Bowing: the celebrant asks the Lord to look down from heaven upon those who have bowed their heads before the awesome God, and to smooth the way for each according to individual need: sailing with those who sail, journeying with travelers by land, and healing the sick as the Physician of souls and bodies.
[^303]: Trinitarian Exclamation of the Inklisis: "Through the grace and compassions and love for mankind of Thine Only-begotten Son, with Whom Thou art blessed, together with Thine all-holy, good, and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages."
[^304]: The Prayer before the Elevation: the celebrant prays secretly: "Give ear, O Lord Jesus Christ our God, from Thy holy dwelling place, and from the throne of glory of Thy kingdom, and come to sanctify us..." (Вньмѝ гн҃и і҆ѵ҃ хрⷵте бж҃е на́шъ, ѿ ст҃го жили́ща твое́го...), invoking Christ Who sits on high with the Father and is invisibly present to impart His all-pure Body and precious Blood to the clergy and through them to all the people.
[^305]: The Triple Metanias and Prayer of Cleansing: both celebrant and deacon bow thrice before the altar, reciting secretly: "O God, cleanse me a sinner, and have mercy on me" (Бж҃е ѡ҆чи́сти мѧ̀ грѣ́шнаго, и҆ поми́лꙋй мѧ̀; cf. Luke 18:13); the priest kisses the holy Table, while the faithful in the nave likewise bow down with reverence in awe of the imminent elevation.
[^306]: The Rubric of the Elevation (*Voznoshenie*): the deacon attentively watches the celebrant; as soon as the priest extends his hands and touches the Holy Lamb to elevate it above the diskos, the deacon prepares to proclaim aloud the exclamation: "Let us be attentive!" (*Vozmem* / *Proschōmen*).

[^307]: The Deacon's exclamation "Let us be attentive!" (*Vozmem* / Вь́нмемъ / *Proschōmen*), concluding the rubrical actions of the elevation begun on fol. 98r.
[^308]: The Elevation exclamation (*Sancta Sanctis* / Ст҃а́а ст҃ы̑мъ) and the responsive Christological confession of the choir: "One is holy, one is Lord, Jesus Christ, unto the glory of God the Father. Amen" (cf. Phil. 2:11), acknowledging that Christ alone possesses essential holiness, of which the faithful partake.
[^309]: The singing of the Communion Hymn (*kinonikon* / причастен), the communion verse appointed for the liturgical day, feast, or commemorated saint.
[^310]: The deacon's preparation: he crosses his orarion over his back and shoulders in the form of a cross (crosswise / крⷵтоѡ҆бра́зно), enters the sanctuary through the northern doors, stands to the right of the celebrant, and bids him: "Break, Master, the Holy Bread" (Раздробѝ влⷣко ст҃ы́и хлѣ́бъ).
[^311]: The Fraction Formula (*Klasis* / Раздробленіе): the celebrant breaks the consecrated Lamb into four quarters along the cross-incisions made during the Prothesis, reciting: "Broken and divided is the Lamb of God, the Son of the Father, broken and not divided, Who is ever eaten and never consumed, but sanctifies those who partake thereof" (Раздроблѧ́етсѧ и҆ раздѣлѧ́етсѧ а҆́гнецъ бж҃їи сн҃ъ ѻ҆ц҃ь...).
[^312]: Heading for the detailed manual rubrics: "Concerning the breaking of the Holy Lamb: The priest ought to know (Behold)" (*Ѡ҆ раздроблє́нїи ст҃го а҆́гнца · Подобѣ́ти вѣ́дати сщ҃ен́ни́кꙋ, зрѝ*), introducing the arrangement of the four quarters on the diskos.
[^313]: Rubric on the orientation of the fractured quarters: the quarters are placed with the inscribed/stamped cross downward upon the diskos and the cut soft side facing upward, mirroring their orientation during the sacrificial incision at the Prothesis.
[^314]: The cruciform arrangement of the four particles upon the diskos: IC (І҆С҃) is placed at the upper (eastern) side of the diskos; XC (ХС҃) at the lower (western) side toward the celebrant; NI (НИ́) to the right (southern) side facing the chalice; and KA (КА́) to the left (northern) side.
[^315]: The IC portion is reserved exclusively for the commixture (infusion into the chalice to signify the fullness of the Holy Spirit).
[^316]: The XC portion is reserved exclusively for the communion of the clergy (celebrating priests and deacons).
[^317]: The NI and KA portions are reserved exclusively for the communion of the laity, to be divided into small particles according to the number of communicants.
[^318]: Strict anti-confusion prohibition: the celebrant is explicitly forbidden from communing anyone from the commemorative particles of the Most Holy Theotokos, the nine orders of angelic and saintly ranks, or the living and dead; Holy Communion may be administered solely and exclusively from the consecrated Lamb itself.
[^319]: Liturgical realia: *okropnyk* (оу҆кро́пникъ / *ukropnyk*), the vessel containing the hot or tepid water (*teplota* / ζέον) used for the commixture; the celebrant or deacon must estimate the amount of hot water with discernment according to the number of communicants.
[^320]: Rubric regarding the proportion of wine and water poured into the chalice during the Prothesis: sufficient volume must be prepared from the outset to accommodate all expected communicants.
[^321]: Prohibition of subsequent additions: once the Anaphora is completed, no additional wine or water may ever be poured into the chalice; the single infusion of *zeon* (*teplota*) at the elevation is the only permitted addition prior to communion.
[^322]: Diaconal bidding for the Commixture (*Pleroma*): pointing toward the chalice with his orarion, the deacon says: "Fill, Master, the holy cup" (І҆сполнѝ влⷣко ст҃ꙋю ча́шꙋ).
[^323]: The Commixture Formula: the celebrant makes the sign of the cross over the chalice with the IC portion and places it into the Precious Blood, saying: "The fullness of the Holy Spirit" (*Pleroma Pneumatos Hagiou* / И҆сплъне́нїе ст҃го дх҃а), signifying the reunion of Christ's Body and Blood in the Resurrection through the vivifying operation of the Holy Spirit.
[^324]: The *Zeon* (*Teplota* / теплота́): the hot water brought to the altar, signifying the fervor of faith, the living fire of the Holy Spirit, and the blood and warm water that flowed from Christ's side (John 19:34).
[^325]: Diaconal petition for blessing the warm water: "Bless, Master, this holy warmth" (Бл҃вѝ влⷣко ст҃ꙋю теплотꙋ̀ сїю̀).
[^326]: The celebrant's blessing formula over the *zeon*: "Blessed is the warmth of Thy holy things, always, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen" (Бл҃венна теплота̀ ст҃ы́хъ твои́хъ...), adhering strictly to the Father Paul Doxology Standard.
[^327]: The Infusion Formula: pouring the hot water crosswise into the chalice, the deacon exclaims: "The warmth of faith, full of the Holy Spirit. Amen" (Теплота̀ вѣ́ры и҆спо́нъ дх҃а ст҃го, а҆ми́нь).
[^328]: The celebrant's liturgical invitation summoning the deacon to receive the Holy Bread: "Deacon, draw near" (Дїа́коне пристꙋпѝ).
[^329]: Ceremonial actions of the deacon: making a metania (reverent prostration), asking forgiveness, kissing the celebrant's giving hand, and receiving the Holy Body in the palm of his right hand supported by the left.
[^330]: The deacon's formal communion petition: "Impart unto me, Master, the precious and holy Body of our Lord and God and Savior Jesus Christ" (Препода́ждь ми влⷣко чⷵтное и҆ ст҃о́е тѣ́ло...).
[^331]: The administration formula to the deacon, inserting the deacon's baptismal name: "Unto the deacon [Name] is imparted the precious and holy and all-pure Body of our Lord and God and Savior Jesus Christ..."
[^332]: Conclusion of the administration formula: "unto the remission of his sins and unto life eternal" (въ ѡ҆ставле́нїе грѣхѡ́въ въ жи́знь вѣ́чнꙋю).
[^333]: Rubric for the deacon's private devotions: he withdraws behind the holy Table (to the eastern side), bowing his head and reciting the pre-communion prayers silently in unison with the celebrant.
[^334]: The celebrant's self-communion formula: taking a portion of the Holy Bread from the XC quarter, he names himself: "The precious and all-pure Body of our Lord and God and Savior Jesus Christ is imparted unto me, the priest [Name], unto the remission of my sins and unto life eternal."
[^335]: Secret Pre-Communion Prayer of the Celebrant: *Mnozhestva radi hriêkh moikh* ("For the multitude of my sins, turn not away from me, O Master, Lord my God..."). A distinctive feature of early modern Kyivan and South Slavic Euchologies, providing an extended personal penitential apologia for the officiating minister before the dread altar.
[^336]: Doxological conclusion of the prayer *Mnozhestva radi*: the celebrant contemplates the fractured Lamb upon the altar and asks the Holy Spirit, the Comforter and Purifier, to strengthen him, offering Trinitarian praise "unto ages of ages. Amen."
[^337]: Second Secret Pre-Communion Prayer: *Hospody, nêsmy dostoyn* ("Lord, I am not worthy that Thou enter under the defiled roof of my soul..."), an ancient patristic prayer adapting the centurion's humble confession (Matt. 8:8; Luke 7:6) and recalling Christ's mercy in the Nativity manger (Luke 2:7), the home of Simon the Leper (Matt. 26:6), and toward the penitent sinful woman (Luke 7:37–38).
[^338]: Conclusion of the prayer *Hospody, nêsmy dostoyn*, imploring Christ not to shrink in abhorrence from the priest's dead and leprous body, but to vouchsafe him communion in His all-holy Body and Blood.
[^339]: Third Secret Pre-Communion Prayer: *Bozhe nash, oslaby, opusty, prosty* ("O our God, remit, pardon, forgive me my transgressions..."), beseeching remission of sins committed knowingly or unknowingly, in word or in deed, through the intercessions of the Most Holy Theotokos.
[^340]: Conclusion and Trinitarian doxology of the third pre-communion prayer, praying that the reception of the Holy Body be unto the healing of soul and body "unto ages of ages. Amen."
[^341]: The Pre-Communion Confession of Faith: *Vêruiu, Hospody, i ispovêduiu* ("I believe, O Lord, and I confess that Thou art truly the Christ..."), based on 1 Tim. 1:15 and Matt. 16:16, the canonical Byzantine confession of faith in the Incarnation, the Divinity of Christ, and the true presence in the Eucharist.
[^342]: The Great Thursday Troparion: *Vecheri Tvoieia tainyia dnes* ("Of Thy mystical supper, O Son of God, receive me today as a partaker..."), confessing the mystery without betrayal and crying out with the penitent thief (Luke 23:42).
[^343]: The Final Supplication: *Da ne v sud ili v osuzhdenie* ("Let not the communion of Thy holy mysteries, O Lord, be unto me for judgment or for condemnation..."), based on 1 Cor. 11:29, entreating that communion be for the healing of soul and body.
[^344]: The Celebrant's Communion of the Body: consuming the particle of the Holy Bread with fear and all caution from his right palm.
[^345]: The Celebrant's Communion of the Blood: taking the holy chalice with both hands using the liturgical veil (*pokrovets*), sipping thrice from the Precious Blood, and wiping his lips and the rim of the chalice with the veil.
[^346]: The Isaiah Post-Communion Formula: the celebrant recites Isa. 6:7 (*Se prykosnusia ustnam moym...* / "Behold, this has touched my lips, and shall take away mine iniquities, and cleanse my sins"), recalling the seraph touching the prophet's lips with a live coal from the altar.
[^347]: The Celebrant summons the deacon for communion from the holy chalice.
[^348]: The Deacon's approach: bowing once and reciting the incipit *Se prykhozhdu k bezsmertnomu Tsariu* ("Behold, I draw near unto the immortal King"), followed by the entire prayer *Vêruiu, Hospody* (*all of it* / *vse*).
