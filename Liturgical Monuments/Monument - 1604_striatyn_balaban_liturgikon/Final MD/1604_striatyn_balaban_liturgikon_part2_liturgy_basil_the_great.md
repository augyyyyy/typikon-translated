# PART II: THE DIVINE LITURGY OF SAINT BASIL THE GREAT [^393] [^392]

## Order of the Divine Service of Our Holy Father Basil the Great, Archbishop of Caesarea in Cappadocia

*The Prothesis is performed according to all the customary order, exactly as in the Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom, all the way to the dismissal.*[^394]

*Order of the Divine Liturgy*

*The priest ought to know (Behold) that the Divine Liturgy of our holy and great father Basil is sung ten times throughout the year:[^395] On the five Sundays of the Holy Great Fast; and on Holy and Great Thursday; and on Holy and Great Saturday, without any variation whatsoever; likewise also on the commemoration of our holy and great father Basil; and on the Eve of the Nativity of Christ, and of the Holy Theophany.*[^396]

*of Saint Basil the Great*

*If, however, it happens that the Eve of either feast falls on a Saturday or on a Sunday: on the Eve the Liturgy of Chrysostom is sung according to custom in its proper time and order; and in the morning, on the very day of the feast, the Liturgy of the great Basil is sung; according to all the order, exactly as in the Liturgy of Chrysostom, the Prothesis being performed completely unchanged, all the way to the dismissal.*[^397] [^398]

*The Order of the Holy Liturgy*

*Concerning the Eves, if they happen during the Fast, and concerning Holy and Great Thursday, and concerning Holy and Great Saturday:*[^399]
*Behold: It ought to be known that whenever it happens to be thus: At the tenth hour of the day, we strike the great klepalo;*[^400] *and when the brethren have assembled in the church according to custom, the priest having arrived, likewise also the deacon, and having made their bow according to custom, and the priest having made the beginning of Saint Basil the Great*

*quietly within himself: "Blessed is our God;" then the Trisagion; after the "Our Father," the priest: "For Thine is the kingdom;" then the troparia: "Have mercy on us, O Lord, have mercy on us;" "Glory... O Lord, have mercy on us;" "Both now... The doors of compassion;" then, if the superior is present in the church, they take the blessing (the time)*[^401] *from him together with the deacon; and having bowed to him, standing together in the midst of the church, they say the prayer:*
**Priest & Deacon:** "O Lord, send forth Thy hand:"[^402]

*The Order of the Holy Liturgy*

*Then they bow thrice, saying:*
**Priest & Deacon:** "O God, cleanse me a sinner, and have mercy on me."
*Having turned again toward the superior, they bow; and they kiss the holy icons according to custom, saying the troparion: "To Thy most pure [image]:"*[^403] *"Being a fountain of compassion:" Then they enter into the holy altar; having taken the sacred vestments, they bow before the holy table thrice, saying:*

*of Saint Basil the Great*

**Priest & Deacon:** "O God, cleanse me a sinner, and have mercy on me."
*Then the deacon, having inclined his head to the priest, says:*
> **Deacon:** "Bless, Master, the sticharion with the orarion."
*And the priest, blessing him, says:*
> **Priest:** "Blessed is our God, always, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen."
*And thus they vest in all the priestly vestments, saying the same things as in the Liturgy of Chrysostom, as [indicated] above in the Prothesis.*[^404]

*The Order of the Holy Liturgy*

> **Deacon:** "Again and again;" "Help us, save us;" "Commemorating our most holy, most pure:"
*Then having entered again into the altar, he says:*
> **Deacon:** "Bless, Master, the time of the Trisagion."
*And the priest:*
> **Priest:** "For holy art Thou, our God, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever."
*Then the deacon exclaims in a loud voice:*
> **Deacon:** "And unto ages of ages."
*And the singers:*

*of Saint Basil the Great*

> **Choir:** "Amen,"
*and they sing the Trisagion; and all in order. And after the Great Entrance, the deacon goes forth, and says:*[^412]
> **Deacon:** "Let us complete our evening prayer [unto the Lord];"
*and the other [petitions]: "A whole evening, perfect;" and the rest according to custom, the Divine Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great.*
*Concerning the Feast of the Meeting of the Lord:*[^413]
*According to this same order the Liturgy of Chrysostom is also celebrated, whenever the feast of the Meeting of the Lord happens [on a weekday] in the Holy Great Forty Days (Lent).*

*The Order of the Holy Liturgy*

*Concerning the Annunciation of the Most Holy Theotokos:*[^414]
*Likewise also on the feast of the Conception of the Lord, that is to say, the Annunciation, the service is performed according to this same order, completely unchanged.*

The initial `Б` is set in an elaborate woodcut cartouche of renaissance strapwork and acanthus foliation.
[^502]: Fol. 172r (p. 331). The petition for worthy communion: that the communicants may have Christ dwelling in their hearts (Ephesians 3:17) and become a temple of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians 6:19), without incurring judgment or weakness of soul and body (1 Corinthians 11:29–30).

The conclusion of the priestly prayer before the Lord's Prayer in Saint Basil: *досто́йно поимла́ти наде́ждꙋ ст҃нь твои́хъ въ напоу́тїе жи́зни вѣ́чныѧ* ("worthily to receive the pledge of Thy holy things as provision for the journey unto eternal life" / εἰς ἐφόδιον ζωῆς αἰωνίου). The Greek witness has τὴν μερίδα τῶν ἁγιασμάτων σου / τὴν ἐλπίδα ("pledge/hope/portion"). The eschatological citation of 1 Corinthians 2:9 (*и҆́хже ᲂу҆гото́ва́лъ є҆сѝ любѧ́щимъ тебѐ*) concludes the secret prayer.

The priest follows immediately with the exclamation leading into the Lord's Prayer (*И҆ сподо́би на́съ, Владꙑ́ко, съ дерзнове́нїемъ...*).
[^505]: Fol. 173v (p. 334). The introduction and recitation of the Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9–13), followed by the priestly doxology (*Ꙗ҆́ко твоѐ є҆́стъ црⷵтво...*), the pax (*Ми́ръ въсѣ̑мъ*), and the diaconal call to bow heads. The priest's Prayer at the Bowing of Heads begins with woodcut initial `В` (*Владꙑ́ко Го́споди, О҆́тче щедро́тъ, и҆ Бо́же всѧ́кїѧ ᲂу҆тѣ́хи...* / Δέσποτα Κύριε, ὁ Πατὴρ τῶν οἰκτιρμῶν καὶ Θεὸς πάσης παρακλήσεως, citing 2 Corinthians 1:3).
[^506]: Fol. 174r (p. 335). The concluding clauses of the Prayer at the Bowing of Heads, praying that the faithful be joined to every good work and receive the Holy Mysteries unto remission of sins and communion of the Holy Spirit (2 Corinthians 13:13). Note the signature `ф` at the foot of the folio.
[^507]: Fol. 174v (p. 336). The secret priestly Prayer of Elevation before the fractio (*Вонмѝ, Го́споди І҆исоу́се Хрїстѐ Бо́же на́шъ...* / Πρόσχες, Κύριε Ἰησοῦ Χριστὲ ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν), identical in both the Chrysostom and Basil liturgies. The prayer asks Christ, enthroned with the Father on high and present invisibly with the clergy, to impart His sacred Body and Blood through the hands of the celebrant to all the people.
[^508]: Fol. 175r (p. 337). The Elevation and Communion Hymn rubrics: *Во́нмемъ* (Πρόσχωμεν), *Ст҃а́ѧ ст҃ы̑мъ* (Τὰ ἅγια τοῖς ἁγίοις), and the choir's response *Є҆ди́нъ ст҃ъ...* (Philippians 2:11). The manual acts of fractio, commixtio, and zeon are cross-referenced to the Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom (*ꙗ҆́коже преᲂуказа́сѧ въ златооу́стовѣ лїтоургі́и*). Woodcut initial `Б` introduces the Post-Communion Thanksgiving of Saint Basil.
[^509]: Fol. 175v (p. 338). The Basilian Post-Communion thanksgiving prayer (*Благодари́мъ тѧ̀, Го́споди Бо́же на́шъ, ѡ҆ причаще́нїи...* / Εὐχαριστοῦμέν σοι, Κύριε ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν), asking that the holy Mysteries bring faith unashamed (*въ вѣ́рꙋ непосты́днꙋ*), love unfeigned (*въ любо́вь нелицемѣ́рнꙋ*, Romans 12:9; 2 Corinthians 6:6), and spiritual wisdom.
[^510]: Fol. 176r (p. 339). The post-communion showing of the Holy Gifts to the people: the deacon raises the chalice proclaiming *Съ стра́хомъ Бо́жїимъ и҆ вѣ́рою приступи́те*, answered by the choir with Psalm 117:26–27 (LXX). The rubric notes the deacon gathering the holy things (*съпрѧ́тавъ ст҃а́а по чи́ноу*). Note signature `ф г҃` at bottom.
[^511]: Fol. 176v (p. 340). The priestly blessing with the chalice (*Сп҃сѝ, Бо́же, лю́ди твоѧ̑...*, Psalm 27:9, LXX) and the choir's traditional Greek acclamation *И҆сполла̀ та̀ є҆́ти де́спота* (Εἰς πολλὰ ἔτη, δέσпота), translated glossatively in the printed text itself (*Си́рѣчь: На мнѡ́га лѣ́та, Владꙑ́ко*). The priest censes the holy things reciting Psalm 56:6, 12 (LXX 56:5, 11).
[^512]: Fol. 177r (p. 341). The post-communion transfer of the Holy Gifts to the table of oblation (*въ проскомиді́ю*), the choir's hymn *Да и҆спо́лнѧтсѧ ᲂу҆ста̀ на̑ша...* ("Let our mouths be filled with Thy praise", Psalm 70:8, LXX), and the diaconal Litany of Thanksgiving (*Про́сти прїе́мше...*). Signature `ф д҃` at bottom.
[^513]: Fol. 177v (p. 342). The post-communion dismissal sequence: the final doxology (*Ꙗ҆́ко ты̀ є҆сѝ ѡ҆сщ҃е́нїе на́ше...*), the dismissal command *Съ ми́ромъ и҆зꙑ́демъ*, and the introduction to the Prayer behind the Ambo (*Моли́тва заа҆мвѡ́ннаѧ възглаша́ема ѿ їере́а*).
[^514]: Fol. 178r (p. 343). The Prayer behind the Ambo (*Благословлѧ́ѧй благословѧ́щыѧ тѧ̀, Го́споди...*), common to the Divine Liturgies of Chrysostom and Basil, interceding for the preservation of the Church, the sanctification of those who love the beauty of God's house (Psalm 25:8, LXX), and peace for the entire world. Woodcut initial `Б` at incipit. Signature `ф є҃` at bottom.
[^515]: Fol. 178v (p. 344). The conclusion of the Prayer behind the Ambo, quoting James 1:17 (*въсѧ́ко даа́нїе бл҃го, и҆ въсѧ́къ да́ръ съверше́нъ съвы́шше є҆́стъ...*), followed by the rubric for the prayer at the consumption of the Holy Gifts (*Моли́тва вънегда̀ потрѣ́бити ст҃а́а*).

The consummate concluding Basilian prayer of consumption (*И҆спо́лнишасѧ и҆ соверши́шасѧ, є҆ли́ко по си́лѣ на́шей, Хрїстѐ Бо́же на́шъ, та́инство твоегѡ̀ смотре́нїѧ...* / Ἤνυσται καὶ τετέλεσται, ὅσον εἰς τὴν ἡμετέραν δύναμιν, Χριστὲ ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν, τὸ τῆς σῆς οἰκονομίας μυστήριον). The rubrics following the Liturgy: consumption of the remaining Holy Gifts by the deacon, distribution of the antidoron (*по раздаа́нїи а҆нті́дѡры* / ἀντίδωρον) by the priest, the final blessing (*Благослове́нїе Госпо́дне на ва́съ...*), and the opening formulas for the Apolysis / Dismissal on Sundays (*Воскресы́й и҆з̾ ме́ртвыхъ...*) versus weekdays.
[^518]: Fol. 180r (p. 348). The weekday Apolysis naming Saint Basil the Great (*и҆ и҆́же въ ст҃ы́хъ ѿца̀ на́шего а҆рхїепи́скопа кесарї́а каппадокі́йскыѧ васи́лїа вели́каго*), followed by the choral polychronion (*мнѡ́га лѣ́тствꙋютъ*) for the King of Poland (Sigismund III) and the local bishop.
[^519]: Fol. 180v (p. 349). Post-liturgical thanksgiving rubrics: the priest washes his hands, face, and lips (*ѡ҆мꙑва́етъ рꙋ́цѣ и҆ лицѐ, и҆ кра́й ᲂу҆сте́нъ свои́хъ*), divests (*съвлачи́тсѧ*), recites the Canticle of Simeon (*Нынѣ̀ ѿпоуща́еши...*, Luke 2:29–32), Trisagion, and the Dismissal Troparion of Saint Basil the Great in Tone 4 (*Въ всѧ́коую зе́млѧ и҆зꙑ́де вѣща́нїе твоѐ...* / Εἰς πᾶσαν τὴν γῆν ἐξῆλθεν ὁ φθόγγος σου, Psalm 18:5 LXX / Romans 10:18), celebrating Basil's theological exposition of the nature of things (*є҆ство̀ сꙋ́щи ᲂу҆ꙗсни́ є҆си*) and royal priesthood (1 Peter 2:9).
[^520]: Fol. 181r (p. 350). The Kontakion of Saint Basil in Tone 4 (*Ꙗ҆ви́сѧ сте́пень недви́жимы цр҃кви...* / Ὤφθης βάсїс ἀκλινὴς τῆς Ἐκκλησίας), modeled on the podoben *Thou hast appeared today* (*Ꙗ҆ви́сѧ дне́сь* / Ἐπεφάνης σήμερον). Followed by the incipit of the post-liturgical dismissal Theotokion (*И҆́же ѿ а҆́ггела сло́во прїе́мши...*).
[^521]: Fol. 181v (p. 351). Concluding dismissal prayers of Saint Basil: the end of the post-liturgical Theotokion, the twelvefold *Kyrie eleison*, the Little Dismissal (*ѿпоу́стъ*), and the priest's departure from the altar after giving thanks to God. Followed by the explicit concluding colophon: *Коне́цъ бж҃тве́нныѧ лїтоургі́а ст҃го васи́лїа вели́каго* ("The End of the Divine Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great").
