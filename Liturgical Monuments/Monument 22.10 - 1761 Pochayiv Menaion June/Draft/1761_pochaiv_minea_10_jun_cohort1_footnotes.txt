[^1]: Augustus III of Poland (Frederick Augustus II of Saxony), King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania (1733–1763).
[^2]: Sylvester Lubieniecki-Rudnytsky (Sylwester Rudnycki-Lubieniecki), OSBM, Bishop of Lutsk and Ostroh (1756–1777), and Exarch of the Metropolitan See of Kyiv, Halych, and all Rus'.
[^3]: Hypatius (Ipatij) Biliansky (Bilynsky), OSBM, Protoarchimandrite of the Order of Saint Basil the Great from 1759 to 1771.
[^4]: The year of creation 7269 in the Byzantine era corresponds to 1761 A.D. (September year calculation: 7269 - 5508 = 1761).
[^5]: Justin the Philosopher (Justin Martyr), martyred in Rome under Emperor Marcus Aurelius around A.D. 165, along with Chariton, Charita, Euelpistus, Hierax, Peon, and Valerian. The Pochaiv Menaion also commemorates "another Martyr Justin", reflecting the distinction sometimes preserved in the Byzantine Synaxaria between Justin Martyr and Justin the Companion.
[^6]: Saint Charita (whose name in Greek, *Charito*, means "grace" or "grace-filled"), martyred together with Saint Justin.
[^7]: Saint Nikephoros I (ca. 758–828), Patriarch of Constantinople (806–815), theologian, historian, and staunch defender of the veneration of holy icons against the iconoclast Emperor Leo V the Armenian. He was deposed and exiled, reposing on June 2, 828.
[^8]: Cf. Heb. 8:2 ("of the true tabernacle, which the Lord pitched, and not man").
[^9]: The common Troparion for a Hierarch (*Rule of faith...*), Tone 4.
[^10]: The acrostic in the Greek Menaion reads: *Νικηφόρον ὡς τρόπαιον ᾄσμασιν μέλπω. Θεοφάνους.* Saint Theophanes the Branded (Graptos, ca. 778–845), renowned hymnographer and fellow confessor who defended icon veneration under Leo V and Theophilus.
[^11]: An explicit punning allusion to Emperor Leo V the Armenian (reigned 813–820; Greek *λέων*, Slavonic *левъ*, "lion"), who revived the iconoclast persecution, deposing Patriarch Nikephoros in 815. Leo was assassinated in 820 ("swiftly shattered near Hades").
