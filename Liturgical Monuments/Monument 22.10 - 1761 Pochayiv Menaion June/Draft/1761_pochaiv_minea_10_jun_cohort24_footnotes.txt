[^939]: The second canon for the Synaxis of the Twelve Apostles on June 30, attributed to St. Theophanes the Branded (c. 778–845), Metropolitan of Nicaea, celebrated hymnographer and confessor for the holy icons.
[^940]: The acrostic of the canon: "I honor the wise Apostles of Christ" (*Христовы почитаю мудрыя апостолы*), corresponding to the Greek acrostic Χριστοῦ γεραίρω τοὺς σοφοὺς Ἀποστόλους.
[^941]: An allusion to the calling of Simon Peter through his brother Andrew (John 1:40–42) and the baptism of Saul (Paul) by Ananias in Damascus (Acts 9:17–18).
[^942]: A festal Sessional Hymn in Tone 8 honoring the pre-eminent Apostles Peter and Paul, highlighting their role as luminaries who dispelled the darkness of idolatry.
[^943]: An allusion to the Apostle Peter rebuking Simon the sorcerer for attempting to purchase the gift of the Holy Spirit with money (Acts 8:18–24), and the Apostle Paul striking Elymas the sorcerer with temporary blindness at Paphos (Acts 13:6–12).
[^944]: The hymnographer contrasts the unlettered speech of the Galilean fisherman Peter and the eloquence of secular Greek poets and philosophers such as Homer (*Омиръ* / Ὅμηρος), noting that apostolic preaching, seasoned with divine salt (cf. Col. 4:6), captivated and converted philosophers to faith in Christ.
[^945]: Habakkuk 3:3 (LXX): "God shall come from Teman [the south], and the Holy One from mount Paran: the shaded mountain." Patristic exegesis interprets this prophecy as prefiguring the Incarnation of the Son of God from the ever-virgin Mother of God.
[^946]: The contrast between Peter's threefold denial (Matt. 26:69–75) followed by his threefold restoration through love (John 21:15–17), and Paul's former persecution of the Church (Acts 9:1–2; Gal. 1:13) followed by his apostolic mission.
[^947]: The distinct charisms of the pre-eminent Apostles: Peter receiving the keys of the Kingdom of Heaven (Matt. 16:19), and Paul being caught up to the third heaven and Paradise to hear ineffable words (2 Cor. 12:2–4).
[^948]: Cf. Acts 5:15 (the shadow of Peter falling upon the sick to heal them) and Acts 19:11–12 (handkerchiefs and aprons brought from Paul's body healing diseases and casting out evil spirits).
[^949]: The festal Kontakion for the Synaxis of the Twelve Apostles in Tone 2 (*Камень Христос, каменя веры*), celebrating the Apostles as foundational pillars of the Church.
[^950]: Cf. Galatians 2:7–9: the partition of the apostolic ministry, with Peter entrusted with the Gospel to the circumcised (the Jews) and Paul with the Gospel to the uncircumcised (the Gentiles).
[^951]: The parallel resurrection miracles performed by the chief Apostles: Peter raising Tabitha (Dorcas) at Joppa (Acts 9:36–41), and Paul raising the youth Eutychus at Troas after he fell from the third loft (Acts 20:9–12).
[^952]: Peter receiving the heavenly vision of the vessel descending and baptizing the Roman centurion Cornelius (Acts 10), and Paul baptizing early converts: Crispus, Gaius, and the household of Stephanas (cf. 1 Cor. 1:14–16; Acts 18:8).
[^953]: Divine liberations from bondage: the Angel of the Lord leading Peter out of Herod's prison (Acts 12:6–11), and the midnight earthquake releasing Paul and Silas from their chains and stocks in the prison at Philippi (Acts 16:23–30).
[^954]: Cf. Romans 10:15; Isaiah 52:7: "How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him that brings good tidings, that publishes peace; that brings good tidings of good, that publishes salvation."
[^955]: The patristic tradition of the martyrdom of the holy Apostle Peter in Rome during the reign of Nero (c. 64–67 AD), where according to Origen and Eusebius he asked to be crucified upside down, deeming himself unworthy to die in the same manner as the Lord Jesus Christ.
[^956]: The ancient liturgical and patristic tradition regarding the martyrdom of the Apostle Paul outside the walls of Rome on the Via Ostiense: when he was beheaded with the sword, milk rather than blood flowed forth from his neck, leading several onlookers and soldiers to convert to faith in Christ.
[^957]: An explicit affirmation of the Roman episcopate and primacy of the Apostle Peter and the perpetuity of the Church founded upon the rock of the apostolic confession against the gates of Hades (cf. Matt. 16:18).
[^958]: Cf. Galatians 1:15–16 ("When it pleased God, Who separated me from my mother's womb, and called me by His grace, to reveal His Son in me...") and 2 Corinthians 12:2–4 (Paul's ascent to the third heaven).
[^959]: St. Cosmas of Maiuma (the Hymnographer), foster brother of St. John of Damascus, author of many festal idiomelon compositions in the Byzantine Menaia.
[^960]: Epistolary reading for the Synaxis of the Twelve Apostles: 1 Corinthians 4:9–16 (pericope 131), detailing apostolic sufferings and humiliation for the sake of Christ.
[^961]: Gospel reading for the Synaxis of the Twelve Apostles: Mark 3:13–19 (pericope 21), recounting the ascent into the mountain and the calling and appointment of the Twelve Apostles.
