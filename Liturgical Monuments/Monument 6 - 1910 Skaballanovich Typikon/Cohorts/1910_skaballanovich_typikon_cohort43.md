From 1695 onward, the Typikon is printed almost without alterations; there are introduced only commemorations of newly canonized saints [^1833].

### The Present-Day Greek Typikon

The Greek Church presently uses for cathedral and parish churches not the Jerusalem Typikon, but a special one, representing an adaptation of the Jerusalem Typikon unto the practice of the Studite tradition and even of Hagia Sophia (the Typikon of the Great Church). This practice, living for centuries as an unwritten tradition, found expression for itself in a printed edition of the Greek Typikon produced in Constantinople in 1828

=== LEAF p422 ===
by Constantine, Protopsaltes of the Great Church, under the title *Ecclesiastical Typikon according to the Order of the Great Church of Christ* (*Τυπικὸν ἐκκλησιαστικὸν κατὰ τὴν τάξιν τῆς τοῦ Χριστοῦ Μεγάλης Ἐκκλησίας*), reprinted several times (1851, 1868, 1884) and translated into the Slavic language for the Bulgarians by Hieromonk Neophytus of Rila. Being destined not for monasteries, unto which in the preface the right is acknowledged to be guided by former Typika [^1834], and differing from the Jerusalem Typikon chiefly by the absence of Vigils, *(Orig. p. 444)* this Typikon lacks likewise the monastic-disciplinary section. Its general section (in 6 octavo pages) consists of the order of Sunday services, the peculiarity of which is the reading of the Gospel after the 8th Ode of the canon, and the omission of the Hours (they are not mentioned in the Typikon and in practice are not read); of observations concerning the distinction therefrom of festal services; and of the time of celebrating the 3 different Liturgies; unto this are subjoined Canon 75 of the Trullan Council concerning decorum in chanting, Canon 15 of the Council of Laodicea permitting only canonical clerics to chant in church, and the prohibition of the Jerusalem Typikon against conversing in church. Peculiarities of the Menologion: the services of the Nativity of Christ and Theophany have no Great Compline, but consist solely of Matins with Litiya at the beginning; if the Annunciation occurs on Great and Holy Friday or Saturday, it is transferred unto Pascha, with the exception of monasteries (this was introduced by Patriarch Gregory VI in 1835–1840 in view of the rubrical difficulties in such a coincidence, wherewith parish priests were unable to cope); on September 29 occurs the local Greek commemoration of the Miracle of the Mother of God upon the Island of Corfu; Great-Martyr Catherine on November 25; Martyr Charalampus on February 10 and Martyr Euphemia on November 11 [July 11] have festal services (for the former, the Presanctified Liturgy even in the 1st week of Great Lent, except Monday). Peculiarities in the Triodion: in the 1st week the Great Canon is read at Compline following the Doxology, and at the end of Compline is the Gospel (a vestige of the Chanted Pannykhis); on the 1st Friday of Great Lent the Great Canon is chanted either at Small Compline before **"It is truly meet"** (*Dostoyno*), or at Matins, as among us; the Akathist is chanted at Small Compline before the 1st, and after the 3rd, 6th, and 9th Odes of the canon; the distribution of palms takes place at the Praises; the Shroud (*Plashchanytsia*) on Great and Holy Saturday is carried into the altar permanently after the Great Doxology at Matins; at Paschal Matins following **"Thy Resurrection, O Christ our Savior"** (*Voskreseniye Tvoye, Khriste Spase*) is read the Gospel of Matthew, chapter 28; the Homily of Chrysostom is read after the Prayer behind the Ambon; the Gospel at the Paschal Divine Liturgy is read ordinary [in the vernacular], and in diverse tongues at Vespers. Following the Triodion, there are in the Typikon supplementary articles: the order of the consecration of a church, the rite of the induction of the Patriarch into the Patriarchate, explanations of the titles for diverse ecclesiastical hymns and among them the names of the pieces of silver ("Concerning the 30 Pieces of Silver"), the hours of the bell-ringing for diverse services and the days of the Patriarch's celebration, marriage regulations, and the funeral rite.
