Theodoret, *A History of the Monks of Syria* [*Philotheos Historia*], ch. 27 [Ἡ φιλόθεος ἱστορία]; Migne, *Patrologia Graeca*, vol. 82; Russian translation, St. Petersburg, 1853: the work consists of biographies of all the Syrian ascetics known to the author and contemporary with him [† 456]). Likewise St. Marcian (*ibid.* 3). St. Eusebius placed an iron chain around his neck and fastened it to a similar cincture around his waist, so that "being bent in this manner, he was constantly of necessity looking toward the earth" (*ibid.* 4).

555. St. James, a contemporary of Bl. Theodoret, "had neither hut nor tent, refused to use the shelter of a tree, but lived in a completely open field, where rain drenched him, snow covered him, frost froze him, and the sun burned him" (*ibid.* 21). So also lived many Syrian ascetics (*ibid.* 27).

556. St. Salaman (late fourth century) lived in a "refuge" without windows or entrance, into which one could crawl only beneath the wall, and he crept out of it once a year to make a provision of food (*ibid.* 19).
