349. "An elder came to another elder. The latter said to his disciple: 'Cook some lentils for us.' He cooked them and soaked bread (a rusk) for them. They remained in conversation on spiritual matters until the sixth hour of the following day. The elder said again to his disciple: 'Child, cook some lentils for us.' 350. "Abba Joseph asked Abba Poemen (fifth century): 'How ought one to fast?' Abba Poemen answered him: 'It is necessary, in my opinion, to eat every day, but to eat a little, not unto satiety.'—'How then didst thou, abba, in thy youth fast for two days at a time?' said Abba Joseph. 'In truth I fasted for three days, and for four, and even for a week,' answered the elder; 'but the fathers tested all these things, being strong in virtue, and found that it is better to eat a little every day, and they indicated the royal path, for it is more expedient for us'" (*Sayings of the Holy Fathers*, Poemen 186; the same in *Ancient Paterikon* IX, 59 [44]). "To Megethius, a contemporary of Poemen, who ate one loaf of bread every two days, a 'holy man' who visited him advised him to eat half a loaf every day: 'and doing so, Megethius found peace for himself'" (*Sayings of the Holy Fathers*, Megethius 2).

354. St. Arsenius († 450) "passed the whole night in vigil, and when towards morning nature compelled him to sleep, he would say to sleep: 'Come hither, wicked servant', and having slept a little while sitting, he immediately arose" (*Sayings of the Holy Fathers*, Arsenius 14). He used to say that for a monk, if he is an ascetic, it is enough to sleep for one hour (*ibid.* 15). St. Bessarion († 466) "did not lie down upon his side for forty years, but slept sitting or standing" (*Sayings of the Holy Fathers*, Bessarion 8). "God is witness", recounts Palladius concerning St. Dorotheus of Thebes, "I never saw him stretch out his feet, or lie down to sleep upon a mat or upon a bed; all night long he would sit and plait ropes. And from his youth he lived in this way for sixty years; he never lay down to sleep, but only while working or at table would he close his eyes for a few minutes, so that a morsel would sometimes fall from his mouth while drowsing" (*Lausiac History* 2).

355. St. Isaac (fifth century) said: "Our fathers and Abba Pambo († after 386) wore worn-out, tattered, and palm-leaf garments" (*Sayings of the Holy Fathers*, Isaac of the Cells 7). St. Pambo and Isaac said: "A monk ought to wear clothing which no one would pick up even if it were cast out of the cell for three full days" (*Sayings of the Holy Fathers*, Pambo 6; Isaac of the Cells 12).
