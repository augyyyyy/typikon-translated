819. Differently Kellner, *Heortologie*, 99. Various explanations are given as to what caused the separation of the feast of the Nativity of Christ from the Theophany. It is thought that the cessation of martyrdom played a role here in part, allowing earthly life and its beginning to be valued more highly; perhaps there was a desire to counterpose the Christian feast to the pagan festivals commemorating the December solstice. In Rome the Saturnalia was celebrated on December 18–23; corresponding to the somber character of the god Saturn or Cronus (who devoured his own children), this was a mournful celebration accompanied by gladiatorial combats (St. Cyril of Alexandria, *Against Julian*, 4); after it followed the joyful festival of the "unconquered sun" (*Sol Invictus*), celebrated with particular splendor by Emperor Julian in honor of the rising sun and on the threshold of the new year, falling at the time when the solstice was already becoming noticeable to all (Emperor Julian, *Oration 4*).
