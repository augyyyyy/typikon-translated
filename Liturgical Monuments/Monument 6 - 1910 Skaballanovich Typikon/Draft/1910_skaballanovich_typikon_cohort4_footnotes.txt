On a purely neuropathic basis there may also appear in certain individuals speech in a foreign tongue previously heard by them and unconsciously retained. Both phenomena were known also to early Christians, yet were sharply distinguished by them from the gift of tongues imparted by the Holy Spirit; they were explained as the influence upon man by evil spirits. Eusebius cites the narrative of an ancient historian concerning the glossolalia of the Montanists: "A certain one named Montanus, it is related, from among the recent converts, through an inordinate desire for preeminence, fell under the influence of the adversary, and suddenly, falling into a state of possession and ecstasy, began to speak and utter strange sounds (*lalein kai xenophōnein*), that is, he prophesied contrary to the custom transmitted from of old and preserved by succession in the Church... He [the devil] even raised up two women and filled them with a lying spirit, so that they also, like the aforementioned Montanus, spoke senselessly, inappropriately, and strangely" (Eusebius, *Historia Ecclesiastica*, V, 16). According to the account of Bl. Jerome, a certain Frankish official, having fallen under demonic possession from childhood, in the presence of St. Hilarion began to speak, scarcely touching the ground with his feet and turning exceedingly red, in Syriac and Greek—languages completely unknown to him—with entirely correct pronunciation (*Life of St. Hilarion*, ch. 22). Among Russian and foreign sectarians, however, speaking in unknown foreign languages has not been observed, but solely the utterance of unintelligible sounds.
