The Coptic and Abyssinian [Ethiopian] Churches, which contain in their other services so much that is similar to our own, do not possess the First Hour; however, the latter, at a service analogous to our Matins, contains a prayer quite close to **"O Christ, the True Light"** (B. Turayev, *Chasoslov efiopskoy tserkvi* [Horologion of the Ethiopian Church], p. 13).
— In the Roman Catholic Church, the First Hour [Prime] differs considerably from the others in structure and is longer than they. Into the customary beginning the Creed is inserted; the hymn at dawn (*Jam lucis orto sidere*) invites to prayer that God may preserve us throughout daily affairs from everything harmful, bridle the tongue, shield sight from vanity, cleanse the heart, crush the pride of the flesh, so that toward the close of day, purified by abstinence, we may sing His glory; the antiphon on Sunday is *Alleluia*, on ordinary weekdays: **"Blessed are the undefiled in the way, who walk in the law of the Lord"** (Ps 118:1 [LXX 118:1; Ps 119:1]), Psalm 53 [LXX 53; Ps 54], then on Sunday Psalm 117 [LXX 117; Ps 118], Monday 23, Tuesday 24, Wednesday 25, Thursday 22, Friday 21, Saturday and feasts—nothing; then always two sections of Psalm 118, vv. 1–16 and 17–32; on Sunday the Athanasian Creed with the antiphon *Alleluia* (3); the *capitulum* on Sunday 1 Timothy 1:17 (the doxology **"Unto the King of ages"**), on weekdays Zechariah 8:19 (according to the Vulgate: *"Love ye truth and peace, saith the Lord of hosts"*) with the responsory **"Thanks be to God"** (*Deo gratias*). **"O Christ, Son of the living God, have mercy on us"** (twice). **"Thou Who sittest at the right hand of the Father, have mercy on us. Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit. O Christ, Son... Arise, O Christ, help us, and deliver us for Thy name's sake."** Then follow prayers with confession (omitted on major feasts): *Kyrie eleison, Christe eleison, Kyrie eleison*, the Our Father and the Creed in secret with the audible proclamation of the final verse in each; responsories: verses from Psalm 87:14 [LXX 87:14; Ps 88:13]; 70:8 [LXX 70:8; Ps 71:8] (= with us); 50:11–14 [LXX 50:11–14; Ps 51:9–12] (on weekdays, a further 12 responsories from Psalms 139:1; 88:1; 60:9; 64:1; 69:1 [LXX], containing petitions for deliverance from enemies, vows to God, divine assistance, our Trisagion, and again 5 responsories from Psalm 102:1–5 [LXX] concerning the mercies of God); 123:8 [LXX 123:8; Ps 124:8] (for help); the priest pronounces the confession of sins (*confessio*) unto God and various saints, with a petition to the saints for their prayers (*Confiteor*); the choir responds: **"May Almighty God have mercy upon thee (*Misereatur*), forgive thee thy sins, and bring thee unto everlasting life. Amen"**; then the choir makes confession, and the priest gives the same response, adding: **"May the Almighty and merciful Lord grant us pardon (*indulgentiam*), absolution, and remission of our sins,"** and the responsories: **"Vouchsafe, O Lord, to keep us this day without sin. Have mercy on us, O Lord, have mercy on us,"** and Psalm 32:22 [LXX 32:22; Ps 33:22]; 106:2 [LXX 106:2; Ps 107:2], **"The Lord be with you."** A prayer to Almighty God for a sinless and virtuous coming day, **"The Lord be with you..."**, **"Let us bless the Lord, Thanks be to God,"** and the reading of the Martyrology followed by the responsories: **"Precious in the sight of the Lord"** (Ps 115:6 [LXX 115:6; Ps 116:15]), a brief prayer to the Most Holy Virgin Mary and to all the saints for obtaining assistance and salvation from God, again Psalm 123:8 [LXX], **"Glory to the Father..."**, *Kyrie eleison*... Our Father in secret with the audible proclamation of the end; responsory Psalm 89:16–17 [LXX 89:16–17; Ps 90:16–17] (= with us at Matins at the Aposticha); a prayer to God the Savior of the world for the direction and sanctification of our feelings, words, and deeds of this day according to the commandments of God; the blessing: *"Iube, Domine, benedicere"* (Bid, master, a blessing); **"May the Lord Almighty order our days and our deeds in His peace. Amen."** A brief reading from 2 Thessalonians 3:5, responsory again from Psalm 123:8 [LXX], and the final blessing: **"Blessed be God. May the Lord bless us and preserve us from all evil, and bring us unto life eternal; and may the souls of the faithful departed, through the mercy of God, rest in peace. Amen"** (*Breviarium Romanum*, I, 24–43; II, 25–44; III, 23–41; IV, 24–42).
