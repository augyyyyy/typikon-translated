Such an abbreviation was first of all the martyrology of Bede († 735), the renowned English monk and writer, 'who strove carefully to note by what manner of torment and under which judge the martyrs conquered the world,' for which reason he retained for each day from the Hieronymian Martyrology only one or two saints, and left certain days without any commemorations at all. Nevertheless, the martyrology of Bede hath come down unto us only in the redaction expanded c. 830 by Florus Drepanius, canon of the diocese of Lyons; in this latter redaction, likewise, commemorations do not occur for all days. This last martyrology was reworked (with rearranged commemorations) and slightly augmented: in the diocese of Trier by Wandalbert c. 848; in Mainz by Archbishop Rabanus Maurus in the mid-9th century (commemorations for all days); in Lyons c. 858 by Ado, subsequently Archbishop of Vienne (containing very extensive narratives concerning the saints, beginning on December 25); by Usuard, a Benedictine near Paris, c. 858; and by Notker in Switzerland c. 894. (Archbishop Sergius, *Polnyi mesiatseslov Vostoka*, Vol. I, pp. 62–73.)
