The order of life in these three principal monasteries of ancient Christian Egypt was so simple that it could not be defined by a written typikon. For this reason and by internal evidence one must recognize as spurious the rules for monks allegedly established by an assembly of four Nitrian and Scetic fathers (Serapion, the two Macarii, and Paphnutius) (Migne, *PG*, t. 34, cols. 971–978), the collection of which is divided into four parts, each part being ascribed unto one of these fathers. The most characteristic of these rules are: "Let there be one superior over all the brethren; let none depart from his counsel and command, but let everyone obey him with joy, as God Himself. In the church assembly let none begin the singing of the psalms without the command of the superior; if the superior delay, let him be notified and proceed according unto his instruction. Let those entering the monastery lie before the gates for an entire week. From the 1st hour until the 3rd, time is dedicated unto God; from the 3rd until the 9th, unto work. Everything in the monastery—vessels, tools, and the like—is sacred. A visiting cleric, even of the lowest rank, begins the prayers at divine service."
