There is a distinct order for the midday meal (*prandium*, strictly speaking luncheon) and the evening meal (*coena*). Each consists of two parts: prayers before the taking of food and after. The order begins with the exclamation of the priest: **“Bless”** (*Benedicite*), repeated by those present. Before the midday meal (or luncheon), the priest begins: **“The eyes of all...”**, and those present complete this passage from Psalm 144:15–16 [145:15–16]. **“Glory... Both now... Kyrie eleison, Christe eleison, Kyrie eleison, Our Father.”** The prayer with the customary exclamation before it, **“Let us pray”**: “Bless us, O Lord, and these Thy gifts, which we are about to receive from Thy bounty, through Christ our Lord, amen.” The reader: “Vouchsafe (*jube*), master, to bless.” The blessing: “May the King of eternal glory make us partakers of the heavenly table, amen.” After the midday meal (or luncheon): When the reader says: “Thou, O Lord, have mercy upon us”, the response: “Thanks be to God”, all arise, and the priest says: **“Let all Thy works praise Thee, O Lord...”** (Ps. 144:10 [145:10]), **“Glory... Both now...”** and the prayer (*absolute*): “We give thanks unto Thee, Almighty God, for all Thy benefits, Who livest and reignest unto the ages of ages.” There is recited alternately Psalm 50 [51] or 116 [117], **“Glory... Both now... Kyrie eleison”**, etc., **“Our Father”** (secretly, with the aloud proclamation of the conclusion). The response from Psalm 111:9 [112:9] (“He hath dispersed, He hath given to the poor...”), Psalm 33:2–4 [34:2–4], Psalm 112:2 [113:2] (“Blessed be the name of the Lord...”). The prayer (without **“Let us pray”**): “Vouchsafe, O Lord, to reward with eternal life all who do good unto us for Thy name's sake, amen.” The response: “Let us bless the Lord. Thanks be to God. May the souls of the faithful, through the mercy of God, rest in peace.” **“Our Father...”** (secretly). The priest: “May God grant us His peace, amen.” Before the evening meal the priest, following an introduction identical to that before the midday meal, begins: **“The poor shall eat...”** (Ps. 21:27 [22:26]) (cf. our own practice!), **“Glory... Both now...”** and further as at the midday meal, only with the concluding blessing: “May the King of eternal glory bring us unto the supper of eternal life.” At the conclusion of the evening meal there is said: **“He hath made a remembrance...”** (Ps. 110:5 [111:5]), **“Glory... Both now...”** The priest: “Blessed is God in His gifts, and holy in all His works, Who liveth and reigneth unto ages of ages, amen.” Psalm 116 [117] and all further as at the midday meal.
