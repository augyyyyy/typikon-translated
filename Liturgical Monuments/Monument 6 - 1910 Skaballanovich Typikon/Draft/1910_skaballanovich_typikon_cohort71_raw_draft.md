# The Litiya (*Litiya* — *Litē*)

Following the Litany of Supplication and the Prayer of the Bowing of Heads linked with it, wherewith Vespers properly concludes, there is inserted into it a special additional, non-obligatory part, called the Litiya. *Litiya* (from Greek *litē* [Gr. λιτή], earnest supplication), according unto constant ecclesiastical usage, signified supplication outside the temple. The present Typikon knows four kinds of litiya, which according unto their degree of solemnity may be arranged in the following order:
a) "litiya outside the monastery", appointed on certain Twelve Great Feasts and during Bright Week before the Liturgy;
b) litiya at Great Vespers connected with a Vigil;
c) litiya at the conclusion of festal and Sunday Matins;
d) litiya for the reposed following daily Vespers and Matins.

By the content of their hymnody and prayers and by their rite, these kinds of litiya differ greatly from one another, yet what they have in common is specifically the procession forth [exit / *iskhozhenie*] from the temple. This exit in the first kind (of those enumerated) of litiya is complete, while in the remainder it is partial. Yet both there and here it is performed most immediately with the aim of expressing prayer not only in words, but also in bodily movement, changing its place to revive prayerful attention; a further aim of the litiya is the expression—by departure from the temple—of our unworthiness to pray within it: "we pray, standing before the doors of the holy temple, as before the doors of heaven... like Adam, the publican, the prodigal son" [^2303]. Hence the somewhat penitential and sorrowful character of the litiya prayers. Finally, in the litiya the Church goes forth from her grace-filled communion into the outer world—in the literal sense—or into the narthex [*pritvor*], as that part of the temple contiguous with this world, open unto all who are not yet received into the Church or are excluded from her, for the purpose of a prayerful mission in this world. Hence the all-popular and universal character (concerning the whole world) of the litiya prayers [^2304].

*(Orig. p. 586)*

## General History of the Litiya

By its original purpose, the litiya, having something analogous also in Roman cult (see Introductory Chapter, p. 354, note 2), was a supplication in times of public calamities in the places stricken or threatened by them (ibid., p. 152); such was the litiya also in the Typikon of the Great Church of Constantinople, and in the Western Church (ibid., pp. 353–354) only unto such supplications was the name litiya assigned.

=== LEAF p702 ===
Yet alongside these litai there existed already in the 3rd century supplications very close unto them of a joyfully festive character, consisting, like those, in processions outside the city, unto which, however, the name of litai and litanies was not assigned. These processions received special development in the Church of Jerusalem, where they were performed, presumably, at first on feast days unto the holy places commemorated by the festive events (ibid., p. 251ff.), and then, already in the 4th century, became a regular part of every Sunday Vespers; this litiya (likewise not called so) consisted in a simple repetition of the second, prayerful part of Vespers in various holy places (ibid., p. 142). The present litai before the Liturgy are a remnant of the festal Jerusalem litai, while those at Vespers and Matins are a direct heritage of the Sunday-Vesperal litiya there, which, under the influence of the Great Church and ancient universal practice, adopted elements from the penitential-prayerful litiya.

## The Procession unto the Litiya

The litiya at Great Vespers [^2305] is composed of three elements: rites, hymns, and prayers. The rite of the litiya consists in the solemn exit of the clergy and people into the narthex. Since "the narthex is appointed for the penitents and catechumens", the Church, "as if bearing the image of penitents and catechumens, humbling herself before God and entreating His mercy, performs a certain part of the service in the narthex" [^2306]. "Standing before the sacred temple, as before the doors of heaven, we entreat Him" [^2307]. This exit is so essential an attribute of the litiya that in Slavic manuscripts of the Jerusalem Typikon, instead of the term *litiya*, the term "procession" [*iskhozhenie*] is constantly used [^2308]. This *(Orig. p. 587)* exit is performed according unto an order close unto the Vesperal Entrance. The priest [^2309] with the deacon, as there, go forth through the north doors, with the Holy Doors closed (according unto the *Sluzhebnik*) [^2310]. The deacon walks beside the priest [^2311], holding the censer. Before them are carried two candlesticks [*manoualia*, Gr. μανουάλια]. The singers ("we") follow after the clergy. All this is performed during the singing of the stichera appointed for the litiya. Upon arriving in the narthex, the deacon "censes the holy icons (there), the superior, and the choirs, and stands in his place", that is, before the priest slightly to the right.

## The Procession according unto Ancient Typika

Studite typika did not know the litiya at festal Vespers.

The most ancient redactions of the Jerusalem Typikon indicated an exit for the litiya into other churches and oratories (*eukteria*) of the monastery, and in conclusion into the narthex of the main church, wherein on the way to each of these churches its stichera were sung, and in each of them one petition of the litiya litany was pronounced. Thus the Georgian Shiomgvime manuscript of the 13th century and two of the library of the Monastery of St. Sabas near Jerusalem of the 16th century [^2312]. As regards the rite of the exit, it had the following development:

Georgian:
"The priest goes with the censer, preceded by a lighted taper."

Greek MSS and printed editions:
"We chant the idiomelon, performing the litiya (*litaneuontes* [Gr. λιτανεύοντες]) into the narthex (*en tō narthēki* [Gr. ἐν τῷ νάρθηκι]), preceded by the priest with the censer and a lamp (*lampados* [Gr. λαμπάδος])", in printed editions "lamps" [^2313]; of censing in the narthex no mention is made.

"Order" (*Diataxis* / *Chin*) of Patriarch Philotheus:
"At the singing of them (the stichera of the litiya), the deacon, having entered (the altar) and taken the blessing as usual, goes forth together with the priest through the northern side with the Holy Doors barred, preceded by lamps, and having passed through the beautiful doors, and after them all the brethren, they take their stand in the narthex; during the singing of the brethren, the deacon censes all as usual; and upon the completion of the stichera, having taken his stand in the customary place, he says: Save, O God" [^2314].

In the most ancient Slavic MSS:
"The priest and deacon make the procession into the narthex, a taper going before with the censer, and we following them; and the priest having passed the church doors and having censed crosswise, stands there, and the choirs stand according to their order" [^2315].

Later MSS replace the one taper with "2 candlesticks" [*2 svetil'nikoma*]; concerning censing *(Orig. p. 588)* they add "the abbot and the brethren" [^2316]. Still later ones: "the priest goes forth with the deacon through the Holy Doors—and there censes (the priest?) the holy icons and the superior" [^2317].

## Stichera of the Litiya

The exit unto the litiya of Sunday Vespers (customary) is performed during the singing of the "sticheron of the temple". The singing of such a sticheron is natural during a solemn procession through the temple; and it is natural to remember during the entire Vigil at least once the saint in whose honor the temple is dedicated (ancient typika, as we shall see, speak here of singing the sticheron specifically of the temple saint), even as he is remembered also in the liturgical chants once (in the troparion after the Entrance), and who is conceived of as spiritually co-present with the faithful and participating in their service, "sealing the singing and initiating the celebration" [^2320]. Which of the temple stichera to sing at the litiya, the Typikon does not specify [^2321]. In the Octoechos, in the service of Tone 1, it is noted: "we chant the idiomelon sticheron of the Saint of the monastery, making the litiya into the narthex, at which we chant the stichera of Paul of Amorion or what the superior wisheth." It is assumed that the temple, as was almost always the case in antiquity, is dedicated unto a saint, and from his stichera there is indicated for singing an idiomelon, as the most solemn in melody, wherever it may be appointed in the saint's service; for the most part, idiomela are appointed at "Glory, Both now" of *Lord, I have cried* or of the Aposticha. As regards the stichera of Paul of Amorion, placed in the Octoechos at *Lord, I have cried*, which are Theotokia in content, the Typikon nowhere assumes their singing at the litiya, since in the rubrics concerning Sunday service on Triodion Sundays it constantly speaks of chanting at the litiya only one sticheron of the temple, not counting the Doxastikon from the Triodion and the Theotokion [^2322]. "Upon the completion of the stichera," continues the Typikon in the present place, allowing the possibility of singing at the litiya also several stichera (which happens upon the coincidence with Sunday of certain commemorations), "Glory, Both now: Theotokion"; which Theotokion is not specified, but in the Triodion Sundays there is always specified here a Theotokion according to the Tone of the last sticheron (at Glory), and for the most part from the Sunday aposticha *(Orig. p. 589)* Theotokia [^2323]. Consequently, even when singing at the litiya one sticheron of the temple (on an ordinary Sunday), the Theotokion unto it must be taken from the aposticha Sunday ones according to the Tone of the sticheron. But if the temple is dedicated unto the Lord or the Mother of God, then as Theotokion at the litiya there can serve again only one of the stichera of the feast unto which the temple is dedicated [^2324], because...

=== LEAF p705 ===
...then the litiya sticheron of the temple belongs unto the number of those which are generally never joined with ordinary Theotokia; perhaps in view of this the expression of the Typikon concerning the Theotokion at the litiya is so indefinite. Ancient copies of the Typikon on this point—concerning litiya stichera—were more definite; in particular, some of them indicate chanting at the litiya the sticheron of the temple according to the Tone of the Octoechos, that is, choosing from the temple stichera a sticheron of that Tone which falls upon the given Sunday, in order to maintain musical correspondence with other parts of the service (see below). The sticheron of the temple at Sunday litiya is not sung on all Twelve Great Feasts, their forefeasts and leavetakings, the Forefeast of the Nativity of Christ, Palm Sunday, Thomas Sunday, Sunday of the Myrrh-bearers, and the 7th Sunday after Pascha (see there).

## Stichera of the Litiya according unto Ancient Typika

The Georgian 13th-century MS of the Typikon, as noted above, indicates at the litiya the stichera of those saints unto whose church the litiya is directed; hence, perhaps, the Typikon's general requirement to sing at the litiya the sticheron of the temple; several stichera (not one) are indicated for each church in the Tone of the day; in the narthex of the main church after them: Glory, Both now: Theotokion; Greek MSS and printed editions: "we chant the idiomelon of the Saint of the monastery (*tou kata monēn* [Gr. τοῦ κατὰ μονῆς]) in the Tone of the day; Glory, Both now: Theotokion" [^2325]. In the most ancient 14th-century Slavic MSS of the Typikon: "we begin the idiomelon (other MSS: idiomela) of the saint whose church it is, corresponding unto the present Tone in the Octoechos (15th-c. MS: "and upon the completion of the stichera, Glory, Both now: Theotokion"; 16th-c. MS: "we chant the stichera of the saint whose temple it is, in the Tone of the day, if he hath them; and upon the completion of the stichera, Glory, Both now: Theotokion" [^2326].

Old Believer Typikon: "and we chant the sticheron of the saint whose temple it is; *(Orig. p. 590)* then the stichera of Paul of Amorion or of the saint, if he hath them—"

## The Litiya Prayers

After the stichera at the litiya there follows a supplication, called "prayers" (*molitvy* in the *Sluzhebnik*; the Typikon calls so its first part: **"Save, O God"**), but which is in structure a litany of 5 petitions with an ecphonesis. This litany in its petitions is more extensive even than the Augmented Litany and fuller than the Great Litany.

It begins with a general petition in the words of Ps. 27:9 [LXX]: **"Save, O God, Thy people, and bless Thine inheritance"** for the whole people (*laon* [Gr. λαόν]—"people") as the allotment (*klēronomian* [Gr. κληρονομίαν], "inheritance") of God, and for the whole world of God, with the addition, however, of a special request for Orthodox Christians ("exalt the horn"—give them pre-eminence before others for the good of the world itself); at the same time, immediately at the beginning of the litany (not at the end, as at the Great Litany), there are presented unto God as intercessors all the saints, the whole Church is moved unto prayer, including the Church Triumphant—with detailed indication of the choirs of saints and naming of the most important (in contrast unto the general reference unto such intercession in the Great Litany), namely: the Mother of God, the Cross, the Angels, the Forerunner, the apostles, hierarchs, martyrs, venerable ascetics, the righteous, and the temple saint. The same petition, besides evening litiya, is pronounced at festal and Great-Fast Matins before the canon.

After this general and introductory petition, the litiya litany transitions unto more particular prayer for the various classes of people and their special needs, beginning, according to the apostle's commandment, with the sovereign; the petition for the sovereign is the same as in the Augmented Litany, but in view of the greater size of the petitions in this litany and their fewer number, there are combined with it into one general second petition also the supplications of the Augmented Litany for the reigning house.

The 3rd petition is for the spiritual authority and all who are most in need of God's help: for all afflicted Christians (perhaps an echo of difficult times of Christianity), for the local temple or monastery, for the indispensable condition of all well-being and development—peace and stability...

=== LEAF p707 ===
...("estate" / *sostoyanie*) [^2328] of the world, especially of the Holy Churches (repetition of petitions from the Great Litany), more particularly concerning the various kinds and degrees of afflicted condition: concerning those of the brethren engaged in hard labor (*kopiontōn* [Gr. κοπιώντων], "those who labor") and lower services ("those who serve"; *diakonountōn* [Gr. διακονούντων]), which do not permit them even presence in the temple or cause absences from the monastery ("concerning those who remain behind", *apoleiphthentōn* [Gr. ἀπολειφθέντων], properly left behind by order at work, "and those traveling", *apodēmias* [Gr. ἀποδημίας; printed ἀποδημίοας], being on a journey), concerning the sick, the depar*(Orig. p. 591)*ted, their "repose" (*koimēseōs* [Gr. κοιμήσεως], that is, resting, properly peaceful lying), "respite" (*aneseōs* [Gr. ἀνέσεως], that is, resting from earthly labors), "blessed memory" of them on earth and "remission (*apheseōs* [Gr. ἀφέσεως]) of sins", concerning captives and those engaged in various services now and formerly.

The 4th petition—for the whole (external) world (return unto the beginning) and its deliverance from physical calamities: famine, pestilence, earthquake, flood, fire,—and civil: war and civil strife; in general for the mercy of God and turning away from us of God's wrath, both already bearing down upon us and possible in the future ("the threat... that hangeth over us", *apeilēs* [Gr. ἀπειλῆς], threat) [^2329].

The 5th petition—for the hearing of prayer. After this petition in the *Sluzhebnik* is the note: "Then he commemorates whom he wishes, living and dead, secretly." Instead of the usual ecphonesis, consisting merely in glorification of God and serving as the conclusion of a prayer, unto this extraordinary litany there is joined as an ecphonesis an entire priestly prayer, beginning with the words of Ps. 64:6 [LXX: "Hear us, O God our Savior, the hope of all the ends of the earth..."], unto God as Savior and hope of all the ends of the earth and the sea, for hearing and mercy, with the doxology of the Augmented Litany.

Since the litiya is a special service inserted into others, unto its litany there is joined the impartation of peace customary at the end of services, and it concludes as a dismissal prayer with the prayer of the bowing of heads unto Jesus Christ [^2330], as the All-Merciful (*polyelee* [Gr. πολυέлеε]) Master, for the acceptance of prayer, sheltering, driving away of enemies, pacification, and showing of mercy unto us and the world. In this prayer the priest appeals unto the intercession of the same saints unto whom the deacon appealed at the beginning of the litiya, whereby this prayer is also an expansion of the last petition in the Great Litany, **"Calling to remembrance our All-Holy, Immaculate"**. Unto the petitions of the litiya litany, in view of the special fervor of its supplications, surpassing in this respect also the Augmented Litany (ever-increasing fervor of supplications), the choir responds dozens of times **"Lord, have mercy"**, moreover not an identical number of times, but in accordance with the complexity of the petition: unto the 1st—40, unto the 2nd—30,

=== LEAF p708 ===
unto the 3rd—50, and unto the two last, as supplementary—3 times each,—various symbolic numbers being taken. The final prayer of the litiya the Typikon and *Sluzhebnik* require to be pronounced in a loud voice and listened to, in accordance with the special fervor and sorrowful-penitential character of the litiya prayer, not merely with bowed heads as ordinary concluding prayers, but prostrate upon the ground, while it, as a prayer of blessing, is pronounced facing west (concerning turning to the west the *Sluzhebnik* makes no mention).

## History of the Litiya Prayers

The fundamental prayer of the litiya, **"Save, O God, Thy people"**, originated, perhaps, from a brief prayer with the same beginning in certain liturgies, for example, the Liturgy of the *Apostolic Constitutions*: before the consecration of the Gifts: "Save Thy people, O Lord, and bless Thine inheritance, which Thou hast gotten and acquired by the Precious Blood of Thy Christ and called a royal priesthood and a *(Orig. p. 592)* holy nation" (cf. in the Liturgy of Basil the Great and Chrysostom at the end of the Liturgy). In the Liturgy of the Apostle James, such a prayer, completely in the present redaction with minor variants (see above, p. 570), constitutes the last petition of the litany corresponding unto our Augmented Litany; in that same liturgy in the Great Litany in one petition "concerning every Christian soul... in need" (see above, p. 525). The frequently mentioned Georgian manuscript of the 13th century, Greek MSS of the 13th–14th centuries, and, apparently, all Slavic MSS, even of the 16th century, have only the first three petitions; likewise the first Slavic printed editions, ending with the Moscow *Sluzhebnik* of 1647 and the Old Believer Typikon; in the Kyiv *Sluzhebnik* of 1629 there are in small print also the two last petitions with the note that they are "found in the Greek only"; while in Greek editions, already the earliest, the two last petitions are present.

The first petition in the most ancient Greek MS of the 13th century (Moscow Rumyantsev Museum, Sevastyanov collection No. 491/35) contains among the saints only the Most-Holy Theotokos, the Cross, the Forerunner, the apostles, the saint N. (of the day or temple?) "and all the saints"; likewise the printed Greek edition of the Typikon, but with omission of the Cross and addition of the bodiless powers; Greek MSS of the 14th century add after the apostles: "our venerable and God-bearing fathers, the holy and righteous Ancestors of God Joachim and Anna"; likewise the most ancient Slavic MSS of the 14th century (Moscow Synodal Library No. 328/383), but without the Cross; Slavic MSS of the 15th century, restoring the mention of the Cross, add the Holy Archangels; Slavic MSS of the 16th century further add: "the holy right-victorious martyrs"; for the first time later Greek MSS and the first printed editions of the Euchologia append after...
