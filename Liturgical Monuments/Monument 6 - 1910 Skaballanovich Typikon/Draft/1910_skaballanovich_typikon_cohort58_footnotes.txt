[^1956]: Obscure expressions in the psalm: "For the sparrow [bird]" [Ps. 83:4 / MT 84:3], literally from the Hebrew "even the bird," that is, just like a bird. "Blessed is the man whose help is in Thee" [v. 6 / MT 5], whom God helps on the pilgrimage to the temple, which is especially arduous from the lands of the diaspora. "He hath set ascents in his heart," that is, he undertakes the journey to the temple across both mountains and "into the vale of tears [weeping]" [v. 7 / MT 6]—a valley of weeping on account of its barrenness and perils, "unto the place which He hath appointed," keeping ever before him the longed-for goal of the pilgrimage; but from the Hebrew: "they (those journeying to Jerusalem) make it (the valley of weeping) a place of springs"—God miraculously sends them water. "For the lawgiver shall give blessings" [v. 8 / MT 7]: God, Who prescribed in the Law such pilgrimages to the temple (three times a year), blesses these pilgrims (from the Hebrew: "the early rain also covereth it—the valley—with blessings"), and they "shall go from strength to strength," that is, during the arduous pilgrimage their vigor will continually increase, whereas with ordinary travelers the opposite occurs. In relation to the crucified Christ, the vale of weeping is earthly life with its sufferings and death, through which He elevated His human nature unto heaven.
