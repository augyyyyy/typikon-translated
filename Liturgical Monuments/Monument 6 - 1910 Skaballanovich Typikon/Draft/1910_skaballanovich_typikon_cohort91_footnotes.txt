In the Coptic and Ethiopian (Abyssinian) Horologia, the Sixth Hour has Psalms 53, 56, 62, 66, 69, 83–86, 90, and 92; the troparion and Theotokion following the Psalms are our own, but at *Glory...* there is an additional troparion petitioning the Crucified One to put passions to death and turn the mind from passions to the commandments; the troparia after the Trisagion are ours, but the Theotokion is solely "Most praised art Thou" (*Preproslavlena yesi*); the concluding prayer gives thanks for attaining so momentous an hour and petitions for the mortification of passions, a virtuous life, and non-condemnation at the Dread Judgment (Bishop Porfiry, *Verouchenie, bogosluzhenie... koptov* [Doctrine, Worship... of the Copts], 64–65; Turayev, B., *Chasoslov efiopskoy tserkvi* [Horologion of the Ethiopian Church], 55–61). In the Roman Catholic Sext (Sixth Hour), the hymn petitions the mighty Ruler (*Rector*), the true God Who orders the alternation of things, the Dispenser of light and fire, to extinguish the flames of strife, remove harmful heat, and grant health to the body and peace to the heart; as Psalms, three divisions of Psalm 118 [LXX]: vv. 81–96, 97–112, 113–128; the antiphon before them is usually Alleluia on Sunday, and on weekdays "Help me" (*Adjuva me*, v. 117); after them, Alleluia thrice; a *capitulum* from the New or Old Testament suited to the season of the year or feast, with corresponding responsories; on weekdays, identical prayers (*preces*) for each hour and an oration (prayer) varying according to seasons of the year and feasts (*Breviarium Romanum*, I, 46–49; II, 48–51, et al.).
