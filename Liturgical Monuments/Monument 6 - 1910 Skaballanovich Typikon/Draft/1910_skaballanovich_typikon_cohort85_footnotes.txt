In correspondence with the inter-ode interval of Odes 3 and 6, a reading would be required after the exapostilarion. But it is absent, presumably in order not to interrupt the unbroken stream of hymnody in this portion of Matins, where the solemnity of the divine service reaches its apex. Moreover, the readings of Matins increase in their importance and compunction in the course of its progression: first the commentary on the Epistle, then on the Gospel, then the *Margarita* (the finest works of the greatest Father [St. John Chrysostom]), and finally the Prolog (frequently replacing the entire service of a saint); here an even superior reading would be required; such could be only Sacred Scripture itself (cf. the conclusion of Vespers); in antiquity here there was indeed a Gospel reading (as also at Roman Catholic Matins). As a replacement for a reading here serves the "Catechesis" [Oglashenie] after Matins.
