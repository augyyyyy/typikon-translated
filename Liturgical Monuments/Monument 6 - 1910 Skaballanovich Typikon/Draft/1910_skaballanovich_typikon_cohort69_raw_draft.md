=== LEAF p681 ===
concentrates upon the immediate participants in the present divine service, upon those having relation unto this given temple alone: its benefactors and more specifically its adorners ("those who do good works" [*dobrodeyushchikh*], *kalliergountōn* [Gr. καλλιεργούντων]), those who bear the more arduous (manual) labors in it ("those who labor" [*trudzhdayushchikhsya*], *kopīōntōn* [Gr. κοπιώντων] [^2260]), those who sing (*psallontōn* [Gr. ψαλλόντων]), and those simply standing in the temple; by its final words: **"that await of Thee great and rich mercy"**, the petitions of the Augmented Litany return unto their beginning and express the entire essence of their content. The ecphonesis of the litany appeals not unto God in general with His might and power, as do the preceding litanies, but in accordance with the general thought of the litany—unto His "mercy" (compassion—more specific than unto goodness in general, as in the Litany of Supplication) and love for mankind. The Vesperal Augmented Litany possesses as complete a form, and not one truncated at the beginning (which occurs at Matins), as at the Liturgy; yet in comparison with the Liturgy it hath not the petition **"For our brethren the priests..."** (which pertaineth more unto the Liturgy), but in exchange for it, in another position, the petition: **"Furthermore we pray for mercy, life..."**, a petition of more general content; thereafter it abbreviates one petition of the Liturgy's litany: in the petition **"For the blessed and ever-memorable holy Orthodox patriarchs, and pious tsars and right-believing tsaritsas"**, this beginning is omitted, and it proceeds directly unto **"the founders"**.

*(Orig. p. 570)*

## History of the Augmented Litany

In the Liturgy of the Apostle James in all manuscripts thereof, beginning with the most ancient 10th-century Messina MS, continuing with the 11th-century Rossano MS and the 12th-century Sinai MS (No. 1040), and concluding with the 14th-century manuscripts (Paris National Library Nos. 2509 and 476), there is a litany close unto our Augmented Litany, appointed immediately before the Gospel following the Epistle and the Alleluia (whereas immediately after the Gospel there is a litany close unto our Litany of Supplication). In all these manuscripts, excluding the Sinai MS, this litany is nearly identical. It possesses its most complete form in the most ancient 10th-century Messina MS, according unto which we set it forth, with variants from the remaining ones apart from the Sinai MS (the 5th MS hath only the opening words of the petitions):

"After the Alleluia the priest stands up. The deacon recites the litany (*ektenēn* [Gr. ἐκτενῆ / έκτενήν]; other MSS instead of all this: 'And the deacon', 'The deacon', 'The deacon before the Gospel'): **'Let us all say: Lord, have mercy. O Lord Almighty, God of our fathers, we pray Thee, hear. For the peace of the whole world and the union of all the Holy Churches, we pray, hear'** (other MSS:

=== LEAF p682 ===
'let us pray', *deēthōmen* [Gr. δεηθῶμεν], without 'hear', and so on until the 17th petition): **'For our holy father N.** (other MSS: **For the salvation and protection of our most holy father N., Patriarch), all the clergy, and the Christ-loving people, let us pray. For our most pious and Christ-loving emperor [tsar], all the palace and armed forces, and their victory, we pray** (absent in Rossano and Paris 1). **For the holy city of Christ our God and the imperial city [reigning city], every city and countryside, we pray** (absent in the same). **That we may be delivered from all affliction, wrath** (in the same: + **danger** [*napasti*], *kindynou* [Gr. κινδύνου / printed κυνδύνου]) **and necessity, captivity, [and] bitter death** (in the same: + **and from our iniquities**), **we pray** (Rossano: **let us pray**; Paris: **we pray Thee, hear**). **And** (absent in Paris 2) **for the people standing present and awaiting the rich and great mercy that cometh from Thee, O Lord** (the italicized words are absent in other MSS), **we pray Thee, have compassion and have mercy.** (Paris 1: + Instead of **'Have mercy on us, O God'**, this:) **Save, O God, Thy people, and bless Thy heritage** (other MSS: + **Thy**), **visit Thy world with mercy and compassions, exalt the horn of Christians, and send down upon us Thy rich mercies** (instead of the italicized words other MSS: **by the power of the Precious and Life-giving Cross**), **through the intercessions of our All-Holy** (Rossano: **All-Pure**) **blessed Lady, the Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary** (instead of the italicized words other MSS: **of the Forerunner and Thy Apostles**) **and of all Thy saints; we pray Thee, O Most Merciful Lord, hear us sinners praying** (other MSS: + **unto Thee**) **and have mercy. People: Lord, have mercy (thrice)** (absent in Paris 2)" [^2261].

The litany, therefore, represents a repetition of the Great Litany (just as in the Liturgy of the *Apostolic Constitutions* the litany close unto our Great Litany and appointed before the consecration of the Gifts is repeated also after them), but with the addition at the beginning and end of intensified prayerful and mournfully penitential petitions. Such additions were bound to multiply also in subsequent monuments, in later liturgies; such an element in this litany doth indeed increase, yet it is concentrated preeminently at its beginning. Even more than the Great Litany, the Augmented Litany possesses one common history across all liturgies, coinciding unto literal exactness, as we shall see, *(Orig. p. 571)* in the Liturgies of the Apostle James, Basil the Great, and John Chrysostom within the very same manuscripts, while differing endlessly within one and the same liturgy in different epochs. Therefore, the following (if not preceding) formula thereof after the one cited above may be acknowledged in the order of development as

that given in an ancient redaction of the Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom:

"Deacon: **'Let us all say with our whole soul and with our whole mind let us say.'** Ecphonesis of the priest: **'O Lord Almighty, God of our fathers, rich in mercy and good in compassions, Who willest not the death of a sinner, but awaitest conversion and repentance, that he should turn and live.'** Deacon: **'For this holy temple...'** and further the petitions of the Great Litany down unto **'That we may be delivered...'** Inaudible [secret] prayer: **'O Lord our God, accept this fervent supplication'** (the present prayer). Deacon: **'That the Lord may be merciful, favorable, and gracious, let us pray.'** People: **'Lord, have mercy'** (and thereafter all petitions end with **'let us pray unto the Lord'** and **'Lord, have mercy'**). **'That the Lord God may hear the voice of the supplication of us sinners. For all those who have labored before us** (*prokopiasantōn* [Gr. προκοπιασάντων]) **and have consecrated** (*aphierōsantōn* [Gr. ἀφιερωσάντων]) **us. For the people standing present and awaiting rich mercy from Thee'**" [^2262].

Thus, in the 1st petition certain Greek MSS have instead of "with our whole mind"—"in all our mind"; certain Greek and Slavic MSS omit "our", while certain ones add at the end "all". In the 2nd petition **"O Lord Almighty"**, certain MSS add after "hear"—"us". In the 3rd petition certain Greek and Slavic MSS after "Thy mercy" or after "we pray Thee" add + "O Lord". ## The Threefold "Lord, Have Mercy"

The threefold **"Lord, have mercy"** in the capacity of the people's response unto the petitions of the Augmented Litany became a distinctive feature of the post-Gospel litany scarcely at an early date. Yet in general the threefold **"Lord, have mercy"** was customary in the Church from the most ancient times and served as an expression of the most fervent prayer. In the Syriac Liturgy of the Apostle James it serves as the response of the people unto the invocation of the Holy Spirit for the consecration of the Gifts [^2276]. In Greek manuscripts of the Liturgy of the Apostle James, at the end of each of the three longest litanies before the prayer there stands the rubric: 'People: Lord, have mercy (thrice)', whereas beneath the first petition of the same litanies there sometimes stands: 'People: Lord, have mercy.' In the litany occupying the place of our Augmented Litany, there is only the first rubric, while the second is omitted [^2277], presumably as self-evident. Consequently, in the Liturgy of the Apostle James the threefold **"Lord, have mercy"** was an appurtenance of the final petition of each great litany, while all the remaining petitions of these litanies, including the litany corresponding unto our Augmented Litany, had a single **"Lord, have mercy"**. (A remnant of this practice in the present Augmented Litany is the single **"Lord, have mercy"** at the first two petitions). In the Liturgies of Basil the Great and John Chrysostom, Greek and Slavic manuscripts make no rubrics regarding the people's response unto the petitions of the litanies, excluding one MS of the 12th–14th centuries, wherein it is noted after the litany *(Orig. p. 577)* **'Let us all say'**: 'The people at the end: Lord, have mercy (12 times)' [^2278]; in Greek printed editions at each petition of the Augmented Litany, as also of the Great Litany, there stands: 'Choir: Lord, have mercy', and at the last: *Eis polla etē, despota* [Gr. Εἰς πολλὰ ἔτη, δέσποτα; Many years, Master]; the latter is explained by the custom indicated by certain Greek MSS of the 11th–13th centuries, for the hierarch or priest to sign with the cross and bless (*staurōnei, sphragizei* [Gr. σταυρώνει, σφραγίζει]) the people before the ecphonesis of this litany; the first Slavic printed editions have no such rubrics, nor do the Old Believer ones. For the first time Slavic printed editions, beginning with the Moscow *Sluzhebnik* of 1653, appoint such responses unto the petitions of the Augmented Litany as are customary at present, that is, for the first two—a single **"Lord, have mercy"**, and for the remainder—the threefold response (editions, for example the Moscow edition of 1655, that have at the end of the litany a petition for the tsar, appoint for it

=== LEAF p690 ===
a 12-fold **"Lord, have mercy"**) [^2279].

## The Augmented Litany at Vespers

At Vespers the Augmented Litany, it must be supposed, was for the most part of the same composition as at the Liturgy. This is evident already from the fact that many manuscripts do not provide its text in the order of Vespers; where it is provided, however, it coincides with the liturgical text of the Augmented Litany of that same epoch. Thus in a 16th-century Georgian MS of the Tiflis Ecclesiastical Museum No. 450, in the order of Vespers, which bears traces of the Chanted Office [*asmatike akolouthia*], after the prokeimenon there is the following litany:

"**'Let us all say... O Lord Almighty... Rich in mercy... Who willest not the death of a sinner... For this holy temple... For our archbishop... That He may help and subdue unto him... For this city... For seasonable weather... For those who sail, travel... That we may be delivered... And for all who require help from God... Protect, save...'**" [^2280].

The 3rd and 4th of these petitions are indicated at the Vesperal litany also by certain Slavic MSS of the 13th–15th centuries [^2281]. In the order of Vespers that has preserved fewer traces of the Chanted Office than the preceding Georgian MS, according unto a manuscript of the Patmos Library No. 105 of the 13th century, the Augmented Litany has the first three petitions as at present, and thereafter:

"**'Furthermore we pray that the Lover of mankind, God, may be merciful, good, and gracious unto our transgressions... For our most pious and right-believing emperors [tsars], their might, victory, abiding... For the servant of God N. and all our brotherhood in Christ, their health, salvation...'** Here he commemorates the living, whomsoever he willeth. **'For the health, salvation, and remission of transgressions of all... And for our emperors who have been in pious memory... For the departed fathers, hegumens, and brethren... Furthermore we pray for those who remain and those who are departing, for the healing of those lying in infirmity, for the repose, relief, and blessed memory, the remission of sins of all who have fallen asleep before us...'**" [^2282].

*(Orig. p. 578)*

In these and in the majority of manuscripts prior unto the 12th century, at the Vesperal Augmented
