996. Kodinos [Pseudo-Kodinos], *De officialibus palatii Constantinopolitani*, p. 7. In the Roman Catholic Church there is likewise an order of table blessing, reminiscent of our own. There is a distinct order for dinner (*prandium*, properly luncheon) and supper (*coena*). Each consists of two parts: prayers before taking food, and after. The order begins with the priest's exclamation “Bless ye” (*Benedicite*), repeated by those present. Before dinner (or luncheon) the priest begins: “The eyes of all look unto Thee, O Lord...” and those present conclude this passage from Ps. 144:15–16 [LXX 144:15–16; MT 145:15–16]. “Glory... Both now... *Kyrie eleison, Christe eleison, Kyrie eleison*, Our Father.” The prayer, preceded by the customary exclamation “Let us pray”: “Bless us, O Lord, and these Thy gifts, which of Thy bounty we are about to receive, through Christ our Lord. Amen.” The reader: “Vouchsafe (*jube*), master, to bless.” The blessing: “May the King of eternal glory make us partakers of the heavenly table. Amen.” After dinner (or luncheon): When the reader says: “Thou, however, O Lord, have mercy upon us” [*Tu autem, Domine, miserere nobis*], the response is: “Thanks be to God” [*Deo gratias*]; all rise, and the priest [says]: “Let all Thy works praise Thee, O Lord...” (Ps. 144:10 [LXX 144:10; MT 145:10]), “Glory... Both now...”, and the prayer (*absolute* [without “Let us pray”]): “We give Thee thanks, Almighty God, for all Thy benefits, Who livest and reignest unto ages of ages.” Psalm 50 or 116 is recited alternately, “Glory... Both now... *Kyrie eleison*,” etc., “Our Father” (secretly, with the conclusion proclaimed aloud). Responses from Ps. 111:9 (“He hath dispersed, He hath given to the poor...” [LXX 111:9; MT 112:9]), Ps. 33:2–4 [LXX 33:2–4; MT 34:1–3], and Ps. 112:2 (“Blessed be the name of the Lord...” [LXX 112:2; MT 113:2]). The prayer (without “Let us pray”): “Vouchsafe, O Lord, to reward with eternal life all who do good to us for Thy name's sake. Amen.” Response: “Let us bless the Lord. Thanks be to God. May the souls of the faithful departed, through the mercy of God, rest in peace.” “Our Father...” (secretly). The priest: “May God grant us His peace. Amen.” Before supper the priest, after an introduction identical to that before dinner, begins: “The poor shall eat...” (Ps. 21:27 [LXX 21:27; MT 22:26]) (cf. our own rite!), “Glory... Both now...”, and the rest as at dinner, except for the concluding blessing: “May the King of eternal glory bring us unto the supper of eternal life.” At the end of supper is said: “He hath made a memorial...” (Ps. 110:5 [LXX 110:4–5; MT 111:4–5]), “Glory... Both now...” The priest: “Blessed is God in His gifts, and holy in all His works, Who liveth and reigneth unto ages of ages. Amen.” Psalm 116 and all following as at dinner. If there is only one meal in the day, then the second order is performed.
