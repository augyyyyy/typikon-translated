# THE ORDER OF THE SIXTH HOUR

## THE BEGINNING OF THE SIXTH HOUR[^294]

*O come, let us worship... (3).*
*And 3 prostrations. Then:*

*To the end, in songs of instruction, of David; when the Ziphites came and said to Saul: 'Doth not David hide himself with us?' Psalm 53:*[^295]
Save me, O God, in Thy name, and judge me in Thy power.
O God, hear my prayer, give ear to the words of my mouth.
For strangers have risen up against me, and the mighty have sought after my soul, and have not set God before (cont. on fol. 87v) *The Sixth Hour*

[before them.] For behold, God helps me, and the Lord is the protector of my soul.
He shall turn evil upon mine enemies; consume Thou them by Thy truth.
Willingly will I sacrifice unto Thee; I will confess unto Thy name, O Lord, for it is good.
For Thou hast delivered me out of every affliction, and mine eye has looked down upon mine enemies.

*To the end, in songs of instruction, of Asaph, 54:*[^296]
Give ear, O God, unto my prayer, and despise not my supplication; attend unto me, and hear me.
I was grieved in my sorrow, and was troubled by the voice of the enemy, and by the oppression of the sinner.
For they brought iniquity upon me, and in wrath they were hostile against me.
My heart (cont. on fol. 88r) *The Sixth Hour. [Fol. 88r]*

was troubled within me, and the fear of death fell upon me.
Fear and trembling came upon me, and darkness covered me.
And I said: Who will give me wings like a dove? And I will fly away, and be at rest.
Lo, I have fled far off, and lodged in the wilderness.
I waited for God that saves me from faintheartedness and tempest.
Drown them, O Lord, and divide their tongues, for I have seen iniquity and gainsaying in the city.
Day and night it shall go round about it upon its walls; and iniquity and toil are in the midst thereof, and injustice;
and usury and deceit have not ceased from its streets.
For if an enemy had reproached me, I would have borne it;
and if he that hates me had magnified himself against me, I would have hidden myself (cont. on fol. 88v) *The Sixth Hour*

from him.
But it was thou, a man like-minded, my guide and my familiar friend,
who didst sweeten food together with me; in the house of God we walked in concord.
Let death come upon them, and let them go down alive into hades;
for wickedness is in their dwellings, in the midst of them.
As for me, I cried unto God, and the Lord heard me.
Evening, and morning, and at noonday, will I tell and declare it, and He shall hear my voice.
He shall deliver my soul in peace from them that draw near unto me, for they were in many with me.
God shall hear, and humble them, He that exists before the ages.
For there is no change for them, because they have not feared God.
He has stretched forth His hand in retribution; they have profaned (cont. on fol. 89r) *The Sixth Hour. [Fol. 89r]*

His covenant. They were divided by the wrath of His countenance, and their hearts drew near.
Their words were softer than oil, yet are they darts.
Cast thy care upon the Lord, and He shall sustain thee; He shall not suffer the righteous to be shaken unto the ages.[^297]
But Thou, O God, shalt bring them down into the pit of corruption.
Men of blood and deceit shall not live out half their days;
but as for me, O Lord, I will hope in Thee.

*A Praise of a Song, of David, not inscribed among the Hebrews, 90:*[^298]
He that dwells in the help of the Most High shall abide in the shelter of the God of heaven.
He shall say unto the Lord: Thou art my protector and my refuge (cont. on fol. 89v) *The Sixth Hour*

my God, and in Him will I hope.
For He shall deliver thee from the snare of the hunters, and from troubling words.
He shall overshadow thee with His shoulders, and under His wings shalt thou hope; His truth shall encircle thee with a shield.
Thou shalt not be afraid for the terror by night, nor for the arrow that flies by day,
nor for the thing that walks in darkness, nor for the encounter and the noonday demon.
A thousand shall fall at thy side, and ten thousand at thy right hand, but it shall not come near thee.
Only with thine eyes shalt thou behold, and see the reward of sinners.
For Thou, O Lord, art my hope; Thou hast made the Most High thy refuge.
No evil shall come unto thee, and no scourge shall draw near unto thy (cont. on fol. 90r) *The Sixth Hour. [Fol. 90r]*

dwelling.
For He shall give His Angels charge over thee, to keep thee in all thy ways.
In their hands they shall bear thee up, lest at any time thou dash thy foot against a stone.
Thou shalt tread upon the asp and basilisk, and the lion and dragon shalt thou trample under foot.
Because he has set his hope upon Me, I will also deliver him; I will shelter him, because he has known My name.
He shall call upon Me, and I will hear him; I am with him in affliction; I will deliver him, and glorify him.
With length of days will I satisfy him, and show him My salvation.

Glory... Both now... Alleluia (3). Lord, have mercy (3). Glory... *Troparion of the day, or of the Saint. Both now... Theotokion:* 'Seeing that we have no boldness...'[^299]
*And if it be a fast, we say these Troparia, in Tone 6:*[^300] (cont. on fol. 90v) *The Sixth Hour*

*Troparion, Tone 6:* Thou Who on the sixth day and hour didst nail to the Cross the sin which Adam daringly committed in paradise: tear asunder also the handwriting of our sins, O Christ God, and save us.
*Verse 1:* As for me, I cried unto God, and the Lord heard me.
*Verse 2:* Evening, and morning, and at noonday, will I tell and declare it, and He shall hear my voice.
*And at these verses we make one prostration at each, singing the refrain:* 'Thou Who on the sixth day and hour...' *the whole unto the end. Then: Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*
*Theotokion:* Seeing that we have no boldness because of our many sins, do thou entreat Him that was born of thee, O Virgin Birthgiver of God; for the supplication of a mother avails much unto the pleading of the Master. Despise not the supplications of sinners, O all-pure one; for He is merciful and able to save, Who willed to suffer (cont. on fol. 91r) *The Sixth Hour. [Fol. 91r]*

for our sake.
Let Thy tender compassions speedily prevent us, O Lord, for we are become exceedingly poor. Help us, O God our Savior, for the sake of the glory of Thy name; O Lord, deliver us, and purge away our sins, for Thy name's sake.[^301]
Then, Trisagion. And after Our Father: *Kontakion of the day, or of the Saint. But if it be a fast, we say these Troparia, in Tone 2:*[^302]
*Troparion, Tone 2:* Thou hast wrought salvation in the midst of the earth, O Christ God, having stretched out Thine all-pure hands upon the Cross, gathering all the nations who cry: O Lord, glory to Thee!
Glory...
*Troparion, Tone 2:* We worship Thine all-pure icon, O Good One, asking forgiveness of our transgressions, O Christ God; for of Thine own will (cont. on fol. 91v) *The Sixth Hour*

Thou didst deign to ascend the Cross, that Thou mightest deliver them whom Thou hadst created from the bondage of the enemy. Therefore giving thanks we cry unto Thee: Having filled all things with joy, O our Savior, Thou art come to save the world!
Both now... *Theotokion:*
Being a well-spring of tender compassion, vouchsafe mercy unto us, O Birthgiver of God. Look down upon the people that have sinned; manifest as ever thy power; for hoping in thee, we cry unto thee: Rejoice! even as once did Gabriel, the supreme commander of the bodiless hosts.
*And if it be Wednesday or Friday, say this Stavrotheotokion:*[^303]
*Stavrotheotokion:* Most glorified art thou, O Birthgiver of God; we praise thee; for by the Cross of thy Son hades was overthrown, and death was slain; we that were dead have risen, (cont. on fol. 92r) *The Sixth Hour. [Fol. 92r]*

and have been accounted worthy of life; we have received paradise, our ancient enjoyment. Therefore giving thanks we cry unto thee: Rejoice, thou that art full of grace, the Lord is with thee!

Lord, have mercy (40). Then, the Prayer of Saint Basil the Great: *Thou Who at every time and at every [hour]...* Then: Lord, have mercy (3). Glory... Both now... *More honorable than the Cherubim... In the name of the Lord [bless, father].*
> **Priest:** "Through the prayers of our holy fathers..."
*And if it be the Great Fast, make 3 great prostrations with the prayer of Saint Ephrem: 'O Lord and Master of my life...' and the other 12 [prostrations], even as was indicated in the First Hour (see fol. 74).[^304] Then, the Prayer of Saint Basil the Great:*[^305] (cont. on fol. 92v) *The Sixth Hour*

O God and Lord of hosts, and Maker of all creation, Who through the tender compassion of Thine ineffable mercy didst send down Thine Only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, for the salvation of our race, and by His precious Cross didst tear asunder the handwriting of our sins, and thereby didst triumph over the principalities and powers of darkness: do Thou Thyself, O Master, Lover of mankind, accept from us sinners these thanksgiving prayers, and deliver us from every all-destructive and dark transgression, and from all them that seek to harm us, enemies visible and invisible. Nail our flesh with the fear of Thee, and incline not our hearts unto words or reason- (cont. on fol. 93r) *The Sixth Hour. [Fol. 93r]*

ings of malice; but pierce our souls with Thy love, that looking ever unto Thee and guided by the light that is from Thee, beholding Thee, the unapproachable and everlasting Light, we may send up unceasing confession and thanksgiving unto Thee: the Father without beginning, with Thine Only-begotten Son, and Thine All-holy, and Good, and Life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*And if it be a fast, then after the versicle, from the priest:* We make the 15 usual prostrations, according to what was previously indicated (see fol. 22).[^306] *After this, the Prayer:* 'O Master God, Father Almighty...' *(see fol. 22).[^307] And the Mid-Hour of the Sixth Hour.* (cont. on fol. 93v)

## THE MID-HOUR OF THE SIXTH HOUR

*The beginning is likewise as that of the others:*
O come, let us worship... (3). And 3 prostrations.

*To the end, for the people that were removed far off from the holy things, by David, for a pillar-inscription, when the foreigners seized him in Gath, 55:*[^308]
Have mercy on me, O God, for man has trodden me down; fighting all the day long, he has afflicted me.
Mine enemies have trodden me down all the day long, for they be many that fight against me from on high.
By day I shall not fear, but I will hope in Thee. In God will I (cont. on fol. 94r) *The Mid-Hour of the Sixth Hour. [Fol. 94r]*

praise my words, in God have I hoped; I will not fear what flesh shall do unto me.
All the day long they abominated my words; all their thoughts were against me for evil.
They will dwell and hide; they will watch my heel,
even as they have waited for my soul. On no account shalt Thou deliver them; in anger shalt Thou bring down the peoples, O God.
My life have I declared unto Thee; Thou hast set my tears before Thee, even as in Thy promise.
Let mine enemies be turned back; in what day soever I shall call upon Thee, behold, I know that Thou art my God.
In God will I praise the saying, in the Lord will I praise the word;
in God have I hoped, I will not fear what man shall do unto me. In me, (cont. on fol. 94v) *The Mid-Hour of the Sixth Hour*

O God, are vows, which I will render in praise of Thee.
For Thou hast delivered my soul from death, mine eyes from tears, and my feet from falling;
I will be well-pleasing before the Lord in the light of the living.

*To the end, destroy not, by David, for a pillar-inscription, when he fled from the face of Saul into the cave, 56:*[^309]
Have mercy on me, O God, have mercy on me, for my soul has hoped in Thee;
and in the shadow of Thy wings will I hope, until iniquity pass away.
I will cry unto God Most High, unto God that has dealt bountifully with me.
He sent from heaven and saved me; He gave them to reproach that trod upon me.
God sent forth His mercy and His truth, and delivered my soul from the midst (cont. on fol. 95r) *The Mid-Hour of the Sixth Hour. [Fol. 95r]*

of lions' whelps; I fell asleep troubled.
As for the sons of men, their teeth are weapons and arrows, and their tongue a sharp sword.
Be Thou exalted above the heavens, O God, and Thy glory above all the earth.
They prepared a snare for my feet, and bowed down my soul;
they digged a pit before my face, and fell into it themselves.
Ready is my heart, O God, ready is my heart; I will sing and chant in my glory.
Awake up, my glory; awake, psaltery and harp; I myself will awake early.
I will confess Thee among the peoples, O Lord; I will chant unto Thee among the nations.
For Thy mercy is magnified unto the heavens, and Thy truth unto the clouds.
Be Thou exalted above the heavens, O God, and Thy glory above all the earth. (cont. on fol. 95v) *The Mid-Hour of the Sixth Hour*

*To the end, of David, in remembrance, that the Lord may save me, 69:*[^310]
O God, attend unto my help; O Lord, make haste to help me.
Let them be put to shame and confounded that seek after my soul.
Let them be turned back and brought to shame that wish me evil; let them be turned back immediately shameful, who say unto me: Aha, aha!
Let them be glad and rejoice in Thee, all they that seek Thee, O God;
and let them say continually: The Lord be magnified, who love Thy salvation.
But as for me, I am poor and needy; O God, help me.
Thou art my helper and my deliverer, O Lord, make no long tarrying. (cont. on fol. 96r) *The Mid-Hour of the Sixth Hour. [Fol. 96r]*

Glory... Both now... Trisagion, with three prostrations, if it be a fast. And after Our Father:
*These Troparia, in Tone 1:*
Save, O Lord, Thy people, and bless Thine inheritance... *(see fol. 45).[^311]*
Glory...
*Troparion of the Martyrs, Tone 1:* By the sufferings of the Saints which they endured for Thy sake, be Thou entreated, O Lord; and heal all our sicknesses and passions, O Lover of mankind, we pray Thee.
Both now...
*Theotokion, Tone 1:* Through the prayers, O Lord, of all the Saints and of the Birthgiver of God, give us Thy peace, and have mercy on us, as Thou alone art compassionate.

Lord, have mercy (30). Glory... Both now... *More honorable than the Cherubim... In the name of the Lord [bless, father].*
> **Priest:** "Through the prayers of our holy fathers, O Lord Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us."
*And we make three great prostrations, if it be a fast,* (cont. on fol. 96v) *The Mid-Hour of the Sixth Hour*

with the prayer: 'O Lord and Master of my life...' *(see fol. 22).[^312]* After this, the Prayer: 'O God and Lord of hosts, and Maker of all creation...' *Written at the end of the Sixth Hour (see fol. 92).[^313]*
Then, the Obednitsa. But when it is a fast, we first chant the Ninth Hour, and after that, the Obednitsa.[^314]
*(Decorative tailpiece vignette: mascaron with coronet and Baroque foliage)* (cont. on fol. 97r) *(Engraved headpiece: Christ the Man of Sorrows in the tomb with Cross and Passion instruments)*

### Notes

[^294]: The Sixth Hour opens with an ornate engraved headpiece depicting Christ in glory with angels. The service commemorates the crucifixion of Christ at the sixth hour (noon) of the day.
[^295]: Psalm 53 (LXX 53 / MT 54), titled 'To the end, in hymns of understanding, of David; when the Ziphites came...', the first of the three fixed psalms of the Sixth Hour.
[^296]: Psalm 54 (LXX 54 / MT 55), titled 'To the end, in songs of instruction, of Asaph' (Въ коне́цъ в̾ пѣ́снехъ, ра́зоу Аса́фоу). In standard Septuagint and liturgical Psalters, this psalm is ascribed to David (συνέσεως τῷ Δαυιδ / ра́зꙋма Дави́дꙋ); the 1617 Kyiv Horologion typesetter introduces an anomalous typographical substitution attributing it to Asaph.
[^297]: Slavonic text reads 'не да́стъ въ вѣ́къ мл҃твы пра́ведникꙋ' (with the titlo abbreviation for 'prayer', мл҃твы), a graphic scribal/typographical corruption of 'молвы́' or 'смяте́нїя' (LXX: οὐ δώσει εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα σάλον τῷ δικαίῳ; 'He shall not suffer the righteous to be shaken unto the ages').
[^298]: Psalm 90 (LXX 90 / MT 91), titled 'A Praise of a Song, of David, not inscribed among the Hebrews', the third fixed psalm of the Sixth Hour, prophesying divine protection.
[^299]: The fixed Theotokion of the Sixth Hour ('Seeing that we have no boldness...'), appealing to the motherly boldness of the Theotokos before Christ.
[^300]: The Lenten troparion of the Sixth Hour ('Thou Who on the sixth day and hour didst nail to the Cross...'), commemorating the crucifixion of Christ at the sixth hour (noon), chanted with prostrations on fasting days.
[^301]: Psalm 78:8-9 (LXX 78 / MT 79:8-9), standard liturgical petition for divine pardon and redemption.
[^302]: Lenten Troparia in Tone 2 for the Sixth Hour ('Thou hast wrought salvation in the midst of the earth...' and 'We worship Thine all-pure icon...'), celebrating the Passion and holy icon of the Savior.
[^303]: Stavrotheotokion in Tone 2 ('Most glorified art thou, O Birthgiver of God...'), appointed on Wednesdays and Fridays in honor of the Cross.
[^304]: Fol. 74r-75r corresponds to physical leaves p97-p98 in the First Hour, where the full rubric of Ephremic prostrations is set forth.
[^305]: The solemn concluding prayer of the Sixth Hour, attributed to St. Basil the Great ('O God and Lord of hosts, and Maker of all creation...').
[^306]: Fol. 22r corresponds to physical leaf p43 in the Midnight Office.
[^307]: The Prayer of St. Mardarios ('O Master God, Father Almighty...'), cross-referenced to fol. 22r (physical leaf p43).
[^308]: Psalm 55 (LXX 55 / MT 56), titled 'To the end, for the people that were removed far off from the holy things, by David, for a pillar-inscription, when the foreigners seized him in Gath', the first psalm of the Mid-Hour of the Sixth Hour.
[^309]: Psalm 56 (LXX 56 / MT 57), titled 'To the end, destroy not, by David, for a pillar-inscription, when he fled from the face of Saul into the cave', the second psalm of the Mid-Hour of the Sixth Hour.
[^310]: Psalm 69 (LXX 69 / MT 70), titled 'To the end, of David, in remembrance, that the Lord may save me', the third psalm of the Mid-Hour of the Sixth Hour.
[^311]: The Troparion of the Holy Cross in Tone 1 ('Save, O Lord, Thy people...'), cross-referenced to fol. 45r (physical leaf p68) in Matins.
[^312]: Fol. 22r corresponds to physical leaf p43 in the Midnight Office.
[^313]: Fol. 92v-93r corresponds to physical leaf p116, the end of the Sixth Hour.
[^314]: Rubric prescribing that during fasting seasons, the Ninth Hour is chanted immediately following the Mid-Hour of the Sixth Hour, before the Obednitsa (Typika).
