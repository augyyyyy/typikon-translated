# KANONIKON: THE VENERABLE PARAKLESIS TO THE MOST HOLY THEOTOKOS [^450]

*Sung for Every Petition*

> **Priest:** "Blessed is our God, [always, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.]"

*Then, Trisagion. All-Holy Trinity. After Our Father:*

> **Priest:** "For Thine is the kingdom:"

*Then: Lord, have mercy (12). Then: Glory... Both now... Come, let us worship (3). Then we say Psalm 142:* (cont. on fol. 150r) *To the Theotokos. [Fol. 150r]*

O Lord, hear my prayer; give ear unto [my supplication in Thy truth]: *written, (see fol. 53)*[^451]

*After this: Glory... Both now... Alleluia (3). Then:*

God is the Lord, and has appeared unto us; blessed is He that comes in the Name of the Lord. *Tone 4.*

*After this, Troparia, Tone 4:*

Unto the Theotokos let us sinners and humble ones now diligently run, and let us fall down in repentance, crying from the depth of the soul: O Lady, help, having had compassion upon us; make haste, for we perish by reason of the multitude of sins; turn not thy servants away empty, for thee alone do we have as protectress. *(Twice).*

*Glory... Both now...*

Never shall we be silent, O Theotokos, of speaking thy mighty acts, we who are unworthy; for if thou hadst not stood interceding for us, who would have delivered us (cont. on fol. 150v) *Supplication*

from so many dangers? Who would have preserved us free until now? We will not depart from thee, O Lady; save thy servants ever from every adversity, O thou who alone art blessed.

*Then, Psalm 50:* Have mercy on me, O God, according to Thy great mercy: *written, (see fol. 6)*[^452]

*After this, The Supplicatory Canon to the Most Holy Theotokos. The composition of Kyr Theophanes.[^453] Tone 8. Ode 1. Heirmos:*

*Having passed through the water as upon dry land, and having escaped the evil of Egypt, the Israelite cried aloud: Unto our Deliverer and God let us sing!*

*Refrain:* Most Holy Theotokos, save us.

*Troparia:*

Held fast by many temptations, unto thee do I flee, seeking salvation: O Mother of the Word and Virgin, save me from cruel and grievous things. (cont. on fol. 151r) *To the Theotokos. [Fol. 151r]*

The assaults of passions trouble me, and many sorrows fill my soul; bring peace, O Maiden, by the calm of thy Son and God, O all-immaculate one.

*Glory:*

Having given birth unto the Savior and God, I beseech thee, O Virgin: deliver me from cruel things; for unto thee do I now flee, stretching out both soul and thought.

*Both now:*

Being in sorrow and anguish, vouchsafe me the visitation of divine providence which is from thee, O only Mother of God, as thou art good, the bearer of the Good One.

*Katavasia:*

*Save from dangers thy servants, O Theotokos; for all of us after God flee unto thee, as (cont. on fol. 151v) Supplication*

an unbreakable rampart and protectress.

*This Katavasia is sung after each Ode.*

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*

*O Lord, Vault-Framer of the heavenly dome and Builder of the Church, do Thou establish me in Thy love, O Summit of desires and Confirmation of the faithful, O only Lover of mankind.*

*Troparia:*

As the protection and shelter of my life have I set thee, O Bearer of God; do thou, O Virgin, steer me into thy haven, who art the cause of good things and confirmation of the faithful, O only all-praised one.

I beseech thee, O Virgin, to disperse the trouble of my soul and the tempest of my grief; for thou, O Bride of God, hast given birth to Christ, the Prince of peace, O only all-pure one.

*Glory:* (cont. on fol. 152r) *To the Theotokos. [Fol. 152r]*

Having given birth to the Benefactor, the Cause of good things, pour out the wealth of beneficence unto all; for thou canst do all things, being mighty in strength, as having given birth to Christ, O divinely blessed one.

*Both now:*

Assailed by cruel infirmities and painful passions, O Virgin, do thou help me; for I know thee to be an inexhaustible treasury of healings, unstolen and unspent.

*Katavasia:* Save from dangers thy servants, O Theotokos...

*Then, Little Litany, in which commemoration is made for whomsoever the Supplication is sung. Priest or Deacon:* Have mercy on us, O God: *and the rest according to the need. Lord, have mercy (40). Then, after the prayer, the exclamation:* Hear us, O God our Savior. *Sessional Hymn, Tone 2:* (cont. on fol. 152v) *Supplication*

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 2:*

Fervent prayer, and rampart unassailable, fountain of mercy, haven of the world; earnestly do we cry unto thee, O Lady Theotokos: Hasten, and deliver us from dangers, O thou who alone dost swiftly protect.[^454]

*Sessional Hymn, which we sing only at Compline. If thou wilt, read it also here. Tone 8:*[^455]

As the all-immaculate bride of the Creator, as the unwedded Mother, the receptacle of the Deliverer, as being [the temple] of the Comforter, O all-praised one: since I am a vile dwelling of lawlessness and have consciously become a plaything of demons, hasten to deliver me from their evildoing, making me a radiant dwelling by virtues, O giver of light (cont. on fol. 153r) *To the Theotokos. [Fol. 153r]*

and undefiled one; disperse the cloud of passions, and vouchsafe me by thy prayers to partake of what is highest and of the never-setting light.

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*

*I have heard, O Lord, the mystery of Thy dispensation; I have considered Thy works, and glorified Thy Divinity.*

*Troparia:*

Having given birth to the Lord Who is the Helmsman, calm the disturbance of my passions and the tempest of my transgressions, O Bride of God.

O thou who didst bear the Tenderhearted One, the Savior of all who hymn Thee, grant unto me who call upon Thee the abyss of thy compassions.

*Glory:*

Having acquired thee, O all-immaculate one, as hope, confirmation, and an immovable rampart of salvation, we are delivered from all cruel evils.

*Both now:* (cont. on fol. 153v) *Supplication*

Lying in sins upon the bed of my sickness, as thou art a lover of mankind, help me, O Theotokos, Mother and ever-virgin.

*Katavasia:* Save from dangers...

*Ode 5. Heirmos:*

*Enlighten us by Thy commandments, O Lord, and by Thy high arm; and grant Thy peace unto us, O only Lover of mankind.*

*Troparia:*

Fill our life with gladness, O pure one, giving thine undefiled joy, as having given birth to the cause of gladness.

Deliver us from dangers, O pure Theotokos, who didst bear the eternal deliverance, and the peace that passes all understanding.

*Glory:*

Disperse the darkness of my transgressions, O Bride of God, by the radiance of thy brightness, O thou who didst give birth to the Light (cont. on fol. 154r) *To the Theotokos. [Fol. 154r]*

divine and pre-eternal.

*Both now:*

Heal the infirmity of my soul, O pure one, having visited it with thy visitation, and grant me health by thy prayers.

*Katavasia:* Save from dangers...

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*

*My prayer will I pour out unto the Lord, and unto Him will I declare my sorrow; for my soul is filled with evils, and my life has drawn near unto hades; but like Jonah I pray: From corruption, O my God, raise me up.*

*Troparia:*

As He has saved [me] from death and corruption, having surrendered Himself unto death, do thou, O Virgin, pray to the Lord and thy Son to deliver me from the evildoing of enemies, since my nature was held fast by corruption and death. (cont. on fol. 154v) *Supplication*

I know thee as the protectress of my life and a steadfast guardian, O Virgin, who dost dispel the turmoil of temptations and drive away the assaults of demons; and I pray thee ever to deliver me from my passions, O all-immaculate one.

*Glory:*

We have acquired thee as a rampart and refuge, and as perfect salvation for our souls, and as relief in afflictions, O Maiden; and in thy light we ever rejoice: O Lady, save us now from passions and dangers.

*Both now:*

Upon a bed of infirmity do I now lie prostrate, and there is no healing for my flesh; but as thou hast given birth unto God and the Savior of the world, and the Deliverer from infirmities, I pray thee, the good one: raise me up from the corruption of diseases.

*Katavasia:* Save from dangers thy servants... (cont. on fol. 155r) *To the Theotokos. [Fol. 155r]*

*Little Litany:* Again and again:

> **Priest:** "For Thou art the King of peace:"

*After this, Kontakion of the Theotokos, Tone 6:* Unashamed protectress of Christians: *written, (see fol. 100)*[^456]

*After this, Another Kontakion, Same Tone:*[^457]

We have no other help, we have no other hope, but thee, O all-pure Virgin; do thou help us; in thee do we hope, and in thee do we boast, for we are thy servants: let us not be put to shame.

*Then, the Ikos, which we read only at Compline; if thou wilt, read it also here:*[^458]

Spread out thy hands, with which thou didst hold the Master of all as an infant, by reason of the multitude of goodness; forsake us not who ever hope (cont. on fol. 155v) *Supplication*

in thee; by thy vigilant prayer and boundless simplicity have compassion upon us, and grant unto our souls thy mercy, pouring it forth unto the ages; for thee do we sinners have as protectress against the dangers and evils that come upon us; but as having the compassions of mercy, hasten unto prayer and speed unto supplication, ever protecting, O Theotokos, them that honor thee.

*After this, Prokeimenon, Tone 4:* I will remember thy name in every generation and generation.[^459]

*Verse:* Hear, O daughter, and see, and incline thine ear; and forget [also thine own people, and thy father's house].[^460]

*Then, Deacon:* Let us pray to the Lord.

> **Priest:** "For holy art Thou, O our God:"

*Then:* Let every breath praise the Lord.

*After this, Deacon:* And that we may be vouchsafed (cont. on fol. 156r) *To the Theotokos. [Fol. 156r]*

[to hear the Holy Gospel]... Wisdom. Stand upright.

> **Priest:** "Peace be unto all."

*And again the Priest:* The Reading of the Holy Evangelist [Luke / N.].[^461]

> **Deacon:** "Let us attend."

*The Priest reads whatever Gospel he desires, according to each specific need; and to the Theotokos, this from Luke:* In those days, Mary arose and went into the hill country: *unto the end according to the custom.*[^462]

*And at the conclusion of the Gospel, we sing, Glory, Tone 2:*

O Father, Word, and Spirit, Holy Trinity: cleanse the multitude of our sins.

*Both now:*

Through the prayers of the Theotokos, O Merciful One: cleanse the multitude of our sins.

Have mercy on us, O God, according to Thy great mercy, and according to the multitude of Thy compassions (cont. on fol. 156v) *Supplication*

cleanse our transgressions.

*Then, Sticheron. Tone 6, Podoben: "Having laid aside all earthly hope" (Всѐ ѿло́жше):*[^463]

Cast us not away unto human protection, O Most Pure Lady, but receive the supplications of thy servants, for afflictions hold us fast; we cannot bear the demonic arrow-shootings; shelter we have not acquired, nor anywhere to flee, miserable ones that we are, ever vanquished; consolation we have none save thee, O Lady of the world, hope and protectress of the faithful: despise not our supplications, but make them profitable.

*The Deacon [says] The Prayer:*

Save, O God, Thy people: *in its entirety unto the end.*

> **Choir:** "Lord, have mercy (12)."

**Priest, exclamation:** By the mercy and compassions: (cont. on fol. 157r) *To the Theotokos. [Fol. 157r]*

*Ode 7. Heirmos:*

*The youth who came from Judea to Babylon of old, established by faith, trampled upon the flame of the furnace, crying: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!*

*Troparia:*

When Thou didst desire to accomplish our salvation, O Savior, Thou didst dwell in the Virgin's womb, whom Thou didst reveal unto the world as protectress: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!

O Mother who didst bear the Lover of mercy, beseech Him now to deliver me from transgressions and spiritual defilement, as I cry unto thy Son: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!

*Glory:*

A treasury of salvation, and fountain of incorruption, a pillar of confirmation, and door of repentance hast Thou revealed her that bore Thee unto them that cry: (cont. on fol. 157v) *Supplication*

O God of our fathers, blessed art Thou!

*Both now:*

Bodily infirmities and spiritual sins, O Mother of God, vouchsafe to heal for them that with love draw near unto thy protection, O Virgin, who didst bear Christ the Savior for us.

*Save thy servants from perils, O Virgin Theotokos:*

*Ode 8. Heirmos:*

*The King of heaven, Whom all the angelic hosts praise: praise ye and supremely exalt Him unto all ages![^464]*

*Troparia:*

Despise not them that require help from thee, O Virgin, who praise and supremely exalt thee, O Maiden, unto the ages.

Thou healest the infirmity of my soul and the bodily sicknesses, O Virgin, that we may praise and supremely exalt thee, the pure one, unto the ages.

*Glory:*

A wealth of healings dost thou pour forth (cont. on fol. 158r) *To the Theotokos. [Fol. 158r]*

unto them that faithfully praise thee, O Virgin, and supremely exalt thee unto all ages.

*Both now:*

Thou drivest away the assaults of temptations and the onslaughts of passions, O Virgin, that we may praise and supremely exalt thee, the pure one, unto the ages.

*Save thy servants from perils, O Virgin Theotokos:*

*Ode 9. Heirmos:*

*Truly confessing thee to be the Theotokos, saved through thee, O pure Virgin, together with the heavenly hosts do we magnify thee.[^465]*

*Troparia:*

Turn not away from the stream of my tears, O Virgin who didst bear Christ, Who hath taken away every tear from every face.

Fill my heart with joy, O Virgin, who didst receive the fullness of joy, consuming sinful sorrow.

*Glory:* (cont. on fol. 158v) *Supplication*

Illumine with the rays of thy light, O Virgin, driving away the darkness of ignorance, them that faithfully confess thee to be the Mother of God.

*Both now:*

In the place of affliction, heal him that lies low in infirmity, O Virgin, transforming sickness into health.

*Katabasia:*[^466]

O most radiant cloud, Mother of God, vanquish them that conquer us by thy sovereign and almighty right hand; and stand before them that are in afflictions; protect the wronged; and loose the sins of thy supplicants: for whatsoever thou desirest, thou art able to do.

*Then:*

It is truly meet [to bless thee, the Theotokos]:

*Then, Supplicatory Stichera. Tone 2, Podoben: "When from the Tree" (Е҆гда̀ ѿ дре́ва тя̀ мр҃тва, а҆рїмаѳе́й):*[^467] (cont. on fol. 159r) *To the Theotokos. [Fol. 159r]*

Thou prayest for all, O Good One, who with faith flee unto thy sovereign protection; for we sinners have no other continuous deliverance unto God in perils and afflictions, burdened with many sins, O Mother of God the Most High: therefore do we fall down before thee: deliver thy servants from every distress.

*Verse:* I will remember thy name in every generation and generation.[^468]

Joy of all that grieve, and protectress of the wronged, nourisher of the hungry, comfort of wanderers, haven of the storm-tossed, visitation of the sick, shelter and protectress of the infirm, staff of old age, O Mother of God the Most High, (cont. on fol. 159v) *Supplication*

thou art, O Most Pure One: make haste, we pray, that thy servants may be saved.

*Verse:* Give ear, O daughter, and see, and incline thine ear; and forget thine own people, and thy father's house.[^469]

Rejoice, O most pure Virgin; rejoice, honorable scepter of Christ the King; rejoice, who didst bud forth the mystical cluster of grapes; rejoice, heavenly gate and bush unburned; rejoice, light of all the world; rejoice, joy of all; rejoice, salvation of the faithful; rejoice, protectress and refuge of all Christians, O Lady.

*Glory... Both now... Tone 8:*

Rejoice, boast of the universe; rejoice, temple of the Lord; rejoice, overshadowing mountain; rejoice, refuge; rejoice, golden lampstand; (cont. on fol. 160r) *To the Theotokos. [Fol. 160r]*

rejoice, glory of the Orthodox, O Pure One; rejoice, Mary, Mother of Christ God; rejoice, paradise; rejoice, divine table; rejoice, temple; rejoice, golden jar; rejoice, joy of all!

*After this, Trisagion.*

*All-Holy Trinity. After Our Father: Troparion: Unto the Theotokos let us now run diligently: Glory... Both now... We shall never be silent, O Theotokos: seek backward, (see fol. 150)*[^470]

*Then, the Deacon's [Litany]:*

Have mercy on us, O God: *and all the rest according to the custom, for whatsoever one prays.*

*And: Lord, have mercy (50).*

*Then, the Priest [says] the prayer of exclamation:*

Hear us, O God our Savior:

*Or:* For a merciful God and Lover of mankind [art Thou]: *After this:* More honorable than the Cherubim, and more glorious [beyond compare than the Seraphim]: *Then:* (cont. on fol. 160v) *Supplication*

Glory... Both now... Lord, have mercy (3).

*Then, the Priest [gives] the Dismissal:*

Christ our true God:

*End of the Supplicatory Service to the Holy Theotokos.*

*At Compline, therefore, whether there be a Paraklesis or not, concerning this it was indicated before (see fol. 148)*[^471] *For if there be only the Canon, then after the Canon we say: It is truly meet; Trisagion; and after Our Father: Troparion of the day, and of the Saint whose temple it is, and the other Troparia as thou hast concerning these in Small Compline. But if it be the holy Forty Days, we sing these Troparia in Tone 6:*

*The right choir begins:*

O Lord of hosts, be with us, for none other helper in afflictions have we save (cont. on fol. 161r) *Great Compline. [Fol. 161r]*

Thee; O Lord of hosts, have mercy on us.

*And the other choir says the same verses: O Lord of hosts, be with us...*

*After this, the choir that began previously sings:*

*Verse 1:* Praise God in His saints; praise Him in the firmament of His power.[^472]

*And again the second choir sings: O Lord of hosts, be with us. And thus the initial choir says the remaining four verses, and the second choir responds at every verse: O Lord of hosts, be with us.*

*Verse 2:* Praise Him for His mighty acts; praise Him according to the multitude of His greatness.[^473] *O Lord of hosts...*

*Verse 3:* Praise Him with the sound of the trumpet; praise (cont. on fol. 161v) *Great Compline*

Him with the psaltery and harp. *O Lord of hosts, be with us.*

*Verse 4:* Praise Him with the timbrel and dance; praise Him with strings and flute.[^474] *O Lord of hosts, be with us.*

*Verse 5:* Praise Him with tuneful cymbals; praise Him with cymbals of jubilation. Let every breath praise the Lord.[^475] *O Lord of hosts, be with us.*

*Then, both choirs together sing this first verse with a most elevated voice:*

Praise God in His saints; praise Him in the firmament of His power. *O Lord of hosts, be with us.*

*Glory: Troparion, in the same tone [Tone 6]:*

Had we not Thy saints as intercessors, and Thy goodness (cont. on fol. 162r) *Great Compline. [Fol. 162r]*

having mercy on us, how should we have dared, O Savior, to hymn Thee, Whom angels unceasingly glorify? O Knower of hearts, spare our souls!

*Both now: Theotokion:*

Great are the multitudes of my sins, O Theotokos; unto thee have I fled, O Pure One, requiring salvation: visit my ailing soul, and pray unto thy Son and our God to grant me forgiveness of the evils I have committed, O only blessed one.

*Then:*

All-Holy Theotokos, in the time of my life forsake me not, and commit me not unto human protection; but do thou thyself defend me and have mercy on me.

*After this:*

All my hope I place in thee, Mother (cont. on fol. 162v) *Great Compline*

of God, preserve me under Thy shelter.

*Then: Lord, have mercy (40). After this, the Prayer of Saint Basil: Thou Who at all times:*

*Lord, have mercy (3). Glory... Both now... More honorable than the Cherubim: In the name of the Lord [father, bless]. Priest: God be bountiful unto us [and bless us]: And we make 3 great prostrations, with the prayer of Saint Ephrem: O Lord and Master of my life: and 12 other [small bows], slowly, so that all may be in unison (see fol. 22)*[^476]

*And after this, the Prayer to the Most Holy Theotokos, of Saint Basil the Great, or of Paul, the first [superior] of the Monastery of the Evergetis:[^477]*

Spotless, undefiled, incorrupt, unblemished, pure Lady, Bride of God, who by thy glorious birthgiving didst unite God the Word unto men, (cont. on fol. 163r) *Great Compline. [Fol. 163r]*

and joined the fallen nature of our race unto heavenly things; who art the only hope of the hopeless, and helper of the vanquished; the ready protection of them that flee unto thee, and the refuge of all Christians: despise me not, a sinner and defiled, who by foul thoughts and words and deeds and acts have made myself altogether unprofitable, and by slothful disposition have become a slave unto pleasures and dejection; but as the Mother of the God Who loves mankind, show thy love for mankind and have compassion upon me, a sinner and prodigal, and accept my supplication offered unto thee from defiled lips; and beseech thy Son, our Master and Lord, with thy maternal (cont. on fol. 163v) *Great Compline*

boldness, that He may open unto me also the loving-kindness of His goodness; and, overlooking my countless transgressions, turn me unto repentance, and show me forth an approved doer of His commandments. And be thou ever present with me, merciful, compassionate, and full of loving-kindness in this present life, a fervent protectress and helper, repulsing the assaults of adversaries and guiding me unto salvation; and at the time of my departure, preserving my wretched soul, and driving far away from it the dark visages of evil demons; and in the dread day of judgment, delivering me from eternal torment, and making me an heir of the ineffable (cont. on fol. 164r) *Great Compline. [Fol. 164r]*

glory of thy Son and our God: which may I obtain, O my Lady, most holy Theotokos, through thy mediation and protection, by the grace and love for mankind of Thine Only-Begotten Son, our Lord, God, and Savior Jesus Christ: unto Whom is due all glory, honor, and worship, with His Father Who is without beginning, and with His All-Holy, Good, and Life-Creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*If Great [Compline] is sung, we say the final Trisagion; 3 prostrations; after Our Father:*

*Lord, have mercy (12). Then this Prayer of Saint Antiochus the Monk:[^478]* (cont. on fol. 164v) *Great Compline*

And grant unto us, O Master, as we depart unto sleep, rest of soul and body; and preserve us from the gloomy sleep of sin, and from all dark and nocturnal sensuality. Calm the impulses of passions, and quench the fiery darts of the evil one which are deceitfully hurled against us. Assuage the rebellions of our flesh, and lull to sleep all our earthly and carnal mind. And grant unto us, O God, a watchful mind, a chaste thought, a sober heart, a light sleep, and free from every satanic fantasy. And raise us up at the time of prayer, established in Thy commandments, and holding within ourselves the remembrance (cont. on fol. 165r) *Great Compline. [Fol. 165r]*

of Thy judgments continually. And grant unto us all-night doxology, to hymn, and bless, and glorify Thy most honorable and majestic name, of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

O most glorious, ever-virgin Theotokos, Mother of Christ our God: accept our prayers, and bring them before thy Son and our God, that for thy sake He may save and enlighten our souls.

*Then, the Prayer of Saint Joannicius:[^479]*

God is our hope, and Christ is our refuge, and the Holy Spirit is our protector.[^480]

*Glory... Both now... Lord, have mercy (20). Lord, bless.*

*Priest: Dismissal: Christ our true God: (see fol. 25)*[^481]

*And after the Amen, the brethren make bows, saying:* (cont. on fol. 165v) *Great Compline*

Bless, holy fathers, and forgive me, a sinner, wherein I have sinned in word, and in deed, and in thought, and with all my senses.

*And they respond:* May God forgive thee, venerable father.

*Then the brethren also bend their knees unto the earth, saying this mutual forgiveness:[^482]*

Forgive me, holy father, and bless all wherein I have sinned all the days of my life; and this day I have sinned countlessly, in soul and in body, through sleep and sloth, through demonic darkening, in unclean thoughts, in forgetfulness of mind, and in condemnation; I have sinned in heart, and with all my senses: by hearing and sight, voluntarily and involuntarily; and there is no sin which I have not committed, yet of all do I repent: (cont. on fol. 166r) *Great Compline. [Fol. 166r]*

forgive me, holy father, and bless, and pray for me, a sinner.

*The Priest responds:*

By His grace may God forgive and have mercy [upon you].

Let us pray for orthodox Christians: *(see fol. 26)*[^483] *And the Dismissal.*

Remit, pardon, forgive, O God, our offenses, voluntary and involuntary, in word and deed and thought, in (cont. on fol. 166v) *Great Compline*

*Then we say this Prayer:*

Them that hate and wrong us: *(see fol. 27)*[^484]

O Master of many mercies, Lord Jesus Christ our God: through the intercessions of our most pure Lady, the Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary; by the power of the precious and life-creating Cross; by the protection of the precious bodiless heavenly hosts; of the precious and glorious Prophet, Forerunner, and Baptist John; of the holy, glorious, and all-praised Apostles; of the holy and victorious Martyrs; (cont. on fol. 167r) *Great Compline. *And while this is being said, we all fall prostrate upon the earth, praying within ourselves. And when we have arisen, the Priest makes a bow unto the earth to the brethren, saying:* (cont. in cohort 20) *Great Compline*

*And after this the Priest says the Litany:* Let us pray for orthodox Christians: *And the rest: (see fol. 26).*[^486] *And after this, all the brethren make a bow unto the earth to the superior, two by two, from both sides according to their rank, each one saying the same words as he, having first bowed, spoke unto them.*

*And after this, the Priest says:* Through the prayers of our holy fathers:

*And we depart unto our own cells.*
