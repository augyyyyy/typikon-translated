**[Left Page — Fol. 17v]**

Luke writes: *A man full of leprosy, seeing Jesus, fell on his face and besought Him.* (Lk. 5:12)[^97] But as for those who make a bow with the foot, nor bow their head, only bending the knees a little, nowhere is such a custom found, not only unto God but not even unto men, except for the soldiers of Pilate, who, bending their knees, mocked Christ.[^98] But it is meet to worship God, as being the most sovereign of all, with the most sovereign member in the body, that is, with the head, in which are the higher powers of the soul and the mind. Moreover, Christ Himself, as Matthew testifies, *fell upon His face, praying.* (Mt. 26:39)[^99] And Luke: *When He was praying earnestly,* he says, *His sweat became as drops of blood falling down* (Lk. 22:44)[^100]

**[Right Page — Fol. 18r]**

*upon the ground.* (Lk. 22:44)[^101] For it could not be (preserving the divine dispensation whole) that the sweat should become as drops of blood falling down, had He not wearied His body by falling down and rising up frequently. It is written also concerning the Most Pure Birthgiver of God, that where she made prostrations upon a marble stone, even unto this day the marks exist, that is, depressions from her holy hands, head, and knees.[^102] Sozomen also testifies concerning James, the Brother of the Lord, the first bishop of Jerusalem,[^103] that he gave himself over unto so great labors of prayer that all his members were mortified, and his brow, from continual prostrations for help, became so hardened that it was in no wise better than the knees of a camel.[^104]

=== LEAF p22 ===

**[Left Page — Fol. 18v]**

And Cassian the Roman, who visited all the monasteries and sketes in the eastern lands, testifies concerning the Egyptian and Thebaid monks, that they made prostrations falling down unto the ground and rising up again; in book 2, chapter 17, and in book 8, chapter 58.[^105] And that the Church maintains praying while standing, David also prophesied: *Behold now, bless the Lord, all ye servants of the Lord, who stand in the house of the Lord, in the courts of the house of our God.* (Ps. 133:1-2)[^106] And Christ revealed that it is acceptable to pray standing, saying: *When ye stand praying, forgive.* (Mk. 11:25)[^107] Nor without reasoned word and mystery did the Eastern Church teach to pray standing, and falling prostrate; witness hereof is Basil the Great, writing to Amphilochius, bishop

**[Right Page — Fol. 19r]**

of Iconium, in the book *On the Holy Spirit.*[^108] He discloses that mystery in these words: *Necessarily on that day, that is to say on Sunday, the Church teaches her children to offer their prayers standing, that by continual remembrance of that never-ending life we may not neglect to prepare provision for our journey thither.*[^109] *Moreover, the entire period until Pentecost is a remembrance of the resurrection which we await in that age.* (And below thus:) *The ecclesiastical ordinances have taught us to pray in an upright posture of the body, as though transferring our mind from things present unto things to come.*[^110] And concerning prostrations, the same Father in the same place speaks thus: *Furthermore, as often as we bow the knees*[^111]

=== LEAF p23 ===

**[Left Page — Fol. 19v]**

*and rise up again, we show by our deed that through sin we fell down unto the earth, and that by the love of Him Who made us toward mankind we have been summoned unto heaven.* Thus far Basil.[^112] And so, O Christian, behold here that Basil the Great, the eye and pillar of the Church, showed not only these things in that tradition, but also the other manner with the concealment of mystery: twofold, that from antiquity to pray standing, and to make prostrations falling down and rising up, the Church maintains; and twofold, what mystery there is when we pray standing, and what is the mystery of the prostrations performed by falling down and rising up.[^113] And so, O orthodox Christian, thou hast the order of the figured prayers, concerning which, according to Basil, at no time of prayer, neither by day

**[Right Page — Fol. 20r]**

nor by night, should one flee away; even if they find themselves living far off anywhere by reason of business or local circumstance, they are bound of necessity to fulfill each thing there which is commanded in common, causing no division.[^114] In this it is evident that neither for the sake of a journey, nor urgent business, nor for hospitality is it permitted to leave off the rule;[^115] nor does any foreign law give release on account of necessity or befalling affliction, but constrains to fulfill all things lawfully instituted.[^116] Therefore, hearing and seeing such an orderly rule of prayer transmitted from the East, make haste to fulfill and keep it in a manner pleasing unto God; and unto the Church of Constantinople, her Mother, unto whom we of Rus' are subject,


=== LEAF p24 ===

**[Left Page — Fol. 20v]**

unalterably to submit; which in all things accords with the first-born Jerusalem Church,[^117] having only this difference between themselves, which is by local circumstance; not easily turning aside nor moving over unto others walking contrariwise; lest falling away from the piety handed down from the beginning by the fathers, we be deprived of the hope of salvation, and inherit the condemnation of transgressors of the law.[^118] And from this may Master Christ preserve us; but through the customary prayers according to the tradition of the fathers, propitiating our all-good God, by the intercession of His most blessed Mother, the Ever-Virgin Mary, and our Venerable Fathers, Founders of the Coenobitic Life, Anthony and Theodosius of the Caves (who from the holy Mount Athos

**[Right Page — Fol. 21r]**

having founded, adorned the Caves Lavra of Kyiv with godliness and an honorable order),[^119] and of all the saints, may we be vouchsafed to obtain forgiveness of sins and the meet mercy unto the final ages.[^120]

Fare thee well in the Lord, O prudent and orthodox Reader; and receiving small things with thanksgiving, pray that by the grace of Christ thou mayest be vouchsafed greater things.[^121]

## The Order upon Rising Swiftly from Sleep[^142]

*Without slumbering, without laziness, and having collected thyself upon rising from sleep, step away from the bed, and say this, thrice:*

Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee for all things.[^143]

Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner.
