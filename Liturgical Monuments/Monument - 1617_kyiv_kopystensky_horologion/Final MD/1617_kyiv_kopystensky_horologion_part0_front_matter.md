## PREFACE

**Eliseus Pletenetsky,[^4] Archimandrite of the Caves of Kyiv,[^5] unto the Orthodox Russian Race,[^6] the most beloved sons of the Eastern Church: soul-saving health!**

Even as, therefore, according unto the grace given unto me, over those entrusted unto me within the monastery of the Holy Caves Lavra, both inwardly and outwardly, the Lord working together with me, I labor, and in zeal for the piety of the Eastern Church do abide: even so, with soul-profiting works, to gladden the hearts of her orthodox sons, and in orthodoxy to confirm them, and to adorn them, did it seem good [to provide] ecclesi- astical books. And at the beginning I deemed it meet to send forth a small book as a forerunner,[^7] that it might prepare the way for greater ones; for neither does the day dawn without its forerunner, the morning star. And nothing did I judge more fitting than this prayer book, named the *Horologion*, wherein for ordinary and fasting days God-inspired prayers are found. And for this reason also, that from the Holy Caves Monastery—which has blossomed forth with the beauty of prayer and fasting unto humility, to such a degree that wonderworking saints were made manifest therein, and unto this day many abide in the incorruption of their bodies—by those very virtues whereby she has blossomed into so great a flower, with these it was fitting to greet you, that ye likewise by these two virtues might blossom unto the glory of God. Truly great is the power of this book, that is, of prayer; for this is the morning star that enlightens souls and disposes the faithful to converse with God. And thereby it is possible to escape visible and invisible enemies, and to be delivered from present afflictions. For King Hezekiah, it is said, and the Prophet Isaiah prayed and cried aloud even unto heaven, and the Lord sent His angel, who smote all the mighty men of valor and the warriors and the leaders of the hosts in the camp of the King of Assyria.[^8] And again, King Manasseh prayed diligently in Babylon, and the Lord heard his prayer and his cry, and restored him unto Jerusalem unto his kingdom;[^9]

...his kingdom. And it is delivered down in remembrance by Africanus,[^10] that when Manasseh made prayer, his iron bonds were rent asunder, and he obtained for himself all health. And let no one say: "Seeing that already not once, nor from a single printing press has this book gone forth, why then this one?" Good merchandise is ever in the marketplace; necessary sustenance is continually in the shambles; and an instrument wherewith we frequently make use, the craftsman ever constructs.[^11] Ought it not all the more so to be with prayer, the spiritual instrument? Which is a continual work, and greater labor lies therein, even as Abba Agathon said: "Every way of life that a man undertakes has a guard therein, acquiring rest; but as for prayer, until his last breath it requires labor."[^12] In addition unto these things, I was also entreated by the orthodox, that we might fulfill the need which is in the school in the Orthodox City of Kyiv,[^13] and among the rest. Even so was it pleasing unto us, when by God's help we purposed to publish books unto the world, that it was not fitting to begin otherwise than from prayer, which surpasses all things. Therefore, as a single blessing from our venerable Caves Fathers, receive ye lovingly this book of greeting, hoping, if the Lord will, shortly to be deemed worthy of embracing others also; and pray to God One in Trinity, that He may prosper what we have devised through the typographical craft to please the Orthodox Churches present, and committing the undertaking that is to be unto the judgment and correction of the Eastern Church. Fare ye well in the Lord!

## FOREWORD TO THE HOROLOGION[^14]

*All flesh shall come to worship before Me in Jerusalem, says the Lord.* (Isa. 66:23)[^15]

Lo, orthodox Christian, and every devout reader: from the renowned Kyivan sites in Rus', that is, from the Caves Lavra of Kyiv, by the grace of God, and by the diligence and care of the Most Venerable among the Fathers, Kyr Eliseus Pletenetsky,[^16] Archimandrite of the Caves of Kyiv, there comes forth printed the prayer book *Horologion*, which is interpreted in Slavonic as the *Chasoslov*; containing within itself the day and night prayers, arranged according to the order of the Church of Jerusalem and of the Lavra of Saint Sabas the Sanctified, archimandrite thereof.[^17] For, lo, it is meet to follow the dogma, typikon, and order of the Eastern Church. And this we do as in acceptable comeliness. For when we pray, we turn our eyes unto the east, where our fatherland of Paradise is, concerning which it is written: *And God planted Paradise eastward in Eden;* (Gen. 2:8)[^18] unto which God, disposing us ever to yearn, commanded Moses to turn the Tabernacle toward the east; and the tribe of Judah pitched their tents toward the east; (Num. 2:3)[^19] for from thence also do we behold the sun enlightening the universe. Therefore, it is lawful for us to receive from the east piety with all ecclesiastical good order, and to embrace it. And all the more so, since the righteous throne of Jesus Christ our Savior upholds the Church of Jerusalem; from thence, as formerly from Mount Sinai, the world received the covenant of the new grace; from thence, when God was incarnate and dwelt with men, (Bar. 3:38)[^20] the human race attained unto its salvation; from thence the universe manifestly learned the mysteries of the Tri-hypostatic Godhead; and, to speak simply: from thence, as from an ever-flowing fount according unto the God-speaking Prophet: *For out of Zion shall go forth the law, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem;* (Isa. 2:3)[^21] and into all the world the rivers of theology are poured forth, nor do they fail in flowing from thence. From that Church Christ commanded the word of grace to begin, as Luke says: *and that repentance and remission of sins should be preached in His name among all nations, beginning at Jerusalem.* (Lk. 24:47)[^22] In that Church the Apostles, having been filled with the Holy Spirit, went forth to confirm the universe; and unto her they turned again, and assembled together; and diverse discords and the upspringing of heresies, by divine counsel and judgment, as with a sword, they cut off from the fullness of the Church; sending forth from Jerusalem dogmas and injunctions unto the Churches of Christ they delivered, and transmitted them to be kept. And that Church of Jerusalem alone remained pure from heresy, for the Arians (as Hermias Sozomen testifies) did not enter into her.[^23] That Church is the Mother of Churches, according unto the Prophet: *A man will say, "Mother Zion," and a man was born in her, and the Highest Himself has established her;* (Ps. 86:5)[^24] and according unto Isaiah, who said: *And afterward thou shalt be called the righteous city, Mother of faithful cities, Zion;* (Isa. 1:26)[^25] and Damascene: *Rejoice, O holy Zion, Mother of Churches, dwelling-place of God; for thou first didst receive remission of sins on account of the Resurrection;*[^26] and the Second Ecumenical Council, in its synodical letter, calls the Church of Jerusalem the Mother of all Churches, speaking thus:[^27]

"Thus: And of the Mother of all Churches, which is in Jerusalem, we make known unto you that the most honorable and most God-beloved Cyril is bishop."[^28] From that Church of Jerusalem, the Mother of Churches, the foremost among the Apostles, Andrew, after the apportioning among the nations, having enlightened the Thracian lands with the light of the Gospel, and in the Thracian city of Byzantium, which is now Constantinople, having laid firm foundations of faith; and having been the first Patriarch there; traversing the ends of the universe, he reached the hills of Kyiv,[^29] and by the erection of the sign of the life-creating Cross, having put to shame the aerial adversaries,[^30] prophetically [proclaimed] that the readers of the true faith of Christ should dwell there, and that unfeigned worshipers of God in Trinity should abide and foreseeing this from afar by many ages, he prophesied it; which also in our Russian race, in the times of the Sovereign Vladimir—who was named a second Constantine Equal-to-the-Apostles—by the grace of Christ was radiantly fulfilled.[^31] When, filling the place of the fallen through ecclesiastical deprivation, and [coming into] light (the darkened stars), as a most radiant dawn with his peoples, he was abundantly deemed worthy of holy Baptism; and was numbered among those forechosen by God, in these latter times, with all his host, being illumined with the light of piety from the east and from Jerusalem; receiving all ecclesiastical traditions, order, and administration, as a patristic heritage from the Church of Constantinople Church and her Patriarchs, he inherited; and concerning this, the Rus'ians of that time and of future times, rendering submission and obedience unto him and to no other Patriarch, as unto their head and mother. This Church Emperor Justinian confesses to be the head of all Churches.[^32] And the August Emperors Leo and Anthemius confess her to be the mother of all Orthodox Christians, and sanction her tradition by law.[^33] Unto that same Patriarch, even unto this day, the wonderworking Lavra of the Monastery of the Caves, guarded by God, with all its dwellers, according to the order of the Patriarchal Stauropegion, has been subject from of old, and yields obedience.[^34]

And as for what pertains to the order and spiritual understanding of the prayers set forth in this book, I have judged good to write briefly, that there may be an answer unto heretics, and unto the Orthodox a warmer zeal for spiritual labors of prayer. According to the apostolic teaching, we are bound to pray without ceasing, as he says: *Rejoice evermore. Pray without ceasing. In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you.* (1 Thess. 5:16–18)[^35] And to fulfill this is possible, even as Basil the Great teaches: Sometimes with the tongue, when this is possible, and especially necessary, and unto the edification of faith;[^36] but if not, then with the heart, praising God in psalms, and hymns, and spiritual songs, as it is written, singing and fulfilling prayer in the midst of work.[^37] Nevertheless, since it is commanded to pray without ceasing, it is not fitting to despise (as that same father said) the appointed times of prayer, which we have chosen by night, each having its own particular remembrance.[^38]

*At midnight* we begin our prayers, returning from sleep as from the darkness akin to death; and as having risen from the dead, we hymn God, preparing ourselves beforehand for the morning. And since the Midnight prayers are placed first, let no one marvel; for not without reason did the Church transmit that they should begin from thence, especially for two reasons.

The first, because the day has its beginning from midnight. Even if Chrysostom says that the day is counted from the evening according to the Hebrew tradition,[^39] yet Theophylact says that from the eighth hour of the night, and Saint Theodore Balsamon, Patriarch of Antioch, says that the day begins from the seventh hour of the night;[^40] which signifies nothing other than midnight. Outside philosophers also, such as Plutarch and Macrobius, testify that the day begins from midnight.[^41] And thus we do not begin the first prayers, called the Midnight Office, as from the night, but as from the light which God called day, as Moses says: *And God called the light Day, and the darkness He called Night.* (Gen. 1:5)[^42] For God also created day first, as Basil in the *Hexaemeron* testifies thus: *And God said, Let there be light:* The first voice of God (he says) created the nature of light, drove away the darkness, and enlightened the world.[^43]

And Athanasius [speaks] thus: In that God said before all things, *Let there be light*, it is manifest that the day precedes the night.[^44] And Damascene: Neither was night called first, but day; and on this account day was first, and night thereafter.[^45] And thus when we begin prayers from midnight, we consider that we have begun from the day, because midnight is the beginning of the day; and those first [prayers] we understand as proper. For it is nocturnal unto us at that time not to be weighed down with sleep, but as the day is beginning, it is meet also for our mind to be stirred toward its Creator, and equally with the sun for the mind to ascend: the one to enlighten the universe, and the eye to soar on high unto the Lord, that it may be deemed worthy of enlightenment, and for the adverse darkness to be driven away.

The second [reason]: Because toward midnight we are assured of the Second Coming of Christ and the general resurrection, even as the Gospel truth testifies: *At midnight,* it says, *there was a cry made, Behold, the bridegroom comes; go ye out to meet him.* (Mt. 25:6)[^46] *Behold, He comes* [signifies] the Coming; and *Go ye out* manifests our resurrection, even as the divine Chrysostom, interpreting these things, says: Either it remained in a parable, or again it shows that the resurrection takes place in the night.[^47] Furthermore, He Himself, the Firstborn from the dead, having risen at midnight, manifestly prefigured this; following whom the divine Fathers of the Sixth Ecumenical Council and Theophylact the church teacher clearly teach us that Christ arose from the dead toward midnight.[^48] And to pray at midnight we have many indications, even as the narrative of the Acts presents, saying: *And at midnight Paul and Silas prayed, and sang praises unto God.* (Acts 16:25)[^49] And the Psalmist also says: *At midnight I arose to give thanks unto Thee because of the judgments of Thy righteousness.* (Ps. 118:62)[^50] And in another [psalm]: *In the nights lift up your hands unto the holies, and bless the Lord.* (Ps. 133:2)[^51] And Christ commands: *Watch ye therefore: for ye know not when the master of the house comes, at even, or at midnight, or at the cockcrowing, or in the morning: lest coming suddenly He find you sleeping.* (Mk. 13:35–36)[^52] And keeping vigil and sending up midnight prayers unto God, let us instruct our mind: that Christ after the supper went unto the garden, according to Chrysostom: For at midnight (he says) the Lord journeys, passes over the brook of Cedron, and hastens unto the place known to the betrayer.[^53] At midnight, as He prayed there fervently and was in an agony, His sweat fell down to the ground as drops of blood. (Lk. 22:44)[^54] At midnight He was taken by the soldiers. (Jn. 18:12)[^55] At midnight He was led first unto Annas, and afterward unto Caiaphas; and whatsoever things He, the Sinless One, voluntarily endured there for the sake of our salvation. And meditating upon these things in contrition of heart, we perform the midnight supplications not idly nor unfruitfully.

*The Matins prayers* we perform anticipating the day, that we may not on our bed sleeping be overtaken by the day, according to him that said: *Mine eyes have anticipated the morning dawn, that I might meditate on Thy words.* (Ps. 118:148)[^56] And in another Psalm: *In the morning my prayer shall anticipate Thee.* (Ps. 87:14)[^57] And (according to Basil) to receive nothing else into our care before the remembrance rejoices [in God], according to what is written: *I remembered God, and was glad.* (Ps. 76:4)[^58] And at that time we remember that Christ stood before Caiaphas and his council, receiving mockings and unrighteous examinations; even as John Chrysostom testifies, that from midnight even unto morning, Christ was examined by Caiaphas, being in nothing overcome.[^59]

*At the First Hour* of the day we perform prayers, not giving our bodies unto work before we do that which was spoken: *For unto Thee will I pray, O Lord; in the morning Thou shalt hear my voice; in the morning will I stand before Thee, and Thou shalt look upon me;* (Ps. 5:4–5)[^60] praying that *the light of Thy countenance may be signed upon us, O Lord;* and that He may direct our steps unto work; saying with David: *And let the brightness of the Lord our God be upon us; and establish Thou the works of our hands upon us, yea, the work of our hands establish Thou.* (Ps. 89:17)[^61] And in all these things we remember that at that hour our Savior was led into the praetorium unto Pilate, (Mt. 27:2; Jn. 18:28)[^62] and before his judgment the Judge of all the world stood; and we meditate upon all that He suffered there.

*At the Third Hour* we perform prayers, at which Basil commands the brethren to be gathered, even if they happen to be divided unto different tasks.[^63]

At that same hour we remember the Descent of the Holy Spirit, and the bestowing of spiritual gifts upon the Apostles; (Acts 2:15)[^64] praying that we may be worthy of receiving Sanctification, and asking of Him guidance and instruction unto what is profitable, according to him that said: *Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me. Cast me not away from Thy presence; and take not Thy Holy Spirit from me. Restore unto me the joy of Thy salvation, and establish me with Thy governing Spirit.* (Ps. 50:12–14)[^65] And: *Thy good Spirit shall lead me into the land of uprightness.* (Ps. 142:10)[^66] Furthermore, we remember that at the third hour Pilate judged Christ to be crucified upon the Cross; and the painful sufferings which He received there, with a contrite heart.[^67]

*At the Sixth Hour* we are bound to pray, as David says: *Evening, and morning, and at noon, will I declare and tell; and He shall hear my voice;* (Ps. 54:18)[^68] and that we may be delivered from the mishap and the demon of noonday. (Ps. 90:6)[^69] And Daniel three times [a day] was kneeling upon his knees, praying and confessing before his God. (Dan. 6:10)[^70] And we remember that at the sixth hour Christ hung upon the Cross for our sins, saying with the Prophet: *He bears our sins, and suffers for us;* and that same: *He was wounded for our sins, and was tormented for our iniquities; the chastisement of our peace was upon Him; and with His stripe we are healed.* (Isa. 53:4–5)[^71]

*At the Ninth Hour* to pray, from the very [Apostles] unto us from the very Apostles in the Acts it has been transmitted, narrating that Peter and John went up into the temple at the hour of prayer, being the ninth hour. (Acts 3:1)[^72] And we remember that at that time Christ gave up the ghost, and until evening remained crucified upon the Cross, being ever-living: He died in the flesh that we who were dead might live unto ages; calculating the great grace and love of Jesus; for in truth (as John says) *Hereby perceive we the love of God, because He laid down His life for us: and we ought to lay down our lives for the brethren.* (1 Jn. 3:16)[^73] And all the more in this present time of persecution, fearing nothing, but having ever in mind what was spoken by the Prophet: *For Thy sake are we killed all the day long;* (Ps. 43:23)[^74] *we are accounted as sheep for the slaughter.* (Ps. 43:23; Rom. 8:36)[^75] Again we remember with tears that beside the Cross stood the Mother of Jesus, Mary, and John His beloved bosom-disciple, and all His acquaintances, and many women from Galilee and Jerusalem; (Jn. 19:25; Lk. 23:49)[^76] and how weeping they looked upon our crucified Savior, sweetest Jesus.

*The Vespers prayers* we perform not as first before the others, even though from evening is the beginning for saints and feasts, nor is evening prior to morning and day, even though in Genesis it says: *And the evening and the morning were one day.* (Gen. 1:5)[^77] For evening (as Basil says in the *Hexaemeron*) is the designation of day and night, or a common boundary of both; and again, evening is the end of the day, according to Basil, and let (he says) the pre-eminence of the first creation be granted unto the day; the end of that day he remembers here first.[^78] Nor because David says: *The lifting up of my hands as an evening sacrifice,* (Ps. 140:2)[^79] art thou bound to deem the evening prayers prior or higher; for he says this in the Psalm (as Basil interpreted it): It was a law with the priests, that from their own and of themselves, even if no one else should bring [an offering], they should offer one lamb in the morning, and another in the evening; the one they called the morning lamb, and the other the evening lamb; God revealing that He wishes to be worshiped ever at the beginning and at the end of the day;[^80] (and others [say] thus:) that the morning sacrifice was not complete, whereas the evening had the completed service. Then the evening prayers are the completion of the prayers of night and day; while for saints and feasts occurring on the morrow, it becomes in part a beginning. And these prayers called evening prayers we perform when the day has ended, and having seen the evening light, we hymn God, one in Trinity: the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit;[^81] and furthermore, rendering thanksgiving unto our Benefactor God, our Creator, for the gifts given in the day and our uncorrected shortcomings. And we remember and meditate that at the evening hour one of the soldiers with a spear pierced His side, and forthwith came there out blood and water; (Jn. 19:34)[^82] and that Joseph and Nicodemus, having taken Christ down from the Cross, wound Him in linen clothes, and laid Him in a sepulchre; (Jn. 19:38–40)[^83] and that Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses beheld where they laid Him. (Mk. 15:47)[^84] Again we reflect that Christ at late evening, that is, on the evening of the day of His Resurrection, when the doors were shut, stood in the midst of the disciples, and showed them His hands and His feet and His side, giving peace unto them. (Jn. 20:19–20)[^85]

And after these things, at last we perform the prayers called the Compline prayers, that is to say, after-evening prayers. And as the night begins, sending up supplication unto God that our sleep may be without stumbling and free from fantasies, and asking the Guardian Angel to preserve us from the snares of demons;[^86] and moreover, according to the tradition of the fathers, we make confession of all our sins committed either in word, or in deed, or in the very heart; which [fathers] teach concerning confession; as Basil the Great (at the end of the homily whose beginning is: *Man was created according to the image and likeness of God*) teaches us thus: Keep not hidden the audacious transgression in any wise, but confess it in the midst of all that hear, that through the common prayer his infirmity may be healed, and all evil fall away.[^87] And we reflect in ourselves that then at late evening, at the time of Compline, they sat over against the sepulchre of Jesus; as Matthew says: *And there was Mary Magdalene, and the other Mary, sitting over against the sepulchre.* (Mt. 27:61)[^88] And others, having returned, prepared spices and ointments, (Lk. 23:56)[^89] thinking quickly to see Christ and anoint Him.

Nor do I deem it unprofitable to set down here concerning the great prostrations, which we, sons of the Eastern Church, perform, falling upon our knees, striking the brow to the ground, and making worship unto God with all the members of our flesh.[^90] For such a prostration is as the whole burnt offering in the Old Law; for the whole calf was burnt unto the praise of God. After such a manner in ancient times also did they worship God: *Moses made haste,* it says, *and bowed his head toward the earth, and worshiped God.* (Ex. 34:8)[^91] And Joshua the son of Nun did the same at length: *And he fell,* it says, *to the earth upon his face before God, lying until eventide, he and the elders of Israel.* (Josh. 7:6)[^92] Inasmuch as if in such manner we bow also unto men, we sin not; for this is an ancient custom, and even now it exists among those that dwell in the East; even as we find in Scripture: Joseph bowed himself with his face to the earth before his father. (Gen. 48:12)[^93] Again, concerning the Shunammite woman it is written: *And the woman went in, and fell at Eliseus' feet, and bowed herself to the ground.* (4 Kgs. 4:37)[^94] And Jacob, not having made a bow once only, bowed unto his brother Esau: *Jacob himself,* it says, *passed over before them, and bowed himself to the ground seven times, until he came near to his brother.* (Gen. 33:3)[^95] How much more is it meet to make more abundant prostrations unto God: that is, falling prostrate and rising up again; even as we read concerning Daniel, who three times a day was kneeling upon his knees, praying unto God. (Dan. 6:10)[^96] And in the Gospels Luke writes: *A man full of leprosy, seeing Jesus, fell on his face and besought Him.* (Lk. 5:12)[^97] But as for those who make a bow with the foot, nor bow their head, only bending the knees a little, nowhere is such a custom found, not only unto God but not even unto men, except for the soldiers of Pilate, who, bending their knees, mocked Christ.[^98] But it is meet to worship God, as being the most sovereign of all, with the most sovereign member in the body, that is, with the head, in which are the higher powers of the soul and the mind. Moreover, Christ Himself, as Matthew testifies, *fell upon His face, praying.* (Mt. 26:39)[^99] And Luke: *When He was praying earnestly,* he says, *His sweat became as drops of blood falling down* (Lk. 22:44)[^100] *upon the ground.* (Lk. 22:44)[^101] For it could not be (preserving the divine dispensation whole) that the sweat should become as drops of blood falling down, had He not wearied His body by falling down and rising up frequently. It is written also concerning the Most Pure Birthgiver of God, that where she made prostrations upon a marble stone, even unto this day the marks exist, that is, depressions from her holy hands, head, and knees.[^102] Sozomen also testifies concerning James, the Brother of the Lord, the first bishop of Jerusalem,[^103] that he gave himself over unto so great labors of prayer that all his members were mortified, and his brow, from continual prostrations for help, became so hardened that it was in no wise better than the knees of a camel.[^104]

And Cassian the Roman, who visited all the monasteries and sketes in the eastern lands, testifies concerning the Egyptian and Thebaid monks, that they made prostrations falling down unto the ground and rising up again; in book 2, chapter 17, and in book 8, chapter 58.[^105] And that the Church maintains praying while standing, David also prophesied: *Behold now, bless the Lord, all ye servants of the Lord, who stand in the house of the Lord, in the courts of the house of our God.* (Ps. 133:1-2)[^106] And Christ revealed that it is acceptable to pray standing, saying: *When ye stand praying, forgive.* (Mk. 11:25)[^107] Nor without reasoned word and mystery did the Eastern Church teach to pray standing, and falling prostrate; witness hereof is Basil the Great, writing to Amphilochius, bishop of Iconium, in the book *On the Holy Spirit.*[^108] He discloses that mystery in these words: *Necessarily on that day, that is to say on Sunday, the Church teaches her children to offer their prayers standing, that by continual remembrance of that never-ending life we may not neglect to prepare provision for our journey thither.*[^109] *Moreover, the entire period until Pentecost is a remembrance of the resurrection which we await in that age.* (And below thus:) *The ecclesiastical ordinances have taught us to pray in an upright posture of the body, as though transferring our mind from things present unto things to come.*[^110] And concerning prostrations, the same Father in the same place speaks thus: *Furthermore, as often as we bow the knees*[^111] *and rise up again, we show by our deed that through sin we fell down unto the earth, and that by the love of Him Who made us toward mankind we have been summoned unto heaven.* Thus far Basil.[^112] And so, O Christian, behold here that Basil the Great, the eye and pillar of the Church, showed not only these things in that tradition, but also the other manner with the concealment of mystery: twofold, that from antiquity to pray standing, and to make prostrations falling down and rising up, the Church maintains; and twofold, what mystery there is when we pray standing, and what is the mystery of the prostrations performed by falling down and rising up.[^113] And so, O orthodox Christian, thou hast the order of the figured prayers, concerning which, according to Basil, at no time of prayer, neither by day nor by night, should one flee away; even if they find themselves living far off anywhere by reason of business or local circumstance, they are bound of necessity to fulfill each thing there which is commanded in common, causing no division.[^114] In this it is evident that neither for the sake of a journey, nor urgent business, nor for hospitality is it permitted to leave off the rule;[^115] nor does any foreign law give release on account of necessity or befalling affliction, but constrains to fulfill all things lawfully instituted.[^116] Therefore, hearing and seeing such an orderly rule of prayer transmitted from the East, make haste to fulfill and keep it in a manner pleasing unto God; and unto the Church of Constantinople, her Mother, unto whom we of Rus' are subject, unalterably to submit; which in all things accords with the first-born Jerusalem Church,[^117] having only this difference between themselves, which is by local circumstance; not easily turning aside nor moving over unto others walking contrariwise; lest falling away from the piety handed down from the beginning by the fathers, we be deprived of the hope of salvation, and inherit the condemnation of transgressors of the law.[^118] And from this may Master Christ preserve us; but through the customary prayers according to the tradition of the fathers, propitiating our all-good God, by the intercession of His most blessed Mother, the Ever-Virgin Mary, and our Venerable Fathers, Founders of the Coenobitic Life, Anthony and Theodosius of the Caves (who from the holy Mount Athos having founded, adorned the Caves Lavra of Kyiv with godliness and an honorable order),[^119] and of all the saints, may we be vouchsafed to obtain forgiveness of sins and the meet mercy unto the final ages.[^120]

Fare thee well in the Lord, O prudent and orthodox Reader; and receiving small things with thanksgiving, pray that by the grace of Christ thou mayest be vouchsafed greater things.[^121]

Written: On the 20th day of the month of December, according to the ancient true Calendar, in the Year from the Nativity of Christ 1616.[^122]

Z. A. K. [Zacharias Archimandrite Kopystensky][^123]
