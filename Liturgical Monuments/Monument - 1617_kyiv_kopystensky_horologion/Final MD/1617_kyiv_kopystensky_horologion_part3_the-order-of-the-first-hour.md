# THE ORDER OF THE FIRST HOUR

## THE BEGINNING OF THE FIRST HOUR[^259]

*O come, let us worship... (3).*

*Psalm 5:*
Give ear to my words, O Lord; consider my cry.
Attend unto the voice of my supplication, my King and my God; for unto Thee will I pray, O Lord.
In the morning Thou shalt hear my voice; in the morning I will stand before Thee and look up; for Thou art not a God that has pleasure in wickedness.
Neither shall the evil-doer dwell with Thee, nor shall the transgressors continue before Thine eyes. (cont. on fol. 67r) *The First Hour. [Fol. 67r]*

...Thou hast hated all them that work iniquity; Thou shalt destroy all them that speak a lie.
The Lord abhors the man of blood and deceit.
But as for me, in the multitude of Thy mercy I will enter into Thy house; I will worship toward Thy holy temple in Thy fear.
Lead me, O Lord, in Thy righteousness, because of mine enemies; make my way straight before Thee.
For there is no truth in their mouth; their heart is vain; their throat is an open sepulchre; with their tongues they have dealt deceitfully. Judge them, O God;
Let them fall from their counsels; cast them out according to the multitude of their ungodliness, for they have provoked Thee, O Lord.
And let all them that hope in Thee rejoice; they shall be glad unto the ages, and (cont. on fol. 67v) *The First Hour*

...Thou shalt dwell in them, and all they that love Thy name shall boast in Thee. For Thou, O Lord, wilt bless the righteous; Thou hast crowned us as with a shield of good pleasure.

*A Prayer of Moses, the Man of God. Psalm 89:*[^260]
Lord, Thou hast been our refuge from generation to generation.
Before the mountains were brought forth, or the earth and the world were formed, even from age to age Thou art.
Turn not man away unto humiliation; for Thou hast said: Turn again, ye sons of men.
For a thousand years in Thine eyes, O Lord, are but as yesterday when it is past, and as a watch in the night.
Their years shall be things of naught: in the morning as grass (cont. on fol. 68r) *The First Hour. [Fol. 68r]*

...it shall pass away. In the morning let it bloom and pass away; in the evening let it fall, become hardened, and wither.
For we have consumed away in Thy wrath, and in Thine anger have we been troubled.
Thou hast set our iniquities before Thee; our lifetime into the light of Thy countenance.
For all our days are failed, and in Thine anger we have consumed away.
Our years were reckoned as a spider's web; the days of our years are seventy years, and if we be in strength, eighty years, and what is more than these is labor and sorrow.
For meekness is come upon us, and we shall be chastened.
Who knows the power of Thy wrath, and from Thy fear to number Thine anger?
So make known to me Thy right hand, (cont. on fol. 68v) *The First Hour*

...and them that are chained in heart to wisdom.
Turn Thee, O Lord, how long? And be Thou entreated for Thy servants.
We were filled in the morning with Thy mercy, O Lord, and we rejoiced and were glad.
In all our days let us be glad, for the days wherein Thou hast humbled us, for the years wherein we have seen evils.
And look upon Thy servants and upon Thy works, and guide their sons.
And let the brightness of the Lord our God be upon us, and direct Thou the works of our hands upon us; yea, the work of our hands direct Thou.

*Psalm of David, 100:*[^261]
Of mercy and judgment will I sing unto Thee, O Lord.
I will sing and have understanding in a blameless way, when Thou shalt come unto me. (cont. on fol. 69r) *The First Hour. [Fol. 69r]*

...I walked in the innocence of my heart in the midst of my house.
I set no unlawful thing before mine eyes; them that commit transgressions I hated.
A crooked heart cleaved not unto me; the deceitful man who turned away from me I knew not.
Whoso secretly slandered his neighbor, him I drove away.
With him that had a proud eye and an insatiable heart I did not eat.
Mine eyes were upon the faithful of the earth, that they might sit with me; he that walked in a blameless way, this one ministered unto me.
He that works pride dwelt not in the midst of my house; he that speaks unjust things directed not his way before mine eyes.
In the morning I slew all the sinners of the earth, (cont. on fol. 69v) *The First Hour*

...to destroy out of the city of the Lord all them that work iniquity.

Glory... Both now... Alleluia (3). Lord, have mercy (3). Glory...

*Troparion of the Day, or of the Saint. Both now...:*
*What shall we call thee... But if it be a fast, we say this Troparion, in Tone 6:*[^262]
In the morning hear my voice, O my King and my God.
*Verse 1:* Give ear to my words, O Lord.
*Verse 2:* For unto Thee will I pray, O Lord.

*Prostrations: 3. Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*
What shall we call thee, O thou that art full of grace? Heaven, for thou hast shone forth the Sun of righteousness; Paradise, for thou hast blossomed the flower of incorruption; Virgin, for thou didst remain incorrupt; pure Mother, for (cont. on fol. 70r) *The First Hour. [Fol. 70r]*

...thou hadst in thy holy arms a Son, Who is the God of all: entreat Him that our souls may be saved.

Order my steps according to Thy word, and let not any iniquity have dominion over me. Deliver me from the slander of men, and I will keep Thy commandments. Make Thy face to shine upon Thy servant, and teach me Thy statutes.

Let my mouth be filled with praise, that I may sing of Thy glory and of Thy majesty all the day long.

*Trisagion. And after Our Father: Kontakion of the Saint, or of the Day.*

*When it is a fast, we say these Troparia, in Tone 4:*[^263]
Speedily prevent us before we are enslaved by enemies that blaspheme Thee and threaten us, O Christ our God; but overcome by Thy Cross them that wage war (cont. on fol. 70v) *The First Hour*

...that fight against us, that they may understand how powerful is the Orthodox faith, through the prayers of the Birthgiver of God, O Thou only Lover of mankind.

Glory...
*Troparion:*
O Guide of wisdom and Giver of understanding, Instructor of the unwise and Defender of the poor: confirm and give understanding to my heart, O Master. Give me speech, O Thou Word of the Father, Only-begotten; for behold, I will not refrain my lips from crying unto Thee, O Merciful One: have mercy on me that am fallen.

Both now... *Theotokion:*
The all-glorious Mother of Christ, and more holy than the holy angels, let us unceasingly praise with heart and lips, confessing thee to be the Birthgiver of God, as having truly brought forth for us God incarnate, and praying unceasingly for our souls. (cont. on fol. 71r) *The First Hour. [Fol. 71r]*

...Lord, have mercy (40).
*Thou Who at every time and every hour...*
Lord, have mercy (3). Glory... Both now... *More honorable than the Cherubim... In the name of the Lord [bless, father].*
> **Priest:** "God be bountiful unto us, and bless us..."
*Then this prayer:*[^264]
O Christ, the true Light, Who enlightenest and sanctifiest every man that comes into the world: let the light of Thy countenance be marked upon us, that walking in it we may behold the unapproachable light; and direct our steps to the fulfilling of Thy commandments, through the prayers of Thy most pure Mother and of all Thy saints. Amen.
Glory... Both now... Lord, have mercy (3). Lord, bless.
*And the final Dismissal.*
*If it be a fast, after 'God be bountiful unto us' we make 3 great (cont. on fol. 71v) The First Hour*

...prostrations, with the Prayer of Saint Ephrem: *O Lord and Master of my life... (see fol. 22).[^265]* After this, 11 other small bows, saying secretly: *'O Lord Jesus Christ, Son [of God, have mercy on me, a sinner].'* And we say also this: *'O God, be merciful unto me, a sinner. O God, cleanse my sins and have mercy on me. Without number have I sinned, O Lord, forgive me.'* And again we say the whole aforesaid prayer: *O Lord and Master of my life...* and the twelfth great prostration.
Then, the Trisagion. And after Our Father: Lord, have mercy (12). And the prayer: *O Christ, the true Light...*
Then the **Priest:** Glory to Thee, O Christ God, our hope, glory to Thee.
And we: Glory... Both now... Lord, have mercy (3). Lord, bless.
*And the usual Dismissal.* (cont. on fol. 72r)

## THE MID-HOUR OF THE FIRST HOUR[^266]

*If thou wishest to chant in church, then after the prayer 'O Christ, the true Light...' and after the dismissal, we begin:* O come, let us worship... (3). *If it be a fast, 3 prostrations.*
*But let this also be known: that the Mid-Hour of the First Hour we never chant in church; but after the dismissal of Matins and the First Hour in church, having gone each to his cell, and having made the Trisagion and after Our Father:* O come, let us worship... (3). *Then:*
*To the end, for the sons of Korah, concerning secret things. Psalm 45:*[^267] (cont. on fol. 72v) *The Mid-Hour of the First Hour*

God is our refuge and strength, a help in afflictions that have found us exceedingly.
Therefore will we not fear when the earth is troubled, and when mountains are removed into the hearts of the seas.
Their waters roared and were troubled; the mountains were troubled by His strength.
The rushing of the river gladdens the city of God; the Most High has sanctified His tabernacle.
God is in the midst thereof, and it shall not be moved; God shall help it early in the morning.
The nations were troubled, the kingdoms tottered; the Most High uttered His voice, the earth shook.
The Lord of hosts is with us; our defender is the God of Jacob.
Come and see the works of God, (cont. on fol. 73r) *The First Hour. [Fol. 73r]*

...what wonders He has wrought upon the earth.
He makes wars to cease unto the ends of the earth; He will crush the bow, and shatter the weapon, and burn the shields with fire.
Be still, and know that I am God; I will be exalted among the nations, I will be exalted in the earth.
The Lord of hosts is with us; our defender is the God of Jacob.

*A Psalm of a Song on the Sabbath Day, 91:*[^268]
It is a good thing to give thanks unto the Lord, and to sing praises unto Thy name, O Most High; to proclaim Thy mercy in the morning, and Thy truth every night; upon a psaltery of ten strings, with a song upon the harp.
For Thou, O Lord, hast made me glad through Thy work, and in the works of Thy hands will I rejoice. For (cont. on fol. 73v) *The Mid-Hour of the First Hour*

...Thy works are magnified, O Lord; Thy thoughts are made very deep.
A senseless man shall not know, and a fool shall not understand this:
When the sinners spring up like the grass, and all the workers of iniquity appear, that they may be destroyed unto ages of ages.
But Thou, O Lord, art the Most High unto the ages.
For lo, Thine enemies, O Lord, for lo, Thine enemies shall perish, and all the workers of iniquity shall be scattered.
But my horn shall be exalted like the horn of a unicorn, and mine old age shall be anointed with rich oil.
And mine eye has looked upon mine enemies, and mine ear shall hear of the wicked that rise up against me.
The righteous shall flourish like a palm tree; he shall be increased like a cedar in Lebanon, (cont. on fol. 74r) *The First Hour. [Fol. 74r]*

...those that are planted in the house of the Lord, shall flourish in the courts of our God.
They shall still increase in a ripe old age, and they shall be full of vigor, to proclaim that the Lord our God is upright, and there is no unrighteousness in Him.

*For the Sabbath Day, when the earth was first inhabited. A Praise of a Song by David, 92:*[^269]
The Lord is King, He is robed in majesty; the Lord is robed in strength, and has girt Himself.
For He has established the world, which shall not be shaken.
Thy throne is prepared from of old; Thou art from everlasting.
The floods have lifted up, O Lord, the floods have lifted up their voices;
The floods will lift up their waves with the voices (cont. on fol. 74v) *The Mid-Hour of the First Hour*

...of many waters. Marvelous are the billows of the sea; marvelous in the heights is the Lord.
Thy testimonies are made very sure; holiness becomes Thy house, O Lord, unto length of days.

Glory... Both now... *without Alleluia. Trisagion. If it be a fast, 3 prostrations. And after Our Father, these Troparia, in Tone 6:*[^270]
Have mercy on us, O Lord, have mercy on us; for, destitute of all defense, we sinners offer this supplication unto Thee as Master: have mercy on us, O Lord, have mercy on us.

Glory...
Lord, have mercy on us, for in Thee have we put our trust; be not exceedingly wroth with us, neither remember our iniquities, but look upon us even now as Thou art compassionate, and deliver us from our enemies; (cont. on fol. 75r) *The First Hour. [Fol. 75r]*

...for Thou art our God, and we are Thy people; and we are all the work of Thy hands, and we call upon Thy name.

Both now... *Theotokion:*
Open unto us the doors of thy loving-kindness, O blessed Birthgiver of God and Virgin; hoping in thee, let us not perish, but by thee let us be delivered from tribulations, for thou art the salvation of the Christian race.

Lord, have mercy (30).[^271] Glory... Both now... *More honorable than the Cherubim... In the name of the Lord [bless, father].*
> **Priest:** "O Lord Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us. Amen."
*And we make 3 great prostrations, if it be a fast, with the prayer: O Lord and Master of my life... (see fol. 22). And immediately this prayer, of Saint Basil the Great:*[^272] (cont. on fol. 75v) *The Mid-Hour of the First Hour*

O Eternal, Unoriginate, and Ever-existing Light, Maker of all creation, Fountain of mercy, Ocean of goodness, and Unsearchable Abyss of the love of mankind: show forth upon us the light of Thy countenance. Shine forth, O Lord, in our hearts the noetic Sun of righteousness, and fill our souls with Thy gladness; and teach us to proclaim Thy judgments, and to meditate continually to confess unto Thee, our Master and Benefactor. Direct the works of our hands unto Thy will, and prosper us to do that which is well-pleasing and beloved unto Thee, that even by us unworthy ones Thy most holy name may be glorified, of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, of one Godhead (cont. on fol. 76r) *The First Hour. [Fol. 76r]*

...and Kingdom: unto Whom is due all glory, honor, and worship, unto ages of ages. Amen.

*Another Prayer, of the same [Saint Basil]:*[^273]
O Thou Who sendest forth light, and it goes, Who makest the sun to rise upon the righteous and the unrighteous, upon the evil and the good, Who makest the morning and enlightenest the whole world: enlighten our hearts, O Master of all, grant us on this present day to be well-pleasing unto Thee, preserving us from all sin and from every evil deed, delivering us from every arrow that flies by day, and from every adverse power, through the prayers of our most holy Lady, the Birthgiver of God, and of Thine immaterial ministers, and of all (cont. on fol. 76v) *The Mid-Hour of the First Hour*

...the saints that from the ages have been well-pleasing unto Thee. For Thine it is to have mercy and to save us, O our God, and unto Thee we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

Lord, have mercy (2).[^274] Lord, bless. *And there is the final Dismissal. And with the dismissal we say:*
The Lord God receive the supplications of us sinners, and have mercy on us, as He is good and the Lover of mankind. (cont. on fol. 77r)

### Notes

[^259]: The First Hour opens with an ornate headpiece and the three introductory invitations to worship ('O come, let us worship...'). In monastic practice, the First Hour is joined directly to the conclusion of Matins.
[^260]: Psalm 89 (LXX 89 / MT 90), titled 'A Prayer of Moses, the Man of God', is the second psalm of the First Hour.
[^261]: Psalm 100 (LXX 100 / MT 101), titled 'A Psalm of David', is the third psalm of the First Hour, embodying the royal vow of upright governance and purity of heart.
[^262]: The Lenten troparion of the First Hour ('In the morning hear my voice...'), drawn from Psalm 5:3-4, is sung with 3 prostrations (metanias) when the Great Fast or minor fasts are observed.
[^263]: In the Lenten order of the First Hour, the Kontakion is replaced by the penitential troparia in Tone 4 ('Speedily prevent us...', 'O Guide of wisdom...', 'The all-glorious Mother of Christ...').
[^264]: The Prayer of the First Hour, traditionally attributed to St. Basil the Great ('O Christ, the true Light...').
[^265]: Fol. 22r corresponds to physical leaf p43, where the Prayer of St. Ephrem the Syrian is set forth with prostrations.
[^266]: The Mid-Hour of the First Hour (Mesorion / Междоча́сїе), an ancient monastic inter-hour recited during fasting seasons, here specifically prescribed to be prayed privately in cells rather than corporately in the nave.
[^267]: Psalm 45 (LXX 45 / MT 46), the first psalm appointed for the Mesorion of the First Hour.
[^268]: Psalm 91 (LXX 91 / MT 92), the second psalm appointed for the Mesorion of the First Hour.
[^269]: Psalm 92 (LXX 92 / MT 93), the third psalm of the Mesorion of the First Hour, extolling the cosmic sovereignty of God.
[^270]: The penitential troparia in Tone 6 ('Have mercy on us, O Lord...', 'Lord, have mercy on us...', 'Open unto us the doors of thy loving-kindness...'), recited at the conclusion of the Mid-Hour.
[^271]: The printed Horologion specifies 'Гдⷭ҇и помилꙋй • л҃ •' (Lord, have mercy, 30 times) for the Mid-Hour, rather than the 40-fold repetition of the main Hour.
[^272]: The first concluding prayer of the Mid-Hour of the First Hour, composed by St. Basil the Great.
[^273]: The second prayer of the Mid-Hour of the First Hour, also attributed to St. Basil the Great.
[^274]: The printed Slavonic witness prescribes 'Гдⷭ҇и помилꙋ́й • в҃ •' (Lord, have mercy, 2 times), an archaic Ruthenian Horologion practice before the final dismissal formula.
