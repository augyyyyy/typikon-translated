[^1]: Physical front cover: Contemporary early seventeenth-century brown calfskin binding over wooden boards, adorned with blind-tooled ornamental borders and a central floral rhombus medallion. At the top left of the upper cover is affixed the printed paper shelfmark label of the Main Library of the Holy Trinity–Saint Sergius Lavra (*Свято-Троицкой Сергіевой Лавры Главная Библіотека*), with nineteenth-century inventory numbers entered by hand.
[^2]: Inside front board pastedown: Handmade laid paper pastedown containing historical library shelfmarks in ink and Russian bibliographical dating notes in pencil (*Киев 1616 / 1617*), referencing the printing dates of the Pechersk Lavra Horologion.
[^3]: Provenance inscriptions on the preliminary flyleaf verso detail the bibliographic history of this copy: "Book very rare — firstling of the Kyiv Printing House, published in the year 1616 (see the end of the Epilogue of the Horologion). Lacks leaves 1 and 189." The note indicates that this copy lacks the title leaf (fol. [1]) and final leaf 189. Foliation starts on the facing recto with printed signature *а* as leaf 1.
[^4]: Archimandrite Eliseus (Yelysei) Pletenetsky (c. 1550–1624), Archimandrite of the Kyiv Caves Lavra (1599–1624), founder of the Kyiv Caves Lavra Printing House (1615), and a foremost champion of Orthodox cultural and theological renewal in Ukraine.
[^5]: Church Slavonic: *Кї́евскїй* (Kyivan / of Kyiv). In Early Modern Ruthenian publications, the Caves Lavra is consistently titled *Лавра Печерская Кіевская*.
[^6]: Church Slavonic: *Правосла́вному Ро́дꙋ Рѡссі́йскомꙋ* (unto the Orthodox Rus' / Russian / Ruthenian race/people). Here *Ро́дъ Рѡссі́йскїй* denotes the Orthodox Ruthenian/East Slavic people of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth under the Kyivan metropolitanate.
[^7]: Greek loanword: *ѠРОЛОГІ́ОНЪ* (*Horologion*, the Book of Hours). Archimandrite Eliseus Pletenetsky characterizes the 1616/1617 Horologion as the firstling and forerunner (*предитеча*) of the monumental publishing program of the Kyiv Caves printing press.
[^8]: 2 Chronicles (2 Paralipomenon) 32:20–21.
[^9]: 2 Chronicles (2 Paralipomenon) 33:12–13; cf. the Prayer of Manasseh.
[^10]: Sextus Julius Africanus (c. 160 – c. 240 AD), early Christian chronicler, whose *Chronographiae* preserve traditions regarding the repentance and miraculous release of King Manasseh from his bronze and iron fetters in Babylon.
[^11]: Greek loanwords: *Маке́лла* (*macellum*, meat market / shambles); *Ѻ҆́рганъ* (*organon*, instrument / tool).
[^12]: *Apophthegmata Patrum* (Sayings of the Desert Fathers), Agathon 9: Abba Agathon taught that of all spiritual struggles, prayer requires warfare and labor unto the very last breath.
[^13]: A reference to the Kyiv Epiphany Brotherhood School (*Братська школа*), founded around 1615 on land donated by Halshka Hulevychivna, for which Archimandrite Eliseus Pletenetsky and the Kyiv Caves Lavra printed this Horologion as an essential primer and liturgical text.
[^14]: *Предосло́віе Часосло́ва*: The theological and ecclesiological Foreword written by the scholar, theologian, and future archimandrite Zacharias Kopystensky (d. 1627). It sets forth the Ruthenian Orthodox understanding of Jerusalem as the Mother Church and liturgical archetype, defended against both Roman Catholic and Protestant claims.
[^15]: Septuagint citation of Isaiah 66:23: *Прїи́детъ всѧ́ка пло́ть, поклони́тисѧ предомно́ю в̾ І҆ерл҃имѣ́, речѐ Гд҃ь* ("All flesh shall come to worship before Me in Jerusalem, says the Lord"). The printed margin marks *И҆са́. ѯ҃ѕ*.
[^16]: Honorific title: *Кѵ́ръ* (Greek *Kyr*, Lord/Master), standard liturgical and diplomatic styling for hierarchs and archimandrites.
[^17]: Reference to the Jerusalem Typikon of the Lavra of Saint Sabas the Sanctified (Mar Saba), which governed the liturgical cycles of the Ruthenian Church.
[^18]: Genesis 2:8.
[^19]: Numbers 2:3: The camp of Judah was commanded to pitch toward the sunrise on the east side of the Tabernacle.
[^20]: Baruch 3:38 (3:37 in Greek/LXX): "Afterward did He show Himself upon earth, and conversed with men."
[^21]: Isaiah 2:3.
[^22]: Luke 24:47.
[^23]: Hermias Sozomen, *Historia Ecclesiastica*, Book 3, Chapter 20 (as cited in the printed marginal rubric *Кни: г. за: к.*), recording that the orthodox Christians of Jerusalem and their bishop remained untainted by Arianism.
[^24]: Psalm 86:5 (LXX): *Ма́ти Сїѡ́нъ рече́тъ чл҃къ, и҆ чл҃къ роди́ся в̾не́й, и҆ то́й ѡ҆снова̀ ю̀ вы́шнїи* (MT Psalm 87:5).
[^25]: Isaiah 1:26.
[^26]: St. John of Damascus, Sunday Sticheron at "Lord, I have cried", Tone 4 (Octoechos), as cited in the margin (*Ѡ҆кт: гл҃: д҃. Стї: въскр҃снꙋ*).
[^27]: The Synodical Letter of the Second Ecumenical Council of Constantinople (381 AD), preserved in Theodoret of Cyrus, *Historia Ecclesiastica*, Book 5, Chapter 9 (*Ѳеѡдо́ кни: є҃ з: ѳ҃*), which addresses the Church of Jerusalem as "the Mother of all Churches" (*τῆς γε μητρὸς ἁπασῶν τῶν ἐκκλησιῶν*).
[^28]: The Synodical Letter of Constantinople (381 AD) confirms Cyril of Jerusalem (*Кѵрі́лла є҆ппа*) as bishop of Jerusalem, declaring Jerusalem to be the Mother of all Churches.
[^29]: Church Slavonic: *Кї́евски бре́гѡ доспѣ́лъ* (reached the Kyivan banks/hills). This recounts the foundational Kyivan transmission narrative from the *Primary Chronicle* (*Tale of Bygone Years*), wherein the Holy Apostle Andrew the First-Called erects a Cross on the hills of Kyiv and prophesies that great churches and divine grace shall shine forth there. The marginal note cites Symeon Metaphrastes (*Сѵмеѡ́: Мета: Фев: к҃д*).
[^30]: Allusion to Ephesians 2:2: "the prince of the power of the air" (*въз̾доу́шны непрїѧ́зни*, the aerial adverse spirits / demons).
[^31]: Saint Vladimir the Great, Equal-to-the-Apostles, baptizer of Rus' (988 AD), analogized to Saint Constantine the Great. The marginal note cites 1 John 2:8 (*І҆оан: в҃*, "because the darkness is past, and the true light now shines").
