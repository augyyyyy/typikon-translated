[^233]: Psalm 37 in the Septuagint (LXX 37; MT 38), the second psalm of the Hexapsalmos, bearing the inscription *A Psalm of David, for a remembrance, concerning the Sabbath* (*Ѱало́мъ Давы́ду, въ воспомина́ніе, ѡ҆ суббѡ́тѣ* / *Ψαλμὸς τῷ Δαυΐδ, εἰς ἀνάμνησιν περὶ σαββάτου*).
[^234]: Psalm 62 in the Septuagint (LXX 62; MT 63), the third psalm of the Hexapsalmos, depicting the soul's morning longing for God in the Judean wilderness.
[^235]: The rubric prescribes *безъ поклоновъ* (*without bows/prostrations*), highlighting the solemn stillness required during the reading of the Six Psalms: the faithful stand motionless in silent contemplation of the Judgment without making metanias or bows during the central doxology.
[^236]: Psalm 87 in the Septuagint (LXX 87; MT 88), the fourth psalm of the Hexapsalmos, known as the most sorrowful lament in the Psalter, chanted as a contemplation of Christ's descent into Hades and tomb.
[^237]: This rubric directs the Priest to come out from the sanctuary through the Holy Doors (or from his stall in the choir) and stand before the icon of Christ the Master (or before the Royal Doors with uncovered head) to recite silently the twelve Morning Prayers of Matins (*Еѵхаὶ ἑωθιναί* / *Ѹтренніѧ мл҃твы*) while the Six Psalms are being read.
[^238]: Psalm 102 in the Septuagint (LXX 102; MT 103), the fifth psalm of the Hexapsalmos, a hymn of radiant thanksgiving for God's boundless mercy, forgiveness, and paternal compassion.
[^239]: Psalm 142 in the Septuagint (LXX 142; MT 143), the sixth and final psalm of the Hexapsalmos, depicting the soul's total surrender and cry for divine guidance in the face of spiritual enemies.
[^240]: The Great Litany (*Вели́каѧ є҆кте́ніа* / *Μεγάλη συναπτή*), or Litany of Peace (*Ми́рнаѧ є҆кте́ніа* / *Εἰρηνικά*), is proclaimed by the deacon or priest immediately following the conclusion of the Six Psalms.
[^241]: Psalm 117:1, 10, 17, 22-23 (LXX 117; MT 118), chanted antiphonally with the solemn refrain *God is the Lord, and has appeared unto us* (*Богъ Господь и явися намъ* / *Θεὸς Κύριος καὶ ἐπέφανεν ἡμῖν*).
[^242]: The rubric specifies that during Lenten or penitential periods (*Егда́ же есть По́стъ*), the festal chant *God is the Lord* is replaced by the penitential *Alleluia* chanted four times to the Tone of the week with the Scriptural verses from Isaiah 26:9, 11, 15.
[^243]: The Hymns to the Trinity of the Eight Tones (*Тро́йчны ѻ҆смѝ гла́сѡвъ* / *Τριαδικά*), chanted on weekdays during Lenten and non-festal periods in place of the festal troparia, following the singing of *Alleluia*.
[^244]: The Triadika (*Троичны*) of the Oktoechos: eight sets of triadica corresponding to the Eight Tones, chanted at weekday Matins. Each tone consists of three stanzas celebrating the consubstantial Trinity followed by a Exaposteilarion (*Свѣтиленъ* / *Φωταγωγικόν*).
[^245]: The Exaposteilarion (*Свѣти́ленъ* / *Φωταγωγικόν*, literally "Hymn of Light"), chanted after the Triadika, with daily variable intercessory endings appointed for the day of the week: Angels on Monday, the Forerunner on Tuesday, the Holy Cross on Wednesday and Friday, and the Holy Apostles on Thursday.
