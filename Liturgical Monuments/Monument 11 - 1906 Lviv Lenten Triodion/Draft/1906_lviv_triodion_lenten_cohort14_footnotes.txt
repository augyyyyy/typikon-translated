[^334]: Thursday of the First Week of the Holy and Great Fast at Vespers (*Въ четверто́къ а҃. седми́цы ст҃а́гѡ поста̀ ве́чера*): Physical leaf p131 (Printed fol. 127). Conclusion of the second Old Testament lesson from Genesis (Gen. 2:10–19), recounting the four rivers flowing from Eden (Pishon, Gihon, Tigris, Euphrates), the Lord placing Adam in the Garden of Eden to till and keep it, the divine prohibition against eating of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil upon pain of death, and the creation of animals brought to Adam for naming; followed by the second Prokeimenon from Psalm 16 (Ps. 16:8, *Keep me, O Lord, as the apple of Thine eye*); the third Old Testament lesson from Proverbs (Prov. 3:1–18) delivering paternal wisdom on keeping God's commandments, trusting in the Lord with all one's heart, honoring Him with firstfruits, enduring divine chastening, and exalting divine Wisdom as more precious than jewels and a tree of life unto all who hold her fast; concluded by the Aposticha Idiomelon of the day, Tone 1 (*Desiring to partake of the divine Pascha, not that which cometh from Egypt, but that which cometh forth from Zion...*), comparing the spiritual discipline of Great Lent to the Passover preparation of Israel (Exod. 12:11), girding loins with mortification and shodding feet with the sandals of spiritual vigil.
[^335]: Thursday of the First Week of the Holy and Great Fast at Great Compline (*Въ четверто́къ пе́рвыѧ седми́цы ст҃а́гѡ поста̀ на повече́рїи*): Physical leaf p132 (Printed fol. 128). Conclusion of Clean Thursday Vespers with the Martyrikon and the Cross-Theotokion, Tone 1, Model: *As one valiant among martyrs* (Ꙗ҆́кѡ до́блѧ); followed by the fourth and concluding section of the Great Penitential Canon of St. Andrew of Crete (*Вели́кїй Канѡ́нъ преподо́бнагѡ ѻ҆ц҃а̀ на́шегѡ А҆ндре́а Кри́тскагѡ и҆ И҆ерꙋсали́мскагѡ*), Tone 6, Ode 1, Heirmos: *A Helper and a Protector* (*Помо́щникъ и҆ покрови́тель*, Exod. 15:2), sung twice. The rubrics prescribe three prostrations (*покло́ны г҃*) at each troparion, accompanied by the penitential refrain: *Have mercy on me, O God, have mercy on me* (*Поми́лꙋй мѧ̀ Бж҃е, поми́лꙋй мѧ̀*). The opening troparia address Christ as the Lamb of God (John 1:29) and beseech the gift of compunction (*сле́зы ᲂу҆миле́нїѧ*), confessing the squandering of the soul's divine substance in sins, and turning in the final stanza to St. Mary of Egypt (*Марі́и*).
[^336]: Great Canon Part 4, Odes 2 and 3, Tone 6: Physical leaf p133 (Printed fol. 129). Ode 2, Heirmos: *See, see, that I am God* (*Ви́дите ви́дите, ꙗ҆́кѡ а҆́зъ є҆́смь Бг҃ъ*, Deut. 32:39), meditating on Lamech's song of violence (Gen. 4:23), the pride of the builders of the Tower of Babel (Gen. 11:4), the rain of fire and brimstone upon Sodom (Gen. 19:24), and invoking St. Mary of Egypt's prayer in the abyss of evils; followed by Ode 3, Heirmos: *Establish, O Lord, upon the rock* (*Оу҆твердѝ Гдⷭ҇и на ка́мени*, 1 Sam. 2:2), reproving the soul for bringing forth the insolence of Ishmael through voluntary enslavement to passion (Gen. 16:1–16), urging it to mount Jacob's ladder of virtues (Gen. 28:12), to emulate Melchizedek the righteous king-priest (Gen. 14:18; Heb. 7:1–3), and warning against looking back like Lot's wife becoming a pillar of salt (Gen. 19:26).
[^337]: Great Canon Part 4, Ode 4, Tone 6: Physical leaf p134 (Printed fol. 130). Conclusion of Ode 3 and Ode 4, Heirmos: *The Prophet heard of Thine advent, O Lord, and was afraid* (*Оу҆слы́ша проро́къ*, Hab. 3:2). St. Andrew reflects on the sudden ruin, disease, and poverty that befell the blameless and wealthy Patriarch Job (Job 1:1–22; 2:1–10), asking how the sinful soul will endure divine judgment if even the righteous suffered such adversity; prays against the boastfulness of the Pharisee while imploring the contrite humility of the Publican (Luke 18:10–14); mourns the defilement of the bodily temple and neglect of the divine Law; and praises St. Mary of Egypt's extraordinary conversion from the depths of sin to a life astonishing to angelic nature.
[^338]: Great Canon Part 4, Odes 5 and 6, Tone 6, with Kontakion: Physical leaf p135 (Printed fol. 131). Ode 5, Heirmos: *Watching early from the night* (*Ѿ но́щи ᲂу҆́треннююща*, Isa. 26:9), urging the soul to emulate the bowed-down woman whom Christ straightened (Luke 13:11–13), to thirst for the living water revealed to the Samaritan woman at the well of Sychar (John 4:7–14), and to wash spiritual eyes in the spiritual pool of Siloam (John 9:7); Ode 6, Heirmos: *I cried with my whole heart* (*Возопи́хъ всѣ́мъ се́рдцемъ*, Ps. 118:145; Jonah 2:2), presenting the repentant soul as the lost coin of the Gospel (Luke 15:8–9) and calling for warfare against the passions like Joshua against Amalek and the deceptive Gibeonites (Josh. 8–10); concluded by the Kontakion of the Great Canon, Tone 6 (*My soul, my soul, arise! Why sleepest thou? The end draws near...*), composed by St. Romanos the Melodist.
[^339]: Great Canon Part 4, Odes 7 and 8, Tone 6: Physical leaf p136 (Printed fol. 132). Ode 7, Heirmos: *We have sinned, we have transgressed* (*Согрѣши́хомъ*, Dan. 9:5), where the soul weeps like King Hezekiah upon his sickbed seeking an extension of life (Isa. 38:1–5; 2 Kgs. 20:1–6), confesses transgressions exceeding the sinful woman in the Gospel (Luke 7:37–38), and pleads for renewal according to the penitential prayer of David (Ps. 50:12); Ode 8, Heirmos: *Him Whom the hosts of heaven glorify* (*Е҆го́же во́инства*, Dan. 3:52–90), pouring out tears as an alabaster vessel of myrrh upon Christ's feet, entreating the Good Shepherd to recover His lost sheep (Luke 15:4–7), and shuddering before the intolerable judgment seat and fiery furnace of the Last Judgment (Matt. 25:31–46).

[^342]: On the melodic model *Tone 2, Podoben: "Being the wellspring of loving-kindness"* (Slavonic: *Мѵ́рꙋ сꙋ́щи:*): Physical leaf p138 (Printed fol. 134).
