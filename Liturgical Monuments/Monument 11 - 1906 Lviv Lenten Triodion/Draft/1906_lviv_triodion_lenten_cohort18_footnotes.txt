The opening strophe of leaf p171 completes the first ode of St. Theophanes's Compline canon with a Theotokion celebrating the prophets as "God-proclaiming trumpets" (*Трꙋбы̑ бг҃огла́сныѧ*) who foreannounced the Virgin's virginal childbearing of the Incarnate Word in the fullness of time.
[^389]: Ode 3 of the Compline Canon to the Holy Prophets: Physical leaf p171 (Printed fol. 167). Heirmos in Tone 5: *Водрꙋзи́вый на ничесо́мже* (Greek: *Ὁ πήξας ἐπ' οὐδενός* — "Who didst establish upon nothing the earth by Thy command"). The hymnody portrays the prophets as spiritual lyres (*цѣвни̑цы дх҃ѡ́вныѧ*) struck by the plectrum of the Holy Spirit, and as mystical chalices (*мы́сленныѧ ча̑шы*) pouring forth the knowledge of the divine dispensation unto all the ends of the earth.
[^390]: Ode 4 of the Compline Canon: Physical leaf p171 (Printed fol. 167). Heirmos in Tone 5: *Бж҃е́ственное твоѐ разꙋмѣ́въ истоща́нїе* (Greek: *Τὴν θείαν σου ἐννοήσας κένωσιν* — "Perceiving Thy divine kenosis, O Habakkuk"). The troparia celebrate the prophets as unerring torches of truth who foresaw the calling of the Gentiles (*ꙗ҆зы́кѡвъ приведенїе*), the rejection of ancient Israel, and the manifestation of God from the Virgin, depicted as the overshadowing Mountain from Habakkuk 3:3.
[^391]: Ode 5 of the Compline Canon: Physical leaf p171 (Printed fol. 167). Heirmos in Tone 5: *Ѡ҆дѣѧ́йся свѣ́томъ ꙗ҆́кѡ ри́зою* (Greek: *Ὁ ἀναβαλλόμενος φῶς ὡς ἱμάτιον* — "Thou that clothest Thyself with light as with a garment"). The ode invokes the morning vigil of Isaiah (Isa. 26:9), who beheld the Lord sitting upon the high and lifted up throne surrounded by Seraphim (Isa. 6:1–3), prefiguring the incarnation of the merciful God.
[^392]: Ode 6 of the Compline Canon: Physical leaf p172 (Printed fol. 168). Heirmos in Tone 5: *Неи́стовствꙋющесѧ бꙋ́рею* (Greek: *Μαινομένην κλύδωνι* — "Raging with the tempest and soul-destroying surge, O Christ"). The troparia celebrate the Old Testament prophets who by divine power restrained the tyranny of kings, stopped the mouths of lions (Daniel), and were delivered unhurt from the belly of the sea-monster (Jonah).
[^393]: Sessional Hymn at Compline: Physical leaf p172 (Printed fol. 168). Tone 2 Automelon (*Ли́къ пррⷪ́ческїй съ Мѡѷсе́омъ и҆ А҆арѡ́номъ* / *Ὁ τῶν προφητῶν χορός*). The hymn unites Moses and Aaron with the prophetic company, rejoicing that the prophetic shadows have reached their consummation in the radiance of the Life-Giving Cross of Christ.
[^394]: Ode 7 of the Compline Canon: Physical leaf p172 (Printed fol. 168). Heirmos in Tone 5: *Превозноси́мый ѻ҆тце́въ Гдⷭ҇ь* (Greek: *Ὁ ὑπερυψούμενος τῶν πατέρων Κύριος* — "The supremely exalted Lord of our fathers quenched the flame"). The troparion recalls Daniel in the lions' den, while the Theotokion extols the Mother of God as having surpassed the laws of nature by giving birth unto the Lawgiver Himself.
[^395]: Ode 8 of the Compline Canon: Physical leaf p172 (Printed fol. 168). Heirmos in Tone 5: *Тебѣ̀ всемꙋдѣ́телю* (Greek: *Σοὶ τῷ παντουργῷ* — "Unto Thee, the Creator of all, the children in the furnace sang"). The hymnody depicts the prophets rejoicing in the bliss of Paradise (*внꙋ́трь сла́дости ра́йскїѧ*), serving as fragrant spiritual censers that instruct all nations to praise Christ unto the ages.
[^396]: Ode 9 of the Compline Canon: Physical leaf p172 (Printed fol. 168). Heirmos in Tone 5: *И҆са́їе ликꙋ́й* (Greek: *Ἡσαΐα χόρευε* — "Rejoice, O Isaiah, the Virgin has conceived"). The opening troparion exhorts the faithful to form a single chorus of praise for the prophetic lineage of Jacob, who performed supernatural wonders and foretold the Advent of Christ.
[^397]: Stichera to the Holy Prophets at Compline: Physical leaf p173 (Printed fol. 169). Chanted at the end of Compline in Tone 1, to the automelon *Прехва́льнїи мꙋ́ченицы* (*Πανεύφημοι μάρτυρες* — "O all-praised martyrs, whom the earth did not conceal"). The three stanzas draw upon Hebrews 11:37–38, commemorating how the prophets were sawn asunder (*претре́ни*, alluding to the martyrdom of Isaiah), stoned (Jeremiah), and slain with the sword, wandering in deserts and caves. The leaf concludes with an ornamental woodcut tailpiece vignette depicting King David kneeling with his harp.
[^398]: Monday of the Second Week of the Great Fast at Matins (*Въ понедѣ́льникъ в҃-ѧ седми́цы ст҃а́гѡ вели́кагѡ поста̀ на ꙋ҆́трени*): Physical leaf p174 (Printed fol. 170). Display headpiece featuring an ornamental woodcut band depicting Christ the Pantocrator in an architectural frame flanked by foliate vine tendrils. Matins resumes the full weekday Lenten cursus: the Psalter kathismata are read with prostrations, followed by the compunctionate sessional hymns (*сѣда́льны ꙋ҆мили́тельныѧ*) of the Octoechos in the Tone of the week.
[^399]: Sessional Hymns after the Second and Third Kathismata: Physical leaf p174 (Printed fol. 170). After the second kathisma, the Sessional Hymn in Tone 4 (*Престꙋпле́нїѧ плодо́мъ ꙋ҆мерщвле́на дꙋше́ю...*, Model: *Joseph marveled* / *Оу҆диви́сѧ І҆ѡ́сифъ*) laments the spiritual death caused by the fruit of transgression in Eden, praying for vivification through fasting and repentance. After the third kathisma, the Sessional Hymn in Tone 6 (*Гдⷭ҇и напра́ви ны̀ по́стъ повторя́ющыѧ...*, Model: *Lord, have mercy on us*) asks divine guidance for the faithful who are renewing their fast in the second week, concluding with the Annunciation-themed Theotokion of Gabriel's greeting.
[^400]: Canons at Matins of the Second Monday: Physical leaf p175 (Printed fol. 171). The rubrics appoint the canon from the Menaion for the saint of the day, followed by the three-canto Triodion (*трїпѣ́снецъ*) comprising Odes 1, 8, and 9. The first canon is authored by St. Joseph the Hymnographer in Tone 4, and the second by St. Theodore the Studite in Tone 2.
[^401]: Ode 1 of the Triodion Canon by St. Joseph: Physical leaf p175 (Printed fol. 171). Tone 4, Heirmos: *Трїста́ты крѣ́пкїѧ* (Greek: *Τριστάτας κραταιούς* — "The mighty captains of Pharaoh"). St. Joseph defines the true, holistic nature of Christian fasting: not mere abstinence from foods, but "departure from sin, estrangement from passions, love toward God, diligence in prayer, tears with compunction, and care for the poor."
[^402]: Ode 1 of the Triodion Canon by St. Theodore the Studite: Physical leaf p175 (Printed fol. 171). Tone 2, Heirmos: *Помо́щникъ и҆ покрови́тель* (Greek: *Βοηθὸς καὶ σκεπαστής* — "He was my helper and protector unto salvation", from the Song of Moses, Ex. 15:2). St. Theodore greets the second week of Lent as a spiritual, non-carnal feast (*непра́здничнꙋю ра́дость* / ἀνέορτον χαρὰν), urging the brethren to enter upon it with compunction and fervent sighing before the approaching judgment of Christ.
[^403]: Ode 8 of the Triodion Canon by St. Joseph: Physical leaf p175 (Printed fol. 171). Tone 4, Heirmos: *И҆зба́вителю всѣ́хъ всеси́льне* (Greek: *Λυτρωτὰ τοῦ παντὸς παντοδύναμε* — "O Redeemer of all, Almighty One"). St. Joseph uses the parable of the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:30) and Peter sinking beneath the waves (Matt. 14:30) as archetypes of the soul's desperate need for Christ the divine Physician.
[^404]: Ode 8 of the Triodion Canon by St. Theodore the Studite: Physical leaf p176 (Printed fol. 172). Tone 2, Heirmos: *Е҆го́же вѡ́инства нбⷭ҇наѧ сла́вѧтъ* (Greek: *Ὃν στρατιαὶ οὐρανῶν δοξάζουσι* — "Whom the heavenly hosts glorify"). St. Theodore warns the soul against the intemperance of Esau, who sold his birthright for a single bowl of pottage (Gen. 25:29–34), and contrasts water-drinking with the drunkenness of Noah (Gen. 9:20–23) and Lot (Gen. 19:30–38). In the second troparion, the printed Slavonic text reads *ѡ҆богатѝ Велїа́ра воздержа́нїемъ* ("enrich Belial through abstinence"), an evident typographic or recensional corruption for *ѡ҆бнища́й* / *ѡ҆бокрадѝ* (Greek: πτώχευσον Βελίαρ — "impoverish Belial").
[^405]: Ode 9 of the Triodion Canon by St. Joseph: Physical leaf p176 (Printed fol. 172). Tone 4, Heirmos: *Е҆́ѵа ꙋ҆́бѡ недꙋ́гомъ преслꙋша́нїѧ* (Greek: *Εὔα μὲν τῷ τῆς παρακοῆς νοσήματι* — "Eve, indeed, through the disease of disobedience brought in the curse"). St. Joseph exhorts the faithful to extinguish the wrath of the coming judgment through almsgiving, tears of repentance, and the renewal of their baptismal robe.
[^406]: Ode 9 of the Triodion Canon by St. Theodore the Studite: Physical leaf p176 (Printed fol. 172). Tone 2, Heirmos: *Безсѣ́меннагѡ Зача́тїѧ Ржⷭ҇тво̀ неска́занное* (Greek: *Ἀσπόρου συλλήψεως ὁ τόκος ἀνερμήνευτος* — "The ineffable Childbirth of a seedless conception"). St. Theodore compares the ascetic fasters to soldiers of Christ (*ꙗ҆́кѡ во́ини Хрⷭ҇то́вы*) contending in the spiritual arena to receive incorruptible crowns from Christ at His glorious second coming.
[^407]: Aposticha at Matins of the Second Monday: Physical leaf p177 (Printed fol. 173). Idiomelon sticheron in Tone 5 (*Ди́вное ѻ҆рꙋ́жїе моли́тва и҆ по́стъ...*). The hymn celebrates prayer and fasting as an invincible spiritual weapon (*ди́вное ѻ҆рꙋ́жїе*), citing the biblical exemplars Moses, who received the Law on Sinai after fasting forty days (Ex. 34:28), and Elijah, who offered acceptable sacrifice on Mount Carmel (1 Kings 18:36–38).
[^408]: Sixth Hour Troparion of the Prophecy: Physical leaf p177 (Printed fol. 173). Tone 5 (*Созда́вый наеди́нѣ сердца̀ на́ша...*), based on Psalm 32:15 (LXX: *ὁ πλάσας κατὰ μόνας τὰς καρδίας αὐτῶν* — "He that fashioned their hearts individually"). The verse is taken from the opening of the great Passion psalm: *O God, my God, attend unto me; why hast Thou forsaken me?* (Ps. 21:1, LXX).
[^410]: Sixth Hour Old Testament Reading from Isaiah: Physical leaf p178 (Printed fol. 174). Isaiah 4:2–5:7 (section 4:2–6 and 5:1–7). This classic Lenten reading combines the Messianic prophecy of the Branch of the Lord who will cleanse the daughters of Zion with the Spirit of judgment and burning, followed by the famous "Song of the Vineyard" (Isa. 5:1–7). God planted a fertile vineyard with choice vines and a watchtower, but it brought forth wild grapes and thorns; therefore the hedge will be removed and the clouds commanded not to rain upon it, prefiguring the judgment upon unrepentant Israel.

St. Theodore thanks God for granting the faithful to complete the cycle of the first week and cross the threshold into the second week's ascetic arena, praying that all may manfully run the course and attain the glorious Day of the Lord's Resurrection.
[^415]: Vespers First Prokeimenon: Physical leaf p179 (Printed fol. 175). Tone 6, Psalm 23:8 (LXX: *Гдⷭ҇ь крѣ́покъ и҆ си́ленъ, Гдⷭ҇ь си́ленъ въ бра́ни* / *Κύριος κραταιὸς καὶ δυνατός, Κύριος δυνατὸς ἐν πολέμῳ*). The verse is Psalm 23:1 (LXX: *The earth is the Lord's, and the fullness thereof*).
[^416]: Vespers First Old Testament Reading from Genesis: Physical leaf p179 (Printed fol. 175). Genesis 3:21–4:7. The narrative recounts God clothing Adam and Eve with garments of skins (*ри̑зы кѡ́жаны*), their expulsion from Paradise, and the placement of the Cherubim with the turning flaming sword to guard the tree of life. It then describes the birth of Cain and Abel, their sacrifices, God's acceptance of Abel's firstlings and rejection of Cain's fruit of the ground, and God's warning to Cain concerning the lurking snare of sin.

King Solomon teaches the path of divine wisdom, warning against the proud and calling the youth to hold fast to parental instruction as the foundation of life and glory.
[^419]: Aposticha at Monday Evening Vespers: Physical leaf p180 (Printed fol. 176). Idiomelon sticheron of the day in Tone 3 (*Дꙋхо́внымъ посто́мъ пости́мсѧ...*), sung twice. The hymn sets forth the gospel law of mutual forgiveness (Matt. 6:14–15) as the indispensable prerequisite for authentic fasting: by forgiving our brethren their debts, our prayer ascends before God as fragrant evening incense (Ps. 140:2).
[^420]: Typikon Rubric on the Displacement of Menaion Canons at Compline: Physical leaf p180 (Printed fol. 176). The rubric (*Зрѝ: Вѣ́домо же да є҆́сть...*) codifies the ancient Studite-Sabbaitic rule for resolving calendar collisions during Great Lent. Beginning on the second Monday of the Great Fast, the canons of saints commemorated in the Menaion during the liturgical window between Lazarus Saturday and Thomas Sunday (when the festal services of Holy Week and Bright Week completely displace the Menaion) are transferred and chanted in advance at weekday Compline throughout Lent. The order prescribes first the Theotokion canon, followed by the saint's canon from the Menaion, with the saint's Kontakion and Sessional Hymn after Ode 3, the Theotokion Sessional Hymn after Ode 6, and the Menaion stichera at the conclusion of the canon.
