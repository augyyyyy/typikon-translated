[^1169]: Sessional hymn following the third stichology of the Psalter on Monday of Palm Week. The hymn marks the formal liturgical entry into the Sixth Week of the Great Fast (Седми́ца ва́їй / Ἑβдоμὰς τῶν βαΐων), interweaving the ascetic completion of the Forty Days with the approaching resurrection of Lazarus and the triumphal entry of the Lord into Jerusalem.
[^1170]: The two Matins canons (triodia) of Monday of Palm Week: the first triodion (Odes 1, 8, and 9) in Tone 1 is by St. Joseph the Hymnographer, emphasizing personal compunction, repentance from spiritual sloth, and liberation from the fate of the unmerciful rich man. The second triodion in Tone 1 is by St. Theodore the Studite, dramatizing the impending journey of Christ from beyond Jordan toward Bethany and Bethphage to raise Lazarus.
[^1171]: Ode 8 of the Studite triodion addresses Bethany directly as the honored home of Lazarus, Martha, and Mary, portraying the village as the holy threshold of the Lord's victory over hades and death.
[^1172]: The Aposticha Idiomelon of Monday Matins in Tone 5 takes up the Gospel archetype of the Rich Man and Poor Lazarus (Luke 16:19–31), invoking the imagery of the beggar cast before the gate to plead for drops of divine mercy before the dread tribunal.
[^1173]: The Sixth Hour of Monday of Palm Week appoints the Troparion of the Prophecy in Tone 6 ("This is, O God, the fearful day..."), accompanied by two prokeimena from the Psalter (Psalms 104 and 105 LXX) and the prophetic reading from Isaiah 48:17–49:4, which speaks of the redemption of Jacob and introduces the Servant Song of the Messiah called from the maternal womb.
[^1174]: Monday of Palm Week at Vespers: the stichera at "Lord, I have cried" combine St. Joseph's hymn of penitential healing on the bed of slothfulness with St. Theodore's contemplation of Christ walking beyond the Jordan and announcing that the sickness of Lazarus is not unto death, but for the glory of God (John 11:4).
[^1175]: The Old Testament reading at Vespers from Genesis 27:1–41 recounts the patriarchal narrative of Isaac blessing Jacob with the dew of heaven and the fatness of the earth, prefiguring the spiritual inheritance granted to the Church from among the Gentiles.
[^1176]: The second reading at Vespers from Proverbs 19:16–25 provides moral exhortations on keeping the divine commandments, showing mercy to the poor as lending unto the Lord, and heeding parental instruction.
[^1177]: Epigraphic defect on physical leaf p427 (printed folio 423) in the concluding Aposticha Theotokion at Monday Vespers. Due to an inking void / printing defect in the Stauropegion edition, words between the left and right fragments are lost: '...го̀ Бг҃а, ро́ждшаѧ всѣ́хъ Творца̀, да всѝ' and 'зове́... хъ на́шихъ.' Restored according to the standard Byzantine-Slavonic Triodion witness: '[И҆зба́ви на́съ ѿ нꙋ́ждъ на́шихъ, Мт҃и Хрⷭ҇та̀] го̀ Бг҃а, ро́ждшаѧ всѣ́хъ Творца̀, да всѝ зове́[мъ тѝ: ра́дꙋйсѧ є҆ди́но предста́тельство дꙋ́ш]хъ на́шихъ.'
[^1178]: Tuesday of Palm Week at Matins: the service continues the prefestal contemplation of Lazarus's sickness and death, appointing penitential sessional hymns from the Octoechos and proper sessional hymns by St. Joseph and St. Theodore.
[^1179]: Appointment of Ode 2 on Tuesday of Palm Week. Outside of the Great Fast, the second biblical ode (the Song of Moses in Deuteronomy 32) is omitted from the daily Matins canon. In the Triodion, Tuesdays retain Ode 2, here paired with triodia by St. Joseph (Tone 6) and St. Theodore (Tone 5).
[^1180]: Ode 9 of Tuesday Matins portrays Lazarus on the brink of physical death ('being about to die tomorrow') and Bethany preparing his tomb, setting the dramatic timeline leading up to Lazarus Saturday.
