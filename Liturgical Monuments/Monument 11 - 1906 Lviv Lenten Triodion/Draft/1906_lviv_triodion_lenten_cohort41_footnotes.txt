[^1149]: Synaxarion of the Saturday of the Akathist (Praise of the Most Holy Theotokos), commemorating the miraculous divine deliverance of Constantinople from the joint siege of the Persians (under King Khosrow II and his general Shahrbaraz / Sarbaros) and the Avars/Scythians (under the Chagan / Khagan) in 626 AD during the reign of Emperor Heraclius (610–641). While the emperor was campaigning in Persia, Patriarch Sergius I led liturgical processions along the city walls bearing the Icon of Christ Not-Made-by-Hands, the relic of the True Cross, and the Robe (Maphorion) of the Virgin Mary, and Patrician Bonus directed the military defense. A miraculous storm destroyed the enemy fleet at Blachernae in the Golden Horn, prompting the entire populace to maintain an all-night thanksgiving vigil standing upright, giving origin to the title "Akathist" (unseated hymn).
[^1150]: Continuation of the Akathist Synaxarion, documenting subsequent historical deliverances of the Imperial City attributed to the Mother of God: the first Arab siege under Emperor Constantine IV Pogonatus (674–678 AD) with the destruction of the Saracen fleet at Syllaeum, and the second Arab siege under Emperor Leo III the Isaurian (717–718 AD) through the protection of the Holy Cross and the Hodigitria icon, resulting in the destruction of the Muslim fleet by storms and famine. The rubric notes that whereas during other readings the congregation sits, during this thanksgiving office all stand upright.
