The rubrics appoint the reading of the Psalter kathismata followed by the compunctionate sessional hymns of the Octoechos in the Tone of the week (Tone 8) with their Theotokia.
[^422]: Sessional Hymns after the Second Psalter Reading: Physical leaf p181 (Printed fol. 177). Tone 8, Podoben: "Thou hast risen" (*Воскре́слъ є҆сѝ* / *Ἀνέστης ἐκ νεκρῶν*). The troparion calls the faithful to restrain bodily passions through the blameless bridle of fasting in order to elevate the noetic faculties toward divine contemplation with unshakeable faith. The accompanying Theotokion laments the depth of despondency caused by sinful deeds, invoking the Mother of God as the sole propitiation and saving purification of sinners.
[^423]: Sessional Hymn after the Third Psalter Reading: Physical leaf p181 (Printed fol. 177). Tone 8, Podoben: "The shepherd's pipe" (*Па́стырскаѧ свирѣ́ль* / *Ὁ ποιμενικὸς αὐλός*). The hymn describes Great Lent as a mystical banquet table spread by God, inviting the faithful to partake richly of the vivifying gifts of the Holy Spirit as food and divine tears as spiritual drink.
[^424]: Ode 2 of the Triodion Canon by St. Joseph the Hymnographer: Physical leaf p181 (Printed fol. 177). Tone 8, Heirmos: *Ви́дите ви́дите, ꙗ҆́кw а҆́зъ є҆́смь бг҃ъ ва́шъ* (Greek: *Ἴδετε ἴδετε, ὅτι ἐγώ εἰμι ὁ Θεὸς ὑμῶν* — "See, see that I am your God, born of the Father before the ages, and in these latter times conceived of the Virgin without seed of man, Who destroyed the sin of our forefather Adam, as the Lover of mankind", based on Deut. 32:39). St. Joseph urges the soul to hasten in repentance because the Judge stands at the door and the day of reckoning is at hand.
[^425]: Ode 2 of the Second Triodion Canon by St. Theodore the Studite: Physical leaf p182 (Printed fol. 178). Tone 8, Heirmos: *Ви́дите ви́дите* (*Ἴδετε ἴδετε*). St. Theodore reminds the ascetic of the imminent tribunal of Christ and contrasts the salvation of the repentant Ninevites through fasting with the fiery destruction of the self-indulgent Sodomites. The canto concludes with St. Theodore's own full text of the biblical heirmos (Deut. 32:39), which explicitly characterizes Lent as a spiritual tithing (*ѡ҆десѧтствова́нїе*) of the whole year given unto God's people for the eradication of the passions.
[^426]: Ode 8 of the Triodion Canon by St. Joseph: Physical leaf p182 (Printed fol. 178). Tone 8, Heirmos: *Бг҃оглаго́ливіи ѻ҆́троцы* (Greek: *Οἱ θεορρήμονες Παῖδες* — "The God-proclaiming children in the furnace trampled down the flame"). The troparia set forth the Publican and the Harlot as models of compunction, summoning all nine orders of angels, apostles, and martyrs to intercede for the forgiveness of sins.
[^427]: Ode 8 of the Triodion Canon by St. Theodore the Studite: Physical leaf p183 (Printed fol. 179). Tone 8, Heirmos: *На горѣ̀ ст҃ѣ́й просла́вльшасѧ* (Greek: *Τὸν ἐν ὄρει ἁγίῳ δοξασθέντα* — "Him Who was glorified on the holy mountain, and in the bush through fire revealed the mystery of the Ever-Virgin unto Moses"). St. Theodore draws a stark biblical warning from 1 Kings 13:11–24, where the unnamed man of God from Judah was devoured by a lion after being deceived into breaking his fast by the old prophet of Bethel, warning the soul against the venomous serpent of gluttony (*чревобѣ́сїѧ ѕмі́й*).
[^428]: Ode 9 of the Triodion Canons by St. Joseph and St. Theodore: Physical leaf p183 (Printed fol. 179). Tone 8, Heirmos: *Проꙗвле́нное на горѣ̀* (Greek: *Τὴν προτυπωθεῖσαν ἐν ὄρει* — "The Childbirth of the Ever-Virgin, prefigured on the mountain unto the Lawgiver in the fire and the bush"). St. Joseph describes fasting as the spiritual cultivator of the soul, yielding the ripe wheat-ear of virtues. St. Theodore extols the biblical ascetics Samuel and Samson, declaring that fasting nurtures and crowns true priests and prophets.
[^429]: Aposticha at Tuesday Matins: Physical leaf p184 (Printed fol. 180). Idiomelon of the day in Tone 5 (*Вскꙋ́ю оу҆ныва́еши дꙋшѐ моѧ̀...*), sung twice. The hymn urges the sluggard soul not to despair in sickness but to run swiftly unto Christ the divine Physician, washing the countenance with tears of repentance and trimming the spiritual lamp with the oil of good deeds (alluding to the Parable of the Ten Virgins, Matt. 25:1–13).

The troparion introduces the Sixth Hour Scripture reading, beseeching the Lord Who is easily propitiated (*бл҃гопремѣни́тель*) not to destroy us along with our manifold transgressions.

[^432]: Sixth Hour Old Testament Reading from Isaiah: Physical leaf p185 (Printed fol. 181). Isaiah 5:7–16. The prophet pronounces God's judgment upon the house of Israel and Judah, the "new and beloved vineyard" that produced only wild thorns and bloodshed instead of justice. Isaiah proclaims woes against greedy land-grabbers who join house to house and field to field, and against drunken carousers who spend their days in music and strong drink while ignoring the works of the Lord, concluding with the expansion of Hades to swallow the proud and unrighteous.

Tone 3, Psalm 26:1a (LXX: *Гдⷭ҇ь просвѣще́нїе моѐ и҆ Сп҃си́тель мо́й, когѡ̀ ꙋ҆бою́сѧ;* / *Κύριος φωτισμός μου καὶ σωτήρ μου, τίνα φοβηθήσομαι;*). [^434]: Tuesday of the Second Week of the Great Fast at Vespers (*Во вто́рникъ в҃-ѧ седми́цы ст҃а́гw вели́кагw поста̀ ве́чера*): Physical leaf p185 (Printed fol. 181). Preceded by a decorative ornamental frieze. Vespers appoints the evening Kathisma 18 from the Psalter (*Songs of Ascents*), followed at "Lord, I have cried" by 6 stichera: 3 from the Triodion (by St. Joseph and St. Theodore) and 3 from the Menaion.
[^435]: Stichera at "Lord, I have cried" by St. Joseph the Hymnographer: Physical leaf p185 (Printed fol. 181). Tone 1, Podoben: "O all-lauded martyrs" (*Прехва́льнїи мꙋ́ченицы* / *Πανεύφημοι μάρτυρες*). St. Joseph contemplates Christ stretching forth His hands upon the Cross to gather the dispersed nations, praying that the Savior will gather the sinner's scattered mind and make him a partaker of the divine Passion through fasting, citing the Three Holy Children who proved stronger than Nebuchadnezzar's furnace (Dan. 3).
[^436]: Sticheron at "Lord, I have cried" by St. Theodore the Studite: Physical leaf p185 (Printed fol. 181). Tone 3, Podoben: "O valiant martyrs" (*До́блїи мꙋ́ченицы* / *Γενναῖοι μάρτυρες*). St. Theodore extols the joyous nature of the fast as a season enriched with purity, love, and radiant prayer, bidding the faithful to venerate the Precious Cross of Christ. The sticheron crosses the folio break and concludes on leaf p186.
[^437]: Vespers First Prokeimenon: Physical leaf p186 (Printed fol. 182). Tone 6, Psalm 27:9 (LXX: *Спасѝ Гдⷭ҇и лю́ди твоѧ̑, и҆ бл҃гословѝ достоѧ́нїе твоѐ* / *Σῶσον τὸν λαόν σου καὶ εὐλόγησον τὴν κληρονομίαν σου*). The verse is Psalm 27:1 (LXX: *Unto Thee, O Lord, will I cry, O my God*).
[^438]: Vespers First Old Testament Reading from Genesis: Physical leaf p186 (Printed fol. 182). Genesis 4:8–15. The historical narrative of the first murder: Cain lures his brother Abel into the open field and kills him. When interrogated by God, Cain utters his defiant response: "Am I my brother's keeper?" God declares that Abel's blood cries out from the ground, curses Cain to be a fugitive and a vagabond (*стенѧ́й и҆ трѧсы́йсѧ*) upon the earth, and marks him with a protective sign against blood vengeance.

Tone 7, Psalm 28:11 (LXX: *Гдⷭ҇ь крѣ́пость лю́демъ свои̑мъ да́стъ, Гдⷭ҇ь бл҃гослови́тъ лю́ди своѧ̑ ми́ромъ* / *Κύριος ἰσχὺν τῷ λαῷ αὐτοῦ δώσει, Κύριος εὐλογήσει τὸν λαὸν αὐτοῦ ἐν εἰρήνῃ*). [^440]: Vespers Second Old Testament Reading from Proverbs: Physical leaf p186 (Printed fol. 182). Proverbs 5:1–15. [^441]: Aposticha at Tuesday Evening Vespers: Physical leaf p187 (Printed fol. 183). Idiomelon of the day in Tone 3 (*Пло́тїю распны́йсѧ Гдⷭи...*), sung twice. The sticheron establishes the theological link between the Crucifixion of Christ and the Christian's ascetic mortification: Christ's pierced side and death on the Cross slay the primordial serpent, inspiring the believer to nail his flesh to divine fear, restrain all the sensory portals of sin, and offer a contrite heart as acceptable sacrifice.
[^442]: Stavrotheotokion at the Aposticha: Physical leaf p187 (Printed fol. 183). Tone 3, Podoben: "Great is the power of Thy martyrs" (*Ве́лїѧ мꙋ́ченикъ* / *Μεγάλη τῶν Μαρτύρων σου*). The Mother of God stands at the foot of the Cross, weeping aloud as she beholds her beloved Son stripped of His radiant beauty which once adorned human nature.
[^443]: Wednesday of the Second Week of the Great Fast at Matins (*Въ сре́дꙋ в҃-ѧ седми́цы ст҃а́гw вели́кагw поста̀ на оу́трени*): Physical leaf p187 (Printed fol. 183). The Theotokos laments as she witnesses her Lamb hanging on the Cross between two criminals, recalling the prophetic words of Simeon (Luke 2:35).

St. Joseph cites the miracle of the Prophet Elisha raising the deceased Shunammite's son (2 Kings 4:32–37) as a figure of the soul's resurrection from spiritual death through fasting and bodily mortification.
[^451]: Ode 9 of the Second Triodion Canon by St. Theodore the Studite: Physical leaf p190 (Printed fol. 186). Tone 3, Heirmos: *На Сїна́йстѣй горѣ̀* (Greek: *Ἐν Σιναίῳ τῷ ὄρει* — "On Mount Sinai Moses beheld thee in the bush"). St. Theodore depicts the cosmic horror of creation at the Crucifixion: angels trembling, the sun darkened, and the earth shaken as the Creator suffers mockery, blows, and piercing in the flesh to bestow incorruptible life upon mankind.

The sticheron contrasts the primordial death brought into the world through eating of the forbidden tree in Paradise with the eternal life purchased by the Cross of Christ, exhorting the faithful to labor through fasting for the food that endures unto everlasting life (John 6:27).
