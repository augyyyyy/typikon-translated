[^1431]: The First and Second Findings of the Precious Head of the Holy Prophet, Forerunner, and Baptist John (*Честно́е ѡ҆брѣ́тенїе честны́ѧ Главы̀ І҆ѡа́нна Предте́чи*), celebrated on February 24. These Markian chapters govern the complex liturgical intersections that occur when February 24 coincides with the movable cycle of the Pre-Lenten period and Great Lent, ranging from Meatfare Wednesday through the Tuesday of the First Week of the Great Fast, and subsequent Lenten weekdays and Sundays.

[^1432]: Meatfare Saturday is dedicated universally to the general commemoration of all departed Orthodox Christians (the Universal Memorial Saturday of Souls, *Сꙋббѡ́та мѧсопꙋ́стнаѧ ᲂу҆со́пшихъ*). Because the solemn ancestral requiem office cannot be displaced or easily combined with a joyous saintly festival of Polyeleos rank, the feast of the Forerunner is transferred in its entirety to the preceding Friday.

[^1433]: On Monday and Tuesday of Cheesefare Week, although the strict dietary transition has begun (meat is excluded, but dairy and eggs are permitted throughout), the liturgical order remains festive, permitting the celebration of the Polyeleos, Great Doxology, and the readings of the Forerunner alongside the movable Triodion hymnography.

[^1434]: On Wednesday and Friday of Cheesefare Week, the services already assume an aliturgical, penitential character: prostrations are appointed, the Prayer of St. Ephrem is recited, and the Divine Liturgy is not celebrated. Consequently, the festal celebration of the Forerunner cannot be held on these days and is transferred to Tuesday or Thursday respectively.

[^1435]: On Cheesefare Thursday, because the eve of the day (Wednesday afternoon) contains Lenten elements, Vespers is served after the Ninth Hour and the Prayer of St. Ephrem, with readings and Great Prokeimena. At the monastic refectory, a dispensation for cheese and eggs is granted in honor of the feast.

[^1436]: On Cheesefare Saturday, the Church commemorates all the Holy Ascetic Fathers and Mothers who shone forth in the monastic life (*Па́мѧть всѣ́хъ прпⷣбныхъ ѻ҆тє́цъ въ по́двизѣ провозсїя́вшихъ*). When the Finding of the Head of the Forerunner coincides with this day, the hymnography of the Ascetic Fathers is harmonized with that of the Forerunner.

[^1437]: Pure Monday (*Чи́стый понедѣ́льникъ*), the opening day of Clean Week and of the Great Fast, is kept with the strictest fasting and mournful penitence; no festal celebration is permitted. The entire service of the Forerunner is therefore transferred backward to Cheesefare Sunday.

[^1438]: When the feast falls on any weekday of Clean Week (Tuesday through Friday), the penitential services of the Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete cannot be displaced; the festal service of the Forerunner is transferred either backward to Cheesefare Sunday or forward to the First Saturday of Lent (commemoration of the Miracle of the Kolyva of the Great Martyr Theodore the Tyro).

[^1439]: The Provincial Synod of Lviv (*Synodus Provincialis Ruthenorum Galiciae*), convened under Metropolitan Sylvester Cardinal Sembratovych in 1891 (Slavonic year `҂аѡч҃а` = 1891 AD), which codified and standardized liturgical practices across the Greek Catholic Metropolitanate of Kyiv-Halych and the Archeparchy of Lviv, resolving local rubrical adaptations, here sanctioning Small Compline instead of Great Compline.

[^1440]: The First Sunday of Great Lent (Sunday of the Triumph of Orthodoxy, *Недѣ́лѧ правосла́вїѧ*). The service of the Forerunner is combined with that of the Triumph of Orthodoxy following the rubrics prescribed for the Third Sunday of Lent (Veneration of the Cross), omitting only the ceremony of the veneration of the Cross.

[^1441]: When the Finding of the Head of the Forerunner falls on Monday of the Second, Third, or Fourth Weeks of Great Lent, Great Vespers with the Great Prokeimenon is served on Sunday evening, followed by Matins with Polyeleos and the celebration of the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts on Monday.

[^1442]: An exceptional rubrical contingency: when, due to grave necessity or the absence of a priest authorized to serve the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts on a major feast with Polyeleos, the Presanctified Liturgy cannot be celebrated, the Typika (with Typika Psalms, Beatitudes, and festal Epistle and Gospel) are chanted in place of the Liturgy.

[^1443]: The *paraecclesiarch* (*параекклисїа́рхъ* / church warden / candle-lighter) tolls two bells (*зна́менаетъ къ два̀ кампа́на*) to signal the monastic assembly's transition from the Typika to Great Lenten Vespers.

[^1444]: St. Tarasios, Patriarch of Constantinople (commemorated February 25), defender of the holy icons at the Seventh Ecumenical Council (Nicaea II, 787 AD), whose commemoration immediately follows the Finding of the Head of the Forerunner (February 24).

[^1445]: When the Finding of the Head of the Forerunner occurs on an ordinary fasting weekday of the Second, Third, or Fourth Weeks of Lent, Vespers is united with the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts in the afternoon, or served standalone without the Liturgy if none is celebrated.

[^1446]: On the Second or Third Saturday of Great Lent (traditionally appointed as ancestral memorial Saturdays of Souls), the celebration of the Finding of the Head of the Forerunner displaces the requiem service and is combined with the hymns of the Octoechos and the Triodion.

[^1447]: The Second Sunday of Great Lent (commemoration of St. Gregory Palamas, Archbishop of Thessalonica). The Sunday Resurrectional service is combined with the hymns of the Triodion and the Forerunner, with Polyeleos, Magnification, and the Great Doxology.

[^1448]: The Third Sunday of Great Lent (Sunday of the Veneration of the Precious and Life-Giving Cross, *Недѣ́лѧ крⷭ҇топокло́ннаѧ*). The service harmonizes the Resurrectional hymns, the veneration and hymns of the Precious Cross, and the feast of the Forerunner.

[^1449]: The Holy Forty Martyrs of Sebaste (*Святи́и четыре́десѧть му́чєницы въ Севасті́йстѣмъ є҆́зерѣ му́чєнически сконча́вшїисѧ*), commemorated on March 9. When March 9 falls on the First, Second, Third, or Fourth Saturdays of Great Lent, the order of services follows precisely the rubrics established for the Finding of the Head of the Forerunner.

[^1450]: On Wednesday of the Fourth Week of Lent (Mid-Lent / Wednesday of the Cross), the commemoration of the Forty Martyrs is transferred backward to Tuesday, because Wednesday is dedicated pre-eminently to the Veneration of the Precious Cross.

[^1451]: On Thursday of the Great Canon (Fifth Week), the Forty Martyrs are transferred backward to Tuesday of that week because the entire Thursday office is occupied by the Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete and the Life of St. Mary of Egypt.

[^1452]: On the Saturday of the Akathist (Fifth Saturday of Great Lent), the Forty Martyrs are transferred to the Fifth Sunday of the Fast, so that the festive Akathist Hymn to the Mother of God may be sung without curtailment.

[^1453]: The Forefeast of the Annunciation of the Most Holy Theotokos (March 24) coinciding with weekdays of Great Lent (Monday through Friday), combining the forefestal hymnography with the Lenten Triodion at Vespers and the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts.

[^1454]: Forefeast of the Annunciation coinciding with the Third or Fourth Saturday of Great Lent. A celebratory chime of two small bells (*трезво́нъ въ дво́и ма́лыѧ колокола̀*) is sounded, and the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts is celebrated on Friday evening.

[^1455]: Forefeast of the Annunciation on the Third Sunday of Lent (Veneration of the Cross). The hymnography of the Precious Cross is joined with the Resurrection and Forefeast, taking precedence over the daily Triodion commemorations.

[^1456]: Forefeast of the Annunciation on Mid-Lent Wednesday (Cross-Veneration Wednesday). Prostrations are made at all services, the Cross is venerated at the First Hour, and the Triodion cross hymnography takes prominence alongside the forefestal stichera and canon.

[^1457]: Forefeast of the Annunciation on Thursday of the Fourth Week of Great Lent. The Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts is celebrated on Wednesday evening, followed by the combined forefestal and Triodion office on Thursday morning.

[^1458]: Forefeast of the Annunciation on the Fourth or Fifth Sunday of Great Lent. The customary secondary commemorations of St. John Climacus (Fourth Sunday) or St. Mary of Egypt (Fifth Sunday) are postponed or transferred to another day at the discretion of the ecclesiarch.

[^1459]: Forefeast of the Annunciation coinciding with the Fifth Saturday of Great Lent (Saturday of the Akathist), combining the Akathist hymnography with that of the forefeast.

[^1460]: Forefeast of the Annunciation on Lazarus Saturday. The forefestal service is transferred to Great and Holy Thursday because Lazarus Saturday is dedicated exclusively to the Raising of Lazarus by the Lord.

[^1461]: The comprehensive Markian Chapters governing the Feast of the Annunciation of the Most Holy Theotokos (March 25) coinciding with the movable cycles of Great Lent, Lazarus Saturday, Palm Sunday, Passion Week, Holy Pascha, and Bright Week. In the Ruthenian Typikon, the movable date of the Annunciation can range from Thursday of the Third Week of Lent to Wednesday of Bright Week.

[^1462]: The general order of Old Testament readings for the Annunciation when it falls on a Saturday or Sunday of Lent: five festal Old Testament readings are appointed (three at Vespers and two at the Liturgy, or all five at Great Vespers on the eve of Saturday/Sunday).

[^1463]: An ancient monastic procession (*лїті́а*) outside the monastery walls with crosses and banners, conducted at the Third Hour of the day on the Annunciation.

[^1464]: St. John Chrysostom's renowned festive homily for the Annunciation of the Theotokos, beginning *"Again the joy of the Annunciation..."* (*Πάλιν χαρᾶς εὐαγγέλια* / *Па́ки ра́дость Бл҃говѣ́щенїа*).

[^1465]: Psalm 103:4 (LXX) [Psalm 104:4 Masoretic]: "Who maketh His angels spirits, and His ministers a flame of fire"; and Hebrews 1:7.

[^1466]: Hebrews 2:11–18 (Pericope 306): "For both He that sanctifieth and they who are sanctified are all of one: for which cause He is not ashamed to call them brethren..."

[^1467]: Psalm 71:6, 17 (LXX) [Psalm 72:6, 17 Masoretic]: "He shall come down like rain upon the fleece; and as drops falling upon the earth... His name shall be blessed unto ages."

[^1468]: Luke 1:24–38 (Pericope 3): The Conception of St. Elizabeth and the Annunciation of the Archangel Gabriel to the Most Holy Theotokos.

[^1469]: Psalm 131:13 (LXX) [Psalm 132:13 Masoretic]: "For the Lord hath chosen Zion; He hath desired it for His habitation."

[^1470]: An authoritative canonical and rubrical decree attributed to St. John the Faster (*Ioannes Nesteutes*), Patriarch of Constantinople (582–595 AD), defending the celebration of the full Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom and the dispensation for fish on the Annunciation during Great Lent, and forbidding penitential laments such as *"Let my prayer be set forth"* (*Да испра́вится моли́тва моя̀*) when the full Liturgy is served.

[^1471]: Rubrics for when, due to grave pastoral necessity or lack of a priest, the Divine Liturgy cannot be celebrated on the Annunciation; fish and wine are still permitted at the monastic table once during the day.

[^1472]: The Synaxis of the Archangel Gabriel (*Собо́ръ ст҃а́гѡ а҆рха́ггела Гаврїи́ла*), commemorated on March 26, the day following the Annunciation.

[^1473]: Annunciation coinciding with the Third or Fourth Saturday of Great Lent. On Friday evening, the Presanctified Liturgy is combined with Vespers of the Annunciation, and Great Compline is chanted at the first hour of the night.

[^1474]: Annunciation on the Third Sunday of Great Lent (Sunday of the Veneration of the Precious and Life-Giving Cross). The Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great is celebrated with the Hymn to the Cross (*"Before Thy Cross we bow down, O Master..."*) in place of the Trisagion.

[^1475]: An inviolable rubrical rule of the Byzantine and Ruthenian Typika: whenever the Annunciation falls on any weekday of Great Lent, the Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great is celebrated without exception (*быва́етъ Літургі́а вели́кагѡ Васїлїа непосмѣ́ннѡ*), united with Vespers on fasting days, or in its regular order on Saturday and Sunday.

[^1476]: Annunciation on Monday of the Second, Third, or Sixth (Palm) Weeks of Great Lent.

[^1477]: Annunciation on Mid-Lent Wednesday (Cross-Veneration Wednesday). The stichera, Doxastica, and canon of the Cross are postponed to Monday or Friday of that same week, while the Annunciation with the Triodion Mid-Lent triode is celebrated on Wednesday; veneration of the Cross takes place at the First Hour.

[^1478]: Annunciation on the Fourth or Fifth Sunday of Great Lent.
