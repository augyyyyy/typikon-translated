[^299]: Clean Tuesday Matins: Sessional Hymn (Kathisma) following the third poetic stichology of the Psalter, Tone 2, Model: *O Lord, the cup of torment* (*Господи, чашу муки*), praising the keeping of the second day of the Fast (*двоеденствующе*), asking for peaceful hearts and a well-traversed course of fasting.
[^300]: Matins Canon, Ode 2, Tone 2, Heirmos: "See, see that I am He Who saved in the sea" (Deut. 32:39), appointed with the second biblical canticle of Moses, urging the soul to sobriety, tears, and spiritual ascent unto the mountain of virtues.
[^301]: Matins Canon, Ode 8, Tone 2, Heirmos: "In the bush to Moses" / "Creator of creation", recalling the Three Holy Youths and Daniel delivered through fasting, and urging the soul to cast off sloth and go forth to meet the torch-bearing Bridegroom.
[^302]: Matins Canon, Ode 9, Tone 2, Heirmos: "Who from among the earthborn has heard" / "Rejoice, O Isaiah", calling the faithful to put on the armor of light (Rom. 13:12) and overcome the adversary through fasting, even as the Lord vanquished the tempter in the wilderness.
[^303]: Matins Aposticha self-governing sticheron (*самогласенъ*), Tone 3: "Let us begin, O peoples, the blameless fast", anointing our heads with the oil of almsgiving and washing our faces with the water of purity (Matt. 6:16–18), crying aloud the Lord's Prayer without vain babbling.
[^304]: Sixth Hour of Clean Tuesday: Troparion of prophecy, Tone 1 (*As sojourners are we upon the earth*, 1 Chron. 29:15), Prokeimenon from Psalm 5 (*Hearken unto the voice of my prayer*), and the reading from the Prophet Isaiah (Isa. 1:19–2:3) warning of the sword and fire upon Jerusalem's unrighteous leaders, followed by the prophecy of the mountain of the Lord's house exalted above the hills in the last days.
[^305]: Clean Tuesday Vespers: Stichera on *Lord, I have cried*, Tone 2, meditating upon the bitter eating in Eden and the restoration wrought by Christ's Cross, gall, and vinegar; followed by the second Genesis reading (Gen. 1:14–23) on the fourth and fifth days of creation (the sun, moon, and stars; fish, sea creatures, and birds).
[^306]: Clean Tuesday Vespers: The second reading from Proverbs (Prov. 1:20–33), in which divine Wisdom cries aloud in the public squares, warning fools and mockers of swift calamity while promising security to the obedient; followed by the Aposticha sticheron, Tone 8 (*Let us accomplish not only abstinence from foods, but estrangement from every material passion*).
[^307]: Tuesday of the First Week at Great Compline (*на повече́рїи вели́комъ*): The Great Penitential Canon of St. Andrew of Crete (Part 2), Ode 1, Tone 6, with troparia examining the murderous envy of Cain, the righteousness of Abel, and the fatherly mercy of God receiving the repentant prodigal.
[^308]: Great Canon, Ode 2, Tone 6, Heirmos: "Give ear, O heaven, and I will speak" (Deut. 32:1), meditating upon the coats of skins (Gen. 3:21), the garment of shame woven by the passions, the woman who was a sinner anointing Christ with tears (Luke 7:38), and the Publican's cry for justification.
[^309]: Great Canon, Ode 3, Tone 6, Heirmos: "Establish, O Lord, my wavering heart upon the rock of Thy commandments", with troparia recalling the carnal corruption of those in the days of Noah, the mockery of Ham exposing his father's nakedness, and the soul's imperative flight like Lot from the burning of Sodom and Gomorrah.
[^310]: Great Canon, Ode 4, Tone 6, Heirmos: "The prophet heard of Thy coming, O Lord", reflecting on Jacob's ladder of divine ascent, the twelve patriarchs as steps of virtue, the rejection of Esau (*Edom*) selling his birthright for a single mess of pottage, and the exemplary patience of Job upon the dunghill.
[^311]: Great Canon, Ode 5, Tone 6, Heirmos: "Early out of the night waking early", meditating on Moses preserved in the ark upon the Nile, slaying the Egyptian taskmaster, fleeing into the Midian wilderness, beholding the Burning Bush, and the contrasting profane fire offered by Hophni and Phinehas (1 Sam. 2:12–17).
[^312]: Great Canon, Ode 6, Tone 6, Heirmos: "I cried with my whole heart unto the merciful God", recalling Israel's sinful longing for the fleshpots of Egypt over the heavenly manna, Moses striking the rock as a type of Christ's pierced side, Joshua exploring the Promised Land, and the penitential Kontakion: "My soul, my soul, arise! Why sleepest thou?".
[^313]: Great Canon, Ode 7, Tone 6, Heirmos: "We have sinned, we have transgressed", with troparia warning against the irreverence of Uzzah (*За́нъ* / *Оза*, 2 Sam. 6:6–7) touching the Ark, the unnatural rebellion and incest of Absalom against David (2 Sam. 16:21–22), and the deceitful counsel of Ahithophel confounded by Christ.
