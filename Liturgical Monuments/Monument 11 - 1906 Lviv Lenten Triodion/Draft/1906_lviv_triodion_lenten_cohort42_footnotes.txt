[^1158]: Commemoration of our Venerable Mother Mary of Egypt on the Fifth Sunday of the Great Fast. The Slavonic rubric notes the historical layering of the Byzantine rite: the ancient Sabaite and Constantinopolitan Typika appointed only the Resurrectional services of the Octoechos with the Menaion of the calendar date, while the later Studite and neo-Sabaitic synthesis (from the 11th–12th centuries onwards) definitively transferred the commemoration of St. Mary of Egypt (historically celebrated on April 1) to the Fifth Sunday of Lent, alongside the Great Canon sung on the preceding Thursday.
[^1159]: Slavonic: Ѿ чꙋдесѐ но́вагѡ (Greek: Ὢ καινοῦ θαύματος). Sessional/Theotokion of Tone 2, Dogmatikon of the Octoechos.
[^1160]: Liturgical structure of Matins for the Fifth Sunday of Lent: the three canons appointed are (1) the Resurrectional Canon from the Octoechos of the current Tone (with its Theotokion stanzas) counted for 6 troparia; (2) the Triodion Canon on the Rich Man and Lazarus in Tone 8 (composed by St. Theodore the Studite) for 4 troparia; and (3) the Triodion Canon for St. Mary of Egypt in Tone 6 (also attributed to the Studite tradition) for 4 troparia, totaling 14 troparia per ode.
[^1161]: The Lenten Sunday themes in the Studite Triodion are organized typologically: the Fifth Sunday pairs the Gospel parable of the Rich Man and Poor Lazarus (Luke 16:19–31) with the life of St. Mary of Egypt as the archetypal model of radical repentance and ascetic transformation.
[^1162]: Order of the Sunday Matins conclusion: the Great Doxology is sung, followed by the Dismissal Troparion of the Resurrection, the Great Dismissal, the Doxasticon of the Resurrectional Matins Gospel (the Evangelical Sticheron), and the First Hour.
[^1163]: Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great on the Fifth Sunday of Lent: the Beatitudes from the Octoechos for 8 troparia. Prokeimenon of the Resurrectional Tone, and for St. Mary of Egypt in Tone 4, Psalm 67:36 ("God is wonderful in His saints").
[^1164]: Scriptural readings for the Fifth Sunday of Lent: Epistle of the Sunday, Hebrews 9:11–14 (Pericope 321 from the midpoint); Epistle of the Venerable Woman, Galatians 3:23–29 (Pericope 208); Gospel of the Sunday, Mark 10:32–45 (Pericope 37, Christ's third prophecy of His Passion and the request of the sons of Zebedee); Gospel of the Venerable Woman, Luke 7:36–50 (Pericope 33, the woman who was a sinner anointing the feet of Jesus in the house of Simon the Pharisee).
[^1165]: Refectory rule for Sunday of Lent: two cooked meals with wine and oil are permitted, with abstention from meat, dairy, eggs, and fish.
[^1166]: The opening sticheron of Sunday evening marks the liturgical transition to the Sixth Week of the Great Fast (Palm Week), introducing the prefestal themes of the Entrance of our Lord into Jerusalem and the raising of Lazarus.
[^1167]: Physical leaf p179 = Printed Folio 175 + 4. Cross-reference to the Great Prokeimenon of Sunday evening in Tone 8: "Thou hast given an inheritance to them that fear Thy name" (Psalm 60:6 LXX), first printed with its verses at Vespers on the Fourth Sunday of Lent.
[^1168]: Monday of the Sixth Week of Lent initiates the solemn cycle of Palm Week (Седми́ца ва́їй / Ἑβδομὰς τῶν βαΐων), directing the Church's contemplation toward the raising of the four-days-dead Lazarus in Bethany and the triumphal entry of the Lord into Jerusalem.
