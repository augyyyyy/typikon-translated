[^1108]: Ode 9 troparia of the Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete, Tone 6, continuing the New Testament scriptural witness. St. Andrew passes from Old Testament archetypes to the Gospel dispensations: Christ incarnate calling tax collectors and harlots (Matthew 9:10–13), the worship of the Magi (Matthew 2:1–12), the Massacre of the Innocents as the first martyr-witnesses (Matthew 2:16–18), the righteous Simeon and Anna (Luke 2:25–38), the Lord's forty-day fast and threefold wilderness temptation (Matthew 4:1–11), the preaching and martyrdom of St. John the Baptist under Herod Antipas and Herodias (Matthew 14:1–12), and the marriage feast in Cana of Galilee (John 2:1–11).
[^1109]: Continuation of Ode 9 of the Great Canon, celebrating Christ's healing ministry and the evangelical figures of penitence: the paralytic carrying his bed (Mark 2:1–12; John 5:1–15); the raising of the son of the widow of Nain (Luke 7:11–17); the healing of the centurion's servant (Matthew 8:5–13); the Samaritan woman at the well of Sychar (John 4:5–42); the woman with the issue of blood (Matthew 9:20–22); the healing of lepers, blind, and lame (Luke 7:22); the raising of Jairus' daughter (Mark 5:21–43); the justification of the Publican over the proud Pharisee (Luke 18:9–14); the conversion of Zacchaeus (Luke 19:1–10); the sinful woman anointing Christ's feet in the house of Simon the Pharisee (Luke 7:36–50); the prophetic woes against Chorazin, Bethsaida, and Capernaum (Matthew 11:20–24); the persistent faith of the Syro-Phoenician/Canaanite woman (Matthew 15:21–28); and the confession of the Good Thief on the Cross (Luke 23:39–43).
[^1110]: Conclusion of the Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete, Ode 9. St. Andrew invokes the cosmic sympathy of creation at Christ's Crucifixion (Matthew 27:45–54), offers spiritual brokenness of heart (LXX Ps. 50:19) in place of worthy fruits, and concludes with fervent supplications to St. Mary of Egypt (honoring her miraculous, immaterial crossing of the Jordan River) and to St. Andrew of Crete himself as pastor and guide of repentance, sealed by a Trinitarian triadicon and the imperial city protection theotokion. At the Aposticha, the penitential automelon in Tone 8 ("Having fallen into robber-infested paths" / Въ разбо́йническїѧ пꙋти̑ впа́дши) develops the parable of the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:30–37).

[^1112]: Sixth Hour Paremia: Isaiah 42:5–16, the First Servant Song of Isaiah, prophesying the Messiah as the Covenant of the people and Light to the Gentiles, opening the eyes of the blind and delivering prisoners from the dungeon.

[^1114]: Thursday of the Fifth Week of the Great Fast, at Vespers. Following the extensive vigil of the Great Canon (the Matins of Thursday celebrated on Wednesday evening), this service marks the return to the standard Lenten weekday cycle, leading into the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts.
[^1115]: Codicological and typikon rubric on the Kathisma and sounding of the semantron (klepalo): On account of the immense physical labor of the preceding nocturnal vigil of the Great Canon, Kathisma 12 (Psalms 85–90) replaces the standard 18th Kathisma ("Unto the Lord when I was afflicted" / Пѣ́сни степе́ней), and the lamplighting semantron is struck softly (поко́рꙋ, lit. with subdued or gentle cadence) to spare the exhausted brethren.

[^1118]: Vespers Prokeimenon, Psalm 98 (LXX 98:5, 1), Tone 7: "Exalt the Lord our God, and worship the footstool of His feet, for He is holy" (Ὑψοῦτε Κύριον τὸν Θεὸν ἡμῶν / Возноси́те Гдⷭ҇а Бг҃а на́шего).
[^1119]: Vespers First Paremia: Genesis 18:20–33, recounting Abraham's intercession before the Lord for the righteous of Sodom and Gomorrah, bargaining from fifty righteous down to ten.
[^1120]: Historical monastic fasting rubric (трапеза): Documenting the divergence between the Studite monastic rule (which mandates xerophagy / dry fasting throughout the fifth week on account of its penitential character) and the Athonite Typikon of the Holy Mountain (which permits two cooked dishes seasoned with oil and two cups of wine on the Great Canon vigil, on the feast of the Forty Martyrs of Sebaste, on the Finding of the Head of the Forerunner, on Wednesday of the Third Week of Lent / Mid-Lent, and on the Akathist Saturday), leaving the choice to individual ascetic conscience before God.
[^1121]: Markian rubric (Зрѝ / Nota bene): Typikon resolution governing the rare calendar concurrence of the Feast of the Annunciation of the Most Holy Theotokos (March 25) falling on Thursday of the Fifth Week of Lent. In such an occurrence, the celebration of the Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete is transferred earlier in the week, sung at Matins on Tuesday (served on Monday evening), in order to maintain the uncompromised festal integrity of the Annunciation.
[^1122]: Friday of the Fifth Week of the Great Fast, at Matins. This day serves as the liturgical preparation and threshold for the great celebration of Saturday of the Akathist (the Praise of the Most Holy Theotokos).
