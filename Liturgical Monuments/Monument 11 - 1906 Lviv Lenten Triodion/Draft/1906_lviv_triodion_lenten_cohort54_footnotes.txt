[^1479]: When the Annunciation falls on Wednesday of the Fifth Week of Great Lent, the Great Penitential Canon of St. Andrew of Crete (ordinarily appointed for Thursday Matins of the Fifth Week) is transferred backward to Tuesday Matins of that same week. Because Wednesday is the festal day of the Annunciation, kathisma readings and prostrations are suspended, and the Apodosis of the feast is celebrated on Thursday; thus the Great Canon cannot be sung on Thursday and is anticipated on Tuesday.

[^1480]: When the Annunciation falls on Thursday of the Fifth Week of Great Lent—the appointed day of the Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete—the Great Canon is likewise transferred to Tuesday of that week and chanted with the Tuesday triode. On Thursday itself, the festal office of the Annunciation is celebrated, united with the apostolic triode from the Triodion.

[^1481]: When the Annunciation falls on Friday of the Fifth Week of Great Lent, the Forefeast of the Annunciation (ordinarily kept on Thursday, March 24) is transferred backward to Wednesday, because Thursday is fully occupied by the celebration of the Great Canon.

[^1482]: The concurrence of the Annunciation (March 25) with the Saturday of the Akathist (the Fifth Saturday of Great Lent). This creates a magnificent liturgical synthesis of the two paramount Byzantine celebrations of the Mother of God, uniting the four strophes of the Akathist Hymn with the festal Annunciation hymnography at Compline, Matins, and the Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom.

[^1483]: The four-ode canon (*Четверопѣ́снецъ*) of the Triodion for the Saturday of the Akathist, composed by St. Joseph the Hymnographer (Odes 6, 7, 8, and 9). Because the Matins of the combined feast is already exceptionally dense with the full Akathist and the Annunciation canon, this triode/tetrode is transferred to Thursday Compline.

[^1484]: The descent (*схо́дъ* / *katabasis* / gathering into the center of the nave): The liturgical rubrics direct both choirs to descend from their respective kliros stalls and assemble together in the middle of the nave to solemnly chant the Ninth Heirmos (*"As the living ark of God..."*) and make a unified prostration.

[^1485]: The concluding colophon of the 1906 Lviv Stauropegion Lenten Triodion: *Коне́цъ и҆ Бг҃ꙋ Сла́ва* ("The End, and to God be the Glory"), printed on page 528 (physical leaf 532) beneath a decorative woodcut tailpiece. This marks the complete conclusion of the entire monument, issued by the Stauropegion Institute of Lviv under the archpastoral blessing of Metropolitan Andrey Sheptytsky of Halych, Bishop Hryhoriy Chekhovych of Peremyshl, and Bishop Hryhoriy Khomyshyn of Stanyslaviv.
