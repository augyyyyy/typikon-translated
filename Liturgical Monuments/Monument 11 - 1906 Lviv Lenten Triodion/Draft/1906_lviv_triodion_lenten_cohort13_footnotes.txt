[^324]: Clean Wednesday at Great Compline (*на повече́рїи вели́комъ*): The Great Penitential Canon of St. Andrew of Crete (Part 3), Ode 2, Tone 6, Heirmos: "Give ear, O heaven, and I will speak" (*Вонмѝ нб҃о, и҆ возглаго́лю*), followed by the second Ode 2, Heirmos: "See, see, that I am God" (*Ви́дите ви́дите, ꙗ҆́кѡ а҆́зъ є҆́смь Бг҃ъ*), and Ode 3 opening (*Оу҆твердѝ Гдⷭ҇и на ка́мени*), contemplating David's fall and tears (2 Sam. 12), the lost beauty of Eden, Cain's fratricide and Lamech's murder (Gen. 4:8, 23), Seth, Enos, Enoch's translation (Gen. 5:24), Noah and the deluge (Gen. 6–7), the repentance of St. Mary of Egypt in the Transjordan desert, and the blessing of Shem and Japheth (Gen. 9:26–27).
[^325]: Clean Wednesday at Great Compline: The Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete (Part 3), Ode 3 conclusion and Ode 4, Tone 6, Heirmos: "The prophet heard of Thy coming" (*Оу҆слы́ша проро́къ*), meditating upon Abraham leaving Haran and entertaining Angels at the oak of Mamre (Gen. 12:1–4; 18:1–8), the offering of Isaac on Mount Moriah (Gen. 22:1–14), the casting out of Ishmael and Hagar (Gen. 21:9–14), Christ shedding His Blood and water from His pierced side as font and chalice for the Church (John 19:34), the voluntary crucifixion opening Eden, and the parable of the foolish virgins excluded from the heavenly bridal chamber (Matt. 25:1–13).
[^326]: Clean Wednesday at Great Compline: The Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete (Part 3), Ode 5, Tone 6, Heirmos: "From night early morning seeking Thee" (*Ѿ но́щи ᲂу҆́тренююща*), and Ode 6, Tone 6, Heirmos: "I cried aloud with my whole heart" (*Возопи́хъ всѣ́мъ се́рдцемъ*), recalling Pharaoh's magicians Jannes and Jambres (2 Tim. 3:8; Exod. 7:11), the washing of the soul in tears, Christ's bodily miracles (cleansing lepers, healing the paralytic and the woman with an issue of blood, Mark 2; 5), Joshua conquering Amalek and making a covenant with the Gibeonites (Josh. 8–10), the ark crossing the Jordan into the Promised Land (Josh. 3), and Peter sinking in the sea (Matt. 14:28–31).
[^327]: Clean Wednesday at Great Compline: The Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete (Part 3), Kontakion, Tone 6: "My soul, arise, why sleepest thou?" (*Дꙋшѐ моѧ̀ воста́ни*), followed by Ode 7, Heirmos: "We have sinned, we have committed iniquity" (*Согрѣши́хомъ, беззако́нновахомъ*), and Ode 8, Heirmos: "Him Whom the heavenly hosts glorify" (*Е҆го́же вѡ́инства нб҃наѧ сла́вѧтъ*), lamenting the idolatrous sins of Manasseh (2 Chron. 33:1–13) and Ahab (1 Kings 21), Elijah shutting the heavens and being fed by the widow of Zarephath (1 Kings 17:1–16), destroying the prophets of Baal and the two captains of fifty (1 Kings 18; 2 Kings 1:9–12), and crying out with the penitent thief, weeping Peter, the publican, the sinful woman, the Canaanite woman, and Martha and Mary over Lazarus (Luke 23:42; 22:62; 18:13; 7:37–50; Matt. 15:22–28; John 11).
[^328]: Clean Wednesday at Great Compline: The Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete (Part 3 conclusion), Ode 9, Tone 6, Heirmos: "Seedless conception" (*Безсѣ́меннагѡ зача́тїѧ*), with troparia extolling Christ healing all diseases and raising Jairus' daughter (Mark 5:21–43), the salvation of the publican Zacchaeus (Luke 19:1–10) in contrast to Simon the Pharisee, the sinful woman anointing the Savior's feet with an alabaster jar of myrrh and wiping them with her hair (Luke 7:36–50), the condemnation of Chorazin, Bethsaida, and Capernaum down to Hades (Matt. 11:20–24), the faith of the Syrophoenician woman, and the final petition to St. Andrew of Crete, Archbishop of Gortyna (*па́стырю Кри́тскїй*); followed by rubrics for concluding Great Compline.
[^329]: Thursday of the First Week of the Holy and Great Fast at Matins (*Въ четверто́къ пе́рвыѧ седми́цы ст҃а́гѡ поста̀ на ᲂу҆́трени*): Sessional Hymns (Kathismata) following the Psalter stichologies, Tone 2, Model: *Having made wiser* (*Оу҆мꙋдри́вый па́че*), and Tone 5, Model: *Brighter than the Cherubim* (*Свѣтлѣ́йшаѧ Херꙋві́мъ*), followed by the Triodion Canons by St. Joseph the Hymnographer and St. Theodore the Studite, Ode 4, Tone 2 and Tone 5, Heirmoi: "Thou hast come from the Virgin" (*Прише́лъ є҆сѝ ѿ Дв҃ы*) and "The works of Thy dispensation, O Lord" (*Дѣла̀ смотре́нїѧ твоегѡ̀ Гдⷭ҇и*), extolling the twelve apostles as luminaries of the Sun of righteousness illuminating the world and guiding the faithful through the Forty-Day Fast.
[^330]: Clean Thursday Matins: Triodion Canons, Ode 8, Tone 2, by St. Joseph, Heirmos: "Unto the tyrant's decree" (*Велѣ́нїю мꙋчи́телевꙋ*), and the second Ode 8, Tone 2, by St. Theodore, Heirmos: "Thy venerable children" (*Преподо́бнїи твоѝ ѻ҆́троцы*), summoning the faithful to fast from all pleasure, ascend the chariot of divine virtues, and implore the holy apostles when they sit upon twelve thrones to judge the tribes of Israel with Christ (Matt. 19:28).
[^331]: Clean Thursday Matins: Triodion Canons, Ode 9, Tone 2, by St. Joseph, Heirmos: "The uncontainable God" (*Невмѣсти́маго Бг҃а*), and the second Ode 9, Tone 2, by St. Theodore, Heirmos: "We magnify, O Christ, Thy pure and all-immaculate Mother" (*Велича́емъ Хрⷭ҇тѐ*), praying to be delivered from the deep abyss of pleasures and to venerate Christ's holy Resurrection and Passion; followed by the Photagogicon and the Aposticha Idiomelon of the day, Tone 3 ("O Lord, Thou hast appointed repentance for me, a sinner...", Ps. 102:10; 2 Pet. 3:9), the Martyrikon, and Theotokion.
[^332]: Clean Thursday at the Sixth Hour (*На ѕ҃-мъ часѣ̀*): Troparion of prophecy, Tone 1 (*Deliver us from enemies visible and invisible, O Lord*, Ps. 78:9–10), the first Prokeimenon from Psalm 13 (*When the Lord turns back the captivity of His people*), followed by the prophecy of Isaiah (Isa. 2:11–21) proclaiming the Day of the Lord of Hosts humbling all human pride, tall cedars of Lebanon, oaks of Bashan, high towers, fortified walls, and merchant ships of Tarshish, when idolaters shall cast their gold and silver images to the bats and hide in rocky clefts; concluded by the second Prokeimenon from Psalm 14 (*Lord, who shall abide in Thy dwelling-place?*).
[^333]: Thursday of the First Week of the Holy and Great Fast at Vespers (*Въ четверто́къ а҃. седми́цы ст҃а́гѡ поста̀ ве́чера*): Rubrics for ordinary Lenten Vespers (when no Presanctified Liturgy is prescribed), ten stichera on *Lord, I have cried* including prosomoia by St. Joseph (Tone 2, Model: *Our Enlightener*, "Illumine me who am darkened by the deceits of hostility...", and "As a Vine hanging upon the Tree, O Savior...") and St. Theodore (Tone 2, Model: *O the great mystery*, "O the power of Thy Cross! It blossomed forth abstinence for the Church..."), Prokeimenon from Psalm 15 (*I will bless the Lord Who hath given me understanding*), and the second Genesis reading (Gen. 2:4–9) recounting the creation of heaven and earth, the mist watering the ground, the formation of Adam from the dust of the earth, the breathing of the breath of life, and the planting of the Garden of Eden with the Tree of Life and the Tree of Knowledge.
