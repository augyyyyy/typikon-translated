[^360]: First Saturday of the Holy Fast at Matins: Ode 3 of the Matins Canons of St. Theodore the Recruit (*Въ сꙋббѡ́тꙋ пе́рвꙋю ст҃ы́хъ постѡ́въ, на ᲂу҆́трени, пѣ́снь г҃*): Physical leaf p151 (Printed fol. 147). Following Ode 1, the service continues with Ode 3 of the two canons composed by John Mauropous, Metropolitan of Euchaita (11th century). The first canon is sung with the Heirmos *Thy hymn-singers, O Theotokos* (*Твоѧ̑ пѣсносло́вцы*), followed by five troparia praising the martyr's courage and a Theotokion. The second canon is sung with the Heirmos *There is none holy like Thee* (*Нѣ́сть ст҃ъ*), followed by troparia, Glory, Both now, the Katavasia of Ode 3, and the Sessional Hymn of St. Theodore in Tone 8, Model: *Of the Wisdom and Word* (*Премꙋ́дрости сло́ва*), which extols the martyr's spiritual armor and his burning of the pagan idol shrine.
[^361]: Matins Canon of St. Theodore: Ode 4 (*Пѣ́снь д҃*): Physical leaf p152 (Printed fol. 148). The leaf opens with the second Sessional Hymn of the saint in Tone 8, Model: *Of the Wisdom and Word*, recounting his trial before Maximian and his unharmed passage through fire, followed by the Sessional Theotokion. Ode 4 follows with the Heirmos of the first canon *He Who sits in glory* (*Сѣдѧ́й въ сла́вѣ*), meditating on the incarnation of the God-Man and the uncrushed bones of the martyr in his fiery sacrifice; and the second canon with Heirmos *Christ is my power* (*Хрⷭ҇то́съ моѧ̀ си́ла*), recounting Julian the Apostate's wicked decree to secretly sprinkle market provisions with idol sacrifice blood during Clean Week, and the gathering of the faithful in the holy temple of God upon discovering the plot; concluding with the Katavasia of Ode 4.
[^362]: Matins Canon of St. Theodore: Odes 5 and 6 (*Пѣ́снь є҃ и҆ ѕ҃*): Physical leaf p153 (Printed fol. 149). Ode 5 begins with the Heirmos of the first canon *All things were amazed* (*Оу҆жасо́шасѧ всѧ́чєскаѧ*), celebrating the opening of the portals of grace and the living presence of the martyr defending the Orthodox from the snares of the enemy, and the second canon with Heirmos *By Thy divine light, O Good One* (*Бж҃їимъ свѣ́томъ твои́мъ*), recounting Archbishop Eudoxius's terror and his tearful all-night prayers to God, and the dispatch of St. Theodore from heaven to instruct the pastor; concluding with the Katavasia of Ode 5. Ode 6 follows with the Heirmoi *This divine and all-honorable feast* (*Бж҃е́ственное сїѐ и҆ всечестно́е*) and *The sea of life* (*Жите́йское мо́ре*), depicting the martyr's dialogue with the archbishop.
[^363]: Kontakion, Ikos, and Synaxarion of Nicephorus Callistus Xanthopoulos for the Miracle of the Kolyvo: Physical leaf p154 (Printed fol. 150). Following Ode 6, the Kontakion in Tone 8 (*Having received the faith of Christ within thy heart as a shield...*) and Ikos are appointed, followed by the full historical Synaxarion composed by Nicephorus Callistus Xanthopoulos (c. 1256–1335). The Synaxarion details the historical context of Julian the Apostate's reign (361–363 A.D.), who, seeking to subtly contaminate the Christians during their holy fast in Clean Week, ordered the Prefect of Constantinople to remove all standard provisions from the market and substitute food and drink mingled with blood from pagan sacrifices. The all-seeing Eye of God dispatched the Great-Martyr Theodore the Recruit (from the cohort of the Tyrones) to appear not in a dream but visibly in bodily form to Archbishop Eudoxius, commanding him to warn the Christian flock and provide boiled wheat (*kolyvo*, according to the Euchaita dialect) to sustain the faithful.
[^364]: Conclusion of the Synaxarion and Matins Canon: Odes 7 and 8 (*Пѣ́снь з҃ и҆ и҃*): Physical leaf p155 (Printed fol. 151). The leaf opens with the conclusion of Xanthopoulos's Synaxarion, narrating the defeat and shame of Julian the Apostate, the restoration of clean market provisions, and the institution of the annual Saturday kolyvo memorial; followed by a summary of St. Theodore's original martyrdom under Maximian (c. 306 A.D.) in Amasea of Pontus, his burning of the temple of Cybele, and his entry into the fiery furnace where he yielded his soul unto God. Ode 7 follows with Heirmoi *The godly-minded youths did not serve the creature* (*Не послꙋжи́ша тва́ри*) and *O Thou Who didst make the furnace dewy* (*Росода́тельнꙋ ᲂу҆́бѡ пе́щь*), shaming Julian and exalting Christ the King; followed by the opening strophes of Ode 8 with Heirmos *O Redeemer of all* (*И҆зба́вителю всѣ́хъ*), sanctifying Saturday as the memorial of great salvation.
[^365]: Matins Canon of St. Theodore: Conclusion of Ode 8 and Ode 9 (*Пѣ́снь и҃ и҆ ѳ҃*): Physical leaf p156 (Printed fol. 152). Ode 8 concludes with the troparia recounting the deliverance from the defiled banquet, the second canon with Heirmos *Out of the flame for the venerable ones* (*И҆з̾ пла́мене преподо́бнымъ*), and the Katavasia of the Three Youths (*The childbearing of the Theotokos saved the godly youths in the furnace...*). Ode 9 follows with the Heirmos of the first canon *Thy childbearing was incorrupt* (*Ржⷭ҇тво̀ твоѐ нетлѣ́нно*), bidding the Church of Christ to take courage against her adversaries through the advocacy of her martyrs; and the second canon with Heirmos *For man to see God is impossible* (*Бг҃а человѣ́кѡмъ не возмо́жно*), extolling St. Theodore as the radiant defender of all who keep his eternal memory.
[^366]: Conclusion of Ode 9, Exapostilarion, and Praises of St. Theodore (*На хвали́техъ*): Physical leaf p157 (Printed fol. 153). The leaf concludes Ode 9 with the troparia, Glory, Both now, and the Katavasia *Let every earthborn mortal leap for joy* (*Вся́къ земноро́дный*); followed by the Exapostilarion of St. Theodore in Tone 2, Model: *With the disciples* (*Со ᲂу҆чени́ки*), and its Theotokion. At the Praises (*На хвали́техъ*), four Stichera are appointed in Tone 1, Model: *Of the celestial orders* (*Нбⷭ҇ныхъ чинѡ́въ*), calling the faithful to gather in harmony and celebrate the memory of St. Theodore the Recruit (*Тѵ́рѡнъ*), honoring his endurance, spiritual riches, and heavenly gifts.
[^367]: Praises Doxastikon and Aposticha of St. Theodore (*На стїхо́внѣ*): Physical leaf p158 (Printed fol. 154). The Praises conclude with the Doxastikon in Tone 6 (*A gift of sanctification and wealth of divine life...*), followed by the Theotokion *Thou art the true vine* (*Бцⷣе, ты̀ є҆сѝ лоза̀ и҆́стиннаѧ*). At the Aposticha, three Idiomelon Stichera are sung in Tone 2 (*Ликꙋ́ютъ собо́ры мꙋ́ченикѡвъ...*), set to Psalm verses (Ps. 91:13 and Ps. 91:14), extolling the clean fast combined with the miracles of the martyrs, by which the faithful are cleansed from spiritual defilement; followed by the Doxastikon in Tone 8 (*Having devoutly armed thyself with suffering courage...*) and the concluding Theotokion *O Sovereign Lady, receive the prayers of Thy servants*.
[^368]: Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom and Monastic Typikon on Fasting Mitigations: Physical leaf p159 (Printed fol. 155). The rubrics appoint the First Hour joined to Matins. At the Divine Liturgy, the Typika and Beatitudes are sung with Odes 3 and 6 of the Saint's Canon; Prokeimenon in Tone 7 (Ps. 63:11, *The righteous man shall be glad in the Lord*); Epistle readings for the day (Heb. 3:12–16, pericope 313) and for the Saint (2 Tim. 2:1–10, pericope 292); Alleluia in Tone 4 (Ps. 91:13); Gospel lessons for the day (Mark 1:35–44, pericope 7) and for the Saint (John 15:17–16:2, pericope 52); and Communion Hymn (Ps. 111:6). The Trapeza rubric orders the reading of the Encomium of St. Theodore by St. Gregory of Nyssa (*In Praise of the Great Martyr Theodore*), prescribing cooked beans, white and black olives, cooked food with oil, and kolyvo, with wine permitted in moderation (*по крагово́лю*) for the feast, according to the ancient Palestinian rule of the Lavras of St. Sabbas the Sanctified and St. Euthymius the Great; concluding with the general Lenten statute that fish is permitted only twice during the Great Fast: on the Feast of the Annunciation (March 25) and on Palm Sunday (Flower-bearing Sunday). Below this text is a detailed woodcut engraving representing Faith as an allegorical crowned maiden seated upon clouds, bearing the Cross and palm branch.
[^369]: First Sunday of the Great Fast (Sunday of the Triumph of Orthodoxy) at Little Vespers (*Недѣ́лѧ пе́рваѧ ст҃а́гѡ вели́кагѡ поста̀, въ ню́же Правосла́вїе воспомина́етсѧ, на ма́лѣй вече́рни*): Physical leaf p160 (Printed fol. 156). Display headpiece featuring a rectangular woodcut engraving of the Holy Eucharist—a chalice surmounted by a crucifix and supported by open Scriptures, emitting radiant rays of divine light within an ornamental foliate frame. The rubrics appoint the customary All-Night Vigil (*Бдѣ́нїе ѻ҆бы́чное*), beginning on Saturday evening with Little Vespers, setting four Stichera in Tone 1, Model: *Of the celestial orders* (*Нбⷭ҇ныхъ чинѡ́въ*), composed by Nicholas. The stichera celebrate the restoration of holy icons in Constantinople on the First Sunday of Lent in 843 A.D. under Empress Theodora and Patriarch Methodius, explicitly invoking the theological definition of St. Basil the Great from *De Spiritu Sancto* 18.45 (*The honor rendered unto the image passes unto the Prototype*); concluded by the Doxastikon-Theotokion in Tone 1, Model: *Of the celestial orders*, extolling the mystery of the Incarnation whereby the invisible God became visible man through the pure blood of the Virgin, permitting the veneration of His bodily image.
