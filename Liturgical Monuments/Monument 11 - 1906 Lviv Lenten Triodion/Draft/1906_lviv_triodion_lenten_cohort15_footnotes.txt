[^345]: Friday of the First Week of the Holy and Great Fast at Matins (*Въ пѧто́къ а҃. седми́цы ст҃а́гѡ поста̀ на ᲂу҆́трени*): Physical leaf p141 (Printed fol. 137). Conclusion of the second canon at Ode 9 by St. Theodore the Studite with Trinity troparion, Theotokion, Cross-troparion, and Heirmos (*Pure and all-immaculate Mother and Virgin...*); followed by the Exaposteilarion of the Tone, the Aposticha Idiomelon of the day in Tone 8 (*Let us joyfully receive the bequest of the fast...*), the Martyrikon, and the Cross-Theotokion in Tone 8, Model: *O most marvelous wonder* (*Ѡ҆ пресла́внагѡ чꙋдесѐ*), meditating on the lament of the Virgin at Golgotha. The rubrics order the conclusion of Matins (*It is good to give praise to the Lord...*), specify that the First Hour is read without kathisma stichology on all Fridays of Great Lent, and provide the Troparion of the Sixth Hour in Tone 5 (*Prevent us with Thy compassions, O Lord...*).
[^346]: Prophecy of Isaiah (Isa. 3:1–14) at the Sixth Hour: Physical leaf p142 (Printed fol. 138). Preceded by the First Prokeimenon from Psalm 17 (Ps. 17:2, *I will love Thee, O Lord, my strength*). The prophetic reading depicts the total collapse of societal leadership and moral order in Jerusalem and Judea as divine chastisement for sin, where the Lord removes warriors, judges, prophets, counselors, and artisans, leaving immature youths to rule, leading to civil strife, lawlessness, and rebellion against God, prophetically prefiguring the unjust condemnation of the Righteous One (*Let us bind the righteous man, for he is cumbersome unto us*, Isa. 3:10; cf. Wis. 2:12), followed by the Second Prokeimenon from Psalm 18 (Ps. 18:15, *O Lord, my Helper and my Redeemer*).
[^347]: Friday of the First Week of the Holy and Great Fast at Vespers with the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts (*Въ пѧто́къ пе́рвыѧ седми́цы ст҃а́гѡ поста̀ ве́чера*): Physical leaf p142 (Printed fol. 138). The introductory rubric directs that any occurring Menaion saint for Saturday or Sunday be transferred to Compline at the discretion of the ecclesiarch; followed by the major display headpiece for Clean Friday Vespers combined with the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts, appointing 10 stichera at *Lord, I have cried*: the Idiomelon of the day twice in Tone 5 (*Come, ye faithful, let us work the works of God in the light...*), calling the faithful to active works of mercy and ethical integrity during the fast.
[^348]: Commemoration of the Holy Great-Martyr Theodore the Recruit (Teron) at Vespers: Physical leaf p143 (Printed fol. 139). Following four Martyrika of the Oktoechos, four Idiomela are sung in Tone 2 in honor of St. Theodore the Recruit (*Ѳео́дѡръ Тѵ́рѡнъ*), celebrating his steadfastness under torture, his miracles in delivering captives, healing the sick, finding lost property, and rescuing a widow's son on a white horse; followed by the Doxastikon in Tone 6 (*Using the apostate tyrant as his vessel...*), which narrates the foundational miracle of Kolyvo: in 362 A.D., Emperor Julian the Apostate sought to contaminate the food supply of Constantinople with blood from pagan sacrifices during Clean Week, but St. Theodore appeared in a dream to Archbishop Eudoxius, instructing the Christians to boil wheat (*kolyvo*) instead of purchasing defiled market provisions. Concluded by the Entrance without Gospel, *O Gladsome Light*, and the Prokeimenon from Psalm 19 (Ps. 19:2, *May the Lord hear thee in the day of affliction*).
[^349]: Old Testament Reading from Genesis (Gen. 2:20–3:20) at Vespers: Physical leaf p144 (Printed fol. 140). The second reading of Clean Friday Vespers, recounting the naming of the animals by Adam, the creation of Eve from Adam's rib as his suitable helpmeet, the primordial institution of marriage (*and they two shall be one flesh*), the temptation and deception of Eve by the serpent, the Fall of man through partaking of the forbidden tree, the divine confrontation in Eden, the curses pronounced upon the serpent, woman, and man, the Protoevangelium (*He shall watch against thy head, and thou shalt watch against His heel*, Gen. 3:15), the expulsion into labor and mortality (*earth thou art, and unto earth shalt thou go*), and Adam naming his wife Life (*Жи́знь* / Eve); followed by the Second Prokeimenon from Psalm 20 (Ps. 20:14, *Be Thou exalted, O Lord, in Thy strength*).
[^350]: Old Testament Reading from Proverbs (Prov. 3:19–34) at Vespers: Physical leaf p145 (Printed fol. 141). The third lesson of Clean Friday Vespers, extolling divine Wisdom as the cosmic foundation of heaven and earth, counseling the seeker of wisdom to maintain sound judgment, prudence, and peace, assuring divine protection against terrors, commanding immediate charity unto the needy (*Withhold not to do good unto the needy, when thine hand hath power to help*), forbidding malice, strife, and envy toward violent men, and concluding with the classic aphorism of divine justice: *The Lord resists the proud, but gives grace unto the humble* (Prov. 3:34; cf. Jas. 4:6; 1 Pet. 5:5).
[^351]: The Supplicatory Canon to the Holy Great-Martyr Theodore the Recruit and the Setting Forth of Kolyvo: Physical leaf p145 (Printed fol. 141). Following the Prayer behind the Ambo (*По Заамво́нной же мл҃твѣ*) of the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts, a dish of boiled wheat sweetened with honey and dried fruits (*kolyvo*) is set forth on the Tetrapod; the celebrants chant Psalm 142 (*O Lord, hear my prayer*), *God is the Lord*, the Apolytikion of St. Theodore in Tone 2 (*Great are the accomplishments of faith! In the fountain of flame, as upon the water of rest...*), the Theotokion (*All beyond comprehension...*), Psalm 50, and begin the Supplicatory Canon.
[^352]: The Supplicatory Canon to St. Theodore by St. John of Damascus, Tone 8 (Odes 1–4): Physical leaf p146 (Printed fol. 142). The acrostic of the canon is explicitly noted in the rubric: *I sing of thee, namesake of God's gifts* (*Бж҃їихъ тѧ̀ пою̀ дарѡ́въ тезоимени́те*, playing on the Greek etymology of Theodore, *Θεόδωρος* = "Gift of God"). St. John of Damascus commemorates the saint's refusal to offer pagan sacrifice, his enlistment as a soldier of Christ, his testimony before the persecutors, and his defense of true doctrine against pagan polytheism.
[^353]: Supplicatory Canon to St. Theodore (Odes 5–7 and Kontakion): Physical leaf p147 (Printed fol. 143). Ode 5 recounts the burning of the pagan temple of Cybele by St. Theodore; Ode 6 reflects on the vanity of the idols and the steadfastness of the martyrs; followed by the Kontakion of St. Theodore in Tone 8 (*Having received the faith of Christ within thy heart as a shield...*); and Ode 7, meditating on the saint's imprisonment and the miraculous visitation of Christ comforting him in his cell as once the three youths in the Babylonian furnace.
[^354]: Rubrics for the Blessing of Kolyvo and Concluding Rites: Physical leaf p148 (Printed fol. 144). Conclusion of the Supplicatory Canon with Odes 8 and 9, Trisagion, Troparion, Kontakion, and Theotokion; followed by the special prayer of blessing over the kolyvo with censing by the priest, *Blessed be the Name of the Lord*, Psalm 33 (*I will bless the Lord at all times*), the distribution of blessed bread (*antidoron*), and the dismissal. An additional rubric outlines the alternate order of the service if the Presanctified Liturgy is not celebrated.
[^355]: Monastic Typikon on Fasting Mitigation and Lenten Refectory: Physical leaf p149 (Printed fol. 145). The rubric records the ancient tradition of the Palestinian Lavras of St. Sabbas the Sanctified and St. Euthymius the Great permitting wine and oil on Clean Friday on account of the solemnity of St. Theodore's feast, while noting that contemporary Ruthenian practice refrains from oil and wine on this day out of reverence for Clean Week, prescribing instead cooked food with prunes without oil or pickles (*armea*, sauerkraut/brine dishes), while those desiring stricter asceticism practice xerophagy (*сꙋхоѧде́нїе*).
[^356]: Ruthenian Synodal Decree on Compline and Memorial Rites: Physical leaf p149 (Printed fol. 145). The rubric explicitly cites the Provincial Synod of Lviv held in 1740 (*Собо́ръ, бы́вшїй въ гра́дѣ Льво́вѣ въ лѣ́то ҂аѱч҃а*), which decreed that in conformity with Greek usage, Small Compline (*повече́рїа ма̑лаѧ*) be celebrated on all Fridays of Great Lent rather than Great Compline, and regulates the singing of the resting canons (*поко́йныѧ канѡ́ны*) in the cemetery crypt chapel (*въ гробни́цѣ*) as well as the distribution of the Saturday and Sunday Menaion canons at Compline.
[^357]: First Saturday of the Holy Fast at Matins (*Въ сꙋббѡ́тꙋ пе́рвꙋю ст҃ы́хъ постѡ́въ, на ᲂу҆́трени*): Physical leaf p149 (Printed fol. 145). The service commemorates the Holy Great-Martyr Theodore the Recruit and his miracle of kolyvo; after the Six Psalms and *God is the Lord* in Tone 2, the Troparion of the saint is sung twice (*Great are the accomplishments of faith...*), followed by Sessional Hymns from the Oktoechos after the first kathisma and two Sessional Hymns of St. Theodore in Tone 3, Model: *Of the divine faith* (*Бж҃їѧ вѣ́ры*), praising the saint for quenching the deception of idols and dispensing healings throughout the world.
[^358]: First Matins Canon of St. Theodore by John Mauropous, Tone 4: Physical leaf p150 (Printed fol. 146). Following the reading of the Saint's Life/Passio (*чте́мъ мꙋче́нїе ст҃а́гѡ*) and Psalm 50, the first canon is appointed on 8, composed by John Mauropous, Metropolitan of Euchaita (11th century). The acrostic reads: *Most glorious Tyro, the glory of the first Saturday* (*Тѵ́рѡнъ пресла́вный, сла́ва пе́рвѣй сꙋббѡ́тѣ*), with an additional acrostic in the opening troparia. Ode 1 begins with a praise of the Theotokos before extolling the fame of St. Theodore across the universe.
[^359]: Second Matins Canon of St. Theodore by John Mauropous, Tone 6: Physical leaf p150 (Printed fol. 146). The second canon in honor of the saint for Matins, also composed by John Mauropous, Metropolitan of Euchaita, in Tone 6, Ode 1, Heirmos: *Traversing the deep as on dry land* (*Ꙗ҆́кѡ по сꙋ́хꙋ*), opening with a prayer for divine eloquence to praise the martyr's wonders, celebrating his steadfast confession, and condemning Julian the Apostate's betrayal of the Christian faith.
