[^1181]: The Matins Aposticha Idiomelon of Tuesday of Palm Week in Tone 5 combines two biblical warnings against spiritual sloth: Lot fleeing the destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah without looking back (Gen. 19:17, 26), and the parable of Dives and Lazarus (Luke 16:19–31), wherein the beggar is received into Abraham's bosom while the unmerciful rich man burns in torment.

[^1182]: The Sixth Hour on Tuesday of Palm Week appoints the Troparion of the Prophecy in Tone 5 ("An invincible rampart hast Thou given unto our city..."), followed by two prokeimena from the Psalter (Psalms 108 and 109 LXX) and the prophetic reading from Isaiah 49:6–10.

[^1183]: Reading from the Prophecy of Isaiah (49:6–10) at the Sixth Hour of Tuesday: the passage continues the Servant Songs of the Messiah, declaring Christ to be given as a covenant for the people and a light to the Gentiles, establishing the earth and liberating prisoners from spiritual bonds.

[^1184]: The Tuesday Vespers stichera at "Lord, I have cried" in Tone 2 by St. Joseph the Hymnographer allegorize the death of Lazarus into the interior state of the sinner, whose heart lies buried in the tomb of despondency and covered with the stone of insensibility, crying for Christ to awaken it by the Wood of the Cross.

[^1185]: Sticheron by St. Theodore the Studite at Tuesday Vespers in Tone 3 marking the dramatic progression of Palm Week: "Today Lazarus hath breathed his last, and Bethany weeps for him" (Днесь издыше Лазарь), anticipating the Lord's arrival to raise him on Lazarus Saturday.

[^1186]: Reading from Genesis 31:3–16 at Tuesday Vespers: Jacob receives the divine command to depart from Padan-aram and return to Canaan with Leah, Rachel, and his flocks, recounting God's protective intervention against Laban's deceit.

[^1187]: Reading from Proverbs 21:3–21 appointed across Tuesday Vespers (fol. 429–430 / physical leaves p433–p434), contrasting the blessings of righteousness, mercy, and diligent labor with the destruction awaiting deceit and pride.

[^1188]: The Tuesday Vespers Aposticha Idiomelon in Tone 1 contrasts the royal purple robe of dominion and the scarlet mantle of incorruption bestowed upon the soul with the purple robe of mockery placed upon Christ before His crucifixion (Matt. 27:28), calling the Christian to spiritual poverty.

[^1189]: Wednesday of Palm Week at Matins: following the second stichology of the Psalter, the sessional hymn by St. Joseph the Hymnographer in Tone 2 portrays the sinner weighed down by sins as with a grave stone, pleading for Christ to raise him from the tomb of negligence.

[^1190]: Sessional hymn by St. Theodore the Studite following the third stichology on Wednesday Matins in Tone 3: "Today Lazarus, having died, is buried" (Днесь Лазарь умерый погребается), tracking the chronological sequence of the four days in the tomb.

[^1191]: The two Matins canons (triodia) of Wednesday of Palm Week: the first triodion (Odes 3, 8, and 9) in Tone 2 is by St. Joseph the Hymnographer, focused on awakening the mind from the slumber of despondency. The second triodion in Tone 3 is by St. Theodore the Studite, dramatizing Christ's announcement to the disciples that Lazarus is fallen asleep and St. Thomas's courageous resolve to accompany Him into Judea (John 11:16).

[^1192]: Ode 8 of the Studite triodion on Wednesday Matins portrays Lazarus being carried out to the grave ("Today Lazarus, having died, is carried out for burial") while Christ approaches with joy to reveal His divine power as the Life of all.

[^1193]: Ode 9 of the Studite triodion depicts Mary and Martha weeping under the bitter stone of the sepulcher, while Christ declares from afar that He rejoices for the apostles' sakes that He was not there, in order that they may believe (John 11:15).

[^1194]: The Matins Aposticha Idiomelon of Wednesday of Palm Week in Tone 1 offers an allegorical and patristic exposition of the Parable of Dives and Lazarus (Luke 16:19–31): Israel, formerly robed in priestly and royal vesture under the Law and the Prophets, now thirsts outside the Kingdom for having rejected the crucified Christ, while the Gentiles, formerly destitute of truth, are now comforted in Abraham's bosom, clothed in the purple of Christ's blood and the scarlet of baptism.

[^1195]: The Sixth Hour on Wednesday of Palm Week appoints the Troparion of the Prophecy in Tone 5 ("Deal Thy mercies with us, O Lord..."), accompanied by two prokeimena from the Psalter (Psalms 112 and 113 LXX) and the prophetic reading from Isaiah 58:1–11.

[^1196]: Reading from the Prophecy of Isaiah (58:1–11) at the Sixth Hour of Wednesday: the definitive prophetic charter on the true fast, rejecting empty ascetic posturing accompanied by contention and oppression, and demanding active works of mercy, the liberation of the bound, and the feeding of the hungry.

[^1197]: Wednesday of Palm Week at Vespers: the idiomelon at "Lord, I have cried" in Tone 5 resumes the theme of Dives and Lazarus, imploring Christ to grant the speaker the poverty in sins of Lazarus (Лазара мя сотвори, нищаго грѣхами) rather than the fiery torment of the heartless rich man.

[^1198]: The prosomoia stichera by St. Theodore the Studite at Wednesday Vespers in Tone 5 track Christ's physical journey from beyond the Jordan towards Bethany, announcing to the apostles that Lazarus is dead and that He goes to conquer death.

[^1199]: St. Theodore's sticheron in Tone 6 marks the second day of Lazarus in the grave: "Lazarus passeth his second day in the tomb" (Двоедневствуетъ Лазарь во гробѣ), describing his vision of the departed from all ages held in the bonds of hades.

[^1200]: Reading from Genesis 43:26–31 and 45:1 at Wednesday Vespers, prefiguring the revelation of Christ unto His brethren. The heading in the Stauropegion edition contains an epigraphic misprint, reading Chapter 33 ("Глава̀ л҃г") instead of Chapter 43 ("м҃г").
