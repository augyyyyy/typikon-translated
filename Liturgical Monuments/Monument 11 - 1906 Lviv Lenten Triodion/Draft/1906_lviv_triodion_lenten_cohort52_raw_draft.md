=== LEAF p511 ===

—sinking them swiftly, she accomplished this deed through the sea's currents; for placing their sole hope of salvation in her, she preserved her heritage together unharmed from the cruel assailants. For then even their chief, the Khagan himself, having become an eyewitness of the destruction of his own forces, standing there somewhere upon a high place with his valiant horsemen, beat his hands upon his face and breast.

Now those within the city, waging battle against the enemies, when they beheld the destruction that had befallen the foes in the sea, immediately waxing strong by divine power and fortified by the might of the Virgin Theotokos, opened the gates of the city, and shouting and crying aloud, rushed upon the enemies. Such great joy and strength possessed the city's warriors, while great fear seized the barbarians. And it was a marvelous sight to behold the glorious generalship of our Sovereign Lady the Theotokos: for even young youths and women rushed upon the barbarians and attacked the very necks of their enemies; and they wrought such slaughter among the barbarians as is impossible to recount in words. There truly was fulfilled that which was spoken: one chased a thousand, and two put ten thousands to flight. Such strength did the Most Pure Virgin Theotokos and Sovereign Lady grant unto the weak, and such power unto the infirm.

When the sun had set and night had fallen, the engines of war which the barbarians had brought upon wheels for the taking of the city were all surrendered to the flames; and by the gracious favor of the most compassionate Theotokos toward them, the very makers of those labors and stratagems were compelled to become the burners of them.

The Prefect and all the citizens, lifting up their hands unto heaven, offered thanksgiving with tears, saying: "Thy right hand, O Lord, is glorified in strength; Thy right hand, O Lord, hath broken the enemies, and in the multitude of Thy glory Thou hast crushed the adversaries!"[^1410] And thus the foolish Khagan, having arrived with a multitude of countless troops, returned in shame. In like manner also the general who had led the Persian troops, laying his hand upon his mouth and covering his face, because out of so many thousands he led away only a small number with himself, reached his own country in disgrace.

Thus the servant of divine providence and goodness, the Most Pure and Undefiled Theotokos, the sovereign helper of Christians, showed her strength in our defense and granted us so great and glorious a salvation. Therefore, in remembrance of such a benefaction, we assemble this present solemn congregational gathering of all the people, and we celebrate an all-night feast, offering songs of thanksgiving. The Universal Church hath named this feast the "Unseated Hymn" (*Akathistos*), to be celebrated in honor of the Mother of God at this season when the victory was wrought by the Mother of God, calling it *Akathistos*, that is, *Unseated*; because thus did the clergy and all the people of the city perform it at that time.[^1411]

*The Second Miracle against the Saracens.*

After the passing of thirty-six years, in the reign of Constantine the Bearded, the great Saracen fleet (*Rhodostolos*) arrived once again with a multitude of ships against that imperial city.[^1412] And they anchored immediately along the shore at the inlet, from which they waged warfare by sea against the city daily, from the springtime until the autumn, departing to spend the winter in Cyzicus. And returning again in the spring to the imperial city, they again waged warfare from the sea, contending fiercely for seven years.

They lost many of their ships, together with the soldiers stationed aboard them and all the equipment which they had brought with them for their need; and they also returned with great loss unto their own lands. And when they were in the regions of Syllaeum, fierce and contrary winds blew upon them, and by the righteous wrath of God they perished, all being drowned in the sea through the intercession of the Mother of God. In like manner, at diverse times the wretched ones, throwing themselves against that God-guarded city to fulfill their furious impulse, returned empty-handed and filled with shame.

*The Third Miracle against the Hagarenes.*

Yet once again, because their violence was exalted into greatness on every side, it increased from the east unto the west and grew into a multitude.[^1413] And first indeed they entered into the Persian kingdom; and from thence into Egypt and Libya, giving their word to the Christians that they would not cause them to transgress the orthodox and blameless faith in Christ our God—which promise they did not keep, but showed forth many martyrs, because they refused to trample upon the Precious Cross, for this they compelled the Christians to do. And so to speak, they encompassed the whole earth with captivity, having overrun India, Ethiopia, Spain, the lands of the Moors, and Libya.

Last of all, they encamped against that very imperial city, desiring to take it. The Emperor being then the Isaurian, who had received the imperial crown, promised to pay them tribute. They, however, demanded that their own garrison be placed over the city; for they relied upon the multitude of their own troops and mercenaries, and upon the preparation of countless warriors in ships, the number of the vessels being one thousand and eight hundred, as those who have labored over chronicles have recorded.

These errant fighters against Christ placed their tents before the city; yet they brought not a single assault against the walls, for they were occupied in building houses, imagining that they would dwell there as at home, having woven such plans in their mind and intent, while the Lord frustrated their counsels. Meanwhile, the Christ-loving people within the city unceasingly celebrated the divine services, as was their custom to do, in peace and good order. Then taking up the all-venerable Wood of the Cross of God and our Savior Jesus Christ, and the holy Icons of the Most Pure Mother of God, they walked—

=== LEAF p512 ===

—around the walls of the city, lifting up their hands unto God and saying: "Arise, O Lord, help us, and cast not off Thy people unto the end; for lo, Thine enemies have made a tumult, and they that hate Thee have lifted up their head! Give not Thine inheritance unto reproach, that the heathen should rule over us, lest they should ever say: 'Where is their God?' But let them know that Thou, Lord Jesus Christ, art Lord unto the glory of God the Father!"[^1414]

Now a certain horseman from among the opposing Saracens, boastfully mocking the city of Constantine by name and calling the church by a common name, "Sophia," fell down together with his horse into a ditch and breathed his last. Furthermore, their herald, having climbed a tall tree to proclaim their impure prayer, likewise fell down and wretchedly cast forth his godless soul.

After this they resolved to wage war against the Bulgars; for there were two generals, and one remained behind to destroy the imperial city, while the other went forth to fight against the Bulgars. Contending against them, he was defeated by them; and more than twenty thousand of his troops fell, while the survivors returned in shame unto their own.[^1415]

Having returned from the Bulgars, they attempted to reach the region of Blachernae with the ships they had brought. The stretching of iron chains from the imperial city unto Galata, however, prevented them, so that they were unable to accomplish what they had planned.[^1416] Sailing therefore as far as the place called Sosthenion and into other small harbors, the greater part of them were crushed and broken by a severe winter and dreadful winds; and the Greeks burned a multitude of other great ships.

Now the city was under great duress from the scarcity of human necessities. The adversaries, however, consumed without restraint whatever fell into their hands; and falling into a bitter extremity of famine, they were reduced even to tasting human flesh and the carcasses of unclean beasts, mingling unclean substances in their desperation. By reason of such desperate affliction, many of their notable and valiant men fled to the city and surrendered.[^1417]

For as of old He drowned the Egyptians who pursued Israel in the depths like a stone and brought the waters of the sea upon them, so also the Lord brought destruction upon the Saracens fleeing from the imperial city: for stirring up the sea with a dark wind and a dreadful, fiery tempest, He destroyed those thousands of ships. For while the majority of the vessels were sailing upon the Aegean deep, hail suddenly fell from heaven; and just as cold iron when brought near to fire boils up, so also the hailstones falling into the seawater caused it immediately to boil up. And by such boiling it melted the pitch of the opposing ships, and all sank into the depths, only three remaining to bring the report.[^1418]

And there were to be seen upon all the islands, upon the shores, in the harbors, and in the bays, mounds of dead cast up and lying about.

Let all such things be recounted unto generations to come, to speak in the words of the Psalm, and let every Christian mouth proclaim the sacred word, saying with David: "Thou, O Lord, hast crushed the heads of the dragons in the water!"[^1419] Let there also be added the song sung with a loud voice at the exodus of Israel: "Let us sing unto the Lord, for He is gloriously glorified!"

Having reached this point in my discourse, and gazing up unto the height of the surpassing miracles of God and the Sovereign Lord, and of the unique Most Pure Theotokos, I become speechless with amazement, and I find no words worthy of these deeds. For what eloquent tongue can worthily praise the Virgin Theotokos? Or how can anyone gloriously hymn her glorious grace, to glorify the fountain of the love of mankind and to offer thanksgiving?

For it was fitting for us to offer thanksgiving unto the Mother of God by our deeds; for she herself and her Son rejoice far more in such deeds than in pipes, organs, and musical songs. And this is, as the Apostle also said: love without hypocrisy, which is the head of the Law and the Prophets; unswerving brotherly love; unrelenting compassion; divine virginity; lawful marriage; unsearchable chastity; conjoined righteousness; lawful courage against the passions; a God-directed understanding; inoffensive mercy; temperance without pride; well-contrite humility; and in addition to these, a blameless life, an upright character, a humble disposition, a prudent mind, God-proclaiming lips, a God-praising tongue, an unadulterous eye, hands free from plunder, feet not swift unto evil, an anger that is not unreasonable, subduing the fiery arrows of passionate thoughts; and all the other virtues whereby man, created according to the image of God and joined unto God, is adorned as with precious and beautiful raiment.[^1420]

With such virtues ought we to give thanks unto God; for the wise have said that thanksgiving is composed of deeds and not of words alone. Yet since the weakness of our nature and the sluggishness of our mind make us slothful in virtuous labor, and we touch upon virtue only sparingly, as it were with the tip of our finger; for this reason we fall short in offering our due thanksgiving, so that we may at least offer songs in words.

Lifting up our voices, therefore, and elevating our understanding, let us speak thus with acclamation:

Thou, O Sovereign Lady Theotokos, possessing love for mankind by nature, hast not forsaken our Christian race, defending us as a mother who loves her children and is compassionate. Thus Thou dost ever grant Thy benefactions unto us, saving, covering, and preserving us, delivering us from perils and turning away tribulations. Therefore we give thanks for these things, and we proclaim Thy grace; we hide not Thy benefactions; we chant Thy miracles with a loud voice; we glorify Thine aid; we magnify Thy providence; we hymn Thy protection; we possess Thee as our beneficent helper; we glorify Thy tender mercy, and we remember Thy many great gifts, and from how many perils we have been delivered by Thee!

These songs of thanksgiving we offer unto Thee as a debt, not as though they were worthy of Thy benefaction, but rather as the lisping of smiling children. And concerning the present evils that afflict us, we implore Thy speedy aid; we have need of Thy protection: resolve the stumbling-blocks that exist in our midst! Dispel the cloud and darkness that encompass us, through which, as in a nocturnal battle, we are wounded—or rather to say, as though mad, we consume our own flesh, not understanding our kinship and not considering our common brotherhood, so that some among us dare to do such things, having been made worthy of that same Spirit, of the one Christ, of the same faith, of the same baptism, of the one Church, and of the same Mysteries!

=== LEAF p513 ===

Yet let us not extend our discourse further. For this reason we pray, O all-holy Sovereign Lady, unto Thy compassionate heart, that Thy mercies may be received by Thy people and Thine inheritance: and stand Thou as an intercessor before God Who was born of Thee, that He may help us who are perishing utterly, that He may deliver us from incurable misfortunes!

Thou seest, O Sovereign Lady, with how many tears we are inundated: have mercy therefore, and cast us not off unto the end! Why, O Queen, dost Thou turn Thy face away, and forget our poverty and our affliction? Scatter the fear and trembling that lie upon us; quiet the wrath of God that hath been stirred up against us; subdue destruction; make peace among the strife and turmoil that exist in our midst; and grant tranquility and peace unto Thy servants, that having added these also unto Thy many benefactions, we may ever proclaim Thy miracles!

And having sailed thus far, let us lower the sails of our discourse, and anchor in the firm haven of silence.[^1421] Calling these things again to the remembrance of those assembled, let us not appear ungrateful concerning the benefaction of the Sovereign Lady, nor, having been benefited in deed, let us be slothful in offering thanksgiving in words; so that an increase of blessing may return unto us again. For if we are thankful for the benefits that have come upon us, we shall prepare for ourselves great boldness toward those things that are to come, and we shall obtain unutterable blessings, by the grace and love for mankind of our Lord Jesus Christ, unto Whom be glory and dominion, honor and worship, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*If the Apodosis of the Meeting of the Lord occurs on Cheesefare Saturday:*

On Cheesefare Friday evening: at **"Lord, I have cried"**, 3 stichera of the feast, and 3 of the Fathers. *Glory:* of the Fathers. *Both now:* of the feast. Prokeimenon and Prophecy of the day, and after it the prokeimenon: **"Let Israel hope in the Lord"**.

At the Aposticha, the Idiomelon of the Triodion for the day, twice, and the Martyrikon. *Glory:* of the Fathers. *Both now:* of the feast. Troparion of the Fathers. *Glory, both now:* of the feast.

At Matins, at **"God is the Lord"**, the Troparion of the feast, twice. *Glory:* of the Fathers. *Both now:* of the feast. After the Kathismata, the Sessional Hymns of the feast.

Canon of the feast with its Heirmos to 8, and of the Fathers to 6. Katavasia of the feast.

After Ode 3, the Kontakion and Ikos of the Fathers; and their Sessional Hymn, twice. *Glory, both now:* of the feast.

After Ode 6, the Kontakion and Ikos of the feast.

At Ode 9, we chant **"More honorable"**; the Exapostilarion of the feast. *Glory:* of the Fathers. *Both now:* of the feast.

At the Praises, 3 stichera of the feast, and 3 of the Fathers. *Glory:* of the Fathers. *Both now:* of the feast. Great Doxology; Troparion of the Fathers. *Glory, both now:* of the feast. And the usual procession into the narthex, and the First Hour, and the final dismissal.
