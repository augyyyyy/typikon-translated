On this day we celebrate the Akathist Hymn of our Most Holy Lady Theotokos, for the following reason:[^1149] While Heraclius was ruling the imperial realm of the Greeks, Khosrow, the Persian king, seeing the Greek scepter brought low by Phocas the tyrant emperor, sent one of his grandees named Sarbaros with many thousands of troops to subjugate all the eastern lands unto himself; for Khosrow had previously destroyed ten times ten thousand Christians, selling them to the Jews who bought them and put them to death. The chief grandee Sarbaros, having conquered all the East, reached even to the Golden City, which is now called Scutari. Emperor Heraclius, having become impoverished in gold, converted the sacred church vessels into gold coins with the promise to return them in greater and more complete measure; and sailing through the Black Sea with ships to the Persian lands, he laid them waste. Khosrow was utterly defeated by him along with his remaining troops; and Siroes, Khosrow's son, learning of this, rebelled against his father, seized the rule for himself, slew his father Khosrow, and formed an alliance with Emperor Heraclius. But the Chagan of Mysia and ruler of the Scythians, learning that the emperor had departed across the Pontic Sea, broke the peace with the Greeks, moved countless legions, and came from the western parts against Constantinople, crying out blasphemous words against God. Immediately the sea was filled with ships, and the land was full of countless foot-soldiers and horsemen. But Sergius, the Patriarch of Constantinople, greatly comforted the people, urging them not to lose heart or weaken, but from their soul to place all their hope in God and in His most pure Mother, the Theotokos. Likewise Bonus the Patrician, who was then governing the city, prepared what was needed to equip the warriors; for it behooves us also, with help from on high, to act fittingly. The Patriarch, bearing the divine icons of the Mother of God, along with the whole multitude, went in procession along the walls from above, giving them courage from thence. For while Sarbaros from the east and the Chagan from the west were advancing to burn the environs of the city, the Patriarch went along the walls carrying the Icon of Christ Not-Made-by-Hands, the precious and life-creating Wood of the Cross, and the venerable Robe of the Mother of God. The Chagan, a Scythian, attacked the walls of Constantinople by land with an innumerable multitude, fortified with strong siege weapons; and so great was their number that one Greek had to fight against ten Scythians. But the invincible Champion, through the few soldiers who were near Her church called Pigi [the Spring], destroyed the greater part of them. Hence the Greeks, taking courage and rejoicing, by the aid of their unconquerable Leader, the Mother of God, continually defeated them unto the end. And when the citizens looked toward reconciliation, they were rejected; for the Chagan answered them: "Be not deceived in the God in Whom you believe, for tomorrow by all means I shall take your city." Hearing this, the citizens stretched out their hands unto God. The Chagan and Sarbaros having agreed together, attacked the city by land and sea, seeking to work destruction by their wiles; but they were so defeated by the Greeks that the living were not sufficient to burn the dead. And their monoxyla [canoes], filled with armed men, in the gulf called the Golden Horn, near the church of the Mother of God at Blachernae, were suddenly struck by a violent tempest that fell upon the sea and tore it asunder, so that all the ships of the enemies were scattered. And one could see that most glorious and marvelous sight of the Most Pure Mother of God: all were cast upon the seashore at Blachernae. The people quickly opened the gates and slew them all together, and even the children and women fell upon them. Their commanders returned weeping and wailing together. Therefore the God-loving people of Constantinople, rendering thanks unto the Mother of God,

=== LEAF p405 ===
rendering thanks, sang unto Her an all-night and unseated song [the Akathist], as unto Her who had kept vigil over them and by supernatural power had wrought victory over their enemies. From that time forth, in remembrance of so great and supernatural a wonder, the Church received this feast, to offer it unto the Mother of God at this present season, wherein the victory also was won by the Mother of God. And it was called "unseated" [Akathistos] because the city choir and all the people then stood upright while singing it.[^1150]

After the passage of thirty-six years, in the reign of Constantine Pogonatus, the Hagarenes, leading a countless army, attacked Constantinople again; and warring against it for seven years, during which they wintered in the regions of Cyzicus, they lost many of their men. Then, giving up the war and returning with their army, when they were near the sea called Syllaeum, they were all drowned through the intercession of the most pure Mother of God.

And yet a third time, under Leo the Isaurian, the Hagarenes, in numbers far exceeding the former, first laid waste the Persian kingdom, then Egypt and Libya; and having overrun India, Ethiopia, and Spain, finally they waged war against the Imperial City itself, borne upon one thousand eight hundred ships. Having encircled it, they expected immediately to plunder it. But the sacred citizens, bearing about the precious Wood of the venerable and life-creating Cross and the venerable icon of the Mother of God Hodigitria, circled the wall, propitiating God with tears. Then it seemed good to the Hagarenes to divide themselves into two parts: some made war against the Bulgarians and there fell more than twenty thousand; while the others remained to take Constantinople, but were prevented by the chain that stretched from Galata unto the walls of the Imperial City. Having sailed up as far as a place called Sosthenes, there a north wind blew upon them, and many of their ships were shattered and destroyed; and those who remained fell into severe famine, so that they even tasted human flesh and kneaded dung and ate it. Then fleeing, when they reached the Aegean deep, all their light vessels and they themselves were delivered unto the depth of the sea; for a violent hailstorm fell from heaven, stirring up the sea, and melted the pitch of the ships. And so that countless multitude of ships perished, only three remaining to bring the tidings.

For the sake of all these supernatural wonders of the All-Pure Mother of God, we celebrate the present feast. And it was called "unseated" because all the people, standing upright on that night, sang the hymn unto the Mother of the Word; and whereas it is customary to sit during all others, during these hymns of the Mother of God we all listen standing upright.
