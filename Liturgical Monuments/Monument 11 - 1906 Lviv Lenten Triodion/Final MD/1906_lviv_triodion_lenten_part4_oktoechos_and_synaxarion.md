# PART IV: LENTEN OKTOECHOS SUPPLEMENT AND SYNAXARION READINGS (TRINITY HYMNS, STICHERA, LIFE OF ST. MARY OF EGYPT, AND TALE OF THE AKATHIST)

## LENTEN OKTOECHOS SUPPLEMENT: TRINITY HYMNS AND COMPUNCTIVE STICHERA

## Trinity Hymns in the Eight Tones[^1242]

*Sung in the eight Tones during the Holy Great Fast, and in the other fasts, when we do not sing "God is the Lord", nor a troparion, but "Alleluia" with its verses:*

*My spirit seeks Thee early in the night: and the rest.*

*Be it known that in the Trinity Hymns we say,*
*On Monday, at their conclusion, thus:*
By the intercessions of Thy bodiless hosts, have mercy on us!

*On Tuesday:*
By the prayers of Thy Forerunner, have mercy on us!

*On Wednesday:*
By the power of Thy Cross, preserve us, O Lord!

*Likewise also on Friday.*

*On Thursday:*
By the prayers of Thy holy Apostles and of the Hierarch Nicholas, have mercy on us!

*And at the other two Trinity Hymns, say as is written at their conclusions.*

*Trinity Hymns, Tone 1:*
Being elevated in mind through bodily representations of the Bodiless Powers unto that which is spiritual and immaterial, and receiving the radiance of the Tri-hypostatic Godhead through the Thrice-Holy Hymn, let us cry aloud in Cherubic manner unto the One God: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God! *(Add:)*[^1243]

*Glory:*
With all the Heavenly Hosts, let us cry aloud in Cherubic manner unto Him Who is in the highest, sending up the Thrice-Holy Praise: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God, by the prayers of all Thy saints have mercy on us!

*Both now:*
Having risen from sleep, we fall down before Thee, O Good One, and we cry aloud unto Thee the angelic hymn, O Mighty One: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God, through the Theotokos have mercy on us!

*Exaposteilarion, Tone 1:*
Thou that causest light to shine forth, O Lord, cleanse my soul from every sin: *(Add:)*

*And at the conclusions of the Photagogika:*
*On Monday, unto the first:*
By the intercessions of Thy bodiless hosts, and save me!

*On Tuesday:*
By the prayers of Thy Forerunner, O Lord, and save me!

*On Wednesday and Friday:*
By the power of Thy Cross, O Lord, and save me!

*On Thursday:*
By the prayers of Thine Apostles, O Lord, and of the Hierarch Nicholas, and save me!

*At the second verse, on "Glory", say always at the conclusion:*
By the prayers of Thy saints, O Lord, and save me!

*And at the third, on "Both now", say at the conclusion:*
By the prayers of the Theotokos, O Lord, and save me!

*[Ornamental Divider Band]*

*Trinity Hymns, Tone 2:*
Emulating the Higher Powers upon earth, we offer the song of victory unto Thee, O Good One: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God! *(Add:)*

*Glory:*
O Uncreated Nature, Creator of all, open our lips, that we may proclaim Thy praise, crying aloud: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God, by the prayers of all Thy saints have mercy on us!

*Both now:*
From bed and sleep Thou hast raised me up, O Lord; enlighten my mind and heart, and open my lips, that I may hymn Thee, O Holy Trinity: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God, through the Theotokos have mercy on us!

*Exaposteilarion, Tone 2:*
Send down Thy co-eternal Light, O Christ God, and enlighten the secret eyes of my heart, *(Add:)* and save me!

*Trinity Hymns, Tone 3:*
O consubstantial and undivided Trinity, Tri-hypostatic and co-eternal Unity, unto Thee as God we cry aloud the angelic hymn: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God! *(Add:)*

*Glory:*
Daring to glorify in Cherubic manner the unoriginate Father, the co-unoriginate Son, and the co-eternal Spirit, one Godhead, we say: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God, by the prayers of all Thy saints have mercy on us!

*Both now:*
Suddenly the Judge shall come, and the deeds of each shall be laid bare; but with fear we cry at midnight: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God, through the Theotokos have mercy on us!

*Exaposteilarion, Tone 3:*
Send down Thy light, O Christ God, and enlighten my heart: *(Add:)* and save me!

*[Ornamental Divider Band]*

*Trinity Hymns, Tone 4:*
Daring as mortals to offer the hymn of Thy spiritual servants, we say: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God! *(Add:)*

*Glory:*
Even as the angelic ranks now in heaven, and with fear the assemblies of men upon earth, we offer the song of victory unto Thee, O Good One: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God, by the prayers of all Thy saints have mercy on us!

*Both now:*
Daring to glorify in Cherubic manner Thine unoriginate Father, and Thee, O Christ God, and Thy Most Holy Spirit, we say: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God, through the Theotokos have mercy on us!

*Exaposteilarion, Tone 4:*
O Thou that causest light to shine upon Thy world, cleanse my soul that is in darkness from every sin: *(Add:)* and save me!

*[Ornamental Divider Band]*

*Trinity Hymns, Tone 5:*
It is the time for singing and the hour for prayer; fervently let us cry aloud unto the One God: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God! *(Add:)*

*Glory:*
Daring to depict Thy spiritual hosts, O unoriginate Trinity, with unworthy lips we cry aloud: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God, by the prayers of all Thy saints have mercy on us!

*Both now:*
O Thou Who wast contained in the virginal womb and wast not separated from the Father's bosom, receive us also, O Christ God, who cry unto Thee with the Angels: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God, through the Theotokos have mercy on us!

*Exaposteilarion, Tone 5:*
O Giver of Light, O Lord, send down Thy light, and enlighten my heart: *(Add:)* and save me!

*[Ornamental Divider Band]*

*Trinity Hymns, Tone 6:*
The Cherubim standing before Thee with fear, and the Seraphim trembling with dread, offer the Thrice-Holy Hymn with unceasing voice; with them also we sinners cry aloud: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God! *(Add:)*

*Glory:*
With bodiless lips and unceasing glorifications the six-winged cry aloud unto Thee the Thrice-Holy Hymn, O our God; and we upon earth send up praise unto Thee with unworthy lips: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God, by the prayers of all Thy saints have mercy on us!

*Both now:*
The Godhead of the Trinitarian Unity we glorify in unconfused union, and we cry aloud the angelic hymn: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God, through the Theotokos have mercy on us!

*Exaposteilarion, Tone 6: on Monday:*
By the intercessions of Thy bodiless hosts, O Lord, send down Thy co-eternal Light upon our souls!

*Here in the Sixth Tone, at the beginning of the Exaposteilarion, instead of "By the intercessions of Thy bodiless hosts, O Lord: and the rest,"*
*If it be Tuesday, it is said thus:*
By the prayers of Thy Forerunner, O Lord: *and recite the rest unto the end.*

*On Wednesday and Friday, instead of "By the intercessions of Thy bodiless hosts, O Lord,"*
*At the beginning say thus:*
By the power of Thy Cross, O Lord, send down Thy co-eternal Light upon our souls!

*And on Thursday thus:*
By the prayers of Thy holy Apostles, O Lord, and of the Hierarch Nicholas, send down Thy co-eternal Light upon our souls!

*At the second, on "Glory", say at the beginning of the Exaposteilarion:*
By the prayers of all the saints, O Lord, send down Thy co-eternal Light upon our souls!

*And at the third, on "Both now", say at the beginning of the Exaposteilarion thus:*
By the prayers of the Theotokos, O Lord, send down Thy co-eternal Light upon our souls!

*[Ornamental Divider Band]*

*Trinity Hymns, Tone 7:*
Hymned by the Higher Power in Cherubic manner, and worshipped with divine glory in Angelic manner, receive us also upon earth who send up praise unto Thee with unworthy lips: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God! *(Add:)*

*Glory:*
Putting away sluggishness like sleep, O my soul, show forth amendment unto praise before the Judge, and cry aloud with fear: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God, by the prayers of all Thy saints have mercy on us!

*Both now:*
Sending up the Seraphic Thrice-Holy Praise unto the Inaccessible Godhead in the Trinity as Unity, with fear let us cry aloud: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God, through the Theotokos have mercy on us!

*Exaposteilarion, Tone 7:*
Raise me up, O Lord, unto Thy hymnody, and teach me to do Thy will, O Holy One, *(Add:)* and save me!

*[Ornamental Divider Band]*

*Trinity Hymns, Tone 8:*
Having our hearts on high, let us emulate the angelic rank, and with fear let us fall down before the Judge, seeking after the song of victory: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God! *(Add:)*

*Glory:*
Not daring to gaze upon Thee, the Cherubim fly, crying aloud with exultation the divine song of the Thrice-Holy voice; with them also we cry unto Thee: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God, by the prayers of all Thy saints have mercy on us!

*Both now:*
Bent down by the multitude of our transgressions, and not daring to look up unto the height of heaven, bowing soul and body, with the Angels we cry the hymn unto Thee: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, our God, through the Theotokos have mercy on us!

*Exaposteilarion, Tone 8:*
Being Light, O Christ, enlighten me through Thyself: *(Add:)* and save me!

*COMPUNCTIVE STICHERA*[^1244]

*Chanted on Sunday evening, at "Lord, I have cried": and the Sessional Hymns, with the Martyria, chanted at Matins; unto this also the Praises Stichera, with those at the Aposticha, chanted on Saturdays, from the Oktoechos, according to the Tone.*

*At "Lord, I have cried", Penitential Stichera, Tone 1:*
Great is the abyss of my transgressions, O Savior, and grievously am I submerged by mine iniquities; reach out Thy hand unto me, as unto Peter, and save me, O God, and have mercy on me!

Seeing that in wicked thoughts and in deeds Thou hast condemned me, O Savior, grant me the thought of repentance, O God, that I may cry unto Thee: Save me, O Good Benefactor, and have mercy on me!

Another age awaits thee, O my soul, and the Judge desires to expose thy secret and grievous deeds; remain not therefore in the things of this world, but anticipate the judgment, crying aloud unto the Judge: O God, cleanse me and save me!

Despise me not, O my Savior, who am held fast by sinful sloth; raise up my thoughts unto repentance, and show me to be an approved laborer of Thy vineyard, granting me the reward of the eleventh hour, and great mercy!

*Then three from the Triodion, and three for the Saint from the Menaion.*

*ON MONDAY AT MATINS*
*After the first stichology, Compunctive Sessional Hymns, Tone 1:*
In iniquities was I conceived, prodigal that I am, and I dare not look up unto the height of heaven; yet daring upon Thy love for mankind, I cry: O God, cleanse me and save me!

*Verse: O Lord, rebuke me not in Thine anger, nor chasten me in Thy wrath.*[^1245]
If the righteous scarcely be saved, where shall I appear, a sinner? I have not borne the burden and the heat of the day; yet number me with the laborers of the eleventh hour, O God, and save me!

*Glory, both now, Theotokion:*
Beholding in thee the miracle of miracles, O Full of Grace, the creation rejoices; for thou hast conceived without seed, and hast given birth ineffably unto Him Whom the ranks of Angels cannot behold: Him, O Theotokos, entreat for our souls!

*At the Sixth Ode, the Martyricon is said, if the Menaion hath no kontakion; but if it hath one, this is said together with the sessional hymns.*
*Martyricon, Tone 1:*
With the praise of suffering and with the honor of crowns, the glorious passion-bearers have been clothed by Thee, O Lord; for by the endurance of wounds they vanquished the lawless, and by divine power received victory from heaven: by their prayers grant us, O God, Thy great mercy!

*Tone 1.*

*ON TUESDAY AT MATINS*

*After the first stichology, Compunctive Sessional Hymns, Tone 1:*

Haste Thou to open Thy Fatherly embrace unto me, for prodigally have I spent my life, looking upon the inexhaustible wealth of Thy compassions, O Savior; despise not now my heart grown poor, for unto Thee, O Lord, in compunction do I cry: I have sinned against heaven and before Thee!

*Verse: O Lord, rebuke me not in Thine anger, nor chasten me in Thy wrath.*[^1246]

Thy judgment seat is fearful, and Thy judgment is just, and my deeds are grievous; but do Thou Thyself, O Merciful One, anticipate and save me, and deliver me from torment; rescue me, O Master, from the portion of the goats, and count me worthy to stand at Thy right hand, O most righteous Judge!

*Glory, both now, Theotokion:*

Having conceived without being consumed the Fire of the Godhead, and having given birth without seed unto the Lord, the Fountain of life: O Full of Grace, Theotokos, save those who magnify thee!

*At the Sixth Ode, Martyricon:*

As noble soldiers, of one mind having believed, not having feared the threats of the tyrants, O holy ones, ye approached eagerly unto Christ, taking up the precious Cross, and having finished your course, ye received victory from heaven: glory to Him Who strengthened you; glory to Him Who crowned you; glory to Him Who through you works healings for all!

*ON WEDNESDAY AT MATINS*

*After the first stichology, Cross Sessional Hymns, Tone 1:*

The weapon of the Cross in battles was shown forth of old unto the pious Emperor Constantine as an invincible victory over enemies for the sake of the faith, at which the adverse powers tremble; this has become salvation unto the faithful, and the boast of Paul.

*Verse: Exalt ye the Lord our God, and worship the footstool of His feet, for He is holy.*[^1247]

*The same verse is also said on Friday:*

Before the Tree of Thy Cross do we bow down, O Lover of mankind, for upon it wast Thou nailed, O Life of all; Thou hast opened paradise, O Savior, unto the thief who approached Thee with faith, and hast accounted that penitent worthy of delight, who confessed unto Thee: "Remember me, O Lord!" Receive also us as that thief, who cry aloud: We all have sinned; in Thy tender compassion despise us not!

*Glory, both now, Stavrotheotokion:*

Beholding upon the Tree the Lamb, enduringly crucified with two thieves, Thee, O Word, and pierced in the ribs with a spear, the Ewe-lamb cried out, weeping with motherly voice: What is this strange and terrible mystery, O my Savior? How does a tomb cover the uncontainable God? Unspeakable is that which is accomplished; forsake me not who bore Thee, O my sweetest Son!

*At the Sixth Ode, Martyricon:*

By the sufferings of the saints which they endured for Thy sake, be Thou entreated, O Lord, and heal all our sicknesses, O Lover of mankind, we pray Thee!

*Sessional Hymns of the Oktoechos*

*ON THURSDAY AT MATINS*

*After the first stichology, Apostles' Sessional Hymns, Tone 1:*

By the net of the word, casting apart the rhetorical intricacies of the philosophers with the rod of the Cross, the fishermen enlightened the nations in piety to glorify Thee, the true God; therefore unto Thee Who didst strengthen them do we cry aloud: Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and glory to the consubstantial Spirit; glory unto Him Who through them enlightened the world!

*Verse: Their sound has gone forth into all the earth, and their words unto the ends of the world.*[^1248]

O most wise fishers of the universe, having received mercy from God, pray now for us who cry aloud: O Lord, save the Emperor,[^1249] and Thy city, and deliver our souls through the Apostles from the afflictions that encompass us!

*Glory, both now, Theotokion:*

Him Who in heaven is without a mother, beyond thought and hearing, upon earth hast thou borne without a father; Him, O Theotokos, entreat for our souls!

*At the Sixth Ode, Martyricon:*

O martyrs of Christ, we all entreat you, for they pray for our salvation; and let us all approach unto them with faith; these pour forth the grace of healings, these drive away the legions of demons, as guardians of the faith.

*ON FRIDAY AT MATINS*

*After the first stichology, Cross Sessional Hymns, Tone 1:*

When Thou wast crucified, O Christ, tyranny was destroyed, and the enemy's power was trampled down; for neither an Angel nor a man, but Thou Thyself, O Lord, hast saved us: glory to Thee!

*Verse: Exalt ye the Lord our God, and worship the footstool of His feet, for He is holy.*

The weapon of the Cross in battles was shown forth of old unto the pious Emperor Constantine as an invincible victory over enemies for the sake of the faith, at which the adverse powers tremble; this has become salvation unto the faithful, and the boast of Paul.

*Glory, both now, Stavrotheotokion:*

Having thine intercession, O Most Pure One, and by thy prayers being delivered from grievous ills, guarded everywhere by the Cross of thy Son, as is our duty we all piously magnify thee!

*At the Sixth Ode, Martyricon:*

With the praise of suffering:

*Tone 1.*

*ON SATURDAY AT MATINS*

*At the Praises, Stichera of the Martyrs, Tone 1:*

Passion-bearers of Christ, come, all ye peoples, let us honor them with songs and spiritual hymns: the luminaries of the world and preachers of the faith, ever-flowing fountains whence healings flow forth unto the faithful; by their prayers, O Christ our God, grant peace unto Thy world, and unto our souls great mercy!

These soldiers of the Great King resisted the commands of the tyrants, valiantly took no account of tortures, and trampled down all delusion; having been worthily crowned, they ask of the Savior peace for our souls, and great mercy!

Neither tribulation, nor distress, nor famine, nor persecution, nor wounds, nor rage of wild beasts, nor sword, nor threatening fire were able to separate you from God, O all-praised martyrs; but through love toward Him, having striven as if in bodies not your own, ye forgot nature and despised death; therefore also worthily did ye receive the reward of your labors, having become heirs of the kingdom of heaven: pray ye unto Christ for our souls!

Rejoice in the Lord, O martyrs, for ye have fought the good fight; ye resisted kings, and vanquished tyrants; ye feared not fire nor sword, nor savage beasts that devoured your bodies; sending up hymnody unto Christ with the Angels, ye received crowns from heaven: ask that peace be granted unto the world, and unto our souls great mercy!

*Glory, for the Departed:*

In deed, O my Savior, dost Thou show forth that Thou art the Resurrection of all; by a word, O Word, Thou didst raise Lazarus from the dead, when the dead arose from their tombs, and the gates of Hades were shattered; then unto men death was shown to be sleep; but do Thou Who camest for the salvation of Thy creation and not for condemnation, give rest unto those whom Thou hast chosen, as the Lover of mankind!

*Both now, Theotokion:*

Rejoice from us, O holy Theotokos Virgin: pure vessel of the whole universe, unquenchable lamp, tabernacle of the Uncontainable One, undefiled church; rejoice, from whom was born the Lamb of God, Who takes away the sins of the whole world!

*At the Aposticha, Stichera for the Departed:*

*Tone 1, Podoben: "O All-Praised Martyrs" (Прехва́льнїи мч҃ницы):*

Unto Thee, O Savior, do we pray: vouchsafe those who have departed to partake of Thy sweetness, and settle them in the mansions of the righteous, together with Thy saints, and in heavenly habitations, in Thy tender compassion overlooking their transgressions, and granting them rest!

*Verse: Blessed are they whom Thou hast chosen and taken to Thyself, O Lord.*[^1251]

Higher than visible things are Thy promises, O Savior, which eye has not seen, nor ear heard, neither have they ever entered into the heart of man;[^1252] we pray Thee therefore, O Master, count those who have departed unto Thee worthy to obtain Thy sweet communion, and grant them life eternal!

*Sessional Hymns of the Oktoechos*

*Verse: Their souls shall dwell among good things.*[^1253]

Manifestly rejoicing in Thy Cross, in hope Thy servants have departed unto Thee, O Lover of mankind; unto them do Thou now grant Thy Cross and the Blood poured out for the life of the world as the redemption of their transgressions, in Thy tenderheartedness pardoning their falls into sin, and enlightening them with the light of Thy countenance!

*Verse: And their memory is from generation to generation.*[^1254]

No one is sinless, no one among men has ever been, save Thee alone, O Only Immortal One; therefore place Thy servants (as Thou art a compassionate God) in the light, together with Thine angelic choirs, in Thy tender compassion overlooking their iniquities, and granting them forgiveness!

*Glory, both now, Theotokion:*

Entreat Christ, Thine Offspring, O Virgin Mother, to grant forgiveness of transgressions unto thy servants who have piously proclaimed thee as Theotokos and been taught by the true word; account them worthy of the joy of the radiance of the saints in Thy kingdom!

*ON SUNDAY AT VESPERS*

*At "Lord, I have cried", Penitential Stichera, Tone 2:*

I have sinned against Thee, O Savior, as the prodigal son; receive me, O Father, as I repent, and have mercy on me, O God!

I cry unto Thee, O Christ Savior, with the voice of the publican: Cleanse me as him, and have mercy on me, O God!

Thinking upon the improper evils which I have committed, I flee unto Thy compassions, resembling the publican, and the harlot who wept, and the prodigal son; therefore also I fall down before Thee, O Merciful One: before Thou judgest me, spare me, O my God, and have mercy on me!

Overlook mine iniquities, O Lord Who wast born of the Virgin, and cleanse my heart, making it a temple for Thy Holy Spirit; cast me not away from Thy presence, Thou Who hast boundless and great mercy!

*Then three from the Triodion, and three for the Saint from the Menaion.*

*ON MONDAY AT MATINS*

*After the first stichology, Compunctive Sessional Hymns, Tone 2:*

As the waves of the sea have mine iniquities risen up against me, and like a small ship in the deep, I alone am tossed by a tempest of many transgressions; but guide me, O Lord, by repentance into the calm haven, and save me!

*Verse: O Lord, rebuke me not in Thine anger [nor chasten me in Thy wrath]:*[^1255]

I am a barren tree, O Lord, bringing forth no fruit of compunction whatsoever; and I fear the felling, and am terrified of that unquenchable fire; therefore I pray Thee: before that extremity, turn me and save me!

*Tone 2.*

*Glory, both now, Theotokion:*

Being the Fountain of tenderheartedness, count us worthy of compassion, O all-hymned Theotokos; look down upon a people that has sinned; show forth as ever thy power; for hoping in thee, we cry aloud unto thee: "Rejoice!" even as of old did Gabriel, the supreme commander of the bodiless hosts.

*At the Sixth Ode, Martyricon:*

Having Thee, Who dost clothe heaven with clouds, as their raiment in the world, the holy ones endured the torments of the lawless, and abolished the delusion of idols; by their prayers deliver us also from the invisible foe, O Savior, and save us!

*ON TUESDAY AT MATINS*

*After the first stichology, Compunctive Sessional Hymns, Tone 2:*

"Have mercy on me," said David, and I also cry unto Thee: I have sinned, O Savior; having cleansed my sins by repentance, have mercy on me!

*Verse: O Lord, rebuke me not in Thine anger [nor chasten me in Thy wrath]:*

Have mercy on me, O God, have mercy on me! For two sins did David weep:[^1256] yet for ten thousand transgressions do I cry unto Thee; he drenched his bed with tears, while I have not even a single drop; despairing, I pray: Have mercy on me, O God, according to Thy great mercy!

*Glory, both now, Theotokion:*

O Theotokos, despise me not who need thy protection; for in thee has my soul hoped: have mercy on me!

*At the Sixth Ode, Martyricon:*

Thou Who didst enlighten Thy holy ones more than gold, and didst glorify Thy venerable ones, in that Thou art Good: being entreated by them, O Christ God, bring peace unto our life, as the Lover of mankind, and set forth our prayer as incense, O Thou Who alone restest in the saints!

*ON WEDNESDAY AT MATINS*

*After the first stichology, Cross Sessional Hymns, Tone 2:*

The life-creating Cross of Thy goodness, which Thou hast granted unto us who are unworthy, O Lord, we offer unto Thee in prayer: save the Emperor, and Thy city which prays unto Thee, through the Theotokos, O only Lover of mankind!

*Verse: Exalt ye the Lord our God, and worship the footstool of His feet, for He is holy.*

Even as the enemy took Adam captive through the tree of eating, O Lord, so likewise hast Thou taken captive the enemy through the Tree of the Cross and by Thy Passion; for unto this didst Thou come as the second Adam, to seek out the straying and make alive him that was slain: O God, glory to Thee!

*Sessional Hymns of the Oktoechos*

*Glory, both now, Stavrotheotokion:*

Thy Virgin Mother, O Christ, beholding Thee stretched out dead upon the Tree, weeping bitterly, said: What is this fearful mystery, O my Son? Thou Who grantest eternal life unto all, how dost Thou die of Thine own will upon the Cross an ignominious death?

*At the Sixth Ode, Martyricon:*

O passion-bearers of the Lord, blessed is the earth that was watered by your blood, and holy are the dwellings that received your bodies; for in the stadium ye vanquished the enemy, and with boldness proclaimed Christ; entreat Him as the Good One, we pray, that our souls may be saved!

*ON THURSDAY AT MATINS*

*After the first stichology, Apostles' Sessional Hymns, Tone 2:*

Thou Who didst make the fishermen wiser than rhetoricians, and didst send them as preachers unto all the earth by Thine unspeakable love for mankind, O Christ God: through them confirm Thy Church, and send down Thy blessing upon the faithful, O only Merciful One and Lover of mankind!

*Verse: Their sound has gone forth into all the earth, and their words unto the ends of the world.*

Having caught the nations, the fishermen, and taught the ends of the earth to worship Thee through Thine unspeakable love for mankind, O Christ God: through them confirm Thy Church, and send down Thy blessing upon the faithful, O Thou Who alone restest in the saints!

*Glory, both now, Theotokion:*

Thee do we magnify, O Theotokos, crying aloud: Rejoice, Cloud of the unwaning Light, who didst bear in thy womb the Lord of glory!

*At the Sixth Ode, Martyricon:*

Apostles, martyrs, and prophets, hierarchs, venerable ones, and righteous, who have fought the good fight and kept the faith, having boldness toward the Savior, entreat Him as the Good One for us, we pray, that our souls may be saved!

*ON FRIDAY AT MATINS*

*After the first stichology, Cross Sessional Hymns, Tone 2:*

We worship Thine immaculate icon, O Good One, asking forgiveness of our transgressions, O Christ God; for of Thine own will Thou wast pleased to ascend the Cross in the flesh, that Thou mightest deliver those whom Thou hast created from the bondage of the enemy; therefore with thanksgiving we cry aloud unto Thee: Thou hast filled all things with joy, O our Savior, Who camest to save the world!

*Verse: Exalt ye the Lord our God, and worship the footstool of His feet, for He is holy.*

Thou hast worked salvation in the midst of the earth, O Christ God; upon the Cross Thou didst stretch out Thine all-pure hands, gathering together all the nations, who cry: O Lord, glory to Thee!

*Tone 2.*

*Glory, both now, Stavrotheotokion:*

Most glorified art thou, O Theotokos Virgin, we hymn thee; for by the Cross of thy Son Hades was overthrown, and death was put to death; we that were dead have risen, and have been accounted worthy of life; we have received paradise, our ancient delight; therefore with thanksgiving we give glory, as unto Christ our mighty God, and the only Plenteous in mercy!

*At the Sixth Ode, Martyricon:*

Having been founded upon the immovable rock, O holy ones, ye fought valiantly against the tyrants, and obtained crowns of martyrdom; through them, O God, save us!

*ON SATURDAY AT MATINS*

*At the Praises, Stichera of the Martyrs, Tone 2:*

Having suffered for Christ even unto death, O passion-bearing martyrs, your souls indeed are in the hand of God in heaven, and throughout all the world your relics are venerated; hierarchs and kings bow down, and all the peoples rejoicing, with one accord cry out: Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of His venerable ones![^1257]

Every city and country honors your relics, O passion-bearing martyrs; for having suffered lawfully, ye received crowns from heaven; and therefore are ye the boast of hierarchs, the victory of kings, and the beauty of the churches.

Woe is me! Unto whom have I become like? Unto the barren fig tree,[^1281] and I fear the curse together with the cut of the axe; but O heavenly Husbandman, Christ God, make my frozen soul fruitful, and as the prodigal son, receive me and have mercy on me![^1282]

The strange mystery of the Virgin was shown forth unto the world as saving; for of her wast Thou born without seed, and in flesh wast Thou revealed without corruption, O Joy of all: Lord, glory to Thee!

*At the Sixth Ode, Martyricon:*

Thou Who hast granted us the wonders of Thy holy martyrs as an impregnable wall, O Christ God: by their prayers destroy the counsels of the heathen, and strengthen the scepters of the realm, as Thou alone art Good and the Lover of mankind!

At the righteous achievements of the holy martyrs, the heavenly powers were astonished above measure: how in mortal flesh, having fought valiantly by the power of the Cross, they invisibly conquered the bodiless enemy, and they pray unto the Lord that our souls may find mercy!

*Verse: Exalt ye the Lord our God [and worship the footstool of His feet, for He is holy]:*

The Tree of Thy Cross, O our Savior, was shown forth as saving unto the world; for being willingly nailed upon it, Thou didst deliver the earth-born from the curse, O Joy of all: Lord, glory to Thee!

*Glory, both now, Stavrotheotokion:*

Beholding Thee upon the Cross, O Christ, willingly hanging between thieves, Thy Mother, torn in maternal compassion, said: O sinless Son, how art Thou wrongfully nailed to the Cross as an evildoer, desiring to quicken the human race, as Thou art Most Good?

Clothed with the shield of faith, and having strengthened yourselves with the sign of the Cross, ye manfully gave yourselves over to torments, and abolished the pride and deceit of the devil, O Thy holy ones, O Lord; by their prayers, as Almighty God, send down peace upon the world, and great mercy unto our souls!

*Glory, both now, Theotokion:*

Rejoice, impassable gate of the Lord! Rejoice, rampart and protection of those who run to thee! Rejoice, haven unassailed by storms, who knewest not wedlock, who didst bear in the flesh thy Creator and God; cease not to intercede for those who hymn and worship thy childbearing!

*At the Praises, Martyrica, Tone 5:*

Blessed is the army of the heavenly King! For even though the passion-bearers were earthly-born, yet they hastened to attain the angelic dignity, taking no heed of bodies; and because of their sufferings they were counted worthy of the honor of the bodiless powers; therefore by their prayers, O Lord, save our souls!

Thy passion-bearers, O Lord, having made themselves like unto the angelic ranks, endured torments as though they were bodiless; and having unanimous hope, the enjoyment of the promised good things, by their prayers, O Christ God, have mercy on us!

The holy martyrs, having contended on earth, endured frost, and surrendered themselves to fire; water received them, and theirs is the voice: "We went through fire and water, and Thou broughtest us out into a place of rest";[^1292] by their prayers, O God, have mercy on us!

Being in the midst of torments, the holy ones cried aloud rejoicing: These things are our merchandise unto eternity! In place of the wounds inflicted on our bodies, radiant raiment shall blossom forth for us at the resurrection; instead of dishonor, crowns; instead of prison bonds, paradise; and instead of condemnation with evildoers, life with the Angels; by their prayers, O Lord, save our souls!

*Glory, for the Departed:*

Thou Who didst create me, O Lord, and didst lay Thy hand upon me, and gavest commandment unto me, saying: "Unto the earth shalt thou go again,"[^1293] direct me into Thy straight path, forgiving me my transgressions; pardon me and save me as I pray, as the Lover of mankind!

*Both now, Theotokion:*

We bless thee, O Virgin Theotokos, for from thee shone forth the Sun of righteousness, Christ,[^1294] Who hath great mercy!

With the light of Thy countenance, O Christ, as the Compassionate One, enlighten those who have departed; settle them in a green place, beside the waters of Thy pure and divine rest, in the longed-for bosom of Abraham our forefather,[^1295] where Thy light shines purely, and the fountains of goodness pour forth; where the choirs of all the righteous dance clearly, rejoicing in Thy goodness; with them rank Thy servants, granting them great mercy!

*Verse: Blessed are they whom Thou hast chosen and received, O Lord.*[^1296]

Vouchsafe, O Compassionate One, that those who have departed from temporal things unto Thee, the Master of all and our God, may sing with unceasing voices and glorify Thy dominion, granting them to be enlightened by Thy beauty, and to receive and purely enjoy sweet and fair communion with Thee; where Angels dance around Thy throne, and the ranks of the saints stand rejoicing; with them grant rest unto Thy servants, and great mercy!

Where is the choir of Prophets, the ranks of Apostles and martyrs, and all who from the beginning have been justified by Thy saving Passion and blood, by which Thou didst redeem captive man; there give rest with faith unto those who have fallen asleep, as the Lover of mankind, forgiving their transgressions; for Thou alone in truth didst live without sin upon earth, Thou alone art free among the dead;[^1298] therefore grant rest unto Thy servants, and great mercy!

*Glory, both now, Theotokion:*

From the law of sin hast thou freed us who were enslaved, O Lady, having conceived in thy womb Christ the Lawgiver and King, O only Virgin Mother, by Whose gift and grace we are justified; Whom do thou now entreat, that He may write the souls of those who acknowledge thee as the Mother of God in the Book of Life;[^1299] that being saved through thine intercession, O all-immaculate one, we may obtain the beloved redemption of thy Son, worshipping Him Who grants great mercy unto the world!

At Thy dread coming, O Christ, may we not hear: "I know you not";[^1300] for in Thee, O Savior, have we placed our hope; and even though through our negligence we kept not Thy commandments, yet spare our souls, we pray Thee!

I have not acquired repentance, nor yet tears; therefore I pray Thee, O Christ God, before the end turn me, and grant me compunction, that I may be delivered from torment!

Finding me stripped of virtues, the enemy wounded me with the arrow of sin; but Thou, as Physician of souls and bodies, heal the wounds of my soul, O God, and have mercy on me!

Light is ever with the righteous;[^1303] for the holy ones, having been enlightened by Thee, shine continually as luminaries, having quenched the lamp of the ungodly; by their prayers, O our Savior, Thou dost enlighten my lamp, O Lord, and save me!

Thy Cross, O Lord, is sanctified; for in it healings are bestowed upon those ailing in sins; falling down before Thee through it, have mercy on us!

## The Life and Manner of Living of our Venerable Mother Mary of Egypt

*Written by the Most Holy Sophronius, Patriarch of Jerusalem.*[^1378]

It is good to conceal the secret of a king, but it is glorious to proclaim the works of God! For so said the Angel unto Tobit, after the glorious restoration of his sight following his blindness.[^1379] For not to keep the secrets of a king is perilous and a cause of stumbling, whereas to pass over in silence the all-glorious works of God brings peril unto the soul. Therefore, I also fear to keep silent concerning the works of God, remembering the punishment of that servant who received the Lord's talent and hid it in the earth, and made no gain therewith.[^1380] Having heard a sacred narrative, I can in no wise conceal it; and let none of you disbelieve me upon hearing these writings, nor think that I speak in pride while marveling at this great miracle; far be it from me to speak falsely concerning holy things! And if there be certain persons who read these books and, marveling at the loftiness of the account, refuse to believe, may the mercy of the Lord be upon them; for they, considering only human weakness, count as unacceptable the things spoken by us concerning men. Now, therefore, it is fitting for me to begin this narrative and most marvelous event which took place in our generation.

There was a certain elder in one of the monasteries of Palestine, adorned in manner of life and in word, raised from his very swaddling clothes in monastic customs, and faithful; and the name of that elder was Zosimas. And let no one suppose that this man was that Zosimas the heretic, even though the two shared a single common name;[^1381] for this righteous Zosimas practiced every form of fasting and ascetic labor, and kept all the traditions, and while doing all this, that elder never grew slack in the meditation of the divine words; whether lying down or rising up, holding his work in his hands or partaking of food—if indeed one ought to call food that of which the elder partook—he had one unceasing work, which was to chant psalms continually. For from his very infancy he had been entrusted to the monastery, and he completed up to his fiftieth year therein.

And while that elder was living thus in the monastery, he reasoned within himself, saying: "Is there then a monk upon the earth who can show me a form of ascetic life which I have not accomplished? Can a man be found in the wilderness who surpasses me in deed?" And as the elder pondered thus, an Angel appeared unto him and said: "O Zosimas, nobly indeed hast thou struggled so far as a man is able, yet no man is perfect. But that thou mayest understand how many other ways of salvation there are, go forth out of thy land, like Abraham out of the house of his father,[^1382] and go unto the monastery that lies beside the Jordan."

Immediately the elder went forth from his monastery, and followed the one speaking unto him; and being led by the will of God, he arrived at the monastery beside the Jordan. And having knocked at the gate, word was brought to the abbot; and entering in, Zosimas made a prostration according to the monastic custom. And the abbot questioned him: "Whence art thou, brother, and for what cause hast thou come unto us poor old men?" Zosimas answered: "Inquire not of me whence I have come; but I have come for the sake of spiritual profit, for I have heard of your great and praiseworthy rule, which is able to lead souls unto Christ our God." And the abbot said unto him: "God alone, O brother, Who heals the human race—may He Himself teach both thee and us His will, and guide thee to perform all things that are profitable."

And when the abbot had spoken these things unto Zosimas, the elder bowed down, offered a prayer, saying "Amen," and remained in that monastery. And he beheld there monks shining in actions and deeds, and fervent in spirit; their chanting was unceasing, their standing lasted all through the night, manual labor was ever in their hands, and psalms were in their mouths; there was no idle word among them, and their sole care was to count themselves dead in body; their nourishment was the words of God, and they sustained the body with bread and water. Seeing these things, Zosimas marveled, and strove eagerly toward the course set before him.

And when many days had passed, the season of the Holy and Great Fast drew near; the gates of the monastery were kept locked and were never opened, save only when one of the brethren went out for some necessity, for the place was solitary, and not only untrodden, but unknown to people of the world. Now this was the rule handed down in that monastery, for the sake of which God had brought Zosimas thither: On the First Sunday of the Fast, the presbyter celebrated the Divine Liturgy, and all partook of the Most Pure Body and Blood of Christ our God, and tasted a little food; afterward they gathered in the church, and having offered lengthy prayer and genuflections, the elders kissed one another and the abbot, and having received a blessing, they opened the monastery gates, chanting in unison: "The Lord is my light and my Savior; whom shall I fear? The Lord is the defender of my life; of whom shall I be afraid?"[^1383] And chanting the rest of that psalm, they all went forth, leaving one or two brethren behind—not to guard the monastery, for there was nothing therein to be stolen by thieves, but so that the church might not remain without divine service.

And each carried provisions for himself, according as each was able and wished: one took a little bread, another figs, another dates, another pulse soaked in water, and another nothing at all save his own body and the ragged garments which he wore; and whenever their body was in need, they fed upon the herbs and plants of the wilderness. Thus they crossed the Jordan and separated far from one another, and none knew how his neighbor fasted or how he labored in asceticism. And if anyone happened to catch sight of his brother walking toward him, he immediately turned aside in another direction, and lived alone by himself, chanting continually unto God.

Having thus completed the entire Fast, they returned to the monastery on the Sunday before the Resurrection of Christ, on which the Church celebrates the Feast of Palms;[^1384] and each returned bearing the fruits of his own labor, having his own conscience as witness of what he had accomplished, and no one questioned another as to how he had labored; for such was the rule of that monastery.

Then Zosimas also, according to the custom of the monastery, crossed the Jordan, carrying a little food for the needs of the body and the garments which he wore; and he fulfilled his customary rule of prayer while walking through the wilderness, partaking of food when necessity required, and sleeping a little while lying upon the ground; and rising very early again, he pursued his course. And he longed greatly to penetrate into the innermost wilderness, as he said, hoping that he might find at least one holy father dwelling therein and fasting; and desire was added unto his desire.

After walking for seven days, he halted a little from his journey, praying at the time of the sixth hour, and turning toward the east, he offered the customary prayers; for he used to pause a little from his journey and rest at each canonical hour, chanting and making prostrations. And while he stood chanting, he saw to his right side a shadow, as if of a human body; at first he was afraid, supposing it to be a demonic apparition, and trembling, he signed himself with the sign of the Cross; and having cast off fear, he remained without dread, for he was already concluding his prayer. Turning then his eyes toward the south, he saw someone walking, unclothed in body, and darkened in appearance from the burning of the sun; and the hair upon her head was white as wool, and short, reaching only down to the neck.

Seeing this, Zosimas rejoiced over that remarkable sight, and began to run in the direction where he saw the figure, rejoicing with great joy; for during all those days he had seen no human form, nor bird, nor beast, nor creeping thing. When that figure saw Zosimas approaching from afar, she began to run and flee into the inner wilderness. And Zosimas, as if forgetting his old age and the fatigue of his journey, ran swiftly, wishing to overtake the one fleeing. He pursued, and she fled; yet swifter was the running of Zosimas than of the one fleeing. And when he drew near enough that his voice could be heard, Zosimas began to cry aloud with tears in these words: "Why dost thou flee from me, a sinful old man, O servant of the true God, for Whose sake thou dwellest in this wilderness? Wait for me, who am sinful and unworthy and weak! Halt, and bestow upon me, an old man, thy prayer and blessing, for the sake of God, Who never rejects anyone!"

While Zosimas spoke these things with tears, they were running near to one another and came to a dry torrent bed—though I do not think a torrent ever flowed there. When she reached that place, the fleeing one descended to the other side; and Zosimas, exhausted by old age and unable to run any further, stood on his side of the torrent bed, and added tears unto tears, and lamentation unto lamentation. Then the fleeing figure called out with a voice unto him, saying: "Abba Zosimas, I cannot turn and show myself before thy face, for I am a woman without clothing, as thou seest; but if thou wishest to bestow thy prayer upon me, a sinful woman, cast me the cloak which thou wearest, that I may cover my feminine weakness, and turn toward thee, and receive thy prayer."

Then Zosimas was struck with fear in body and amazed in mind upon hearing himself called by name, and said within himself: "If this woman were not clairvoyant, she would not have called me by my name!" And he quickly did as he had been told, and taking off the old and tattered cloak which he wore, he cast it toward her while turning his face away. Having taken it, she girded herself therewith and covered her body, and turning toward Zosimas, she said unto him: "Why did it seem good to thee, Abba Zosimas, to wish to see a sinful woman? What dost thou wish to hear or learn from me, that thou hast not shrunk from undertaking so great a labor?"

And bending her knees to the ground, she asked a blessing from him; and he likewise bowed down to the ground, and both lay upon the earth asking a blessing from one another, and nothing was heard spoken by either of them save only: "Bless!" And when a long time had passed, the woman said unto Zosimas: "It befits thee to offer the prayer, for thou art honored with the dignity of the presbyterate, and for many years hast stood before the holy altar, and hast oftentimes offered the Holy Gifts unto God."[^1385]

These words cast Zosimas into aún greater awe, and the elder trembled, covered with sweat, and groaned, his voice failing him. And Zosimas said unto her with shortened and exhausted breath: "O spiritual Mother, it is manifest that thou hast drawn near unto God, and hast died in the greatest measure unto the world; and the grace given unto thee is shown clearly in that thou callest me by name, and hast named me a presbyter, whom thou hast never seen before. Therefore do thou rather bless, for the Lord's sake, and grant thy prayer unto one who stands in need of thy perfection!"

Yielding then to the elder's persistence, she said: "Blessed is God, Who wills the salvation of human souls!" And when Zosimas had said "Amen," both rose from the ground. And the woman said unto the elder: "For what cause hast thou come unto me, a sinful woman, O man of God? Why hast thou desired to see a woman devoid of every virtue? Yet the grace of the Holy Spirit hath guided thee to fulfill a certain service needed for my body in due time. Tell me, therefore, O father: how do the Christians live now? How are the emperors? How are the holy Churches regulated?"

Zosimas answered, saying: "By your holy prayers, O Mother, God hath granted lasting peace; but receive the supplication of an unworthy old man, and pray for the Lord's sake on behalf of the whole world, and for me a sinner, that this my journey through the wilderness may not be fruitless." And she answered him: "It befits thee rather, Abba Zosimas, who holdest the priestly rank, to pray for me and for all, for unto this hast thou been appointed; nevertheless, since we are commanded to practice obedience, I will perform what is bidden me by thee."

Having said this, she turned toward the east, and lifting her eyes toward heaven and raising her hands, she began to pray in a low voice, so that her words could not be heard distinctly; therefore Zosimas understood nothing of the words of her prayer, and stood trembling, looking downward and saying nothing. And calling God to witness, the elder later testified: "When I saw her continuing long in prayer, I raised my eyes a little from looking at the ground, and I beheld her lifted up in the air about one cubit above the earth, standing and praying in the air!"

When Zosimas saw this, he fell into still greater fear, and cast himself upon the ground, wetting the earth with his tears, and saying nothing save only: "Lord, have mercy!" And as the elder lay upon the ground, a troubled thought entered his mind, wondering whether she might be a spirit pretending to prayin order to deceive him. Turning around, the woman raised the elder up and said: "Why do thoughts trouble thee, Abba Zosimas, to suspect that I am an apparition? Assuredly, I pray thee, blessed father, know of a truth that I am a sinful woman, albeit sealed with holy Baptism, and no spirit at all, but earth and dust and ashes, wholly flesh, having never known spiritual wisdom." And saying this, she signed her forehead, eyes, lips, and breast with the sign of the Cross, saying thus: "Abba Zosimas, may God deliver us from the evil one and from his snares, for great is his warfare against us."

Hearing and seeing these things, the elder fell at her feet, saying with tears: "I adjure thee by Jesus Christ our God, Who was born of the Virgin, for Whose sake Thou strivest thus in this wilderness, conceal not Thy life from me, but relate all things unto me, that Thou mayest make manifest the magnificent works of God! Tell me all things for God's sake; for Thou shalt speak not for vainglory, but to edify me, a sinner and unworthy man. I believe my God, for Whom Thou livest, that for this very purpose I was guided into this wilderness, that God might make manifest Thy works; for it is not in the power of our weakness to resist the judgments of God. For if Christ our God had not willed that Thou and Thy struggle should be made known, He would neither have revealed thee unto me, nor strengthened me for so great a journey—one who was never willing or able before to go forth out of my cell."

When Zosimas had spoken these things and many more besides, she sighed deeply and said unto him: "I am ashamed, O father, to recount unto thee my shameful deeds; yet since thou hast seen my state, I will lay bare before thee my actions also, that thou mayest know with how much shame and reproach my soul is filled. For not for the sake of praise, as thou hast said, will I relate my life unto thee, since I was formerly a chosen vessel of the adversary.[^1386] Yet know that if I begin to recount my former life, thou wilt flee from me as a man flees from a serpent, unable to endure hearing with thine ears the unworthy things that I wrought. Nevertheless, I will confess without hiding anything; only I adjure thee first, cease not to pray for me, that I may find mercy in the Day of Judgment."

And while the elder waited and wept unceasingly, she began to relate her life, speaking thus: "I, O father, was born in Egypt; and when I was twelve years of age, while my parents were still living, I cast off their affection and went away to Alexandria. And having lost my first modesty, I gave myself over unrestrainedly to a life of vanity and sin. I am ashamed even to call to mind that dishonor, yet I will speak briefly, that thou mayest know the excess of my fallen passions: for more than seventeen years I lived in public dissipation, receiving payment from no one, but living in poverty and spinning coarse flax, because I counted it my very life to wallow continually in earthly desires.

"While living thus, I saw in the season of harvest a great multitude of Libyan and Egyptian men hastening toward the sea; and I asked one of those who met me: 'Whither are these men hastening?' And he said unto me: 'To Jerusalem, for the Exaltation of the Precious and Life-giving Cross, which will take place shortly.' And I said unto him: 'Will they take me also, if I go with them?' And he said:

"'If thou hast the fare for the passage and provisions, no one will hinder thee.' And I said unto him: 'Truly, brother, I have neither fare nor provisions; yet I will go and embark upon the ship with them, and they will provide for me.' For I told thee, Abba Zosimas, compel me not to utter my disgrace; for the Lord God knows that I tremble lest my very words defile both thee and the air!" And Zosimas, wetting the ground with his tears, answered her: "Speak for the Lord's sake, O my Mother, speak, and cease not from a narrative so profitable unto me!"

She therefore resumed her account: "That young man, hearing my bold words, laughed and departed; and I, casting away the distaff which I occasionally carried, ran toward the sea whither they were hastening. And seeing ten or more young men standing by the seashore while others had already boarded the vessel, I approached them with my customary boldness, saying: 'Take me also with you whithersoever ye go, for I shall not be unwelcome among you.' And speaking other idle words, I moved them all to laughter; and they took me aboard their ship, and thence we set sail.

"How shall I relate unto thee what followed, O father? What tongue can utter, or what ear can bear to hear the evil deeds committed by me along the way and upon the ship? Believe me, O father, I marvel how the sea endured my lawlessness! How did the earth not open its mouth and swallow me down alive into hades, who had led astray so many souls? Yet I think that God was seeking my repentance; for He desires not the death of sinners, but awaits their conversion with long-suffering.[^1387]

"Thus I arrived in Jerusalem, and during all the days before the feast I continued in the same evil ways, and even worse. And when the holy feast of the Exaltation of the Precious Cross arrived,[^1388] I went about as before; and seeing everyone going very early to the church, I also went and ran along with those who were running, and entered with them into the courtyard of the church. And when the hour of the holy Exaltation arrived, as the multitude was entering into the church, I tried to enter with them, but was pushed back; and supposing that this happened because of the press of the crowd, I forced my way forward with great effort until I reached the very doors of the church within which the Life-giving Tree was displayed.

"Yet when I stepped upon the threshold of the church doors, all the others entered in unhindered, whereas a certain divine power held me back, not permitting me to enter; and again I attempted to enter, and was thrust far back from the doors, standing alone in the vestibule. Supposing that this happened to me because of feminine weakness, I mingled again with others who were entering, pushing with my elbows and straining with all my might; yet whenever my sinful foot touched the threshold, the church received everyone else and hindered no one, while refusing to admit me, the wretched one; as if a drawn-up detachment of soldiers were barring the entrance, so did that sudden divine power hold me back, and again I found myself in the vestibule.

"Having suffered this three and four times, and being exhausted and no longer able to push among those entering, I withdrew and stood in a corner of the church courtyard; and only then did I come to my senses and understand why I was prevented from beholding the Life-giving Cross. For the light of saving knowledge touched the eyes of my heart, showing me that the mire of my deeds barred the entrance against me.

"Then I began to weep and lament, and to beat my breast, pouring forth sighs from the depths of my heart. And as I stood weeping in that place, I looked up and saw above me an icon of the Most Holy Theotokos standing on the wall; and fixing my gaze upon her, I said: 'O Virgin Lady, who didst bear in the flesh God the Word! I know, yea, I know that it is neither fitting nor right for me, a defiled sinner, to look upon the venerable icon of thee, the Ever-Virgin Mary, who hast both body and soul pure and undefiled. Rightly indeed am I, a prodigal, worthy to be hated and abhorred before thy virginal purity! Yet I have heard that God, Whom thou didst bear, became man for this very purpose, that He might call sinners to repentance.[^1389] Help me who am alone and have help from nowhere! Command that the entrance of the church be opened unto me also, and forbid me not to behold the Tree upon which Christ God was crucified in the flesh, shedding His own Blood for my redemption! Command, O Sovereign Lady, that the doors be opened unto me for the holy veneration of the Cross; and be thou my sure mediatrix and surety before Him Who was born of thee, that henceforth I will never again defile this flesh; but as soon as I have beheld the Tree of the Cross of Thy Son, I will renounce this world and its deeds, and will immediately go forth whithersoever thou, as my surety, shalt guide me!'

"Having spoken these words, and receiving as it were an assurance through the ardor of faith, trusting in the compassion of the Theotokos, I moved from that place where I had stood offering my prayer; and returning, I mingled again with those who were entering, and there was no longer anyone pushing me back or hindering me from entering the church. Fear and amazement seized me, and I trembled all over; and reaching the doors that had previously been closed against me, I entered within without difficulty. And I was counted worthy to behold the Precious and Life-giving Cross, and I saw the mysteries of God, and how ready He is to receive the penitent! Falling then to the ground and kissing that Precious Wood with dread, I hastened forth to return to my Surety. And coming to the place where the icon of the Most Holy Theotokos stood, and bending my knees— "—before the icon of the Most Holy Theotokos, I began to speak in these words: 'Thou indeed, O blessed Lady Theotokos, Sovereign Mistress, hast shown thy loving-kindness toward me, and hast not abhorred the supplication of me, an unworthy sinner. For I have beheld the glory which in truth I, a prodigal, was unworthy to look upon; glory be to God, Who through thee receives the repentance of sinners! What more have I to ponder or to utter, being a sinful woman? It is time now, O Sovereign Lady, for me to fulfill the vow which I promised under thy surety. Direct me now whithersoever thou willest, and henceforth be unto me a teacher of salvation, leading me by the hand along the path of repentance.'

"And while I was speaking these words unto her, I heard a voice from afar saying: 'If thou crossest the Jordan, thou shalt find good rest!'[^1390] Hearing that voice, and believing with certainty that it had been uttered for my sake, I cried out with tears, gazing upon the icon of the Theotokos: 'O Sovereign Lady, Mother of God, forsake me not!'

"Having cried out thus, I went forth from the courtyard of the church and walked rapidly. And a certain man, seeing me walking, gave me three copper coins, saying: 'Accept these, my mother.' Taking them, I bought three loaves of bread, and asked the baker who sold me the loaves: 'Sir, which is the way to the Jordan?' Learning which city gate led in that direction, I went forth running, and walked along the road weeping. Asking my way as I went, I completed the day's journey—for it was about the third hour of the day when I beheld the Precious Cross—and toward sunset I reached the Church of St. John the Baptist, which stands near the banks of the Jordan.[^1391]

"Having prayed first in the church, I went down immediately to the Jordan and washed my face and hands in that holy water; and afterward I partook of the Most Pure and Life-giving Mysteries of Christ in the Church of the Forerunner. Then I ate half of one loaf, drank of the water of the Jordan, and slept that night upon the ground. Early the next morning, finding a small boat there, I crossed over to the opposite bank of the Jordan; and again I prayed to my Guide, the Theotokos, to direct me whithersoever seemed good to her.

"Thus I came into this wilderness; and from that time until this present day I have fled afar off, dwelling in this desert, waiting upon God Who saves me from faintheartedness and tempest whenever I turn unto Him."[^1392]

Zosimas said unto the venerable one: "How many years have passed, my lady, since thou didst take up thy dwelling in this wilderness?" And she answered: "I think that forty and seven years have elapsed since I went forth from the Holy City." Zosimas said unto her: "And what dost thou find for thy nourishment, my lady?" She said: "I brought two and a half loaves of bread with me when I crossed the Jordan; and little by little they dried up and became hard as stone, and partaking of them sparingly a little at a time, I subsisted on them for several years."

Zosimas asked: "How then hast thou passed so many years without harm, suffering no distress from so sudden a change of life?" She answered: "Thou hast questioned me now, Abba Zosimas, concerning a matter of which I tremble even to speak; for if I call to mind all those perils which I endured, and the fierce thoughts that assailed me, I fear lest I be seized by them once again." Zosimas said unto her: "O my lady, I beseech thee, leave nothing untold that thou canst declare unto me; for since thou hast begun, relate the whole matter unto me until the end."

And she said unto him: "Believe me, Abba Zosimas, for seventeen years I struggled in this wilderness against my own passionate thoughts as against fierce wild beasts! For whenever I began to taste a little food, immediately there came upon me a craving for the meats and fish that were in Egypt;[^1393] and I craved also the wine which I had loved so much—for when I was in the world I drank much wine, whereas here, not having even a drop of water to taste, I burned with thirst and endured grievous distress.

"There came upon me also a perverse desire for worldly songs, troubling my mind and stirring me to chant the vain melodies I had learned in the world; but immediately shedding tears and beating my breast, I called to mind the vows which I had made before departing into this wilderness. In spirit I would stand before the icon of the Most Holy Theotokos, my Surety, weeping before her and imploring her to drive away from me the thoughts that tormented my wretched soul. And when I had wept long enough and beaten my breast fervently, I would behold a calm light shining round about me from every side, and a great stillness would replace the tempest.

"Whenever turbulent thoughts rose up again within my heart, I cast myself down upon the earth and watered it with tears, believing that my Surety herself stood before me, arraigning me as a transgressor and showing me the punishment due to my breach of covenant; and I would not rise from the ground night or day until that sweet light shone upon me again and scattered the thoughts that troubled me. Thus I lifted the eyes of my mind unceasingly toward my Surety, beseeching her to succor me in this desert; and truly I had her as my helper and the furtherance of my repentance.

"In this manner I completed seventeen years, enduring ten thousand perils; and from that time until this day my Helper hath preserved me in all things." Zosimas said unto her: "And hast thou had no need since then of food or clothing?" She answered: "After finishing those loaves of bread over the course of seventeen years, as I told thee, I was nourished by the wild herbs and roots found in this desert. As for the garment which I wore when I crossed the Jordan, it became tattered and fell away completely; and thereafter I suffered great affliction from the cold of winter and the scorching heat of summer, burning under the sun and freezing and shivering in the frost, so that many times I fell to the earth and lay motionless as if without breath.

"Having struggled thus against manifold afflictions and temptations, from that time until this present day the manifold power of God hath preserved my sinful soul and humble body. When I reflect only upon the great evils from which the Lord hath delivered me, I possess inexhaustible nourishment in the hope of my salvation; for I am fed and clothed by the word of God, Who upholds the universe. For man doth not live by bread alone, and because of having no shelter, those who have stripped off the garment of sin have embraced the rock!"[^1394]

Hearing her quote the words of Scripture from Moses, the Prophets, and the Psalms, Zosimas asked her: "Hast thou then learned the Psalms and the other sacred books, my lady?" Smiling gently at this question, she said unto him: "Believe me, O father, I have seen no other human being since I crossed the Jordan until I beheld thy— "—face today; Indeed, I have not even seen a wild beast or any other living creature since that day. And I never learned to read from books, nor have I ever heard anyone chanting or reading since then; but the living Word of God Himself teaches man knowledge! Here ends the narrative of my life, O father; and now, as I besought thee at the beginning, I adjure thee by the Incarnation of the Word of God to pray unto the Lord for me, a prodigal sinner."

Having spoken thus and concluded her account, she bowed down to make a prostration before the elder; and the elder cried aloud with tears: "Blessed is God, Who works great and marvelous things, glorious and unsearchable, whereof there is no number![^1395] Blessed is God, Who hath shown me how great are the gifts He bestows upon those who fear Him! Truly, O Lord, Thou dost never forsake them that seek Thee!"

And when the elder wished to fall at her feet, she took hold of him and permitted him not to prostrate himself, saying unto him: "Concerning all these things which thou hast heard, O father, I adjure thee by Jesus Christ our God and Savior to speak of them to no man until God shall take me from the earth. Now depart in peace, and in the coming year thou shalt see me again, preserved by the grace of God. Only do for the Lord's sake that which I now enjoin upon thee: During the Great Fast of the coming year, cross not over the Jordan as ye are accustomed to do in the monastery."

Zosimas marveled upon hearing that she knew even the rule of the monastery, and could say nothing else save only: "Glory be to God, Who bestows great gifts upon them that love Him!" And she continued: "Remain, therefore, in the monastery, Abba, as I tell thee; for even if thou shouldst attempt to go forth before the time, it will not be possible for thee. And on the evening of Holy and Great Thursday, which commemorates the Mystical Supper of Christ,[^1396] place in a sacred vessel worthy of such a mystery a portion of the Life-giving Body and Blood of Christ our God, and bring it for me; and wait for me on the bank of the Jordan that lies near the inhabited world, so that I may come and partake of the Life-giving Gifts. For since the time when I communicated in the Church of the Forerunner before crossing the Jordan, until this day I have not received that sanctification; therefore I thirst for it with unrestrainable longing, and I implore thee not to disdain my request, but certainly to bring me the Divine and Life-giving Mysteries at the very hour when the Lord made His disciples partakers of the Divine Supper. And as for John, the abbot of the monastery in which thou dwellest, say unto him: 'Look to thyself and to thy flock,[^1397] for there are certain things there that require correction.' Yet I wish thee not to say this unto him now, but when the Lord shall permit thee."

Having said these things, and asking the elder once more: "Pray for me!" she vanished into the depths of the inner wilderness. And Zosimas, falling upon his knees and kissing the ground whereon her feet had stood, gave glory and thanks unto God; and he returned through the wilderness rejoicing and praising Christ our God, arriving back at the monastery on the appointed day when the other brethren also returned.

Throughout that entire year the elder kept silence, daring to tell no one anything of what he had seen, while praying earnestly within himself that God would show him again that desired countenance; and he grieved and counted the length of the year heavy to bear, wishing that the whole year might pass like a single day if that were possible.

When the First Sunday of the Holy and Great Fast arrived, the other brethren went forth chanting according to the custom and order of the monastery; Zosimas, however, was seized by a feverish illness and was compelled to remain behind in the monastery. Then Zosimas remembered the words spoken by the venerable one: "Even if thou shouldst wish to go forth from the monastery, it will not be possible for thee." After Not many days he was healed of his infirmity and remained within the monastery.

When the brethren returned for Palm Sunday and the evening of the Mystical Supper arrived, Zosimas did as he had been commanded: placing in a small chalice a portion of the Most Pure Body and Precious Blood of Christ our God, and putting into a small basket a few dried figs and dates and a handful of lentils soaked in water, he went forth late in the evening and sat down upon the bank of the Jordan, awaiting the arrival of the holy one.

Though the saint delayed, Zosimas did not allow himself to slumber, but gazed intently toward the desert, longing to behold her whom he earnestly desired. And sitting on the bank, the elder conversed within himself, saying: "What if my unworthiness hath hindered her from coming? What if she came already, and not finding me, turned back?" Speaking thus, he sighed and wept; and lifting his eyes to heaven, he prayed to God, saying: "Deprive me not, O Sovereign Master, of beholding again that face which Thou didst grant me to see, lest I depart empty, bearing the burden of my sins for my own reproof!"

Having prayed thus with tears, he fell into another thought, saying within himself: "And what will happen even if she comes? For there is no boat here; how then shall she cross the Jordan to come unto me, an unworthy man? Woe to my unworthiness! Woe is me, who hath deprived me of such a blessing?"

While the elder was pondering these things, behold, the holy woman arrived and stood on the opposite bank of the river, coming out of the desert. Zosimas rose up rejoicing with joy and glorifying God, though still wrestling in his mind with the thought that she would not be able to cross the Jordan. Looking across, he saw her sign the waters of the Jordan with the sign of the Precious Cross—for the night, as he said, was bright with the full moon—and simultaneously with making the sign of the Cross, she stepped down upon the water and walked across the surface of the river toward him!

When the elder attempted to make a prostration before her, she forbade him while still walking upon the water, crying aloud: "What dost thou, Abba, seeing that thou art a priest and art carrying the Divine Mysteries?" Stepping off the water onto the bank, she said to the elder: "Bless, father, bless!" He answered her with trembling—for amazement had seized him at that transcendent miracle—saying: "Truly God is not false in promising that those who purify themselves shall become like unto God so far as is possible![^1398] Glory be to Thee, O Christ our God, Who hast shown me through this Thy handmaid how far distant I stand from the measure of perfection!"

When he had spoken thus, she asked him to recite the sacred Symbol of Faith ("I believe in one God") and the Lord's Prayer ("Our Father Who art in the heavens"). When the prayer was concluded, she partook of the Undefiled Mysteries of Christ, and according to the custom gave the kiss of peace to the elder; then raising her hands toward heaven, she sighed and wept, crying aloud: "Lord, now lettest Thou Thy servant depart in peace, according to Thy word; for mine eyes have seen Thy salvation, which Thou hast prepared before the face of all peoples!"[^1399]

And she said again to the elder: "Pardon me, Abba Zosimas, and fulfill one more desire of mine: return now to thy monastery, guarded by the peace of God; and in the coming year come again to that same dry torrent bed where I first conversed with thee. Come, I pray thee for the Lord's sake, and Thou shalt see me again according as the Lord wills." He answered her: "Would that it were possible for me to follow behind thee from this hour and gaze continually upon thy venerable countenance! Yet grant this one entreaty of an old man, and taste a little of the food which I have brought for thee." And saying this, he showed her the basket which he carried.

Touching the lentils with the tips of her fingers, she took only three grains and raised them to her lips, saying that the grace of the Spirit sufficed to preserve the nature of the soul undefiled. And she said again to the elder: "Pray to the Lord for me, O father, pray for me, and remember always my wretchedness!"

He bowed down at her feet, asking her to pray to God for the Church, the emperors, and himself; and having asked this with tears, he let her depart, groaning and lamenting, for he did not dare to detain any longer one who could not be held back even if he wished. Signing the Jordan once more with the sign of the Cross, she walked back across the waters as she had done before. And the elder returned to his monastery filled with great joy and holy fear, reproaching himself only because he had neglected to learn the name of the saint, though hoping to learn it in the following year.

When another year had passed, Zosimas went forth again into the wilderness according to custom, hastening toward that marvelous sight. Walking through the desert and noticing certain signs of the place he sought, he looked to the right and to the left like a keen hunter seeking to capture a most sweet quarry. When he saw no movement anywhere, he began to weep; and lifting his eyes to heaven, he prayed, saying: "Show me, O Lord, the unplundered treasure which Thou hast hidden in this wilderness! Show me, I beseech Thee, the angel in the flesh, of whom the whole world is not worthy!"[^1400]

Praying thus, he reached the place marked by the dry torrent bed; and standing on the bank, he looked toward the east and saw the holy one lying dead, her hands folded decently across her breast as was fitting, and her face turned toward the east. Running to her, he washed the feet of the blessed one with his tears, not daring to touch any other part of her body.

Having wept for a long time and chanted psalms suited to the occasion, he offered the funeral prayers and said within himself: "Ought I to bury the relic of the holy one, or might this perhaps be displeasing to her?" As he said this, he noticed beside her head an inscription traced upon the ground in these words: "Bury, Abba Zosimas, in this place the body of humble Mary; render dust unto dust, and pray to the Lord for me, who departed in the month of Pharmouthi according to the Egyptians, which is April according to the Romans, on the first day of the month, on the very night of the saving Passion of Christ, after partaking of the Divine and Mystical Supper."[^1401]

Reading this inscription, the elder wondered at first who could have written it, since she had told him that she had never learned letters; yet he rejoiced exceedingly to learn the name of the saint, and understood clearly that immediately after partaking of the Divine Mysteries at the Jordan, she had been transported in a single hour to this place—a journey that had taken him twenty days of wearisome travel—and had departed immediately unto God.

Glorifying God, and wetting the earth and the relic of the saint with his tears, the elder said within himself: "It is time now, poor Zosimas, to fulfill what is commanded; yet how shalt thou dig, wretched man, having no tool in thy hands?" Saying this, he saw a small piece of wood lying nearby on the ground; picking it up, he began to dig with it. The ground, however, was parched and hard, and yielded not at all to the elder's efforts as he labored and sweated without accomplishing anything.

Sighing deeply from the bottom of his heart, he looked up and saw a great lion standing beside the relic of the blessed Mary and licking the soles of her feet. At the sight of the wild beast the elder trembled with fear, especially remembering how the holy one had told him that she had never seen a wild beast; but signing himself with the sign of the Cross, he trusted that he would be kept unharmed by the power of the one lying before him. And the lion began to fawn gently upon the elder, greeting him with mild movements as if saluting him.

Zosimas then said to the lion: "O great beast, since this holy one hath commanded me to bury her body, and I am an old man unable to dig—having no mattock with me, and being too far away to return and fetch one—do thou therefore perform the work with thy claws, that we may commit to the earth the all-holy tabernacle of the saint!" Immediately upon hearing these words, the lion dug out with its front paws a trench deep enough to cover the body of her who was being buried.

Having washed the feet of the saint once more with his tears, and entreated her earnestly to pray on behalf of all, the elder covered her body with earth in the presence of the lion, her body being clad solely in the tattered cloak which Zosimas had cast to her at their first meeting. Then both departed: the lion withdrew into the inner wilderness as gently as a lamb, while Zosimas returned to his monastery blessing and praising Christ our God.

Upon reaching the monastery, the elder related to all the monks everything that he had seen and heard from her, concealing nothing from them; and all marveled upon hearing of the magnificent works of God, celebrating with fear and faith and love the commemoration of the saint. And Abbot John found certain matters in the monastery requiring correction, just as the holy one had foretold, and set them right with the help of God. Zosimas himself reposed in that same monastery after reaching nearly a hundred years of age.

The monks of that monastery handed down these words unwritten from one generation to another, setting them forth as a pattern of edification for those who wished to listen; and I, having received this unwritten narrative from them, have committed it to writing. Whether others also have written the life of the saint with fuller knowledge of her deeds has not come to my awareness; yet to the best of my ability I have recorded this account, placing nothing above the truth.

And may God, Who works great miracles and bestows great gifts upon those who take refuge in Him in faith, grant a reward unto those who read and listen to this account, and to the one who arranged for this narrative to be committed to writing; and may He count them worthy of the blessed portion of this saint, Mary, concerning whom this narrative is told, together with all who have pleased Him from the beginning by contemplative vision and virtuous deeds! Let us also give glory unto God the eternal King, that He may grant us to find mercy in the Day of Judgment in Christ Jesus our Lord, to Whom belongs all glory, honor, dominion, and worship, together with the unoriginate Father and the All-Holy, Good, and Life-giving Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

## A Beneficial Tale Concerning the Akathist Hymn

*Compiled from an ancient writing, setting forth a glorious remembrance of the miracle which took place when the Persians and Barbarians besieged the Imperial City with warfare, and perished by the judgment of God after being put to the test, whereas the City was preserved unharmed through the prayers of our Most Pure Lady the Theotokos; from which time this service of thanksgiving has been chanted, calling that day the Akathist.*[^1402]

*Verse:*
*With unsleeping hymns thy City gives thanks unto thee, O Theotokos,*
*Praising thee as its unsleeping Protectress in battle.*

In the days of Heraclius, the Emperor of the Greeks, Khosrow, who held the sovereignty of the Persians, sent his general Shahrbaraz with a great multitude of troops against the entire eastern region under Greek rule, sweeping across it like fiery lightning, burning, scorching, and destroying all things.[^1403] Having laid waste the eastern lands with great boldness and mocked the Greeks whom he encountered there—for there was no one able to resist him or cut short his mighty invasion, the Greek forces having been previously humbled and exhausted by the harsh and ferocious tyranny of the soldier Phocas, who had ruled the empire like a tyrant rather than an emperor—Shahrbaraz advanced as far as Chalcedon; and pitching his camp round about it, he prepared everything for the destruction of the Great and Imperial City, devising and counseling every stratagem toward that end.

Seeing these things, Emperor Heraclius, who had newly assumed the throne after the tyrant Phocas and was weighed down by great affliction and toil, left the capital and sailed across the Euxine Sea—that is, the Black Sea—having gathered whatever military forces remained, and suddenly, without anyone knowing of his movement, marched into the lands of Persia. Learning of the Emperor's departure, the Khagan of the Scythians immediately seized his opportunity: he filled the sea with vessels hollowed out of single logs (which for this reason are called *monoxyla* in the Greek tongue),[^1404] and covered the dry land with horsemen and foot soldiers.

Meanwhile, the people remaining within the Imperial City were comforted and instructed by Sergius, who was then Patriarch, not to lose hope: "Take courage, my children," said the Patriarch, "and let us place the hope of our salvation in God, lifting up our hands and our eyes unto Him with all our soul! He will deliver us from the evils that encompass us, and will scatter all the barbarian counsels devised against us." Strengthened by these words of the Patriarch, the inhabitants of the city persevered in faith toward the Most Pure Theotokos and toward Christ our God, Who for His love of mankind was well-pleased to become incarnate of her; and awaiting His mercy upon themselves, they endured in hope—which shortly thereafter they were counted worthy to receive, not falling short of their expectation.

Now Patrician Bonos, who had been left behind by the Emperor to guard the city, spared no effort in devising every defense possible against the enemy; "For God wills," said he, "that we should not sit idle and inactive, but should labor and take forethought on our own part while placing all our hope of salvation in Him; for thus did Joshua the son of Nun command ambushes and stratagems to be prepared against the men of Ai, and thus did Gideon arm his men with water pitchers and torches against Midian."[^1405] Patrician Bonos therefore fortified the walls of the city and prepared everything needed for defense; while Patriarch Sergius, taking up the sacred icons of the Mother of God—especially that upon which the Infant Savior is depicted borne in His Mother's arms—walked in procession around the walls of the city, establishing thereby a firm bulwark for the citizens, and bringing terror, flight, and ruin upon the opposing barbarians— —delivering the enemy ultimately to destruction. For when the Persians encamped at Chalcedon began to set fire to the suburbs round about Chalcedon and to commit all the other ravages customary for pillagers who sack cities, the Scythians and barbarians on the European side showed themselves doing the very same things as the Persians.

Taking up once more the Icon of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ Not Made by Hands, together with the divine Robe of the Most Pure Theotokos and the Life-giving Wood of the Cross, the Patriarch walked around the walls of the city, praying with tears and saying: "Let God arise and help us, and let Thine enemies be scattered, and let them vanish like smoke, and melt away like wax before the face of fire!"[^1406]

Before three days had passed after the conflagration, the entire host of the Scythian commander advanced against the city; and so vast was the multitude of the adversaries, arrayed in numberless ranks and fortified in full armor, that to speak truly there were ten Scythians gathered against every single Greek! Yet our Champion Leader, the Most Pure Sovereign Lady who fights on behalf of Christians and is a speedy helper to those who call upon her, came to the aid of a detachment of soldiers at her divine Church called the *Pege* (the Life-giving Spring),[^1407] where they slew a great number of the attacking Scythians. This manifest assistance granted to the Christians by the all-powerful Commander, the Mother of God, not only cast down the enemy before the Greeks, but also reduced the boastful arrogance of the Scythian warlord to bewilderment through such generalship on the part of the Mother of God, foreshadowing the complete destruction that was shortly to overtake them.

Beholding so great a fall of the enemy before the citizens, and recognizing it as a sign and beginning of the foes' ruin, the Greeks took heart and were filled with great courage; and day by day they stood ready to join battle against them, having the Mother of God fighting alongside them, rendering them stronger and frustrating the stratagems, strength, and assaults of the Scythians.

After these things, by the common consent of the Patriarch, the Prefect Bonos, and the people, envoys were sent forth from the city bearing gifts to speak words of peace to the Scythians and invite them to a truce. The Khagan, however, being beast-like in temper rather than human, and exceedingly covetous in character, accepted the gifts while spurning the proposals of peace, and sent the envoys back empty-handed, saying only this unto them: "Deceive not yourselves concerning the God in Whom ye trust! Assuredly tomorrow I will seize your city with my hand like a bird in a nest, and will lay it waste; and out of my own clemency I will let you all go free wearing only a single garment apiece! Depart then from the city whithersoever ye wish, for I shall show you no greater humanity than this"—after that wretched man had first uttered many blasphemies against Christ our God and against Her who bore Him.

Hearing these words from the returning envoys, the inhabitants of the city—all the priests, the clergy, the monastics, and the entire populace—groaned from the depths of their hearts; and hastening to the holy churches of God, they lifted up their hands toward heaven and prayed with abundant tears, saying: "O Lord our Defender, O Lord, look down from Thy holy dwelling-place and behold this foul barbarian and those with him, who have dared to blaspheme Thy holy and great Name! Cast him down, O Lord our Protector, lest it be said among the nations: 'Where is their God?'[^1408] For Thou art our God, Who resistest the proud and lookest upon the humble! Thy dominion is incomparable and Thy Kingdom indestructible; hear the words which this barbarian hath sent to reproach Thee, Who reignest over all! Deliver the city of Thine inheritance and the people called by Thy Name!"

Thus did the weeping people pray; and God prevented the Persians from crossing over to join the Khagan as they had agreed, slaying no small number of the envoys whom they sent back and forth to one another. Fighting along the outskirts for two and three days and assaulting various sectors without rest, both enemy hosts prepared a simultaneous general assault according to their pact: by land they brought up siege engines to batter down the walls of the city, while by sea they armed the countless Scythian *monoxyla*, intending at one and the same day and hour to strike the Imperial City simultaneously by land and by sea.

While this assault was being prepared, the Khagan took his chosen horsemen and rode toward the mouth of the great strait to show himself to the Persian army opposite, while the Persian general did likewise on the Asian shore; the one from Asia and the other from Europe roared like wild beasts against the Imperial City, supposing that they already held it as their sure prey.

Yet who can recount the miracles of God wrought at that hour, or who can declare the tender compassion of the Virgin toward us? For when the Khagan filled the bay of the Golden Horn with *monoxyla* and armed warriors therein—attempting by land to overthrow the walls while by sea the forces in the Golden Horn stood ready to scale the city—our Lord and God, Whom they had blasphemed and Who is God over all, through the prayers of the Virgin Theotokos who bore Him, showed their hopes to be vain and fruitless! On every side of the land walls the Greek soldiers slew so vast a multitude of the enemy that the survivors were no longer able even to burn their dead.

Such was the fate of those who fought by land and strove to cast down the walls. As for the *monoxyla* together with all the warriors aboard them, opposite the divine Church of our Sovereign Lady the Theotokos at Blachernae, she sank them all suddenly in a single moment by raising a dreadful and violent tempest! For as soon as those vessels drew near her divine Church, the sea rose up exceedingly high and divided at the divine command, engulfing and sinking them all immediately together with the *monoxyla* themselves.

And there was to be seen an unutterable and great miracle: for the sea raised waves equal to mountains, roaring like a fierce tempest; and leaping furiously upon the enemies of the Mother of God, the billows mercilessly destroyed them, just as of old the sea did unto the Egyptians when they pursued ancient Israel, who walked through the midst thereof on foot in a marvelous manner![^1409] Thus did the Theotokos fight in the forefront for her city and defend it, granting bloodless victories through such generalship against those who had brought measureless warfare upon her inheritance and had lifted up weapons against her— —sinking them swiftly, she accomplished this deed through the sea's currents; for placing their sole hope of salvation in her, she preserved her heritage together unharmed from the cruel assailants. For then even their chief, the Khagan himself, having become an eyewitness of the destruction of his own forces, standing there somewhere upon a high place with his valiant horsemen, beat his hands upon his face and breast.

Now those within the city, waging battle against the enemies, when they beheld the destruction that had befallen the foes in the sea, immediately waxing strong by divine power and fortified by the might of the Virgin Theotokos, opened the gates of the city, and shouting and crying aloud, rushed upon the enemies. Such great joy and strength possessed the city's warriors, while great fear seized the barbarians. And it was a marvelous sight to behold the glorious generalship of our Sovereign Lady the Theotokos: for even young youths and women rushed upon the barbarians and attacked the very necks of their enemies; and they wrought such slaughter among the barbarians as is impossible to recount in words. There truly was fulfilled that which was spoken: one chased a thousand, and two put ten thousands to flight. Such strength did the Most Pure Virgin Theotokos and Sovereign Lady grant unto the weak, and such power unto the infirm.

When the sun had set and night had fallen, the engines of war which the barbarians had brought upon wheels for the taking of the city were all surrendered to the flames; and by the gracious favor of the most compassionate Theotokos toward them, the very makers of those labors and stratagems were compelled to become the burners of them.

The Prefect and all the citizens, lifting up their hands unto heaven, offered thanksgiving with tears, saying: "Thy right hand, O Lord, is glorified in strength; Thy right hand, O Lord, hath broken the enemies, and in the multitude of Thy glory Thou hast crushed the adversaries!"[^1410] And thus the foolish Khagan, having arrived with a multitude of countless troops, returned in shame. In like manner also the general who had led the Persian troops, laying his hand upon his mouth and covering his face, because out of so many thousands he led away only a small number with himself, reached his own country in disgrace.

Thus the servant of divine providence and goodness, the Most Pure and Undefiled Theotokos, the sovereign helper of Christians, showed her strength in our defense and granted us so great and glorious a salvation. Therefore, in remembrance of such a benefaction, we assemble this present solemn congregational gathering of all the people, and we celebrate an all-night feast, offering songs of thanksgiving. The Universal Church hath named this feast the "Unseated Hymn" (*Akathistos*), to be celebrated in honor of the Mother of God at this season when the victory was wrought by the Mother of God, calling it *Akathistos*, that is, *Unseated*; because thus did the clergy and all the people of the city perform it at that time.[^1411]

*The Second Miracle against the Saracens.*

After the passing of thirty-six years, in the reign of Constantine the Bearded, the great Saracen fleet (*Rhodostolos*) arrived once again with a multitude of ships against that imperial city.[^1412] And they anchored immediately along the shore at the inlet, from which they waged warfare by sea against the city daily, from the springtime until the autumn, departing to spend the winter in Cyzicus. And returning again in the spring to the imperial city, they again waged warfare from the sea, contending fiercely for seven years.

They lost many of their ships, together with the soldiers stationed aboard them and all the equipment which they had brought with them for their need; and they also returned with great loss unto their own lands. And when they were in the regions of Syllaeum, fierce and contrary winds blew upon them, and by the righteous wrath of God they perished, all being drowned in the sea through the intercession of the Mother of God. In like manner, at diverse times the wretched ones, throwing themselves against that God-guarded city to fulfill their furious impulse, returned empty-handed and filled with shame.

*The Third Miracle against the Hagarenes.*

Yet once again, because their violence was exalted into greatness on every side, it increased from the east unto the west and grew into a multitude.[^1413] And first indeed they entered into the Persian kingdom; and from thence into Egypt and Libya, giving their word to the Christians that they would not cause them to transgress the orthodox and blameless faith in Christ our God—which promise they did not keep, but showed forth many martyrs, because they refused to trample upon the Precious Cross, for this they compelled the Christians to do. And so to speak, they encompassed the whole earth with captivity, having overrun India, Ethiopia, Spain, the lands of the Moors, and Libya.

Last of all, they encamped against that very imperial city, desiring to take it. The Emperor being then the Isaurian, who had received the imperial crown, promised to pay them tribute. They, however, demanded that their own garrison be placed over the city; for they relied upon the multitude of their own troops and mercenaries, and upon the preparation of countless warriors in ships, the number of the vessels being one thousand and eight hundred, as those who have labored over chronicles have recorded.

These errant fighters against Christ placed their tents before the city; yet they brought not a single assault against the walls, for they were occupied in building houses, imagining that they would dwell there as at home, having woven such plans in their mind and intent, while the Lord frustrated their counsels. Meanwhile, the Christ-loving people within the city unceasingly celebrated the divine services, as was their custom to do, in peace and good order. Then taking up the all-venerable Wood of the Cross of God and our Savior Jesus Christ, and the holy Icons of the Most Pure Mother of God, they walked— —around the walls of the city, lifting up their hands unto God and saying: "Arise, O Lord, help us, and cast not off Thy people unto the end; for lo, Thine enemies have made a tumult, and they that hate Thee have lifted up their head! Give not Thine inheritance unto reproach, that the heathen should rule over us, lest they should ever say: 'Where is their God?' But let them know that Thou, Lord Jesus Christ, art Lord unto the glory of God the Father!"[^1414]

Now a certain horseman from among the opposing Saracens, boastfully mocking the city of Constantine by name and calling the church by a common name, "Sophia," fell down together with his horse into a ditch and breathed his last. Furthermore, their herald, having climbed a tall tree to proclaim their impure prayer, likewise fell down and wretchedly cast forth his godless soul.

After this they resolved to wage war against the Bulgars; for there were two generals, and one remained behind to destroy the imperial city, while the other went forth to fight against the Bulgars. Contending against them, he was defeated by them; and more than twenty thousand of his troops fell, while the survivors returned in shame unto their own.[^1415]

Having returned from the Bulgars, they attempted to reach the region of Blachernae with the ships they had brought. The stretching of iron chains from the imperial city unto Galata, however, prevented them, so that they were unable to accomplish what they had planned.[^1416] Sailing therefore as far as the place called Sosthenion and into other small harbors, the greater part of them were crushed and broken by a severe winter and dreadful winds; and the Greeks burned a multitude of other great ships.

Now the city was under great duress from the scarcity of human necessities. The adversaries, however, consumed without restraint whatever fell into their hands; and falling into a bitter extremity of famine, they were reduced even to tasting human flesh and the carcasses of unclean beasts, mingling unclean substances in their desperation. By reason of such desperate affliction, many of their notable and valiant men fled to the city and surrendered.[^1417]

For as of old He drowned the Egyptians who pursued Israel in the depths like a stone and brought the waters of the sea upon them, so also the Lord brought destruction upon the Saracens fleeing from the imperial city: for stirring up the sea with a dark wind and a dreadful, fiery tempest, He destroyed those thousands of ships. For while the majority of the vessels were sailing upon the Aegean deep, hail suddenly fell from heaven; and just as cold iron when brought near to fire boils up, so also the hailstones falling into the seawater caused it immediately to boil up. And by such boiling it melted the pitch of the opposing ships, and all sank into the depths, only three remaining to bring the report.[^1418]

And there were to be seen upon all the islands, upon the shores, in the harbors, and in the bays, mounds of dead cast up and lying about.

Let all such things be recounted unto generations to come, to speak in the words of the Psalm, and let every Christian mouth proclaim the sacred word, saying with David: "Thou, O Lord, hast crushed the heads of the dragons in the water!"[^1419] Let there also be added the song sung with a loud voice at the exodus of Israel: "Let us sing unto the Lord, for He is gloriously glorified!"

Having reached this point in my discourse, and gazing up unto the height of the surpassing miracles of God and the Sovereign Lord, and of the unique Most Pure Theotokos, I become speechless with amazement, and I find no words worthy of these deeds. For what eloquent tongue can worthily praise the Virgin Theotokos? Or how can anyone gloriously hymn her glorious grace, to glorify the fountain of the love of mankind and to offer thanksgiving?

For it was fitting for us to offer thanksgiving unto the Mother of God by our deeds; for she herself and her Son rejoice far more in such deeds than in pipes, organs, and musical songs. And this is, as the Apostle also said: love without hypocrisy, which is the head of the Law and the Prophets; unswerving brotherly love; unrelenting compassion; divine virginity; lawful marriage; unsearchable chastity; conjoined righteousness; lawful courage against the passions; a God-directed understanding; inoffensive mercy; temperance without pride; well-contrite humility; and in addition to these, a blameless life, an upright character, a humble disposition, a prudent mind, God-proclaiming lips, a God-praising tongue, an unadulterous eye, hands free from plunder, feet not swift unto evil, an anger that is not unreasonable, subduing the fiery arrows of passionate thoughts; and all the other virtues whereby man, created according to the image of God and joined unto God, is adorned as with precious and beautiful raiment.[^1420]

With such virtues ought we to give thanks unto God; for the wise have said that thanksgiving is composed of deeds and not of words alone. Yet since the weakness of our nature and the sluggishness of our mind make us slothful in virtuous labor, and we touch upon virtue only sparingly, as it were with the tip of our finger; for this reason we fall short in offering our due thanksgiving, so that we may at least offer songs in words.

Lifting up our voices, therefore, and elevating our understanding, let us speak thus with acclamation:

Thou, O Sovereign Lady Theotokos, possessing love for mankind by nature, hast not forsaken our Christian race, defending us as a mother who loves her children and is compassionate. Thus Thou dost ever grant Thy benefactions unto us, saving, covering, and preserving us, delivering us from perils and turning away tribulations. Therefore we give thanks for these things, and we proclaim Thy grace; we hide not Thy benefactions; we chant Thy miracles with a loud voice; we glorify Thine aid; we magnify Thy providence; we hymn Thy protection; we possess Thee as our beneficent helper; we glorify Thy tender mercy, and we remember Thy many great gifts, and from how many perils we have been delivered by Thee!

These songs of thanksgiving we offer unto Thee as a debt, not as though they were worthy of Thy benefaction, but rather as the lisping of smiling children. And concerning the present evils that afflict us, we implore Thy speedy aid; we have need of Thy protection: resolve the stumbling-blocks that exist in our midst! Dispel the cloud and darkness that encompass us, through which, as in a nocturnal battle, we are wounded—or rather to say, as though mad, we consume our own flesh, not understanding our kinship and not considering our common brotherhood, so that some among us dare to do such things, having been made worthy of that same Spirit, of the one Christ, of the same faith, of the same baptism, of the one Church, and of the same Mysteries!

Yet let us not extend our discourse further. For this reason we pray, O all-holy Sovereign Lady, unto Thy compassionate heart, that Thy mercies may be received by Thy people and Thine inheritance: and stand Thou as an intercessor before God Who was born of Thee, that He may help us who are perishing utterly, that He may deliver us from incurable misfortunes!

Thou seest, O Sovereign Lady, with how many tears we are inundated: have mercy therefore, and cast us not off unto the end! Why, O Queen, dost Thou turn Thy face away, and forget our poverty and our affliction? Scatter the fear and trembling that lie upon us; quiet the wrath of God that hath been stirred up against us; subdue destruction; make peace among the strife and turmoil that exist in our midst; and grant tranquility and peace unto Thy servants, that having added these also unto Thy many benefactions, we may ever proclaim Thy miracles!

And having sailed thus far, let us lower the sails of our discourse, and anchor in the firm haven of silence.[^1421] Calling these things again to the remembrance of those assembled, let us not appear ungrateful concerning the benefaction of the Sovereign Lady, nor, having been benefited in deed, let us be slothful in offering thanksgiving in words; so that an increase of blessing may return unto us again. For if we are thankful for the benefits that have come upon us, we shall prepare for ourselves great boldness toward those things that are to come, and we shall obtain unutterable blessings, by the grace and love for mankind of our Lord Jesus Christ, unto Whom be glory and dominion, honor and worship, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.
