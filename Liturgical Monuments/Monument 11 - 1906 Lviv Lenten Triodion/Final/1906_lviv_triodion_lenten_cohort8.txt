On this day we make remembrance of the expulsion of the first-fashioned Adam from the delight of Paradise, which our divine and holy Fathers ordained before the holy Forty Days, showing thereby how profitable the medicine of fasting is for human nature, and how shameful again is that which comes from gluttony and disobedience. The Fathers, therefore, leaving aside the recounting of the numberless things that occurred in the world sequentially, present first-fashioned Adam, showing beforehand what great evil he suffered from not fasting even for a little while, and what he thereby brought upon our nature. And as the first commandment of God to mankind was the good commandment of fasting, which he did not keep, but having yielded to his belly, or rather to the deceiver serpent through Eve, not only did he not become God, but he also brought in death and handed over ruin to the whole human race. Because of the food of the first Adam, therefore, the Lord fasted forty days and was obedient; therefore also the present holy Forty Days was devised by the holy Apostles, that what he, not having kept, suffered, having lost incorruption, we, having kept, may regain through fasting. In another way, as we said before, the intention of the saints is this: that they may briefly encompass the deeds done by God from the beginning even unto the end. And since the transgression and expulsion of Adam from food is the primal cause of all things that concern us, according to this reasoning they present this to us, so that making remembrance of that, we may flee it, and above all not emulate intemperance in all things. For on the sixth day Adam was fashioned by the hand of God, being honored with the divine image by inbreathing; and having received the commandment, he lived in Paradise immediately until the sixth hour; then having transgressed it, he was expelled thence. Philo the Jew said that Adam spent one hundred years in Paradise; but others say seven days or years, on account of the honor of the number seven. And as at the sixth hour he stretched forth his hands and touched the fruit, Christ the New Adam also revealed this, stretching forth His hands on the Cross at the sixth hour and the sixth day, healing ruin thence. For he was created between corruption and incorruption, so that whatever he should choose by free will, that he might acquire. For it was indeed possible for God to make Adam sinless, but that there might also be the amendment of his own free will, for this reason He gave a law to touch all the trees, but this one not at all; that is, to contemplate through all creations the knowledge of divine power that comes to be, but concerning the divine nature, in no wise to inquire. St. Gregory the Theologian philosophizes that the trees were divine thoughts, and the garden was divine contemplation.[^211] That is, God commanded Adam (the Theologian says) to care for all the other elements and qualities, and to contemplate them with the mind, and to glorify God thence; for this is true food; but concerning his own nature, and concerning God, who He is in nature, and where, and how He brought all things from non-being, in no wise to seek. But he, having left the other things, rather investigated the things concerning God, and closely searched out His nature; since he was yet imperfect and very simple, an infant, he fell into such things, Satan through Eve having cast into him the fantasy of deification. But the great and divine Chrysostom says that the tree had a twofold power; and he says that Paradise was upon earth, and philosophizes that it was both intelligible and sensible, as Adam was; and both were between corruption and incorruption, together observing the Scripture, and yet not remaining strictly at the letter. Some say that the tree of disobedience was a fig tree; for having immediately perceived their nakedness, they used its leaves to cover themselves. For this reason Christ cursed it, as having been the cause of disobedience; for it has also a certain likeness to sin: first, that which sweetens, then that which is rough from the leaves and clings with milky sap. There are also those who have wrongly imagined that tree to be the intercourse and knowledge of Adam with Eve. Having transgressed, therefore, he was clothed in mortal flesh, and receiving the curse, was expelled from Paradise; and it was commanded to the flaming sword to keep its gates. And he, sitting straight opposite, wept over how many good things he was deprived of, for not fasting for a season; and from him all the race was reckoned equal to him, until He Who fashioned us, having pitied our nature,

=== LEAF p74 ===
perishing through Satan, was born of the Holy Virgin, and having lived excellently, showed us the path by the opposites of those things, that is, by fasting and humility, and having wisely overcome him who deceived us, brought us back again to our ancient inheritance. Desiring therefore to present all these things, the God-bearing Fathers present throughout the Triodion first the Old Testament events, of which the first is the creation and the expulsion of Adam from food, of which we now make remembrance; then also the rest through the words of Moses and the prophets, and briefly of David, adding certain things from grace. Then in order also the events of the New Testament, of which the first is the Annunciation, by the unutterable providence of God, ever found in the holy Forty Days; then through Lazarus and the Bearing of Palms, and the Holy Great Week through the reading of the sacred Gospel, and the very holy and saving Passions of Christ, chanted according to tone.[^212] Then also through the Resurrection and the rest, even unto the Descent of the Holy Spirit, through the reading of the sacred Acts, how the proclamation was made and gathered all the saints; for the Acts confirm the Resurrection because of the miracles. Because, therefore, on account of Adam's not fasting even once, we suffered so great things, the remembrance of this is presented now at the entrance of the holy Forty Days, that remembering how great an evil not fasting introduced, we may hasten joyfully to embrace and keep the Fast; so that, having obtained through weeping, fasting, and humbling ourselves until God visit us what Adam failed to attain (namely deification), for without these it is not easy to receive what we lost. Be it known, moreover, that this holy and Great Forty Days is the tithe of the whole year.[^213] For since through laziness we do not wish to fast continually and to cease from evil deeds, the Apostles and divine Fathers handed this down as a kind of harvest for souls, that whatever unseemly things we committed during the whole year, we may cleanse with contrition and fasting, being humbled; which Fast we are also bound to keep most carefully. But also the other three fasts: of the holy Apostles, I mean, of the Mother of God, and of the Nativity of Christ, the divine Apostles established for the four seasons of the year, honoring this Forty Days above all on account of the holy Passions, and because Christ fasted this and was glorified, and Moses, having fasted forty days, received the law; and Elijah likewise, and Daniel, and as many as were proven before God. And that fasting is a good thing is shown by the contrary through Adam. For this reason the expulsion of Adam was devised here by the holy Fathers. By Thine unutterable tender-heartedness, O Christ our God, make us worthy of the food of Paradise, and have mercy on us, as the only Lover of mankind. Amen.

## Monday of the First Week
*Beginning of the Holy and Great Fast.*[^230]

*The paraecclesiarch sounds the klepalo before dawn, on account of the evening collation, and we all gather into church as usual. We sing the Midnight Office without prostrations, only at the end making three great prostrations, with the prayer: "O Lord and Master:" This takes place on the first day only. On the other days of the Holy and Great Fast we sing the entire Midnight Office with 16 prostrations, as usual.*[^231]

*Then the Priest, having arisen, makes the usual metania to the superior, and enters into the holy Altar; and taking the censer, stands before the holy Table, and censing crosswise, exclaims:*
**Priest:** Blessed is our God:
*And when we have answered:*
**Reader:** Amen.
*The Priest goes forth and censes. And the appointed psalm-reader says: Trisagion, always without metanias. After "Our Father," the Priest exclaims:*
**Priest:** For Thine is the kingdom:
*And after the Amen:*
**Reader:** Lord, have mercy, 12 times. O come, let us worship: thrice, without metanias. *And Psalms 19 and 20; at their conclusion, Trisagion, without metanias. After "Our Father:" the usual troparia. While these are being said, the Priest censes the holy Altar and the entire church. Then the litany:*
**Priest:** Have mercy on us, O God:
*Then:*
**Priest:** Again we pray for our pious Emperor:
*Exclamation:*
**Priest:** For a merciful and man-befriending God art Thou:
**Reader:** Amen.
*And he says:* In the name of the Lord bless, father.
*And immediately the Priest:*
**Priest:** Glory to the holy, consubstantial: *And the rest.*

*And the brother whose day it is, standing in the appointed place, says the Six Psalms with all attention, not rushing, but with the fear of God, as invisibly conversing with God Himself.*[^232]

*And no one has authority to whisper, nor to spit or clear the throat, but rather to give heed to the words spoken by the psalm-reader, having their hands folded upon the breast, heads bowed, and eyes cast down, looking with inward spiritual eyes toward the east, praying for our sins, remembering death, future torment, and eternal life.*

*At the beginning of the 120th Psalm, the Priest says the morning prayers. At the conclusion of the psalms: Glory, and now and ever, Alleluia, thrice. Then the Great Litany, and the exclamation.*

*And the appointed monk sings Alleluia in the Tone that happens to be in order from the Octoechos.*[^233]
*Verse 1: From the night my spirit awakes early unto Thee, O God, for Thy commandments are a light upon the earth.*
*Verse 2: Learn righteousness, ye that dwell upon the earth.*
*Verse 3: Envy shall seize an untaught people.*
*Verse 4: Bring evil upon them, O Lord, bring evil upon the glorious of the earth.*
*And after each verse, Alleluia, thrice.*

*Then the Triadica of the Tone: We sing them with compunction, in a loud voice and slowly with chanting. At the second verse: Glory: and at the third: Now and ever:*[^234]

*Then we recite one kathisma of the Psalter. After the antiphons, at Alleluia we make three prostrations, all equally, following the superior or the ecclesiarch.*

*After the first kathisma, the penitential sessional hymns of the Octoechos.*
*Then the reading.*
