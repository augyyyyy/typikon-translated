On this day, the most divine Fathers instituted the commemoration of all people who have piously fallen asleep from the ages, for the following reason:[^47] Forasmuch as some people often met an untimely death while journeying abroad, in the sea, in impassable mountains, on precipices and chasms, in pestilences, famines, conflagrations, freezes, wars, cold,

=== LEAF p22 ===

and having endured all other manners of death; likewise being poor and infirm, they were not accounted worthy of the prescribed psalms and memorial hymns. Moved by love for mankind, the divine Fathers decreed that the Catholic Church celebrate a general commemoration of all these, having received this tradition from the holy Apostles, so that those who did not receive the individual prescribed services may also be remembered in this common commemoration, showing that what is done on their behalf procures great benefit for them. In one way, then, the Church of God makes commemoration for souls thus. In the second place, since they intended to set the Second Coming of Christ upon the following day, they fittingly make commemoration of souls, as it were supplicating the dread and impartial Judge to show His customary mercy unto them and to establish them in the promised delight. In another way, as the holy Fathers intend to set forth Adam’s expulsion on the coming Sunday, they devise this day as a kind of resting-place, and most conclusively of all, by our present resting-place today, they might begin as it were from the beginning; for the last of all who have lived among us shall be examined at the unendurable judgment, and by terrifying men with these things, they may make them well-disposed toward the ascetical struggles of the Fast. We always make commemoration of souls on Saturday, because "Sabbath" signifies "rest" in Hebrew; and for the dead, as having rested from worldly and all other cares, on the day of rest we make supplications for them; which indeed is observed on every Saturday, but today we commemorate them universally, praying for every pious soul. For the divine Fathers knew that the memorials made for the reposed—I mean alms and liturgies—bring them great relief and benefit; and they urge the Church to perform this both privately and in common, having received this from the holy Apostles, as has been said. Even as Dionysius the Areopagite says, that the things done for them benefit souls;[^49] and this is testified by many others as well. Gregory the Theologian also establishes that offerings for the departed are good, in his funeral oration on his brother Caesarius; and the great Chrysostom, writing to the Philippians, says thus: "Let us devise benefit for the departed; let us give them fitting assistance—alms, I mean, and oblations; for this brings great alleviation to them, and especially gain and benefit. For not in vain was this decreed, nor was it by chance that it was handed down to the Church of God by His all-wise disciples, that the priests should make commemoration of the reposed in faith at the dread Mysteries." And again: "Include in thy testament thy children and relatives, and let thy document have an heir and one name; but let not thy memorial leave the poor without a portion, and I will be surety for them." Whatsoever things are done for the departed provide benefit, as the divine Fathers declare. For this reason we make commemoration of souls; do Thou, O Master Christ, establish them in the dwellings of Thy righteous ones, and have mercy on us, as Thou alone art immortal. Amen.

On this day we make commemoration of the Second and Incorrupt Coming of Christ,[^78] which the most divine Fathers appointed after the two parables, lest anyone, learning of God's love for mankind in them, should live negligently, saying: "God is the Lover of mankind, and whenever I turn away from sin, I have all things ready to be fulfilled." They appointed this dread day here, so that by terrifying the negligent with death and the expectation of future evils, they might lead them back unto virtue, not relying solely on His love for mankind, but also beholding that the Judge is righteous, and renders unto each according to his works. In another way, after the departed souls, it was fitting that the Judge should come: and the present feast is a certain type, because the end of all feasts is set today, even as it shall itself be our final end. For it is fitting to observe that on the coming Sunday they will set the beginning of the world and the very fall of Adam from Paradise, while the present day is the end of all days and of the world itself. They placed it during Meatfare, curtailing, I think, food and gluttony for the sake of the awe of the feast, and summoning us unto mercy toward our neighbor. And in another way: since through feeding we were cast out of Eden, and came under judgment and curse, for this reason they placed the present feast here; and because on the next Sunday we are to be cast out with Adam after the pattern of Eden, until Christ, having come, shall lead us back into Paradise. It is called the "Second Coming," because He first came to us in the flesh, but quietly and without glory; but now He will come with supernatural wonders and manifest radiance from the heavens, and with a body, that He may be recognized by all: that this is He Who also came at first and redeemed our race, and Who will Himself judge whether they kept well what was given them. When this coming will take place, no one knows; for this the Lord concealed even from the Apostles. Certain signs, however, will precede it, which some of the Saints have explained at greater length. Before His coming, the Antichrist will come, and he will be born, as Saint Hippolytus of Rome says, of a polluted woman and supposed virgin, from among the Jews of the tribe of Dan, who was the servant of Jacob;[^79] and he will live in imitation of Christ, and perform miracles such as Christ worked, and will raise the dead. Yet all these he will do in delusion: both his birth, and his flesh, and all the rest, as the Apostle says. And then, he says, the son of perdition will be revealed in all power, signs, and lying wonders. Yet the devil himself will not be transformed into flesh, as John of Damascus says; but a man born of fornication will receive all Satan's working, and will arise suddenly, and then show himself good and gentle unto all. And there will be a great famine then: he will please men, study the divine Scriptures, and practice fasting; and he will be compelled by men and proclaimed king; and he will love the Jewish race exceedingly, arrive in Jerusalem, and rebuild their temple. Enoch and Elijah will come, preaching unto the people not to receive him: but he will openly kill them and cut off their heads. He will give abundant freedom to those found doing mad deeds, who will hide in the mountains; yet those days will be shortened for the sake of the elect, and there will be a great famine, all the elements being transformed, so that almost all will be consumed. After these things, suddenly, like lightning from heaven, the coming of the Lord will take place, His precious Cross going before Him, and a river of fire boiling before Him, cleansing the whole earth from defilement. The Antichrist will immediately be seized, and his servants, and they will be delivered unto eternal fire. And when the Angels sound the trumpet, suddenly from the end of the earth and from every element, all the human race will come unto Jerusalem, because it is the center of the earth, and there the thrones will sit for judgment: nevertheless with their own bodies and souls,

all having passed over unto incorruption, and having one appearance, and the elements having received a transformation for the better.[^80] And with a single word the Lord will separate the righteous from the sinners. And the doers of good will go, receiving eternal life; but the sinners again unto eternal torment, and never will their end be. It is to be known that Christ will not require fasting then, nor nakedness, nor wonders, though these also are good: but what is many times better than these: alms and simplicity of heart. For He will speak unto the righteous and the sinners six commandments: "For I was hungry, and ye gave Me food; I was thirsty, and ye gave Me drink; I was a stranger, and ye took Me in; naked, and ye clothed Me; I was sick, and ye visited Me; I was in prison, and ye came unto Me; for forasmuch as ye did it unto one of the least of these, ye did it unto Me": which everyone can do according to his strength.[^81] Then everyone will confess that the Lord Jesus Christ is to the glory of God the Father.[^82] The torments which the holy Gospel has handed down are these: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth, their worm shall not die, and their fire shall not be quenched, and cast him into outer darkness.[^83]

*Ode 7. Heirmos: "We have sinned, we have transgressed:"*

Let us fall down and weep before that judgment, O ye faithful, when the heavens shall perish, the stars fall, and all the earth be shaken, that at the end we may find the God of our fathers merciful.

Unconstrained is the examination, dread is the judgment there, where the Judge cannot be deceived, where there is no accepting of persons for gifts: then spare me, O Master, and deliver me from all Thy dread wrath.

The Lord comes to judge: who shall endure the sight of Him? Tremble, O wretched soul, tremble, and prepare thy deeds for departure, that thou mayest find Him merciful and peaceful, the blessed God of our fathers.

The unquenchable fire disturbs me, the bitterest worm gnaws me, soul-destroying Hades terrifies me, yet I am not moved unto compunction at all: but O Lord, Lord, before the end establish me in Thy fear.

I fall down before Thee, and I offer unto Thee my words as tears: I have sinned as the harlot never sinned, and have committed iniquity as no other upon earth: but have compassion, O Master, upon Thy creature, and call me back.

Turn, O soul, repent, uncover what is secret, speak unto God Who knows all things: "Thou knowest my secrets, O only Savior; but do Thou Thyself have mercy on me, as David sings, according to Thy mercy."[^84]

*Ode 8. Heirmos: "Him Whom the heavenly hosts glorify:"*

Pondering the meeting of Thy dread Second Coming, O Lord, I tremble at Thy threat, I fear Thy wrath: from this hour, I cry: save me unto the ages!

When Thou, O God, shalt judge all things, who among the earthborn shall endure, being subject to passions? For the unquenchable fire then, and the intensely gnawing worm shall receive the condemned unto the ages.

When Thou shalt summon all breath to judge together, O Christ: great fear then, great distress, only deeds being of help unto the ages.
