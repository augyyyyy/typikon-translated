On this same day, the Third Sunday of the Fasts, we celebrate the veneration of the precious and life-creating Cross, for this reason: Since during the forty-day fasts we also are in a certain manner crucified, being put to death from the passions, and we experience a sense of bitterness, falling into despondency and growing faint: the precious and life-creating Cross is set forth, both refreshing and strengthening us, and reminding us of the Passions of our Lord Jesus Christ, and comforting us. If our God was crucified for our sake, how much

=== LEAF p264 ===
how much does it behoove us to labor for His sake, lightening our afflictions by the setting forth of the Master's sorrows, and by the remembrance and expectation of the Cross of glory? For as our Savior, having ascended the Cross, was glorified through an ignominious life and through embitterment, so it behooves us also to do, that we may be glorified together with Him, even if at any time we suffer something sorrowful. And furthermore, just as those traversing a long and rough path, weighed down by labor, if they find somewhere a leafy tree offering good shade, rest for a little while by sitting down under it, and being as it were rejuvenated, finish the remainder of the way: so also now in this time of fasting, in this sorrowful way and ascetic struggle, the life-bearing Cross was planted in the midst by the holy Fathers, granting us respite and refreshment, and rendering those who have labored courageous and light-hearted for the remaining labor. Or as at the arrival of kings, their standards and scepters go before them, and then the king himself arrives, rejoicing and making merry over the victory, and his subjects rejoice together with him: so also our Lord Jesus Christ, desiring to show forth all the more His victory over death, and to go forth with glory on the day of the Resurrection, sent forth beforehand His scepter, the royal standard, the life-creating Cross, comforting and refreshing us as ones filling up the excessive measure, and preparing us to be ready to receive the King Himself as far as possible, and to praise Him radiant in victory. Moreover, in the midst of the week of the Holy Forty Days: because the Holy Forty Days is like unto a bitter spring, on account of compunction and the bitterness and grief that befall us from the fast; as therefore the divine Moses cast the tree into the midst of that spring and sweetened it,[^688] so also God, Who brought us across the spiritual Red Sea and delivered us from Pharaoh, through the life-creating tree of the precious and life-creating Cross sweetens the bitterness of the fasts of the Forty Days, and comforts us as ones dwelling in the wilderness, until He brings us up unto the spiritual Jerusalem through His Resurrection. Or, since the Cross is called and is the tree of life, and that tree was planted in the midst of the Paradise of Eden: fittingly also did the divine Fathers plant the tree of the Cross in the midst of the Holy Forty Days, both commemorating the greed of Adam and depicting his redemption through the present Tree; for tasting of this, we no longer die, but rather are made alive. By its power, O Christ God, preserve us also from the temptations of the evil one; and account us worthy to bow down in worship before Thy divine Passions and life-bearing Resurrection, having brightly passed through the forty-day course; and have mercy on us, as Thou alone art good and the Lover of mankind. Amen.

# FOURTH WEEK OF THE GREAT FAST

## On Monday of the Fourth Week of the Great Fast
*At Matins:*  
*Trinity Hymns of the Tone.*  
*After the first stichology, we say the penitential Sessional Hymns of the Octoechos, of the occurring Tone.*  
*After the second stichology, we say the present Sessional Hymn, Tone 8, of Lord Joseph. Podoben: "Having arisen" (Воскре́съ є҆сѝ):*[^713]  
Let us, the faithful, bow down in worship before the all-honorable Tree whereon the Creator of all was lifted up; it is set forth, sanctifying those who draw near in soul and body, cleansing the defilement of sin from those who fast faithfully, and who ever praise Christ, the only Benefactor.

*Glory... the same. Both now... Theotokion, Podoben:*  
The Word of the Father came down upon the earth, and an Angel of light cried unto the Theotokos: Rejoice, O blessed one, who alone hast preserved the chamber, having received the conception of the pre-eternal God and Lord, that as God He may save the human race from deception!

*After the third stichology, Sessional Hymn, Tone 3. Podoben: "Beholding the beauty of thy virginity" (Красотѣ̀ дѣ́вства):*[^714]  
Having now reached the midpoint of self-control, and having anticipated the veneration of Thy life-bearing Cross, O Christ, falling down before Thee we cry aloud: Great art Thou, O Lover of mankind, and great are Thy works, in that Thou hast shown forth Thy precious Cross, before which bowing down in worship with fear we cry out: Glory to Thy boundless tender-heartedness!

*Glory... the same. Both now... Theotokion, Podoben:*  
As an uncultivated vine, O Virgin, thou hast blossomed forth the most fair Cluster, which pours out for us saving wine that gladdens the souls and bodies of all; therefore, calling thee blessed as the cause of good things, we ever cry out unto thee with the Angel: Rejoice, O full of grace!

*Canon of the Menaion, and the present triode, and the Ode is sung with verses.*  
*Ode 6, Tone 8. Heirmos: "The charioteer of Pharaoh" (Колесницегони́телѧ фараѡ́на):*[^715]  
The radiant veneration of the Cross has appeared unto all, sending forth the sun-like ray of salvation, and illumining all who are held fast in the darkness of passions: let us hasten to embrace it in purity!
