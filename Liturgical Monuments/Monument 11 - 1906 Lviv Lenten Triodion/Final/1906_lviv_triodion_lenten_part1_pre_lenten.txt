# PART I: PRE-LENTEN PERIOD (SUNDAYS OF PUBLICAN & PHARISEE, PRODIGAL SON, MEATFARE, AND CHEESEFARE)

[Headpiece: ornate rectangular woodcut depicting Christ the Savior amidst foliate scrollwork]

*The Lenten Triodion*[^7]

*That is, the Three-Ode Canon, containing the proper liturgical sequence, with God the Holy One.*

*The Sunday on which is read the Holy and Sacred Gospel of the parable of the Publican and the Pharisee.*

*On Saturday evening, after the Introductory Psalm, we chant: "Blessed is the man," the entire first Kathisma.*

*At "Lord, I have cried,"*[^8] *we set 10 stichera: and we chant 3 Resurrectional stichera of the Octoechos, and 4 Anatolian:*[^9] *and 2 idiomelon stichera of the Triodion, repeating the first.*

Let us not pray after the manner of the Pharisee, brethren; for he that exalts himself shall be humbled. Let us humble ourselves before God, calling out with fasting as the Publican: Cleanse us sinners, O God!

The Pharisee, overcome by vainglory, and the Publican, bowed down by repentance, drew near unto Thee, the only Master. But the one, having boasted, was deprived of good things; while the other, uttering nothing, was accounted worthy of gifts. In his sighing confirm me, O Christ God, as Thou art the Lover of mankind.

*Glory, Tone 8:*

O Almighty Lord, I know how great is the power of tears: for they brought up Hezekiah from the gates of death, and delivered the sinful woman from the transgressions of many years, and justified the Publican above the Pharisee; and I pray Thee: Numbering me with them, have mercy on me.

*Now and ever, the first Theotokion of the Tone of the week.*

*At the Litiya, the sticheron of the Saint of the monastery, according to custom.*

*Glory, Tone 3:*

Having understood the difference between the Publican and the Pharisee, O my soul, abhor the haughty voice of the one, and emulate the prayer of deep compunction of the other, and cry aloud: O God, cleanse me a sinner, and have mercy on me!

*Now and ever, the Resurrectional Theotokion, in the same Tone.*

*At the Aposticha, the Resurrectional stichera of the Octoechos in alphabetical order.*

*Glory, Tone 5:*

With mine eyes weighed down by mine iniquities, I cannot gaze and behold the height of heaven; but receive me, O Savior, as the repenting Publican, and have mercy on me!

*Now and ever, Theotokion, in the same Tone: "Thou art the temple and the gate:"*

*Troparion: "Rejoice, O Virgin Theotokos," is said thrice. And there takes place the blessing of loaves, and the distribution: and the great reading from the Epistles of the Apostles.*

*The sequence of the Saint who falls on this Sunday is chanted on Friday at Compline, or behold when the ecclesiarch shall decide; unless there happen to be a great Saint, or the Saint of the church: for the temple...*

*...and not of a great Saint, follows more the great Saint, on whatever church feast is his, for which a vigil must take place, and in no way is it to be left out in his commemoration, nor is it transferred to another day. But we chant the service of the church, as also to the Meeting of the Lord, on all days on which the Meeting of the Lord also occurs.*

*At Matins, after the Six Psalms, "God is the Lord," in the Tone of the Octoechos. And we say the Resurrectional Troparion twice, the Theotokion once, and the customary Kathisma readings. Sessional Hymns of the Octoechos. After the Evlogitaria, the Troparia: "The angelic council:" the Hypakoe, the Graduals, and the Prokimenon of the Tone: "Let every breath:" the Resurrectional Gospel of the week. "Having beheld the Resurrection of Christ:" Psalm 50.*

*Glory, Tone 8:*[^10]

Open unto me the doors of repentance, O Giver of life; for my spirit rises early toward Thy holy temple, bearing the temple of my body all defiled: but as Thou art compassionate, cleanse me by Thy loving-kindness.

*Now and ever, Theotokion:*

Guide me into the paths of salvation, O Theotokos; for with shameful sins have I stained my soul, and in laziness have I squandered all my life: but by thine intercessions deliver me from all uncleanness.

*Then, Tone 6:*

*Have mercy on me, O God, according to Thy great mercy; and according to the multitude of Thy tender mercies blot out my transgression.*

Pondering the multitude of the dread deeds I have committed, wretched that I am, I tremble at the fearful day of judgment: but trusting in the mercy of Thy loving-kindness, like David I cry unto Thee: Have mercy on me, O God, according to Thy great mercy!

*The Resurrectional Canon of the Octoechos, with the Heirmos to 4; and the Cross-Resurrectional to 2; and the Theotokos to 2; and in the Triodion to 6.*

*A composition of George. Whose acrostic in the Theotokia is: OF GEORGE.*[^11]

*Ode 1, Tone 6. Heirmos: "Traversing the deep on foot as on dry land, Israel:"*

Christ, Who by parables leads all unto the correction of life, lifts up the Publican from humility, showing the Pharisee humbled by pride.

Beholding high-exalting honor from humility, and a dire fall from haughtiness, emulate the good deeds of the Publican, and abhor the malice of the Pharisee.

Every good is emptied out through haughtiness, but every evil is destroyed through humility: let us believers embrace this, openly abhorring the image of the vainglorious.

Wishing His disciples to be humble-minded, the King of all, instructing them, taught them to emulate the sighing and humility of the Publican.

*Glory:*

Like the Publican I groan, and with unceasing laments, O Lord, I now draw near to Thy loving-kindness: show pity also on me, who now spend my life in humility.

*Now and ever, Theotokion:*

*[Margin: G]* Understanding, counsel, expectation, body, soul, and spirit, O Sovereign Lady, I commit unto thee: from cruel enemies and tribulations, and from future condemnation deliver me, and save me.

*Katavasia: "I shall open my mouth:"*

*Ode 3. Heirmos: "There is none holy like unto Thee:"*

From the dunghill of passions the humble one is exalted, but from the height of virtues every haughty one falls grievously: let us flee the image of his malice.

Vainglory squanders the wealth of righteousness, but humility scatters the multitude of passions: show us, O Savior, who imitate this, to be partakers with the Publican.

Like the Publican let us also, smiting our breasts, cry out with compunction: Cleanse us sinners, O God, that we may receive the forgiveness of our sins!

Let us come to zeal, O believers, cultivating meekness, dwelling together with humility in the groaning of the heart, in weeping and in prayer, that we may obtain forgiveness from God.

*Glory:*

Let us cast away, O believers, boastful pride, cruel haughtiness, and abominable arrogance, and the most wicked, unbecoming ferocity of the Pharisee toward God.

*Now and ever, Theotokion:*

*[Margin: E]* Fleeing unto thee, the only refuge, I hope that I may not fall away from good expectation: but that I may obtain thy help, O Pure One, delivered from every harm of evil things.

*Sessional Hymns, Tone 4. Model: "Speedily anticipate:"*

Humility exalted the Publican held fast by evils, who groaned and cried unto the Creator: "Cleanse me!" But haughtiness brought down from righteousness the wretched Pharisee who spoke with boasting. Therefore let us emulate the good, departing from evil.

*Glory:*

Humility of old exalted the Publican, who cried out with weeping: "Cleanse me!" and was justified. Let us all imitate him, who have fallen into the depth of evils, and let us cry to the Savior from the depth of the heart: We have sinned, cleanse us, O only Lover of mankind!

*Now and ever, Theotokion:*

Speedily accept our supplications, O Sovereign Lady, and bear them unto Thy Son and God, O all-immaculate Lady: loosen the tribulations of those who run unto thee, shatter the snares, and cast down the audacity of those who impiously arm themselves against thy servants, O all-pure one.

*Ode 4. Heirmos: "Christ is my power:"*

The Word, Who humbled Himself even unto the form of a servant, showed an excellent way of exaltation, even humility: imitating which, everyone who humbles himself is exalted.

The righteous man was exalted, and the Pharisee fell down; but weighed down by a multitude of evils, the Publican humbled himself, yet he was exalted, unexpectedly justified.

Folly proved to be an intercessor of poverty from the wealth of virtues; but humility immediately proved to be an abundance of righteousness from extreme poverty: which let us acquire.

Thou didst foretell, O Master, that Thou wouldst oppose the proud in every way, and giving Thy grace unto the humble, O Savior: send down Thy grace upon us who are now humbled.

*Glory:*

The Savior and Master, Who ever leads us up unto divine exaltation, showed forth humility that works on high: for with His own hands He washed the feet of His disciples.

*Now and ever, Theotokion:*

*[Margin: O]* As thou didst bring forth the Unapproachable Light, O Virgin, disperse the darkness of my soul with the illuminating radiance, and guide my life into the paths of salvation.

*Ode 5. Heirmos: "With Thy divine light, O Good One:"*

Let us strive to emulate the virtues of the Pharisee, and to emulate the humility of the Publican, hating in both the senseless conceit and the ruin of their falls.

The vain course of righteousness was exposed when the Pharisee joined conceit unto it; but straightway the Publican acquired as a companion humility, that virtue which works on high.

The Pharisee thought to run as a charioteer in the virtues; but running on foot, the Publican outran the Lybian chariot, having yoked humility unto compassion.

Having all understood with our minds the parable of the Publican, come, let us emulate his tears, offering a contrite spirit unto God, seeking the remission of our sins.

*Glory:*

Let us who are prudent cast far away the boastful, wicked, haughty, and arrogant disposition of the Pharisee, cruel and high-vaunting, lest we be stripped of divine grace.

*Now and ever, Theotokion:*

*[Margin: R]* Send down upon all of us who run unto thee the rod of power, O Mother of God, granting us to rule in the midst of all enemies, and delivering us from every harm.

*Ode 6. Heirmos: "The sea of life:"*

Together the Publican and the Pharisee ran the course of life; but the one, held fast by conceit, sank shamefully, while the other was saved by humility.

As we sail the perilous course of life, let us zealously emulate the mind of the Publican; but let us flee the haughtiness of the abominable Pharisee, and we shall live.

Let us emulate the ways of Jesus the Savior and His humility, desiring to obtain the unceasing dwelling of joy, taking up our abode in the land of the living.

Thou hast shown unto Thy disciples, O Master, humility that works on high: girded with a towel about Thy loins, Thou didst wash their feet, commanding them to imitate this example.

*Glory:*

The Pharisee traversed life in virtues, and the Publican in transgressions; but the one, through foolish pride, fell away from humility, while the other was exalted, having shown himself humble-minded.

*Now and ever, Theotokion:*

*[Margin: G]* Created naked in simplicity and artless life, the enemy deceived me through the deceit of transgression and the coarseness of the flesh; but now through thine intercession, O Maiden, I am saved.

*Kontakion, Tone 4. Model: "Thou hast appeared today:"*

Let us flee the haughty speaking of the Pharisee, and let us learn the height of the humble words of the Publican, crying out with repentance: O Savior of the world, cleanse Thy servants!

*Second Kontakion, Tone 3. Model: "The Virgin today:"*

Let us bring Publican-like groanings unto the Lord, and let us sinners draw near unto Him as to a Master: for He wills the salvation of all men, and grants forgiveness unto all that repent, for for our sake He was incarnate, being God co-beginningless with the Father.

*Ikos:*

Let us all humble ourselves, brethren, with groanings and with weeping let us strike our conscience, that at that eternal judgment we may appear there as faithful, innocent, having obtained forgiveness: for there truly is forgiveness, which let us now beseech that we may behold; there pain, sorrow, and sighing from the depth have fled away, in marvelous Eden, whereof Christ is the Creator, being God co-beginningless with the Father.

*Short Preface of the Synaxaria, that is, the collections of Xanthopoulos, for the Triodion Synaxaria.*[^12]

*The collections of Nikephoros Kallistos Xanthopoulos for the notable feasts of the Triodion, each giving the historical reason how and when it came to be from the beginning, and for what reason they are held thus now, and how they were instituted by the holy and God-bearing Fathers with certain known particular details, beginning from the Publican and the Pharisee and ending even unto All Saints.*

*The monthly Menaion Synaxarion should be read first at the Seventh Ode according to custom, and after that the present one.*

*These verses are spoken after the readings. Verses on the Triodion:*

*Unto the Creator of things above and things below:*
*Accept the Thrice-Holy hymn from the Angels,*
*And the Three-Ode Canon also from men.*

*On the Publican and the Pharisee:*
*Whoso lives like the Pharisee is far from the Church:*
*For Christ is within, received by humility.*

On the present day, with God, we also begin the Triodion: which indeed many of our holy and God-bearing beauty-making Fathers, well and as was fitting, being moved by the Holy Spirit, composed as hymnody. The first of all who conceived this—I mean the Three Odes, after the pattern of the Persons of the Holy and Life-Originating Trinity—was the great hymnodist Cosmas,[^13] who conceived these odes for the great and holy Passion of the Lord God and our Savior Jesus Christ, sitting by the day's acrostics, briefly completing them. From him also the other Fathers, especially Theodore and Joseph the Studites, zealous for his emulation, composed also for the remaining weeks of the Holy and Great Forty Days, and first delivered them to their holy monastery, especially having instituted and left the hymns; and the other books, gathered and acquired here and there from the Fathers. And since Sunday concludes the first of the days as resurrectional, being the first, and the eighth, and the final, making for the second day—that is, Monday—they appointed the first Ode. Likewise for the third day—that is, Tuesday—the second Ode. For the fourth day—that is, Wednesday—the third. For the fifth, the fourth Ode, which is Thursday. And for the sixth day—that is, Friday—the fifth Ode. For the sixth, Saturday, as also for the seventh, and the other two, the eighth and the ninth, which all days commonly possess as most true. Thus indeed the divine Cosmas placed the Four-Ode Canon for Great Saturday, and so they did: even if later the most wise Emperor Leo, having commanded the monk Mark, who was revealed as a bishop, to complete the canons, accomplished it. By need it is called the Triodion, although it does not always contain three odes: for it presents completely perfected rules, but received its title from the greater part; or because of Great Week, as was said before. It was the intention of our holy Fathers, by the whole book of the Triodion, briefly to commemorate all the divine benefactions toward us from the beginning, and to set forth for the remembrance of all: how we were created by Him, and were cast out from the delight of Paradise, having transgressed the commandment given to us for our knowledge, and were rejected through the envy of the prime-evil serpent and enemy...

...cast down for his pride. And how we remained rejected from good things and led by the devil. And how the Son and Word of God, having suffered through His bowels of compassion, bowed down the heavens and came down, and dwelt in the Virgin, and for our sake became man, and by His life after that showed forth the ascent unto heaven, that is to say, with humility leading the way, and by fasting, and by the rejection of evil things, and by His other deeds. And how He suffered and arose, and ascended again into heaven, and sent down the Holy Spirit upon His holy disciples and Apostles: and how the Son of God and perfect God was proclaimed concerning all these things. And what again the divine Elias worked together by the grace of the All-Holy Spirit, so that at the end of all the saints, they gathered them together by preaching, filling the world on high: which was also the intention from the beginning of Him Who created us. But in these thoughts of the Triodion, the present three feasts—of the Publican and the Pharisee, of the Prodigal Son, and of the Second Coming—were conceived by the holy Fathers as a kind of preliminary training and instruction. That we might be pre-disposed and prepared for the spiritual labors of the fasts, putting away our defiled habits from custom. And first of all, they set before us the parable of the Publican and the Pharisee, and name it the Pre-announced Week. For even as those who are about to go forth to bodily warfare learn beforehand from the commanders the time of battle, so that having cleansed and polished their weapons, and having ordered all other things well, and having removed every stumbling block from their midst, they eagerly brace themselves for the contests, and provide what is needed. And often before the engagement they bring to mind words, narratives, and parables, sharpening the souls of the young unto zeal, and driving away laziness, fear, despondency, and whatever else is harmful. So also the divine Fathers sound the trumpet beforehand, sending fasting as an army against the demons, so that for our souls they might cleanse away any pre-existing passion, and the poison wrought by long time; and further, that what we do not possess of good things we might eagerly acquire, and having armed ourselves fittingly, might thus go forth prepared unto the labors of the fast. For since the first weapon unto virtue is repentance and humility, and conversely the greatest obstacle unto humility is pride and arrogance, they set forth this present venerable parable from the divine Gospel first of all. Teaching us through the Pharisee to put away the passion of pride and arrogance, and through the Publican conversely to produce the contrary of this passion, humility and repentance. For the first and worst passion is pride and arrogance, through which the devil fell from heaven, who before that was Lucifer, but through these became darkness and was so called. And the forefather Adam on account of these was cast out from paradise. Through these the saints teach us in a certain manner that no one should exalt himself over his good deeds, nor rise up against his neighbor, but always be humble: for the Lord resists the proud, but gives grace unto the humble; for it is better for the sinner to turn back than for him who does right to exalt himself. For I say unto you, He said, that the Publican went down justified rather than the Pharisee. Therefore the parable shows that no one should exalt himself, even if he has good deeds; but always humble himself, and pray from the soul unto God, even if he fall into the lowest evils, for he is not far from salvation. For the Publican is he who takes taxes from the princes, and through extreme injustice buys and acquires therefrom. And the Pharisee is as one cut off, perhaps, and surpassing others in knowledge. And the Sadducee from a certain Zadok, which means righteous: for tsedek is righteousness. And there were three sects among the Jews: Essenes, Pharisees, and Sadducees, who accepted neither the resurrection, nor an angel, nor the spirit to exist. By the prayers of all Thy holy hymnodists, O Christ our God, have mercy on us. Amen.

*Ode 7. Heirmos: "The God-given furnace:"*

Exalted by deeds of righteousness, the Pharisee was grievously puffed up by the snares of vainglory, boasting immeasurably; but the Publican, borne up on the light wings of humility, drew near unto God.

Using humility as a ladder after a certain manner, the Publican was raised up to heavenly height; but the wretched Pharisee, lifted up by rotten pride and foolishness, descended to the nethermost hell.

The deceitful one ensnares the righteous by images of vainglory, and binds sinners with the nets of despair; but let us who emulate the Publican strive to be delivered from both evils.

With prayer let us fall down before our God, with tears and warm groanings, imitating the Publican's humility that works on high, chanting, O believers: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!

*Glory:*

Instructing Thy disciples, Thou didst foretell, O Master, not to mind high things, but teaching them to associate with the humble, O Savior. Therefore we cry unto Thee, O believers: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!

*Now and ever, Theotokion:*

*[Margin: I]* Knowing thee, O Pure One, as beauty and the divine ladder which Jacob saw of old stretched out on high, descending from above God incarnate, and leading earthly men up again.

*Ode 8. Heirmos: "From the flame for the venerable ones:"*

With a humble-minded disposition, sighing in prayer to the Lord, the Publican found mercy and was saved; but by the image of the cruel, boastful tongue, the Pharisee fell away from righteousness.

Let us believers flee the arrogance, lineage, and named purity of the Pharisee, emulating the well-pardoned humility and disposition of the Publican.

Let us believers utter the words of the Publican in the holy church: "O God, cleanse me," that together with the Publican we may obtain forgiveness, delivered from the ruin of the high-vaunting Pharisee.

Let us all emulate the groaning of the Publican, and speaking unto God with warm tears, let us cry out unto Him: O Lover of mankind, we have sinned, O compassionate and merciful One, cleanse and save us!

*Let us bless the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit, the Lord.*

God was inclined by the groaning of the Publican, and having justified him, showed unto all to be ever inclined, asking forgiveness of transgressions with groanings and tears.

*Now and ever, Theotokion:*

*[Margin: O]* We know of no other defense besides thee; I set thee forth as an intercessor, O all-immaculate, pure Lady, for I make thee an advocate unto Him Who was born of thee: show me free from all that afflict me.

*Ode 9. Heirmos: "God cannot be seen of men:"*

Having taken from Christ the way of exaltation, humility, as an image of salvation, let us emulate the manner of the Publican, casting far away the arrogance of pride, and moving the Lord to compassion by a disposition of humble-mindedness.

Let us cast away spiritual pride, and acquire the right way of humble-mindedness, not striving to justify ourselves; let us hate the haughtiness of vainglory, and with the Publican let us entreat God.

Let us offer Publican-like prayers of mercy unto the Creator, turning away from the ungrateful prayers of the Pharisees and their boastful voices which bring judgment upon their neighbor, that we may draw God's mercy and light unto ourselves.

Weighed down by an accumulation of many transgressions, I have surpassed the Publican in abundance of malice, and I have acquired the boastful haughtiness of the Pharisee; being utterly bereft of all good things, O Lord, spare me!

Count those worthy of Thy beatitude who are poor in spirit for Thy sake: for by the teaching of Thy commandment, we offer a contrite spirit unto Thee, O Savior; receiving it, save those who serve Thee.

*Glory:*

The Publican, having once prayed in the temple, went in faithfully and was justified: for coming with groanings and tears, and with contrition of heart, he laid aside the whole burden of sins through cleansing.

*Now and ever, Theotokion:*

*[Margin: U]* Grant unto us who worthily honor thee, O all-pure one, and who magnify thy childbearing, to sing, glorify, and bless thee, O only blessed one: for thou art the boast of Christians and an acceptable intercessor unto God.

*The Resurrectional Exapostilarion.*[^14]

*Then, of the Triodion. Model: "Let us go up with the disciples:"*

Let us flee the boastful speaking of the most wicked Pharisee, and let us learn the excellent humility of the Publican, that we may be exalted, crying unto God with him: Cleanse Thy servants, O Christ Savior, Who wast born of the Virgin of Thine own will, and didst endure the Cross, raising up the world by Thy divine power!

*Now and ever, Theotokion, same model:*

The Creator of creation, and God of all, took human flesh from thine immaculate womb, O all-immaculate Sovereign Lady, and renewed all my corrupt nature, leaving thee again after childbirth as thou wast before childbirth; therefore, praising thee with faith, we all cry out: Rejoice, O glory of the world!

*Glory, Tone 8:*

Condemning the Pharisee who justified himself by boasting of his deeds, O Lord, and justifying the Publican who humbled himself and asked for cleansing with sighs, Thou dost not give heed to haughty thoughts, nor dost Thou despise contrite hearts. Therefore we also fall down before Thee in humility, Who didst suffer for our sake: grant forgiveness, and great mercy.

*It should be known that during this week the thrice-accursed Armenians keep their abominable fast, called Artzivurion.*[^19] *We, however (the monastics), on each day (that is, on Wednesday and Friday as well) eat cheese and eggs (at the 4th hour); and the laity eat meat, overturning the decree of that great heresy. If the feast of the Meeting of the Lord happens to fall on Meatfare Saturday, the entire service for the deceased is chanted in the crypt, evening and morning; but if there is no crypt, it is chanted on this Saturday before the Sunday of the Prodigal Son; and there on Meatfare Saturday, the service is chanted for the Feast alone.*

## SUNDAY ON WHICH IS READ THE HOLY GOSPEL OF THE PARABLE OF THE PRODIGAL SON

*On Saturday evening at the Vigil, after the customary Kathisma reading of the Psalter, at "Lord, I have cried," we set 10 verses: and we sing 2 Resurrectional stichera of the Octoechos, and 3 Anatolian; and of the Triodion 5, repeating them. Tone 1:*

Having entrusted myself to a sinless and living land, sowing sin, I reaped the ears of sloth with a sickle, and bound the sheaves of my deeds into bundles, which I spread out not upon the threshing-floor of repentance. But I pray Thee, our pre-eternal Farmer and God, with the wind of Thy loving-kindness winnow the chaff of my deeds, and grant unto my soul the wheat of forgiveness, shutting me up into Thy heavenly granary, and save me.

Let us recognize, brethren, the power of the mystery: for when the prodigal son returned from sin unto the fatherly house, the all-good Father went forth to meet him and kissed him, and again bestowed upon him the knowledge of His own glory; and He made a mystical rejoicing for those on high, slaying the fatted calf, that we might worthily live together with the loving Father Who sacrificed, and with the glorious Offering, the Savior of our souls.

*Glory, Tone 2:*

Oh, of how many good things have I, wretch, deprived myself! Oh, from what a kingdom have I, passionate one, fallen away! I exhausted the wealth which I received; I transgressed the commandment. Woe is me, passionate soul! Thou art henceforth condemned to the eternal fire. Therefore, before the end, cry aloud unto Christ God: Receive me as the prodigal son, O God, and have mercy on me.

*Now and ever, Theotokion of the First Tone. Entrance. "O Gladsome Light:" Prokeimenon: "The Lord reigns:" At the Litiya, the sticheron of the temple as usual.*

*Glory, Tone 4:*

As the prodigal son I also came, O compassionate One, having spent my whole life in wandering; I scattered the riches which Thou gavest me, O Father: receive me repenting, O God, and have mercy on me.

*Now and ever, Theotokion of the same Tone. At the Aposticha of the Octoechos, in alphabetical order. Glory, Idiomelon, Tone 6:*

Having squandered the wealth of the fatherly gift, I pastured with senseless beasts, wretched one; and desiring their food, I wasted with hunger and was not satisfied; but returning unto the compassionate Father, I cry with tears: Receive me as a hired servant falling down before Thy love for mankind, and save me.

*Now and ever, Theotokion:*

*On this day we celebrate the return of the prodigal son,*[^27] *which our divine fathers instituted second in the Triodion, for the following reason: Because there are certain men who, conscious in themselves of many terrible deeds, and having lived very prodigally from youth, spending their time in drunkenness and impurities, and having thus fallen into the depth of evils, fall into despair (which is the offspring of pride); and hence they are by no means willing to come unto the care of virtue, and proposing evil deeds, they fall headlong into yet worse evils. The holy Fathers, having a loving and fatherly disposition toward such men, and desiring to rescue them from despair, instituted this parable here after the first, uprooting the passion of despair from the very root and raising them up to the acquisition of virtue, showing forth the loving and all-good compassions of God unto those who have sinned greatly and lived prodigally; demonstrating from this parable of Christ that there is no sin that can overcome His love for mankind. For the two sons of the man (that is, of the God-Man, the Word) are the righteous and the sinners. The elder is he who abides continually in the commandments of God and in His goodness, never departing from Him. The younger is he who loved sin, and by shameful deeds of self-will broke away from fellowship under the yoke; and in him the love of God for mankind was revealed, though he had lived prodigally: for he did not preserve whole that which was according to the image, but followed the wicked demon, and was enslaved through pleasures to the will of the evil one, yet was unable to satisfy his desire; for sin is an insatiable thing, tempting by custom for the sake of temporary enjoyment. This the parable likens unto husks, which are the food of swine: for husks at first offer something sweet, but afterward become something harsh and like chaff, which is very much the nature of shame and sin. Scarcely therefore recovering himself, the prodigal, perishing as it were from hunger for virtue, comes to the Father, saying: Father, I have sinned against heaven and before Thee, and am no more worthy to be called Thy son. And He receives him repenting, not reproaching him, but spreading forth His arms and embracing him, showing forth His divine and fatherly tender compassions. And He gives him a robe, that is, Holy Baptism; and a ring and pledge, the grace of the All-Holy Spirit; and unto these also sandals, that his steps toward God may no more be wounded by serpents and scorpions, but that he may rather be able to crush their heads. Afterward, with exceeding great joy, the Father slays the fatted calf for his sake, His Only-begotten Son, and gives him to partake of His Flesh and Blood. And though the elder son marveled at His overflowing mercy, saying whatever he said, the Lover of mankind silences him also, entering in with quiet, meek, and affectionate words: "Thou art ever with Me," saying, "and it was fitting to rejoice and celebrate with the Father, for this my son was dead in sin before, and is alive again, having repented of the irrational things he did; and he was lost, having been far from Me by custom of pleasures, and is found by Me Who suffered through My tender compassion and called him with a merciful disposition." This parable may also be applied to the Jewish people and to us. For this reason therefore this parable was instituted here by the Holy Fathers: uprooting, as was said, despair and fear of repenting by good deeds, and instructing unto repentance and compunction those who have sinned like the prodigal; which is indeed the greatest weapon for driving away the arrows of the adversary, and a mighty help. Through Thine ineffable love for mankind, O Christ our God, have mercy on us. Amen.*

*Verses:*
Call not to mind the transgressions of the departed, O Word;
Show not Thy good compassions to be dead.

On this day, the most divine Fathers instituted the commemoration of all people who have piously fallen asleep from the ages, for the following reason:[^47] Forasmuch as some people often met an untimely death while journeying abroad, in the sea, in impassable mountains, on precipices and chasms, in pestilences, famines, conflagrations, freezes, wars, cold, and having endured all other manners of death; likewise being poor and infirm, they were not accounted worthy of the prescribed psalms and memorial hymns. Moved by love for mankind, the divine Fathers decreed that the Catholic Church celebrate a general commemoration of all these, having received this tradition from the holy Apostles, so that those who did not receive the individual prescribed services may also be remembered in this common commemoration, showing that what is done on their behalf procures great benefit for them. In one way, then, the Church of God makes commemoration for souls thus. In the second place, since they intended to set the Second Coming of Christ upon the following day, they fittingly make commemoration of souls, as it were supplicating the dread and impartial Judge to show His customary mercy unto them and to establish them in the promised delight. In another way, as the holy Fathers intend to set forth Adam’s expulsion on the coming Sunday, they devise this day as a kind of resting-place, and most conclusively of all, by our present resting-place today, they might begin as it were from the beginning; for the last of all who have lived among us shall be examined at the unendurable judgment, and by terrifying men with these things, they may make them well-disposed toward the ascetical struggles of the Fast. We always make commemoration of souls on Saturday, because "Sabbath" signifies "rest" in Hebrew; and for the dead, as having rested from worldly and all other cares, on the day of rest we make supplications for them; which indeed is observed on every Saturday, but today we commemorate them universally, praying for every pious soul. For the divine Fathers knew that the memorials made for the reposed—I mean alms and liturgies—bring them great relief and benefit; and they urge the Church to perform this both privately and in common, having received this from the holy Apostles, as has been said. Even as Dionysius the Areopagite says, that the things done for them benefit souls;[^49] and this is testified by many others as well. Gregory the Theologian also establishes that offerings for the departed are good, in his funeral oration on his brother Caesarius; and the great Chrysostom, writing to the Philippians, says thus: "Let us devise benefit for the departed; let us give them fitting assistance—alms, I mean, and oblations; for this brings great alleviation to them, and especially gain and benefit. For not in vain was this decreed, nor was it by chance that it was handed down to the Church of God by His all-wise disciples, that the priests should make commemoration of the reposed in faith at the dread Mysteries." And again: "Include in thy testament thy children and relatives, and let thy document have an heir and one name; but let not thy memorial leave the poor without a portion, and I will be surety for them." Whatsoever things are done for the departed provide benefit, as the divine Fathers declare. For this reason we make commemoration of souls; do Thou, O Master Christ, establish them in the dwellings of Thy righteous ones, and have mercy on us, as Thou alone art immortal. Amen.

On this day we make commemoration of the Second and Incorrupt Coming of Christ,[^78] which the most divine Fathers appointed after the two parables, lest anyone, learning of God's love for mankind in them, should live negligently, saying: "God is the Lover of mankind, and whenever I turn away from sin, I have all things ready to be fulfilled." They appointed this dread day here, so that by terrifying the negligent with death and the expectation of future evils, they might lead them back unto virtue, not relying solely on His love for mankind, but also beholding that the Judge is righteous, and renders unto each according to his works. In another way, after the departed souls, it was fitting that the Judge should come: and the present feast is a certain type, because the end of all feasts is set today, even as it shall itself be our final end. For it is fitting to observe that on the coming Sunday they will set the beginning of the world and the very fall of Adam from Paradise, while the present day is the end of all days and of the world itself. They placed it during Meatfare, curtailing, I think, food and gluttony for the sake of the awe of the feast, and summoning us unto mercy toward our neighbor. And in another way: since through feeding we were cast out of Eden, and came under judgment and curse, for this reason they placed the present feast here; and because on the next Sunday we are to be cast out with Adam after the pattern of Eden, until Christ, having come, shall lead us back into Paradise. It is called the "Second Coming," because He first came to us in the flesh, but quietly and without glory; but now He will come with supernatural wonders and manifest radiance from the heavens, and with a body, that He may be recognized by all: that this is He Who also came at first and redeemed our race, and Who will Himself judge whether they kept well what was given them. When this coming will take place, no one knows; for this the Lord concealed even from the Apostles. Certain signs, however, will precede it, which some of the Saints have explained at greater length. Before His coming, the Antichrist will come, and he will be born, as Saint Hippolytus of Rome says, of a polluted woman and supposed virgin, from among the Jews of the tribe of Dan, who was the servant of Jacob;[^79] and he will live in imitation of Christ, and perform miracles such as Christ worked, and will raise the dead. Yet all these he will do in delusion: both his birth, and his flesh, and all the rest, as the Apostle says. And then, he says, the son of perdition will be revealed in all power, signs, and lying wonders. Yet the devil himself will not be transformed into flesh, as John of Damascus says; but a man born of fornication will receive all Satan's working, and will arise suddenly, and then show himself good and gentle unto all. And there will be a great famine then: he will please men, study the divine Scriptures, and practice fasting; and he will be compelled by men and proclaimed king; and he will love the Jewish race exceedingly, arrive in Jerusalem, and rebuild their temple. Enoch and Elijah will come, preaching unto the people not to receive him: but he will openly kill them and cut off their heads. He will give abundant freedom to those found doing mad deeds, who will hide in the mountains; yet those days will be shortened for the sake of the elect, and there will be a great famine, all the elements being transformed, so that almost all will be consumed. After these things, suddenly, like lightning from heaven, the coming of the Lord will take place, His precious Cross going before Him, and a river of fire boiling before Him, cleansing the whole earth from defilement. The Antichrist will immediately be seized, and his servants, and they will be delivered unto eternal fire. And when the Angels sound the trumpet, suddenly from the end of the earth and from every element, all the human race will come unto Jerusalem, because it is the center of the earth, and there the thrones will sit for judgment: nevertheless with their own bodies and souls, all having passed over unto incorruption, and having one appearance, and the elements having received a transformation for the better.[^80] And with a single word the Lord will separate the righteous from the sinners. And the doers of good will go, receiving eternal life; but the sinners again unto eternal torment, and never will their end be. It is to be known that Christ will not require fasting then, nor nakedness, nor wonders, though these also are good: but what is many times better than these: alms and simplicity of heart. For He will speak unto the righteous and the sinners six commandments: "For I was hungry, and ye gave Me food; I was thirsty, and ye gave Me drink; I was a stranger, and ye took Me in; naked, and ye clothed Me; I was sick, and ye visited Me; I was in prison, and ye came unto Me; for forasmuch as ye did it unto one of the least of these, ye did it unto Me": which everyone can do according to his strength.[^81] Then everyone will confess that the Lord Jesus Christ is to the glory of God the Father.[^82] The torments which the holy Gospel has handed down are these: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth, their worm shall not die, and their fire shall not be quenched, and cast him into outer darkness.[^83]

## CHEESEFARE TUESDAY

O all-good Word, Who with the Father and the Spirit didst create things visible together with things invisible, by Thine ineffable mercy, O Tender-hearted One, be pleased that Thy servants pass through the season of the radiant Fast in peace, dissolving the deception of bitter sin, granting compunction, and tears of healing, and forgiveness of transgressions, so that with an undoubting soul, fasting with a fervent spirit, we may sing praise with the Angels unto Thy sovereign power. Glory: the same. Now and ever, Theotokion:

Having ineffably conceived Wisdom and the Word in thy womb, O Mother of God, thou didst give birth unto Him Who holds the world, and in thine arms didst hold Him Who embraces all, the Nourisher of all and Creator and Lord; therefore I pray thee, O all-hymned Virgin, and in faith I glorify thee, that I may be delivered from my transgressions when I am to stand before the face of my Creator, O Sovereign Lady, pure Virgin; do thou grant me thy help then: for thou canst do whatsoever thou wilt, O All-immaculate One.

For Thou, O God of all, art abundant in mercy, O Christ, ever receiving unto repentance; therefore we all ever glorify Thee.

It is the season of remorse: hasten, O my soul, arise, to show forth fruits of repentance worthy of God, and cry aloud with diligence in fastings and prayers: "Sing, bless, and exalt Christ unto all ages!"

Let us pre-purify our spiritual senses, brethren, and let us now cleave unto the Lord with images of compunction, crying out purely with pure repentance: "Sing, bless, and exalt Christ unto all ages!"

Let us hasten by diligent prayer to put to death the snaring enemy who through gluttonous memories and intemperance inflames the sense with brief pleasure, singing, blessing, and exalting Christ unto all ages.

*Theotokion:*

Let us pre-purify ourselves now not with foods alone, but also with deeds, and let us faithful begin with fervent contemplation: that we may appear radiant unto the Creator unto all ages.

*Be it known that in Palestine, which is in the land of Judea, we have not received from the holy fathers to celebrate the full Divine Liturgies on Wednesday and Friday of Cheesefare Week, nor the Presanctified; for on those two days we chant Alleluia with prostrations, evening and morning, as was stated above.*[^123]

*After Vespers on these two days we eat cheese and eggs, observing also the rule of our father among the saints Nicephorus, Patriarch of Constantinople the Confessor, who says: "It is fitting for monastics to fast on Wednesday and Friday of Cheesefare." This rule overturns the Jacobite tradition and the Tetradite heresy.*[^124]

*On Cheesefare Wednesday evening, we chant Small Compline. And we chant the canon which is in the Menaion in order, of the Saint who is to fall on Saturday.*[^125]

*Triodion. Heirmos: "For the laws of the fathers:"*

Having not kept our paternal inheritance, we have been enslaved unto sinful laws; but stretching forth Thy palms upon the divine Cross, Thou didst grant freedom unto them all: have mercy on us, O Merciful One, who come unto Thee with faith in these holy days, supremely exalting Thee unto all ages.

By Thy Cross tearing down the partition and the middle wall of the fence, and renewing rich peace unto those living peacefully at the ends of the earth: grant us, O Christ, with steadfastness to pass through the coming fast, as we hymn the Lord unceasingly and supremely exalt Him unto all ages.[^138]

The grace of fasting today offers solar rays unto all, pre-cleansing darkness from sin; held fast by diverse passions, having come to joy, let us receive the gift with philosophical wisdom, crying aloud: Hymn the Lord, and supremely exalt Him unto all ages.

When of old in paradise the first-formed man bitterly and irrationally ate, he was rejected and condemned, and cast out from delight; but nailed with nails upon the Tree, Thou didst nail the evil handwriting of his sin; therefore we hymn Thine abundant tender mercy unto all ages.[^139]

Having cast away the law of fasting, we slipped into the abyss of sin, and have need of the fast now being introduced; as we pass through it, O Compassionate One, send down grace and rich peace from heaven: Hymn the works of the Lord, and supremely exalt Him unto all ages.

## CHEESEFARE SATURDAY

*We perform the commemoration of all holy, venerable, and God-bearing Fathers who shone forth in ascetic struggle.*[^146]

*On Friday evening at Lucernarium, after the introductory psalm, the customary psalmody: "Unto the Lord when I was afflicted:"*

*At "Lord, I have cried," we chant prosomoia stichera, setting 6 verses.*
*Tone 8, repeating twice:*
Come, all ye faithful, let us hymn the choirs of the venerable Fathers: Anthony the foremost, radiant Euthymius, and each and all together; and spiritually passing through their way of life as through another paradise of delight, beautifully let us cry aloud: "These are the trees which our God planted, these having blossomed forth the fruits of incorruptible life have offered them unto Christ, nourishing our souls;" unto whom let us cry: "Ye God-bearing blessed ones, pray that we may be saved!"[^147]

*Model:*
Rejoice, faithful Egypt; rejoice, venerable Libya; rejoice, chosen Thebaid; rejoice, every place and city and country that reared citizens of the kingdom of heaven, and nurtured them in abstinence and labors, and showed them forth unto God as men perfect in desires; these have appeared as luminaries of our souls; they themselves have shone forth spiritually unto all the ends of the earth by the radiance of miracles and the signs of deeds; unto them let us cry aloud: "All-blessed Fathers, pray that we may be saved!"[^148]

*Model:*
Who among the earthborn shall recount your marvelous lives, O ecumenical Fathers? And what tongue shall utter your sacred ascetic struggles in deed and your sweat, the sufferings of virtues, the mortifications of the flesh, the wrestlings with passions, in vigils, in prayers, and in tears? Ye truly appeared in the world as Angels; ye yourselves utterly cast down demonic powers, having performed marvelous and astounding signs; therefore pray with us, O Most Blessed ones, that we may obtain unending joy.

*Glory, Tone 6:*
Having preserved that which is according to the image unharmed, ascetically setting the mind as ruler over ruinous passions, ye ascended as far as possible unto that which is according to the likeness; for manfully compelling nature, ye were diligent to subject the worse unto the better, and to enslave the flesh unto the spirit; therefore ye appeared as the summit of monastics, desert dwellers, trainers of those running well, most exact rules of virtue, and now in the heavens, the mirrors having been dissolved, O all-venerable ones, ye purely behold the Holy Trinity, praying directly for those who honor you with faith and love.[^149]

Thus says the Lord Almighty: Yet shall there come many people, and inhabitants of many cities: and the inhabitants of five cities shall come together into one city, saying: Let us go to pray before the face of the Lord, and to seek the face of the Lord Almighty; I will go also. To the righteous souls whose memory ever abides,
I offer words as sacrifices of praise.

On this day we celebrate the memory of all the saints who have shone forth in ascetic fasting, both men and women: for since the God-bearing Fathers, having instructed us through the introductory feasts, prepared us beforehand for the stadium, having removed us from luxury and satiety, and having admonished us with fear of the future judgment, and having duly pre-purified us through Cheesefare Week, gracefully set two periods of fasting in the midst, that little by little they might lead us unto it. Lo, also all the venerable ones who lived thus, and in other manners, through many afflictions and labors, both men and women together, they set before us in the midst: that through the memory of their struggles they might make us stronger for the stadium, having their life as a certain pattern and way. And that receiving from them assistance and help, we may be drawn unto spiritual struggles, considering that they also shared the same nature with us. For even as commanders encourage their armed hosts already standing in battle array, by words, examples, and commemorations of ancient men who fought eminently and acted valiantly, and thereby being fortified, the soldiers arm themselves with whole soul in the hope of victory: so also now the God-bearing Fathers wisely act: by those who lived venerably in the male and female sex, fortifying us for spiritual struggles, they thus go before unto the stadium of fasting, that looking upon their archetypal, unenvious life, we may work diverse and manifold virtues, as each has strength. Having love as our forerunner, and that which occurs with understanding, the turning away from dishonorable deeds and actions. And this fast is not of foods only, but also of the tongue, and of anger, of the eyes, and simply to speak, cessation and estrangement from every evil. For this reason the holy Fathers instituted here the present commemoration of all the saints, instructing us by the presentation of those who pleased God by fasting and other good deeds, by the example of these unto the stadium of the virtues, and to arm ourselves valiantly against the passions and against demons: and setting forth a certain pattern, that if we also offer equal diligence unto them, there shall be no obstacle to accomplish all that they also accomplished, and to be accounted worthy of the same honors: for these also shared the same nature with us. And concerning the eating of cheese, some say that Emperor Heraclius instituted this, having previously been observed in few places: for having waged war against Chosroes and the Persians for six years, the Emperor promised unto God that if he should prevail over them, he would propose this, and leave it in the midst between fasting and food, which he also did. But I deem rather that even if this occurred, it was also devised by the holy Fathers for the sake of a certain pre-purification, that being led from meats and much eating straightway into extreme non-eating, we might not be terrified. And in another manner, we are harmed by bodily custom, but having gently and little by little instructed us, and holding us back from delicacies, like unbridled horses running unto food, we may receive the bridle of the Fast. For as they did with parables concerning the soul, so also they prudently ordered concerning the body, cutting off little by little the stumbling blocks unto the Fast. By the prayers of all Thy venerable ones, O Christ our God, have mercy on us. Amen.

## ON CHEESEFARE SUNDAY: THE EXPULSION OF ADAM

*The Expulsion of Adam.*
*Usual Vigil.*

*On Saturday evening at the Lamplighting psalms, after the introductory psalm we chant the stichologia: "Blessed is the man:" the entire kathisma.*
*At "Lord, I have cried," we set 10 stichera, and we chant 3 Resurrectional stichera of the Octoechos, and 3 Anatolian stichera, and 4 of the Triodion, Tone 6. Model: "Having laid aside all:"*

My Creator and Lord, having taken dust from the earth, having quickened me with a life-bearing breath, made me alive, and honored me upon earth as ruler over all visible things, and companion of the angels; but crafty Satan, having used the serpent as a vessel, deceived me with food, and separated me from the glory of God, and handed me over unto death in the nethermost parts of the earth: but as Master and tender-hearted, call me back again.[^196]

I, wretched one, stripped off the God-woven garments, having disobeyed Thy divine command, O Lord, by the counsel of the enemy, and have now clothed myself in fig leaves and coats of skins; for by sweat was I condemned to eat bread of labor, and the earth was cursed to bring forth thorns and thistles unto me; but Thou Who in latter years wast incarnate of the Virgin, having called me back, lead me again into Paradise.[^197]

O all-honorable Paradise, most fair beauty, God-fashioned dwelling, unending gladness and delight, glory of the righteous, beauty of the prophets, and dwelling of the saints: by the sound of thy leaves pray the Creator of all to open unto me the gates which I closed by transgression, and make me worthy to receive the tree of life, and the joy of which I formerly tasted in thee.[^198]

Adam was expelled from Paradise through disobedience, and was cast out of sweetness, deceived by woman's words, and he sits naked, wailing straight opposite the garden, alas for me! Therefore let us all make haste to receive the season of the Fast, obeying the evangelical traditions: that having thereby become well-pleasing unto Christ, we may regain the dwelling of Paradise.

*Glory, Tone 6:*

in perplexity! But, O Paradise, no longer shall I enjoy thy sweetness; no longer shall I behold the Lord and my God and Creator; for I shall depart into the earth, from which I also was taken. O Merciful and Compassionate One, I cry aloud unto Thee: Have mercy on me who am fallen!"

*Ode 6. Heirmos: The sea of life:*
Thou didst clothe me with a God-woven garment in Eden, O Savior, as One compassionate; but I transgressed Thy commandment, believing the deceitful one, and was seen naked, wretched that I am.

O my all-passionate soul, thou hast departed from God; by thy carelessness thou hast been deprived of the enjoyment of Paradise; thou hast been separated from the angels; thou hast brought thyself into corruption. Oh, what a fall!

*Glory:*
Have mercy, take pity, O God the Almighty, upon the work of Thy hands; despise me not, I pray Thee, O Good One, who have separated myself from the choir of Thine angels.

*Now and ever, Theotokion:*
O Mary, called of God, all-venerable Lady, who didst bear the Lord, the King of all and Redeemer: call me back, captive as I am, to the glory of Paradise.

*Katavasia:*
*Beholding the sea of life surging with the tempest of temptations, fleeing unto Thy calm haven I cry aloud unto Thee: Lead up my life from corruption, O Greatly Merciful One.*

*Kontakion, Tone 6:*
O Instructor in wisdom, Giver of prudence, Discipliner of the foolish, and Defender of the poor: establish and give understanding to my heart, O Master! Do Thou give me speech, O Word of the Father; for behold, I shall not restrain my lips from crying unto Thee: O Merciful One, have mercy on me who am fallen![^209]

*Ikos:*
Adam sat then and wept, straight opposite the delight of Paradise, striking his face with both hands, and saying: O Merciful One, have mercy on me who am fallen!

Adam, beholding the angel who had cast him out and shut the door of the divine garden, groaned greatly and said: O Merciful One, have mercy on me who am fallen!

Grieve together, O Paradise, with thy master who has become impoverished, and with the sound of thy leaves implore the Creator that He shut thee not: O Merciful One, have mercy on me who am fallen!

O Paradise all-virtuous, all-holy, all-rich, planted for Adam's sake and shut on account of Eve, implore God for the fallen one: O Merciful One, have mercy on me who am fallen!

*Synaxarion:*[^210]
*Verses:*
*Let the world weep bitterly with its first parents,*
*Having fallen with the fallen through sweet food.*

On this day we make remembrance of the expulsion of the first-fashioned Adam from the delight of Paradise, which our divine and holy Fathers ordained before the holy Forty Days, showing thereby how profitable the medicine of fasting is for human nature, and how shameful again is that which comes from gluttony and disobedience. The Fathers, therefore, leaving aside the recounting of the numberless things that occurred in the world sequentially, present first-fashioned Adam, showing beforehand what great evil he suffered from not fasting even for a little while, and what he thereby brought upon our nature. And as the first commandment of God to mankind was the good commandment of fasting, which he did not keep, but having yielded to his belly, or rather to the deceiver serpent through Eve, not only did he not become God, but he also brought in death and handed over ruin to the whole human race. Because of the food of the first Adam, therefore, the Lord fasted forty days and was obedient; therefore also the present holy Forty Days was devised by the holy Apostles, that what he, not having kept, suffered, having lost incorruption, we, having kept, may regain through fasting. In another way, as we said before, the intention of the saints is this: that they may briefly encompass the deeds done by God from the beginning even unto the end. And since the transgression and expulsion of Adam from food is the primal cause of all things that concern us, according to this reasoning they present this to us, so that making remembrance of that, we may flee it, and above all not emulate intemperance in all things. For on the sixth day Adam was fashioned by the hand of God, being honored with the divine image by inbreathing; and having received the commandment, he lived in Paradise immediately until the sixth hour; then having transgressed it, he was expelled thence. Philo the Jew said that Adam spent one hundred years in Paradise; but others say seven days or years, on account of the honor of the number seven. And as at the sixth hour he stretched forth his hands and touched the fruit, Christ the New Adam also revealed this, stretching forth His hands on the Cross at the sixth hour and the sixth day, healing ruin thence. For he was created between corruption and incorruption, so that whatever he should choose by free will, that he might acquire. For it was indeed possible for God to make Adam sinless, but that there might also be the amendment of his own free will, for this reason He gave a law to touch all the trees, but this one not at all; that is, to contemplate through all creations the knowledge of divine power that comes to be, but concerning the divine nature, in no wise to inquire. St. Gregory the Theologian philosophizes that the trees were divine thoughts, and the garden was divine contemplation.[^211] That is, God commanded Adam (the Theologian says) to care for all the other elements and qualities, and to contemplate them with the mind, and to glorify God thence; for this is true food; but concerning his own nature, and concerning God, who He is in nature, and where, and how He brought all things from non-being, in no wise to seek. But he, having left the other things, rather investigated the things concerning God, and closely searched out His nature; since he was yet imperfect and very simple, an infant, he fell into such things, Satan through Eve having cast into him the fantasy of deification. But the great and divine Chrysostom says that the tree had a twofold power; and he says that Paradise was upon earth, and philosophizes that it was both intelligible and sensible, as Adam was; and both were between corruption and incorruption, together observing the Scripture, and yet not remaining strictly at the letter. Some say that the tree of disobedience was a fig tree; for having immediately perceived their nakedness, they used its leaves to cover themselves. For this reason Christ cursed it, as having been the cause of disobedience; for it has also a certain likeness to sin: first, that which sweetens, then that which is rough from the leaves and clings with milky sap. There are also those who have wrongly imagined that tree to be the intercourse and knowledge of Adam with Eve. Having transgressed, therefore, he was clothed in mortal flesh, and receiving the curse, was expelled from Paradise; and it was commanded to the flaming sword to keep its gates. And he, sitting straight opposite, wept over how many good things he was deprived of, for not fasting for a season; and from him all the race was reckoned equal to him, until He Who fashioned us, having pitied our nature, perishing through Satan, was born of the Holy Virgin, and having lived excellently, showed us the path by the opposites of those things, that is, by fasting and humility, and having wisely overcome him who deceived us, brought us back again to our ancient inheritance. Desiring therefore to present all these things, the God-bearing Fathers present throughout the Triodion first the Old Testament events, of which the first is the creation and the expulsion of Adam from food, of which we now make remembrance; then also the rest through the words of Moses and the prophets, and briefly of David, adding certain things from grace. Then in order also the events of the New Testament, of which the first is the Annunciation, by the unutterable providence of God, ever found in the holy Forty Days; then through Lazarus and the Bearing of Palms, and the Holy Great Week through the reading of the sacred Gospel, and the very holy and saving Passions of Christ, chanted according to tone.[^212] Then also through the Resurrection and the rest, even unto the Descent of the Holy Spirit, through the reading of the sacred Acts, how the proclamation was made and gathered all the saints; for the Acts confirm the Resurrection because of the miracles. Because, therefore, on account of Adam's not fasting even once, we suffered so great things, the remembrance of this is presented now at the entrance of the holy Forty Days, that remembering how great an evil not fasting introduced, we may hasten joyfully to embrace and keep the Fast; so that, having obtained through weeping, fasting, and humbling ourselves until God visit us what Adam failed to attain (namely deification), for without these it is not easy to receive what we lost. Be it known, moreover, that this holy and Great Forty Days is the tithe of the whole year.[^213] For since through laziness we do not wish to fast continually and to cease from evil deeds, the Apostles and divine Fathers handed this down as a kind of harvest for souls, that whatever unseemly things we committed during the whole year, we may cleanse with contrition and fasting, being humbled; which Fast we are also bound to keep most carefully. But also the other three fasts: of the holy Apostles, I mean, of the Mother of God, and of the Nativity of Christ, the divine Apostles established for the four seasons of the year, honoring this Forty Days above all on account of the holy Passions, and because Christ fasted this and was glorified, and Moses, having fasted forty days, received the law; and Elijah likewise, and Daniel, and as many as were proven before God. And that fasting is a good thing is shown by the contrary through Adam. For this reason the expulsion of Adam was devised here by the holy Fathers. By Thine unutterable tender-heartedness, O Christ our God, make us worthy of the food of Paradise, and have mercy on us, as the only Lover of mankind. Amen.

*Ode 7. Heirmos: A dewy furnace:*[^214]
Thou Who rulest over all the ages, O Lord, Who didst fashion me by Thy will: despise me not, O God, who was once envied by the deceitful serpent and provoked Thee to anger, O Savior, but call me back.

I have clothed myself in shameful garments, woe is me, instead of radiant apparel; I weep over my ruin, O Savior, and with faith cry aloud unto Thee, O Good One: Despise me not, O God, but call me back.

*Glory:*
The all-wicked serpent craftily wounded all my soul, and made me an exile from the sweetness of Paradise; but O compassionate Savior, despise me not as God, but call me back.

*Now and ever, Theotokion:*
Receive my suppliant prayer, O All-Immaculate One, by thy mercy, and grant me forgiveness of transgressions, O Pure One, who with weeping diligently cry aloud unto thee: Despise me not, O Good Lady, but call me back.

*Katavasia:*
The angel made the furnace produce dew for the venerable youths, but the burning command of God persuaded the Chaldeans to cry aloud to the tormentor: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers.

*Ode 8. Heirmos: From the flame unto the venerable ones:*
Of old, with manifold gifts Thou didst honor the work of Thy hands, O only Lover of mankind; but the fierce serpent, woe is me, deceived me with his hissing, stripping me of present good things.

Why didst thou listen to bitter counsel, and wast disobedient to the divine command? Woe is me, humble soul that hast grieved God, though ordained to glorify Him ever with the angels!

*We bless the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit, the Lord.*
Having been mistress of reptiles and beasts, how didst thou converse with the soul-destroying reptile, accepting the deceiver as an upright counselor? Oh, thy deception, O my all-passionate soul!

*Now and ever, Theotokion:*
We hymn thee as the light-bearing dwelling of God's incarnation, O Mary full of divine grace; therefore, enlighten me who am grievously darkened with passions by the light of mercy, O hope of the hopeless.

*We praise, we bless, and we worship the Lord.*
*Katavasia:*[^215]
*From the flame Thou didst shed dew upon the venerable ones, and the sacrifice of the righteous one Thou didst consume with water; for Thou, O Christ, doest all things by the mere act of willing: Thee do we exalt supremely unto all ages.*

*Ode 9. Heirmos: God cannot be seen by men:*
Sweet was the fruit of knowledge that appeared in Eden, bringing destruction through food, yet its end became gall. Woe is me, passionate soul, how intemperance made thee an exile from eating in Paradise!

O God of all, Lord of mercy, look with compassion upon my lowliness, and send me not far away from divine Eden, so that beholding the beauty whence I fell, I may strive again with lamentations to regain what I lost.

I lament, groan, and weep, beholding the Cherubim with the flaming sword, commanded to guard the entrance of Eden against all transgressors. Woe is me! Inaccessible is it, unless Thou, O Savior, shalt make it open unto me.[^216]

*Glory:*
I take courage in the multitude of Thy mercy, O Christ Savior, and in the blood of Thy divine side, by which Thou didst sanctify human nature, and didst open, O Good One, the gates of Paradise unto those who serve Thee, which were formerly shut to Adam.

*Now and ever, Theotokion:*
O gate of life impenetrable, intelligible, Virgin Mother of God unwedded: open unto me by thy prayers the gates of Paradise that were formerly shut, that I may glorify thee, next to God, as my helper and mighty refuge.

*Katavasia:*
It is not possible for men to see God, upon Whom the ranks of angels dare not gaze; but through thee, O All-Pure One, the incarnate Word was manifested unto men, Whom magnifying, with the heavenly hosts we call thee blessed.

*Exapostilarion of the Resurrection.*
*Glory, the present one. Model: Give ear, ye women:*
Thy commandment, O Lord, I, wretched one, disobeyed, and stripped of Thy glory, I was filled with shame, woe is me! and was cast out from the delight of Paradise, O Tender-Hearted One. O Merciful One, have mercy upon him who has justly been deprived of Thy goodness.

*Now and ever, another model: Let us go with the disciples:*
Adam who was formerly expelled from Paradise through the food of the tree, Thou hast brought back again, O Savior and my God, through Thy Cross and Passion, by which do Thou strengthen us to complete the Fast in purity, and to venerate the divine Resurrection, the saving Pascha, through the prayers of her who bore Thee.

*At the Praises, we set 4 Resurrectional stichera of the Octoechos, and one Anatolian, and these self-governing stichera of the Triodion. Tone 5:*[^217]
Woe is me! Adam cried out with weeping: "The serpent and the woman have cast me out from divine boldness, and the food of the tree has estranged me from the sweetness of Paradise! Woe is me, no longer can I bear the reproach! Once I was king of all earthly creatures of God, but now I have appeared as a captive through one lawless counsel; and once clothed with the glory of immortality, as a mortal I wretchedly wear the skin of deadness. Woe is me, whom shall I make the companion of my lamentation? But Thou, O Lover of mankind, Thou, O Lover of mankind, Who didst fashion me from the earth, having clothed Thyself in tender-heartedness, deliver me from the slavery of the enemy, and save me!"

*Verse: I will confess Thee, O Lord, with my whole heart.*
The arena of the virtues has opened; let those who wish to contend enter, having girded themselves with the good contest of the Fast; for those who contend lawfully are justly crowned; and having taken up the panoply of the Cross, let us oppose the enemy, having faith as an unshakeable wall, and prayer as a shield, and almsgiving as a helmet; instead of a sword, fasting, which cuts off all malice from the heart. He that does these things shall receive a true crown from Christ the King of all on the day of judgment.[^218]

*Verse: I will be glad and rejoice in Thee.*
*Tone 6:*
Adam is driven out of Paradise, having partaken of food as a disobedient one; Moses became a God-beholder, having purified the eyes of his soul through fasting.[^219] Therefore, desiring to become dwellers of Paradise, let us change from unprofitable food; and wishing to behold God, let us fast the Mosaic four decades of days, enduring purely in prayer and supplication; let us calm the passions of the soul, drive away the pleasures of the flesh, and pass lightly on the journey above, where the angelic choirs with unceasing voices hymn the undivided Trinity, to behold the incomprehensible and Master's beauty. There make us worthy, O Savior, God, Giver of life, who hope in Thee, to dance with the angelic hosts, by the prayers of Thy Mother who bore Thee, O Christ, and of the Apostles, martyrs, and venerable ones.

*Verse: Arise, O Lord my God, let Thy hand be lifted up:*
The season has arrived, the beginning of spiritual contests, victory over demons, all-armored temperance, angelic splendor, boldness before God; for through it Moses became a conversationalist with the Creator, and invisibly received a voice into his ears. Lord, thereby make us also worthy to venerate Thy Passions and holy Resurrection, as Lover of mankind.

*Glory, the same.*
*Now and ever: Most blessed art thou, O Virgin Mother of God:*
*The Great Doxology. And the usual Litiya in the narthex, wherein we sing, Glory, and now and ever: the Gospel sticheron. We also join the First Hour; and the catechetical instructions of our venerable father Theodore the Studite are read, and the Dismissal.*

*At the Liturgy, the Typika. The Beatitudes of the Tone to 6, and of the Triodion Canon, Ode 6 to 4.*
*Prokeimenon, Tone 8: Pray ye, and render unto the Lord our God.*
*Verse: In Judea God is known; His name is great in Israel. Epistle to the Romans, section 112 (Rom. 13:11–14:4). Alleluia, Tone 6: It is good to confess unto the Lord, and to sing unto Thy name, O Most High.*[^220]
*Gospel from Matthew, section 17 (Matt. 6:14–21).*
*And the divine Liturgy of Chrysostom.*
*Communion verse: Praise the Lord from the heavens:*

*Note: It is proper to know that from this Sunday even unto the Exaltation of the Precious Cross, the Polyeleos is omitted; it is sung only on the Master's Vigil feasts and of the great saints.*[^221]

## Cheesefare Sunday at Vespers

*On that same Cheesefare Sunday at Vespers, after the introductory psalm:*[^222]

*At "Lord, I have cried," we set 10 verses, beginning from "Bring my soul out of prison:" And we sing 6 penitential stichera of the Octoechos in the order of the Tone, which are written as two for the evening aposticha and two for the morning aposticha; (likewise also in the other Sundays, from this one even unto the fifth of the Holy Fast, at "Lord, I have cried" we sing in the Tone that is in order in the Octoechos), and of the Triodion 4, and of the Menaion 3.*

*Stichera, Tone 2. Model: When from the tree:*
*Composition of Lord Joseph.*
Let us all make haste to humble the flesh through temperance, traversing the divine arena of the blameless Fast, and with prayers and tears let us seek the Lord Who saves us, and make a complete forgetting of evil, crying aloud: We have sinned against Thee, save us, as of old Thou didst save the Ninevites, O Christ King, and make us partakers of the heavenly Kingdom, O Tender-Hearted One.[^223]

Despairing of myself, I consider my deeds, O Lord, worthy of all torment; for behold, I have despised Thy precious commandments, O Savior, and have spent my life prodigally; therefore I pray Thee, having cleansed me with showers of repentance, enlighten me through fasting and supplication, as the only Merciful One, and abhor me not, O Benefactor of all and Transcendent in goodness.

*Another, of Lord Theodore, same Tone. Model: Surpassing in mind:*
Let us begin the season of the Fast brightly, subjecting ourselves to spiritual contests; let us cleanse the soul, let us cleanse the flesh; let us fast as from foods so from every passion, enjoying the virtues of the Spirit; in which being perfected in love, may we all be made worthy to see the all-venerable Passion of Christ God, and holy Pascha, rejoicing spiritually.

*Then in the Menaion to the saint, 3 prosomoia.*
*If there is an idiomelon: Glory; if not, Glory, and now and ever, Theotokion, in the same Tone of the Menaion.*

*Entrance with the censer: "O gladsome Light:"*
*Prokeimenon, Tone 8:*[^224]
Turn not away Thy face from Thy servant, for I am afflicted; hear me speedily: draw near unto my soul, and redeem it.
*Verse: Let Thy salvation, O God, receive me.*
*Verse: Let the poor see and rejoice.*
*Verse: Seek ye God, and your soul shall live.*
*And again in a higher voice: Turn not away Thy face:*
*(It is proper to know that on all the Sundays of the Holy Forty Days the entrance takes place at the Lamplighting service on account of the Great Prokeimena.)*
*Vouchsafe, O Lord: 3 prostrations: and the litanies.*

*At the Aposticha, self-governing sticheron, Tone 4:*
Thy grace has shone forth, O Lord, the illumination of our souls has shone forth; behold the acceptable time, behold the season of repentance! Let us cast off the works of darkness, and let us put on the armor of light, so that having traversed the great ocean of the Fast, we may attain unto the third-day Resurrection of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, Who saves our souls.[^225]

*Verse: Unto Thee have I lifted up mine eyes: And we repeat the same.*
*Then the verse: Have mercy on us, O Lord, have mercy on us:*
*And the Martyrikon:*
Glorified in the memorials of Thy saints, O Christ God, being implored by them, send down upon us great mercy.

*Glory, idiomelon in the Menaion, if there is one; and now and ever, Theotokion according to the Tone of the Menaion. If there is not, Glory, and now and ever, Theotokion, the same Tone. Model: As one valiant:*
The angelic ranks glorify thee, O Mother of God; for thou, O All-Pure One, didst give birth to God, co-eternal with the Father and the Spirit, Who established the angelic armies from non-being by His will; Whom do thou implore to save from corruption and to enlighten the souls of the Orthodox who sing of thee, O All-Venerable One.

*Now lettest Thou Thy servant depart: Trisagion, three prostrations. O All-Holy Trinity: Our Father: And the present troparia, Tone 4:*[^226]
O Birth-giver of God and Virgin, rejoice: *And we make one prostration.*
*Glory:*
O Baptizer of Christ: *One prostration.*
*Now and ever:*
Intercede for us, holy Apostles: *One prostration. Then,*
Beneath thy tender mercy: *Without prostration.*
*Lord, have mercy, 40 times, in a meek and quiet voice. Glory, and now and ever: More honorable than the Cherubim: In the name of the Lord bless, father. Priest: Blessed is Christ our God, Who is, always, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Reader: Amen. And the prayer: O Heavenly King: And we make three great prostrations, and say the prayer of our venerable father Ephrem the Syrian:*[^227]

**Priest:** O Lord and Master of my life, a spirit of idleness, despondency, love of power, and idle talk give me not.
*Great prostration.*
But a spirit of chastity, humility, patience, and love, bestow upon me Thy servant.
*Great prostration.*
Yea, O Lord King, grant me to see mine own transgressions, and not to judge my brother; for blessed art Thou unto ages of ages. Amen.
*Great prostration.*

*And we kiss the holy and venerable icons. And the superior sings:*
**Choir:** Glory to Thee, O Christ God, our hope, glory to Thee.
*And there is a prayer from the priest, and the complete dismissal.*
*After Vespers there is a collation for the brethren in the refectory.*

*On Cheesefare Sunday at Compline:*[^228]
*After rising from the refectory, we sing Small Compline as usual, with metanias and Lenten prostrations.*
*Note:*
*At "Glory to God in the highest," 3 metanias. (If there is no Vigil, the Canon to the Mother of God is not sung.) "It is truly meet:" Trisagion. 3 metanias, and the usual troparia. Lord, have mercy, 40. "Thou Who at every season:" Priest: "God be compassionate unto us:" 3 great prostrations, with the prayer of Saint Ephrem: "O Lord and Master:" And 12 other [small metanias]. Then, Trisagion. After "Our Father:" Lord, have mercy, 12, and the prayer: "O Spotless, undefiled:" and "And grant unto us:" And the Dismissal.*

*And in the same manner as the order of Vespers and Compline of this Sunday was indicated, so let it be done also on the remaining Sundays, even unto the fifth Sunday of the Holy Forty Days, with certain exceptions, concerning which notice will be given in their proper places.*

*It is proper to know:*[^229]
*That if a brother of ours departs unto the Lord on these holy days in the middle of the week, his third-day remembrance is not observed until Friday evening, for then his pannikhida is celebrated, and likewise his Liturgy on Saturday. On the following Saturday his ninth-day remembrance is observed, whether it coincides or not. But his fortieth-day remembrance is observed when the number of his days is completed; his offerings and commemorations begin from New Sunday [St. Thomas Sunday], until the completion of forty days.*
