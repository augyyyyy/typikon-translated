## The Life and Manner of Living of our Venerable Mother Mary of Egypt

*Written by the Most Holy Sophronius, Patriarch of Jerusalem.*[^1378]

It is good to conceal the secret of a king, but it is glorious to proclaim the works of God! For so said the Angel unto Tobit, after the glorious restoration of his sight following his blindness.[^1379] For not to keep the secrets of a king is perilous and a cause of stumbling, whereas to pass over in silence the all-glorious works of God brings peril unto the soul. Therefore, I also fear to keep silent concerning the works of God, remembering the punishment of that servant who received the Lord's talent and hid it in the earth, and made no gain therewith.[^1380] Having heard a sacred narrative, I can in no wise conceal it; and let none of you disbelieve me upon hearing these writings, nor think that I speak in pride while marveling at this great miracle; far be it from me to speak falsely concerning holy things! And if there be certain persons who read these books and, marveling at the loftiness of the account, refuse to believe, may the mercy of the Lord be upon them; for they, considering only human weakness, count as unacceptable the things spoken by us concerning men. Now, therefore, it is fitting for me to begin this narrative and most marvelous event which took place in our generation.

There was a certain elder in one of the monasteries of Palestine, adorned in manner of life and in word, raised from his very swaddling clothes in monastic customs, and faithful; and the name of that elder was Zosimas. And let no one suppose that this man was that Zosimas the heretic, even though the two shared a single common name;[^1381] for this righteous Zosimas practiced every form of fasting and ascetic labor, and kept all the traditions, and while doing all this, that elder never grew slack in the meditation of the divine words; whether lying down or rising up, holding his work in his hands or partaking of food—if indeed one ought to call food that of which the elder partook—he had one unceasing work, which was to chant psalms continually. For from his very infancy he had been entrusted to the monastery, and he completed up to his fiftieth year therein.

And while that elder was living thus in the monastery, he reasoned within himself, saying: "Is there then a monk upon the earth who can show me a form of ascetic life which I have not accomplished? Can a man be found in the wilderness who surpasses me in deed?" And as the elder pondered thus, an Angel appeared unto him and said: "O Zosimas, nobly indeed hast thou struggled so far as a man is able, yet no man is perfect. But that thou mayest understand how many other ways of salvation there are, go forth out of thy land, like Abraham out of the house of his father,[^1382] and go unto the monastery that lies beside the Jordan."

Immediately the elder went forth from his monastery, and followed the one speaking unto him; and being led by the will of God, he arrived at the monastery beside the Jordan. And having knocked at the gate, word was brought to the abbot; and entering in, Zosimas made a prostration according to the monastic custom. And the abbot questioned him: "Whence art thou, brother, and for what cause hast thou come unto us poor old men?" Zosimas answered: "Inquire not of me whence I have come; but I have come for the sake of spiritual profit, for I have heard of your great and praiseworthy rule, which is able to lead souls unto Christ our God." And the abbot said unto him: "God alone, O brother, Who heals the human race—may He Himself teach both thee and us His will, and guide thee to perform all things that are profitable."

=== LEAF p503 ===
And when the abbot had spoken these things unto Zosimas, the elder bowed down, offered a prayer, saying "Amen," and remained in that monastery. And he beheld there monks shining in actions and deeds, and fervent in spirit; their chanting was unceasing, their standing lasted all through the night, manual labor was ever in their hands, and psalms were in their mouths; there was no idle word among them, and their sole care was to count themselves dead in body; their nourishment was the words of God, and they sustained the body with bread and water. Seeing these things, Zosimas marveled, and strove eagerly toward the course set before him.

And when many days had passed, the season of the Holy and Great Fast drew near; the gates of the monastery were kept locked and were never opened, save only when one of the brethren went out for some necessity, for the place was solitary, and not only untrodden, but unknown to people of the world. Now this was the rule handed down in that monastery, for the sake of which God had brought Zosimas thither: On the First Sunday of the Fast, the presbyter celebrated the Divine Liturgy, and all partook of the Most Pure Body and Blood of Christ our God, and tasted a little food; afterward they gathered in the church, and having offered lengthy prayer and genuflections, the elders kissed one another and the abbot, and having received a blessing, they opened the monastery gates, chanting in unison: "The Lord is my light and my Savior; whom shall I fear? The Lord is the defender of my life; of whom shall I be afraid?"[^1383] And chanting the rest of that psalm, they all went forth, leaving one or two brethren behind—not to guard the monastery, for there was nothing therein to be stolen by thieves, but so that the church might not remain without divine service.

And each carried provisions for himself, according as each was able and wished: one took a little bread, another figs, another dates, another pulse soaked in water, and another nothing at all save his own body and the ragged garments which he wore; and whenever their body was in need, they fed upon the herbs and plants of the wilderness. Thus they crossed the Jordan and separated far from one another, and none knew how his neighbor fasted or how he labored in asceticism. And if anyone happened to catch sight of his brother walking toward him, he immediately turned aside in another direction, and lived alone by himself, chanting continually unto God.

Having thus completed the entire Fast, they returned to the monastery on the Sunday before the Resurrection of Christ, on which the Church celebrates the Feast of Palms;[^1384] and each returned bearing the fruits of his own labor, having his own conscience as witness of what he had accomplished, and no one questioned another as to how he had labored; for such was the rule of that monastery.

Then Zosimas also, according to the custom of the monastery, crossed the Jordan, carrying a little food for the needs of the body and the garments which he wore; and he fulfilled his customary rule of prayer while walking through the wilderness, partaking of food when necessity required, and sleeping a little while lying upon the ground; and rising very early again, he pursued his course. And he longed greatly to penetrate into the innermost wilderness, as he said, hoping that he might find at least one holy father dwelling therein and fasting; and desire was added unto his desire.

After walking for seven days, he halted a little from his journey, praying at the time of the sixth hour, and turning toward the east, he offered the customary prayers; for he used to pause a little from his journey and rest at each canonical hour, chanting and making prostrations. And while he stood chanting, he saw to his right side a shadow, as if of a human body; at first he was afraid, supposing it to be a demonic apparition, and trembling, he signed himself with the sign of the Cross; and having cast off fear, he remained without dread, for he was already concluding his prayer. Turning then his eyes toward the south, he saw someone walking, unclothed in body, and darkened in appearance from the burning of the sun; and the hair upon her head was white as wool, and short, reaching only down to the neck.

Seeing this, Zosimas rejoiced over that remarkable sight, and began to run in the direction where he saw the figure, rejoicing with great joy; for during all those days he had seen no human form, nor bird, nor beast, nor creeping thing. When that figure saw Zosimas approaching from afar, she began to run and flee into the inner wilderness. And Zosimas, as if forgetting his old age and the fatigue of his journey, ran swiftly, wishing to overtake the one fleeing. He pursued, and she fled; yet swifter was the running of Zosimas than of the one fleeing. And when he drew near enough that his voice could be heard, Zosimas began to cry aloud with tears in these words: "Why dost thou flee from me, a sinful old man, O servant of the true God, for Whose sake thou dwellest in this wilderness? Wait for me, who am sinful and unworthy and weak! Halt, and bestow upon me, an old man, thy prayer and blessing, for the sake of God, Who never rejects anyone!"

While Zosimas spoke these things with tears, they were running near to one another and came to a dry torrent bed—though I do not think a torrent ever flowed there. When she reached that place, the fleeing one descended to the other side; and Zosimas, exhausted by old age and unable to run any further, stood on his side of the torrent bed, and added tears unto tears, and lamentation unto lamentation. Then the fleeing figure called out with a voice unto him, saying: "Abba Zosimas, I cannot turn and show myself before thy face, for I am a woman without clothing, as thou seest; but if thou wishest to bestow thy prayer upon me, a sinful woman, cast me the cloak which thou wearest, that I may cover my feminine weakness, and turn toward thee, and receive thy prayer."

Then Zosimas was struck with fear in body and amazed in mind upon hearing himself called by name, and said within himself: "If this woman were not clairvoyant, she would not have called me by my name!" And he quickly did as he had been told, and taking off the old and tattered cloak which he wore, he cast it toward her while turning his face away. Having taken it, she girded herself therewith and covered her body, and turning toward Zosimas, she said unto him: "Why did it seem good to thee, Abba Zosimas, to wish to see a sinful woman? What dost thou wish to hear or learn from me, that thou hast not shrunk from undertaking so great a labor?"

=== LEAF p504 ===
And bending her knees to the ground, she asked a blessing from him; and he likewise bowed down to the ground, and both lay upon the earth asking a blessing from one another, and nothing was heard spoken by either of them save only: "Bless!" And when a long time had passed, the woman said unto Zosimas: "It befits thee to offer the prayer, for thou art honored with the dignity of the presbyterate, and for many years hast stood before the holy altar, and hast oftentimes offered the Holy Gifts unto God."[^1385]

These words cast Zosimas into aún greater awe, and the elder trembled, covered with sweat, and groaned, his voice failing him. And Zosimas said unto her with shortened and exhausted breath: "O spiritual Mother, it is manifest that thou hast drawn near unto God, and hast died in the greatest measure unto the world; and the grace given unto thee is shown clearly in that thou callest me by name, and hast named me a presbyter, whom thou hast never seen before. Therefore do thou rather bless, for the Lord's sake, and grant thy prayer unto one who stands in need of thy perfection!"

Yielding then to the elder's persistence, she said: "Blessed is God, Who wills the salvation of human souls!" And when Zosimas had said "Amen," both rose from the ground. And the woman said unto the elder: "For what cause hast thou come unto me, a sinful woman, O man of God? Why hast thou desired to see a woman devoid of every virtue? Yet the grace of the Holy Spirit hath guided thee to fulfill a certain service needed for my body in due time. Tell me, therefore, O father: how do the Christians live now? How are the emperors? How are the holy Churches regulated?"

Zosimas answered, saying: "By your holy prayers, O Mother, God hath granted lasting peace; but receive the supplication of an unworthy old man, and pray for the Lord's sake on behalf of the whole world, and for me a sinner, that this my journey through the wilderness may not be fruitless." And she answered him: "It befits thee rather, Abba Zosimas, who holdest the priestly rank, to pray for me and for all, for unto this hast thou been appointed; nevertheless, since we are commanded to practice obedience, I will perform what is bidden me by thee."

Having said this, she turned toward the east, and lifting her eyes toward heaven and raising her hands, she began to pray in a low voice, so that her words could not be heard distinctly; therefore Zosimas understood nothing of the words of her prayer, and stood trembling, looking downward and saying nothing. And calling God to witness, the elder later testified: "When I saw her continuing long in prayer, I raised my eyes a little from looking at the ground, and I beheld her lifted up in the air about one cubit above the earth, standing and praying in the air!"

When Zosimas saw this, he fell into still greater fear, and cast himself upon the ground, wetting the earth with his tears, and saying nothing save only: "Lord, have mercy!" And as the elder lay upon the ground, a troubled thought entered his mind, wondering whether she might be a spirit pretending to prayin order to deceive him. Turning around, the woman raised the elder up and said: "Why do thoughts trouble thee, Abba Zosimas, to suspect that I am an apparition? Assuredly, I pray thee, blessed father, know of a truth that I am a sinful woman, albeit sealed with holy Baptism, and no spirit at all, but earth and dust and ashes, wholly flesh, having never known spiritual wisdom." And saying this, she signed her forehead, eyes, lips, and breast with the sign of the Cross, saying thus: "Abba Zosimas, may God deliver us from the evil one and from his snares, for great is his warfare against us."

Hearing and seeing these things, the elder fell at her feet, saying with tears: "I adjure thee by Jesus Christ our God, Who was born of the Virgin, for Whose sake Thou strivest thus in this wilderness, conceal not Thy life from me, but relate all things unto me, that Thou mayest make manifest the magnificent works of God! Tell me all things for God's sake; for Thou shalt speak not for vainglory, but to edify me, a sinner and unworthy man. I believe my God, for Whom Thou livest, that for this very purpose I was guided into this wilderness, that God might make manifest Thy works; for it is not in the power of our weakness to resist the judgments of God. For if Christ our God had not willed that Thou and Thy struggle should be made known, He would neither have revealed thee unto me, nor strengthened me for so great a journey—one who was never willing or able before to go forth out of my cell."

When Zosimas had spoken these things and many more besides, she sighed deeply and said unto him: "I am ashamed, O father, to recount unto thee my shameful deeds; yet since thou hast seen my state, I will lay bare before thee my actions also, that thou mayest know with how much shame and reproach my soul is filled. For not for the sake of praise, as thou hast said, will I relate my life unto thee, since I was formerly a chosen vessel of the adversary.[^1386] Yet know that if I begin to recount my former life, thou wilt flee from me as a man flees from a serpent, unable to endure hearing with thine ears the unworthy things that I wrought. Nevertheless, I will confess without hiding anything; only I adjure thee first, cease not to pray for me, that I may find mercy in the Day of Judgment."

And while the elder waited and wept unceasingly, she began to relate her life, speaking thus: "I, O father, was born in Egypt; and when I was twelve years of age, while my parents were still living, I cast off their affection and went away to Alexandria. And having lost my first modesty, I gave myself over unrestrainedly to a life of vanity and sin. I am ashamed even to call to mind that dishonor, yet I will speak briefly, that thou mayest know the excess of my fallen passions: for more than seventeen years I lived in public dissipation, receiving payment from no one, but living in poverty and spinning coarse flax, because I counted it my very life to wallow continually in earthly desires.

"While living thus, I saw in the season of harvest a great multitude of Libyan and Egyptian men hastening toward the sea; and I asked one of those who met me: 'Whither are these men hastening?' And he said unto me: 'To Jerusalem, for the Exaltation of the Precious and Life-giving Cross, which will take place shortly.' And I said unto him: 'Will they take me also, if I go with them?' And he said:

=== LEAF p505 ===
"'If thou hast the fare for the passage and provisions, no one will hinder thee.' And I said unto him: 'Truly, brother, I have neither fare nor provisions; yet I will go and embark upon the ship with them, and they will provide for me.' For I told thee, Abba Zosimas, compel me not to utter my disgrace; for the Lord God knows that I tremble lest my very words defile both thee and the air!" And Zosimas, wetting the ground with his tears, answered her: "Speak for the Lord's sake, O my Mother, speak, and cease not from a narrative so profitable unto me!"

She therefore resumed her account: "That young man, hearing my bold words, laughed and departed; and I, casting away the distaff which I occasionally carried, ran toward the sea whither they were hastening. And seeing ten or more young men standing by the seashore while others had already boarded the vessel, I approached them with my customary boldness, saying: 'Take me also with you whithersoever ye go, for I shall not be unwelcome among you.' And speaking other idle words, I moved them all to laughter; and they took me aboard their ship, and thence we set sail.

"How shall I relate unto thee what followed, O father? What tongue can utter, or what ear can bear to hear the evil deeds committed by me along the way and upon the ship? Believe me, O father, I marvel how the sea endured my lawlessness! How did the earth not open its mouth and swallow me down alive into hades, who had led astray so many souls? Yet I think that God was seeking my repentance; for He desires not the death of sinners, but awaits their conversion with long-suffering.[^1387]

"Thus I arrived in Jerusalem, and during all the days before the feast I continued in the same evil ways, and even worse. And when the holy feast of the Exaltation of the Precious Cross arrived,[^1388] I went about as before; and seeing everyone going very early to the church, I also went and ran along with those who were running, and entered with them into the courtyard of the church. And when the hour of the holy Exaltation arrived, as the multitude was entering into the church, I tried to enter with them, but was pushed back; and supposing that this happened because of the press of the crowd, I forced my way forward with great effort until I reached the very doors of the church within which the Life-giving Tree was displayed.

"Yet when I stepped upon the threshold of the church doors, all the others entered in unhindered, whereas a certain divine power held me back, not permitting me to enter; and again I attempted to enter, and was thrust far back from the doors, standing alone in the vestibule. Supposing that this happened to me because of feminine weakness, I mingled again with others who were entering, pushing with my elbows and straining with all my might; yet whenever my sinful foot touched the threshold, the church received everyone else and hindered no one, while refusing to admit me, the wretched one; as if a drawn-up detachment of soldiers were barring the entrance, so did that sudden divine power hold me back, and again I found myself in the vestibule.

"Having suffered this three and four times, and being exhausted and no longer able to push among those entering, I withdrew and stood in a corner of the church courtyard; and only then did I come to my senses and understand why I was prevented from beholding the Life-giving Cross. For the light of saving knowledge touched the eyes of my heart, showing me that the mire of my deeds barred the entrance against me.

"Then I began to weep and lament, and to beat my breast, pouring forth sighs from the depths of my heart. And as I stood weeping in that place, I looked up and saw above me an icon of the Most Holy Theotokos standing on the wall; and fixing my gaze upon her, I said: 'O Virgin Lady, who didst bear in the flesh God the Word! I know, yea, I know that it is neither fitting nor right for me, a defiled sinner, to look upon the venerable icon of thee, the Ever-Virgin Mary, who hast both body and soul pure and undefiled. Rightly indeed am I, a prodigal, worthy to be hated and abhorred before thy virginal purity! Yet I have heard that God, Whom thou didst bear, became man for this very purpose, that He might call sinners to repentance.[^1389] Help me who am alone and have help from nowhere! Command that the entrance of the church be opened unto me also, and forbid me not to behold the Tree upon which Christ God was crucified in the flesh, shedding His own Blood for my redemption! Command, O Sovereign Lady, that the doors be opened unto me for the holy veneration of the Cross; and be thou my sure mediatrix and surety before Him Who was born of thee, that henceforth I will never again defile this flesh; but as soon as I have beheld the Tree of the Cross of Thy Son, I will renounce this world and its deeds, and will immediately go forth whithersoever thou, as my surety, shalt guide me!'

"Having spoken these words, and receiving as it were an assurance through the ardor of faith, trusting in the compassion of the Theotokos, I moved from that place where I had stood offering my prayer; and returning, I mingled again with those who were entering, and there was no longer anyone pushing me back or hindering me from entering the church. Fear and amazement seized me, and I trembled all over; and reaching the doors that had previously been closed against me, I entered within without difficulty. And I was counted worthy to behold the Precious and Life-giving Cross, and I saw the mysteries of God, and how ready He is to receive the penitent! Falling then to the ground and kissing that Precious Wood with dread, I hastened forth to return to my Surety. And coming to the place where the icon of the Most Holy Theotokos stood, and bending my knees—

=== LEAF p506 ===
"—before the icon of the Most Holy Theotokos, I began to speak in these words: 'Thou indeed, O blessed Lady Theotokos, Sovereign Mistress, hast shown thy loving-kindness toward me, and hast not abhorred the supplication of me, an unworthy sinner. For I have beheld the glory which in truth I, a prodigal, was unworthy to look upon; glory be to God, Who through thee receives the repentance of sinners! What more have I to ponder or to utter, being a sinful woman? It is time now, O Sovereign Lady, for me to fulfill the vow which I promised under thy surety. Direct me now whithersoever thou willest, and henceforth be unto me a teacher of salvation, leading me by the hand along the path of repentance.'

"And while I was speaking these words unto her, I heard a voice from afar saying: 'If thou crossest the Jordan, thou shalt find good rest!'[^1390] Hearing that voice, and believing with certainty that it had been uttered for my sake, I cried out with tears, gazing upon the icon of the Theotokos: 'O Sovereign Lady, Mother of God, forsake me not!'

"Having cried out thus, I went forth from the courtyard of the church and walked rapidly. And a certain man, seeing me walking, gave me three copper coins, saying: 'Accept these, my mother.' Taking them, I bought three loaves of bread, and asked the baker who sold me the loaves: 'Sir, which is the way to the Jordan?' Learning which city gate led in that direction, I went forth running, and walked along the road weeping. Asking my way as I went, I completed the day's journey—for it was about the third hour of the day when I beheld the Precious Cross—and toward sunset I reached the Church of St. John the Baptist, which stands near the banks of the Jordan.[^1391]

"Having prayed first in the church, I went down immediately to the Jordan and washed my face and hands in that holy water; and afterward I partook of the Most Pure and Life-giving Mysteries of Christ in the Church of the Forerunner. Then I ate half of one loaf, drank of the water of the Jordan, and slept that night upon the ground. Early the next morning, finding a small boat there, I crossed over to the opposite bank of the Jordan; and again I prayed to my Guide, the Theotokos, to direct me whithersoever seemed good to her.

"Thus I came into this wilderness; and from that time until this present day I have fled afar off, dwelling in this desert, waiting upon God Who saves me from faintheartedness and tempest whenever I turn unto Him."[^1392]

Zosimas said unto the venerable one: "How many years have passed, my lady, since thou didst take up thy dwelling in this wilderness?" And she answered: "I think that forty and seven years have elapsed since I went forth from the Holy City." Zosimas said unto her: "And what dost thou find for thy nourishment, my lady?" She said: "I brought two and a half loaves of bread with me when I crossed the Jordan; and little by little they dried up and became hard as stone, and partaking of them sparingly a little at a time, I subsisted on them for several years."

Zosimas asked: "How then hast thou passed so many years without harm, suffering no distress from so sudden a change of life?" She answered: "Thou hast questioned me now, Abba Zosimas, concerning a matter of which I tremble even to speak; for if I call to mind all those perils which I endured, and the fierce thoughts that assailed me, I fear lest I be seized by them once again." Zosimas said unto her: "O my lady, I beseech thee, leave nothing untold that thou canst declare unto me; for since thou hast begun, relate the whole matter unto me until the end."

And she said unto him: "Believe me, Abba Zosimas, for seventeen years I struggled in this wilderness against my own passionate thoughts as against fierce wild beasts! For whenever I began to taste a little food, immediately there came upon me a craving for the meats and fish that were in Egypt;[^1393] and I craved also the wine which I had loved so much—for when I was in the world I drank much wine, whereas here, not having even a drop of water to taste, I burned with thirst and endured grievous distress.

"There came upon me also a perverse desire for worldly songs, troubling my mind and stirring me to chant the vain melodies I had learned in the world; but immediately shedding tears and beating my breast, I called to mind the vows which I had made before departing into this wilderness. In spirit I would stand before the icon of the Most Holy Theotokos, my Surety, weeping before her and imploring her to drive away from me the thoughts that tormented my wretched soul. And when I had wept long enough and beaten my breast fervently, I would behold a calm light shining round about me from every side, and a great stillness would replace the tempest.

"Whenever turbulent thoughts rose up again within my heart, I cast myself down upon the earth and watered it with tears, believing that my Surety herself stood before me, arraigning me as a transgressor and showing me the punishment due to my breach of covenant; and I would not rise from the ground night or day until that sweet light shone upon me again and scattered the thoughts that troubled me. Thus I lifted the eyes of my mind unceasingly toward my Surety, beseeching her to succor me in this desert; and truly I had her as my helper and the furtherance of my repentance.

"In this manner I completed seventeen years, enduring ten thousand perils; and from that time until this day my Helper hath preserved me in all things." Zosimas said unto her: "And hast thou had no need since then of food or clothing?" She answered: "After finishing those loaves of bread over the course of seventeen years, as I told thee, I was nourished by the wild herbs and roots found in this desert. As for the garment which I wore when I crossed the Jordan, it became tattered and fell away completely; and thereafter I suffered great affliction from the cold of winter and the scorching heat of summer, burning under the sun and freezing and shivering in the frost, so that many times I fell to the earth and lay motionless as if without breath.

"Having struggled thus against manifold afflictions and temptations, from that time until this present day the manifold power of God hath preserved my sinful soul and humble body. When I reflect only upon the great evils from which the Lord hath delivered me, I possess inexhaustible nourishment in the hope of my salvation; for I am fed and clothed by the word of God, Who upholds the universe. For man doth not live by bread alone, and because of having no shelter, those who have stripped off the garment of sin have embraced the rock!"[^1394]

Hearing her quote the words of Scripture from Moses, the Prophets, and the Psalms, Zosimas asked her: "Hast thou then learned the Psalms and the other sacred books, my lady?" Smiling gently at this question, she said unto him: "Believe me, O father, I have seen no other human being since I crossed the Jordan until I beheld thy—

"—face today; Indeed, I have not even seen a wild beast or any other living creature since that day. And I never learned to read from books, nor have I ever heard anyone chanting or reading since then; but the living Word of God Himself teaches man knowledge! Here ends the narrative of my life, O father; and now, as I besought thee at the beginning, I adjure thee by the Incarnation of the Word of God to pray unto the Lord for me, a prodigal sinner."

Having spoken thus and concluded her account, she bowed down to make a prostration before the elder; and the elder cried aloud with tears: "Blessed is God, Who works great and marvelous things, glorious and unsearchable, whereof there is no number![^1395] Blessed is God, Who hath shown me how great are the gifts He bestows upon those who fear Him! Truly, O Lord, Thou dost never forsake them that seek Thee!"

And when the elder wished to fall at her feet, she took hold of him and permitted him not to prostrate himself, saying unto him: "Concerning all these things which thou hast heard, O father, I adjure thee by Jesus Christ our God and Savior to speak of them to no man until God shall take me from the earth. Now depart in peace, and in the coming year thou shalt see me again, preserved by the grace of God. Only do for the Lord's sake that which I now enjoin upon thee: During the Great Fast of the coming year, cross not over the Jordan as ye are accustomed to do in the monastery."

Zosimas marveled upon hearing that she knew even the rule of the monastery, and could say nothing else save only: "Glory be to God, Who bestows great gifts upon them that love Him!" And she continued: "Remain, therefore, in the monastery, Abba, as I tell thee; for even if thou shouldst attempt to go forth before the time, it will not be possible for thee. And on the evening of Holy and Great Thursday, which commemorates the Mystical Supper of Christ,[^1396] place in a sacred vessel worthy of such a mystery a portion of the Life-giving Body and Blood of Christ our God, and bring it for me; and wait for me on the bank of the Jordan that lies near the inhabited world, so that I may come and partake of the Life-giving Gifts. For since the time when I communicated in the Church of the Forerunner before crossing the Jordan, until this day I have not received that sanctification; therefore I thirst for it with unrestrainable longing, and I implore thee not to disdain my request, but certainly to bring me the Divine and Life-giving Mysteries at the very hour when the Lord made His disciples partakers of the Divine Supper. And as for John, the abbot of the monastery in which thou dwellest, say unto him: 'Look to thyself and to thy flock,[^1397] for there are certain things there that require correction.' Yet I wish thee not to say this unto him now, but when the Lord shall permit thee."

Having said these things, and asking the elder once more: "Pray for me!" she vanished into the depths of the inner wilderness. And Zosimas, falling upon his knees and kissing the ground whereon her feet had stood, gave glory and thanks unto God; and he returned through the wilderness rejoicing and praising Christ our God, arriving back at the monastery on the appointed day when the other brethren also returned.

Throughout that entire year the elder kept silence, daring to tell no one anything of what he had seen, while praying earnestly within himself that God would show him again that desired countenance; and he grieved and counted the length of the year heavy to bear, wishing that the whole year might pass like a single day if that were possible.

When the First Sunday of the Holy and Great Fast arrived, the other brethren went forth chanting according to the custom and order of the monastery; Zosimas, however, was seized by a feverish illness and was compelled to remain behind in the monastery. Then Zosimas remembered the words spoken by the venerable one: "Even if thou shouldst wish to go forth from the monastery, it will not be possible for thee." After Not many days he was healed of his infirmity and remained within the monastery.

When the brethren returned for Palm Sunday and the evening of the Mystical Supper arrived, Zosimas did as he had been commanded: placing in a small chalice a portion of the Most Pure Body and Precious Blood of Christ our God, and putting into a small basket a few dried figs and dates and a handful of lentils soaked in water, he went forth late in the evening and sat down upon the bank of the Jordan, awaiting the arrival of the holy one.

Though the saint delayed, Zosimas did not allow himself to slumber, but gazed intently toward the desert, longing to behold her whom he earnestly desired. And sitting on the bank, the elder conversed within himself, saying: "What if my unworthiness hath hindered her from coming? What if she came already, and not finding me, turned back?" Speaking thus, he sighed and wept; and lifting his eyes to heaven, he prayed to God, saying: "Deprive me not, O Sovereign Master, of beholding again that face which Thou didst grant me to see, lest I depart empty, bearing the burden of my sins for my own reproof!"

Having prayed thus with tears, he fell into another thought, saying within himself: "And what will happen even if she comes? For there is no boat here; how then shall she cross the Jordan to come unto me, an unworthy man? Woe to my unworthiness! Woe is me, who hath deprived me of such a blessing?"

While the elder was pondering these things, behold, the holy woman arrived and stood on the opposite bank of the river, coming out of the desert. Zosimas rose up rejoicing with joy and glorifying God, though still wrestling in his mind with the thought that she would not be able to cross the Jordan. Looking across, he saw her sign the waters of the Jordan with the sign of the Precious Cross—for the night, as he said, was bright with the full moon—and simultaneously with making the sign of the Cross, she stepped down upon the water and walked across the surface of the river toward him!

When the elder attempted to make a prostration before her, she forbade him while still walking upon the water, crying aloud: "What dost thou, Abba, seeing that thou art a priest and art carrying the Divine Mysteries?" Stepping off the water onto the bank, she said to the elder: "Bless, father, bless!" He answered her with trembling—for amazement had seized him at that transcendent miracle—saying: "Truly God is not false in promising that those who purify themselves shall become like unto God so far as is possible![^1398] Glory be to Thee, O Christ our God, Who hast shown me through this Thy handmaid how far distant I stand from the measure of perfection!"

When he had spoken thus, she asked him to recite the sacred Symbol of Faith ("I believe in one God") and the Lord's Prayer ("Our Father Who art in the heavens"). When the prayer was concluded, she partook of the Undefiled Mysteries of Christ, and according to the custom gave the kiss of peace to the elder; then raising her hands toward heaven, she sighed and wept, crying aloud: "Lord, now lettest Thou Thy servant depart in peace, according to Thy word; for mine eyes have seen Thy salvation, which Thou hast prepared before the face of all peoples!"[^1399]

And she said again to the elder: "Pardon me, Abba Zosimas, and fulfill one more desire of mine: return now to thy monastery, guarded by the peace of God; and in the coming year come again to that same dry torrent bed where I first conversed with thee. Come, I pray thee for the Lord's sake, and Thou shalt see me again according as the Lord wills." He answered her: "Would that it were possible for me to follow behind thee from this hour and gaze continually upon thy venerable countenance! Yet grant this one entreaty of an old man, and taste a little of the food which I have brought for thee." And saying this, he showed her the basket which he carried.

=== LEAF p508 ===
Touching the lentils with the tips of her fingers, she took only three grains and raised them to her lips, saying that the grace of the Spirit sufficed to preserve the nature of the soul undefiled. And she said again to the elder: "Pray to the Lord for me, O father, pray for me, and remember always my wretchedness!"

He bowed down at her feet, asking her to pray to God for the Church, the emperors, and himself; and having asked this with tears, he let her depart, groaning and lamenting, for he did not dare to detain any longer one who could not be held back even if he wished. Signing the Jordan once more with the sign of the Cross, she walked back across the waters as she had done before. And the elder returned to his monastery filled with great joy and holy fear, reproaching himself only because he had neglected to learn the name of the saint, though hoping to learn it in the following year.

When another year had passed, Zosimas went forth again into the wilderness according to custom, hastening toward that marvelous sight. Walking through the desert and noticing certain signs of the place he sought, he looked to the right and to the left like a keen hunter seeking to capture a most sweet quarry. When he saw no movement anywhere, he began to weep; and lifting his eyes to heaven, he prayed, saying: "Show me, O Lord, the unplundered treasure which Thou hast hidden in this wilderness! Show me, I beseech Thee, the angel in the flesh, of whom the whole world is not worthy!"[^1400]

Praying thus, he reached the place marked by the dry torrent bed; and standing on the bank, he looked toward the east and saw the holy one lying dead, her hands folded decently across her breast as was fitting, and her face turned toward the east. Running to her, he washed the feet of the blessed one with his tears, not daring to touch any other part of her body.

Having wept for a long time and chanted psalms suited to the occasion, he offered the funeral prayers and said within himself: "Ought I to bury the relic of the holy one, or might this perhaps be displeasing to her?" As he said this, he noticed beside her head an inscription traced upon the ground in these words: "Bury, Abba Zosimas, in this place the body of humble Mary; render dust unto dust, and pray to the Lord for me, who departed in the month of Pharmouthi according to the Egyptians, which is April according to the Romans, on the first day of the month, on the very night of the saving Passion of Christ, after partaking of the Divine and Mystical Supper."[^1401]

Reading this inscription, the elder wondered at first who could have written it, since she had told him that she had never learned letters; yet he rejoiced exceedingly to learn the name of the saint, and understood clearly that immediately after partaking of the Divine Mysteries at the Jordan, she had been transported in a single hour to this place—a journey that had taken him twenty days of wearisome travel—and had departed immediately unto God.

Glorifying God, and wetting the earth and the relic of the saint with his tears, the elder said within himself: "It is time now, poor Zosimas, to fulfill what is commanded; yet how shalt thou dig, wretched man, having no tool in thy hands?" Saying this, he saw a small piece of wood lying nearby on the ground; picking it up, he began to dig with it. The ground, however, was parched and hard, and yielded not at all to the elder's efforts as he labored and sweated without accomplishing anything.

Sighing deeply from the bottom of his heart, he looked up and saw a great lion standing beside the relic of the blessed Mary and licking the soles of her feet. At the sight of the wild beast the elder trembled with fear, especially remembering how the holy one had told him that she had never seen a wild beast; but signing himself with the sign of the Cross, he trusted that he would be kept unharmed by the power of the one lying before him. And the lion began to fawn gently upon the elder, greeting him with mild movements as if saluting him.

Zosimas then said to the lion: "O great beast, since this holy one hath commanded me to bury her body, and I am an old man unable to dig—having no mattock with me, and being too far away to return and fetch one—do thou therefore perform the work with thy claws, that we may commit to the earth the all-holy tabernacle of the saint!" Immediately upon hearing these words, the lion dug out with its front paws a trench deep enough to cover the body of her who was being buried.

Having washed the feet of the saint once more with his tears, and entreated her earnestly to pray on behalf of all, the elder covered her body with earth in the presence of the lion, her body being clad solely in the tattered cloak which Zosimas had cast to her at their first meeting. Then both departed: the lion withdrew into the inner wilderness as gently as a lamb, while Zosimas returned to his monastery blessing and praising Christ our God.

Upon reaching the monastery, the elder related to all the monks everything that he had seen and heard from her, concealing nothing from them; and all marveled upon hearing of the magnificent works of God, celebrating with fear and faith and love the commemoration of the saint. And Abbot John found certain matters in the monastery requiring correction, just as the holy one had foretold, and set them right with the help of God. Zosimas himself reposed in that same monastery after reaching nearly a hundred years of age.

The monks of that monastery handed down these words unwritten from one generation to another, setting them forth as a pattern of edification for those who wished to listen; and I, having received this unwritten narrative from them, have committed it to writing. Whether others also have written the life of the saint with fuller knowledge of her deeds has not come to my awareness; yet to the best of my ability I have recorded this account, placing nothing above the truth.

And may God, Who works great miracles and bestows great gifts upon those who take refuge in Him in faith, grant a reward unto those who read and listen to this account, and to the one who arranged for this narrative to be committed to writing; and may He count them worthy of the blessed portion of this saint, Mary, concerning whom this narrative is told, together with all who have pleased Him from the beginning by contemplative vision and virtuous deeds! Let us also give glory unto God the eternal King, that He may grant us to find mercy in the Day of Judgment in Christ Jesus our Lord, to Whom belongs all glory, honor, dominion, and worship, together with the unoriginate Father and the All-Holy, Good, and Life-giving Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

## A Beneficial Tale Concerning the Akathist Hymn

*Compiled from an ancient writing, setting forth a glorious remembrance of the miracle which took place when the Persians and Barbarians besieged the Imperial City with warfare, and perished by the judgment of God after being put to the test, whereas the City was preserved unharmed through the prayers of our Most Pure Lady the Theotokos; from which time this service of thanksgiving has been chanted, calling that day the Akathist.*[^1402]

*Verse:*
*With unsleeping hymns thy City gives thanks unto thee, O Theotokos,*
*Praising thee as its unsleeping Protectress in battle.*

In the days of Heraclius, the Emperor of the Greeks, Khosrow, who held the sovereignty of the Persians, sent his general Shahrbaraz with a great multitude of troops against the entire eastern region under Greek rule, sweeping across it like fiery lightning, burning, scorching, and destroying all things.[^1403] Having laid waste the eastern lands with great boldness and mocked the Greeks whom he encountered there—for there was no one able to resist him or cut short his mighty invasion, the Greek forces having been previously humbled and exhausted by the harsh and ferocious tyranny of the soldier Phocas, who had ruled the empire like a tyrant rather than an emperor—Shahrbaraz advanced as far as Chalcedon; and pitching his camp round about it, he prepared everything for the destruction of the Great and Imperial City, devising and counseling every stratagem toward that end.

Seeing these things, Emperor Heraclius, who had newly assumed the throne after the tyrant Phocas and was weighed down by great affliction and toil, left the capital and sailed across the Euxine Sea—that is, the Black Sea—having gathered whatever military forces remained, and suddenly, without anyone knowing of his movement, marched into the lands of Persia. Learning of the Emperor's departure, the Khagan of the Scythians immediately seized his opportunity: he filled the sea with vessels hollowed out of single logs (which for this reason are called *monoxyla* in the Greek tongue),[^1404] and covered the dry land with horsemen and foot soldiers.

Meanwhile, the people remaining within the Imperial City were comforted and instructed by Sergius, who was then Patriarch, not to lose hope: "Take courage, my children," said the Patriarch, "and let us place the hope of our salvation in God, lifting up our hands and our eyes unto Him with all our soul! He will deliver us from the evils that encompass us, and will scatter all the barbarian counsels devised against us." Strengthened by these words of the Patriarch, the inhabitants of the city persevered in faith toward the Most Pure Theotokos and toward Christ our God, Who for His love of mankind was well-pleased to become incarnate of her; and awaiting His mercy upon themselves, they endured in hope—which shortly thereafter they were counted worthy to receive, not falling short of their expectation.

Now Patrician Bonos, who had been left behind by the Emperor to guard the city, spared no effort in devising every defense possible against the enemy; "For God wills," said he, "that we should not sit idle and inactive, but should labor and take forethought on our own part while placing all our hope of salvation in Him; for thus did Joshua the son of Nun command ambushes and stratagems to be prepared against the men of Ai, and thus did Gideon arm his men with water pitchers and torches against Midian."[^1405] Patrician Bonos therefore fortified the walls of the city and prepared everything needed for defense; while Patriarch Sergius, taking up the sacred icons of the Mother of God—especially that upon which the Infant Savior is depicted borne in His Mother's arms—walked in procession around the walls of the city, establishing thereby a firm bulwark for the citizens, and bringing terror, flight, and ruin upon the opposing barbarians—

=== LEAF p510 ===
—delivering the enemy ultimately to destruction. For when the Persians encamped at Chalcedon began to set fire to the suburbs round about Chalcedon and to commit all the other ravages customary for pillagers who sack cities, the Scythians and barbarians on the European side showed themselves doing the very same things as the Persians.

Taking up once more the Icon of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ Not Made by Hands, together with the divine Robe of the Most Pure Theotokos and the Life-giving Wood of the Cross, the Patriarch walked around the walls of the city, praying with tears and saying: "Let God arise and help us, and let Thine enemies be scattered, and let them vanish like smoke, and melt away like wax before the face of fire!"[^1406]

Before three days had passed after the conflagration, the entire host of the Scythian commander advanced against the city; and so vast was the multitude of the adversaries, arrayed in numberless ranks and fortified in full armor, that to speak truly there were ten Scythians gathered against every single Greek! Yet our Champion Leader, the Most Pure Sovereign Lady who fights on behalf of Christians and is a speedy helper to those who call upon her, came to the aid of a detachment of soldiers at her divine Church called the *Pege* (the Life-giving Spring),[^1407] where they slew a great number of the attacking Scythians. This manifest assistance granted to the Christians by the all-powerful Commander, the Mother of God, not only cast down the enemy before the Greeks, but also reduced the boastful arrogance of the Scythian warlord to bewilderment through such generalship on the part of the Mother of God, foreshadowing the complete destruction that was shortly to overtake them.

Beholding so great a fall of the enemy before the citizens, and recognizing it as a sign and beginning of the foes' ruin, the Greeks took heart and were filled with great courage; and day by day they stood ready to join battle against them, having the Mother of God fighting alongside them, rendering them stronger and frustrating the stratagems, strength, and assaults of the Scythians.

After these things, by the common consent of the Patriarch, the Prefect Bonos, and the people, envoys were sent forth from the city bearing gifts to speak words of peace to the Scythians and invite them to a truce. The Khagan, however, being beast-like in temper rather than human, and exceedingly covetous in character, accepted the gifts while spurning the proposals of peace, and sent the envoys back empty-handed, saying only this unto them: "Deceive not yourselves concerning the God in Whom ye trust! Assuredly tomorrow I will seize your city with my hand like a bird in a nest, and will lay it waste; and out of my own clemency I will let you all go free wearing only a single garment apiece! Depart then from the city whithersoever ye wish, for I shall show you no greater humanity than this"—after that wretched man had first uttered many blasphemies against Christ our God and against Her who bore Him.

Hearing these words from the returning envoys, the inhabitants of the city—all the priests, the clergy, the monastics, and the entire populace—groaned from the depths of their hearts; and hastening to the holy churches of God, they lifted up their hands toward heaven and prayed with abundant tears, saying: "O Lord our Defender, O Lord, look down from Thy holy dwelling-place and behold this foul barbarian and those with him, who have dared to blaspheme Thy holy and great Name! Cast him down, O Lord our Protector, lest it be said among the nations: 'Where is their God?'[^1408] For Thou art our God, Who resistest the proud and lookest upon the humble! Thy dominion is incomparable and Thy Kingdom indestructible; hear the words which this barbarian hath sent to reproach Thee, Who reignest over all! Deliver the city of Thine inheritance and the people called by Thy Name!"

Thus did the weeping people pray; and God prevented the Persians from crossing over to join the Khagan as they had agreed, slaying no small number of the envoys whom they sent back and forth to one another. Fighting along the outskirts for two and three days and assaulting various sectors without rest, both enemy hosts prepared a simultaneous general assault according to their pact: by land they brought up siege engines to batter down the walls of the city, while by sea they armed the countless Scythian *monoxyla*, intending at one and the same day and hour to strike the Imperial City simultaneously by land and by sea.

While this assault was being prepared, the Khagan took his chosen horsemen and rode toward the mouth of the great strait to show himself to the Persian army opposite, while the Persian general did likewise on the Asian shore; the one from Asia and the other from Europe roared like wild beasts against the Imperial City, supposing that they already held it as their sure prey.

Yet who can recount the miracles of God wrought at that hour, or who can declare the tender compassion of the Virgin toward us? For when the Khagan filled the bay of the Golden Horn with *monoxyla* and armed warriors therein—attempting by land to overthrow the walls while by sea the forces in the Golden Horn stood ready to scale the city—our Lord and God, Whom they had blasphemed and Who is God over all, through the prayers of the Virgin Theotokos who bore Him, showed their hopes to be vain and fruitless! On every side of the land walls the Greek soldiers slew so vast a multitude of the enemy that the survivors were no longer able even to burn their dead.

Such was the fate of those who fought by land and strove to cast down the walls. As for the *monoxyla* together with all the warriors aboard them, opposite the divine Church of our Sovereign Lady the Theotokos at Blachernae, she sank them all suddenly in a single moment by raising a dreadful and violent tempest! For as soon as those vessels drew near her divine Church, the sea rose up exceedingly high and divided at the divine command, engulfing and sinking them all immediately together with the *monoxyla* themselves.

And there was to be seen an unutterable and great miracle: for the sea raised waves equal to mountains, roaring like a fierce tempest; and leaping furiously upon the enemies of the Mother of God, the billows mercilessly destroyed them, just as of old the sea did unto the Egyptians when they pursued ancient Israel, who walked through the midst thereof on foot in a marvelous manner![^1409] Thus did the Theotokos fight in the forefront for her city and defend it, granting bloodless victories through such generalship against those who had brought measureless warfare upon her inheritance and had lifted up weapons against her—
