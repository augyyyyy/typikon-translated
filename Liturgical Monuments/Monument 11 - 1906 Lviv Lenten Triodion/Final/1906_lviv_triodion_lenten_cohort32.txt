*At "Lord, I have cried", we set 10 stichoi; and we sing the Idiomelon twice; and 4 Martyrika of the tone, and in the Menaion 4.*

*Idiomelon, Tone 7:*[^885]  
Having enslaved the dignity of my soul to the passions, I became as a beast; and I am unable to look up unto Thee, the Most High; but bowing down low, O Christ, as the Publican I pray, crying unto Thee: O God, cleanse me, and save me! *(Twice)*

*Glory: For the departed, by John of Damascus.*[^886] *Both now: Theotokion of the 1st tone.*

*Entrance. "O Gladsome Light".*

*Prokeimenon, Psalm 79 [LXX 79:2, 3], Tone 4:*[^887]  
Give ear, O Shepherd of Israel, Thou that leadest Joseph like a sheep!  
*Verse: Thou that sittest upon the Cherubim, show Thyself!*

*Reading from Genesis (Chapter 12:1–7):*[^888]  
The Lord said unto Abram: Go forth out of thy land, and from thy kindred, and from the house of thy father, and go into the land which I shall show thee. And I will make of thee a great nation, and I will bless thee, and magnify thy name, and thou shalt be blessed. And I will bless them that bless thee, and curse them that curse thee; and in thee shall all the tribes of the earth be blessed. And Abram went, as the Lord spoke unto him, and Lot went with him; and Abram was seventy and five years old when he departed from Haran. And Abram took Sarai his wife, and Lot his brother's son, and all their substance that they had acquired, and every soul that they had gotten in Haran; and they went forth to go into the land of Canaan. And Abram traversed the land in the length thereof unto the place of Shechem, unto the high oak; and the Canaanites were then dwelling in the land. And the Lord appeared unto Abram, and said unto him: Unto thy seed will I give this land. And Abram built there an altar unto the Lord Who appeared unto him.

*Prokeimenon, Psalm 80 [LXX 80:2, 3], Tone 4:*[^889]  
Rejoice in God our Helper!  
*Verse: Take a psalm, and bring the timbrel!*

*Reading from Proverbs (Chapter 14:15–26):*[^890]  
The simple believes every word, but the prudent comes unto repentance. A wise man fearing turns aside from evil; but the fool, trusting in himself, mingles with the lawless. A quick-tempered man acts without counsel; but a prudent man endures many things. Fools divide malice; but the prudent shall retain understanding. The evil shall stumble before the good, and the ungodly shall minister at the gates of the righteous. Friends will hate poor friends; but the friends of the rich are many. He that dishonors the needy sins; but he that has mercy on the poor is blessed. The erring (unrighteous) devise evil things; but the good devise mercy and truth; workers of evil know not mercy and faith, but merciful deeds and faith are with workers of good. In everyone that is careful there is abundance; but the pleasure-lover and careless shall be in want. The crown of the wise is their wealth; but the way of fools is evil. A faithful witness shall deliver a soul from evil; but a deceitful man kindles falsehoods. In the fear of the Lord is hope of strength; and unto His children He leaves a confirmation (of peace).

## FOURTH SUNDAY OF THE GREAT FAST: COMMEMORATION OF OUR VENERABLE FATHER JOHN OF THE LADDER (CLIMACUS)

*On Saturday evening at "Lord, I have cried", we sing 3 Resurrection stichera, and 4 Anatolian [Eastern] stichera, and 3 from the Menaion.*

*But other later typika have appointed the office of our Venerable Father John of the Ladder to be sung.*[^915]

*Stichera, Tone 8, Podoben: "O most marvelous wonder" (Ѡ҆ пресла́внагѡ чꙋдесѐ):*[^916]  
*Troparion:*  
O venerable Father John, truly didst thou continually bear the high praises of God in thy throat, meditating all-wisely upon the practical, divinely-inspired words, and wast enriched by the grace flowing thence, becoming blessed, and overturning the counsels of all the ungodly.

*The same Podoben:*  
O all-glorious Father John, having cleansed thy soul with fountains of tears, and having propitiated God by all-night standings, thou didst soar, O blessed one, unto His love and beauty, which thou now worthily enjoyest, rejoicing unceasingly with thy fellow sufferers, O divinely-wise and venerable father!

*The same Podoben:*  
O venerable Father John, having winged thy mind unto God by faith, thou didst abhor the unsteady confusion of the world, and taking up thy cross, didst follow the All-Seeing One: having enslaved the unbridled body to the mind through the exploits of ascetic training, by the power of the Holy Spirit.

*Glory, Tone 5:*[^917]  
O venerable Father, having listened unto the voice of the Lord's Gospel, thou didst forsake the world, counting wealth and glory as nothing; whence thou didst cry aloud unto all: Love God, and ye shall find eternal grace; prefer nothing before His love, so that when He comes in His glory,

=== LEAF p319 ===
...ye shall find rest with all the saints: by whose prayers, O Christ, preserve and save our souls!

*Both now: Theotokion of the 1st tone.*

*At the Aposticha, stichera of the Octoechos.*

*Glory: For the Venerable One, Tone 2, Idiomelon:*[^918]  
Let us honor John, an angel on earth and a man of God in the heavens, the adornment of the world, the sweetness of good things, the boast of ascetic virtues; for planted in the house of God, he flourished righteously, and like a cedar in the wilderness he multiplied the flocks of Christ's rational sheep in holiness and righteousness!

*Both now: Theotokion, the same tone: "O new wonder" (Ѡ҆ чꙋдесѐ но́вагѡ).*

*"Now lettest Thou Thy servant depart..." Troparion, Tone 1:*[^919]  
A dweller of the wilderness and an angel in the body, and a wonderworker wast thou shown to be, O our God-bearing father John: having received heavenly gifts by fasting, vigilance, and prayer, thou healest the diseased and the souls of them that with faith have recourse unto thee: Glory to Him Who gave thee strength! Glory to Him Who crowned thee! Glory to Him Who works healings for all through thee!
