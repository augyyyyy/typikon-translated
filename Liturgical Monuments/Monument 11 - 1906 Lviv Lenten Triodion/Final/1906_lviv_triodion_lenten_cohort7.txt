To the righteous souls whose memory ever abides,

On this day we celebrate the memory of all the saints who have shone forth in ascetic fasting, both men and women: for since the God-bearing Fathers, having instructed us through the introductory feasts, prepared us beforehand for the stadium, having removed us from luxury and satiety, and having admonished us with fear of the future judgment, and having duly pre-purified us through Cheesefare Week, gracefully set two periods of fasting in the midst, that little by little they might lead us unto it. Lo, also all the venerable ones who lived thus, and in other manners, through many afflictions and labors, both men and women together, they set before us in the midst: that through the memory of their struggles they might make us stronger for the stadium, having their life as a certain pattern and way. And that receiving from them assistance and help, we may be drawn unto spiritual struggles, considering that they also shared the same nature with us. For even as commanders encourage their armed hosts already standing in battle array, by words, examples, and commemorations of ancient men who fought eminently and acted valiantly, and thereby being fortified, the soldiers arm themselves with whole soul in the hope of victory: so also now the God-bearing Fathers wisely act: by those who lived venerably in the male and female sex, fortifying us for spiritual struggles, they thus go before unto the stadium of fasting, that looking upon their archetypal, unenvious life, we may work diverse and manifold virtues, as each has strength. Having love as our forerunner, and that which occurs with understanding, the turning away from dishonorable deeds and actions. And this fast is not of foods only, but also of the tongue, and of anger, of the eyes, and simply to speak, cessation and estrangement from every evil. For this reason the holy Fathers instituted here the present commemoration of all the saints, instructing us by the presentation of those who pleased God by fasting and other good deeds,

=== LEAF p66 ===
by the example of these unto the stadium of the virtues, and to arm ourselves valiantly against the passions and against demons: and setting forth a certain pattern, that if we also offer equal diligence unto them, there shall be no obstacle to accomplish all that they also accomplished, and to be accounted worthy of the same honors: for these also shared the same nature with us. And concerning the eating of cheese, some say that Emperor Heraclius instituted this, having previously been observed in few places: for having waged war against Chosroes and the Persians for six years, the Emperor promised unto God that if he should prevail over them, he would propose this, and leave it in the midst between fasting and food, which he also did. But I deem rather that even if this occurred, it was also devised by the holy Fathers for the sake of a certain pre-purification, that being led from meats and much eating straightway into extreme non-eating, we might not be terrified. And in another manner, we are harmed by bodily custom, but having gently and little by little instructed us, and holding us back from delicacies, like unbridled horses running unto food, we may receive the bridle of the Fast. For as they did with parables concerning the soul, so also they prudently ordered concerning the body, cutting off little by little the stumbling blocks unto the Fast. By the prayers of all Thy venerable ones, O Christ our God, have mercy on us. Amen.

## ON CHEESEFARE SUNDAY: THE EXPULSION OF ADAM

*The Expulsion of Adam.*
*Usual Vigil.*

*On Saturday evening at the Lamplighting psalms, after the introductory psalm we chant the stichologia: "Blessed is the man:" the entire kathisma.*
*At "Lord, I have cried," we set 10 stichera, and we chant 3 Resurrectional stichera of the Octoechos, and 3 Anatolian stichera, and 4 of the Triodion, Tone 6. Model: "Having laid aside all:"*

My Creator and Lord, having taken dust from the earth, having quickened me with a life-bearing breath, made me alive, and honored me upon earth as ruler over all visible things, and companion of the angels; but crafty Satan, having used the serpent as a vessel, deceived me with food, and separated me from the glory of God, and handed me over unto death in the nethermost parts of the earth: but as Master and tender-hearted, call me back again.[^196]

I, wretched one, stripped off the God-woven garments, having disobeyed Thy divine command, O Lord, by the counsel of the enemy, and have now clothed myself in fig leaves and coats of skins; for by sweat was I condemned to eat bread of labor, and the earth was cursed to bring forth thorns and thistles unto me; but Thou Who in latter years wast incarnate of the Virgin, having called me back, lead me again into Paradise.[^197]

O all-honorable Paradise, most fair beauty, God-fashioned dwelling, unending gladness and delight, glory of the righteous, beauty of the prophets, and dwelling of the saints: by the sound of thy leaves pray the Creator of all to open unto me the gates which I closed by transgression, and make me worthy to receive the tree of life, and the joy of which I formerly tasted in thee.[^198]

Adam was expelled from Paradise through disobedience, and was cast out of sweetness, deceived by woman's words, and he sits naked, wailing straight opposite the garden, alas for me! Therefore let us all make haste to receive the season of the Fast, obeying the evangelical traditions: that having thereby become well-pleasing unto Christ, we may regain the dwelling of Paradise.

Adam was expelled from Paradise through food; therefore sitting opposite it he wailed, lamenting with a mournful voice, and said: "Woe is me, what have I suffered, wretched that I am! One commandment of the Master I transgressed, and was deprived of all good things! O most holy Paradise, planted for my sake, and shut on account of Eve: pray unto Him Who made thee and fashioned me, that I may be filled with thy flowers." Therefore the Savior said unto him: "I will not that My creation should perish, but I desire it to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth; for him that comes unto Me I will in no wise cast out."[^200]

*Ode 1. Heirmos: As upon dry land:*

Come, my passion-laden soul, lament thy deeds today, remembering the first stripping in Eden, through which thou wast cast out from sweetness and unceasing joy.

O the manifold tender-heartedness and compassions, O Fashioner of creation and Creator of all, Who having quickened me from dust before, didst command me to hymn Thee with Thine Angels!
