In conclusion, he bade them farewell.

## TITULUS I. On the Catholic Faith

### CHAPTER I. On the Profession of the Catholic Faith

I. We decree that the "Profession of Faith" according to the specimen annexed hereto [^49] for Easterners, prescribed by Pope Urban VIII, shall be made by:  
a) The members of the Provincial Synod and of the Diocesan Synod at the opening thereof;  
b) Those elevated to the Episcopate, prior to their consecration;  
c) Cathedral Dignitaries and Canons, prior to their installation;  
d) Generals and Provincials of Monastic Orders and Archimandrites, as also Monks of both sexes, when they are admitted to the Order;  
e) Professors of Theology and Philosophy who are to instruct candidates for the clerical state;  
f) Adjuncts (Assistant Lecturers) of Theology;  
g) Priests and Catechists;  
h) Deans;

---

*(Physical Page 74 / Leaf p76)*

i) Those endowed with any benefices connected with the cure of souls;  
k) Newly appointed Confessors of Nuns (Monastic Sisters);  
l) Those about to receive Holy Orders, prior to the reception of ordination.

### CHAPTER II. On Catholic Doctrine

1. Whereas "without faith it is impossible to please God" [^50], and whereas faith is the foundation and root of justification, this Synod therefore beginneth from it.  
All things, therefore, which the Catholic Church, whether by solemn judgment or by ordinary and universal magisterium, proposeth to be believed as divinely revealed, we believe, by solemn profession we confess, and we teach: namely, that which at the Ecumenical Vatican Council was pronounced in the dogmatic decrees on Catholic faith [^51], on the Church, and on the Roman Pontiff, and on His full, supreme, ordinary, and immediate jurisdiction and government over all and singular pastors and faithful, and on His infallible authority in teaching when He speaketh *ex cathedra* [^52].  
2. We declare that there must be held with the whole soul and a sincere spirit the dogmatic doctrine of Pius IX of holy memory, pronounced on the Immaculate Conception of the Mother of God in the Bull *Ineffabilis Deus* ("The Ineffable God") on the 8th day of December, 1854, solemnly promulgated in the following words: "We declare, pronounce, and define that the doctrine which holds that the most Blessed Virgin Mary, in the first instance of her conception, by a singular grace and privilege granted by Almighty God, in view of the merits of Jesus Christ, the Savior of the human race, was preserved free from all stain of original sin, is a doctrine revealed by God and therefore to be believed firmly and constantly by all the faithful" [^53].

---

*(Physical Page 75 / Leaf p77)*

3. Likewise, all and singular false opinions and doctrines that are gaining ground in our age, which the same Ecumenical Pontiff Pius IX, in his Consistorial Allocutions and in the Encyclical Letter *Quanta cura* ("How Great Solicitude") [^54] and in the annexed Syllabus of the 8th of December, 1864 [^55], by apostolic authority rejected and condemned, we in this Synod recognize and confess to be entirely rejected, and we pronounce that they must be held as rejected and condemned under penalty of the loss of salvation.  
4. We accept the Catholic doctrine concerning the duties and rights of princes and subjects, concerning the domestic family, which is the foundation of every civil society and kingdom, and concerning the right of property and possession, set forth against the errors of socialists, communists, and nihilists in the Encyclical of our Most Holy Father Pope Leo XIII of the 28th of January, 1878 [^56]; we accept and profess that all are bound to receive it, and we likewise promulgate it in this Synod.  
5. We likewise accept and command to be accepted all that the Most Holy Father Pope Leo XIII, on the Christian constitution of states, pronounced and set forth in the Encyclical *Immortale Dei* ("The Immortal God") of the 1st of November, 1885, that the nature and boundary of the rights of the Church and civil society, and her obligations in regard to religion, might be made manifest, and that Catholic people might at the same time learn what, according to the needs of these times, they ought to do in public and private affairs, and what they ought to avoid [^57]. In like manner, that which the Most Holy Pope Leo XIII taught concerning the condition of workers in the Encyclical Letter *Rerum novarum* ("Of New Things") of the 15th of May, 1891, we accept and revere [^58].  
6. In fine, we declare that all that was decreed concerning the Catholic faith at the Synod of Zamość in the year 1720 must be preserved [^59].

### CHAPTER III. On Religious Indifferentism

1. It is a dogma of faith that no one outside the true Church of Christ can attain eternal salvation. But the true and sole Church of Christ is that to which belongeth all that hath been so abundantly and wonderfully brought together by divine arrangement for the evident credibility of the Christian faith, since her wondrous propagation, eminent holiness, inexhaustible fruitfulness in all good things, Catholic unity, and invincible stability provide the greatest and perpetual guarantee of credibility and irrefutable proof of her divine mission. Wherefore, that abominable error which is called religious indifferentism, and which placeth the faithful on an equal footing with those who have not yet attained to the one true faith, we condemn and reject.  
2. Whereas, however, despite the perniciousness of this system of religious indifferentism, an opposing opinion hath in our times grown strong among many, even Catholics, whether through ignorance in Christian doctrine or through coldness in faith: this Synod, striving to root out this kind of hazardous error from the minds of the faithful, earnestly beseecheth them in God and admonisheth them that they preserve with great care the doctrine of the Catholic Church here set forth, since it provideth the only means of attaining the final end for which man was created; and it commandeth pastors in God that they themselves also set forth the same in their public instructions on fitting occasions, praise the grace of holy union whereby they are placed in Christ's true fold, and explain with a dignity worthy of their office the differences of dogmas that exist between the Church of Christ and that which dareth to call herself "Orthodox," lest through their negligence it should perchance happen to them that on the Day of Judgment there will be required at their hands the blood of the sheep redeemed by the precious blood of the Son of God [^60].

### CHAPTER IV. On Communion in Sacred Things with Heretics and Schismatics

1. Although toward non-Catholics who out of manifest perversity and malicious obstinacy rise up against the Catholic Church and her doctrine, Christ's faithful ought to conduct themselves with all circumspection and caution, and it is even fitting to avoid all familiar intercourse with them, as the Holy Spirit admonisheth us by the mouth of the holy Apostles, when He saith: *"If there come any unto you, and bring not this doctrine, receive him not into your house, neither bid him God speed: for he that biddeth him God speed is partaker of his evil deeds"* [^61]; and again: *"A man that is a heretic after the first and second admonition reject; knowing that he that is such is subverted, and sinneth, being condemned of himself"* [^62]; nevertheless, toward others who either live in good faith in their religion or plot nothing hostile against the Catholic Church, Christ's faithful are not forbidden to have friendly relations; moreover, let them strive to show them love, kindness, and goodwill in word and deed, that by this means winning them over, they may turn them away from errors and unite them with the Catholic Church.  
2. Let them take heed, however, that they never have with them such relations as are called "in sacred things" (in spiritual matters); and this the more so, because thereby non-Catholics might be confirmed in the errors of their false religion, as though by a tacit approval of the same on the part of the faithful; wherefore such communion "in sacred things" (in spiritual matters) hath been forbidden in the Church of Christ at all times, both by the canons of councils and by the decrees of the Supreme Pontiffs and the Roman Sacred Congregations, as also by the teachings of the holy Fathers. For in the canon law of the Greeks many canons are cited concerning this subject, the so-called "Apostolic Canons," namely [^63]: *"If anyone shall pray, even in a house, with an excommunicated person, let him be excommunicated"*

[^64]; *"If anyone, being a clergyman, shall pray together with one who is deposed, let him also be deposed"* [^65]; *"If a bishop, or presbyter, or deacon, shall only pray with heretics, let him be excommunicated; but if he hath permitted them to perform any clerical ministration, let him be deposed"* [^66].  
3. Two ancient provincial councils of the East are likewise cited there, namely: of Antioch and of Laodicea, of which the former decreed thus: *"It is not lawful to communicate with those who are excommunicated, nor to assemble in houses to pray with those who do not communicate in the Church, nor can those be received in one Church who have no part in another Church"* [^67]; and the latter thus: *"Heretics must not be permitted to enter into the house of God while they continue in heresy. It is not fitting to permit those who belong to the Church to visit the cemeteries or places called martyria (shrines of martyrs) of any heretics for the sake of prayer or veneration. But such, if they be of the faithful, shall be deprived of communion for an appointed time; yet if they repent and confess that they have sinned, it is fitting to receive them. Likewise it is not fitting to pray with heretics or schismatics"* [^68].  
As for the decrees of the Supreme Pontiffs and the Sacred Congregations, they, explicitly and expressly treating of this matter, forbid communion in sacred things with non-Catholics, as is evident from the instructions of the Congregation of the Holy Office of June 8, 1859, "On communion with heretics" (*Communicatio cum haereticis*); likewise of the year 1871 to the Archbishop of Corfu, "On the conferral of the Sacrament of Baptism" (*In collatione Sacram. baptismi*); likewise of the year 1729, "Whereas often" (*Cum saepe*); likewise of Thursday, May 10, 1753, "To the first" (*Ad primum*); and of Friday, June 1, 1866, to the Latin-rite Archbishop of Lviv, "It may be tolerated" (*Tolerari posse*); and finally of March 30, 1885, which are appended hereto in the appendix of this Synod [^69].
