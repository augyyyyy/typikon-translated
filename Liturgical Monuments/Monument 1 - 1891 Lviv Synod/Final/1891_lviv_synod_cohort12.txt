### Titulus IX: On Priests
### Chapter I: On the Dignity of a Priest of the New Testament

TITULUS IX.

CHAPTER I.
On the Dignity of a Priest of the New Testament.

1. That which the divinely inspired Sirach writeth in the Book of Wisdom concerning the height and glory of the first High Priest of the law of shadows pertaineth unto every priest of the Gospel law—

=== LEAF p223 ===
*(Physical Page 223 / Book Page 219)*

as holy Fathers Augustine [^209] and Gregory the Great [^210] assert. For thus Sirach writeth concerning Aaron the brother of Moses [^211]: "He exalted Aaron, a holy man like unto him, his brother... He gave him the priesthood of the people, and blessed him with splendid ornament." And afterwards, having described the wondrous costliness and beauty of the sacred vestments, he continueth thus: "Their sacrifices were burned every day twice continually... He chose him out of all men living to offer sacrifice unto the Lord, incense, and a sweet savor for a memorial, to make reconciliation for his people. He gave unto him His commandments, and authority in the covenants of judgments, to teach Jacob the testimonies, and to enlighten Israel in His law [^212]." What is more, every priest of the New Testament is so much higher and more glorious than Aaron as reality exceedeth the shadow. For he is chosen by God out of the general multitude of the human race, that he may offer sacrifices unto Him, and that not of calves, or of goats, or of fragrant material incense, to be consumed indeed by fire coming down from heaven, yet material; but the sacrifice of the unblemished Body and the most precious Blood of the very Only-Begotten Son of God for an odor of sweetness penetrating the clouds and ascending even unto the throne of God, to be consumed by a fire far more wondrous, as St. Chrysostom saith [^213], that is, by the Holy Spirit, transforming the proposed gifts. For the Lord, rejecting the sacrifices of the Old Testament together with its priests, glorifieth Himself concerning this alone in Malachi [^214], saying: "I have no pleasure in you, saith the Lord Almighty, neither will I accept a sacrifice at your hands; for from the rising of the sun even unto the going down thereof My name is glorified among the Gentiles; and in every place incense is offered unto My name, and a pure sacrifice: for My name is great among the Gentiles, saith the Lord Almighty." Likewise also at the changing of bread and wine into the most honorable Body and Blood of the Lord, the priest showeth himself to be a far greater and more frequent worker of miracles than was Moses at the turning of the Egyptian rivers into blood and his staff into a serpent. Admiring this, the renowned Teacher of Africa, St. Augustine, exclaimeth: "O re-

=== LEAF p224 ===
*(Physical Page 224 / Book Page 220)*

verend dignity of priests, in whose hands the Son of God is incarnate, as it were in the Virgin's womb!" And again: "O venerable holiness of hands! O happy exercise! He Who created me, if it be lawful so to speak, gave unto me to create Himself; and He Who created me without me, created Himself through me!" [^215]. But what shall we say concerning the power of the priest over the Mystical Body of Christ, or over the Church? If the justification of the ungodly, as St. Thomas teacheth, is the greatest work of God, then the priest who absolveth a man from sins performeth a greater work than the creating of heaven and earth, even as St. Augustine also previously taught, saying: "It is a greater work to make a righteous man out of an ungodly man than to create heaven and earth" [^216].
2. Gathering all this together, Cyril III, Metropolitan of Kyiv, in the middle of the 13th century in his Synod thus addressed the priests: "Hear, O holy council of priests, for unto you do I turn my words: Ye are called earthly angels and heavenly men; ye together with the angels stand at the altar of the Lord; ye with the Seraphim bear the Lord; ye bring down the Holy Spirit from heaven, and change the bread into the Body of Christ, and the wine into the Blood of Christ!... Ye bind on earth, and God doth not loose in heaven; ye loose on earth, and God doth not bind in heaven. Through you doth God accomplish the mystery of the salvation of the human race; you hath He Himself appointed as watchmen and pastors of His sheep, for whom He poured out His most precious blood" [^217].
3. But although the priest of the New Testament is not clothed with such fair material garments as Aaron, yet is he clothed with raiment far more precious, immaterial, the author whereof is not man, but the Holy Spirit Himself, and clad in Whose vesture the priest confesseth himself in the prayer of the Cherubic Hymn, praying: "And strengthen me by the power of the Holy Spirit, clad with the grace of the priesthood": and before the Holy Liturgy, at the putting on of the sticharion (*stikhar*), in the person of the Church, the Bride of Christ, and in the person of Christ, the Bridegroom

=== LEAF p225 ===
*(Physical Page 225 / Book Page 221)*

of the Church [^218], saying: "My soul shall exult in the Lord, for He hath clothed me with the garment of salvation, and with the robe of gladness hath He covered me; He hath set a crown upon me as upon a bridegroom, and as a bride hath He adorned me with beauty" [^219].
4. But unto the dignity of so lofty an excellence ought to correspond excellence of life, that is, a constellation of virtues and a treasure of sacred learning, wherewith, as if with two wings, the priest, being provided, in the fulfillment of his office soareth aloft, and which were prefigured in the very vestments of Aaron.

### Chapter II: On the Righteousness of Life of a Priest

CHAPTER II.
On the Righteousness of Life of a Priest.

1. The constellation of virtues wherewith a priest ought to shine is derived by St. Thomas from the eight sacred garments of Aaron, in such wise that the breeches (*nadrahy*) signify chastity; the linen tunic, purity of life; the girdle, moderation in choice; the tiara or mitre, rectitude of intention; the golden plate upon the forehead, unceasing remembrance of God in meditation; the shoulder-pieces (*naramennyk*), bearing the infirmities of the people upon oneself; the breastplate, solicitude of love which they ought to bear in their heart and bosom toward the people. The hyacinth-blue tunic signifieth heavenly conversation through works of perfection. Afterwards he addeth, saying: for this cause also there are attached unto the hem of the hyacinth tunic golden bells, whereby is signified the knowledge of divine things, which ought to be joined unto the heavenly conversation of the High Priest; there are joined likewise pomegranates, whereby are expressed unity of faith and concord in good morals, since his doctrine ought to be so conjoined that the unity of faith and peace may not be severed through it [^220]. Likewise the ecumenical teacher St. Cyril of Alexandria similarly explaineth the signification of the bells and pomegranates (in the book: *De adoratione in spiritu et veritate*), writing: "For those golden bells and cities' pomegranates are by no means an obscure riddle of saving preaching. For even as a

=== LEAF p226 ===
*(Physical Page 226 / Book Page 222)*

city is indeed surrounded by a single wall on the outside, but containeth far more houses within itself, in that very same manner a pomegranate is surrounded by a single outer rind only, but within the seeds are separated among themselves by delicate membranes, as if by walls, and I might almost say they cluster like little houses. A bell therefore is joined unto each pomegranate, for each city also hath its own Teacher, who by the sound and harmony of divine teachings might proclaim unto all the entrance of our Savior into the Holy of Holies." For the dread of death threatened those who were charged with performing the priestly office in the tabernacle, namely Aaron, if the bells should not resound to the uttermost. For silence is especially perilous for Teachers, and this is evidently that concerning which St. Paul saith: "For woe is unto me, if I preach not the gospel." And St. Jerome addeth: "Such ought to be the learning and knowledge of the High Priest of God that both his walk and his gestures and everything should be a preaching. Let him understand the truth with his mind, and proclaim it by his whole posture and dress, so that whatever he doeth, whatever he speaketh, may be instruction for the nations: for without bells and diverse colors, and precious stones and flowers of virtues, he cannot enter into the Holy Place, nor possess the name of Superior" [^221].
In particular, as to the righteousness of life, the Synod setteth forth for priests the following precepts for observance: first, in the house of God; next, in their own house; and finally, outside the house, that they may know what they ought most of all to avoid and what to perform.
2. "Of all priests," saith St. Leo the Great, "the choice is so excellent that that which in other members of the Church is free from guilt, in them is nevertheless regarded as unlawful" [^222]. Wherefore justly doth the holy Council of Trent [^223] so wisely declare concerning the life of priests: "There is nothing that doth more continually dispose others unto piety and divine worship than the life and example of those who have dedicated themselves unto the service of God. For when they are seen to be raised above the affairs of this world unto a higher sta-

=== LEAF p227 ===
*(Physical Page 227 / Book Page 223)*

tion, the rest turn their eyes upon them as into a mirror, and take from them that which they ought to imitate. Wherefore it behoveth clerics, called unto the lot of the Lord's ministry, so to order their entire life and morals that by their dress, gesture, walk, speech, and all other things they may show forth only that which is grave, moderate, and full of religion; let them avoid even slight faults, which in them would be very great, so that their actions may awaken reverence in all." And to begin with that which a priest ought to avoid, then—first: in the house of God and the church, or during sacred actions, they shall not converse, nor gaze curiously upon the faithful; they shall not celebrate sacred actions, especially the awe-inspiring Sacrifice, superficially, distractedly, hastily, and with an absent, dissipated mind; and the holy rites themselves they shall perform uniformly, and execute accurately according to the exemplar which this Synod presenteth for observance in the rubric specially prepared for that purpose. Furthermore, during sacred actions they shall not scold anyone; they shall not forget also to render honor or reverences (prostrations / *metanias*) before the Most Holy Gifts, not haphazard, but profound; they shall not approach the celebration of the Holy Liturgy without preparation and in a state of distraction, or without a cassock (*reverenda*) and without a collar (*oboychyk*), or with disheveled hair, or with a beard untrimmed for several days, or in torn garments and soiled vestments; nor shall they dare to celebrate sacred actions on unclean vessels; and finally, in the house of God, where His Only-Begotten Son Himself dwelleth with a multitude of angels present, they shall not behave themselves as in human dwellings.
3. At home they shall not neglect the recitation of the Canonical Hours, and they shall not approach the table without prayer, nor depart from it without having given thanks; and priests shall not cease to watch that all members of the family and servants do not pass their lives without daily exercises of piety, that on feast days they fail not to be present at divine services and at appointed times receive the Holy Mysteries; let them endeavor to preserve peace and concord in their house among all members, mindful of the words of the holy

=== LEAF p228 ===
*(Physical Page 228 / Book Page 224)*

Apostle, saying [^224]: "For if a man know not how to rule his own house, how shall he take care of the church of God?" Moreover, this present Synod strictly commandeth that on fast days, and especially on feast days and temple patronal feasts on which fasting is prescribed, such as: on the day of the Exaltation of the Honorable Cross, the Beheading of St. John the Baptist, and others, priests themselves shall not eat meat, whether openly or secretly, nor tolerate that others eat it, and that on such days they shall not permit dancing and music to be held in their house or among the faithful. An evil example given in this matter by a pastor exceedingly scandalizeth the faithful and leadeth them unto depreciation of religion and of the authority of the priest himself; likewise the priests themselves, on the occasion of recreation during any feast, shall not forget that they will celebrate the Divine Liturgy on the following day, and that it behoveth them to be fasting from midnight; likewise they shall avoid games of hazard at cards, played with excessive stakes and for the sake of obtaining gain; likewise they shall not engage in hunting, for such an occupation betrayeth a worldly spirit and levity, incompatible with the dignity of those who on every occasion ought to be fishers of souls by the spotlessness of their life, after the example of St. Josaphat and St. Cajetan, who among the people earned for themselves the name of hunters and catchers of souls. Theatrical performances, dances, and other public spectacles, which frequently cannot be reconciled with the holiness of the priestly life and cannot be visited without scandal to the people, priests must avoid. Appearing publicly, they shall preserve both in dress and in deportment such modesty and gravity as correspondeth unto their station; in particular, as to dress, priests shall wear a long garment, that is, a cassock (*reverenda*) with a collar (*oboychyk*), not only during sacred actions, but also outside of them, whenever they assemble for public functions, whether ecclesiastical or civil; yet appearing on other occasions publicly, they may indeed wear shorter dress, but not according to the worldly cut, but according to the regulation indicated unto the clergy by the Ordinariates of this our province, the color of which garment

=== LEAF p229 ===
*(Physical Page 229 / Book Page 225)*

shall be black or darkish, which ought to be observed also as to head coverings; and in this matter, even as on the one hand immoderate, superfluous elegance is to be avoided, so on the other hand is all neglect; but unto such dress the priestly collar (*oboychyk*) must belong, and not something else according to the custom of the laity. Moreover, priests shall not dare to wear a full beard or side-whiskers. Walking upon public roads, and especially in towns, they shall not permit themselves ostentatiously to perform such actions as they could not do without scandalizing others whether within domestic walls—in a house—or within the precinct of villages: for example, smoking, talking loudly, or making immoderate and unbecoming gestures.
4. At home they shall not perform such labors as are proper unto the lowest servants, and they shall not immerse themselves excessively in purely secular affairs, even though otherwise honorable, such as the medical or surgical art and the like; and they shall not summon the faithful, without very grave cause and permission of the Ordinariate, unto public secular courts; at public markets they shall so conduct themselves that they manage nothing of such sort before the people, nor conduct transactions in selling or purchasing in such a manner that they would appear to be merchants of temporal things; but in necessities of that kind let them employ some honest man who may transact those affairs in their stead, mindful of the words of the Apostle unto Timothy [^225]: "No man that warreth entangleth himself with the affairs of this life; that he may please him who hath chosen him to be a soldier."
5. And although it is just that those who serve the altar should also live from the altar, and the Synod likewise doth not oppose that priests should decently request that which is due unto them by legal title, it strictly forbiddeth, however, that they raise the dues of the stole-fee (*prava epytrakhylia*) above that which is fair and customary, or cover their cupidity and most shameful trafficking in holy things under some outwardly fair title.
6. Let priests consider that unto them, as St. Ambrose saith, is entrusted the office of winning not money,

=== LEAF p230 ===
*(Physical Page 230 / Book Page 226)*

but souls [^226], and let them keep before their eyes the saying of the holy Apostle, admonishing: "And having food and raiment let us be therewith content" [^227]. Moreover, priests shall beware of all usury whatsoever, and shall not squander their own goods, and much less those of the church, on luxury in clothing for themselves or their family, and shall not contract debts which they would not be in a condition to repay to the injury of creditors.
7. As to virtues, first of all in regard of themselves, chastity is indispensable for priests, whether they be married, or widowers, or celibates; wherefore the Synod admonisheth those same priests that they arm themselves with temperance, fasting, and other pious works, as with weapons against the temptations of carnal lusts, following the example of the holy Apostle, who saith: "I keep under my body, and bring it into subjection, lest that by any means, when I have preached to others, I myself should be a castaway" [^228]. Above all, let them avoid familiar cohabitation with strange women, which, as experience teacheth, leadeth exceedingly unto corruption of morals, as everyone may learn from the admonition given unto Palladius the Bishop by St. Isidore of Pelusium: "Avoid the assemblies of women, O good man, as far as possible... but if any necessity compel thee to meet with them, keep thine eyes cast down unto the ground... and when thou hast briefly spoken that which may strengthen and enlighten their souls, flee immediately, lest perchance longer converse weaken and crush thy powers... but however that matter may be, have no relations with women, and then shalt thou gain honor among them... and if thou shouldst say unto me that thou dost frequently converse with them, and yet hast never suffered any harm therefrom, then indeed perhaps I might suffer myself to be persuaded, yet I would that all should believe it: they say that stones are softened by water, and a rock is hollowed out by incessantly falling drops [^229]. Frequent repetition overcometh nature. If therefore nature, which doth not willingly suffer itself to be removed, is removed and aban-

=== LEAF p231 ===
*(Physical Page 231 / Book Page 227)*

doneth that which it ought least of all, by what manner of miracle then and means should the will, which is so easily moved, not be overcome and changed by habit?" [^230].
8. Unto celibate priests and widowers in particular the Synod enjoineth that they endeavor to have for the management of the domestic household such a woman as shall either belong unto their near kin, or, if they cannot have such a one, a stranger indeed, but advanced in age and distinguished by honesty of morals. Likewise female servants, assistants of the same in labors, they shall accept who are equally pious and well-mannered, and they shall unceasingly watch over the preservation of their morality and piety, and those who are suspect and ill-conducted they shall immediately remove from their house.
9. In relations with others, a priest ought above all to preserve gentleness, especially toward his domestic household and parishioners; for frequent occasions present themselves for the exercise of that virtue, whether in bearing human faults, or in correcting their errors and subduing passions, not infrequently sharp and stubborn. A priest therefore ought to hold his heart as it were in his hands, that he may be able without anger, in a spirit of meekness, to deal with his neighbor. For gentleness not only maketh us conformed unto God above other virtues [^231], but moreover contributeth most of all unto the reconciliation of human hearts. From this, however, it doth not follow that punishment ought never to be employed, since Christ the Lord Himself also at times spoke more severely unto His disciples. But that fervor ought not to proceed from anger or from passion, but only from that love which enlighteneth and warmeth all, as St. Thomas saith: "To be angry according unto right reason is praiseworthy" [^232], and St. Gregory the Great: "Let there be love, but not enervating; let there be mercy, but not indulging more than is proper" [^233].
10. In regard of ecclesiastical superiors, the first duty of pastors as well as of other priests, which they have already promised at their ordination and assumption of their office, is true and sincere reverence and obedi-

=== LEAF p232 ===
*(Physical Page 232 / Book Page 228)*

ence unto them. And first of all they shall show the greatest reverence and obedience unto the Roman Pontiff, the Vicar of Christ upon earth and Head of the universal Church, praying for him, defending and vindicating His dignity, and receiving and observing the ordinances of the Apostolic See in humility. With that very same proportionate sentiment ought they to be animated toward their own Bishop, not forgetting the words of St. Cyprian: "A diocese is: a people united around its Priest, and a flock attached unto its Pastor; wherefore thou oughtest to know that the Bishop is in the Church, and the Church in the Bishop, and if anyone be not with the Bishop, he is not in the Church." For the Bishop, as the Apostle saith, is placed by the Holy Spirit to govern the Church of God. Wherefore according to the admonition of St. Jerome: "Fearing the Superior as the Lord, and loving him as a father." All priests shall avoid every manifestation of obstinacy, and in the first place they shall endeavor: 1) that all commands of the Bishop or his vicars be faithfully executed; 2) that for conscience' sake, both in private life and in the administration of their duties, they sincerely and completely observe diocesan regulations (laws), and especially statutes; 3) that they refrain from all judging of the acts and enactments of superiors; 4) that in all doubts or matters wherein they feel themselves aggrieved, they have recourse unto their Bishop with all filial confidence and submissiveness; 5) that they never urge, either by themselves or through others, that contrary to the sentence and decision of superiors they obtain any place or parish, or that of their own will, by unlawful steps, secret compacts, troubling the magnates, canvassed petitions, or other means of that sort incompatible with the holy canons, they procure success for themselves. We do by no means forbid, however, that difficulties and objections, if any exist according to circumstances, be set forth unto superiors according to the dictate of conscience; but where the reasons set forth have not been judged sufficient for departing from the decision, then assuredly the place of Holy Scripture shall be: "An obedient man shall speak being kept [unto victory]" [^234].

=== LEAF p233 ===
*(Physical Page 233 / Book Page 229)*

11. But whereas all authority is from God, priests shall show obedience and honor also unto the supreme Ruler, rendering unto Caesar that which is Caesar's, and by prayers, especially public ones, and namely on his name day and birthday, together with the whole people commend His person unto God. Regional authorities, judges, magistrates, and leading inhabitants they shall endeavor to win over for themselves, that their own authority may be elevated by the authority of the same, at least lest by occasion of offense they create for themselves innumerable difficulties in parochial administrations. To enter into so great a familiarity, however, that it would appear as though one wished to please the prominent alone and seek after their favor, rejecting the lower and poor among one's parishioners, offendeth pastoral dignity and love, and can in no wise be free from danger.—If however perchance a priest cannot gain the goodwill of the hearts of the said superiors, he shall nevertheless so conduct himself toward them that it come not to open discord, and that he give them no occasion for just complaint, refraining also both privately and publicly from every offensive word. If ever conscience should compel him to answer them with the Apostles: "We cannot," let that be done without any arrogant pride, but with wisdom and moderation [^235].
12. Moreover, priests shall maintain mutual love and concord especially among themselves, mindful that the mark of the disciples of Christ is mutual love itself, as the Lord Himself on the Mystical Supper testifieth with the words: "By this shall all men know that ye are My disciples, if ye have love one to another," which also He commanded three times in that same discourse, as if by a last testament, leaving unto them that His own and new commandment [^236]. They shall avoid all disputes, rivalries, and discords among themselves. In this matter they ought to pay no regard unto difference of rite, or diversity of nationality; for there is no difference between the Jew and the Greek; for the same Lord over all is rich unto all that call upon

=== LEAF p234 ===
*(Physical Page 234 / Book Page 230)*

Him. Wherefore, joined by a mutual and fraternal bond, Catholic priests of all rites shall have this one thing especially before their eyes: that, regardless of personal conveniences and private advantages, they become all things unto all men, that they may gain all for the Kingdom of God. Unto the awakening, fostering, and preserving of that peace and concord among priests, the concord and friendship of the Bishops of the diverse rites shall contribute most of all. Concerning this it behoveth to make mention and declare unto all from time to time that the enemy man, that is, the devil, soweth tares, exciteth and fostereth discords, that through divided minds the power and success of the priestly ministry may be diminished; for where peace and love are, there the grace of the Holy Spirit aboundeth.—No less shall priests show love toward the faithful committed unto their care, who one day, at the coming of the Lord, shall be their crown of glory; for the commandment of love is infused by the Bishop into priests being ordained, as he clotheth them in the phelonion (*felon*), saying: "Receive the priestly vestment, whereby love is understood; for God is able to increase love in thee and the perfect work."
13. Love however is twofold: namely, of God and of neighbor. Love of God showeth itself especially in the love of souls; for unto no priest will Christ say: "Feed My sheep," save unto him who shall be able to affirm with Peter: "Thou knowest, Lord, that I love Thee." That love every priest ought to prove not in word and tongue only, but in deed and in truth. The zeal therefore of the house of God ought to consume the priest, and by reason of his office let him be wholly dedicated unto procuring the salvation of souls, not fainting under any burden, unterrified by any human considerations, prudent indeed and moderately wise, yet strong as death, and ready to give himself up for the salvation of souls. Whosoever therefore turneth a sinner from the error of his iniquity, encourageth the wavering and fainthearted, instructeth and teacheth the ignorant and doubting how they ought to walk in the way of the Lord, and directeth other wanderers onto the right way, he shall render an acceptable service unto God and offer a sacrifice pleasing unto Him, and by that means through his solicitude and diligence shall attain that for which Christ offered Himself unto God the Father as a whole burnt offering.

=== LEAF p235 ===
*(Physical Page 235 / Book Page 231)*

14. Let us not ignore the greatness of the labor and the heaviness of the burden which very frequently are laid upon priests, especially pastors, for the purpose of removing abuses and restoring good morals among the people. For before all else it is necessary to contend against those who resist and defend unlawful customs long in use. For no one wisheth to think ill of himself; not many recognize their own errors; what is more, they more frequently defend faults, and if anything better is proposed, they turn away from it, enticed by ancient custom. It is very difficult in such persons to destroy the body of sin, that they should no longer serve sin; nevertheless it is not impossible, for God is able of these stones to raise up children unto Abraham. And the vinedresser planteth new vines, and pruneth the old, diggeth about them, and addeth fertilizer, that he may thereby bring it to pass that they yield more fruit. So also the Lord willeth that we all apply diligence, and that nothing remain uncultivated in His vineyard.
15. But let that difficulty terrify no one; for He Himself shall assist Who saith: "I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succored thee" [^237]; He will not suffer those labors to be in vain, for "He that ministereth," as the Apostle saith, "seed to the sower both minister bread for your food, and multiply your seed sown, and increase the fruits of your righteousness," and finally shall give unto us the reward of the labors of His saints [^238].
16. Furthermore, as to duties toward God, it behoveth above all that the priest of God, if it can be, daily offer the sacrifice of the Divine Liturgy, having preceded the same with suitable preparation through canonical prayers and devout meditation, which shall last at least half an hour; for meditation, according to the words of St. Bernard: "Ruleth the affections, directeth actions, correcteth faults" [^239]; and St. Cyril of Alexandria, upon the words of the Psalm: "And in my meditation a fire shall flame out," saith: "Meditation is the flint whereby the fire of the Holy Spirit is struck within us"; and having added after the Divine Liturgy devout and fervent thanksgiving. Likewise through examination

=== LEAF p236 ===
*(Physical Page 236 / Book Page 232)*

of conscience they shall secure themselves daily, at least once in the day, when they are to go to sleep, which also the blessed John Chrysostom recommendeth and explaineth the profit thereof in his commentary upon the words of Psalm IV, v. 5: "The things ye say in your hearts, be sorry for them upon your beds." "After supper," he saith, "when ye go to sleep, when ye are to lie down, and when no one is present, there is great peace, and when no one hindereth, there is deep silence: awaken the judgment of thy conscience, demand of it an examination, and if through the day thou hast conceived evil designs, or prepared snares, or deceived thy neighbor, or stirred up evil desires, when in the time of that silence thou shalt bring this before thyself and appoint conscience as judge over those dishonorable thoughts, pierce them and tear them and punish them, torment the thought that hath sinned."
17. Likewise daily shall he engage in spiritual reading, especially from Holy Scripture and the lives of the Saints, adhering in that matter unto the admonition of the said ecumenical Teacher St. John Chrysostom, affirming that such reading is indispensable for salvation, and that not only for men dedicated unto God, but also for those married and living in the world: "As unto a quiet haven—he saith—do we bring our soul by the reading of the Holy Scriptures. For a haven it is, untroubled by any waves, an unconquerable wall, an immovable tower, a glory that cannot be wrested away, an impenetrable armor, unconquerable peace of the soul, unceasing delight, every good that thou canst mention: such is the collection of the Divine Scripture. It both driveth away sadness and guardeth peace of mind; it maketh the poor richer, and procureth security for the well-to-do and rich; it maketh the sinner righteous, and protecteth the righteous with a safe covering; the evil that existeth it uprooteth, and the good that is lacking it planteth; it putteth wickedness to flight, and not only leadeth unto virtue, but also strengtheneth it with roots and causeth it to abide unceasingly; for it is a spiritual medicine and a kind of divine and unspeakable enchantment that destroyeth passions" [^240]. Likewise Homily III on Matthew and Homily III on Lazarus, where among other things he saith: "Concerning this do I ever admonish and shall not cease to admonish, that ye not

=== LEAF p237 ===
*(Physical Page 237 / Book Page 233)*

only attend here unto that which is spoken, but also, when ye shall be at home, unceasingly apply yourselves unto the reading of the Holy Scripture."
18. The visit of the Holy Mysteries the priest shall not omit on any day; his conscience he shall cleanse by frequent confession; and finally, at least for eight days in some retired place, every year, he shall give himself unto spiritual exercises [retreat]. In order that priests may be able to perform such exercises in a better manner, so far as may be, the Bishops shall endeavor that for participation in them there be found in the dioceses designated spiritual houses or monasteries where neighboring priests might assemble for retreats, and for that purpose they shall endeavor to appoint spiritual directors chosen by themselves. Until all this shall be arranged, the priests themselves, reflecting upon the brevity and fleeting nature of this life, and in spirit traversing the boundless expanse of eternity and the duration of eternal rewards, and hoping for the eternal salvation of themselves and their flock, shall of their own accord retire into some solitary place, if they have not at home a separate room, for the performance every year of spiritual exercises; choosing for that purpose such days in the year as shall be freer for them from the duties of their station and occupation.

### Chapter III: On the Learning of a Priest

CHAPTER III.
On the Learning of a Priest.

1. A priest, for the fulfillment of his office, ought to be provided as it were with two wings: with holiness of life and with sacred learning. Unto that explanation the sacred vestments of Aaron clearly lead, whereof some, as we have mentioned above, prefigure holiness of life, and some instruction in knowledge; and moreover the authority granted unto Aaron to teach Israel the commandments of the Lord and His laws. Wherefore also through Malachi the Lord, calling the priest an Angel, that is, a messenger of God, addeth: "For the priest's lips should keep knowledge, and they should seek the law at his mouth:

=== LEAF p238 ===
*(Physical Page 238 / Book Page 234)*

for he is the messenger of the Lord of hosts" [^241]. And in the New Testament the chosen vessel Paul admonisheth Timothy with the words: "Take heed unto thyself, and unto the doctrine; continue in them: for in doing this thou shalt both save thyself, and them that hear thee" [^242].
2. But whereas among men there are found also worldly persons who combat true religion among the faithful with false reasoning, for this reason that same Apostle requireth of the priest such learning, "that he may be able by sound doctrine both to exhort and to convince the gainsayers" [^243].
3. Wherefore the Lord rejecteth from the priesthood those who neglect to acquire that learning, with the words: "Because thou hast rejected knowledge, I will also reject thee, that thou shalt be no priest to Me" [^244].
4. Whereas however among books from which safe doctrine is drawn Holy Scripture occupieth the first place as divinely inspired, and as the holy Fathers Augustine and Gregory the Great say, is as it were a letter sent from God unto men, and which St. Ambrose calleth the priestly book [^245], and Origen: manna containing in itself all savor [^246], wherefore it behoveth the priest of God principally to apply himself unto the study of Holy Scripture, "that that heavenly treasure of sacred books, which the Holy Spirit with the greatest bounty hath given unto men, may not lie neglected" [^247]. In particular, let the priest be well-versed in the Four Gospels (Tetraevangelion), since especially from thence ought he to take the subject for holy sermons for the people.
5. But whereas Holy Scripture was translated from the Greek text into the Old Slavonic or sacred language, wherefore in that tongue it behoveth the priest to read the same, and unto that end he ought to acquire for himself adequate knowledge of the grammar of the church language, unto the study of which they ought already in the seminary to apply themselves. Unto that end they ought to procure for themselves also certain commentaries, even though shorter ones, such as: Massl, Allioli, Brentano, Serwatowski, Pelzel, and others. With the reading of Holy Scripture let them frequently interweave the reading of the lives of the Saints, which will supply very savory spiritual food both unto themselves and from their lips also unto the people committed unto their pastoral guidance.

=== LEAF p239 ===
*(Physical Page 239 / Book Page 235)*

The second place shall be occupied by Dogmatic and Moral Theology, the study of which is above all necessary for pastors, and of which latter the surest source are the works of St. Alphonsus Liguori, and as it were compendiums of the same, logically arranged, are the Morals of Gury and Müller. The third place shall be occupied by the Catechism published by authority of the Council of Trent, which, besides dogmatic doctrine, containeth the fullest exposition of the whole Christian Catechism.—The fourth place shall be occupied by the study of Canon Law, collected in compendium, since our clergy is almost entirely dedicated unto the care of souls, so that without such learning, especially in matrimonial matters, a pastor could not duly execute his office.
6. But not the last place ought the science of Liturgics to occupy, combined with unceasing practice; and whereas it is closely bound up with dogmas, the priest ought diligently to investigate both the letter and the spirit of the rites, that from those treasures he may be able to set before the people in his instructions spiritual food. In that matter he ought not only to know the rites and ceremonies as they are set forth in the liturgical books, but also as they are used according to ancient lawful usage; and that uncertainty concerning them may be removed, priests shall observe and learn accurately above all that which this Synod, having already compiled and approved it, presenteth unto the whole clergy of this province for observance [^248].
7. In general, other branches of knowledge also, whether sacred or secular, which can be auxiliary unto supplementing priestly learning, ought not to be neglected.
8. The Synod faileth not, however, to admonish priests here that, neglecting grave and pious reading, they squander not their time on the reading of harmful and empty novelties, and likewise that they read not periodicals which combat the authority of the Church, vex ecclesiastical superiors and their ordinances and statutes, and spit upon them with the spittle of an unclean heart.

=== LEAF p240 ===
*(Physical Page 240 / Book Page 236)*

9. Furthermore, for the progress of sacred learning and the perfection of the spirit, decanal conferences [synodules] as well as libraries can contribute very much. Wherefore, as to decanal conferences, the Synod approveth and for the future deemeth proper to retain and ever more to perfect the custom of holding such conferences at appointed times in individual deaneries, according to the rule submitted and approved by this Synod. As to libraries, the Synod commandeth that individual pastors endeavor to have at their church a small library, the books or works whereof must be entered in the church inventory. In this matter care must be taken that no one remove any book, for the purpose of taking it home to himself, without having deposited a receipt (*revers*), which is to be preserved in some secure place of the parish library itself. Besides such parish libraries, the Synod reneweth the enactments or circular regulations of the Ordinariates according to which in each deanery there shall be established in some more suitable place a decanal library for the convenience of the entire decanal clergy, and that under some competent curator or custodian, who should have care of the books distinctly and accurately described in a catalogue, and designate for those coming to the library a place for reading; and in case it should be necessary to lend any book from the same for home use, he shall permit this for an appointed time only, and that under security upon a receipt. For the augmentation of the library, endeavor shall be made to collect among fellow decanal priests, as members of the same, contributions wherewith new books may be purchased [^249].

## Titulus X. On Monks (*О Монахахъ*)

**1.** It is beyond dispute that the Monastic Orders are to be held in exceedingly high esteem, especially when it is known that the Church hath ever exalted them. Yet among the Monastic Orders, those are customarily to be regarded as most distinguished which unite the active life with the spiritual; since, gathering unto themselves an abundance of heavenly treasures, they pour them forth upon others.

Among such Orders, a most distinguished place is held by the Order of St. Basil the Great, so named after St. Basil the Great, in which the one and the other life are joined in an admirable union; from which have come forth the most glorious luminaries of learning and holiness unto the greatest profit of the Church and civil

=== LEAF p242 ===
*(Physical Page 242 / Book Page 238)*

society. But whereas in these latter times this Order had departed from its pristine luster, the untiringly solicitous Pope Leo XIII, by an issued Constitution beginning *“Singulare praesidium”* of May 12, 1882, recalled it unto pristine discipline, having employed thereto members of the Society of Jesus, whom St. Josaphat himself and Metropolitan Veliamyn Rutsky already in their own times experienced as the best helpers. That reform, happily begun, as we know, hath already brought forth rich fruits and giveth certain hope that this renowned Order shall quickly revive.

Wherefore this Synod admonisheth the faithful that, if anyone feeleth himself called by the Lord unto this Order, they act with trust and readily obey the heavenly calling, knowing how many and how great evils in these times permeate civil society, so that in this manner also, through their salvific influence, society may be protected from corruption. And although this Order, by the aforesaid Constitution, is exempt from under the jurisdiction of the Ordinaries, yet for the sake of the very great benefits which can flow unto the Church from the mutual drawing together of the one and the other clergy, the Synod commendeth unto the Ordinaries that on their part they bestow upon this Order their benevolence and patronage, and unto the Superiors of Monasteries—that they show themselves willing to render spiritual assistance unto Bishops, granting permission unto their subjects to preach the word of God, and above all to hear the confessions of the junior clergy.

**2.** Finally, setting before the eyes of all the faithful of our rite the value of this reform, the Synod admonisheth them in the Lord that, relying upon the singular foresight of the Supreme Pontiff Leo XIII, who happily holdeth the helm of the Universal Church, concerning our Church, they show themselves not only not opposed, but also favorably inclined toward the renewal of this glorious Order; and if they should ever hear that against this regulation of the Supreme Pastor certain faint-hearted or evilly-disposed persons dare to raise any reproaches, let them pay no heed unto them, but place their trust in the solicitude of their Bishops for the good of the Church and of our rite, and that the more so, as regarding their unceasing vigilance for the good of our Church,

=== LEAF p243 ===
*(Physical Page 243 / Book Page 239)*

rite, and faithful Ruthenian people, no reasonable doubt can be raised.

**3.** As to Nuns (*О Инокиняхъ*), their Monasteries, both according unto common law and our custom, are subject directly unto the jurisdiction of the Ordinaries. And that monastic discipline may ever more flourish in them, the Synod submitteth provisional rules, drafted for their testing and the approval of the Fathers constituting this Synod, which subsequently, after the conclusion of the Synod, the Most Reverend Metropolitan shall take care to print and put into practice. Moreover, for the easier and more successful preparation of candidates for the monastic life, this Synod decreeth that a novitiate be established for them in the Slovita Monastery, and that for members of all dioceses, until in time the Bishops shall enact what is more suitable. But whereas the Slovita Monastery is endowed with too modest revenues, which can scarcely suffice for the maintenance of its own members, therefore by common agreement the Ordinaries shall endeavor to equalize the expenses of the monasteries of all dioceses so that the compensation may be equitable.

**4.** The Synod also willeth that nuns engage in the education of girls, and so far as possible prepare their members for this work, with the exclusion of strangers, applying themselves only unto those subjects which correspond unto the calling of nuns. For the rest, as regards the preservation of monastic discipline, the Synod maintaineth what the Synod of Zamość wisely decreed, Titulus XII *On Nuns*, and hath decreed to modify this only: that by reason of changed circumstances of time, there shall not be required from candidates a dowry or tax of at least one thousand five hundred or three thousand gulden, but only whatever according unto the condition of the person and the judgment of the Ordinary [shall be determined].

**5.** As to the election of the Hegumena, until their number reacheth ten professed or choir nuns, who alone have the right of voting in the election of the Hegumena, the Synod decreeth that in the present state the common law be observed, and the Ordinary himself undertake the election of the Hegumena.

Furthermore, although endeavor must be made by all means that in such Monasteries of nuns monastic discipline may flourish above all things, nevertheless, to supplement the

=== LEAF p244 ===
*(Physical Page 244 / Book Page 240)*

deficiency of means, the Ordinaries shall endeavor for the present to support this rule by all means at hand, until in time, by the blessing of God, from Whom proceedeth the beginning, middle, and end of good, the full completion of the work may be attained.

## Titulus XI. On Fasts (*О постахъ*)

**1.** That the fast is to be held in the highest esteem, we know not only from the prescription of God Himself frequently commanded unto His people, and the example of Moses [^251] and of the people of Israel [^252], but also from this: that by the example of our Lord Jesus Christ through forty days in the wilderness, it was sanctified, and thereafter from Apostolic times down to our own hath been preserved.

**2.** Yet besides the greatest and most holy fast of the Great Forty Days [Holy Lent], which beginneth from the Monday after Cheesefare Sunday and concludeth with the Friday before Palm Sunday (in such manner, however, that according unto the present custom of the people, Saturdays and Sundays also are observed as fast days), there are also prescribed in our liturgical books other fasts, and that the following:

1. The six days between Palm Sunday and Pascha [Holy Week]; in such manner, however, that the preceding Palm Sunday and Lazarus Saturday also, according unto the custom of the people, are regarded as fast days.
2. The Forty Days Fast, that is, forty days before the Nativity of the Lord [St. Philip's Fast / Pylypivka].
3. The Fast before the feast of the Supreme Apostles Peter and Paul [Apostles Fast / Petrivka], which beginneth with Monday after the Sunday of All Saints, or the first after Pentecost, and concludeth with the eve of the feast itself.
