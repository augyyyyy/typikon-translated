# SYNODAL DECREES PART I: TITULI I–IV (On the Catholic Faith, Hierarchy, Bishops, and Diocesan Administration)

### CHAPTER I. On the Profession of the Catholic Faith

I. We decree that the "Profession of Faith" according to the specimen annexed hereto [^23] for Easterners, prescribed by Pope Urban VIII, shall be made by:  
a) The members of the Provincial Synod and of the Diocesan Synod at the opening thereof;  
b) Those elevated to the Episcopate, prior to their consecration;  
c) Cathedral Dignitaries and Canons, prior to their installation;  
d) Generals and Provincials of Monastic Orders and Archimandrites, as also Monks of both sexes, when they are admitted to the Order;  
e) Professors of Theology and Philosophy who are to instruct candidates for the clerical state;  
f) Adjuncts (Assistant Lecturers) of Theology;  
g) Priests and Catechists;  
h) Deans;

i) Those endowed with any benefices connected with the cure of souls;  
k) Newly appointed Confessors of Nuns (Monastic Sisters);  
l) Those about to receive Holy Orders, prior to the reception of ordination.

### CHAPTER II. On Catholic Doctrine

1. Whereas "without faith it is impossible to please God" [^24], and whereas faith is the foundation and root of justification, this Synod therefore begins from it.  
All things, therefore, which the Catholic Church, whether by solemn judgment or by ordinary and universal magisterium, proposes to be believed as divinely revealed, we believe, by solemn profession we confess, and we teach: namely, that which at the Ecumenical Vatican Council was pronounced in the dogmatic decrees on Catholic faith [^25], on the Church, and on the Roman Pontiff, and on His full, supreme, ordinary, and immediate jurisdiction and government over all and singular pastors and faithful, and on His infallible authority in teaching when He speaks *ex cathedra* [^26].  
2. We declare that there must be held with the whole soul and a sincere spirit the dogmatic doctrine of Pius IX of holy memory, pronounced on the Immaculate Conception of the Mother of God in the Bull *Ineffabilis Deus* ("The Ineffable God") on the 8th day of December, 1854, solemnly promulgated in the following words: "We declare, pronounce, and define that the doctrine which holds that the most Blessed Virgin Mary, in the first instance of her conception, by a singular grace and privilege granted by Almighty God, in view of the merits of Jesus Christ, the Savior of the human race, was preserved free from all stain of original sin, is a doctrine revealed by God and therefore to be believed firmly and constantly by all the faithful" [^27].

3. Likewise, all and singular false opinions and doctrines that are gaining ground in our age, which the same Ecumenical Pontiff Pius IX, in his Consistorial Allocutions and in the Encyclical Letter *Quanta cura* ("How Great Solicitude") [^28] and in the annexed Syllabus of the 8th of December, 1864 [^29], by apostolic authority rejected and condemned, we in this Synod recognize and confess to be entirely rejected, and we pronounce that they must be held as rejected and condemned under penalty of the loss of salvation.  
4. We accept the Catholic doctrine concerning the duties and rights of princes and subjects, concerning the domestic family, which is the foundation of every civil society and kingdom, and concerning the right of property and possession, set forth against the errors of socialists, communists, and nihilists in the Encyclical of our Most Holy Father Pope Leo XIII of the 28th of January, 1878 [^30]; we accept and profess that all are bound to receive it, and we likewise promulgate it in this Synod.  
5. We likewise accept and command to be accepted all that the Most Holy Father Pope Leo XIII, on the Christian constitution of states, pronounced and set forth in the Encyclical *Immortale Dei* ("The Immortal God") of the 1st of November, 1885, that the nature and boundary of the rights of the Church and civil society, and her obligations in regard to religion, might be made manifest, and that Catholic people might at the same time learn what, according to the needs of these times, they ought to do in public and private affairs, and what they ought to avoid [^31]. In like manner, that which the Most Holy Pope Leo XIII taught concerning the condition of workers in the Encyclical Letter *Rerum novarum* ("Of New Things") of the 15th of May, 1891, we accept and revere [^32].  
6. In fine, we declare that all that was decreed concerning the Catholic faith at the Synod of Zamość in the year 1720 must be preserved [^33].

### CHAPTER III. On Religious Indifferentism

1. It is a dogma of faith that no one outside the true Church of Christ can attain eternal salvation. But the true and sole Church of Christ is that to which belongs all that hath been so abundantly and wonderfully brought together by divine arrangement for the evident credibility of the Christian faith, since her wondrous propagation, eminent holiness, inexhaustible fruitfulness in all good things, Catholic unity, and invincible stability provide the greatest and perpetual guarantee of credibility and irrefutable proof of her divine mission. Therefore, that abominable error which is called religious indifferentism, and which places the faithful on an equal footing with those who have not yet attained to the one true faith, we condemn and reject.  
2. Whereas, however, despite the perniciousness of this system of religious indifferentism, an opposing opinion hath in our times grown strong among many, even Catholics, whether through ignorance in Christian doctrine or through coldness in faith: this Synod, striving to root out this kind of hazardous error from the minds of the faithful, earnestly beseeches them in God and admonishes them that they preserve with great care the doctrine of the Catholic Church here set forth, since it provides the only means of attaining the final end for which man was created; and it commands pastors in God that they themselves also set forth the same in their public instructions on fitting occasions, praise the grace of holy union whereby they are placed in Christ's true fold, and explain with a dignity worthy of their office the differences of dogmas that exist between the Church of Christ and that which dares to call herself "Orthodox," lest through their negligence it should perchance happen to them that on the Day of Judgment there will be required at their hands the blood of the sheep redeemed by the precious blood of the Son of God [^34].

### CHAPTER IV. On Communion in Sacred Things with Heretics and Schismatics

1. Although toward non-Catholics who out of manifest perversity and malicious obstinacy rise up against the Catholic Church and her doctrine, Christ's faithful ought to conduct themselves with all circumspection and caution, and it is even fitting to avoid all familiar intercourse with them, as the Holy Spirit admonishes us by the mouth of the holy Apostles, when He saith: *"If there come any unto you, and bring not this doctrine, receive him not into your house, neither bid him God speed: for he that bids him God speed is partaker of his evil deeds"* [^35]; and again: *"A man that is a heretic after the first and second admonition reject; knowing that he that is such is subverted, and sins, being condemned of himself"* [^36]; nevertheless, toward others who either live in good faith in their religion or plot nothing hostile against the Catholic Church, Christ's faithful are not forbidden to have friendly relations; moreover, let them strive to show them love, kindness, and goodwill in word and deed, that by this means winning them over, they may turn them away from errors and unite them with the Catholic Church.  
2. Let them take heed, however, that they never have with them such relations as are called "in sacred things" (in spiritual matters); and this the more so, because thereby non-Catholics might be confirmed in the errors of their false religion, as though by a tacit approval of the same on the part of the faithful; therefore such communion "in sacred things" (in spiritual matters) hath been forbidden in the Church of Christ at all times, both by the canons of councils and by the decrees of the Supreme Pontiffs and the Roman Sacred Congregations, as also by the teachings of the holy Fathers. For in the canon law of the Greeks many canons are cited concerning this subject, the so-called "Apostolic Canons," namely [^37]: *"If anyone shall pray, even in a house, with an excommunicated person, let him be excommunicated"*

[^38]; *"If anyone, being a clergyman, shall pray together with one who is deposed, let him also be deposed"* [^39]; *"If a bishop, or presbyter, or deacon, shall only pray with heretics, let him be excommunicated; but if he hath permitted them to perform any clerical ministration, let him be deposed"* [^40].  
3. Two ancient provincial councils of the East are likewise cited there, namely: of Antioch and of Laodicea, of which the former decreed thus: *"It is not lawful to communicate with those who are excommunicated, nor to assemble in houses to pray with those who do not communicate in the Church, nor can those be received in one Church who have no part in another Church"* [^41]; and the latter thus: *"Heretics must not be permitted to enter into the house of God while they continue in heresy. It is not fitting to permit those who belong to the Church to visit the cemeteries or places called martyria (shrines of martyrs) of any heretics for the sake of prayer or veneration. But such, if they be of the faithful, shall be deprived of communion for an appointed time; yet if they repent and confess that they have sinned, it is fitting to receive them. Likewise it is not fitting to pray with heretics or schismatics"* [^42].  
As for the decrees of the Supreme Pontiffs and the Sacred Congregations, they, explicitly and expressly treating of this matter, forbid communion in sacred things with non-Catholics, as is evident from the instructions of the Congregation of the Holy Office of June 8, 1859, "On communion with heretics" (*Communicatio cum haereticis*); likewise of the year 1871 to the Archbishop of Corfu, "On the conferral of the Sacrament of Baptism" (*In collatione Sacram. baptismi*); likewise of the year 1729, "Whereas often" (*Cum saepe*); likewise of Thursday, May 10, 1753, "To the first" (*Ad primum*); and of Friday, June 1, 1866, to the Latin-rite Archbishop of Lviv, "It may be tolerated" (*Tolerari posse*); and finally of March 30, 1885, which are appended hereto in the appendix of this Synod [^43].

# Acts and Decrees of the Ruthenian Provincial Synod of Lviv (1891)

## 1. Decrees of the Ruthenian Provincial Synod: Titulus I. On the Catholic Faith (Continued)

### Chapter IV: On Communion in Sacred Things with Heretics and Schismatics (Conclusion)

Further, that doctrine of the Catholic Church concerning communion in sacred things with non-Catholics was also clearly set forth by our Synod of Zamość, which decided thus: *"Moreover, it is not permitted to anyone, of whatever state or station he may be, who professes the Catholic faith, to go unto outside pastors and their churches, whether for the reception of the Mysteries or out of devotion, lest it appear that they have actual communion with those who are without; in the contrary case, Ordinaries shall proceed against them as against suspects."* [^44]

3. Whereas, however, the present Synod hath learned not without great sorrow of its heart that certain laymen at times depart from that doctrine, so that in the church of schismatics during sacred functions together with them, without scruple of conscience, they are observed to be present, and at times also do not hesitate to give stipends for sacred services for the help of the soul of some Ruthenian poet or some political man of greater renown, a schismatic, unto schismatic priests, and seem to regard this as permitted, or at least as indifferent; therefore it strictly admonishes all priests, catechists of public schools, as also other pastors of souls, especially where occasion presents itself, that they duly instruct the faithful committed to their care concerning this matter, and fail not to deter them from that custom, which is contrary to the spirit of Christ's Church, since such communion with those of other faiths in sacred things would prepare undoubted harm for their souls.

### CHAPTER V. On the Censorship of Books

1. By the very law of nature the reading of those books whose use brings the danger of sin is forbidden. Therefore from the very beginning of the Church, Bishops, and above all the Roman Pontiff, have always the duty, and to them

pertains the right to prohibit the reading of bad books and periodicals; for they are appointed pastors of the flock of God [^45]; and the duty of a pastor is to go before his sheep [^46] and lead them unto good pastures, and to restrain them from bad and harmful pastures, whether they be delivered by word or in writing. Therefore already the Apostles strove by their authority to tear bad books from the hands of the faithful, just as, according to the testimony of Saint Luke [^47], many Ephesians who followed curious arts brought together their books and burned them before the eyes of all. Following the example of the Apostles, their successors, the Catholic Bishops, in every age condemned evil writings and strove to uproot them.

2. Greater vigilance in this matter Bishops exercised from the time when, through the invention of printing, books began to be disseminated with great rapidity. — And that the condemnations of books might become more easily known to the faithful, and that the reading of forbidden books might be more safely prevented, through the care and solicitude of the Roman Pontiffs there was compiled an Index of Forbidden Books, and church censures or penalties were prescribed and established against transgressors of the prohibitions of censorship. In addition, Saint Pius V established a special "Congregation of the Index" (*Congregatio Indicis*), which hath the duty to examine books infected with the poison of heresy, permeated with doctrine harmful to religion and good morals, and to prohibit the dissemination, keeping, and reading of such books, subjecting those who act to the contrary to excommunication [^48]. That Congregation therefore from time to time inscribes works harmful to faith and morals into the Index of Forbidden Books and deters from the reading of the same. In more recent times, however, the Supreme Pontiff Pope Pius IX of blessed memory abolished, or rather systematized, the earlier prescriptions regarding censures, imposed by the Constitution *Apostolicae Sedis* ("Of the Apostolic See") of October 12, 1869, in Section 2, against those who would not cease to read forbidden books, and decreed that all and singular, who knowingly without permission of the Apostolic See read the books of apostates and heretics defending heresy, as also the books of whatever author

specifically forbidden by Apostolic Letters, and who retain, print, and in any way defend those same books, shall incur excommunication of judgment passed (*latae sententiae*), in a special manner reserved to the Roman Pontiff.

3. On that foundation the present Synod admonishes pastors constantly and unceasingly to prevent the dangers to faith and morals arising from the reading of bad books, brochures, and periodicals. Therefore let them strive to prohibit the doctrines of those of other faiths, and to see that books containing heresy or schism, or offending against the purity of morals, or superstitious, or in any way contrary to public order, be not sown among the faithful. There are indeed those who deem it an unjust thing to prohibit people from reading any books under the pretext of desiring to know everything; but these are drawn and deceived by the whisperings of the ancient serpent, who under pretext of bringing our first parents to the knowledge of everything, urged them to pluck the forbidden fruit with the words: *"Ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil"* [^49]. Others deem it unbearable not to have books that satisfy curiosity and bring something new to an insatiable spirit, as if Christians ought to suffer nothing burdensome, and as if the words of the Savior and Lord had no meaning: *"If thine eye offend thee, pluck it out, and cast it from thee"* [^50]. There are also not a few who support themselves by scribbling, and for the sake of obtaining gain labor in the writing, printing, and selling of subversive books; but these, having faith and good morals for sale, and enriching their estate from the death of souls, forget the words of the Savior in Saint Matthew: *"What is a man profited, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul? or what shall a man give in exchange for his soul?"* [^51]. Therefore the present Synod, renewing the decisions and enactments concerning forbidden books issued by the Synod of Zamość, as found at the end of Title I, "On the Catholic Faith," commands all that for church use they acquire and admit only such editions of liturgical books as

are printed with the permission of the legitimate Ordinary (Bishop). To memory must be recalled here the words addressed by Pope Gregory XVI of blessed memory to Metropolitan Mykhailo Levytsky on the 17th of July, 1841: *"With a vigilant spirit continually take heed that this clergy and people (Ruthenian) receive not Sluzhebniks or Catechisms, or any other liturgical and religious books whatever, that have issued from schismatic presses."* The same applies also to printed prayer books for the use of the laity, namely that they use no other books of devotion except those that have been printed with the permission of ecclesiastical authority. In addition, the same Synod commands that in the future theological books and any others treating directly of religion shall not, without the prior approval of the Bishop, be published or read by persons of whatever estate. The parish, decanal, and eparchial library shall be provided only with such theological, homiletic, and catechetical works wherein the approval of the Superiors of the Catholic Church is explicitly found.

4. In fine, this our Synod admonishes priests that they lead the laity by their own example, and that they read not bad periodicals, hostile to the faith and the Catholic Church, and especially those prohibited by ecclesiastical authority, and that they strive with all their might to turn away the faithful committed to their pastoral care from reading them. — If, however, any priest for a just cause should need to read forbidden books, let him not do so without the knowledge and permission of his Ordinary, who will be able to grant priests such permission according to the faculties received from the Holy See.

### CHAPTER VI. On the Sect of Freemasons and Other Condemned or Forbidden Societies

1. Already from the beginning of the Church, by the permission of Christ the Lord, who explicitly foretold: *"It must needs be that

offences come"* [^52], subtle men arose who, mingling divine things with human, sowed tares in the field of the Church. Worst among others arose those who flatter human passions and seduce the uneducated and simple, striving with all their might under the guise of honesty and beneficence to overturn the firm and unshakable principles of the Catholic faith and divine revelation, as well as the foundations of civil society. And that these men might more easily attain their subversive purpose, they joined together in secret societies, which go under the name of Freemasons or under other names.

2. Against this kind of secret societies the Roman Pontiffs issued very many constitutions and apostolic decrees, and most recently our Most Holy Father Pope Leo XIII exposed their murderous teachings, contrary to Christ and His Church, and their secret aims for the destruction of both civil and ecclesiastical society, and at the same time seasonably instructed the faithful by what means they may guard themselves against their deception and snares.

3. Yet the Roman Pontiffs were not content merely to condemn and publicly excommunicate them, but willed also that those who in any degree adhere to them, or show them any favor, should incur excommunication reserved to the Pope [^53]. Therefore let all those who strive to avoid shipwreck concerning faith and eternal salvation shun this kind of sects, and those who have unhappily fallen into them, let them separate themselves from them without delay.

4. Sectarians of this kind, and likewise all the faithful cognizant of the matter, are strictly bound to denounce to the Church—that is, unto him who by reason of his pastoral office is bound to watch and guard lest the sheep committed to him fall among wolves—the members of an excommunicated society; and under censure reserved to the Roman Pope, the secret coryphaei and leaders of those sects, which censure upon denunciation ceases [^54].

5. Besides these, there are yet other societies that work against the Church or legitimate authorities, whether secretly or openly, under oath or without an oath concerning the keeping of secrecy, which are forbidden and which under threat of grave guilt must be avoided; among these must be numbered above all those societies that demand of their adherents under oath not to disclose secrets to anyone and to obey secret leaders. Likewise, there are found here and there societies that, although they seem to differ from the above-mentioned, are nevertheless questionable and full of danger both on account of the doctrines they profess and on account of the manner of conduct their leaders maintain. Against these also the Holy Synod warns pastors, fathers of families, and other righteous men, that they deter and restrain everyone, but especially the youth, and that the more carefully, since on account of a certain appearance of honesty and even beneficence maintained by them, the danger of corruption hidden within them cannot so easily be perceived and guarded against [^55].

6. Likewise this Synod decrees that pastors in teaching the catechism frequently explain what kinds of societies these are and whither they tend. And those who instruct the youth for the reception of the Holy Mysteries, let them lead them to promise beforehand that they will never enter any society without taking counsel of their parents, pastor, or confessor.

7. We likewise admonish pastors of souls that they warn the faithful committed to them with all diligence, both against practicing and against having recourse for counsel to that kind of sorceries known under the name of magnetism, spiritism, divination, and witchcraft, which have already and in such manner been forbidden by the Church.

8. Let the Most Reverend Bishops strive diligently that in individual parishes societies of artisans and agriculturalists, for piety and mutual

assistance, having chosen for themselves a heavenly Patron, be founded, and that from day to day they grow ever more in the number of their members.

### CHAPTER VII. On Preaching the Word of God

1. Since the Word of God is spiritual bread to the soul, indispensably necessary for man unto the attainment of eternal salvation, therefore it is manifest that this apostolic office of preaching the Word of God pertains to the chief duties of pastors of souls, because they further fulfill that same mission which the Son of God Himself incarnate received from His heavenly Father, as He Himself saith according to the prophet Isaiah: *"The Spirit of the Lord is upon Me, because He hath anointed Me to preach the gospel to the poor; He hath sent Me... to preach the acceptable year of the Lord, and the day of recompense"* [^56]; and which He enjoined upon the Apostles and their successors in the apostolic office after His glorious Resurrection, saying: *"Go ye into all the world, and preach the gospel to every creature"* [^57]. In obedience to that command, the Apostles not only themselves fearlessly fulfilled this office even unto death, but also strictly enjoined the same upon the pastors appointed by them, and among them most of all Paul, saying unto Timothy: *"Preach the word; be instant in season, out of season; reprove, rebuke, exhort with all longsuffering and doctrine"* [^58].

2. Therefore it hath at all times lain closest to the heart of the Church to lay this commandment upon pastors of souls, whether by laws or by admonitions; and most solemnly the Fathers of the Council of Trent thought fit to recall this duty to the memory of pastors of souls [^59].

3. In particular, regarding our Church, this command was emphatically enjoined at our first

Provincial Synod [^60], and thereafter in many pastoral letters of the Ruthenian Bishops [^61].

4. Therefore the present Synod commands pastors of souls that at least on every Sunday and on feast days, they themselves never omit to preach the Word of God suitably to their own understanding and that of their people, unless they be legitimately hindered. Those pastors to whom assistants are assigned, let them see that they do not cast upon them the entire burden of the preaching office, nor that they entirely exclude them from the fulfillment of that office, but that by turns, together with them, they apply themselves to the duty of preaching the Word of God. In the parishes of larger towns, where there are more priests, the pastor shall see to it that the faithful who are able to be present only at the earlier Divine Liturgy on Sundays and feast days, be nourished at that same Divine Liturgy with the Word of God in a short homiletic instruction.

5. Since the purpose of preaching is to enlighten the minds of the faithful and move their hearts to the pursuit of virtues and the avoidance of errors, let them choose as the subject of their sermons dogmatic and moral truths, often also liturgical ones for the connection of rites with dogmas and morals, as also historical ones, in so far as they relate to dogmas and morals; but above all let them direct their diligence toward drawing the subject of their preaching from the appointed Gospels of Sundays or feasts, whose mysteries let them clearly set forth and explain, and also from Christian doctrine contained in the catechism.

6. As to the manner of their sermons, above all let preachers take care that they go not up to the ambo without due preparation, and thus proclaim whatever first comes into their heads, without any order and arrangement; and in general let them guard lest they set before the faithful anything confused, not pertaining to the subject of the sermon suitable for the day; since

from such a sermon not only could the faithful derive no benefit, but the preacher himself would call down upon himself the wrath of God speaking through the Prophet: *"Cursed be he that does the work of the Lord negligently"* [^62]. Let preachers observe that commendable custom recognized among us as best, to cite at the head of the sermon a text containing, as it were in seed, the entire sermon, and that in the liturgical language; where, however, such a text happens to be difficult to understand, let them immediately subjoin a translation of it in the vernacular tongue; and let them do likewise wherever in the course of the sermon texts of Sacred Scripture are to be proclaimed; these, however, must be taken from the authentic Slavonic edition approved by the Catholic Church; then let the speech of the sermon always be vernacular, that is, accessible to the understanding of the people, yet not disfigured by vulgar expressions. Further, let them take heed not to repeat too frequently one and the same thing to the people. In addition: *"Let the priest preaching Christ be filled with the affairs of Christ, and think not even of his own; let him hold to the rule that it is never fitting to speak of himself, and let him not easily persuade himself that he hath a just cause for an exception. Concerning an injury which he hath suffered or thinks he hath suffered, let him not complain before the parishioners from the sacred elevation (the ambo). He will fulfill his duty if he discuss more thoroughly the sins that more frequently occur among his people, stand up against their abomination, recommend the rewards and beauty of the contrary virtue, but never let him describe the scandals of individual persons in such a manner that withholding the name will be of little avail. Let the pastor summon to himself the man through whom scandal came among the people, and let him confer with him privately, or let him use the assistance of the village elders. Public reproof of sinners, according to the circumstances of the time, is wont rather to embitter them than to incline them unto salvation, and great contentions and difficulties may even arise therefrom"* [^63]. Therefore let the preacher strictly beware lest in

his discourses to the people he speak either of himself, or of his private affairs, or of matters purely political and secular, which have no connection with the doctrine of faith and good morals; and equally let him not revile publicly and by name, nor censure any persons whatever, even private ones, and much less superiors and men distinguished by public authority.

7. And that unto the salvific fulfillment of that duty of preaching, preachers may be duly animated and inspired in spirit, let them fail not to invoke the Teacher of truth and the Comforter, who alone hath the keys and opens when He wills and is able, by His internal operation, to enter into the souls of the faithful and by His divine power penetrate the hearts of the hearers. Further, let them frequently awaken within themselves the pure and holy resolution that they seek the glory of God alone, and not their own, and no other reward for their labor than the salvation of souls.

8. Unto that end, constantly turning their eyes upon their own weakness, let them earnestly beware *"lest by any means, when they have preached to others, they themselves should be castaways"* [^64]. Finally, that they may be able the more surely to trust that, fulfilling their ministry duly, they will merit a portion with those who from the beginning have wielded the Word of God well: let them not cease to shepherd and teach the flock of the Lord not only by word, but also by their own example. *"For great is the condemnation of him who orders his discourse well, but neglects his own life and his own works"* [^65].

### CHAPTER VIII. On the Catechism

1. Since the Catechism aims especially at the instruction of youth in the doctrine of salvation, and since this age is most inclined both unto good and unto evil, and longest retains the first teachings received from childhood,

so that the direction given in that age is the foundation of the religious way of life in mature age, therefore the Synod deems it its duty to turn the greatest attention of pastors unto that most chief duty, that they apply themselves unto it with the utmost exactitude, as unto a very powerful means whereby the faithful committed to their solicitude are most successfully directed unto an honorable life. Let pastors of souls therefore know that they are bound in conscience, and that under grave accountability before God, to teach and expound the principles of Christian doctrine not only in church on every Sunday and feast day immediately after Vespers, and in daughter churches immediately after that divine service which they have celebrated therein, but also in schools according to the regulations issued by the Ordinary, and that above all on account of the present system adopted in modern schools.

2. In expounding the Catechism they shall follow the order of that textbook which is temporarily prescribed by the Ordinary as the rule to be used in elementary schools, so that in the course of a year they may complete the entire subject matter of the Catechism. To the Catechism let them also join the principal events of Biblical history that have a special connection with the dogmas of faith and morals. In the season of the Great Fast (Forty Days), let them choose for their instructions the doctrine concerning the Mysteries of Repentance and the Most Holy Eucharist, that they may worthily prepare the youth, especially those who are to receive those Mysteries for the first time, for the reception of them; and let them strive that they learn all that must be explicitly believed for the attainment of eternal salvation. Let them also turn their attention to this, that the youth learn the daily prayers correctly, so that they interpolate nothing unnecessary, and neither corrupt nor omit words.

3. In addition, this Synod reminds pastors of souls that it is their duty to admonish fathers and mothers to send their children and domestic servants at the appointed times to church for instruction in the Catechism, and to teach them that they are strictly bound to do so, since they ought not only to care for their temporal good, but much more for their spiritual and eternal good; the pastor himself ought to go before them by his own example, sending also his own domestics

to church for the Catechism. And if any parents or guardians show themselves negligent in sending their dependents, let him summon them to himself, that with paternal firmness he may remind them of this so sacred a duty [^66].

4. Finally, this Synod, desiring that pastors of souls undertake and perform catechesis with all diligence both in church and in school—for they must know that this is their first and greatest duty, and therefore any negligence or omission in this matter, however slight, yet culpable, inflicts great harm upon the parish—obliges them before all things to the greatest diligence and zeal in teaching the youth; and unto that end, besides what hath been set forth above, it also recalls to their memory the most luminous instructions and regulations that were enjoined concerning this office and duty by the pastoral letter of the Most Reverend Metropolitan Joseph Sembratovych to the clergy of the Archeparchy of Lviv on the 15th of December, 1876 [^67].

5. For having duly preserved and conscientiously fulfilled the above-mentioned regulations, they may hope not only for a hundredfold fruit from the seed of the Word of God sown by them, but they themselves will surely become partakers of that reward wherewith the Lord will crown the labors bestowed by them in explaining the Catechism; for the rest, already on this earth they will rejoice in that comfort and satisfaction which the Apostle of the Nations experienced when, hoping in the goodness of Christ the Lord, he said: *"I have fought a good fight... henceforth there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness"* [^68]. Especially when catechesis, diligently and in a due manner imparted, will effect that as many spiritual sons as possible are formed unto the glory of God, the consolation of the pastor himself and parents, and unto the crown of an honorable Christian in the future, and unto the adornment of the parish, through whose care religion and piety will ever flourish among the faithful of the parish.

## 2. Decrees of the Ruthenian Provincial Synod: Titulus II. On the Mysteries and Their Administration

### Preamble: Mandate for the Official Ruthenian Trebnik

Retaining without change and in its full force all that the Synod of Zamość [^69] pronounced concerning the dignity and manner of administering the Holy Mysteries, as also that which is prescribed in the Decree of the Sacred Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith promulgated under the name *Concordia* ("Agreement") of the 5th of October, 1863, which is annexed hereto in the appendix [^70], this Synod decrees:

Whereas up to this time the wise regulation of the said Synod of Zamość concerning the publication of a new *Trebnik* (Ritual), on the basis of which all priests of our rite might observe uniformity in the administration of the Holy Mysteries, hath not yet been satisfied in all its parts, the Most Reverend Metropolitan shall take care that such an edition, with all possible diligence and exactness, according to the exemplar prescribed by this Synod, be printed as soon as possible.

### CHAPTER I. On the Holy Mystery of Baptism

Concerning the administration of Holy Baptism, the present Synod decrees that unto what the Synod of Zamość prescribed, the following be added:

1. a) *As to the time*: Pastors shall endeavor that it be administered as soon as can possibly be according to circumstances [^71].  
b) *As to the place*: That the same be ordinarily administered in church [^72]. In our regions, however, on account of the severity of the climate, especially in the winter season, the Synod of Zamość permits that the same may at times be administered also in private houses (Tit. III, § 1); according however to the custom now existing

in the said cases the child to be baptized is wont to be brought to the parish house, which custom, according to the judgment of the present Synod, may be retained.  
c) *As to the manner*: Above all the Synod decrees that the custom received among us from time immemorial, of baptizing by triple pouring (infusion), must be retained.

2. That, except in case of extreme necessity, baptism be not separated from the ceremonies attached thereto in the Ritual (*Ustav*).

3. That the pastor pay heed to the choice of sponsors (godparents / *kumy*), as is already prescribed in the Synod of Zamość (Tit. III, § 1); in particular, let the pastor of souls instruct the sponsors that they are placed in the room of parents, and it properly belongs unto them to instill into the children whom they held at baptism the precepts of faith and good morals, if their parents should either neglect to do so, or be overtaken by death, or by reason of their absence or in any other way hindered, be unable to do so of themselves [^73].

For the rest, for the introduction of perfect uniformity in the administration of this as of all the other Holy Mysteries, the Synod annexes hereto a schema both of the text and of the ceremonies, which ought to serve as an exemplar for the publication of the new *Trebnik*, which all are bound strictly to observe [^74].

### CHAPTER II. On the Holy Mystery of Chrismation

Concerning the Holy Mystery of Chrismation, the Synod commands pastors of souls that they preserve all that the Synod of Zamość prescribed regarding this Holy Mystery; in particular, as to the matter of this Mystery, that is, the Holy Chrism (*sviate Myro*), the present Synod insists that the same be renewed yearly at the appointed time. But whereas in this regard negligence of pastors is at times observed, the Synod strictly admonishes them that they observe this prescription exactly;

and therefore immediately after the consecration of the new Chrism, which ordinarily takes place on Great and Holy Thursday, they shall procure the same through their respective decanal office, and it shall be used only until the consecration of the new Chrism of the following year, which is to be performed on Great and Holy Thursday, so that within a month from the consecration they may already have the new Chrism in their possession; and the strict duty of watching over this is laid upon the respective Deans themselves. The Holy Chrism the Deans themselves, or priests commissioned by them, are ordinarily bound to receive from the Prefect of the Cathedral Sacristy and deliver to the decanate.

But whereas the Synod of Zamość prescribes that the old Chrism be burned upon a clean stone, and its ashes cast into a sacred place (*Sacrarium*) [^75], therefore the present Synod admonishes pastors, all and singular, that in their churches they have such sacred places (*Sacraria*), and where there are none, that they hasten as quickly as possible to construct such, into which shall be cast not only these ashes, but also those from other sacred things, and where also shall be poured out the water that hath served either for Holy Baptism already administered, or for the washing of hands after Chrismation and other holy anointings.

As to the text and rubrics (*ustav*) of this Holy Mystery, the new edition shall conform to the outline which is here annexed and approved by the Synod [^76].

### CHAPTER III. On the Most Holy Mystery of the Eucharist

1. Retaining all that concerning the matter and form of this Mystery, as necessary to be known and done, was prescribed in the Synod of Zamość, we omit not especially to emphasize anew that all priests hold and firmly believe that the words consecrating this Mystery are no other than the words of the Lord alone: *"Take, eat: this is*

*My Body"*, and *"Drink of it, all of you: this is My Blood"*; and not the words invoking the Holy Spirit, which, as we learn, certain ignorant persons maintain in many catechisms, and following them also in recent observations of schismatic liturgical books (*Liturgikons*) against the ancient faith of the Greek Church. Concerning this truth, among others, Saint John Chrysostom teaches thus: *"Christ is present now also to adorn the table, because it is not man who causes the offered gifts to become the Body and Blood of Christ. The priest stands only fulfilling the figure and offers prayer: but the grace and power of God it is that accomplishes all. 'This is My Body' [^77], saith He. This word transubstantiates the offered gifts. And as that voice: 'Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth' [^78], was a word and became an act that gave to human nature the power of procreating children, so also this voice uttered, ever multiplies grace in those who worthily partake"* [^79]. And again: *"For as the words which Christ spoke are the very same which the priest speaks today, so also the oblation is the very same"* [^80]. Saint Irenaeus: *"When therefore the mingled cup and the broken bread receive the Word of God, it becomes the Eucharist of the Body and Blood of Christ"* [^81]. Saint Gregory of Nyssa: *"Immediately by the Word it is changed into the Body, when the word was spoken: 'This is My Body'"* [^82]. Finally, that true doctrine the entire Church solemnly confessed at the Council of Florence. The Synod likewise insists that priests in pronouncing the words of consecration in no wise depart from the received form of individual words wherewith that same consecration is expressed in our present *Liturgikons*; which we have thought necessary to mention here, because it is known to us that one priest in this matter had departed from the common practice.

2. As to the decree of the Synod of Zamość regarding sacred vestments which a priest must use in carrying this Most Holy Mystery to the sick,

the said Synod permits: *"if the house of the sick person be farther removed from the place where the parish church is situated, to carry Holy Communion secretly, and thereby without church vestments"* [^83]; the present Synod, however, hath thought fit to modify the said decree so far, that a priest carrying the Holy Viaticum (*Sviate Naputie*) to a sick person in this our province shall do so openly and at least according to the common and orderly custom, vested in epitrachelion and phelonion, since such custom hath already entered into practice among us.

3. In addition, as to the pattern of prayers which the priest is accustomed to recite for the people to repeat after him when approaching Holy Communion, the Synod admonishes that all conduct the same exactly as it shall be set forth in the rule above mentioned by this Synod, adding nothing and omitting nothing.

4. During the very action of administering this Holy Mystery, let them not add to the formula: *"The servant of God partakes"*, the word: *"unworthy"*, as some have been accustomed to add; and after administering Holy Communion to individual communicants, let them not wipe with the towel the holy spoon (*lozhychka*) moistened in the chalice, without a grave reason.

5. But since Holy Communion in our rite is ordinarily administered under both kinds and that during the Divine Liturgy, although under each kind Christ is present whole and entire: at a great concourse of communicating people, let the priest, before the distribution of Holy Communion, never pour unblessed wine into the particles placed in the chalice for the easier distribution of particles placed in large number in the chalice; but let him above all before the Divine Liturgy inquire concerning the number of those who are to receive Communion, and let him pour in a proportionately necessary quantity of wine; and if it should at times happen that an unexpectedly extraordinary number of communicants approach the Holy Table, then let him distribute Holy Communion with that quantity of consecrated wine which he then hath in the chalice; and in the case where particles for Communion in the consecrated chalice should no longer suffice, and there yet remain

communicants, then he may administer Holy Communion to them only after the manner of the sick, that is: taking consecrated particles from the pyx (*pushka*) and giving them to those approaching; which latter custom may be observed also in those cases when communicants have not the opportunity to be present at the Divine Liturgy, as especially happens in towns, as also on pilgrimages to miraculous places, unto which also not infrequently very late in the afternoon the faithful arrive fasting from distant localities, with the intention of receiving Holy Communion on that same day.

6. Finally, having administered the holy particle to the communicant, let him wipe his mouth with the communion cloth (*ruchnychok*), either himself or the deacon, if there be one; but let him not permit the communicant himself, taking the cloth with his own hand, to wipe his mouth; and it is necessary to wipe it thoroughly, lest accidentally the communicant himself, with his own hand or with his own handkerchief, should wipe his mouth.

7. In fine, regarding the appointed time (*rechyenets*) for Paschal Communion, although it is not unknown to the Synod that according to common law it is comprised within fifteen days, namely in the days from Palm Sunday (*Nedilia Vayi / Tsvitna*) up to Low Sunday (*Antipascha*), called among us Saint Thomas Sunday: — yet since among us the custom hath long since entered, on account of the large number of penitents, to begin that precept from the first day of the Great Fast (Forty Days) and to extend it much later beyond Saint Thomas Sunday: therefore the Synod deems it well to retain the custom as to the first term, and as the second term it appoints the feast of the Ascension of the Lord itself, so that with that feast ends the second term of Paschal Communion to be received by the faithful, and that in their own parish, from their own pastor, or by his permission and knowledge, in another church and from another priest.

8. If however pastors should learn that anyone of the faithful hath not fulfilled the precept of Paschal Communion, they are bound, after unsuccessful admonitions, to announce to them that the penalty of excommunication exists, and finally to report such persons to their own Ordinary, who will not fail to issue against the disobedient the penalty of excommunication already decreed at the Synod of Zamość.

### CHAPTER IV. On the Mystery of Repentance

1. As to the Mystery of Repentance, pastors of souls shall encourage the faithful committed to their solicitude that they receive the same frequently, setting before them earnestly the ancient custom in this matter of the Eastern Church, cited by the Synod of Zamość, that *"at least three times a year, namely at the Resurrection of the Lord (at which time they are bound to do so under penalty of excommunication), at the Dormition of the Most Holy Mother of God, and at the Nativity of Christ the Lord, they approach holy Confession and the Communion of the Divine Body"* [^84]. Although indeed this obligation in later times was relaxed and limited so that these Mysteries should be received at least once a year, around Pascha: nevertheless the present Synod, understanding the importance of the matter, lays upon pastors the duty to admonish the faithful that they preserve that ancient custom in its integrity, confessing at least three times in the year.

2. The doctrine concerning the precept of annual Confession and Paschal Communion, which in our Catechisms is expounded so that that Confession and Communion should take place specifically before one's own pastor, the present Synod decrees to modify so that Confession may be made before any priest authorized to hear confessions, but Communion before one's own pastor, or also before another, yet only with the knowledge and permission of one's own pastor. As to the terms of annual Confession, since it is wont to precede Paschal Communion, it is to be comprised within the very same terms as Communion.

3. As to the very administration of this Mystery: whereas in many places the custom hath taken root that the faithful approaching Confession lay down beside the confessor one or two cents (*kraytsary*), and again that on the occasion of Paschal Confession pastors themselves, before issuing to the faithful the confession ticket (*kartka do spovidi*), demand certain dues to be contributed by them under various titles, whether for the pastor (such as *proskurne* and the like), or for the church cantor; and this can very often be an obstacle to willingly approaching Confession, or at least give someone occasion for malicious interpretation: therefore the Synod commands that pastors of souls explain to the people in public instructions that for the administration of this Mystery they are not bound to any payment, nor to any offerings. In addition, this Synod forbids pastors in the future, on the occasion of Paschal Confession, to demand either the above-mentioned or any other dues whatever, but to postpone the payment of such to a more suitable time.

4. Finally, as to the jurisdiction (*sudovlastia*) of confessors: although according to common law their jurisdiction is limited to their own subjects, yet on account of the custom long since received in our regions and approved by the Bishops of the various rites, they may hear the confessions also of other faithful not subject to them, whether they be of the same or of another rite, as is also declared in the *Concordia*, or whether of their own or of another Eparchy of this Province, without violating however the right of reservation belonging to individual Bishops. Preachers and confessors therefore shall have in their possession a written list of all cases reserved not only to the Pope, as indicated in the Constitution *Apostolicae Sedis* ("Of the Apostolic See") [^85], but also to the Bishops of the three Catholic rites, lest (confessors) through carelessness arrogate authority unto themselves. Yet notwithstanding all this, let confessors know that their jurisdiction doth not extend to monasteries, and especially to monasteries of nuns; only for hearing the confessions of nuns is it necessary to be provided with special approval from the Ordinaries of the respective monasteries.

5. Finally, pastors of souls shall strive that for hearing confessions they have confessional booths (*spovidalnytsi*) in their churches, so that they may receive the confessions of the faithful therein conveniently and decorously.

# Acts and Decrees of the Ruthenian Provincial Synod of Lviv (1891)

## 1. Decrees of the Ruthenian Provincial Synod: Titulus II. On the Mysteries and Their Administration (Continued)

### CHAPTER V. On the Mystery of Anointing (Holy Unction)

1. Concerning this Mystery, the present Synod, renewing that which the Synod of Zamość wisely declared concerning its doctrine and administration, deems it necessary to state one thing only, namely: that the Order (*Chyn*) of the administration of this Mystery according to the pattern *"In Brief"* (*V krattsi*), proposed by the said Synod, shall be generally used not only in case of extreme necessity, but also in other cases, and that on account of the custom already long since introduced among us; although the present Synod doth not forbid, but also recommends, administering the same according to the Greater Order (*Poslidovanie bôlshe*), especially when more priests can be had for its celebration.

2. That perfect uniformity may be preserved by sacred ministers regarding the application of the matter and form, the present Synod prescribes that the anointing shall take place at these words of the form, of all the most essential, that is, at the words: *"Heal, by this anointing, this Thy servant,"* and shall conclude at the uttering of the words: *"giving him life by the grace of Thy Christ."*

3. As to the manner of anointing the various parts of the body, for the establishment of complete uniformity, the Synod complements that wherein the Rubrics (*Ustavy*) are deficient:
1. That the anointing shall be performed with the thumb, and that with its underside; cases of contagious illness excepted, when it is permitted to use a small stick (*patychok* / probe).
2. That it shall be done cruciformly (in the form of a cross).
3. That in dual organs of the senses, a double anointing shall be used, beginning from the right organ.
4. That the anointing of the eyes shall be performed upon the eyelids; of the ears, upon the lowest part of the ear (the lobe); of the nostrils, upon the tip of the apex of the nose with a single anointing; — of the mouth, upon the closed lips, with a single anointing; of the hands and feet, upon the underside, that is, upon the palms and upon the soles.
5. The Roman Ritual of Paul V under the Title on Anointing (*de Sacramento Extremae Unctionis*) teaches: *"The anointing of the loins (the same is to be said also of the breasts) in women, for the sake of modesty, is to be omitted; as also in men, when on account of infirmity they cannot easily be moved; and in that case, both in men and in women, it is not required*

*to anoint any other part of the body instead of the loins."* If anyone should lack any sense, for example sight, or if any member should be amputated, for example an arm or a leg, then the nearest part of that sense or member shall be anointed, since he could have sinned, if not through the external senses, then through the powers corresponding to them, or through the lust of an illicit deed.

4. As to the matter or oil, the present Synod is constrained to renew the injunction of the Synod of Zamość, namely: that the remnants of the oil which remain after the anointing shall be burned and not used anew for a second administration of this Mystery. Therefore let priests, as ministers of this Mystery of Holy Anointing, not bless all the oil which they keep for this purpose in the vessel (*posudets*), but let them pour out from it only a small quantity, such as shall be deemed sufficient for the anointing of one sick person, into a shallow silver or pewter (*tsynkovyi*) vessel, as the Synod of Zamość prescribes; and after the completion of the rite, let them wipe out the remaining portion of oil in the vessel with cotton and burn it in the fire.

5. That the faithful, however, may not be deterred from the reception of this Mystery, let pastors in convenient time instruct them in sermons or catecheses that nothing is due to the priest for the administration of this Mystery; and therefore let pastors themselves beware of demanding anything from the faithful for the administration of this Mystery. What is more, they, as good pastors, shall themselves inquire concerning the state of health of their faithful, and whether personally or through the members of their households, let them bring them unto the willing reception of this Mystery. And that the members of the household themselves, as at times happens, may not delay summoning the priest when the hope of regaining health still gleams, let them instruct the faithful that this Mystery avails also for the health of the body, for the recovery whereof it can truly serve, if that be profitable for the salvation of the soul, as the same Synod of Zamość saith. [^86]

### CHAPTER VI. On the Mystery of Priesthood (Holy Orders)

1. Besides that which the Synod of Zamość enacted concerning the Mystery of Priesthood, the present Synod hath deemed it appropriate to enact the following:
1. *As to the age of those to be ordained*: Although the ancient canons of the Church prescribe for the reception of the Subdiaconate 20 years [^87], for the reception of the Diaconate 24 years [^88], for the reception of the Presbyterate 30 years [^89]; nevertheless, following the enactment of the Council of Trent, which the Synod of Zamość already likewise charged us to follow, there is required for the reception of the Subdiaconate the 21st year, for the Diaconate the 22nd, for the Presbyterate the 24th completed; which enactment must henceforth be strictly preserved among us also.

2. *As to the interstice (intervals of time)*: Although in the canons of Councils an interval of time of one year is prescribed between the reception of one order and another [^90]; nevertheless the Council of Trent left it in this matter to the discretion of Bishops to shorten the time of those same intervals according to what they shall judge to be more suitable to the need and benefit of the Church. However, our Synod of Zamość, without infringing that right of Bishops, enacted, besides the orders of Reader and Subdeacon, which, as it affirms, may be conferred at one ordination and on the same day, an interstice (interval) of ten days between the reception of the Subdiaconate and the Diaconate, and likewise between the Diaconate and the Presbyterate. Yet that prescription of the Synod, in modern times, chiefly for the sake of giving more practice to the newly ordained in the sacred order after the reception of the Diaconate and the Presbyterate, is wont to be still more constricted, and that on the basis of faculties which Bishops usually receive from the Holy See; so that according to present custom, between the orders of Reader and Subdeacon no interstice is observed, as the Synod of Zamość also ena-

cted; between the Subdiaconate and the Diaconate, for the most part, an interstice of one day is observed, and between the Diaconate and the Presbyterate (an interstice of) eight days. The present Synod, consenting to the observance of the custom just cited, enacts in addition that the orders of Deacon and Presbyter may be conferred only on two days, and those solemn feasts, yet not following immediately upon each other; whereas the orders of Reader and Subdeacon, according to ancient practice, may be conferred also on a ferial day (on weekdays).

3. *As to the preparation of those to be ordained for the worthy reception of Holy Orders*: Besides that which the Synod of Zamość prescribes according to the enactments of the Council of Trent, the present Synod declares, namely regarding spiritual exercises (*rekolektsii*), that the requirement of eight days consecutively following one another before the reception of minor orders must likewise be observed at the reception of the Diaconate and the Presbyterate, whenever a longer interval intervenes in their conferring; but when these higher orders are conferred on feast days following upon each other without a long interval, one day suffices for this latter.

The Synod adds, however, that those to be ordained, before receiving the Subdiaconate as well as the Diaconate and the Presbyterate, shall cleanse their conscience through holy Confession. In addition, the Synod turns the attention of Hierarchs (Ordinaries), that they preserve the injunction of the Synod of Zamość, prescribed regarding the making of the profession of faith by those approaching Holy Orders, namely, that the same shall be performed according to the formula of Pope Urban VIII, prescribed for Easterners, and that precisely on the very day of the reception of the order of the Presbyterate.

4. Finally, whereas in our *Archieratikon* (Pontifical), immediately before the reception of the Presbyterate, there is set forth the formula of the oath of fidelity and obedience both to the Supreme Pontiff and to one's own Hierarch, as also of the preservation of the church goods that shall be committed to him for administration, and of perpetual stability in that same Church: — the present Synod, commending this practice, commands that the same be preserved henceforth.

5. *As to the very rite of conferring Orders*: The Synod requires that, if several receive the order of Deacon

or Presbyter together, the Hierarch shall lay his hands upon each individual ordinand separately when he pronounces the essential form of the Holy Order, namely: *"Divine grace..."* and so forth.

### CHAPTER VII. On the Mystery of Matrimony

1. Concerning the Mystery of Matrimony, following in all things the rule and doctrine of the holy Council of Trent and the Synod of Zamość, as also the prescriptions of the *Concordia*, we oblige pastors of souls that they adhere to them strictly, and in cases where any dispensation should be needed, that they have recourse for obtaining the same always to their Ordinary, observing moreover in that matter the particular instructions contained in the pastoral letters issued for that purpose by the respective Ordinaries.

2. Especially doth the Synod admonish pastors of souls that the closer the degrees be from which the parties request a dispensation, the more unyielding shall they show themselves unto them, counseling, so far as possible, that they abstain from such a Matrimony, and take steps toward obtaining such a dispensation only for very grave reasons.

3. In addition, let them beware lest they bless the marriages of those persons who have unlawfully transferred from the Latin rite to the Greek; but let them diligently inquire concerning the lawfulness of such a transfer beforehand, since they ought to know that in a case where such a transfer of both parties was unlawful, such marriages are invalid, as not being contracted in the presence of their own proper pastor, as the Council of Trent and the *Concordia* prescribe.

4. As to mixed marriages between Catholic Christians and non-Catholics (those of other faiths), pastors of souls shall zealously endeavor that such marriages be not contracted; nevertheless, in cases where it appears unto them impossible to prevent such marriages, let them proceed strictly according to the particular instructions for such cases issued by Pope Gregory XVI of blessed memory

dated May 22, 1841, to the Austrian Archbishops and Bishops, and under date of July 16, 1842, extended to the territory of Galicia, and by His Eminence Cardinal Metropolitan Mykhailo Levytsky under date of January 31, 1843, promulgated in the Archeparchy of Lviv [^91]; and likewise by the Sacred Congregation of the Inquisition (*Congregatio Inquisitionis*) dated December 22, 1888 (*Archeparchial Gazette*, No. 4; of the year 1889, No. 29, ord.), which are cited here in full at the end of the Synod. [^92]

5. As to the time of the nuptial blessing to be imparted by the pastor, care must be taken that the same, generally speaking, take place in the morning hours, at least before noon, so that the spouses, having both confessed and received Holy Communion, may obtain the more abundant efficacy and grace of this Mystery.

6. Finally, as to the rite, pastors must adhere to the Rubric (*Ustav*) issued by this Synod. Regarding so-called civil marriages, the present Synod commands pastors of souls in all things to adhere to the instruction issued to all the clergy of this province by our Ordinaries on June 24 (July 6, New Style), 1868, which is inserted here at the end of the Synod: *"Spyrydon Lytvynovych, by the Grace of God, etc., etc., Joseph Sembratovych, etc."* [^93]

## 2. Decrees of the Ruthenian Provincial Synod: Titulus III. On Consecrations and Blessings — (Sacramentals)

### Theological Preamble: On the Nature and Dignity of Sacramentals

Although Consecrations and Blessings (Sacramentals) do not confer that abundance of grace which the Holy Mysteries confer, and do not confer it of themselves (*ex opere operato*), but only from the work of the agent (*ex opere operantis*) unto those duly disposed; nevertheless, Blessings and Consecra-

tions (Sacramentals) are wont in our Church to be used more than in the Greek Church, and to be highly esteemed. And that justly: for if the prayer even of an individual man is acceptable to God and yields unto man precious consequences and spiritual fruits, how much more the prayers of blessings and consecrations which are offered in the name of the Bride of Christ the Lord Himself unto His Heavenly Father! But whereas blessings and consecrations are wont to confer the more abundant graces the greater the spiritual disposition wherewith they are celebrated both by the faithful and by the ministers; therefore the Synod admonishes sacred ministers that they impart such blessings and consecrations unto the faithful devoutly, piously, and with a clean conscience; and likewise it admonishes the faithful that they receive the same devoutly, piously, and with a clean conscience. [^94]

Now just as certain Holy Mysteries, besides conferring grace, are ordained for the destruction of evil, that is, of sin, while others are for conferring or increasing the gifts of grace; so similarly are blessings and consecrations: for some of them are ordained for the expulsion of evil — such as exorcisms and similar adjurations, as also of inanimate things; others for conferring a blessing or sanctification, whether upon persons or upon things.

### Section 1: On Exorcisms

1. *As to exorcisms*: Pastors of souls, in disturbances by evil spirits, shall endeavor before all things accurately to investigate whether their effects more probably proceed from the devil, or rather from some natural illness; and if they should be convinced of the former, and the gravity of the case should require it, let them not proceed to adjuration (exorcism) immediately, especially publicly, but beforehand let them give an accurate report unto their Ordinary; and only when he shall judge that there is need to use adjurations, let them proceed unto them, having first reviewed the rules which are set down concerning this subject in our *Trebniks* (*Euchologia*).

### Section 2: On the Purification of Women After Childbirth

2. *As to the purification of women after childbirth (churching)*: The Synod ordains first of all that the rite of purification over a woman who hath borne an illegitimate child shall be omitted, so that in this manner both she and others may be deterred from such a sin. Concerning this ordinance of the Synod the pastor shall

duly instruct his faithful and with all gravity strictly admonish them to avoid sins and transgressions of this kind; and those who transgress let him regard as public sinners and persons giving scandal so long as they do not repent; and therefore for such a woman, for the expiation of her sin, instead of purification, let him impose the reception of the Holy Mystery of Repentance. Generally, however, concerning all women who after childbirth come to church for the purpose of purification: since immediately after receiving this rite they are wont to give themselves over to domestic and field labor, therefore that they may still be regarded as unfit for such labor by reason of infirmity, the Synod ordains that they shall approach the reception of this rite ordinarily not before 20 days after childbirth; and that all the more since, according to the prescriptions of the *Trebnik* (*Euchologion*), properly speaking, this rite ought to be received on the 40th day after childbirth.

### Section 3: On Blessings — The Blessing of Loaves

3. *As to blessings*: Among others, the most solemn is that which takes place at the All-Night Vigil of any solemn feast and goes under the name of the Blessing of Loaves (*Artoklasia* / *Blahoslovenie khlibiv*), although besides loaves there are also placed for blessing wheat, wine, and oil. Those loaves, as also the wine used according to the Rubric (*Ustav*), were anciently distributed unto the monks who sang the whole night in the church, and that between Vespers and Matins; but whereas now that rite is celebrated not only in monasteries, but also in all parish churches wherein all the faithful take part in that service, therefore the Synod enacts that those loaves, sliced into small pieces, shall be distributed unto each one of the people who approaches for anointing with the blessed oil; and the remnants of that oil, after the anointing of the faithful, shall be poured into the church lamp; while the wine and the wheat shall fall to the pious use of the priest, so that for the subsequent blessing everything may be presented anew.

### Section 4: On the Blessing of Churches, Altars, Bell Towers, and Cemeteries

4. *Concerning the blessing of the foundation and of a newly erected church and of its altar, as also of a bell tower and bells, as well as of cemeteries*: It must be known that every priest may perform these blessings who is provided, however, unto that end with a special mandate (*hramota*) from his Ordinary, and according to the rite proper to simple priests, and not to Hierarchs, as set down in the *Trebniks*.

### Section 5: On the Blessing of Sacred Vestments, Vessels, and Furnishings

5. In addition, a priest may bless all sacred vestments and vessels, as also sacred furnishings: icons, banners (*khoruhvy*), crosses, medals, and so forth; excepting: the antimension, the chalice, the tabernacle (*darokhranylnytsia*), the diskos, the spoon (*lozytsia*), and the monstrance (*monstrantsiia*), the blessing of all of which pertains, properly speaking, unto the Bishop. But by special commission of the Bishop, in certain cases, faculty may be given also to a simple priest; therefore concerning this faculty the Bishop ought always to be petitioned, so that according to the rite prescribed for a simple priest the blessing may take place.

### Section 6: On the Blessing of a New House and the Consecration of Water

6. *As to the blessing of a new house*: Simple priests also perform this, and it is fitting that a house should be blessed. Therefore it is fitting to admonish the faithful that, according to the pious custom of their ancestors, they neglect not to sanctify the houses built by them by the invocation of the Name of the Lord and by ecclesiastical blessing, and only then, after the blessing of the priest, begin to dwell therein. But whereas the rites used at the blessing of a house set down in our *Trebniks* are not entirely the same, and many rites which are wont to be used among us are not given in any *Trebniks*, but are assumed solely from custom, which also, according to different places, is diverse; therefore, for the establishment of uniformity in that rite, the Synod commands that a more detailed rubric (*ustav*) be inserted in the *Trebnik* to be published according to the rules approved by this Synod. [^95]

In addition, the Synod desires that in the future the oil which hath served for the cruciform anointing of the individual walls shall not be burned by candles brought near to the anointed place upon the wall, as is prescribed in many *Trebniks*; but that the place upon the wall anointed with oil shall immediately be wiped with cotton, and thus the holy oil with the same shall afterward be burned.

*As to consecrations*: The Synod commands that, for the preservation of uniformity in the one and the other consecration, or, as it is called among the Latins, at the blessing of water, namely both at the Great Blessing which is proper to Theophany, and at the Lesser Blessing which, the feast of Theo-

phany excepted, is wont to be celebrated in the course of the year according to need, after the commemoration of our Emperor there shall also be, and that in the first place, the commemoration of the Supreme Hierarch, as is already set down in the Pochaiv editions, and that according to the following formula: *"And save, O Lord, Thy servants, the Universal Pontiff N. N., and our right-believing Emperor N. N."*

## 3. Decrees of the Ruthenian Provincial Synod: Titulus IV. On the Public Worship of God

### Preamble: On the Public Adoration of God and the Variety of Catholic Rites

1. Since man, as consisting of a dual nature, is bound to render honor unto God his Creator, internal and external, he is further constrained unto such an obligation by this: that with other men he unites in society; whence arises for society the necessity of rendering unto God public honor. The rule of that same honor in the Old Testament God Himself prescribed unto His people; while in the New Testament its essential parts were determined by the Son of God Himself, sent into this world for the salvation of the human race; and the rest was designated by His Bride the Church herself, taught by Apostolic tradition and guided by the Holy Spirit. She therefore, in a seamless garment for the sake of the unity of faith, and clad with diversity for the sake of the variety of rites, renders unto God the most pleasing honor in the New Law. Concerning that garment the universal teacher Saint Augustine expounded the words of Psalm 44:10: *"At Thy right hand stood the queen in gilded clothing, surrounded with variety,"* writing: *"The mysteries of doctrine (are delivered) in all the diverse languages. One language is African, another Syrian, another Greek, another Hebrew, another this and that; these tongues make the diversity of the garment (vesture) of this Queen; and just as all the diversity of the garment meets together in unity, so also all tongues in the one faith. In the garment let there be diversity, but let there be no tear. Behold, by diversity we understand the variety of tongues, and by the garment we understand unity."* [^96]

2. That this diversity of rites not only doth not hinder, but even contributes in the highest degree unto the multiplication of the beauty and majesty of the Church, Pope Pius IX of immortal memory declared in his Encyclical to the Easterners, saying: *"To the unity of the Catholic Church the manifold diversity of holy and lawful rites is in no way opposed; what is more, it contributes in the highest degree to the increase of the dignity, majesty, beauty, and splendor of the Church herself,"* [^97] which thought almost in the very same words the present Supreme Hierarch Pope Leo XIII also expressed before our clergy, admitted to an audience with Him in the Vatican on the occasion of His sacerdotal jubilee in the year 1888.

3. Of that same public worship in the Catholic Church there are two parts: the first and most important is the Sacrifice, wherein consists the greatest and highest religious act; the second is the Divine Office (*bohosluzhenie*), composed by the Holy Fathers, as also the many other services used in the Catholic Church.

### CHAPTER I. On the Sacrifice of the Liturgy (The Divine Liturgy)

1. The manner wherein in our rite the highest honor is offered unto God is contained in the threefold Liturgy, namely: of Saint Basil the Great, of Saint John Chrysostom, and of the Presanctified Gifts of Saint Gregory the Theologian (the Dialogist).

2. As to the first of these three Liturgies, that is, of Saint Basil the Great: Pastors of souls are bound to celebrate it ten times in the year, according to the prescriptions of the Rubric (*Ustav*). As to the second, that is, of Saint John Chrysostom: At least on every feast and Sunday, those days excepted wherein, as said above, it is prescribed by the Typikons to celebrate the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great; although the Synod desires and admonishes that pri-

ests also on other ferial days (weekdays) celebrate the Holy Liturgy (the Divine Liturgy), even though they should have no stipend; for *"in every Liturgy (Divine Liturgy) is found all the fruit which Christ wrought upon the Cross; such as are the fruits of the Passion of the Lord, such are the fruits of this Sacrifice"*; [^98] further: *"every Liturgy (Divine Liturgy) is worth as much as the death of Christ upon the Cross is worth,"* [^99] through which Liturgy the universe stands. [^100] And therefore *"a priest who, not being lawfully hindered, omits to celebrate the Liturgy, deprives, so far as in him lies, the Holy Trinity of glory, the Angels of joy, sinners of pardon, the just of help, those abiding in purgatory of refreshment, the Church of benefit, and himself of medicine."* [^101]

3. As to the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts: The liturgical books already prescribe that it shall be celebrated on every Wednesday and Friday of the Great Fast (Lent), and in addition on Great Monday, Great Tuesday, and Great Wednesday of Holy Week. On the other days of the Great Fast there is celebrated on Sundays the Liturgy (Divine Liturgy) of Saint Basil the Great, on Saturdays of Saint John Chrysostom; while on Monday, Tuesday, and Thursday, in the rubrics of our rite no Liturgy is indeed prescribed; but whereas from time immemorial the custom hath entered among Ruthenian Catholics of celebrating on those three days the Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom, therefore this Synod declares that that custom ought to be retained for the future also; prescribing moreover for pastors that on Wednesdays and Fridays of the Great Fast and on the first three days of Passion (Holy) Week they shall celebrate the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts; — and if ever there should be need to celebrate yet another Liturgy by other priests, that Liturgy cannot be other than that of Saint John Chrysostom, and read (low Mass), and that only with the special permission of their Ordinary unto that end.

4. The days wherein no Liturgy (Divine Liturgy) is to be celebrated are the following:
1. The Friday before Christmas (the Nativity of Christ) and Theophany of the Lord, when the Royal Hours are to be transferred from the Eve of the feast unto that day. —
2. The Friday of Passion (Holy) Week, unless on that day there should occur the Feast of the Annunciation of the Most Holy Virgin Theotokos; for then the Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom is to be celebrated together with Vespers. —
3. Although according to the Typikons on Wednesday and Friday of Cheesefare Week no Liturgy is prescribed, neither the complete nor the Presanctified, nevertheless the Synod now leaves inviolate our custom of celebrating on those days the Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom.

5. Finally, the solemn Liturgy joined with Vespers is to be celebrated on the Eve of the Nativity of Christ and of the Theophany of the Lord, if the same falls not on a Saturday or a Sunday; likewise on Great Thursday and Great Saturday of Passion (Holy) Week; so that on those days there shall be only one Liturgy, and that sung by one or by many priests concelebrating together (*soborno*), excluding all other Liturgies.

6. For the celebration, however, of so great an action, which is the center and core of our whole religion, there is required in the celebrant a suitable disposition, both as to body and as to soul, according to the words of the holy Council of Trent: *"Since we must confess that no other work can be performed by Christ's faithful so holy and divine as this tremendous Mystery itself, it is also sufficiently clear that all labor and diligence must be bestowed upon this: that it may be celebrated with as great an interior purity and innocence of heart, as well as exterior appearance of piety and devotion, as may possibly be."* [^102]

7. As to the body, the officiating priest must be clad in a long garment (the so-called cassock or *reverenda*) and in a decent, grave, and modest posture [^103]; from midnight fasting, and that under pain of mortal sin, and not six hours from eating, as some imprudently and contrary to the explicit common law of the Church and custom imagine unto themselves. [^104] For the greater decency due

to so great a mystery, and for avoiding scandal unto our people, let them retain the inviolate custom, hitherto existing among us, of not smoking from midnight until the Holy Liturgy. [^105]

8. As to the soul, it is required that he be clean from mortal sin, and so far as may be, from venial sins also. *"Let no one make light of things which appear small, since, as Peter heard, unless Christ wash them, they shall have no part with Him."* [^106] — Moreover, let no one conscious of mortal sin, even though he seem unto himself to be contrite, dare to approach the Holy Eucharist without prior sacramental confession. If, however, he should at that time have no confessor, and thus of necessity should celebrate without prior confession, having stirred up in himself only with the greatest diligence perfect contrition, he is bound as soon as possible to make his confession. [^107]

9. Further — let them prepare themselves for the worthy celebration of so great a mystery with canonical prayers, that is, Matins with the First Hour, and that according to the system of shortened canonical hours determined by this Synod, when it shall obtain approval from the Holy Apostolic See. Let them also use, as far as possible, those prayers composed by the Holy Fathers of the Church which in the Horologia (*Chasoslovy*) are usually placed before Holy Communion.

10. In the Liturgy itself let them strictly preserve the prescriptions drawn up by the Lviv Liturgical Commission, and adopted and ratified by this Synod, when they shall obtain approval by the Holy See. Let them celebrate the Sacrifice of the Holy Divine Liturgy with gravity and composure, both in the modulation of the voice and in gestures; not too quickly, not too slowly, so that, excluding the Proskomide, a read Liturgy (low Mass) may last about half an hour, but not exceed three quarters of an hour. In addition, let them pronounce all words properly and accurately, observing the accents and tone of sacred speech,

and let them not pronounce words incompletely (not entirely) or fragmentarily, or only in thought, but distinctly, observing however at the same time the distinction between prayers said secretly (*v tai*) and aloud (*v holos*), the utterance of which shall be moderate.

11. In a concelebrated Liturgy (*soborna Lyturhiia*), that is, when many priests celebrate together, the concelebrants are bound to pronounce with the principal sacred minister the words of consecration in a voice indeed quiet, yet neither anticipating nor lagging behind the principal celebrant, but simultaneously; likewise let them not neglect to recite the secret prayers with the celebrant, and that in the manner indicated in the Rubric (*Ustav*) when the priest celebrates alone. If, however, it should happen that some of them are merely assisting, not actually concelebrating, then properly speaking nothing more pertains unto them than the obligation to chant the ecphoneses in order, observing however all modesty, both exterior and interior, suitable to so great a religious action; nevertheless, for the edification of the people and the harmonious concord to be preserved during the sacred ministration, it is fitting that they should perform the other liturgical ceremonies together with the principal celebrant and pay heed unto the prayers themselves, omitting only the words of consecration, which are to be pronounced by the celebrants alone.

12. If all this be duly preserved, it will awaken piety in the people. For the Church uses ceremonies: *"that she may set forth the majesty of so great a Sacrifice, and by these visible signs of religion and piety move the minds of the faithful unto the contemplation of the highest things which in this Sacrifice are hidden."* [^108] If these, however, be neglected, then the people will turn away from divine worship, the greatest insult will be offered unto God, and eternal perdition will be prepared for the priests themselves.

13. That the faithful may be able to satisfy their obligation regarding hearing the whole divine service, the Synod enacts that the celebration of the parochial Divine Liturgy shall take place at certain designated hours, neither too early nor too late; generally speaking, the most suit-

able hour for the celebration is the tenth hour (10:00 AM) [^109]; for the rest, regarding the time of celebrating the Divine Liturgy the pastor must take into consideration the circumstances of his parishioners. If ever it should happen that the Divine Liturgy must be later, the prescription of the Synod of Zamość must be preserved, that it shall begin at least before noon, the Eves of the Nativity of Christ and Theophany excepted (unless they fall on a Saturday or Sunday), as also on Thursday and Saturday of Passion (Holy) Week. [^110]

14. If ever on the patronal feast (*khramovyi prazdnyk*), or on account of some special circumstance, there should arise the need to prolong the time of the Divine Liturgy, or to postpone it to a later hour, let them not do so without grave cause.

15. As to the hour of celebrating the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts, the custom received among us of celebrating the same from the hour of about ten before noon ought to be retained, which custom is based upon the Slavonic Typikons. [^111]

16. After the Liturgy the priest ought not, immediately upon taking off the holy vestments, to flee from the church or engage in conversations; but, recollected in some quiet place with due devotion, let him perform the order of thanksgiving unto Christ the Lord, who yet abides in His bosom, and let him add also other pious prayers, so that he spend upon them about a quarter of an hour; lest, behaving unworthily with so great a Guest abiding within him, he become guilty of the gravest insult and bring upon himself, instead of a blessing, a curse.

### CHAPTER II. On the Intention and Stipends of Divine Liturgies

1. Since every Hierarch, that is, priest, is taken from among men by God that he may offer gifts and sacrifices for the sins of the people, and the Church hath designated the time of that offering, restricting

the same unto Sundays and prescribed feast days; therefore it is fitting that all pastors of souls, first of all Bishops, and then proper pastors and those priests who have the administration of parishes, shall offer the Sacrifice of the Liturgy for the portion of the flock committed unto them on every Sunday and prescribed feast day.

As to feast days formerly prescribed and now suppressed, they are likewise bound to offer (apply) Divine Liturgies for the people, as is evident from the declaration of Pope Pius IX of blessed memory in the Encyclical *Amantissimi Redemptoris* of May 3, 1856. [^112]

As to those feast days which are transferred from a weekday unto a Sunday, it suffices to make the intention on the Sunday itself, as is clear both from the said Encyclical of Pope Pius IX and, especially as regards us, from the permission of His Holiness our Father Pope Leo XIII, granted by letter of June 26, 1886, to the Ruthenian Archbishop and Metropolitan of Lviv. [^113]

The feast days transferred from a weekday unto a Sunday, according to the permission above mentioned, are the following: Of the movable feasts: the Feast of the Holy Eucharist (Corpus Christi); of the immovable feasts:
1) Saint John the Evangelist, September 26;
2) the same Saint, May 8;
3) the Protection (*Pokrov*) of the Most Holy Virgin Mary, October 1;
4) Saint Great Martyr George, April 23;
5) Saint Prophet Elijah, July 20;
6) the Beheading of the Head of Saint John the Baptist, August 29.

As to assistants (*sotrudnyky* / parochial vicars) of pastors, these properly speaking are not bound to offer the Divine Liturgy for the people, since that obligation is personal to the pastor, who properly speaking is alone the pastor of his parish; and therefore without any remuneration (stipend) he is bound himself, on the days above mentioned, to offer for the people. Excepted, however, are cases where pastors unconditionally cannot celebrate themselves, and assistants have been assigned on account of their permanent or prolonged infirmity, or impossibility; in that case the assistants are bound to substitute for the pastors.

2. In a concelebrated Liturgy (*soborna Lyturhiia*), although each of the concelebrants is bound to have and apply his own intention; nevertheless, let him make the application for a certain stipend specifically given unto him on another day in an individual (low) Divine Liturgy, since ordinarily such is shown to be the intention of the donor; unless the donor should have agreed thereto.

3. For the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts, since it is not a complete sacrifice, no stipends may be accepted, just as there must be here no application in the strict sense. That which is at times brought to the priest on the occasion of the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts is to be regarded as an offering for the Order of Parastas or Panakhyda, which is to be celebrated after the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts.

4. Pastors of souls who receive an endowment from the Religion Fund (*relihiinyi fond*) are bound to celebrate each year such a number of holy foundation Divine Liturgies as shall be imposed upon them by their respective Ordinary. As to Liturgies founded by private bequests, let pastors endeavor that they be celebrated both in their proper time and according to the conditions laid down in the foundation documents (*hramoty*).

Yet let no beneficiary dare on his own authority to diminish any burdens of Liturgies to be celebrated on account of revenues that may have become insufficient; but for their diminution, regulation, or commutation he is bound to have recourse to his Ordinary. [^114]

5. As to free Divine Liturgies or manual stipends, priests cannot demand a stipend exceeding the stipend established either by eparchial law or by custom, nor may they angle for a smaller one for the sake of shameful gain (*turpe lucrum*). For great diligence must be applied: *"that from the dignity of so great a Sacrifice there may be removed conditions of any kind of payments, contracts, and rather indecent and unnoble transactions than requests for alms, as well as everything else of that kind which is not far removed from the taint of simony or certainly from shameful gain."* [^115]

Further, a priest who, having received a manual stipend, transfers the obligation of celebrating the Divine Liturgy unto another, is bound to transmit unto him the entire stipend. [^116]

6. As to foundation Divine Liturgies: Priests, when they entrust unto other priests the celebration of those Divine Liturgies which they are bound to celebrate as belonging to the parish, that is, on account of or from the obligation of a benefice or parish, may give them the ordinary stipend and retain the surplus for themselves, since the surplus is recognized not for the celebration of the Divine Liturgy, but for sustenance. [^117] What is more, if the liturgical alms are truly extraordinary, that is, incidental or occasional, namely on the occasion of the crowning of matrimony, a funeral, and so forth, yet constitute the *congrua* of the pastor, or belong to stole rights (*prava epytrakhylni*), the pastor who cannot satisfy them himself is permitted to entrust another priest with celebrating the Liturgy, giving him the ordinary local alms, whether for read Liturgies or for sung. [^118]

Likewise, for that same reason, a priest unto whom the celebration of a perpetual Divine Liturgy is entrusted, as also a priest upon whom weighs the celebration of a Divine Liturgy from an ample legacy left for him for sustenance with a liturgical burden, may retain for himself that portion which exceeds the ordinary stipend. [^119]

7. Let priests, however, beware of accumulating so many stipends that they could not satisfy them within the space of one or two months, unless the one offering the stipend explicitly agreed to a postponement. [^120] And beyond that time, let him request faculties from the Holy Apostolic See.

8. If, however, they should be bound to celebrate the Divine Liturgy for some need which brooks no delay, it is not permitted

unto them to postpone; but if they are hindered from celebrating immediately, let them return the stipend unto the donor, stating the reason for the refusal. In a case, however, where anyone should give a priest a stipend smaller than the tax accepted according to law or custom amounts to, the sacred celebrant is bound to apply the Liturgy for the donor, if he himself hath agreed thereto; in the contrary case, let him return the stipend unto the donor. By his own authority, however, let him not dare to combine as many smaller stipends as are due for the celebration of one Divine Liturgy, but he ought to have recourse unto the local Ordinary, as is also wont to be done in miraculous or pilgrimage places, that he himself by virtue of lawful authority may designate that combination of stipends. [^121]

9. That none of the donors or founders may be wronged regarding the discharge of the obligation, priests are bound to have dedicated books for this purpose, wherein they shall note down as accurately as possible the receipt of stipends and the celebration of the accepted obligation. [^122]

### Chapter IV: On the Celebration of Extraordinary Services, Whereof Some are Bound unto Immovable Feasts, and Others unto Moveable Feasts

*As to Services bound unto immovable feasts:*

#### I.

1. On the Feast of the Exaltation of the Precious Cross, on the 14th day of September, pastors are bound to bring forth the Holy Cross for the veneration of the people with all the rites prescribed in the Typikon of that day, and having thus brought it forth and placed it upon the Tetrapod before the Iconostasis, to retain it there until the Apodosis (*Otdaniye*) of the Feast; and if during that time any divine service should take place in Church, then upon the conclusion of that service veneration shall take place during the singing of the Troparion: *"We bow down before Thy Cross, O Master, and we glorify Thy holy Resurrection."*

2. In the months of December and January, on the Eve or on the Friday before the Eve of the Nativity of Christ and Theophany, according to the directions of the Typikon, pastors shall celebrate in Church the Special Hours, commonly called the *Royal Hours*, with the addition of the Order of the *Typika* (*Izobrazitelni*). In particular, as to the Feast of Theophany, they shall celebrate the Blessing of Water: once on the Eve of the Feast upon the conclusion of Vespers, and then on the very day of the Feast more solemnly after the Prayer behind the Ambo.

#### II.

3. On the 1st day of August, on the Feast of the Holy Maccabean Brethren, the Holy Cross shall be brought forth for the veneration of the people, and shall lie upon the Tetrapod for one day, observing all the rites as at the Exaltation of the Precious Cross, with the exception of the rite of the actual elevation (*vozdvyzhaniye*) toward the four corners of the earth, which is proper unto the Feast of the Exaltation. Moreover, they shall also on that day celebrate the Lesser Blessing of Water,

and where such custom exists, they shall bless herbs and seeds.

*As to Services bound unto moveable feasts, the same pastors are bound:*

1. In the midst of the Great Forty Days [Lent], namely on the Third Sunday, called *Cross-Veneration Sunday* (*Krestopoklonna*), to bring forth the Holy Cross for the veneration of the people with all the rites as on the 1st day of August; the same Holy Cross shall remain upon the Tetrapod until the Friday following that Sunday, for then it shall be removed during the Sixth Hour where the same is celebrated, or if not, then before the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts.

2. On the Thursday after the Fourth Sunday of Lent, to celebrate in Church Matins with the Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete and the prostrations joined thereto, yet so that instead of the three prostrations which are prescribed after each troparion, there shall be one only.

3. The liturgical orders which begin together with our Triodion called the *Flowery* (*Tsvitna* / *Pentecostarion*), up to the Sunday of the Resurrection of Christ, pastors shall celebrate daily in Church, and that in the following manner:
a) On the Saturday before Palm Sunday, called *Lazarus Saturday*, let them celebrate in Church a sung Divine Liturgy.
b) On Palm Sunday (*Nedilia Vayiy / Tsvitna*), let them celebrate all things according to custom, as is prescribed for a *Feast of the Lord* of the first class, adding moreover the blessing of palms (*vitiy*) during Matins.
c) On the three subsequent days of Holy (Great) Week, they shall celebrate the Liturgies of the Presanctified Gifts.
d) On Great and Holy Thursday of Passion Week, they shall sing the Divine Liturgy together with Vespers.
e) On the same Thursday after sunset, they shall chant the Office of the Holy Passions with the 12 Gospels.
f) On Great Friday, which is regarded as an extraordinary solemn feast, they shall celebrate in Church in the morning the Hours commonly called *Royal*, and around midday Vespers, at which is carried forth the holy icon called the *Plashchanytsia*, which, once carried forth before the Iconostasis, is placed in the place

called the *Tomb* (*Hrob*), and here let it remain for the continual veneration of the people until the celebration of the Resurrection, and that strictly according to the prescriptions of the Typikon which this Synod hath endeavored to compile.
g) It would be exceedingly desirable that pastors on that same day, after the laying down of the *Plashchanytsia*, at a suitable hour, should deliver an instruction unto the people concerning the Passions and death of Christ.
h) It is likewise commended unto the same pastors that they celebrate the *Burial Matins* (*Utrenia nadhrobna*) in Church on that Friday after sunset, on account of the distinctiveness of the divine service of this day and the solemnity of the rites taking place at the conclusion thereof.
i) On Great and Holy Saturday itself they shall sing the Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great with Vespers.
k) On the Sunday of the Resurrection, they shall chant very early in the morning Matins with all the rites both accompanying and preceding the same, and that according to the Typikon approved by this Synod. In cathedral Churches, as also in other non-cathedral Churches, the custom hath prevailed that the same Matins be taken beforehand, so that it is celebrated on Saturday evening, which custom, as the Synod judges, may be retained.
l) On the first three days of this week, called *Bright Week* (*Svitla*), they shall daily sing in Church Matins, the Divine Liturgy, and Vespers.

#### III.

m) On the very day of Pentecost, pastors shall celebrate immediately after the Divine Liturgy Vespers with the Kneeling Prayers (*Kolinopryklonni Molytvy*), according to the rule indicated in the typikons.
n) As to the Feast of the Most Holy Eucharist in particular, although the memory of its institution is celebrated year by year on Great Thursday at the Mystical Supper of the Lord, and indeed daily in the sacrifice of the Divine Liturgy, nevertheless, on account of the majesty of that Mystery, Pope Urban IV already in the 13th century [^128] established

that it should be commemorated with an annual solemnity throughout the entire Church in the manner of a Feast of the Lord of the first class, with an eight-day afterfeast (*cum octava*), namely on the Thursday after the first Sunday [after Pentecost], *All Saints*. This subsequently the Ecumenical Council of Trent likewise commends in these words: *"Most piously and religiously was that custom introduced into the Church of God, that every year on a certain and solemn day this most sublime and most blessed Sacrament should be celebrated with singular veneration and solemnity, and that It should be borne in processions with honor and majesty through the streets and public places."* [^129] Therefore, following this supreme ecclesiastical authority, not only all the Western Churches, but also almost all the Eastern Churches united with the Catholic Church celebrate this feast solemnly, especially our Ruthenian Church ever since the Union, and that with a special divine service arranged throughout all eight days; likewise do we find that this feast is celebrated among the Italo-Greeks, Greek-Melkites, Armenians, Maronites, Syrians, Chaldeans, and Copts. [^130] Furthermore, although properly this feast ought to be celebrated on Thursday, for on this day the Holy Eucharist was instituted by Christ the Lord, nevertheless, on account of the transference of feasts from weekdays unto the following Sunday, it is transferred unto Sunday; in which case, however, that is, on that Sunday, the entire divine service shall be celebrated, and that one and the same as indicated on the first day in the Triodion, with All-Night Vigil, while on the remaining days observing the rules of divine service prescribed in the general typikon with the afterfeast. On the Sunday itself there shall also be, according to custom, a procession with the Most Holy Mysteries after the recitation of the Prayer behind the Ambo, that is, from the Church unto four altars erected outside the Church, equidistant from one another. Where, however, it should be impossible to do this, it shall suffice to begin the procession from the Altar of the Church and, having thrice circuited the Church with the Most Holy Mysteries, to conclude this sacred rite

upon that same Altar in the Church with the blessing with the Most Holy Mysteries. As to the four Gospels, whereof one is customarily sung at each of the four altars according to the custom adopted here in the north, the Synod decrees that those should be selected which speak of the Most Holy Sacrament, according to the ritual prescriptions for this feast.

#### IV.

As to the other extraordinary divine services which on the occasion of feast days ought to be celebrated, it remains yet to mention the following, namely: 1. Exposition and Blessing with the Most Holy Mysteries; 2. The Order of the Akathist; and 3. The Order of the Paraklesis.

*On Exposition and Blessing with the Most Holy Mysteries.*

As to the exposition of the Most Holy Sacrament and the imparting of benediction therewith, the Synod confirms the custom already formerly introduced among us, that the same be publicly exposed upon the upper part of the Tabernacle (*Kyvot*), and that during the Divine Liturgy on the Feast of the Eucharist and on the Patronal Feast (the dedication or title of the church); in which case, however, sacred ministers are bound to observe the greatest caution, so that during that exposition they may not turn their backs unto the Most Holy Gifts, but when they must turn toward the people, they are bound to do so sideways, omitting in that case complete turns; likewise, if at times they happen to cross in front of the altar, let them never omit making a *metania* or profound bow, reaching with the hand unto the earth; likewise at the ablution of the chalice and hands, so far as possible, they shall face straight toward the altar.

#### V.

On those days on the occasion of this exposition, priests according to custom shall impart the blessing with the Most Holy

Gifts once, and Bishops three times (toward the three sides); and that on the Feast of the Holy Eucharist once at the beginning of the Liturgy, and then at the conclusion of the Liturgy after the procession.
In this latter case, the Most Holy Gifts immediately after the Liturgy shall be returned unto the Tabernacle until Vespers (if the procession is to take place again after Vespers). However, whether this takes place upon the conclusion of the Liturgy or of Vespers, before the blessing there shall be sung the so-called *Supplication* (*Syplikatsiya*) or hymn of contrition (*pisn umylitelna*).

#### VI.

The above-mentioned exposition and at the same time the procession with the Most Holy Gifts may take place also under other more solemn circumstances, such as: on the Feast of Pascha and on the occasion of parish missions, as also during so-called patronal indulgences (*otpusty*), wherewith certain Churches have been privileged by the Holy Apostolic See. Otherwise, however, when the procession is omitted, there shall be the exposition alone of the Most Holy Gifts with the supplication, and that on feast days or other special days whereon there is a larger concourse of the people. At the exposition of the Most Holy Gifts and the procession therewith, on the Feast of the Eucharist and on the patronal feast it is proper to use the Monstrance (*Monstrantsiya* / Ostensorium) for the greater majesty of the solemnity, whereas under other circumstances the exposition may be either in the Monstrance or in the Ciborium / Tabernacle (*Darokhranylnytsia*) itself.

#### VII.

Finally, priests must remember that it is never lawful to expose the Most Holy Gifts upon the altar, whether this takes place during the Liturgy, or at Vespers, or on any other occasion, unless there burn upon the Altar at least six wax candles.
As to the Lamb itself for exposition, before it is consecrated, it shall be fitted unto its vessel called the *Melchizedek* (lunette custodial insert), well positioned therein, and carefully wiped, so that particles may not subsequently fall from it

and having been placed in this manner into the Melchizedek, it shall be consecrated at some preceding Divine Liturgy, and once consecrated, preserved in the Tabernacle until the actual exposition.

#### VIII.

Finally, pastors must know that they ought to preserve the custom, already of old introduced here in the north and long practiced among us, that is, to expose the Most Holy Gifts either in the upper part of the Tomb, or upon the Altar on the side of the Tomb—on Great Friday and Saturday of Passion Week, and to carry the same in circuit around the Church with the *Plashchanytsia* on Great Friday and also before the Matins of the Resurrection of the Lord.

*On the Celebration of the Order of the Akathist.*

1. As on the one hand the present Synod earnestly commends the celebration of the Order of the Akathist, chiefly because the people take a great part in the singing thereof, and repeat easy and moving refrains after the numerous kontakia and ikoi, as also because therein devout affections may frequently be aroused by dart-like, short petitions; so on the other hand it directs that priests who chant this prayer together with the people shall celebrate the same truly devoutly and reverently, and not cursorily or by way of abbreviation. Since, however, the devout celebration of such prayer executed according to the prescription of the typikon requires considerable exertion of the voice and chest, as also a duration of nearly half an hour, while the remuneration for it in many places is far too small, whence it arises that priests have become accustomed when singing it to abbreviate, or at least silently to recite a significant portion thereof: therefore the Synod decrees that in the future this prayer shall always be sung entirely and in a moderate voice. In a case, however, where the remuneration offered is not sufficient, the priest may combine several such stipends, and only when the sum reaches 40 cents, then chant the prayer. If, however, certain of the faithful desire to apply the said prayer for

themselves, priests must know that, generally speaking, they may require for the celebration thereof a stipend of 40 cents.

2. The Synod strictly forbids the celebration of the Akathist in the midst of other liturgical services of the Church, and especially that it should not be used in place of the sermon and catechesis. For the rest, priests may chant it between Matins and the Divine Liturgy, and that customarily one only, or more, but this on the patronal feast and on other extraordinary occasions where a longer interval of time intervenes between Matins and the Liturgy. After the dismissal of the Liturgy and of Vespers, it is permitted to chant these Akathists, yet not in place of or unto the detriment of catechesis. Before Vespers, one or more Akathists may be chanted, in accordance with that which hath been said above regarding the time before the Liturgy. [^131]

*On the Order of the Paraklesis.*

1. This devotion, generally speaking, is a supplicatory prayer (*moleben*) unto the Most Holy Virgin in the event of some distress (affliction); nevertheless among us it is customarily celebrated also on other occasions, though not very frequently. But since this devotion on the one hand by its beautiful sentiments elevates the spirit unto heaven, kindles love toward the Most Blessed Virgin Mary, and directs hearts unto trust in Her; while on the other hand it is exceedingly agreeable unto our people, because they themselves take no small part in the chanting thereof: therefore the Synod judges that it would be highly desirable that priests should endeavor to celebrate it devoutly more frequently during the year, and above all on certain special occasions, such as during parish missions, public calamities, as also on the feasts of the Most Blessed Virgin Mary.

2. The celebration of the Order of the Paraklesis priests are bound to perform properly and accurately, observing always that devotion and gravity which befit divine worship; therefore any abbreviation or curt truncation in the

celebration thereof this Synod condemns as impious and illicit, and utterly forbids.

3. If any of the faithful should desire that this order be celebrated especially for him, this shall be agreed unto, and in such case for the celebration thereof one may accept the stole fee (*taxa*) approved by this Synod, namely 50 cents for sustenance.

### Chapter V: On Church Singing

1. Since the modulation of the human voice hath been given unto man by nature, man is bound therewith above all things to praise the Author of nature, and the more pleasantness that voice possesses in itself, the more capable it is of awakening toward God devout sentiments of the spirit. Therefore not only in the Old Testament was this in use, but likewise in the New it hath customarily been employed throughout all the churches of God, following the example received from the Lord Himself and the Apostles, Who departing unto the Father together with them after the Mystical Supper sang a hymn, as it is said in St. Matthew: *"And when they had sung an hymn, they went out into the mount of Olives."* [^132]

2. That the employment of sacred singing in our rite must be of greater weight is evident already from this, that among us musical instruments are not employed, but only the human voice alone, which is so much more noble than lifeless instruments as it is itself a living instrument of God's praise. And how capable singing is of arousing in the soul devout sentiments toward God, the great Luminary of the Catholic Church, St. Augustine, clearly expresses in the following words: *"When I recall the tears which I shed at the singing of Thy Church in the beginnings of my recovered faith, even now I am moved myself not by the singing, but by the things which are sung, when they are sung with a clear voice and suitable modulation, and I acknowledge again the great utility of that institution."* [^133]

3. That singing may attain its full end, it must be performed suitably unto so high an end, that is, *"with understanding,"* as the Royal Singer admonishes. [^134] Above all things in singing the voice must be so modulated that this modulation may not stifle the clarity of the words, that is, so that not so much the vocal sounds of the singer may please, but rather that the sense of the sacred words may penetrate the soul. [^135]

4. Where verses are to be sung alternately by two choirs, there the second choir shall not begin to sing its verse until the first hath finished its own, which shall likewise be observed in the recitation of the psalms. As to the genre of singing itself, since customarily among us a threefold singing is employed, that is: by ear (*z slukhu* / congregational chant), Heirmologion chant (*heirmolohiyne*), and figured (polyphonic / choral) chant (*fihuralne*), it must be understood that the first and second genres of chant properly correspond unto the divine worship of our rite, and these therefore in general shall be preserved and expanded; figured chant, however, as introduced later, shall be used only at the Divine Liturgy, and that only on certain greater solemnities, provided that an adequate number of singers can be had.

5. As to Heirmologion singing in particular, the Synod commands: that the alumni of Seminaries shall especially apply themselves thereto, although for the rest it doth not wish that figured singing should be abandoned. As to singing by ear, since not infrequently there is observed in various places a diversity in the execution thereof, the Synod ordains that, for the more precise uniformity thereof, the Most Reverend Metropolitan, in agreement with the Bishops, shall endeavor to print a manual of such chants as shall prove most suitable for the ordinary use of our province.

6. As to singing by ear which already exists in many places of this our ecclesiastical province, the Synod commends it to the utmost, as not only universally praiseworthy, but above all as exceedingly profitable and salutary, especially because by means thereof the faithful are more active in the sacred action itself, and become fuller participants in the holy divine service; so that not

the cantors alone, but under their lead in singing the entire congregation may sing the principal responses and hymns, especially at the Divine Liturgy, and that in this manner the praise of God may resound as widely as possible among the faithful, and they themselves more readily and, as we have said, with greater profit may participate in divine worship.

7. That the entire people may be the better trained in vocal singing, it is necessary that parishioners should be instructed therein already from youth, especially in the elementary schools (*narodni shkoly*). Since, however, correct instruction in singing depends almost entirely upon the church cantor (*dyak* / *pivets*), let pastors endeavor, for the proper fulfillment of that duty on Sundays and holy days as days especially dedicated unto God and our salvation, to choose for themselves as cantors not only righteous men, but also those more skilled in church singing, preferring especially those who shall present certificates of having passed an examination, issued by competent authority.
Since, however, Churches for the most part do not possess sufficient funds for the maintenance of cantors, the Synod directs the special attention of Bishops and pastors to the end that in the meantime they may promote the procuring of their maintenance.

8. Moreover, since in public divine worship there exist sacred folk songs composed in metric verses, among which are distinguished the so-called *Koliady* (Carols) composed for the magnification of the Nativity of Christ, which hymns the faithful are accustomed to sing both inside and outside the church, the Synod, being convinced that such hymns are exceedingly effective for fostering devotion in the souls of the faithful, and all the more so because they are composed in a language intelligible unto the people, commends unto pastors that the congregation under the lead of the cantor should chant in church at least one hymn, especially upon the conclusion of Vespers and catechesis. In order, however, that in so important a matter all confusion may be avoided on account of the diversity of editions of those hymns which in these times have begun to appear, the Synod commands that in the future in their editions both the rhythm and the language wherein they were formerly written shall be preserved, allowing only certain changes where necessity requires, and that with the prior approval of the Ordinary.

### Chapter VI: On the Celebration of Feast Days

1. Although God ought to be glorified by every prayer and good works, and as St. John Chrysostom admonishes: we Christians are commanded to keep festival unceasingly, doing nothing evil, for that is truly a feast, but turning our attention unto spiritual things and leaving earthly things, that we may rest with a spiritual rest, withholding our hands from greed, withdrawing the body from superfluous and unnecessary labors; [^136] nevertheless, above all, Sundays and holy days are dedicated unto the glorification of God, or unto divine worship and the sanctification of Christian life. Therefore these days dedicated unto spiritual good the faithful of Christ are bound to celebrate in an extraordinary manner, which obligation is laid upon us by God Himself. For God Himself after the six days of creation blessed the seventh day and sanctified it, ceasing on this day from all His work, [^137] and moreover commanded His people the celebration of this day under pain of death. [^138] In the New Testament the Church of Christ in place of the Sabbath day hath set Sunday, because on this day salvation was accomplished, as it were a second creation or regeneration of the world through the Resurrection of Jesus Christ, and was as it were sealed.

2. But as in the Old Testament, besides the Sabbath day, God Himself instituted the celebration of yet other days, that in them year by year the more important events might be commemorated; so likewise in the New Testament the Church of Christ, guided by the Holy Spirit, besides the celebration of Sunday, hath established the keeping of yet other days, that in them each year the special mysteries of the Christian religion might be celebrated. But since in these chief mysteries together with Christ the Most Pure Virgin Mary had part above all, therefore unto the feasts of the Lord the Church hath joined several feasts of the Most Blessed Virgin, and finally throughout

the entire circle of the year the feasts of the Saints, who together with Christ constitute in the Church triumphant the mystical body, and furthermore by the examples of their lives summon us unto participation in the Heavenly City.
When, therefore, the faithful are bound to keep the days established by the Church, the Church on her part hath designated for such keeping above all the sacrifice of the Divine Liturgy, which is the supreme act of our religion, and thereafter other divine services and the preaching of the Word of God; while on the part of the faithful she requires the duty of presence at those divine services, to hear the Word of God, and moreover to engage in other devout works and utterly to abstain from all servile works, especially evil ones, according to the above-cited admonition of St. John Chrysostom.

3. Therefore, as to the keeping of feast days, pastors shall direct the attention of the faithful especially unto the obligation of hearing the Divine Liturgy, instructing them how grievous a sin it is, not only without grave cause to omit hearing the entire holy sacrifice—the Liturgy—but also any considerable part thereof.

4. As to other divine services and the hearing of the Word of God, although there is not so grievous a liability for every omission whatsoever, nevertheless they shall commend unto them, so far as possible, the most frequent presence thereat. But since regarding the hearing of the Liturgy, liturgical offices, and sermons, there hath been observed in many places that intolerable custom, that during their celebration many of the faithful make bold to promenade outside the church, or to stand and converse, or even to depart before the conclusion of the Divine Liturgy: therefore the Synod commands that pastors, wherever such abuses are found, shall strictly admonish such faithful and point out unto them that in this manner they do not at all satisfy the commandment of the Church; nay rather, they draw down upon themselves the wrath of God, as deceivers of Him and of themselves, following the example of St. John Chrysostom, who for similar abuses thus rebuked his faithful: *"What art thou doing, O man? When Christ is present, the Angels stand by, this awesome Table is set before thee, when as yet thy brethren are being initiated into the Mystery,*

*dost thou leave them and depart? Why, if thou hadst been invited to a banquet, even if thou hadst been filled earlier, yet so long as others are seated, thou darest not depart before thy friends;—and here, when the Mysteries of Christ are being celebrated, when that holy sacrifice is still being offered, dost thou leave everything in the midst and depart? Who then shall judge this worthy of pardon? Who shall cleanse it with a defense? Do ye wish that I should tell whose work they perform who depart before the completion and end, and do not offer thanksgiving hymns after the finished Supper? Bitter perhaps will seem that which I say, yet it must needs be spoken on account of the negligence of many. When on that last night Judas had partaken of the Last Supper, and while all the rest were yet sitting, he himself, breaking away, went out. Him do they imitate who depart before the final act of thanksgiving... With great quietness therefore, with great modesty and fitting reverence let us approach this holy sacrifice, that we may obtain greater favor with God, purify our soul, and attain eternal blessings."* [^139]
Moreover, let them urge upon the faithful that they be present, especially at the sacrifice of the Divine Liturgy, not merely in any casual manner, but with great devotion and reverence, as also that they frequently approach the reception of Holy Communion, which is of all means the most efficacious to sanctify oneself, and consequently also the holy day.
Since the sacrifice of the Liturgy is the center and summit of divine worship and the sun of all spiritual exercises, pouring out upon men abundantly the gifts and graces of God: therefore let them not cease to urge upon the faithful that without grave cause they do not delay from hearing it, not only in their own Church, but also in the other churches of the same parish, demonstrating unto them the groundlessness of the reasons under pretext whereof some endeavor to excuse themselves from the obligation of going to the Church of another village. Nevertheless, since just causes may arise and indeed do arise, on account whereof for many of the faithful it is either

very difficult, or even impossible, to go to another village to hear the Divine Liturgy: therefore, that the faithful may not be deprived of so many and so great gifts of God, let pastors know that they are bound in a Church within the territory of their parish wherein on that day no Divine Liturgy was celebrated, to celebrate at least Vespers with the addition of catechesis. If, however, a territory of that kind possesses more than two churches, let them arrange that in the third and others, the church cantor himself, after the tolling of the bells, shall recite with the people some portion of the catechism, ordinary or daily prayers, and sing together with them some sacred folk hymn, to the end that the faithful at least in this manner may render due honor unto Christ the Lord hidden in the Most Holy Mystery of the Eucharist, and from Him in return be endowed with heavenly gifts.

6. When, however, the faithful have been present in Church at divine services, having rested a little in spirit, let them follow the commendable custom of pious Christians to read in their homes some spiritual book, especially the lives of the saints, or biblical history, or let them engage in some devout conversation.
Although on such days honorable recreation is not forbidden, nevertheless any entertainment worthy of condemnation entailing the corruption of morals is never permitted, as is wont to happen, as is well known, at dances which at times are accustomed to take place in taverns, with the addition of drunkenness and dissolute songs. *"For the Sabbath day,"* saith St. Augustine, *"is not carnally, not in the luxuries of the Jews, who abuse rest unto licentiousness; for it were better that they should dig all day, than dance or drink"*; and St. John Chrysostom saith: *"The time for prayers, not of drunkenness, is indeed always, but above all on a feast day; for a feast day was established for this, not that we should heap up sins, but that we should be rid of them."* [^140]
Since for the celebration of such feasts there is likewise imposed by the Church abstinence from servile works,

let pastors more frequently remind the faithful of the binding nature of that commandment, and at the same time let them thoroughly instruct them that that time of abstinence begins from midnight of the preceding day, and concludes with midnight of the day following the feast.
For the rest, for the deterrence of transgressors of that law, let them set forth unto them the damages whereunto they expose themselves thereby; for if servile (slave) labor was given unto man as a punishment for sin, mitigated by the mercy of God through rest on a feast day, then it appears clearly that they who transgress that law by labor punish themselves with a grievous curse, and moreover deprive themselves of inestimable treasures of grace on account of mortal sin, and prepare eternal punishments for themselves, unmindful of the Providence of God, which—unto those who observe heavenly commandments—never ceases to enrich both with spiritual and with temporal gifts destined unto life eternal.
Since, however, at times during harvest it may happen that husbandmen, whether on account of preceding or impending foul weather, cannot without grave damage leave off work in the fields: then upon the conclusion of divine service in Church, especially after the sacrifice of the Liturgy, they may indeed perform the said works, but this only in extreme necessity, and always according to the direction and judgment of their own Pastor.
Finally, let pastors endeavor to instruct the people that on a feast day also in the event of a fair (market day) they are bound to hear divine service.
As to the patronal feast in particular, let Pastors endeavor that the same, so far as possible, be celebrated in the most solemn manner, arousing especially their faithful unto the reception of the Holy Mysteries of Confession and Communion; and unto this end let them invite neighboring pastors that they may assist them in that matter, of whom one of the more capable and zealous let them not forget to invite to preach the sermon.

### Chapter VII: On the Veneration of Saints, Relics, and Images (Icons)

1. Upon the mystical firmament of the Church, beside the greater luminary, which is Christ the Lord, God hath set

also a lesser luminary, which is the Most Pure Virgin Mary, and the stars, that is, His Saints, so that throughout the entire night of this age, shining with the radiance of their holiness, they might dispel sinful darkness. As to the Most Blessed Mother of our Savior, although Her veneration is so intensely developed in our Church that almost on every page in divine worship Her commemoration occurs, nevertheless the Synod hath it supremely at heart that Her veneration shall ever increase also among us; and therefore pastors shall never cease, in their ancient zeal, to elevate the same daily, whether through sermons concerning Her, or through the establishment of pious brotherhoods to be founded under Her name, or through the solemnity of Her feasts and the frequent reception of the Holy Mysteries at Her solemnities. As to the veneration of the Saints, since, as the Apostle saith, *"one star differs from another star,"* although all the Saints before God are great, nevertheless some are more glorious than others; and that among them Saint Joseph, Spouse of the Most Pure Virgin, especially distinguishes himself, we hold as instructed by the most solemn authority of the Vicar of Christ upon earth; for this Saint by decrees both of Pius IX of blessed memory and of his glorious successor Leo XIII was established Patron of the Universal Church. Therefore also this Synod, promising unto itself renowned spiritual blessings from the patronage of this Saint, desires by the mercy of God to receive Him as the special Patron of the Ruthenian Church [^141].

Unto the veneration of Saint Joseph, Spouse of the Most Pure Virgin Mary, there ought to be joined also the veneration of the most glorious parents of that same Most Blessed Virgin, Saints Joachim and Anna, which likewise through the decrees of the Supreme Pontiffs shines ever more brightly, and whose commemoration among us is celebrated with special divine service again and again. Pastors, therefore, shall endeavor also to cultivate the veneration of these Saints ever more ardently by joining sermons thereto upon given opportunity, and on the days dedicated unto their commemoration, to sing the Divine Liturgy.

Since, however, the Church consists of all generations, tongues, and nations, and all Her members, together with Christ their Head, constitute Her great and glorious Body, which is ever vivified by the very same life of the Holy Spirit; therefore the number of Saints in the Church of God hath increased from Her very beginning unto this day, and until the end of the ages shall not cease to increase. Therefore, as before the schism of the Eastern Church between both Churches the Saints of the Eastern Church found veneration in the Western Church and vice versa, so also now after the schism of the Greeks, when our Church, by virtue of the Union with the Mother of all Churches, constitutes one Body with the Catholic Church, therefore the Synod decrees in the Lord, that both for obtaining unto ourselves intercession before God of those Saints, and for demonstrating the profession of our Catholicity, our Ruthenian Church hath recognized and received also all those Saints who, being solemnly canonized by the Holy Apostolic See, are venerated with special divine worship in the Roman Church.

2. Finally, unto a particularly special veneration of Ruthenians those Saints justly have a right who in our conversion and in our liturgical language bore the greatest labors, namely: the Holy Brothers Cyril and Methodius; as well as those who from our own lineage have long since attained heavenly glory, and are venerated with ecclesiastical service, namely: Saint Vladimir the Great and his two sons, the Martyrs Boris and Gleb; Anthony and Theodosius of the Caves; Saint Paraskeva; and Saint Josaphat, in recent times inscribed into the book of Saints by the Apostolic See and presented unto the whole universal Catholic Church for celebration. Namely, as to the Holy Hieromartyr Josaphat, who sealed with his blood the defense of the Holy Union, the Synod hath resolved to transfer his service from September 16 unto October 31 (November 12), upon which day he was crowned with martyrdom; while the commemoration of our Holy Apostles Cyril and Methodius, from June 23 (July 5)—whereon His Holiness Leo XIII by a special encyclical letter unto the Catholics of the whole world given on September 30, 1880, prescribed their commemoration to be celebrated by the universal Church—shall be transferred unto the nearest Sunday, whether preceding or following, because on the very day of June 23

(July 5) among us there already occurs a service, and that a Polyeleos service, of Saint Peter of Mount Athos.

3. As to Holy Relics, the Synod especially decrees that pastors, having duly investigated their identity, either by the authentic document attached unto them, or at least by the attached seal of some person from the Treasury of Relics of the City of Rome, or by the certification (of identity) of an Ordinariate, shall treat them, as more precious than all pearls, with due reverence and render them worthy honor, especially if they proceed from the body of a Saint which was a living temple of the Holy Spirit and an instrument of so many glorious deeds, keeping well in mind that these relics belong unto those who among the souls destined by God for eternal life have in an extraordinary manner shone forth with virtues unto the whole world. Therefore, if in Churches such relics are found, pastors shall not permit that they remain in an undignified place or in a corner, but shall expose them in Church for the general veneration of the people; exposing the same especially upon certain festive occasions upon the altar, or in some other becoming place, and that having lighted candles around them, and offering them for kissing unto the people approaching with reverence.

4. If ever in their Churches they should find any relics whereof the authenticity is unknown unto them, they are bound to submit them unto the respective Ordinariate and await the judgment thereof. Moreover, they shall not permit any lay person to touch them, but they themselves, at least vested in an epitrachelion, shall bring them unto the altar and carry them unto their own proper treasury, neat and becoming. Since at times it happens that in Churches there are found Antimensia worn out by age, which in their center, as if upon the altar, still contain relics of Holy Martyrs embedded, then if they no longer serve for the use of the Church, they shall be sent unto the Ordinariate, or those old ones shall be replaced by new ones. Those Antimensia, no less than the holy relics themselves, they shall not permit lay persons to touch; they shall not give them unto them for washing, and they themselves shall not dare to wash them, and that all the more because, beside the holy relics embedded therein, they are anointed with Holy Myron, which also lay persons are not permitted to touch, nor is it lawful for anyone whatsoever to wash them.

Whenever necessity arises for the celebration of the Divine Liturgy to place the Antimension between the altar cloths, which often need to be changed, the priest himself shall do this, and not anyone else from the laity serving at the altar.

5. Since man is most moved by things falling under the senses, and through things visible is elevated unto things invisible, it corresponds unto this disposition of his that he should have before his eyes the images of those who by their life and heavenly communion have expressed upon themselves the image of the Heavenly Father. When this custom of venerating holy icons was once opposed by some, they were condemned as heretics by the Seventh Ecumenical Council, and thereafter the veneration of holy icons spread further; and for the witnessing of that dogma, Holy Icons were from that time placed in Greek Churches upon the wall (wooden) for universal veneration, and unto this day occupy under the name of the Iconostasis in our Churches the most important and prominent place. Those images on the Iconostasis, however, must not be just any images and in just any order, placed according to whim, but in such wise that on the lower tier the two principal ones, namely: of the Divine Savior and of the Most Pure Virgin Mary, shall occupy on the one and the other side the first place—the Savior on the right, and the Most Pure Virgin on the left—and beside them there follow, according to greater or lesser space, others of other Saints, especially of the Temple Patron; on the upper tier there shall be arranged in twelve compartments the images of the twelve principal feasts, both Dominical and Theotokian, and in the center the image of the Mystical Supper of the Lord. Above them, the icons of the twelve Apostles, and in their midst Christ the Savior; upon the highest tier, of the Prophets, and in their midst may be placed the image of the Most Blessed Virgin Mary; at the very summit, the Crucifixion between the Most Pure Virgin Mary and Saint John the Theologian. Following therefore that ancient custom of the Church, we command that nowhere such Iconostases shall be lacking in our Churches, as of great importance for the exact celebration of the rites of our order; and moreover we prescribe that the icons therein shall be arranged not in any random manner and not just any, but according to the method indicated above, and those of which we made mention above. Moreover, all those icons shall be painted

according to the style of our Church, corresponding unto the Eastern. Moreover, the custom of our Churches shall be preserved, that is, to keep upon the Tetrapod with the image of the Holy Cross also the Temple Patronal Icon of the Church, and that for kissing by the people coming unto the church. In order that this icon, through frequent kissing, may not be effaced, as happens in some places, it will be appropriate to protect it with glass.

6. As to the other icons placed in church by the faithful out of devotion, the Synod commands above all that they shall not be exposed for public veneration without the prior consent and approval of the respective pastor; and we obligate pastors that they shall not accept any whatsoever without distinction, and shall never permit the introduction of those painted unseemingly or contrary to church style, and finally they shall not allow that they be displayed in places less suitable for veneration. We also approve portable icons for carrying, which maidens are accustomed to carry upon inserted transverse staves during the most solemn processions; nevertheless we desire that pastors shall see to it that in the adornment of those icons nothing unbecoming shall be found, and that nothing shall be introduced that would display a worldly or frivolous taste according to the conceit of the female mind, which may especially occur with statues; and at the same time that the maidens carrying the icon shall be dressed becomingly indeed, but without luxury, and shall have their heads covered with a white veil (*namitka*).

7. Where finally there should be found in Churches any old icons no longer serving for ecclesiastical use, pastors shall not allow them to lie in the dust, but shall send them unto the Ordinariate, because it happens very frequently that among them there are found some distinguished either by antiquity or by art, and in that case their Churches may obtain some compensation therefor; and moreover such sacred monuments may be preserved as a memorial as well as for the further cultivation of church style. As to the form of the Holy Cross which is customarily erected in public places, pastors and faithful shall conform unto the prescription of the Sacred Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith issued on May 29, 1887, No. 5, and promulgated

throughout our entire province, and specifically in the Archeparchy of Lviv on December 4, 1887, No. 358 ord. [^142]

### Chapter VIII: On Holy Processions, Pilgrimages, and Missions

1. That holy processions, which at times take place in our churches, since they are but tokens and incentives of the spirit of devotion and religion, as also of the triumph of Christ the Lord over the prince of this world, ought to be highly esteemed, no one can doubt, whensoever only they are undertaken and performed in a becoming manner. Most of all, therefore, the pastor shall ensure that during a procession with the Most Holy Gifts, people who do not walk in the procession, but stand here and there, shall during the passing of the Most Holy Gifts bend both knees and fall unto the earth with reverence.

The Holy Gifts ought to be carried beneath a Baldachin, surrounded by lighted candles, and unceasingly accompanied by incensation from the front, either by deacons, if there be such, or at least by one of the most worthy servants of the Church, the so-called trustees (*provizory*). As to the priest himself carrying the Most Holy Gifts, it is required that he be vested in all sacred vestments and with head uncovered.

2. As to the manner and order of the procession, the following shall be observed: in front let the cross-bearer proceed with the image of the Crucifixion turned toward the way, that is, forward, which again will be all the more becoming if the cross shall be borne between two candle-bearers. Since the representation of the cross is an image of the exaltation of Christ, who thereby accomplished the salvation of the world, therefore unto the carrying thereof there shall not be assigned the lowest persons among the people, as at times happens, but with that honor shall be distinguished the more honorable and prominent. After the cross let there follow, arranged in two rows, those bearing the banners or holy flags (*khoruhvy*); after them let maidens follow with

their icon; after them let the brotherhoods follow, then the cantors; after them, likewise in two rows, the clerics, if there be such; then priests in Epitrachelia, immediately after them others in Phelonia, and at the end the principal celebrant himself, surrounded by the people.

3. If a circuit takes place around the Church, then, having exited from it, it turns immediately rightward following the sun toward the west, and having circumnavigated the Church, returns unto it from the other side.

In order to attain the purpose of such processions, it is necessary that all, and especially the clerics, and before all the presiding priest, shall walk with spirit directed unto God, and by the exterior posture of the body give an edifying example unto the beholders; all shall walk modestly, singing together with the people hymns more suited unto the character of the procession.

4. That pilgrimages which are undertaken unto holy places renowned for miracles and endowed with indulgences from the Apostolic See, if they are undertaken as is fitting, are salvific for the faithful, everyone knows; so that God Himself, knowing well the disposition of the human spirit, already in the Old Testament prescribed the same unto His chosen people, commanding that every male from all the land of Israel should appear three times before Him in the Temple at Jerusalem; and a visible example of the observance of such a commandment was left unto all the faithful also by the Holy Family itself, as we read in the Gospel [^143].

5. That those pilgrimages may be undertaken as is fitting, it is necessary to keep before eyes the warnings which unto that end above all regarding pastors the Metropolitan Gregory Yakhymovych of blessed memory prescribed to be observed in his Pastoral Letter of December 24, 1861, wherein, among other things, the following ought to be mentioned: "In particular, as to pilgrimages and the visiting of churches endowed with indulgences, we have no reason to oppose them, if they are undertaken for the consolation of the soul—if only a time be chosen free from parochial duties, and if they be not frequent, and the absence of the pastor from his parish do not exceed the time of three days; and during them it is necessary

to observe modesty and decorum becoming the priestly state, enjoined upon all the honorable Clergy by the local circular of July 16, 1825, No. 2723; further, no priest shall dare arbitrarily and without the knowledge, and much less against the will of the respective local pastor, to meddle in the celebration of sacred Rites there, or to administer any assistance unto the faithful gathered there; but when invited, he is bound gladly to render brotherly assistance, far removed, however, from any mercenary gain and suspicion of filthy lucre."

6. As to the faithful, the following admonitions shall be observed: 1) that they themselves through absence shall not neglect the pressing duties of their state and calling; 2) that pilgrimages be undertaken with a proper intention, and not for idle curiosity; 3) that they shall not waste the time of pilgrimage thither and back in jests, drunkenness in taverns, thefts, shameless deeds, and other similar sins; 4) that in the very place unto which the pilgrimage is undertaken, they shall pour forth prayers, listen with all attention unto sermons, and worthily receive the Mysteries of Penance and the Holy Eucharist; and finally, 5) that pilgrimages shall not take place unto the sanctuaries of non-Catholics, such as, among others, at present are the sanctuaries of Pochaiv and Suceava, whereof the former namely, once belonging unto the Catholic Church, is now annexed unto the Church of the Russian Empire, separated from the Catholic Church [^144].

If such pilgrimages take place in a body by the people gathered together, it would be well that their leader should be the pastor himself, so that beneath his guidance abuses which might easily occur may be prevented; if, however, that should be impossible for the pastor, then let him appoint as leader of the pilgrimage some man of proven conduct and exceptional prudence, whom he shall fittingly instruct concerning the duty entrusted unto him.

7. As to the places of pilgrimage themselves, their superiors or pastors shall endeavor to invite unto their Church a sufficient number of priests, especially such as they deem most capable of hearing confessions,

so that through a lack of such, the faithful may not be deprived of the Holy Mysteries, the reception whereof is the chief instrument of their sanctification; moreover they shall invite one or another, burning with greater zeal, for sermons. Nevertheless, pastors of holy places shall take good care that the revenues gathered from such solemnities, especially those wherewith is attached the obligation of celebrating Divine Liturgies or another divine service, they shall not retain entirely for themselves, but shall make partakers thereof also those whose participation with them in labors they experience, laying upon them the obligation of satisfying the intentions of the donors. For in this manner, on the one hand, the superiors of the places themselves will preserve the peace of their own conscience, manifesting a spirit entirely far removed from greed for material gain, and will contribute, according unto the duty and purpose of their calling, unto the glory of God and the salvation of souls; while on the other hand, by this conduct of theirs they will inspire both priests and faithful not only unto greater zeal in celebrating there pious exercises and devotions, but also unto a greater frequentation of the wonderworking shrines, namely in that they will admire the very harmonious order in these holy places, as well as the zealous activity of the sacred ministers, especially when their chief desire, which is the reception of the Holy Mysteries, is fully satisfied, and they will not be constrained, as in some places happens, to return home with grief of heart without that religious consolation.

8. Pastors of holy places are here admonished that they shall accurately record the obligations of intentions together with their purpose in books expressly prepared for this, sitting either in the narthex or in some place near the Church; and if the pastors should be so greatly occupied in Church that it would be impossible for them personally to fulfill that duty, they are bound in their stead to appoint some other priest, or in default thereof, a cleric, but never, generally speaking, any layman, and much less a woman. If anything of the kind should happen, then the faithful would become alienated from pious offerings, suspecting that those offerings might perhaps be diverted unto some other purpose.

9. Finally, superiors of holy places shall endeavor that the faithful coming in great numbers shall have places where they may take shelter from inclement weather, or spend the night between the eve and the feast day, so that they may not be constrained to wander about taverns and Jewish lodgings. Furthermore, we charge the conscience of superiors that, whether in Church or around it, they shall especially not permit anything to be done that would be contrary unto sobriety or the gravity of the Christian faith, or might afford anyone an occasion of scandal or cause for the condemnation of pious Christian institutions.

10. That the most powerful means for the sanctification of the faithful are also parish missions is manifest unto everyone; therefore Saint Alphonsus expresses himself concerning those who did not hesitate to declare that missions bring more harm than benefit, thus: "Those who say this, say it because they have no experience in missions, and therefore do not understand that great winning of souls which through them takes place for God. Whosoever hath experienced it knows well how many inveterate enmities are removed, how many evil customs are uprooted, how many restitutions take place, how many disputes (which later become a hotbed of hatreds) are assuaged, and above all, how many badly made confessions are rectified; especially throughout villages and smaller localities, where in that respect missions are not only profitable as in large cities, but may even be called necessary, because in such localities all the inhabitants know one another, whence it not infrequently happens that they are subsequently ashamed to reveal their sins unto the confessors of the village... It is true also that many after missions fall anew, but would God grant that all who once convert might remain in grace even unto death, never falling anew! Such is human infirmity, that many obtain the grace of God, and then lose it again. But even were nothing else attained, it is certain that during missions at least a multitude of sins is prevented. Furthermore, on account of missions many souls return unto God and remain in His grace even unto death; and many, even though they fall anew, remain (therein) at least for

several months, and in every case during a mission they conceive a greater fear of sin and acquire a greater knowledge of God and of the great importance of their eternal salvation" [^145].

11. Although among us hitherto missions have been employed for the people especially unto the end that the faithful might turn away from the sin of drunkenness, which indeed is exceedingly commendable, nevertheless it cannot be doubted that it is exceedingly profitable to conduct them also for the extirpation of other vices according unto the need of the localities; therefore in missions sacred discourses ought not always to be restricted merely unto the vice of drunkenness alone, but shall be extended also unto other vices.

12. The subject and distribution of those discourses shall be especially those truths of our faith which are designated by the name of the "last things," as also the heinousness of mortal sin, which ought to be arranged in the form of meditation, and interwoven also with other discourses and catechetical teachings, particularly concerning the principal articles of faith, confession, and Holy Communion. Here especially the Synod deems it its duty to direct the attention of pastors unto the utility of missions, and unto that end submits unto them above all that which was cited above from Saint Alphonsus, and then observes the following: People are far less moved by ordinary things unto doing good than by extraordinary things; therefore it is necessary from time to time to propose unto them extraordinary things, that they may be moved unto good; and since it is known that it commonly happens that the faithful, not daring at times to reveal certain things unto their own pastors at confession, being shaken by extraordinary sermons concerning the fearful truths of our holy religion, are accustomed to confess unto strange priests with full liberty and true contrition, therefore it follows clearly from this that the organizing of missions is an exceedingly salvific work.

13. The whole art, therefore, for attaining salvific fruit from missions consists in a good arrangement of exercises

and in the selection of priests capable thereof; therefore the local pastor himself who desires to arrange a mission in his parish shall, with the counsel of pious and prudent priests, draw up before all a plan thereof, and designate the hours of the day whereon what is to take place; then through his deanery office transmit that plan thus composed, together with the opinion of the priests invited unto the work of the mission, unto the Ordinariate for the approval thereof, requesting from it at the same time extraordinary faculties for the confessors, as well as a blessing for the successful outcome of the mission. Having received all this, he shall announce unto the people the time whereon the mission is to begin and end, moving the same by suitable means unto the celebration of the mission with a well-disposed spirit, on which occasion he shall expound unto them also the order and time both of divine services, and of sermons and confessions. When the mission shall already be conducted, he shall endeavor, among other things, to arrange that during sermons confessors shall cease hearing confessions, as also that in participation therein (in the mission) the faithful of that parish shall have the first place, because the mission was arranged especially for his own parishioners. Unto the priests invited for conducting the mission, he shall set forth the principal failings of his parish, so that they may direct the entire weight of their discourses and admonitions, both outside and inside confession, with all force against them. Since, however, it cannot be denied that in the missions undertaken among us against drunkenness priests have labored with fortunate success, so that entire villages began completely to abstain from intoxicating drinks, and for perseverance in their resolution very many enrolled in the society of temperance, chiefly on account of the spiritual graces—indulgences—wherewith the same were endowed by Pius IX of blessed memory; nevertheless, since in that direction much labor is still necessary so that this error may be further eradicated, and that the temperate may be strengthened in their resolution, we admonish pastors in the Lord that they shall not cease henceforth from their labors, "for virtue is constancy in the good" [^146].

14. Unto that end further, the ordinances and instructions given unto us by His Excellency Metropolitan Joseph Sembratovych in his pastoral letter both of April 3, 1874 under the title: "On the High Dignity of Man," as also of April 6, 1878, No. 7628, will most serve; these instructions pastors must keep before their eyes.

15. In order that in this so salvific work a certain permanent method may be established also for the future, the Synod decrees that instead of one of the clerical conferences (*soborchyky*) which are customarily held annually in each deanery, there shall be conducted in a certain parish of each deanery one mission, where precisely, upon mutual consultation of the pastors of that deanery, the dean shall assign the holding of such a mission, having regard unto greater need. Upon the conclusion of such a mission the dean is bound annually to transmit a detailed report thereof unto the Ordinariate.

### Chapter IX: On Domestic Worship

1. Every family represents in its own way the Church of Christ, the head whereof is the father of the family himself, who as a Christian man, animated by living faith, ought to govern the entire family, not only in matters pertaining unto temporal life, but still more in those which relate unto eternal life, as the Apostle admonishes: *"If any provide not for his own, and specially for those of his own house, he hath denied the faith, and is worse than an infidel"* [^147]. Although this relates unto all fathers of families, yet most of all unto spiritual fathers of families, such as pastors are in their own homes: *"for a bishop must be such,"*—saith the holy Apostle—*"one that rules well his own house, having his children in subjection with all gravity. For if a man know not how to rule his own house, how shall he take care of the church of God?"* [^148]

2. Since, however, discourse here turns upon divine worship, fathers of families must watch that not only their domestic servants, but still more their own children, by their instruction and example, shall render unto God at the appointed times due honor.

3. In particular, as to the adornment of the house, especially of a priest, it ought to breathe holiness and the manifestation of faith, before all in the choice of holy images which are customarily hung upon the walls; from among which all images representing vanity ought to be removed; and if at times certain images that are not sacred are retained, they must not be merely such as would adorn rooms, and let them not be such as would in any way offend devotion or Christian modesty or religious feeling. For we are bound wholly and constantly to adhere unto the examples of our ancestors, who in all their deeds were distinguished by a character of religiousness, and never ought we to degenerate from them, lest we gradually be deprived of the so precious gifts of the Holy Spirit, and appear to be filled with a purely worldly spirit. Indeed it is to be lamented that in these our times it gains strength with impunity, removing everything that is Christian, both from whole societies and families, and also from individual persons, so that we must fear an advance not only unto religious indifference, but even unto the destruction of all religious profession.

4. Beside this, the father of the family shall insist that under his leadership all members of the family in the morning and evening, before eating and after eating, shall devoutly recite prayers. *"Make thy home a church,"* saith Saint John Chrysostom, *"for thou hast to give an answer both for the salvation of thy children and of thy servants; and even as an answer will be required of us for you, so an answer will be demanded of each of you for the servant, for the wife, and for the child"* [^149].

5. Moreover, in all his deeds a Christian father of a family ought unto all his household to show

himself as an example of heavenly life, so that *"for Christ's sake and for the eternal salvation of all his own he may admonish, teach, command, show favor, and exercise discipline; thus shall he fulfill in his own house an ecclesiastical and, as it were, episcopal duty, serving Christ, that he may be with Him forever"* [^150].

6. In addition, that the worship of God in the family may ever more expand, pastors before all are bound to take care that, according unto their power, they shall in the observance of such domestic worship give in all things and constantly an example worthy of imitation unto the people committed unto them; for if the neglect of such matters in the house of a layman awakens astonishment, how much more when this is observed in the house of a priest, according unto the teaching of Saint Gregory the Great: *"The conduct of the prelate ought so much to excel the conduct of the people as the life of the shepherd is wont to be distinguished from the flock... after which thought the people are called a flock"* [^151].
