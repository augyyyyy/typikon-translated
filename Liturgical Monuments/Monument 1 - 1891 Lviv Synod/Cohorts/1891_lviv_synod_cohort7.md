# Acts and Decrees of the Ruthenian Provincial Synod of Lviv (1891)
## Cohort 7: Physical Pages 121–140 (Leaves p121–p140 / Book pp. 119–136)

> [!NOTE]
> **Historical & Canonical Significance**  
> Cohort 9 of the **1891 Lviv Provincial Synod** (*Чинности и рѣшеня руского провинціяльного Собора въ Галичинѣ ôтбувшого ся во Львовѣ въ роцѣ 1891*) establishes fundamental canonical, liturgical, hymnographic, and rubrical legislation for public worship in the Ruthenian Greek Catholic Church:  
> 1. **Conclusion of Titulus IV, Chapter III: On the Canonical Hours (The Church Rule) and Other Public Divine Services (Leaves p121–p122 / Book pp. 119–120)**:  
>    - **Parochial and Cathedral Norms for the Divine Office**: Reception of the eight parts of the Byzantine Divine Office (*Pravylo Tserkovne*); distinction between monastics (full public recitation of all hours), cathedral chapters (public celebration of Vespers, Matins, and 1st Hour daily, with Compline in Great Lent), and parish churches (public Matins or All-Night Vigil with 1st Hour, and Sunday afternoon Vespers); private recitation of remaining hours according to the official abbreviated model (*skorochenyi zrazok*) approved by the Holy See; mortal sin attaching to negligent clerical omission of the Office; patristic and conciliar theology of clerical recitation (Fifth Lateran Council canon *Dolentes*, St. Alphonsus Liguori, St. John Cassian, St. Cyprian of Carthage).  
> 2. **Titulus IV, Chapter IV: On the Celebration of Extraordinary Services (Leaves p123–p133 / Book pp. 121–129)**:  
>    - **Immovable Feasts**: Strict rubrics for the Exaltation of the Precious Cross (Sept 14) and Maccabees (Aug 1) with veneration upon the Tetrapod; Royal Hours on Christmas/Theophany eves; Great Water Blessing on Theophany Eve and Feast Day; Lesser Water Blessing and herbal blessings on Aug 1.  
>    - **Moveable Feasts & Holy Week**: Cross-Veneration Sunday (mid-Lent); Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete on Thursday of the 5th Week with single prostrations per troparion; Lazarus Saturday sung Liturgy; Palm Sunday blessing of palms; Presanctified Liturgies on Great Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday; Great Thursday Vesperal Liturgy and 12 Passion Gospels; Great Friday Royal Hours, midday Vespers with deposition of the *Plashchanytsia* into the *Tomb* (*Hrob*) before the Iconostasis, Passion sermons, and evening *Burial Matins* (*Utrenia nadhrobna*); Great Saturday St. Basil Vesperal Liturgy; Paschal Matins early Sunday morning (or Saturday evening in cathedral/urban use); Bright Week daily cycles.  
>    - **Feast of the Most Holy Eucharist (Corpus Christi)**: Reception of the bull *Transiturus* of Pope Urban IV (1264) and Trent Sess. 13, c. 5; full 8-day celebration (*cum octava*) throughout the Ruthenian Church and Sister Eastern Catholic Churches (Italo-Greeks, Melkites, Armenians, Maronites, Syrians, Chaldeans, Copts); Sunday transfer with outdoor 4-altar procession or threefold church circuit; four Eucharistic Gospels.  
>    - **Eucharistic Exposition & Benediction**: Tabernacle (*Kyvot*) summit exposition; rubrical reverences (avoiding turning back on the Sacrament, sideways turns, *metania* to the ground, facing altar at ablutions); single priestly blessing versus threefold episcopal blessing; singing of the *Supplication* (*Syplikatsiya* / *Tantum Ergo* / *O Salutaris* equivalents); requirements of at least six burning wax candles; the *Melchizedek* (lunette custodial insert) pre-fitted and wiped; exposition in the Tomb on Great Friday and Holy Saturday with *Plashchanytsia* circuits.  
>    - **Paraliturgical Devotions**: Strict regulation of the *Order of the Akathist* (encouraging popular participation, moderate tempo, full singing, stipend pooling to 40 cents, prohibiting substitution for preaching or catechesis); *Order of the Paraklesis* (supplicatory moleben to the Mother of God, 50-cent stole fee, strict prohibition against truncations or abbreviations).  
> 3. **Titulus IV, Chapter V: On Church Singing (Leaves p133–p135 / Book pp. 129–131)**:  
>    - **Theology of Vocal Praise**: Total exclusion of musical instruments, exalting the living human voice; patristic foundations (St. Augustine's *Confessions*, St. Jerome on Ephesians 5:19, Psalm 46:8 LXX); threefold genre hierarchy: congregational chant by ear (*z slukhu*), liturgical *Heirmologion* chant, and figured choral chant (*fihuralne*, restricted to solemn high Liturgies); mandatory Heirmologion training in Seminaries; preparation of an official provincial chant manual; certification and examination of cantors (*dyaky*); episcopal and parochial duty of cantor sustenance; canonical preservation of Ukrainian Christmas carols (*Koliady*), retaining ancient rhythm and language against unauthorized modern innovations.  
> 4. **Titulus IV, Chapter VI: On the Celebration of Feast Days (Leaves p136–p140 / Book pp. 132–136)**:  
>    - **Theology and Sanctification of Festivals**: Divine origin of the Sabbath and Sunday substitution as the second creation/Resurrection; patristic theology of festival rest (St. John Chrysostom, St. Augustine); strict obligation of hearing the entire Divine Liturgy under pain of mortal sin; severe condemnation of Sunday abuses (promenading outside, conversing, leaving before the final blessing/thanksgiving, comparing premature leavers to Judas Iscariot); parochial duty to provide Vespers and catechesis or cantor-led devotions in secondary filial churches; prohibition of sinful tavern dances, drunkenness, and dissolute songs; exact canonical calculation of feast day rest (midnight to midnight); limited harvest dispensation (*slota*) under pastoral discretion; mandatory Sunday/feast public announcements from the ambo.

3. The ordinance of the Church to celebrate publicly (with the whole people) all the above-mentioned Canonical Hours (the Church Rule) hath commendably always been observed in our Monasteries, which shall also be preserved in the future. In secular Churches, however, that celebration hath fallen under a certain limitation, since present custom permitteth that daily there be celebrated publicly in cathedral Churches: Vespers, Matins, and the First Hour, while the remaining three Hours as well as Compline (which, however, during the Great Forty Days [Lent] is celebrated also in Church publicly) and the Midnight Office are left unto private recitation. In parochial Churches, however, only on Sundays and feast days are there celebrated by custom Matins or the All-Night Vigil with the First Hour in the morning, and a second Vespers in the afternoon. That same custom, adopted among us of old, pastors shall strictly observe; the remaining parts of divine worship they shall recite privately, and that according to the abbreviated model composed by the Lviv Liturgical Commission and approved by the Holy Apostolic See. That manner of reciting the liturgical orders privately priests are bound to observe also on all weekdays whereon customarily no divine service is celebrated in Church.

4. Every celebration of divine services in Church priests shall perform accurately and according to the model prescribed by the present Synod, which the Most Reverend Metropolitan shall also endeavor to print and communicate unto the other Right Reverend Bishops, so that at one and the same time it may be introduced into use by all throughout our entire ecclesiastical province; to the end that in the public celebration of divine worship there may be no divergence whatsoever, but as the Apostle admonisheth: *"Let all things be done decently and in order"* [^123] and that all may present an image of celestial harmony.

5. As to the actual celebration of the Canonical Hours (the Church Rule) in particular, let priests and deacons understand well that these are not ordinary prayers, but rather

*(Physical Page 122 / Leaf p122 / Book Page 120)*

public ones, and that offered by sacred ministers in the name of the Church, fulfilling an embassy before God on behalf of the people; wherefore also their omission, proceeding from negligence and sloth, is a mortal sin, and that not only when an entire service is omitted, or any considerable part, but also whatever integral part thereof, even were it one of the eight above-mentioned. Wherefore, whatever opinion there may hitherto have been regarding the obligation of reciting that Rule, now, by the decree of the Synod, diversity of opinions shall cease, since the obligation is clearly and expressly laid upon ordained clerics, especially upon approval by the Holy See.

6. Since according to the teaching of St. Alphonsus: *"By the Divine Office God is honored, resistance is offered unto the assaults of enemies, and divine mercy is implored for sinners,"* [^124] and for the attainment of these ends, as this Saint further admonisheth, it is necessary to recite the Church Rule in a proper manner; therefore, that this may be realized, it is incumbent that priests recite the same, according to the words of the Fifth Lateran Council in the renowned canon *Dolentes*, attentively and devoutly. *"Attentively"*—that is, by pronouncing distinctly all the words, and not merely running through them in thought. *"Devoutly"*—that is, with attention; that which is uttered by the lips must be found in the heart, [^125] as if conversing with the Lord and praising Him together with His Angels. For in the contrary case St. Cyprian admonisheth: *"How canst thou demand to be heard of God, when thou hearest not thyself?"* [^126] Therefore—concludeth St. Alphonsus:—*"Prayer performed with attention is sweet-smelling incense, which is exceedingly pleasing unto God and bringeth forth treasures of grace; but prayer performed with voluntary distraction is a foul-smelling smoke wherewith God is disgusted and which draweth down punishments."* [^127]

### Chapter IV: On the Celebration of Extraordinary Services, Whereof Some are Bound unto Immovable Feasts, and Others unto Moveable Feasts

*(Physical Page 123 / Leaf p123 / Book Page 121)*

*As to Services bound unto immovable feasts:*

#### I.

1. On the Feast of the Exaltation of the Precious Cross, on the 14th day of September, pastors are bound to bring forth the Holy Cross for the veneration of the people with all the rites prescribed in the Typikon of that day, and having thus brought it forth and placed it upon the Tetrapod before the Iconostasis, to retain it there until the Apodosis (*Otdaniye*) of the Feast; and if during that time any divine service should take place in Church, then upon the conclusion of that service veneration shall take place during the singing of the Troparion: *"We bow down before Thy Cross, O Master, and we glorify Thy holy Resurrection."*

2. In the months of December and January, on the Eve or on the Friday before the Eve of the Nativity of Christ and Theophany, according to the directions of the Typikon, pastors shall celebrate in Church the Special Hours, commonly called the *Royal Hours*, with the addition of the Order of the *Typika* (*Izobrazitelni*). In particular, as to the Feast of Theophany, they shall celebrate the Blessing of Water: once on the Eve of the Feast upon the conclusion of Vespers, and then on the very day of the Feast more solemnly after the Prayer behind the Ambo.

#### II.

3. On the 1st day of August, on the Feast of the Holy Maccabean Brethren, the Holy Cross shall be brought forth for the veneration of the people, and shall lie upon the Tetrapod for one day, observing all the rites as at the Exaltation of the Precious Cross, with the exception of the rite of the actual elevation (*vozdvyzhaniye*) toward the four corners of the earth, which is proper unto the Feast of the Exaltation. Moreover, they shall also on that day celebrate the Lesser Blessing of Water,

*(Physical Page 124 / Leaf p124 / Book Page 122)*

and where such custom existeth, they shall bless herbs and seeds.

*As to Services bound unto moveable feasts, the same pastors are bound:*

1. In the midst of the Great Forty Days [Lent], namely on the Third Sunday, called *Cross-Veneration Sunday* (*Krestopoklonna*), to bring forth the Holy Cross for the veneration of the people with all the rites as on the 1st day of August; the same Holy Cross shall remain upon the Tetrapod until the Friday following that Sunday, for then it shall be removed during the Sixth Hour where the same is celebrated, or if not, then before the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts.

2. On the Thursday after the Fourth Sunday of Lent, to celebrate in Church Matins with the Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete and the prostrations joined thereto, yet so that instead of the three prostrations which are prescribed after each troparion, there shall be one only.

3. The liturgical orders which begin together with our Triodion called the *Flowery* (*Tsvitna* / *Pentecostarion*), up to the Sunday of the Resurrection of Christ, pastors shall celebrate daily in Church, and that in the following manner:
a) On the Saturday before Palm Sunday, called *Lazarus Saturday*, let them celebrate in Church a sung Divine Liturgy.
b) On Palm Sunday (*Nedilia Vayiy / Tsvitna*), let them celebrate all things according to custom, as is prescribed for a *Feast of the Lord* of the first class, adding moreover the blessing of palms (*vitiy*) during Matins.
c) On the three subsequent days of Holy (Great) Week, they shall celebrate the Liturgies of the Presanctified Gifts.
d) On Great and Holy Thursday of Passion Week, they shall sing the Divine Liturgy together with Vespers.
e) On the same Thursday after sunset, they shall chant the Office of the Holy Passions with the 12 Gospels.
f) On Great Friday, which is regarded as an extraordinary solemn feast, they shall celebrate in Church in the morning the Hours commonly called *Royal*, and around midday Vespers, at which is carried forth the holy icon called the *Plashchanytsia*, which, once carried forth before the Iconostasis, is placed in the place

*(Physical Page 125 / Leaf p125 / Book Page 123)*

called the *Tomb* (*Hrob*), and here let it remain for the continual veneration of the people until the celebration of the Resurrection, and that strictly according to the prescriptions of the Typikon which this Synod hath endeavored to compile.
g) It would be exceedingly desirable that pastors on that same day, after the laying down of the *Plashchanytsia*, at a suitable hour, should deliver an instruction unto the people concerning the Passions and death of Christ.
h) It is likewise commended unto the same pastors that they celebrate the *Burial Matins* (*Utrenia nadhrobna*) in Church on that Friday after sunset, on account of the distinctiveness of the divine service of this day and the solemnity of the rites taking place at the conclusion thereof.
i) On Great and Holy Saturday itself they shall sing the Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great with Vespers.
k) On the Sunday of the Resurrection, they shall chant very early in the morning Matins with all the rites both accompanying and preceding the same, and that according to the Typikon approved by this Synod. In cathedral Churches, as also in other non-cathedral Churches, the custom hath prevailed that the same Matins be taken beforehand, so that it is celebrated on Saturday evening, which custom, as the Synod judgeth, may be retained.
l) On the first three days of this week, called *Bright Week* (*Svitla*), they shall daily sing in Church Matins, the Divine Liturgy, and Vespers.

#### III.

m) On the very day of Pentecost, pastors shall celebrate immediately after the Divine Liturgy Vespers with the Kneeling Prayers (*Kolinopryklonni Molytvy*), according to the rule indicated in the typikons.
n) As to the Feast of the Most Holy Eucharist in particular, although the memory of its institution is celebrated year by year on Great Thursday at the Mystical Supper of the Lord, and indeed daily in the sacrifice of the Divine Liturgy, nevertheless, on account of the majesty of that Mystery, Pope Urban IV already in the 13th century [^128] established

*(Physical Page 126 / Leaf p126 / Book Page 124)*

that it should be commemorated with an annual solemnity throughout the entire Church in the manner of a Feast of the Lord of the first class, with an eight-day afterfeast (*cum octava*), namely on the Thursday after the first Sunday [after Pentecost], *All Saints*. This subsequently the Ecumenical Council of Trent likewise commendeth in these words: *"Most piously and religiously was that custom introduced into the Church of God, that every year on a certain and solemn day this most sublime and most blessed Sacrament should be celebrated with singular veneration and solemnity, and that It should be borne in processions with honor and majesty through the streets and public places."* [^129] Wherefore, following this supreme ecclesiastical authority, not only all the Western Churches, but also almost all the Eastern Churches united with the Catholic Church celebrate this feast solemnly, especially our Ruthenian Church ever since the Union, and that with a special divine service arranged throughout all eight days; likewise do we find that this feast is celebrated among the Italo-Greeks, Greek-Melkites, Armenians, Maronites, Syrians, Chaldeans, and Copts. [^130] Furthermore, although properly this feast ought to be celebrated on Thursday, for on this day the Holy Eucharist was instituted by Christ the Lord, nevertheless, on account of the transference of feasts from weekdays unto the following Sunday, it is transferred unto Sunday; in which case, however, that is, on that Sunday, the entire divine service shall be celebrated, and that one and the same as indicated on the first day in the Triodion, with All-Night Vigil, while on the remaining days observing the rules of divine service prescribed in the general typikon with the afterfeast. On the Sunday itself there shall also be, according to custom, a procession with the Most Holy Mysteries after the recitation of the Prayer behind the Ambo, that is, from the Church unto four altars erected outside the Church, equidistant from one another. Where, however, it should be impossible to do this, it shall suffice to begin the procession from the Altar of the Church and, having thrice circuited the Church with the Most Holy Mysteries, to conclude this sacred rite

*(Physical Page 127 / Leaf p127 / Book Page 125)*

upon that same Altar in the Church with the blessing with the Most Holy Mysteries. As to the four Gospels, whereof one is customarily sung at each of the four altars according to the custom adopted here in the north, the Synod decreeth that those should be selected which speak of the Most Holy Sacrament, according to the ritual prescriptions for this feast.

#### IV.

As to the other extraordinary divine services which on the occasion of feast days ought to be celebrated, it remaineth yet to mention the following, namely: 1. Exposition and Blessing with the Most Holy Mysteries; 2. The Order of the Akathist; and 3. The Order of the Paraklesis.

*On Exposition and Blessing with the Most Holy Mysteries.*

As to the exposition of the Most Holy Sacrament and the imparting of benediction therewith, the Synod confirmeth the custom already formerly introduced among us, that the same be publicly exposed upon the upper part of the Tabernacle (*Kyvot*), and that during the Divine Liturgy on the Feast of the Eucharist and on the Patronal Feast (the dedication or title of the church); in which case, however, sacred ministers are bound to observe the greatest caution, so that during that exposition they may not turn their backs unto the Most Holy Gifts, but when they must turn toward the people, they are bound to do so sideways, omitting in that case complete turns; likewise, if at times they happen to cross in front of the altar, let them never omit making a *metania* or profound bow, reaching with the hand unto the earth; likewise at the ablution of the chalice and hands, so far as possible, they shall face straight toward the altar.

On those days on the occasion of this exposition, priests according to custom shall impart the blessing with the Most Holy

#### VI.

The above-mentioned exposition and at the same time the procession with the Most Holy Gifts may take place also under other more solemn circumstances, such as: on the Feast of Pascha and on the occasion of parish missions, as also during so-called patronal indulgences (*otpusty*), wherewith certain Churches have been privileged by the Holy Apostolic See. Otherwise, however, when the procession is omitted, there shall be the exposition alone of the Most Holy Gifts with the supplication, and that on feast days or other special days whereon there is a larger concourse of the people. At the exposition of the Most Holy Gifts and the procession therewith, on the Feast of the Eucharist and on the patronal feast it is proper to use the Monstrance (*Monstrantsiya* / Ostensorium) for the greater majesty of the solemnity, whereas under other circumstances the exposition may be either in the Monstrance or in the Ciborium / Tabernacle (*Darokhranylnytsia*) itself.

#### VII.

Finally, priests must remember that it is never lawful to expose the Most Holy Gifts upon the altar, whether this taketh place during the Liturgy, or at Vespers, or on any other occasion, unless there burn upon the Altar at least six wax candles.
As to the Lamb itself for exposition, before it is consecrated, it shall be fitted unto its vessel called the *Melchizedek* (lunette custodial insert), well positioned therein, and carefully wiped, so that particles may not subsequently fall from it

and having been placed in this manner into the Melchizedek, it shall be consecrated at some preceding Divine Liturgy, and once consecrated, preserved in the Tabernacle until the actual exposition.

#### VIII.

Finally, pastors must know that they ought to preserve the custom, already of old introduced here in the north and long practiced among us, that is, to expose the Most Holy Gifts either in the upper part of the Tomb, or upon the Altar on the side of the Tomb—on Great Friday and Saturday of Passion Week, and to carry the same in circuit around the Church with the *Plashchanytsia* on Great Friday and also before the Matins of the Resurrection of the Lord.

*On the Celebration of the Order of the Akathist.*

1. As on the one hand the present Synod earnestly commendeth the celebration of the Order of the Akathist, chiefly because the people take a great part in the singing thereof, and repeat easy and moving refrains after the numerous kontakia and ikoi, as also because therein devout affections may frequently be aroused by dart-like, short petitions; so on the other hand it directeth that priests who chant this prayer together with the people shall celebrate the same truly devoutly and reverently, and not cursorily or by way of abbreviation. Since, however, the devout celebration of such prayer executed according to the prescription of the typikon requireth considerable exertion of the voice and chest, as also a duration of nearly half an hour, while the remuneration for it in many places is far too small, whence it ariseth that priests have become accustomed when singing it to abbreviate, or at least silently to recite a significant portion thereof: therefore the Synod decreeth that in the future this prayer shall always be sung entirely and in a moderate voice. In a case, however, where the remuneration offered is not sufficient, the priest may combine several such stipends, and only when the sum reacheth 40 cents, then chant the prayer. If, however, certain of the faithful desire to apply the said prayer for

*(Physical Page 132 / Leaf p132 / Book Page 128)*

themselves, priests must know that, generally speaking, they may require for the celebration thereof a stipend of 40 cents.

2. The Synod strictly forbiddeth the celebration of the Akathist in the midst of other liturgical services of the Church, and especially that it should not be used in place of the sermon and catechesis. For the rest, priests may chant it between Matins and the Divine Liturgy, and that customarily one only, or more, but this on the patronal feast and on other extraordinary occasions where a longer interval of time interveneth between Matins and the Liturgy. After the dismissal of the Liturgy and of Vespers, it is permitted to chant these Akathists, yet not in place of or unto the detriment of catechesis. Before Vespers, one or more Akathists may be chanted, in accordance with that which hath been said above regarding the time before the Liturgy. [^131]

*On the Order of the Paraklesis.*

1. This devotion, generally speaking, is a supplicatory prayer (*moleben*) unto the Most Holy Virgin in the event of some distress (affliction); nevertheless among us it is customarily celebrated also on other occasions, though not very frequently. But since this devotion on the one hand by its beautiful sentiments elevateth the spirit unto heaven, kindleth love toward the Most Blessed Virgin Mary, and directeth hearts unto trust in Her; while on the other hand it is exceedingly agreeable unto our people, because they themselves take no small part in the chanting thereof: therefore the Synod judgeth that it would be highly desirable that priests should endeavor to celebrate it devoutly more frequently during the year, and above all on certain special occasions, such as during parish missions, public calamities, as also on the feasts of the Most Blessed Virgin Mary.

2. The celebration of the Order of the Paraklesis priests are bound to perform properly and accurately, observing always that devotion and gravity which befit divine worship; wherefore any abbreviation or curt truncation in the

*(Physical Page 133 / Leaf p133 / Book Page 129)*

celebration thereof this Synod condemneth as impious and illicit, and utterly forbiddeth.

3. If any of the faithful should desire that this order be celebrated especially for him, this shall be agreed unto, and in such case for the celebration thereof one may accept the stole fee (*taxa*) approved by this Synod, namely 50 cents for sustenance.

### Chapter V: On Church Singing

1. Since the modulation of the human voice hath been given unto man by nature, man is bound therewith above all things to praise the Author of nature, and the more pleasantness that voice possesseth in itself, the more capable it is of awakening toward God devout sentiments of the spirit. Wherefore not only in the Old Testament was this in use, but likewise in the New it hath customarily been employed throughout all the churches of God, following the example received from the Lord Himself and the Apostles, Who departing unto the Father together with them after the Mystical Supper sang a hymn, as it is said in St. Matthew: *"And when they had sung an hymn, they went out into the mount of Olives."* [^132]

2. That the employment of sacred singing in our rite must be of greater weight is evident already from this, that among us musical instruments are not employed, but only the human voice alone, which is so much more noble than lifeless instruments as it is itself a living instrument of God's praise. And how capable singing is of arousing in the soul devout sentiments toward God, the great Luminary of the Catholic Church, St. Augustine, clearly expresseth in the following words: *"When I recall the tears which I shed at the singing of Thy Church in the beginnings of my recovered faith, even now I am moved myself not by the singing, but by the things which are sung, when they are sung with a clear voice and suitable modulation, and I acknowledge again the great utility of that institution."* [^133]

*(Physical Page 134 / Leaf p134 / Book Page 130)*

3. That singing may attain its full end, it must be performed suitably unto so high an end, that is, *"with understanding,"* as the Royal Singer admonisheth. [^134] Above all things in singing the voice must be so modulated that this modulation may not stifle the clarity of the words, that is, so that not so much the vocal sounds of the singer may please, but rather that the sense of the sacred words may penetrate the soul. [^135]

4. Where verses are to be sung alternately by two choirs, there the second choir shall not begin to sing its verse until the first hath finished its own, which shall likewise be observed in the recitation of the psalms. As to the genre of singing itself, since customarily among us a threefold singing is employed, that is: by ear (*z slukhu* / congregational chant), Heirmologion chant (*heirmolohiyne*), and figured (polyphonic / choral) chant (*fihuralne*), it must be understood that the first and second genres of chant properly correspond unto the divine worship of our rite, and these therefore in general shall be preserved and expanded; figured chant, however, as introduced later, shall be used only at the Divine Liturgy, and that only on certain greater solemnities, provided that an adequate number of singers can be had.

5. As to Heirmologion singing in particular, the Synod commandeth: that the alumni of Seminaries shall especially apply themselves thereto, although for the rest it doth not wish that figured singing should be abandoned. As to singing by ear, since not infrequently there is observed in various places a diversity in the execution thereof, the Synod ordaineth that, for the more precise uniformity thereof, the Most Reverend Metropolitan, in agreement with the Bishops, shall endeavor to print a manual of such chants as shall prove most suitable for the ordinary use of our province.

6. As to singing by ear which already existeth in many places of this our ecclesiastical province, the Synod commendeth it to the utmost, as not only universally praiseworthy, but above all as exceedingly profitable and salutary, especially because by means thereof the faithful are more active in the sacred action itself, and become fuller participants in the holy divine service; so that not

*(Physical Page 135 / Leaf p135 / Book Page 131)*

the cantors alone, but under their lead in singing the entire congregation may sing the principal responses and hymns, especially at the Divine Liturgy, and that in this manner the praise of God may resound as widely as possible among the faithful, and they themselves more readily and, as we have said, with greater profit may participate in divine worship.

7. That the entire people may be the better trained in vocal singing, it is necessary that parishioners should be instructed therein already from youth, especially in the elementary schools (*narodni shkoly*). Since, however, correct instruction in singing dependeth almost entirely upon the church cantor (*dyak* / *pivets*), let pastors endeavor, for the proper fulfillment of that duty on Sundays and holy days as days especially dedicated unto God and our salvation, to choose for themselves as cantors not only righteous men, but also those more skilled in church singing, preferring especially those who shall present certificates of having passed an examination, issued by competent authority.
Since, however, Churches for the most part do not possess sufficient funds for the maintenance of cantors, the Synod directeth the special attention of Bishops and pastors to the end that in the meantime they may promote the procuring of their maintenance.

8. Moreover, since in public divine worship there exist sacred folk songs composed in metric verses, among which are distinguished the so-called *Koliady* (Carols) composed for the magnification of the Nativity of Christ, which hymns the faithful are accustomed to sing both inside and outside the church, the Synod, being convinced that such hymns are exceedingly effective for fostering devotion in the souls of the faithful, and all the more so because they are composed in a language intelligible unto the people, commendeth unto pastors that the congregation under the lead of the cantor should chant in church at least one hymn, especially upon the conclusion of Vespers and catechesis. In order, however, that in so important a matter all confusion may be avoided on account of the diversity of editions of those hymns which in these times have begun to appear, the Synod commandeth that in the future in their editions both the rhythm and the language wherein they were formerly written shall be preserved, allowing only certain changes where necessity requireth, and that with the prior approval of the Ordinary.

### Chapter VI: On the Celebration of Feast Days

*(Physical Page 136 / Leaf p136 / Book Page 132)*

1. Although God ought to be glorified by every prayer and good works, and as St. John Chrysostom admonisheth: we Christians are commanded to keep festival unceasingly, doing nothing evil, for that is truly a feast, but turning our attention unto spiritual things and leaving earthly things, that we may rest with a spiritual rest, withholding our hands from greed, withdrawing the body from superfluous and unnecessary labors; [^136] nevertheless, above all, Sundays and holy days are dedicated unto the glorification of God, or unto divine worship and the sanctification of Christian life. Therefore these days dedicated unto spiritual good the faithful of Christ are bound to celebrate in an extraordinary manner, which obligation is laid upon us by God Himself. For God Himself after the six days of creation blessed the seventh day and sanctified it, ceasing on this day from all His work, [^137] and moreover commanded His people the celebration of this day under pain of death. [^138] In the New Testament the Church of Christ in place of the Sabbath day hath set Sunday, because on this day salvation was accomplished, as it were a second creation or regeneration of the world through the Resurrection of Jesus Christ, and was as it were sealed.

2. But as in the Old Testament, besides the Sabbath day, God Himself instituted the celebration of yet other days, that in them year by year the more important events might be commemorated; so likewise in the New Testament the Church of Christ, guided by the Holy Spirit, besides the celebration of Sunday, hath established the keeping of yet other days, that in them each year the special mysteries of the Christian religion might be celebrated. But since in these chief mysteries together with Christ the Most Pure Virgin Mary had part above all, therefore unto the feasts of the Lord the Church hath joined several feasts of the Most Blessed Virgin, and finally throughout

*(Physical Page 137 / Leaf p137 / Book Page 133)*

the entire circle of the year the feasts of the Saints, who together with Christ constitute in the Church triumphant the mystical body, and furthermore by the examples of their lives summon us unto participation in the Heavenly City.
When, therefore, the faithful are bound to keep the days established by the Church, the Church on her part hath designated for such keeping above all the sacrifice of the Divine Liturgy, which is the supreme act of our religion, and thereafter other divine services and the preaching of the Word of God; while on the part of the faithful she requireth the duty of presence at those divine services, to hear the Word of God, and moreover to engage in other devout works and utterly to abstain from all servile works, especially evil ones, according to the above-cited admonition of St. John Chrysostom.

3. Wherefore, as to the keeping of feast days, pastors shall direct the attention of the faithful especially unto the obligation of hearing the Divine Liturgy, instructing them how grievous a sin it is, not only without grave cause to omit hearing the entire holy sacrifice—the Liturgy—but also any considerable part thereof.

4. As to other divine services and the hearing of the Word of God, although there is not so grievous a liability for every omission whatsoever, nevertheless they shall commend unto them, so far as possible, the most frequent presence thereat. But since regarding the hearing of the Liturgy, liturgical offices, and sermons, there hath been observed in many places that intolerable custom, that during their celebration many of the faithful make bold to promenade outside the church, or to stand and converse, or even to depart before the conclusion of the Divine Liturgy: therefore the Synod commandeth that pastors, wherever such abuses are found, shall strictly admonish such faithful and point out unto them that in this manner they do not at all satisfy the commandment of the Church; nay rather, they draw down upon themselves the wrath of God, as deceivers of Him and of themselves, following the example of St. John Chrysostom, who for similar abuses thus rebuked his faithful: *"What art thou doing, O man? When Christ is present, the Angels stand by, this awesome Table is set before thee, when as yet thy brethren are being initiated into the Mystery,*

*(Physical Page 138 / Leaf p138 / Book Page 134)*

*dost thou leave them and depart? Why, if thou hadst been invited to a banquet, even if thou hadst been filled earlier, yet so long as others are seated, thou darest not depart before thy friends;—and here, when the Mysteries of Christ are being celebrated, when that holy sacrifice is still being offered, dost thou leave everything in the midst and depart? Who then shall judge this worthy of pardon? Who shall cleanse it with a defense? Do ye wish that I should tell whose work they perform who depart before the completion and end, and do not offer thanksgiving hymns after the finished Supper? Bitter perhaps will seem that which I say, yet it must needs be spoken on account of the negligence of many. When on that last night Judas had partaken of the Last Supper, and while all the rest were yet sitting, he himself, breaking away, went out. Him do they imitate who depart before the final act of thanksgiving... With great quietness therefore, with great modesty and fitting reverence let us approach this holy sacrifice, that we may obtain greater favor with God, purify our soul, and attain eternal blessings."* [^139]
Moreover, let them urge upon the faithful that they be present, especially at the sacrifice of the Divine Liturgy, not merely in any casual manner, but with great devotion and reverence, as also that they frequently approach the reception of Holy Communion, which is of all means the most efficacious to sanctify oneself, and consequently also the holy day.
Since the sacrifice of the Liturgy is the center and summit of divine worship and the sun of all spiritual exercises, pouring out upon men abundantly the gifts and graces of God: therefore let them not cease to urge upon the faithful that without grave cause they do not delay from hearing it, not only in their own Church, but also in the other churches of the same parish, demonstrating unto them the groundlessness of the reasons under pretext whereof some endeavor to excuse themselves from the obligation of going to the Church of another village. Nevertheless, since just causes may arise and indeed do arise, on account whereof for many of the faithful it is either

*(Physical Page 139 / Leaf p139 / Book Page 135)*

very difficult, or even impossible, to go to another village to hear the Divine Liturgy: therefore, that the faithful may not be deprived of so many and so great gifts of God, let pastors know that they are bound in a Church within the territory of their parish wherein on that day no Divine Liturgy was celebrated, to celebrate at least Vespers with the addition of catechesis. If, however, a territory of that kind possesseth more than two churches, let them arrange that in the third and others, the church cantor himself, after the tolling of the bells, shall recite with the people some portion of the catechism, ordinary or daily prayers, and sing together with them some sacred folk hymn, to the end that the faithful at least in this manner may render due honor unto Christ the Lord hidden in the Most Holy Mystery of the Eucharist, and from Him in return be endowed with heavenly gifts.

6. When, however, the faithful have been present in Church at divine services, having rested a little in spirit, let them follow the commendable custom of pious Christians to read in their homes some spiritual book, especially the lives of the saints, or biblical history, or let them engage in some devout conversation.
Although on such days honorable recreation is not forbidden, nevertheless any entertainment worthy of condemnation entailing the corruption of morals is never permitted, as is wont to happen, as is well known, at dances which at times are accustomed to take place in taverns, with the addition of drunkenness and dissolute songs. *"For the Sabbath day,"* saith St. Augustine, *"is not carnally, not in the luxuries of the Jews, who abuse rest unto licentiousness; for it were better that they should dig all day, than dance or drink"*; and St. John Chrysostom saith: *"The time for prayers, not of drunkenness, is indeed always, but above all on a feast day; for a feast day was established for this, not that we should heap up sins, but that we should be rid of them."* [^140]
Since for the celebration of such feasts there is likewise imposed by the Church abstinence from servile works,

*(Physical Page 140 / Leaf p140 / Book Page 136)*

let pastors more frequently remind the faithful of the binding nature of that commandment, and at the same time let them thoroughly instruct them that that time of abstinence beginneth from midnight of the preceding day, and concludeth with midnight of the day following the feast.
For the rest, for the deterrence of transgressors of that law, let them set forth unto them the damages whereunto they expose themselves thereby; for if servile (slave) labor was given unto man as a punishment for sin, mitigated by the mercy of God through rest on a feast day, then it appeareth clearly that they who transgress that law by labor punish themselves with a grievous curse, and moreover deprive themselves of inestimable treasures of grace on account of mortal sin, and prepare eternal punishments for themselves, unmindful of the Providence of God, which—unto those who observe heavenly commandments—never ceaseth to enrich both with spiritual and with temporal gifts destined unto life eternal.
Since, however, at times during harvest it may happen that husbandmen, whether on account of preceding or impending foul weather, cannot without grave damage leave off work in the fields: then upon the conclusion of divine service in Church, especially after the sacrifice of the Liturgy, they may indeed perform the said works, but this only in extreme necessity, and always according to the direction and judgment of their own Pastor.
Finally, let pastors endeavor to instruct the people that on a feast day also in the event of a fair (market day) they are bound to hear divine service.
As to the patronal feast in particular, let Pastors endeavor that the same, so far as possible, be celebrated in the most solemn manner, arousing especially their faithful unto the reception of the Holy Mysteries of Confession and Communion; and unto this end let them invite neighboring pastors that they may assist them in that matter, of whom one of the more capable and zealous let them not forget to invite to preach the sermon.
