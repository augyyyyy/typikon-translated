# Acts and Decrees of the Ruthenian Provincial Synod of Lviv (1891)
## Cohort 9: Physical Pages 161–180 (Leaves p161–p180 / Book pp. 157–176)

> [!NOTE]
> **Historical & Canonical Significance**  
> Cohort 11 of the **1891 Lviv Provincial Synod** (*Чинности и рѣшеня руского провинціяльного Собора въ Галичинѣ ôтбувшого ся во Львовѣ въ роцѣ 1891*) constitutes the liturgical and euchological pinnacle of the conciliar decrees: the definitive statutory codification of the **Order of the Divine Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom and Saint Basil the Great** (*Titulus V: On the Holy Liturgy*):
> 1. **Vesting & Preparation of the Clergy (Leaves p161–p162 / Book pp. 157–158)**:
>    - Mandatory blessing and kissing of every priestly and diaconal vestment; restoration of the ancient Eastern use of the *Amice* (*Napleshnyk* / *humerale*) citing Pope Benedict XIV (*De sacrificio Missae*); canonical order of vesting (Epitrachelion before Cincture; Phelonion assumed immediately before Proskomide; Diaconal vesting sequence concluding with the Ruthenian crown-like arrangement of the *Orarion*).
> 2. **The Proskomide & Eucharistic Matter (Leaves p162–p165 / Book pp. 158–161)**:
>    - Retention of the *Lance* (*Kopiye*); strict canonical definition of the *Prosphora* (round loaf, four-cornered Lamb, pure wheat flour, natural water, and leaven, baked in the ordinary manner); regulation of pre-cut Lambs for weekday/reserve use (weekly cutting by church clergy, maximum one month storage); celebration upon the Lamb alone if particles are unavailable.
>    - Natural wine mingled with a few drops of pure water.
>    - Diptych particles on the diskos: statutory exclusion of particles for the Precious Cross and Bodiless Powers (Angels) in accordance with Pope Benedict XIV's *Euchologion* and the theological reality that particles represent the three states of the Church (Triumphant, Suffering, Militant), while the Lamb alone represents Jesus Christ.
>    - Solemn commemoration of the Slavic Apostles Saints Cyril and Methodius; promotion of Saint Josaphat from "blessed" to "holy" following his 1867 Roman canonization.
>    - Suppression of spurious interpolated prayers (*"O Lord Jesus Christ receive this sacrifice..."*) and monastic-only rubrics for secular clergy.
>    - Liturgical veils: retention of the Galician rigid square chalice veil, soft diskos veil, and pure linen *Iliton* spread between the altar cloths enclosing the consecrated *Antimension*. Spoken (Low Mass) vs. sung chalice and diskos arrangement.
> 3. **The Order of the Divine Liturgy: Enarxis unto the Holy Gospel (Leaves p165–p172 / Book pp. 161–168)**:
>    - Opening of Royal Doors; initial censing pattern (Altar table, sanctuary eastern wall icon, iconostasis sovereign icons, clergy, choirs, and faithful); secret recitation of preparatory prayers before the Holy Table with joined hands (*finger unto finger*); deep bows at *Gloria in excelsis* and *Domine labia mea*; kissing of Gospel and Altar.
>    - Diaconal dialogue (*"It is time for the Lord to act"*); initial blessing given with the hand (not with the Gospel Book); distinct secret recitation of all prayers and ectenies with the lips during singing.
>    - Philological norm enforcing Old Church Slavonic accents (*Pomolýmsya*, *Predadým*).
>    - Statutory distribution of Antiphons vs. Typika (*Izobrazitelni*): Antiphons prescribed for secular and monastic parish churches; Typika reserved for non-parochial monasteries.
>    - The Small Entrance with the Holy Gospel: acolyte procession, kissing of the Gospel by the church Collator/Patron and nearby faithful; blessing toward the east.
>    - Trisagion ceremonial: celebrants turning toward the people with extended hands at the doxology; deacon raising the orarion; procession of celebrants to the High Place (*horne sidalyshche*); concelebrating priests switching sides.
>    - Epistle reading and censing during the "Alleluia"; Gospel prayer recited before the Holy Table; Deacon exiting through the Royal Doors bearing the Gospel aloft representing Christ Himself; kissing of the Gospel.
>    - Spoken Liturgy norm: Gospel read facing the people from the Royal Doors; prohibition against laity entering the sanctuary.
>    - Ecteny of Fervent Supplication (*Rtsim vsi*) and Catechumens: unfolding of the Iliton upon the Holy Table.
> 4. **The Liturgy of the Faithful, Great Entrance, and the Eucharistic Anaphora (Leaves p173–p180 / Book pp. 169–176)**:
>    - Textual restoration of the Cherubic Hymn: conciliar rejection of the Benedictine Roman edition corruption (*"Yako Tsarya vsekh podyimem"*), mandating the authentic Church Slavonic participle *"Yako Tsarya vsekh podyemlyushche"* (bearing up / receiving the King of all).
>    - Solemn Great Entrance: procession order (candle-bearers, subdeacon with censer and Aer, deacon with diskos and asterisk upon head, concelebrants, principal celebrant with chalice); commemorations at the north and Royal doors; veiling Gifts with Aer; variants for single deacons or Low Mass.
>    - Kiss of Peace; Creed (*Viruyu*) with elevated Aer fanned over Gifts; Anaphora dialogue (*Hori imiyim serdtsa*, *Bilahodarym*); Asterisk tapping diskos at the Sanctus; signing with asterisk.
>    - Anaphora textual corrections for the Liturgy of St. Basil the Great (*"i vozvratil yesi"*; *"K' yezhe iz mertvykh voskreseniyu"* for *ὁдоποιήσας τὴν Ἀνάστασιν*).
>    - Words of Institution: elevation, threefold blessing (+ blessed + sanctified + broken + gave), profound prostrations to the ground, ringing of altar bells, kneeling of all faithful.
>    - Elevation at *Tvoya ot Tvoyikh* crosswise; retention of the Troparion of the Third Hour during Epiklesis without reformist marginal notes.
>    - Diptychs and Megalynarion: censing; Church Slavonic grammatical correction of the locative case (*"O svyatim Ioanni"*, *"S svyatym Ioannom"*, *"O Presvyatii"*).
>    - The Lord's Prayer: hands elevated to head height; deacons cross-binding the orarion across breast and back; "Peace be unto all" from side of Altar; ablution of fingers; prayer *"Hospodi Isuse Khryste"* with triple striking of breast *"O God, be merciful unto me a sinner"*.

### The Order of the Divine Liturgy: Enarxis and Liturgy of the Word

*As to the Holy Liturgy Itself.*

After the completion of the Proskomide, if the Liturgy be sung, the Priest goeth forth with the Deacons (if there be such); they stand before the holy altar [table of the Holy Mysteries], and having made there, all together, a profound bow with the hand reaching even unto the ground (it being understood, if the Liturgy is before an altar with the Holy Mysteries [the reserved Blessed Sacrament]; if however there be no Holy Mysteries, then with the hand unto the knees), they open the Royal Doors, and immediately the Priest, having blessed it, putteth incense into the censer, and saying quietly the psalm: "Have mercy on me, O God," he censeth, or, if there be Deacons, they cense.

The censing taketh place in the following manner: If the Priest celebrateth alone, he censeth first the Holy Table from the front, then from the left side of the Sanctuary, that is, from his right side and from the east, then the Altarpiece Icon situated upon the wall, next the Holy Table from the left side (his left, but the right of the Sanctuary), and having bowed before the Holy Table, he goeth forth into the Church and censeth the Sovereign Icons: of the Savior, then of the Theotokos, and other icons if there be any, and having bowed again before the Holy Table, he turneth toward the people and censeth the Choirs and the people, and having bowed slightly, he returneth into the Holy Sanctuary and handeth over the censer.

As to the Ecteny: "In peace let us pray unto the Lord," etc., everywhere the Slavonic word: "Pomolimsya" shall be used with the stress upon the final "i", and not upon "o"—and it shall not be written with "yat" (ѣ), because here the word "pomolimsya" correspondeth unto the Greek imperative; and wherefore everywhere, where it correspondeth unto the Greek imperative, in the Old Church Slavonic language the accent must be placed upon the final *i* (и́). "Pomólimsya" with the accent upon the "ó" would be indicative, while "pomolěmsya" with "yat" (ѣ) would be Little Russian [Ukrainian] or Russian imperative. [^155]: The solemn canonization of Saint Josaphat Kuntsevych, OSBM, Archbishop of Polotsk and Hieromartyr of Vitebsk, proclaimed by Pope Pius IX on June 29, 1867, in the Vatican Basilica. By virtue of this universal canonization, the Synod decrees that in the Proskomide commemoration the title "blessed" (*блаженнаго*) is definitively replaced with "holy" (*святаго*), and introduces the commemoration of the Slavic Apostles Saints Cyril and Methodius immediately preceding him.

[^156]: The textual criticism of the Cherubic Hymn (*Херувимская пѣснь* / *Hymnus Cherubicus*). The Synod rejects the textual corruption introduced into the recent Benedictine Roman edition of the Euchologion (*Eὐχολόγιον τὸ μέγα*, Grottaferrata / Rome) which printed *"Яко Царя всѣхъ подъимемъ"* (*Ὡς τὸν βασιλέα τῶν ὅλων ὑποδεξόμενοι* understood as future subjunctive "that we may receive"), affirming instead the authentic traditional Church Slavonic reading *"Яко Царя всѣхъ подъемлюще"* (present participle matching the Greek *ὑποδεξόμενοι* / *doryphoroumenon* as "bearing up / receiving in triumph"), invisibly attended by the angelic ranks.

[^157]: Liturgical textual emendation in the Anaphora of the Divine Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great. The Synod amends the obscure Slavonic rendering *"Путесотворивый воскресеніе"* translating the Greek clause *ὁδοποιήσας πάσῃ σаркὶ τὴν ἐк νεκρῶν ἀνάстаσιν* ("having paved the way for all flesh unto the resurrection from the dead"), clarifying that it must be translated and understood as *"Къ еже изъ мертвыхъ воскресенію"*.

[^158]: The Troparion of the Third Hour (*"O Lord, Who didst send down Thy All-Holy Spirit at the third hour upon Thine Apostles..."*) inserted into the Epiklesis of the Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom and St. Basil the Great. Although critical liturgical scholars and certain Roman editions noted that this troparion was an interpolated medieval Slavic and post-Byzantine addition not found in the ancient uncial Greek euchologia (such as the 8th-century Barberini Greek 336), the Fathers of the 1891 Lviv Synod expressly resolved to omit any reformatory marginal note and maintain the unbroken Ruthenian liturgical praxis and received rubrics of the printed Liturgicons.

[^159]: Church Slavonic philological and grammatical emendation in the diptychs following the Megalynarion (*"Especially for our all-holy, immaculate, most blessed and glorious Lady Theotokos..."*). The Synod corrects the erroneous genitive singular *"Святаго Іоанна"* in late editions, restoring the precise Slavonic locative case *"О святѣмъ Іоаннѣ"* for the Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom (corresponding to Greek *ὑπέρ* with genitive, rendered by Slavonic *o* with locative, Latin *pro*) and the instrumental case *"Съ святымъ Іоанномъ"* for the Liturgy of St. Basil the Great (corresponding to Greek *μετά* with genitive, rendered by Slavonic *съ* with instrumental, Latin *cum*), while mandating the archaic locative adjective ending *-ѣ* rather than *-ой* (*"О Пресвятѣй"*).
