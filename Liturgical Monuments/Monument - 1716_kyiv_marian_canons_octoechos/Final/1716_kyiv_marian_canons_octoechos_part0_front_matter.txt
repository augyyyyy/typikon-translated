## PREFACE

"Let us magnify the Mother of God with songs," proclaims the Holy Church unto the people gathered together into the divine temple, who desire every day to magnify the Mother of God, who is blessed by all generations. Yet being unable worthily to praise the true Mother of God, as is truly meet, she blesses her with these her very own songs of praise, saying: "My soul doth magnify the Lord, and my spirit hath rejoiced in God my Savior." In no wise can the rules of ordinary rhetoric encompass her who surpassed all natural laws: "The ordinances of nature are overcome in thee, O pure Virgin." What word can eloquently utter, or what created mind can comprehend, that which is beyond the mind: "Thy childbearing is full of marvels, O All-Pure Bride, Blessed Mother!" What tongue can dare to speak concerning the Mother of God, when even supercelestial luminaries behold her in amazement, being as it were speechless fish? What hand of a scribe can depict with artistic strokes that which is not of nature, but the work of God's good pleasure? Truly all the mysteries of the Theotokos are beyond the mind, all are most glorious. Holy Elizabeth prophesied somewhat in praise, yet being filled with the Holy Spirit she said: "Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb." Another woman also from the crowd cried aloud, saying: "Blessed is the womb that bore Thee, and the breasts which Thou didst suck." Yet she was enlightened by the divine words of Christ preaching the Gospel. The divine prophets also foretold beforehand many feast-day things [p3LA] with types did the divine prophets foretell, not by their own exposition, nor by their own will, but the holy men of God spoke from the Holy Spirit. Yet this small portion of praise, even though introduced from divine revelations, what is it compared to the perfect praise of the Mother of God? It is a drop of water of the same nature as the sea, but small compared to the multitude of the rivers of the sea. It is a star having one radiance with the sun, yet incomparable to the majesty of the sun's brilliance. In order perfectly to magnify the Mother of God, whose majesty the Mighty One hath wrought, scarcely can one find from her very lips anything more acceptable in praise besides her own words, to think upon the dignity of which is the work of the heavenly mind itself. Yet according to the fittingness of love to praise her, even if it is awe-inspiring, nevertheless it is an acceptable deed, as the Church chants: "It were fitting for us through fear to choose silence as being without peril; yet out of love, O Virgin, to weave hymns is a precious work." Great is the work of love, through which are all things, and without which is nothing. To speak with human and angelic tongues is exceedingly glorious, but without love it is a sounding cymbal; to prophesy things to come, to understand secret things, to work miracles—these are something divine, yet without love the worker thereof is as nothing. Love is patient and kind; love does not envy, does not boast, is not proud, does not behave rudely, does not seek its own, is not provoked, loves all things, believes all things, hopes all things. Bearing witness in these manners, unto whom can love fail to be pleasing? Whom does it not properly praise? Magnificent is that which is done by illuminating reason, yet only in part, for we know in part; but in love, what is imperfect? Love is the fulfilling of the Law. Knowledge puffs up, but love builds up. Prophecies shall fail, tongues shall cease, knowledge shall pass away, but love never falls away. Following this most excellent way of love shown forth by the Apostle [p3RA] the God-loving man Saint John of Damascus, having found it, went beyond the words of praise of many saints, and especially of the Mother of God; which canons in the eight tones, usually chanted at Compline, now printed in types, the Holy Church sends forth into the world, if not with voice yet in deed crying out to all the Orthodox, saying: "Let us magnify the Mother of God with songs." For the majesty of her divine childbearing, which magnified our race, blessing the Mother of God, let us chant these supplicatory hymns to her who is our continual intercessor with God. We pray to the Mother of Light, we who after daily labors desire to rest in nocturnal sleep, that she may preserve us from the dark sleep of sin, and from every defiling carnal passion; we prayerfully greet the Star of Jacob, who reveals herself not far from us, but carries upon her hands the Sun of Righteousness, Christ the Lord. That falling asleep in natural sleep, we may not sleep the sleep of death, but in her light we may see again the light of day. And if anyone should walk in the midst of the shadow of death, he shall not fear evil, when he has this Blessed One among women, clothed with the Sun, as his companion. For as here with this Mother he was a son of faith and grace, so also in heaven with her may he be counted worthy to be a son of the day and of light. Which, by the goodness of Christ the true Light, through the intercession of the Virgin Mother of God, and of all the saints who have shone forth in a God-pleasing life, may it be granted unto all of us to obtain.

Prayerfully wishing this,

Archimandrite Ioannikii[^4]

with the whole Brotherhood

of the Holy Great Miraculous Lavra of the Caves in Kyiv.

*The Beginning of Small Compline.*

*When the priest has given the blessing,*

*We say according to the custom as follows:*

Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee.

O Heavenly King, Comforter, the Spirit of Truth, Who art everywhere and fillest all things, Treasury of blessings, and Giver of life: come and abide in us, [f. 1vA] and cleanse us from every stain, and save our souls, O Good One.

Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, have mercy on us. *(Thrice)*

Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

O All-Holy Trinity, have mercy on us; Lord, cleanse our sins; Master, pardon our iniquities; Holy One, visit and heal our infirmities for Thy name's sake.

Lord, have mercy. *(Thrice)* Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

Our Father, Who art in the heavens, hallowed be Thy name. Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done, on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread, [f. 2rA] and forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors. And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from the evil one.

Lord, have mercy. *(12 times)* Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

Come, let us worship God our King.

Come, let us worship and fall down before Christ, our King and our God.

Come, let us worship and fall down before Christ Himself, our King and our God.

*Then Psalm 50:*

Have mercy on me, O God, according to Thy great mercy; and according to the multitude of Thy compassions blot out my transgression.

[f. 2vA] Wash me thoroughly from mine iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin.

For I know mine iniquity, and my sin is continually before me.

Against Thee only have I sinned, and done evil before Thee; that Thou mightest be justified in Thy words, and overcome when Thou art judged.

For behold, I was conceived in iniquities, and in sins did my mother bear me.

For behold, Thou hast loved truth; the hidden and secret things of Thy wisdom hast Thou revealed unto me.

Thou shalt sprinkle me with hyssop, and I shall be cleansed; Thou shalt wash me, and I shall be made whiter than snow.

Thou shalt make me to hear joy and gladness; [f. 3rA] the bones which have been humbled shall rejoice.

Turn Thy face away from my sins, and blot out all mine iniquities.

Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me.

Cast me not away from Thy presence, and take not Thy Holy Spirit from me.

Restore unto me the joy of Thy salvation, and uphold me with Thy guiding Spirit.

I will teach transgressors Thy ways, and the ungodly shall turn unto Thee.

Deliver me from bloodguiltiness, O God, Thou God of my salvation; and my tongue shall rejoice in Thy righteousness.

O Lord, Thou shalt open my lips, and my mouth shall declare Thy praise.

[f. 3vA] For if Thou hadst desired sacrifice, I would have given it; with whole-burnt offerings Thou wilt not be pleased.

A sacrifice unto God is a broken spirit; a heart that is broken and humbled God will not despise.

Do good, O Lord, in Thy good pleasure unto Zion, and let the walls of Jerusalem be built.

Then shalt Thou be pleased with a sacrifice of righteousness, with oblation and whole-burnt offerings; then shall they offer bullocks upon Thine altar.

*Psalm 69:*

O God, attend unto my help; O Lord, hasten to help me.

Let them be ashamed and confounded that seek after my soul.

Let them be turned back and brought to shame that wish me evils.

[f. 4rA] Let them be turned back immediately in shame that say unto me: Well done, well done!

Let all those that seek Thee, O God, be glad and rejoice in Thee; and let them that love Thy salvation say continually: Let the Lord be magnified!

As for me, I am poor and needy; O God, help me.

Thou art my Helper and my Deliverer; O Lord, tarry not.

*Psalm 142:*

O Lord, hear my prayer; give ear unto my supplication in Thy truth; hear me in Thy righteousness.

And enter not into judgment with Thy servant, for in Thy sight shall no man living be justified.

For the enemy hath persecuted my soul; he hath humbled [f. 4vA] my life down to the earth.

He hath made me to dwell in dark places, as those that have been long dead; and my spirit was despondent within me, my heart within me was troubled.

I remembered the days of old, I meditated on all Thy works, I pondered on the creations of Thy hands.

I stretched forth my hands unto Thee; my soul is unto Thee as a waterless land.

Hear me speedily, O Lord; my spirit hath failed.

Turn not Thy face away from me, lest I become like them that go down into the pit.

Cause me to hear Thy mercy in the morning, for in Thee have I hoped.

Cause me to know, O Lord, the way wherein I should walk, for unto Thee have I lifted up my soul.

Deliver me from mine enemies, O Lord, unto Thee have I fled for refuge; teach me to do Thy will, [f. 5rA] for Thou art my God.

Thy good Spirit shall lead me in the land of uprightness.

For Thy name's sake, O Lord, Thou shalt quicken me; in Thy righteousness Thou shalt bring my soul out of affliction.

And in Thy mercy Thou shalt destroy mine enemies, and perish all them that afflict my soul; for I am Thy servant.

*The Little Doxology:*

Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will among men.

We praise Thee, we bless Thee, we worship Thee, we glorify Thee, we give thanks to Thee for Thy great glory.

O Lord, Heavenly King, God the Father Almighty, and O Lord, the Only-Begotten Son, Jesus Christ, and the Holy Spirit; O Lord God, Lamb of God, Son of the Father, Who takest away the sins

[Manuscript inscription at foot: "Belongs to the library of the Trinity-St. Sergius Lavra. In the year 1859."][^6]

[f. 5vA] of the world, have mercy on us. Thou Who takest away the sins of the world, receive our prayer. Thou Who sittest at the right hand of the Father, have mercy on us: for Thou alone art holy, Thou alone art the Lord, Jesus Christ, to the glory of God the Father. Amen.

Every day will I bless Thee, and I will praise Thy name forever, and unto ages of ages.

Lord, Thou hast been our refuge from generation to generation. I said: Lord, have mercy on me, heal my soul, for I have sinned against Thee. Lord, unto Thee have I fled for refuge; teach me to do Thy will, for Thou art my God; for with Thee is the fountain of life: in Thy light shall we see light. Continue Thy mercy unto them that know Thee.

Vouchsafe, O Lord, this night to keep us without sin. Blessed art Thou, O Lord, [f. 6rA] God of our fathers, and praised and glorified is Thy name unto ages. Amen.

Let Thy mercy, O Lord, be upon us, according as we have hoped in Thee. Blessed art Thou, O Lord, teach us Thy statutes. Blessed art Thou, O Master, give me understanding in Thy statutes. Blessed art Thou, O Holy One, enlighten us with Thy statutes.

Thy mercy, O Lord, is forever; despise not the works of Thy hands. To Thee praise is due, to Thee hymnody is due, to Thee glory is due, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit: both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*And immediately, the Confession of the Orthodox Faith of the First Ecumenical Council of Nicaea and the Second of Constantinople:*

[f. 6vA] *The Symbol of Faith:*

I believe in one God, the Father Almighty, Maker of heaven and earth, and of all things visible and invisible.

And in one Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of God, the Only-Begotten, begotten of the Father before all ages, Light of Light, true God of true God, begotten, not made, of one essence with the Father, by Whom all things were made;

Who for us men and for our salvation came down from heaven, and was incarnate of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary, and became man;

And was crucified also for us under Pontius Pilate, and suffered and was buried;

And rose again on the third day according to the Scriptures; [f. 7rA] And ascended into heaven, and sits at the right hand of the Father;

And shall come again with glory to judge the living and the dead, Whose kingdom shall have no end.

And in the Holy Spirit, the Lord, the Giver of life, Who proceeds from the Father, Who with the Father and the Son together is worshipped and glorified, Who spoke by the prophets.

In one holy, catholic, and apostolic Church.

I confess one baptism for the remission of sins.

I look for the resurrection of the dead;

And the life of the age to come. Amen.

*Then the Canon to the Most Holy Theotokos of the current Tone.*

[f. 7vA] *(Full-page woodcut engraving of the Mother of God holding the Christ Child within an oval medallion, surrounded by twelve roundels depicting Old Testament prefigurations: the Temple of Solomon, the Ark of the Covenant, the Tabernacle, the Seven-branched Menorah, the Table of Showbread, the Tower of David and Closed Gate, the Tablets of the Law, the Golden Pot of Manna, Aaron's Budding Rod, Jacob's Ladder, and the Burning Bush.)*

The intangible God is beheld in the arms of the Virgin;[^7]

Hasten unto Him, all ye mortals!

For the All-Good calls all: "Come,

I am the summit of desire; love Me!"

### Notes

[^1]: The front flyleaf contains ink provenance shelfmarks and calligraphic cursive ownership notes of the eighteenth century, including references to Deacon Elijah ("З книгъ Іллїѧ дїакона...") and the archival cataloguing record "1795 года Но: 3."
[^2]: The architectural woodcut border of the master title page depicts Christ Pantocrator flanked by angels at the summit, the Dormition of the Mother of God directly beneath, lateral vertical registers with medallic portraits of Saint Prince Vladimir, Princess Olga, the Holy Passion-Bearers Boris and Gleb, the Venerable Anthony and Theodosius of the Caves, and Saint Andrew the First-Called, and a detailed panoramic engraving of the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra with churches and bastions at the base, signed with the engraver's monogram "A. K." and dated 1707 ("Аѱо҃з.").
[^3]: This full-page woodcut frontispiece is the work of the renowned Kyivan Lavra master woodcut artist Marko (signed "МАРКО" in the lower right foreground), depicting the Dormition with Christ receiving the soul of His Mother amid the assembled Apostles. Beneath the engraving is printed the Troparion of the Dormition in Tone 1 (*Въ Рождествѣ Дѣвство сохранила еси...*).
[^4]: Archimandrite Ioannikii Sennutovych served as Archimandrite of the Holy Dormition Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra from 1715 until his death in 1729, overseeing an active period of liturgical printing and scholarly publication at the Lavra's historic press.
[^5]: The full-page woodcut frontispiece on fol. [Prelim 3v] depicts Christ Pantocrator blessing and holding the open Gospel (inscribed with Matt. 11:28–29) under a classical archway. Below is the Johannine declaration "I am the door: by Me if any man enter in, he shall be saved" (John 10:9) and an Imperial double-headed eagle framed by baroque acanthus scrollwork, symbolizing the official patronage and printing privilege of the Lavra under the Kyivan metropolitanate.
[^6]: A 19th-century cursive Cyrillic library annotation at the foot of fol. 5r records that the volume belonged to the library of the Trinity-St. Sergius Lavra in 1859 ("Принадлежитъ библіотекѣ Троицкой Сергіевой Лавры. 1859 года.").
[^7]: Church Slavonic four-line emblematic verse (*virsh*) set below the full-page woodcut engraving of the Mother of God surrounded by twelve Old Testament prefigurations.
