## Epigram upon the Ancient Crest of the Noble Lords Proskura-Sushchansky

*[Woodcut engraving of the ancestral coat of arms of the noble house of Proskura-Sushchansky, flanked by the armorial initials: (Ѳ Т / П / Σ / П / Z / K), signifying: Theodor Proskura Sushchansky, Land Scribe of Kyiv.[^4] The escutcheon, framed by Baroque foliate mantling and crested with a knight's helm, noble coronet, and three ostrich plumes, bears the ancestral charge of an upward-pointing arrow crossed with a transverse bar in the likeness of a cross, rooted in a split base.[^5]]*

Upon the Ancient Crest of Their Graces,
the Noble Lords Proskura-Sushchansky:

The ancient House of the Proskury-Sushchansky on both sides,
Ever glorious through the valiant, and renowned from of old,
Fittingly boasts of such an armorial jewel,
Which bears both the Arrow from the Earth and the Cross from Heaven.
For Valor and Humility have here built their nest,
That with them the Sushchanski might ever shield themselves.
They shield themselves, for Heaven itself acknowledged their virtue,
And with that same emblem endowed its own Archer.[^6]
O most fortunate House, by such a portent,
Even the Zodiac rejoices in the Sushchansky Jewel!

## Dedicatory Epistle and Theological Foreword of Metropolitan Petro Mohyla

PETRO MOHYLA,
By the Mercy of God, Archbishop,
METROPOLITAN OF KYIV,
Halych, and all Rus', Exarch
of the Holy Apostolic Throne of Constantinople,[^7]
Archimandrite of the Caves.
To the Noble and Pious, His Grace
PAN TEODOR PROSKURA-
SUSHCHANSKY, Land Scribe
of the Kyiv Voivodeship:[^8]
The grace of God, good health, prosperous success,
and the blessing of the Most Holy Metropolitan Throne of Kyiv
and of our humility from the Lord God, we cordially wish.

The people of Israel, following their leader Moses, while on pilgrimage from the Egyptian bondage unto the Promised Land, experienced the heavenly food of delicious manna with varied and marvelous flavors. We also experience this, gracious Sir, in the New Grace: for having been delivered from that ancient injurer of our souls, the devil, through the voluntary death undergone for us by Christ the Savior, and having departed from that Egypt onto the road that leads unto the Kingdom of Heaven, our promised land, we journey on pilgrimage, led invisibly by our Savior. Truly, as we journey toward the Jerusalem above, we experience in this manna varied flavors through diligent prayer

and through varied divine grace. For the Lord Himself recalled: *It is written, That man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word of God.*[^9] Who does not see clearly that our Teacher calls prayer food, since food serves for the preservation of our life? Who cannot easily consider that man, being composed of body and soul, requires one food for the body and another for the soul? There is therefore bread for the body, and there is for the soul, like heavenly manna, holy prayer; for by it the soul is ineffably nourished, which is truly called the voice of God. The Savior says: *Watch therefore, and pray always, that you may be accounted worthy to escape all these things that shall come to pass, and to stand before the Son of Man.*[^10] Thus He taught that man cannot lead his life in true holy faith, guide it spiritually, and enliven it with diverse divine gifts in any other way than through holy prayer. And just as through the manna, whichever food any of the people of Israel desired, he straightway perceived that flavor: so each of us, whenever he desires any grace of God, will perceive it within himself through prayer; for no one can be closer to receiving the grace of the Lord than he who is ever with God, who speaks with God, and God with Him. But to be with God and to speak with Him is accomplished by prayer, as St. Isidore

=== LEAF p5 ===

*The Foreword.*

testifies, saying: *If anyone desires to be always with the Lord at every hour, let him pray and read: for when we pray, we speak with the Lord, and when we read, the Lord speaks with us.*[^11] Therefore the chosen vessel, Holy Paul, rightly urges us unto prayer, saying: *Pray without ceasing.*[^12] The Euripus straits bring wonder when the current changes course and flows backward at set hours; man marvels how such artful and precious ore-deposits can be formed in the bowels of the earth, or how the magnet draws iron unto itself; but even more must everyone marvel as soon as he begins to consider the powers and effects of holy prayer. That very holy teacher of the universal Church acknowledges and declares that the power of prayer surpasses all nature.[^13] What opened the Red Sea and parted it in two, that the people of Israel might pass through sound and without stumbling? Prayer! What transformed the furnace flame in Babylon into dew for the Three Youths? Prayer! What shut and opened heaven that it might sprinkle the earth? Prayer![^14] Indeed, whatever man shall ask, he will receive it all through holy prayer, according to those words of Christ: *Ask, and it shall be given you.* Therefore, as St. Augustine says: Holy prayer is the enlivenment of the soul, comfort to the good Angel, sorrow to the evil one, service unto the Lord,[^15] the support of all

Your Grace yourself, our gracious Sir, many a time out of your piety, while visiting the Caves of our Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra, have seen with your own eyes the incorrupt bodies of so many, indeed countless, holy ones of God;[^16] which they could obtain for themselves from the Lord God in no other way than from the holy Orthodox faith (which the Eastern Catholic Church, our holy Mother, teaches) through prayer. Together with it they endured monastic struggles and sleepless vigils, being zealous for the accomplishment of every spiritual virtue; for when anyone tastes fervent prayer, all pious virtues are straightway pleasing unto him. For these reasons the holy Church has instituted daily prayers according to the season and according to need: for she has prayers unto the Lord God, and she has prayers unto His saints, whom we beseech for their intercession before our Creator and Redeemer. And therefore, in this land of Rus', by the inspiration of the Holy Spirit in the heart of His Grace the King, WŁADYSŁAW THE FOURTH,[^17] happily reigning over us, and by the will of the entire Rus' nation, having received the Rus' hierarchic office with the blessing of the Most Holy Apostolic Throne of Constantinople, I am bound by my pastoral duty, while presiding over all who journey unto the Kingdom of Heaven, to ensure that through a lack

of copies of these prayers, the sheep entrusted unto me should not starve without spiritual pasture and without the spiritual nourishment of prayer. Looking diligently to this, as soon as I perceived that they began to complain of the scarcity of the Triodion or Trichristian Canon-Book, I commanded straightway, amid my great labors, that the previously printed Triodion from the local printing press of the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra should be renewed;[^18] which contains within itself prayers and canons moving unto great contrition, fitting for these days, that they who ought especially to meditate upon repentance during the Forty Days may have a contrite heart. For if the ancient Egyptians during their banquets and revels were accustomed to bring a human skull to the table and then hand it to each guest, that he might see what he shall become after death, so that he might be moved, renounce worldly pleasures, and while living in the world not neglect to serve God; and among others coffins were set out in the midst of the public square, that those passing by might remember death:[^19] how much more ought we, being Christians, ever to meditate at every hour upon death, for it will unfailingly wipe us from this world! And with a more fervent and diligent heart, we ought to take before us during the days of the Holy Great Fast these Canons, which serve for the remembrance of our mortality: therein each will see a human skull, will perceive the coffins set out and appointed for man,

and will easily understand that man is earth, and unto earth he shall return again. As the Psalmist says: In these days the voice of John the Baptist is renewed: *Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.*[^20] And whoever desires to obtain weapons for his rescue to overcome the enemy of his soul will obtain them from these Lenten prayers: as David with three stones from his sling conquered Goliath, so everyone who prays in truth will cast down our enemy under his feet with the three stones of contrition, confession of sins, and satisfaction for them.[^21] Whoever desires to eradicate his sins will destroy them through these Lenten prayers; for holy prayer, together with this Fast of the Forty Days, will accomplish this, so that even as the rod of Moses destroyed the serpents of Egypt, so it will root out and destroy our inner serpents, our infectious sins. Having therefore reviewed this most necessary and exceedingly profitable Book following its edition by my predecessor who fell asleep in God, Kyr Zacharias Kopystensky, Archimandrite of the Caves,[^22] and having made additions in several places for spiritual profit, I resolved to embellish it with the coat of arms of the ancient House of Your Grace, our gracious Sir, and to offer it unto the Orthodox sons for the performance of pious prayers in devout days; for it is fitting to dedicate it unto no one else than unto Your Grace, my gracious Sir, who from your ancestors of many ages

The hope which the citizens of this Voivodeship placed in the youthful years of Your Grace, our gracious Sir, has not proved false, when you trained yourself in nothing other than noble virtues: when, together with other champions, courageously standing up for our beloved Fatherland against the enemies of the Fatherland in national expeditions, you entrusted your life unto doubtful Mars;[^23] whereby you renewed the glory of your house, which for its valor received distinguished praise from the Princes of Rus' and the Kings of Poland.
