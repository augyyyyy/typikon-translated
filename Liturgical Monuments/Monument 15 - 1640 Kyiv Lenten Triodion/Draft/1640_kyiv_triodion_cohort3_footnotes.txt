[^61]: Nikephoros Kallistos Xanthopoulos (c. 1256–1335): Eminent Byzantine church historian, poet, and hymnographer of Constantinople who composed the definitive verse and prose Synaxaria for the movable cycle of the Triodion and Pentecostarion. In this 1640 Kyiv edition, the Lavra press translated the Greek synaxaria into elevated Early Modern Ruthenian (*prosta mova*) prose for the instruction of clergy and faithful.
[^62]: St. Cosmas the Melodist, Bishop of Maiuma (d. c. 760): Renowned 8th-century Byzantine hymnographer, foster-brother of St. John of Damascus, who composed the original three-ode canons (triodia) for Great and Holy Week.

[^64]: Emperor Leo VI the Wise (866–912): Byzantine Emperor, scholar, and hymnographer who authored liturgical poetry, including the Four-Ode Canon for Great and Holy Saturday.
[^65]: Bishop Mark of Hydrus (Otranto, 8th–9th century): A monk of the Lavra of Saint Sabas near Jerusalem and subsequently Bishop of Hydrus (Otranto in Apulia, Southern Italy), who completed the full nine-ode canons of Holy Saturday and other services of the Triodion.

[^67]: Military pedagogy in Mohylan Ruthenian prose: Metropolitan Petro Mohyla and the Kyiv-Pechersk scholars employ a vivid homiletic comparison between Cossack hetmans briefing their regiments before military engagement and the Church Fathers preparing Christians with spiritual armor for the ascetic warfare of Great Lent.

[^71]: Publican (*мытникъ*): A tax-farmer or collector of state customs and road tolls (*myto*), infamous in the ancient and early modern world for extortion and financial injustice.
