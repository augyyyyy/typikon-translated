[^908]: Patriarch John VII the Grammarian (Morocharzanios, patriarch 837–843), designated *Christoborchos* ("fighter against Christ") for directing the imperial iconoclast persecution under Emperor Theophilus.
[^909]: Patristic typological parallel between Arius tearing Christ's seamless tunic (a famous revelation received by Hieromartyr Peter of Alexandria) and John the Grammarian tearing the robe of Christ's Church through iconoclasm.
[^910]: Ode 7 heirmos reference: "holding the praises throughout the four weeks" (*четверичными Седмицами*), referring to the four weeks of Pre-Lent and Lent leading to the triumph of the Orthodox confession.
[^911]: Historical accusations against John the Grammarian, whom iconodule hagiography accused of practicing dark arts, divination (*lekanomanteia*), and serving as the forerunner of the Antichrist.
[^912]: Greek philosophical and mythological polemic: the Studite hymnographer equates John the Grammarian's neo-Platonic and secular learning with pagan polytheism (Pythagoras, Kronos, Apollo).
[^913]: The monumental icon of Christ Chalkites upon the Bronze Gate (Chalke Gate) of the imperial palace in Constantinople, which was attacked, stoned, and taken down by iconoclasts under Leo V and John the Grammarian.
[^914]: The iconoclast rejection not only of holy icons but also of the sacred relics of the martyrs, reflecting the extreme iconoclast policy under Leo V and Theophilus.
[^915]: Longsuffering of God amid the martyrdoms of iconodule confessors, monks, and priests slain for defending the veneration of Christ's holy icon.
[^916]: Ode 9 solemn synodical anathema pronounced in the Canon of Saint Theodore the Studite against John the Grammarian, Antony Kassymatas, Theodore Krithinos, and Theodotus Kassiteras.
[^917]: Liturgical cross-reference in the Kyiv Triodion rubrics to printed folio 307 (physical page 319), where the primary troparion of the Holy Icon (*Пречистому Твоему образу покланяемся, Благий...* / *To Thy most pure image we bow down, O Good One...*) is printed in full.
[^918]: The Synodikon of Orthodoxy (*Соборное святаго вселенскаго Седмаго Собора* / *Предословіе*): the official dogmatic decree and conciliar declaration of the Seventh Ecumenical Council (Nicaea II, 787) and the Synod of Constantinople (843), promulgated by Empress Theodora and Patriarch Methodius I.
[^919]: The theological parallel between the Old Testament Feast of Dedication (*Encaenia*, Hanukkah; 1 Macc. 4:52–59; John 10:22) celebrating the purification and re-dedication of the Temple in Jerusalem, and the Christian Feast of the Restoration of the Holy Icons (*Triumph of Orthodoxy*).
[^920]: Psalm 88:51–52 (LXX versification): "the footsteps of Thine Anointed" (*τὸ ἀντάллаγμα τοῦ χριστοῦ σου*, rendered in Church Slavonic as *измѣненію Христа твоего*), interpreted Patristically as the redeemed members of Christ's Church who confess Him through both oral proclamation and sacred iconography.
[^921]: The first restoration of holy icons under Empress Irene and the Seventh Ecumenical Council (Nicaea II, 787), following the first iconoclast persecution inaugurated by Leo III the Isaurian (726) and Constantine V Kopronymos.
[^922]: The second restoration of holy icons in 843 under Empress Theodora and young Emperor Michael III, ending nearly thirty years of renewed persecution begun by Leo V the Armenian in 814/815.
[^923]: The fundamental Christological argument of Christian iconology contrasting the Old Covenant theophany at Mount Horeb (where Israel heard only a voice and saw no form, Deut. 4:12) with the New Covenant reality of the divine Incarnation, wherein the Word of God was physically seen, touched, and depicted (1 John 1:1–2).
[^924]: The classic dogmatic formula of the Synodikon of Orthodoxy and Nicaea II: distinguishing absolute adoration and worship (*latreia*) rendered to God alone from relative veneration (*proskynesis schetike*, Church Slavonic: *по удержаніи поклоненіе*) rendered to holy icons, wherein the honor passes to the Prototype.
[^925]: The four great iconodule Patriarchs of Constantinople commemorated in the Synodikon: Saint Germanus I (patriarch 715–730, deposed by Leo III), Saint Tarasius (patriarch 784–806, who convoked Nicaea II), Saint Nicephorus I (patriarch 806–815, exiled by Leo V), and Saint Methodius I (patriarch 843–847, who instituted the Triumph of Orthodoxy).
[^926]: Post-iconoclast Orthodox Patriarchs of Constantinople: Saint Ignatius (patriarch 847–858, 867–877), Saint Photius the Great (patriarch 858–867, 877–886), Stephen I (patriarch 886–893), Saint Antony II Kauleas (patriarch 893–901), and Saint Nicholas I Mystikos (patriarch 901–907, 912–925).
[^927]: Saint Stephen the New (c. 715–765), venerable monastic martyr of Mount Saint Auxentios, stoned and dragged through the streets of Constantinople under Constantine V Kopronymos for defending holy icons.
[^928]: Confessor-hierarchs of the iconoclast persecutions: Saint Euthymius, Bishop of Sardis (flogged to death in 831); Saint Theophilus, Bishop of Ephesus; and Saint Emilian, Bishop of Cyzicus (died in exile under Leo V).
[^929]: Metropolitan-confessors: Saint Theophylact, Metropolitan of Nicomedia (exiled 815, died c. 845); Saint Peter, Metropolitan of Nicaea; Saint Michael, Metropolitan of Synnada (exiled by Leo V, died 826); and Saint Joseph the Confessor, Metropolitan of Thessalonica (brother of St. Theodore the Studite, died 825).

[^931]: Saint Theodore the Studite (759–826), abbot of the Stoudios Monastery in Constantinople, theologian, confessor, and leader of monastic resistance against the iconoclasts.
