[^600]: Sessional hymn model (*Podoben*): *Tone 5, Podoben: "O Lord, the cup of torment" (Гдⷭ҇и, ча́шу му́ки)*; Greek: *Κύριε, τὸ ποτήριον τοῦ πάθους*. In the 1640 Kyiv text, the Slavonic heading reads: `По́: Гнⷭ҇и ча́шу му́ки:` (with titlo over *Господи*).
[^601]: Theotokion model (*Podoben*): *Tone 5, Podoben: "The Word co-unoriginate" (Собезнача́лное Сло́во)*; Greek: *Τὸν συνάναρχον Λόγον*. The hymn addresses the Theotokos using Old Testament types: the Rod of Jesse that blossomed (Isa. 11:1), the Ark of the Covenant, the golden Seven-branched Lampstand, the golden Jar of Manna (Heb. 9:4), and the Table of Showbread.
[^602]: Typikon rubric governing weekday Matins hymnody during Great Lent. On ordinary weekdays without a Triode, the Octoechos canons are chanted on 10 and the Menaion on 4. On days when the Triodion prescribes a three-ode canon (Triode—on Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday), the Octoechos canon is suspended: the Menaion canon is sung on 6 (with its Heirmos), and the Triode of the day is sung on 8, totaling the canonical 14 troparia.
[^603]: Biblical Canticle 2: The Song of Moses in Deuteronomy (Deut. 32:1–43). Outside of Great Lent, the Second Canticle is omitted from the daily Matins cycle; in Great Lent, it is chanted only on Tuesdays, accompanied by the penitential Ode 2 of the Triodion.
[^604]: The "Mountain of excellent deeds" and "entering into the darkness of honorable visions" evokes Moses ascending Mount Sinai into the thick darkness (*gnophos*) where God was (Exod. 20:21), a classic patristic and Dionysian image of mystical contemplative ascent (*theoria*) through ascetic purification.
[^605]: The second poetic canon of the Tuesday Triode, composed in Tone 5 by St. Theodore the Studite (the first canon being by St. Joseph the Hymnographer in Tone 2). Both poets utilize the biblical canticle model (*Heirmos*) based on the Song of Moses in Deut. 32 ("See, see that I am God").
[^606]: Ode 8 of the first canon (Tone 2, St. Joseph). The Heirmos incipit refers to the Burning Bush on Mount Sinai: *Tone 2, Heirmos: "In the bush to Moses" (Въ купинѣ̀ Мѡѷсе́ю)*; Greek: *Ἐν τῇ βάτῳ Μωϋσῇ*.
[^607]: Ode 8 of the second canon (Tone 5, St. Theodore the Studite). The Heirmos incipit: *Tone 5, Heirmos: "The Creator of creation" (Творца̀ тва́ри)*; Greek: *Τὸν ποιητὴν τῆς κτίσεως*.
[^608]: Allusion to Christ's parable of the Rich Man and Lazarus (Luke 16:19–31), contrasting luxurious feasting with ascetic poverty and ultimate comfort in the bosom of Abraham.
[^609]: Ode 9 of the first canon (Tone 2, St. Joseph). The Heirmos incipit: *Tone 2, Heirmos: "Who of the earthborn hath heard?" (Земны́й кто̀ слы́ша)*; Greek: *Τίς ἤκουσε τοιοῦτον*.
[^610]: Ode 9 of the second canon (Tone 5, St. Theodore the Studite). The Heirmos incipit: *Tone 5, Heirmos: "Rejoice, O Isaiah" (И҆са́їа лику́й)*; Greek: *Ἡσαΐα χόρευε*. This Heirmos is famously also chanted during the Mystery of Crowning (Holy Matrimony) and Holy Orders.
[^611]: Photagogicon (*Свѣти́ленъ* / *Exapostilarion*): On weekdays of Great Lent, the Trinity Hymns (*Troika*) of the Tone from the Horologion or Octoechos are chanted in place of a festal exapostilarion.
[^612]: Matins Aposticha idiomelon in Tone 3. The hymn paraphrases Christ's instructions in the Sermon on the Mount concerning fasting and prayer without hypocrisy (Matt. 6:7, 16–18), commending charity (anointing the head with the oil of good deeds) and pure interior prayer ("Our Father").
[^613]: First penitential stichos for the weekday Matins Aposticha during Great Lent: *Ps. 89:14 (LXX)*: "We were filled in the morning with Thy mercy, O Lord, and we rejoiced and were glad in all our days."
[^614]: Troparion of the Prophecy at the Sixth Hour on Tuesday of the First Week of Lent. The text draws on David's prayer in *Ps. 38:13 (LXX)* ("For I am a sojourner with Thee, and a stranger, as all my fathers were") and 1 Chron. 29:15.
[^615]: Sixth Hour Prokeimenon taken from *Ps. 5:3 (LXX)*. The response verse is *Ps. 5:2*.
[^616]: Sixth Hour Old Testament reading: Isaiah 1:19–2:3 (continuous liturgical pericope appointed for Tuesday of the First Week of Great Lent). The reading spans folios 212r–213r (leaves p226–p227), rebuking the corruption of Jerusalem and prophesying the exaltation of the Mountain of the Lord in the latter days.
[^617]: Prokeimenon closing the Sixth Hour: *Ps. 6:2 (LXX)*. Stichos: *Ps. 6:3*.
[^618]: Melodic model (*Podoben*): *Tone 2, Podoben: "When Thou didst appear" (Ꙗ҆́кѡ ꙗ҆ви́сѧ)*; Greek: *Ὡς ἐφάνης*. The incipit refers to the appearance of Christ upon the Cross.
[^619]: Melodic model (*Podoben*): *Tone 2, Podoben: "For the deeds done by me" (Съдѣ́ланныхъ ми)*; Greek: *Τῶν πεπραγμένων μοι δейнῶν*. The hymn reflects on the prohibition of the fruit of the tree in Genesis 2:16–17 and connects ascetic obedience during Great Lent with the mystery of Christ's Cross.
[^620]: First Vespers Prokeimenon before the Old Testament readings: *Ps. 7:2 (LXX)*. Stichos: *Ps. 7:2*.
[^621]: First Old Testament reading at Vespers: Genesis 1:14–23, recounting the Fourth and Fifth Days of Creation (the creation of the sun, moon, stars, fish, and fowl). The pericope spans leaves p228–p229.
[^622]: Second Vespers Prokeimenon (between the Genesis and Proverbs readings): *Ps. 8:2 (LXX)*. Stichos: *Ps. 8:2*.
[^623]: Second Old Testament reading at Vespers: Proverbs 1:20–33. Spans leaves p229–p230. Personified Divine Wisdom calls the simple and rebukes the scorners who reject knowledge and the fear of the Lord.
[^624]: Vespers liturgical prayer: "Vouchsafe, O Lord, to keep us this evening without sin" (*Сподо́би, Го́споди*), recited immediately following the second Old Testament reading on Lenten weekdays when the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts is not celebrated.
