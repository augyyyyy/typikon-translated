[^25]: Physical asceticism with iron chains (*желѣзом тѣла ѡковываютъ*): An allusion to the traditional East Slavic and Athonite ascetical practice of wearing heavy iron chains or penitential crosses (*verigi* / *żelaza*), exemplified among the monastic fathers of the Kyiv-Pechersk Caves (such as St. Theodosius of the Caves and subsequent cave-dwelling recluses) to mortify bodily passions and prevent physical sleep during nocturnal vigilance.
[^26]: Dating formula of the Mohyla Foreword: The dedicatory preface concludes on May 25, 7148 AM (1640 AD), precisely two months after the imprint date recorded on the architectural master title page (March 23, 1640 O.S.), demonstrating the chronological window between typesetting and final archpastoral imprimatur.
[^27]: Genesis 1:26: "And God said, Let Us make man in Our image, after Our likeness."
[^28]: Genesis 2:8, 15: God placing man in the Garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it.
[^29]: Psalm 8:5–6 (LXX Ps 8:6): "Thou hast made him a little lower than the angels, and hast crowned him with glory and honor."
[^30]: 1 Peter 5:8: "Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour."
[^31]: Matthew 4:2; Luke 4:1–2: The forty-day fast of Christ our Savior in the wilderness, establishing the archetype and spiritual pattern for the Holy Great Forty Days.
[^32]: Venial and mortal sins (*повше́хныи* vs. *смерте́лныи* / *peccata venialia* et *mortalia*): Metropolitan Petro Mohyla employs the precise moral-theological distinction between venial and mortal sins formulated in the Latin and Eastern theological syntheses of the 17th-century Mohylan circle in Kyiv.
[^33]: Matthew 22:13; Matthew 25:30: The cast outer darkness where there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth.
[^34]: John 3:16: "For God so loved the world, that He gave His only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in Him should not perish, but have everlasting life."
[^35]: The 8th Indiction of 1640: The Byzantine 15-year indiction cycle corresponds exactly: 7148 AM divided by 15 leaves a remainder of 8, confirming the technical accuracy of the Pechersk printing house chronogram.
[^36]: Full-page woodcut engraving of the Crucifixion: Produced by the printshop of the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra, this plate synthesizes Byzantine iconographic conventions (the solemn, standing postures of the Theotokos and St. John, the Golgotha cavern containing Adam's skull) with Western Baroque naturalism in the anatomical rendering and the urban panorama of Jerusalem.
[^37]: 1 Corinthians 2:2 (Slavonic Pericope 129): "For I determined not to know anything among you, save Jesus Christ, and Him crucified."
[^38]: Title formula of the Triodion: *Triodion, that is, the Three-Ode Canon* (*ТРІѠ́ДІѠНЪ си́есть: ТРІПѢ́СНЕЦЪ*). So named because the weekday canons of Great Lent contain only three odes (ordinarily Odes 8 and 9 plus one preceding ode), rather than the customary eight or nine odes found in festal and Sunday canons.
[^39]: Great Vespers hymnographic structure: The typikon combines the Sunday Resurrectional cycle of the Octoechos with the specialized penitential hymnography of the Triodion, signaling the entry into the Pre-Lenten liturgical period.
[^40]: Luke 18:14; Matthew 23:12: "For whosoever exalteth himself shall be abased; and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted."
[^41]: Scriptural paradigms of tears: King Hezekiah delivered from death (4 Kings 20:1–6 [2 Kings 20 MT]; Isaiah 38:1–8), and the repentant sinful woman who washed Christ's feet with her tears (Luke 7:36–50).
[^42]: Mark's Chapters (*Сѵ́главъ Ма́рка І҆еромона́ха*): Liturgical rubrics attributed to Mark, Hieromonk of the Monastery of Saint Sabas near Jerusalem, codified across medieval Byzantine and Slavic Typika to resolve calendar coincidences when movable and immovable feasts intersect.
[^43]: Chapter 42 of the Typikon: Governs the coincidence of the Feast of the Meeting of the Lord (February 2) and its associated Forefeast and Afterfeast with the Pre-Lenten Sundays (Publican and Pharisee, Prodigal Son, Meatfare, Cheesefare).
[^44]: Festal Matins rubrics: Prescribes the Polyeleos and the Sunday Resurrectional Evlogitaria (*The Angelic Council*) when the Great Feast of the Meeting intersects with the Sunday service, showing the fusion of festal and dominical hymnography.
[^45]: Psalm 97:2 (LXX Ps 97:2): Appointed as the festal Entrance Verse (Introit) for the Meeting of the Lord.
[^46]: Patristic Homilies from the *Tolkovoye Yevangeliye* (Commentary Gospel): Appoints the classical homilies of St. John Chrysostom and St. Cyril of Alexandria on the Parable of the Publican and the Pharisee to be read at Sunday Matins.
[^47]: The Triodion Penitential Sticheron (*Мно́жество съдѣ́ѧнныхъ мои́хъ ѕѡ́лъ*): The 1640 Kyivan edition preserves this ancient post-Gospel sticheron based on Davidic compunction, which in later Synodal editions is grouped with *Open unto me the doors of repentance, O Giver of Life*.
[^48]: St. George of Nicomedia (*Творе́нїе Геѡ́ргїа*): Metropolitan George of Nicomedia (9th century), celebrated hymnographer who composed the triodia and tetraodia of the Pre-Lenten and Lenten seasons in the Byzantine rite.
[^49]: Katavasia of Ode 1: *I shall open my mouth* (*Ѡ҆тве́рзꙋ ꙋ҆ста̀ моѧ̑*), theirmos of the Canon to the Most Holy Theotokos (Tone 4), standardly appointed during the Pre-Lenten period.
[^50]: Ode 3 Heirmos: *There is none holy as Thou, O Lord my God* (*Нѣ́сть ст҃ъ, ꙗ҆́коже Ты̀, Гдⷵи Бж҃е мо́й*), based upon the Third Biblical Canticle (1 Kings 2:2 [1 Sam 2:2 MT], the Song of Hannah).
