[^1549]: Slavonic: *трича́стный и҆ вели́кїй* (tripartite and great). In Patristic and Kyivan liturgical symbolism, the tripartite Cross refers to the traditional Byzantine three-bar Cross (titulus, main crossbeam, and suppedaneum) composed mystically of three types of wood (cypress, pine, and cedar; cf. Isa. 60:13 LXX).

The Church Fathers (e.g. Tertullian, St. Justin Martyr, St. Cyril of Jerusalem) interpret this as a prophetic type of Christ the Living Bread nailed to the Wood of the Cross by the Jews.
