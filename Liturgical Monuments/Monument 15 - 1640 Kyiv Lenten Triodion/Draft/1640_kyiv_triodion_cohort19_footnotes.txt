[^483]: Conclusion of Cheesefare Sunday Matins: following the Great Doxology and the dismissal of Matins, the brethren process into the narthex (*притво́ръ*) for the chanting of the Gospel Sticheron (Eothinon) and the reading of the ascetical instruction of St. Theodore the Studite (*Поꙋче́нїе Стꙋді́тово*), a classic feature of Studite monastic horology before the final dismissal.

[^484]: Liturgical order for the Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom on Cheesefare Sunday (Forgiveness Sunday): the appointed Epistle is Romans 13:11–14:4 (pericope 121), urging believers to put on the armor of light and not to pass judgment regarding food; the Gospel is Matthew 6:14–21 (pericope 17), presenting Christ's injunction to forgive others and to fast in secret rather than before men.

[^485]: Monastic Typikon ordinance regarding the Polyeleos: from Cheesefare Sunday until the Exaltation of the Precious Cross (September 14), the Polyeleos (Psalms 134–135) is discontinued at regular Sunday Matins throughout the spring and summer periods, being reserved exclusively for Vigils of the Master's Feasts (Dominical Feasts) or major feast-day Saints.

[^486]: Rubric for Cheesefare Sunday Vespers (Forgiveness Vespers), marking the solemn liturgical threshold of Great Lent. Ten stichera are appointed at *Lord, I have cried*: four penitential stichera in the Tone of the week from the Oktoechos (drawing from the evening and morning Aposticha), three proper stichera from the Triodion, and three from the Menaion.

[^487]: Tone 2 prosomoia composed by Lord Joseph the Hymnographer (*Творе́нїе Гдⷭ҇на І҆ѡ́сифа*), sung to the melodic model *When from the Tree* (Greek: *Ὅτε ἐκ τοῦ ξύλου*; Slavonic: *Е҆гда̀ ѿ дре́ва*). The hymns exhort the faithful to embark upon the stadium of the Fast with humility and to emulate the repentance of the Ninevites (Jonah 3:5–10).

[^488]: Tone 2 prosomoion composed by St. Theodore the Studite (*Гдⷭ҇а Ѳео́дѡра Стꙋди́та*), sung to the melodic model *Surpassing understanding* (Slavonic abbreviation: *Па́че [ᲂу҆ма̀]*; Greek: *Ὑπὲρ νοῦν τῶν δωρεῶν*). This sticheron defines the joyous, luminous theology of Great Lent: entering the fast not with gloom, but with brightness, washing the soul, and delighting in the virtues of the Holy Spirit.

[^489]: Rubric for the Entrance at Forgiveness Vespers: the celebrant vests in dark/purple vestments (*въ Багрѧ́нѡ Фелѡ́ни*) for the Entrance with the censer and taper-bearer, visually establishing the transition from the festal Pre-Lenten period into the solemn, penitential purple of Great Lent.

[^490]: The Great Prokeimenon of Cheesefare Sunday Vespers: *Turn not away Thy face from Thy servant, for I am in trouble...* (Ps. 68:18–19, 30, 33 [LXX]), chanted five times in Tone 8 with its verses to a slow, mournful melody. This rubric also establishes that throughout the Forty Days, the evening prostrations and metanias commence at *Vouchsafe, O Lord*.

[^491]: The Aposticha of Cheesefare Sunday Vespers features the celebrated idiomelon *Thy grace has shone forth, O Lord* (*Возсїя̀ блгⷣть твоя̀ Гдⷭ҇и*), proclaiming the commencement of the "acceptable time" of salvation (2 Cor. 6:2) and calling believers to put on the armor of light (Rom. 13:12). The concluding Theotokion is sung to the Tone 4 model *As one valiant among martyrs* (Greek: *Ὡς γενναῖον ἐν μάρτυσιν*; Slavonic: *Ꙗ҆́кѡ до́бля въ мꙋ́ченицѣхъ*).

[^492]: The Great Lenten Penitential Troparia at the conclusion of Sunday Vespers: *O Mother of God and Virgin, rejoice...*, *O Baptist of Christ, remember us all...*, and *Intercede for us, holy Apostles...*, each accompanied by a full bodily prostration to the ground (*метание великое*). The ancient Marian prayer *Beneath thy tender compassion* (*Подъ твою̀ млⷭ҇ть*) is recited without prostration.

[^493]: The solemn state intercession *O Heavenly King, strengthen our faithful sovereigns...* (*Нб҃ный Цр҃ю, вѣ́рныя Цр҃я на́ша ᲂу҆крѣпѝ...*), preserving the 1640 Kyivan text praying for Orthodox rulers, peace in the world, and the preservation of the monastery, recited by the reader before the Prayer of St. Ephrem.

[^494]: The Prayer of St. Ephrem the Syrian (*Моли́тва Преподо́бнагѡ Є҆фре́ма Сѵ́рина*), the quintessential prayer of Great Lent. The Kyivan rubric specifies that the brethren first stand with hands uplifted in mental supplication, reciting the three parts inwardly, accompanied by three great prostrations.

[^495]: The monastic Rite of Mutual Forgiveness (*Чи́нъ проще́нїя*): following the completion of Vespers, the brethren venerate the Precious Cross and the sacred icons at their stalls, bowing to one another to ask mutual forgiveness before embarking upon the ascetic labors of Clean Week.

[^496]: Small Compline (*Па́вечерница Мала́я*) on Cheesefare Sunday night: ordered with Lenten metanias and prostrations with the Prayer of St. Ephrem, keeping the service concise to allow rest after the labors of the preceding Vigil.

[^497]: Codicological and Typikon legislation for funerals and memorial services (*панихида / парастас*) during Great Lent: because the weekdays of Lent are strictly non-liturgical, no third-day, ninth-day, or special memorial services are celebrated during the week; all such memorials are deferred to Friday evenings and Saturday Liturgies. Regular daily commemorations and prosphora oblations for the newly departed resume only on Thomas Sunday (*Но́вая Недѣ́ля*) for a period of forty consecutive days.

[^498]: Typikon chapter heading: *Concerning the Feast of the Meeting of the Lord, Chapter 49* (*Ѡ҆ Пра́здницѣ Стрѣ́тенїа, Гла́: мл҃* [recte 49 / *м҃ѳ*]). The numeral *мл҃* in the Kyivan print is a typographical inversion or local chapter reckoning for chapter 49 of the Typikon, governing the liturgical confluence of Candlemas (Feb. 2) with Clean Monday.

[^499]: Astronomical and Paschalian note: Monday of the First Week of Great Lent (Clean Monday) is the absolute latest possible date in the Julian calendar on which the Feast of the Meeting of the Lord (February 2) can ever fall. Under the perpetual Julian Paschalion, the earliest possible date of Pascha is March 22, placing Clean Monday precisely on February 2.

[^500]: Comprehensive rubrics for celebrating the Feast of the Meeting of the Lord when falling on Clean Monday: the Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom is celebrated, but the ascetical discipline of Clean Week is preserved at the monastic table (dry food and wine are permitted, but fish is strictly forbidden). At Compline, the first section of the Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete is chanted without prostrations.

[^501]: Typikon rubric for the Feast of the First and Second Finding of the Honorable Head of the Holy Glorious Prophet, Forerunner, and Baptist John (February 24): if this feast coincides with Clean Monday, the entire festal service is anticipated and sung on Cheesefare Sunday, unless the church or monastery is dedicated to the Forerunner, in which case it is celebrated on Clean Monday with the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts.

[^502]: Monastic horology of Clean Week: the paraklesiarches (bell-ringer / sacristan) strikes the simandron or church bell (*знамена́етъ к̾ свѣ́тꙋ*) well before dawn to summon the brotherhood to the Midnight Office and Matins of Clean Monday.

[^503]: The Midnight Office (*Полунощница*) on Clean Monday: celebrated without prostrations during the psalmody, concluding with three great prostrations accompanied by the Prayer of St. Ephrem. The Kyivan rubric notes that this relaxation of psalmody prostrations is unique to the first day of the Fast; on all subsequent weekdays of Great Lent, the Midnight Office includes its full complement of prostrations.

[^504]: The Royal Office and opening of Matins on Clean Monday: the Six Psalms are preceded by the Trisagion and the Royal Psalms (Psalms 19 and 20), chanted without metanias, while the priest censes the sanctuary and nave. The rubric specifies that neither the local bishop nor the igumen is named in the introductory litany.

[^505]: Physical and ascetical discipline during the Six Psalms (*Шестопсалмие*): the monastic rule demands absolute stillness, silence, and internal concentration, hands folded across the chest, heads bowed, and eyes cast down while directing the inner spiritual gaze toward the East, with a total prohibition on whispering, spitting, or throat-clearing, treating the psalmody as an invisible conversation with God.

[^506]: Chanting of the Lenten *Alleluia* at Matins: replacing *God is the Lord*, the appointed monk and choirs chant *Alleluia* three times after each of four Scriptural verses from Isaiah 26 (*From night my spirit seeketh Thee early in the morning, O God...*; Isa. 26:9, 11, 15, 17), followed by the Triadica (Trinity Hymns) of the current Tone of the Oktoechos.

[^507]: Ascetical reading cycle of Great Lent: after the First Kathisma of Matins, the readings are drawn from the homilies and discourses of St. Ephrem the Syrian, a daily monastic instruction that continues throughout Great Lent until Holy and Great Tuesday of Passion Week (*до Вто́рника Цвѣ́тнагѡ* / Palm/Passion Tuesday).

[^508]: Tone 2 Sessional Hymn of Lord Joseph the Hymnographer after the 2nd Kathisma of Matins, sung to the melodic model *Being a wellspring of loving-kindness* (Greek: *Εὐσπλαγχνίας ὑπάρχουσα*; Slavonic: *Милосе́рдїя сꙋ́щи и҆сто́чникъ*), imploring Christ to accept the prayers and abstinence of the Fast as choice incense.

[^509]: Tone 2 Sessional Hymn of St. Theodore the Studite after the 3rd Kathisma of Matins, sung to the melodic model *We venerate Thine all-pure icon* (Greek: *Τὴν ἄχραντον εἰκόνα σου*; Slavonic: *Пречи́стомꙋ ти ѻ҆́бразꙋ покланя́емся*), extolling the virtues of fasting, prayer, chastity, and spiritual courage as radiant armor leading to the glory of the three-day Resurrection.

[^510]: Liturgical sequence preceding the Canons: after the 3rd Kathisma and a reading from the *Lausiac History* (*Лаѵсі́акъ*) of Palladius, Psalm 50 is recited, followed immediately by the First Biblical Ode from the Psalter (*Song of Moses*, Exod. 15:1–19), appointed to 14 verses beginning at Exodus 15:8 (*The waters grew thick as a wall*).

[^511]: Antiphonal performance practice of the Lenten Triodia: the troparia of the two canons (by Lord Joseph and St. Theodore the Studite) are sung alternately by the right and left choirs to the verses of the Biblical Odes. At the Trinity Troparion, both choirs unite in the center of the nave (*съвоку́пльшеся ѻ҆́ба ли́ка*) with the canonarch to sing with elevated voices.

[^512]: Typikon catalog of the Biblical Canticles (Odes) throughout the Holy Forty Days: detailing the assignment of the nine Biblical Odes across the weekdays of Lent, with the Canticle of the Theotokos (*Magnificat*, Luke 1:46–55) sung daily at Ode 9.

[^513]: Typikon ordinance governing the integration of the Menaion and Triodion canons: when the Triodion appoints odes (the Triodia), the daily canon of the Oktoechos is omitted to accommodate the two canons of the Menaion saints alongside the Lenten Triodia of Joseph and Theodore.

[^514]: Ode 1 of the Clean Monday Triodion by Lord Joseph the Hymnographer, Tone 2, Heirmos *Come, ye peoples* (Greek: *Δεῦτε λαοί*; Slavonic: *Грядѣ́те, лю́дїе*), reflecting the soul's deep lamentation and grief over prodigal living as the holy Fast begins.

[^515]: Ode 1 of the Clean Monday Triodion by St. Theodore the Studite, concluding with the ascetical typology of the Old Testament: the fiery ascent of the Prophet Elijah (4 Kings [2 Kings] 2:11) and the forty-day fast of Moses on Sinai (Exod. 34:28) as the biblical exemplars of spiritual warfare and divine vision.
