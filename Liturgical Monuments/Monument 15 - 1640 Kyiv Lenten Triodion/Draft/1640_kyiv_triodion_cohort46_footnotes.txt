[^1253]: The gathering of Polykindynos (Поликиндин) — The hymnographic wordplay on Gregory Akindynos (c. 1300–1348), originally a disciple of Palamas who later adopted Barlaam's anti-hesychast positions. Akindynos in Greek signifies "without danger" (ἀ- + κίνδυνος), inverted here by the hymnographer into "Polykindynos" ("full of many dangers", πολυκίνδυνος) because his nominalist doctrine threatened the Orthodox understanding of real communion with God.
[^1254]: The pious Emperor — Refers to Byzantine Emperor Andronicus III Palaeologus (1328–1341) and co-emperor John VI Kantakouzenos (1347–1354), who presided over synods in Constantinople vindicating the Palamite theology of the divine and uncreated energies.
[^1255]: Barlaam of Seminara (c. 1290–1348) — The Greek-Calabrian philosopher and monk who opposed the hesychasts and attacked their experiential vision of the uncreated light of Mount Tabor, condemned at the Council of Constantinople in June 1341.
[^1256]: Having become a god (Богъ бывъ) — An allusion to the patristic doctrine of deification (theosis), whereby through divine grace and uncreated energy man is made a partaker of the divine nature (2 Pet. 1:4) without confusion of created and uncreated essences.
[^1257]: Habitation of hesychia (безмолвія селеніе) — The inner silence, stillness, and spiritual watchfulness (nepsis) cultivated by the Athonite hesychast monks in the practice of the Jesus Prayer, dogmatically vindicated by St. Gregory Palamas.
[^1258]: Sovereign Lady of all (Владычице всѣхъ) — The incipit of the Sunday Exapostilarion Theotokion in Tone 2, addressed to the Mother of God as Queen and Mediatrix.
[^1259]: fol. 323 (Листъ тк҃г) — Cross-reference pointing back to the order of Sunday Matins and the Great Doxology on physical leaf 336 (printed fol. 323).
[^1260]: Common service for a single Hierarch (якоже Стятелю единому обще) — Directs that the remaining Liturgy variables follow the Common of a Hierarch for St. Gregory Palamas, conjoined with the Sunday Resurrectional variables.
[^1261]: fol. 323 (Листъ тк҃г) — Cross-reference directing to the opening psalmody and Penitential Stichera for Sunday evening at the start of Lent on physical leaf 336 (printed fol. 323).
[^1262]: Tone 8 Podoben: "Innumerable" (Неисчетенъ) — The prosomoion melodic model derived from the Penitential Stichera of the Octoechos, characterized by themes of compunction and the dread of future judgment.
[^1263]: fol. 129 and 194 (Листъ рх҃ѳ и рц҃д) — Directs the reader to the Great Prokeimenon "Turn not away Thy face" (Ps. 68:18 LXX [69:17]) and its accompanying psalm verses, printed on physical leaf 142 (printed fol. 129) and physical leaf 207 (printed fol. 194).
[^1264]: Sunday of Meatfare, at the Aposticha, fol. 72 (Листъ о҃в) — Points to the Sunday of the Last Judgment on physical leaf 85 (printed fol. 72), where the Theotokion "The heavenly choirs sing" (Небесная поютъ) is printed in full.
[^1265]: fol. 354 (Листъ тнд҃) — Points to the continuous order of the Lenten Aposticha and closing prayers on physical leaf 367 (printed fol. 354).
[^1266]: fol. 124 and 109 (Листъ рх҃д и р҃ѳ) — Directs to the rubrical order for Sunday evening Lenten Vespers and the Prayer of St. Ephrem the Syrian on physical leaves 122 (printed fol. 109) and 137 (printed fol. 124).
[^1267]: Compassionate love (соузъ страстное) — A Slavonic calque of the Greek συμпаθές (sympathetic, compassionate love), expressing God's infinite condescension and mercy toward fallen mankind.
[^1268]: Three days of Israel (Exod. 19:10–11) — The typology of Israel's three days of purification before the giving of the Law on Mount Sinai, prefiguring the three weeks of fasting leading up to the veneration of the Precious and Life-Giving Cross on the Third Sunday of Great Lent.
[^1269]: Gideon's three hundred (Judg. 7:7, 19–22) — Patristically interpreted as an image of spiritual warfare: the earthen pitchers broken to display the blazing torches prefigure ascetic self-denial and fasting that break the passions of the flesh to reveal the light of the virtues.
[^1270]: Elisha feeding the prophets (4 Kings 4:38–44 LXX [2 Kings 4]) — The prophetic miracle during the famine at Gilgal, where the Prophet Elisha rendered the bitter pottage wholesome and fed a hundred men with twenty barley loaves, prefiguring the spiritual nourishment of the Fast.
[^1271]: Troparion of the Prophecy (Тропарь пророчества) — The ancient troparion preceding the Old Testament prophecy reading at the Sixth Hour during Great Lent, preserving the archaic Palestinian cathedral structure of the mid-day Lenten office.
