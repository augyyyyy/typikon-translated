[^1286]: Num. 25:3; Ps. 105:28 LXX [106:28] / Ex. 32:6; 1 Cor. 10:7 — The apostasy of Israel at Baal-peor (Веелфегоръ), where the people ate sacrifices offered to the dead and rose up to wanton play, is contrasted in the Tuesday Ode 9 Triodion canticle with the holy fast and ascent of Moses on Sinai.
[^1287]: Luke 15:18, 21 — The confession of the Prodigal Son ("I have sinned against heaven and before Thee, and am no more worthy to be called Thy son") provides the structural text for the Tuesday Aposticha Sticheron in Tone 7.
[^1288]: Tone 7 Aposticha Sticheron — "And as for the handwriting of my hands wherewith I am clothed" (къ грамотѣ же рукы имиже одѣянъ есмъ) alludes both to the handwritten bond of debt canceled by Christ on the Cross (Greek χειρόγραφον, Col. 2:14) and to patristic imagery depicting one's recorded sins as a garment of shame.
[^1289]: Isa. 9:9–14 LXX — Sixth Hour Old Testament reading for Tuesday of the Third Week. The 1640 Kyivan text translates the Septuagint reading against the Masoretic, noting Ephraim and Samaria's defiance and the prophetic judgment upon Israel.
[^1290]: Tone 3 Podoben: "They weighed thirty pieces of silver" (Поставиша) — Byzantine automelon Ἔστησαν τὰ τριάκοντα ἀργύρια (Zech. 11:12; Matt. 26:15), adapted here to the Tuesday Vespers Stichera of St. Joseph the Hymnographer focusing on repentance and the Holy Cross.
[^1291]: Tone 2 Podoben: "Let Him be crucified" (Да распнется) — Byzantine automelon Σταυρωθήτω (Matt. 27:22), sung during Great Lent to evoke joyful ascetic preparation for the veneration of the Precious Cross in Mid-Lent.
[^1292]: Gen. 7:1–5 LXX — Tuesday Vespers Old Testament reading. The preservation of Noah and every living pair within the ark during the forty-day deluge serves in Byzantine patristic typology as a figure of baptism and the forty-day fast of Great Lent.
[^1293]: Prov. 9:6 LXX — The bracketed clause (*въ вѣки царствуйте, и взыщите премдрость да поживете*) translates the Septuagint expansion ἵνα εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα βασιλεύσητε, καὶ ζητήσατε φρόνησιν ἵνα βιώσητε ("that ye may reign for ever, and seek wisdom that ye may live"), preserved in brackets by Mohyla's editors to record collation against Greek manuscripts.
[^1294]: Prov. 9:7 LXX — The bracketed clause (*ѡбличеніа же нечестивому, мозоліе емꙋ*) represents Greek LXX ἔλεγχοι δὲ τῷ ἀσεβεῖ μώλωπες αὐτῷ ("and reproofs to the ungodly are wounds to him"), absent in the Hebrew Masoretic recension but retained in the Greek liturgical text.
[^1295]: Prov. 9:10 LXX — The bracketed gloss (*разꙋмѣти же законъ помысла естъ блгагѡ*) translates Greek LXX τὸ δὲ γνῶναι νόμον διανοίας ἐστὶν ἀγαθῆς ("and to know the law is the sign of a good mind"), documented as a Septuagint witness in the Kyivan print.
[^1296]: Tone 2 Podoben: "When from the Tree" (Егда от древа) — Byzantine automelon Ὅτε ἐκ τοῦ ξύλου σε νεκρόν, sung at the Aposticha of Tuesday Vespers as a Stavrotheotokion anticipating the mid-Lenten veneration of the Holy Cross.
[^1297]: Tone 3 Podoben: "Beholding the beauty of thy virginity" (Красотѣ дѣтва) — Byzantine automelon Τὴν ὡραιότητα τῆς παρθενίας σου, the festive sessional melody adapted here to an ascetic sessional hymn on fasting and the Cross.
[^1298]: Tone 2 Podoben: "Having risen from the tomb" (Воскресъ от гроба) — Byzantine automelon Ἀναστὰς ἐκ τοῦ μνήματος, the Tone 2 Resurrectional sessional hymn adapted to mid-Lenten ascetic contemplation.
[^1299]: Tone 2 Podoben: "Being the wellspring of loving-kindness" (Милосердія) — Byzantine automelon Τῆς εὐσπλαγχνίας ὑπάρχουσα πηγή, sung as a Stavrotheotokion.
[^1300]: Tone 3 Ode 3 Heirmos: "The confirmation" (Утвержденіе) — Greek Τὸ στερέωμα τῶν ἐπὶ σοὶ πεποιθότων, calling upon Christ as the bedrock of believers.
[^1301]: Tone 3 Ode 8 Heirmos: "Unto Him Who walked upon the unstable waters" (Непостоянному) — Greek Τῷ ἐν ἀстаσίαις..., evoking Christ's divine power over creation.
[^1302]: Tone 2 Ode 8 Heirmos: "In the bush unto Moses" (Въ купинѣ Мѡѵсеи) — Greek Ἐν βάτῳ Μωσεῖ, typifying the virgin birth and divine revelation.
[^1303]: Tone 3 Ode 9 Heirmos: "In the shadow of the law" (Въ законѣ сѣни) — Greek Ἐν νόμου σκιᾷ καὶ γράμματι τύπον κατίδωμεν οἱ πιστοί, typologically interpreting Old Testament types (the bronze serpent, Num. 21) as fulfilled on the Cross.
