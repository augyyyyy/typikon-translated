[^291]: The second Heirmos of Ode 4 (`Прише́лъ є҆сѝ ѿ дв҃ы`, Greek: `Ἐλήλυθας ἐκ Παρθένου`) is appointed from the ancient Byzantine Triodion canon of Saint Joseph the Hymnographer for Cheesefare Thursday.

[^292]: The penitential prayer alludes to the Prayer of Manasseh, King of Judah (2 Chron. 33; Ode 12 of the liturgical Psalter), a central patristic paradigm of contrition appointed throughout the services of Great Lent.

[^293]: The second Heirmos of Ode 8 (`ᲊстраши́сѧ ѿроко́въ чⷭ҇тыхъ`, Greek: `Ἔπτηξε παίδων εὐαγῶν`) commemorates the Three Holy Youths in the Babylonian furnace (Dan. 3), whose bodily incorruption through ascetic abstinence typifies the spiritual victory of the Fast.

[^294]: Typological allusion to the murder of Abel by Cain (Gen. 4:3–8), contrasted in Lenten hymnography with the sincere and blameless spiritual offering of bodily mortification and prayer.

[^295]: Kyivan theological rendering: `по ᲂу҆поста́си` (hypostatically / according to hypostasis), articulating Chalcedonian and Damascene Christology regarding the substantial union of divine and human natures in the incarnate Word.

[^296]: The second Heirmos of Ode 9 (`Невъмѣсти́магѡ Бг҃а`, Greek: `Τὸν ἀχώρητον Θεόν`), celebrating the divine maternity of the Theotokos whose womb contained the uncontainable Word.

[^297]: In the 1640 Kyiv Triodion, the rubric reads: `Свѣти́ленъ, Гла́съ, И҆, Сла́ва въ вы́шнихъ:`, omitting the specific Tone number of the Exapostilarion before the Lesser Doxology (*Gloria in excelsis*).

[^298]: Internal cross-reference in body rubric: `ꙗ҆́коже и҆ вы́ше въ вто́ сы́рный ᲂу҆каза́сѧ` (see fol. 88), referencing folio 88 of the 1640 Kyiv Triodion (physical leaf p102), which governs the prostrations and liturgical structure of Cheesefare weekday Vespers.

[^299]: Internal cross-reference in body rubric: `И҆ про́: лн҃, п҃ѕ.` (see fol. 86), referencing folio 86 of the 1640 Kyiv Triodion (physical leaf p100), directing the completion of the service and the order of prostrations at the Dismissal.

[^300]: Internal cross-reference in body rubric: `И҆ тꙋ ᲂу҆ка́ Пн҃: в Срѐ: Сы́рну.` (see fol. 78), referencing folio 78 of the 1640 Kyiv Triodion (physical leaf p92), regulating the rubrics for when the Feast of the Meeting of the Lord or the Finding of the Head of Saint John the Baptist coincides with an aliturgical weekday of Cheesefare Week.

[^301]: Patristic readings appointed for Matins of Cheesefare Friday: Saint Basil the Great's *De Jejunio*, Homilia I (*In illud: Clama ne cesses... Canite in initio mensis tuba*, PG 31:163–184) and his ascetic work *Sermo asceticus* (*Quomodo monacho se gerere oporteat*, PG 31:619–626).

[^302]: Liturgical rule governing the synthesis of the Octoechos, Menaion, and Triodion canons: when the Triodion appoints a double-heirmos Tri-ode (such as those by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer), the corresponding Odes from the Octoechos and the Menaion are entirely omitted to accommodate the twofold heirmological structure.

[^303]: The Heirmos of Ode 5 (`Вскꙋ́ю мѧ ѿри́нꙋлъ є҆сѝ`, Greek: `Ἵνα τί με ἀπώσω`), drawn from Psalm 87:15 (LXX) and Isaiah 26:9, expressing the soul's alienation from God through sin and its longing for restoration through the light of divine commandments.

[^304]: Scriptural allusion to the forty-day fast of the Prophet Moses on Mount Sinai (Exod. 24:18, 34:28) as the biblical prototype of the contemplative vision of God (*theoria*) attained through ascetic purification.

[^305]: Strict Nicene-Constantinopolitan and Damascene Trinitarian formula: `сла́вимъ въ є҆ди́номъ є҆стствѣ̀... и҆ раздѣлѧ́юще ли́цы, несмѣ́снѡ непрело́жнѡ слꙋжа́ще` ("we glorify in one essence... and distinguishing the Persons, serving without confusion and without change"), refuting both Sabellian modalism and Arian polytheism.
