[^1]: Collated from the Greek (*Ѡ Еллинскагѡ изслѣдованъ*): Demonstrates the systematic textual collation undertaken by the scholarly circle of Metropolitan Petro Mohyla and the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra brotherhood directly against Byzantine Greek Triodia (Venice printed editions and Byzantine manuscript witnesses). This collation was intended to rectify scribal errors, corruptions, and divergent redactions that had accumulated in East Slavic liturgical manuscripts, establishing a philologically robust pre-Nikonian Kyivan recension.
[^2]: Now printed the second time (*Второе Тѵпомъ издано*): The first Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra folio edition of the Lenten Triodion was published in 1627 under Archimandrite Zacharias Kopystensky and proto-printer Pamvo Berynda. This 1640 imprint represents the revised second Lavra edition produced under the direct archpastoral supervision of Metropolitan Petro Mohyla.
[^3]: Dating formula: The imprint records the date according to both the Byzantine Creation Era (*Anno Mundi* 7148) and the Christian Era of the Incarnation (*Anno Domini* 1640), on March 23, in the 8th Indiction (March 23, 1640 O.S.).
[^4]: Armorial initials (*Ѳ Т / П / Σ / П / Z / K*): The Greek and Cyrillic initials flanking the heraldic crest stand for: *Ѳеодор Проскура Сущанский, Писарь Земский Киевский* (Theodor Proskura-Sushchansky, Land Scribe [Pisarz Ziemski] of the Kyiv Voivodeship), a prominent Orthodox nobleman and magistrate of the Commonwealth, and major patron of the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra.
[^5]: The Sushchansky-Proskura coat of arms (*Клейнотъ*): An armorial variation belonging to the Polish-Ruthenian heraldic tradition (associated with the *Kozieł* / *Proskura* arms). The escutcheon bears an upward-pointing arrow crossed by a transverse bar resembling the Christian Cross, issuing from a bifurcated base; it is ensigned with a nobleman's crowned helmet and three ostrich feathers.
[^6]: Endowed its own Archer (*своего Стрѣлца даровало*): A learned Baroque conceit (*conceptus*) linking the family's martial symbol of the arrow with the celestial constellation Sagittarius (the Archer of the Zodiac), asserting that the heavens themselves reflect and honor the virtues of the House of Sushchansky.
[^7]: Exarch of the Holy Apostolic Throne of Constantinople: A canonical title borne by the Metropolitans of Kyiv, reflecting their direct canonical subordination and jurisdictional tie to the Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople, as well as the stauropegial dignity enjoyed by the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra.
[^8]: Land Scribe of the Kyiv Voivodeship (*Писарь Земский Киевский* / *Pisarz Ziemski Kijowski*): A high-ranking judicial and administrative magistrate elected by the nobility of the Kyiv Voivodeship to serve in the land court (*sąd ziemski*) and provincial assembly (*sejmik*), responsible for maintaining the official territorial registers and acts.
[^9]: Luke 4:4 (citing Deuteronomy 8:3): "And Jesus answered him, saying, It is written, That man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word of God."
[^10]: Luke 21:36: "Watch therefore, and pray always, that you may be accounted worthy to escape all these things that shall come to pass, and to stand before the Son of Man."
[^11]: St. Isidore of Seville (*Sententiarum libri tres*, Book III, chapter 50: *De oratione*): "Prayer and reading are the two ways by which we converse with God. If anyone wishes to remain always with God, let him pray and read without ceasing; for when we pray, we speak with God, and when we read, God speaks with us."
[^12]: 1 Thessalonians 5:17: "Pray without ceasing."
[^13]: St. John Chrysostom, *Homilies on the Epistle to the Ephesians*, Homily 13: St. John extols the omnipotent efficacy of pure prayer, asserting that its spiritual force transcends all physical laws and the powers of created nature.
[^14]: Scriptural paradigms of the power of prayer: Exodus 14 (the miraculous division of the Red Sea by Moses); Daniel 3 (the preservation of the Three Holy Children Ananias, Azarias, and Misael in the fiery furnace of Babylon); 3 Kings 18 [1 Kings 18 MT] (the Prophet Elijah shutting heaven and praying for rain to moisten the earth); and Matthew 7:7 ("Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and you shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you").
[^15]: St. Augustine on prayer: The holy Father defines prayer as the spiritual breath and enlivenment of the soul, a joy to the holy Angels, a scourge to demons, a sacred offering unto God, and the indispensable foundation of every virtue.
[^16]: Incorrupt bodies of the Pechersk saints: Metropolitan Petro Mohyla directs the reader's attention to the tangible, empirical reality of the hundreds of incorrupt relics of the monastic saints resting in the Near (*St. Anthony's*) and Far (*St. Theodosius's*) Caves of the Kyiv Lavra as living proof of divine grace and the truth of the Orthodox faith.
[^17]: King Władysław IV Vasa (*Władysław IV Waza*, reigned 1632–1648): King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania, who in the "Articles of Pacification" (1632) and royal diplomas formally legalized and re-established the Orthodox hierarchy of Kyiv, recognizing Petro Mohyla as legitimate Metropolitan of Kyiv, Halych, and all Rus'.
[^18]: The 1627 Kyiv Triodion edition: Archimandrite Zacharias Kopystensky produced the editio princeps of the Pechersk Lenten Triodion in 1627. Due to the high demand across Ruthenian parishes and monasteries, copies quickly became scarce (*na skupost'*), necessitating this comprehensive 1640 edition.
[^19]: The Memento Mori tradition: Refers to the well-documented classical and early Christian practice—cited by Herodotus (*Histories* II.78) and Christian moralists—of displaying skulls or coffins at banquets to admonish participants of the vanity of earthly delights and the certainty of divine judgment.
[^20]: Matthew 3:2: "Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand."
[^21]: The three stones of repentance: Petro Mohyla allegorizes the three stones from David's sling (1 Kings 17:40 [1 Sam 17:40 MT]) into the three traditional sacramental components of genuine repentance: contrition of the heart (*skrusheniye* / *contritio cordis*), oral confession (*spovidaniye hrikhov* / *confessio oris*), and satisfaction/acts of penance (*dosyt' chynennya* / *satisfactio operis*).
[^22]: Archimandrite Zacharias Kopystensky (d. 1627): Renowned Kyivan churchman, polemicist (author of the *Palinodiya*), and Archimandrite of the Kyiv-Pechersk Caves Lavra from 1624 until his repose in 1627; spiritual predecessor (*antecessor*) of Petro Mohyla.
[^23]: Doubtful Mars (*vontplyvomu Marsowi*): A typical Baroque humanist mythological allusion to Mars (the god of war), evoking Teodor Proskura's youthful military expeditions defending the southeastern borders of the Commonwealth against Tatar and Ottoman incursions.
[^24]: Matthew 11:12: "And from the days of John the Baptist until now the kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and the violent take it by force."
