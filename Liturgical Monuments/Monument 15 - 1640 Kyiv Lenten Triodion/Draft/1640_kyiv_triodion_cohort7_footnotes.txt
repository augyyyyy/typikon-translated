The heirmos links the theophany of the unburnt bush on Mount Sinai (Exod. 3:1–6) with the virginal childbearing of the Mother of God, through which salvation is granted unto the faithful (Luke 1:46–55).

[^191]: The extensive hymnography of Meatfare Saturday is renowned for its exhaustive enumeration of diverse manners of mortal end—lightning, thunderbolts, earthquakes, maritime shipwreck, venomous beasts, violent assault, falls from precipices, war, hanging, and sudden unprovided death—pleading that Christ the righteous Judge extend His merciful rest unto all who departed in Orthodox faith, regardless of circumstance.

The stichera provide a profound memento mori on the frailty of human flesh, anatomical dissolution in the grave, and the vain pursuits of worldly vanity in contrast to eternal life in Christ.

[^197]: Sticheron of St. John of Damascus (*Дамаскинова творенія*), reflecting upon the primordial catastrophe of the Fall of Adam through tasting the forbidden fruit, the venom of the serpent, the universal entrance of death, and its overthrow by the Incarnate Lord.

[^198]: Internal codex cross-reference: *Зри Листъ, л҃в* ("See fol. 32"), which corresponds to physical leaf p46 of the 1640 Triodion codex, where the troparion *Глубиною мудрости* (*Glubinoyu mudrosti*, "By the depth of wisdom") and theotokion *Тебе градъ* (*Tebe grad*, "Thee, a wall and a haven") are printed in full at Vespers.

[^199]: The liturgical lectionary readings for Meatfare Saturday: Epistle of the day: 1 Cor. 6:12–20 (Pericope 146); Epistle for the departed: 1 Thess. 4:13–17 (Pericope 270); Gospel of the day: Luke 21:8–9, 25–27, 33–36 (Pericope 105); Gospel for the departed: John 5:24–30 (Pericope 16).

[^200]: Rubrical invariant: The entire funeral and memorial order of Meatfare Saturday is repeated without alteration on Trinity Saturday (the Saturday preceding Holy Pentecost), constituting the two universal ancestral Saturdays of the Byzantine liturgical year.
