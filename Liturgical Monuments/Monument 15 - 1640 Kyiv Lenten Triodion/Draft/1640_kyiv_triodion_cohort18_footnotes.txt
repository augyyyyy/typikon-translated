[^461]: Kontakion of Cheesefare Sunday (Tone 6, Idiomelon / Samoglasen). Attributed to Saint Romanos the Melodist (6th century). In Byzantine and Slavonic hymnography, this serves as the prooimion to his celebrated kontakion on the Fall of Adam.

[^462]: Ikos of Cheesefare Sunday. The 1640 Kyivan edition preserves four full strophes of Romanos the Melodist's original kontakion on the Fall of Adam, whereas later Russian Synodal editions abridge the Ikos to the opening strophe alone. This unabridged four-strophe witness reflects the conservative archaism of the 17th-century Kyiv-Pechersk liturgical recension.

[^463]: The Synaxarion for Cheesefare Sunday (Commemoration of the Expulsion of Adam from Paradise) is presented here in the Early Modern Ruthenian (*prosta mova*) prose recension established under Metropolitan Petro Mohyla at the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra.

[^464]: Marginal Scriptural citation: Matt. 4:2 (`Мѳ҃: д҃. в҃.`), the forty-day fast of Christ in the wilderness, fulfilling and reversing the intemperance of Adam.

[^465]: Marginal Patristic and Jewish concordance: Philo of Alexandria (`Фїлѡ́нъ`), *Quaestiones et Solutiones in Genesim* I. The variant opinions regarding the duration of Adam's stay in Paradise (one hundred years according to Philo; seven hours, seven days, or seven years according to various early Christian and Jewish commentaries) reflect ancient rabbinic and patristic chronographical speculation.

[^466]: Marginal Gospel citations: Matt. 27:45 (`Мѳ҃ к҃ѕ. м҃ѕ.` [an epigraphic erratum for Matt. 27:45–46]); Mark 15:33 (`Мр҃к҃: є҃і. л҃г.`); Luke 23:44 (`Лꙋк҃: кг҃. м҃д.`); John 19:14 (`І҆ѡ҃: ѳ҃і. д҃і.`). The typological concordance links Adam stretching forth his hand to the forbidden tree at the sixth hour of Friday with Christ stretching forth His hands upon the Cross at the sixth hour on Great and Holy Friday.

[^467]: Saint Gregory of Nazianzus (the Theologian), *Oration 38: On the Theophany* (and *Oration 45: On Holy Pascha*, §8). St. Gregory allegorizes the trees of Paradise as divine thoughts and intellectual contemplation (*noesis* / *theoria*), and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil as contemplation beyond man's spiritual maturity.

[^468]: Saint John Chrysostom, *Homilies on Genesis* (Homilies 16 and 17). Chrysostom insists on preserving the literal reality of the sensible Paradise while expounding its spiritual significance, rejecting purely allegorical dismissals of the physical garden.

[^469]: Marginal Scriptural citation: Luke 1:26 (`Лꙋк҃: а҃. к҃ѕ.`), the Feast of the Annunciation of the Theotokos (March 25), which consistently falls within the period of Great Lent or Great Week.

[^470]: Marginal Scriptural citation: John 11 (`І҆ѡа́: а҃і.`), the raising of Lazarus commemorated on Lazarus Saturday.

[^471]: Marginal Scriptural citations: Matt. 21:1 (`Мѳ҃: к҃а. а҃.`); Luke 19:29 (`Лꙋк: ѳ҃і. к҃ѳ.`); John 12:13 (`І҆ѡ: в҃і: г҃і.`), Christ's Triumphal Entry into Jerusalem commemorated on Palm Sunday.

[^472]: The liturgical reading of the Acts of the Apostles begins at the Paschal Vigil / Matins and continues throughout the fifty days of the Pentecostarion (from Pascha to Pentecost), bearing witness to the resurrection of Christ through apostolic preaching and miracles.

[^473]: The classical patristic computation of Great Lent as the spiritual tithe (*decima*) of the year is expounded by Saint John Cassian (*Conferences* 21.24–25) and Saint Dorotheus of Gaza (*Instructions* 15). The 36.5 fasting days constitute approximately one-tenth of the 365 days of the solar year.

[^474]: Marginal Scriptural citation: Matt. 4:2 (`Мѳ҃: д҃. в҃.`), the Lord's forty-day fast in the wilderness.

[^475]: Marginal Scriptural citations: Exod. 24:18; 34:28 (`И҆сх: к҃д. и҃і. и҃: л҃д. к҃и.`); Deut. 9:9, 18 (`Девтеро: ѳ҃. ѳ҃. и҃і.`), the forty-day fasts of Moses on Mount Sinai before receiving the tablets of the Law.

[^476]: Marginal Scriptural citation: 3 Kings (1 Kings) 19:8 (`Г҃ Цр҃: ѳ҃і. и҃.`), the forty-day journey of the Prophet Elijah unto Mount Horeb on the strength of the miraculous cake.

[^477]: Marginal Scriptural citation: Dan. 10:2–3 (`Дан: і҃. г҃.`), the three-week fast and mourning of the Prophet Daniel.

[^478]: Tone 2 Exapostilarion melodic model: *Give ear, ye women* (Slavonic: *Же́ны внꙋ́шите* / *Же́ны оу҆слы́шите*; Greek: *Γυναῖκες ἀκουτίσθητε*).

[^479]: Tone 2 Exapostilarion melodic model: *Let us assemble with the disciples* (Slavonic: *Съ ᲂу҆чн҃ки и҆де́мъ* / *Съ ᲂу҆чени́ки взы́демъ*; Greek: *Τοῖς μαθηταῖς συνέλθωμεν*).

[^480]: The Praises (*Lauds* / *Chvalite*) at Sunday Matins combine the psalmody of Psalms 148, 149, and 150 with stichera: 4 Resurrectional stichera in the Tone of the week, 1 Anatolian sticheron, and 3 proper idiomelic stichera from the Triodion for Cheesefare Sunday.

[^481]: The third Triodion sticheron at the Praises synthesizes the overarching theological typology of Cheesefare Sunday: Adam's loss of Paradise through sensual indulgence is countered by the forty-day fast of Moses, which purifies the soul to behold the uncreated glory of God and join the angelic choirs.

[^482]: Liturgical rubric for Cheesefare Sunday: the regular Sunday Gospel Sticheron (the Eothinon hymn celebrating the Resurrection) is omitted and replaced by the solemn idiomelon of Great Lent in Tone 6 (*Приспѣ̀ вре́мя, дх҃о́вныхъ по́двигѡвъ нача́ло*), anticipating the penitential discipline of the Great Fast.
