[^770]: Rubric specifies chanting the Canon from the Menaion for the day's commemorated saint to 6 troparia, followed by the Triodion canons (here the Triodion comprises two complementary canons for Odes 5, 8, and 9, by St. Joseph the Hymnographer and St. Theodore the Studite). The biblical canticles from the Psalter are chanted in the customary Lenten order.

[^771]: Heirmos of Ode 5 (Tone 1) by St. Joseph the Hymnographer. Incipit: "Просвѣще́нїе въ тмѣ̀" (cf. Isa. 9:2; 26:9). [^772]: Heirmos of Ode 5 (Tone 1) of the second Canon, composed by St. Theodore the Studite. Incipit: "Но́щи мимоше́дшей" (cf. Rom. 13:12). [^773]: Heirmos of Ode 8 (Tone 1) by St. Joseph the Hymnographer. Incipit: "Въ пещи ѻ҆́гненной" (cf. Dan. 3:57). [^774]: Heirmos of Ode 8 (Tone 1) by St. Theodore the Studite. Incipit: "Въ кꙋпинѣ̀ Мѡѷсе́ꙋ" (cf. Exod. 3:2). Full text: "In the bush unto Moses, the Virgin's childbearing upon Mount Sinai was prefigured of old: sing, bless, and exalt Him above all unto all ages!"

[^778]: Rubric regarding the Menaion commemoration: on the first Saturday of Great Lent (the commemoration of the Great Martyr Theodore the Recruit and the Miracle of the Kolyva), the commemoration of any minor Menaion saint occurring on Saturday or Sunday is transferred to Compline or sung at the discretion of the Ecclesiarch, so as not to displace the solemn festival of St. Theodore.

[^779]: Rubric directing that at Lord, I have cried on Friday evening (opening the commemoration of St. Theodore the Recruit), 4 Martyrika from the Oktoechos are taken following the Idiomelon of the day, before the 4 Idiomela of St. Theodore.

[^780]: St. Theodore the Recruit (Tyro) is celebrated for numerous posthumous miracles recounted in his encomium by St. Gregory of Nyssa and liturgical tradition: recovering runaway or stolen slaves, revealing stolen property, healing the sick, delivering prisoners, protecting sailors, and delivering the widow's son from Saracen captivity.
