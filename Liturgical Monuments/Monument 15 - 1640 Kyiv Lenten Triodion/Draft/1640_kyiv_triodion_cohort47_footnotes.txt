[^1272]: Isa. 8:13–14 LXX — Sanctify the Lord of Sabaoth Himself (Κύριον τῶν δυνάμεων αὐτὸν ἁγιάσατε). The Kyivan Triodion preserves the Septuagint text against the Masoretic recension, with a marginal gloss indicating the epigraphic Greek reading for the stumbling-stone (*въ язвите[л]...*, alluding to Greek προσκόμματι / πτώματι).
[^1273]: Isa. 8:19 LXX — Ventriloquists and belly-speakers (чревогадателя... чревобѣснующихъ) translates Greek ἐγγαστριμύθους (those who speak from the belly, necromancers/soothsayers), warning Israel against pagan divination and consulting the dead concerning the living.
[^1274]: Isa. 9:1 LXX — "Drink this first quickly!" (Сіе прежде скоро пій) translates the cryptic Septuagint opening of chapter 9: Τοῦτο πρῶτον πίε, ταχὺ ποίει, which contrasts the bitter cup of affliction with the sudden dawn of the Messianic light in Galilee of the Gentiles.
[^1275]: Isa. 9:6 LXX — "Angel of Great Counsel" (великагѡ совѣта агглъ) translates Greek Μεγάλης βουλῆς ἄγγελος, the classic Septuagint messianic title applied by the Church Fathers to the pre-incarnate Logos and Messiah, enclosed in editorial parentheses in the 1640 Kyivan print.
[^1276]: Tone 3 Podoben: "Come, all ye" (Пріидите вси) — Corresponds to the Byzantine automelon Δεῦτε ἅπαντες, sung during Great Lent to evoke joyful ascetic preparation for the veneration of the Precious Cross in Mid-Lent.
[^1277]: Gen. 6:14 LXX — Ark of square timbers (древъ негниющихъ [или кедровыхъ четвероуглныхъ]). The Septuagint reads ἐκ ξύλων τετραγώνων ("of squared timbers"), corresponding to the Hebrew *'atsei-gofer* (gopher wood). The Kyivan editor provides both the traditional Slavic rendering ("uncorruptible wood") and the Greek-collated gloss in brackets ("or squared cedar timbers").
[^1278]: Prov. 8:11 LXX — The bracketed clause (*преизбери́те же чꙋ́вствіа зла́тꙋ чи́стꙋ*) represents Greek καὶ προσλάβεσθε γνῶσιν ὑπὲρ χρυσίον κεκαθαρμένον ("and receive knowledge rather than pure gold"), a Septuagint variant placed in brackets by Mohyla's philological editors to mark collation against Greek manuscripts.
[^1279]: Tone 1 Podoben: "When the stone had been sealed" (Камени знаменану) — Byzantine automelon Τοῦ λίθου σφραγισθέντος, the primary Tone 1 Resurrectional Apolytikion melody, applied here to the Tuesday Matins Sessional Hymn on repentance and almsgiving.
[^1280]: Tone 3 Podoben: "Let the heavens rejoice" (Да веселя́тся) — Byzantine automelon Εὐφραινέσθω τὰ οὐράνια, the Resurrectional Apolytikion of Tone 3, adapted here to an ascetic sessional hymn anticipating the mid-Lenten veneration of the Holy Cross.
[^1281]: Deut. 32:39 LXX — Heirmos: "See, see that I am God" (Видите видите, яко Богъ азъ есмь). The second ode of the Great Canon and Lenten canticles takes its scriptural theme from the Song of Moses in Deuteronomy 32, emphasizing divine majesty, righteous judgment, and repentance.
[^1282]: Tone 3 Ode 8 Heirmos: "Him at Whom the angels tremble" (Егоже ужасаются) — Greek Ὃν φρίττουσιν ἄγγελοι, celebrating the awe of the angelic hosts before the divine throne, introducing the theme of eschatological judgment.
[^1283]: Tone 3 Ode 8 Heirmos: "Unto the children in the flame" (Во пламени к дѣтем) — Greek Ἐν τῇ φλογὶ τοῖς παισὶν Ἑβραίων συγκαταβάντα, referring to the Three Holy Children in the Babylonian furnace (Dan. 3) and typifying Christ's saving descent.
[^1284]: Tone 4 Ode 9 Heirmos: "The light-bearing cloud" (Свѣтоносный облакъ) — Greek Τὴν φωτοφόρον νεφέλην, a Marian hymnographic model depicting the Mother of God as the radiant cloud upon whom Christ, the Sun of Righteousness, descended to enlighten the world.
[^1285]: Tone 3 Ode 9 Heirmos: "On Mount Sinai" (На сїнаетстѣй горѣ) — Greek Ἐν Σιναίῳ τῷ ὄρει, invoking Moses' ascent of Mount Sinai (Ex. 19; 33–34) where fasting prepared the prophet to see the hinder parts of God in type, fulfilled spiritually in Christ.
