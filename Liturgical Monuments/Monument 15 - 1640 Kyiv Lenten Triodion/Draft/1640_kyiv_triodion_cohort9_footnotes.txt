[^234]: Ode 8 Heirmos (*Егоже воинства небесная славят* / *Yego zhe voinstva nebesnaya slavyat*). Greek incipit: *Ὃν στρατιαὶ οὐρανῶν δοξάζουσι*. Structured upon the biblical Canticle of the Three Holy Children in the Babylonian furnace (Dan. 3:57–88 [LXX]: "O all ye works of the Lord, bless ye the Lord: praise and exalt Him above all forever").

[^235]: Eschatological troparia of Ode 8, meditating upon the unquenchable fire (*негасимый огнь*), the gnawing worm (*червь скрежещущій*), and the separation of souls at the dread tribunal (Mark 9:44, 48; Matt. 25:31–46).

[^236]: Triadikon of Ode 8: Classical Cappadocian patristic synthesis professing the unity of the divine essence (*единаго Бога по существу чту*) while distinguishing the three Hypostases (*три составы пою опредѣлителнѣ*) as one single Godhead in three Persons (*бж҃тво едино въ трїехъ лицѣхъ*).

[^237]: Ode 9 Heirmos (*Безсѣменна зачатія рожество несказанное* / *Bezsemyenna zachatiya rozhestvo neskazannoye*). Greek incipit: *Ἀσπόρου συλλήψεως ὁ τόκος ἄφραστος*. A classical Theotokion-heirmos celebrating the virginal conception and birth of Christ as the cosmic renewal of human nature (Luke 1:48).

[^238]: Troparia of Ode 9 invoking Moses trembling when he beheld the back parts of God on Mount Sinai (Exod. 33:20–23; Heb. 12:21) and Daniel filled with dread before the fiery tribunal of the Ancient of Days (Dan. 7:9–15, 28).

[^239]: Triadikon of Ode 9: An emphatic monarchical confession of the Holy Trinity characteristic of the Kyivan Triodion, invoking the Father as Sole Begetter of the Only-begotten Son, the Son as Radiance of the Light, and the Holy Spirit as proceeding solely from the One God (*единый единѣ единаго Бога ст҃ый Дш҃е*), excluding the Western *Filioque* interpolation.

[^240]: Sunday Exapostilaria of Meatfare: Two sequential hymns set to the classic Resurrectional automela: the first to *Соученикомъ* (*Τοῖς μαθηταῖς συνέλθωμεν* / "Let us assemble with the disciples"), and the second to *Жены услышите* (*Γυναῖκες ἀκουτίσθητε* / "Give ear, ye women"), focusing the mind upon the dread separation of sheep and goats at the Second Coming (Matt. 25:31–46; Mal. 3:2; 4:1).

[^241]: Tone 6 Idiomelon Sticheron at the Praises (*Помышляю день оный и часъ* / Greek: *Ἐννοῶ τὴν ἡμέραν ἐκείνην*), depicting the general resurrection where all arise in a single age (*возрастомъ единемъ*), reflecting ancient patristic eschatology concerning the spiritual perfection of the resurrected body (cf. St. Augustine, *De Civitate Dei* XXII.14; Eph. 4:13).

[^242]: Tone 6 Idiomelon Sticheron at the Praises (*О каковый часъ тогда и день страшный* / Greek: *Ὢ ποία ὥρα τότε*), drawing upon the apocalyptic imagery of Daniel 7:9–10 and Revelation 20:11–15, where the books of conscience are opened and all human thoughts and intents are laid bare before the heavenly hosts.

[^243]: Tone 8 Sticheron at the Praises (*Даніилъ Пророкъ, мужъ желаній бывъ* / Greek: *Δανιὴλ ὁ Προφήτης, ἀνὴρ ἐπιθυμιῶν γενόμενος*), linking physical fasting directly with active fraternal charity and mercy, strictly warning that bodily abstinence without the cessation of judging one's neighbor is futile and leads to condemnation (Dan. 7:10; 9:23; 10:11; Rom. 14:10).

[^244]: Tone 1 Sticheron at the Praises (*Предочистимъ себе братіе, Царицею добродѣтелій* / Greek: *Προκαθάρωμεν ἑαυτοὺς ἀδελφοί, τῇ βασιλίδι τῶν ἀρετῶν*), hailing Fasting as the "queen of virtues" and urging the faithful to enter upon the pre-Lenten discipline with joy in expectation of the Paschal Resurrection.

[^245]: Typikon rubric regulating the concurrence of the Feast of the Meeting of the Lord (Hypapante / *Стрѣтеніе*, Feb. 2) with Meatfare or Cheesefare Sunday. The chronological note *якоже бысть лѣтъ, ҂а҃* ("as occurred in year 1000" / A.D. 1640) directly situates this edition within the pontificate of Metropolitan Petro Mohyla.

[^246]: Homiletic rubric prescribing patristic readings at the monastic table (Trapeza) from the festal collection, specifically the *Sermon of Palladius the Monk* (*Слово Палладія Монаха*, incipit: *Инѣ исповѣждься душе* / "Now make thy confession, O soul"), an ancient ascetical discourse on repentance and the Last Judgment.

[^247]: Vespers of Meatfare Sunday: The appointed Tone 8 Idiomelon of the Day (*Ласкосердствовавше, первую подяхомъ наготу* / Greek: *Τρυφῆς μετασχόντες*), contrasting the belly-indulgence that led to the expulsion of Adam from Paradise with the ascetical fasting that prepares the Christian soul to partake of the Paschal Lamb on the night of the Holy Resurrection (Gen. 3:6–7; Heb. 13:9).

[^248]: The rubric heading *Пѡлка Сѵрнагѡ Тѵпїка* (The Order/Chapters of the Cheesefare Typikon) introduces the liturgical regulations for Cheesefare Week (White Week / Butter Week), during which meat is prohibited but dairy products, eggs, and cheese are permitted as a step-down discipline prior to the Great Fast.

[^249]: Comprehensive Kyivan Typikon rubrics detailing the liturgical order when the Feast of the Meeting of the Lord (*Стрѣтеніе*, Feb. 2) or its Afterfeast falls on the weekdays of Cheesefare Week, governing the distribution of the Triodion, Menaion, and Festal hymnody across Small Vespers, Great Vespers, Compline, Matins, and the Divine Liturgy.

[^250]: Typikon rubrics regulating the celebration of the First and Second Finding of the Precious Head of Saint John the Baptist (*Обрѣтеніе честныя Главы Іоанна Предтечи*, Feb. 24) when it coincides with Monday, Tuesday, or Thursday of Cheesefare Week, detailing the hymns of the Octoechos, Triodion, and Menaion.

[^251]: Cross-reference in body rubric: `зри Листъ, к҃д` (see fol. 24), referencing folio 24 of the 1640 Kyiv Triodion (physical leaf p48), which sets forth the general Typikon statutes and fasting regulations for the Triodion period.

[^252]: Homiletic rubrics for Matins of Cheesefare Monday prescribing the reading of two ascetic works of Saint Basil the Great: *On Giving Heed to Thyself* (*In illud: Attende tibi ipsi*, PG 31:197–218; incipit: *Слова потребу даде намъ создавый насъ Богъ*) and *On the Renunciation of the World and on Spiritual Perfection* (*De renuntiatione saeculi*, PG 31:625–648; incipit: *Пріидѣте къ мнѣ вси труждающіися*).

[^253]: The hymnography of Cheesefare Monday at Matins comprises two Tri-odes: the first composed by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer (c. 816–886) in Tone 2, and the second composed by Saint Theodore the Studite (759–826) in Tone 1, greeting Cheesefare Week as the bright vestibule and spiritual springtime of the Holy Forty Days.
