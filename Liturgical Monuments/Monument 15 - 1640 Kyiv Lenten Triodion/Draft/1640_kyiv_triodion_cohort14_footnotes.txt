[^306]: Matins of Cheesefare Friday appoints the Sessional Hymn for the Martyrs (*Martyrion*) from the Octoechos in Tone 6 following Ode 6 of the Canon.

[^307]: The ascetical fast of the Holy Prophet Elijah on Mount Horeb (3 Kgs. 19:8 [LXX] / 1 Kings 19:8) and his fiery ascent (4 Kgs. 2:11 [LXX] / 2 Kings 2:11) are invoked throughout the Lenten Triodion as the supreme biblical paradigm of ascetic purification granting divine vision.

[^308]: Typological inversion of the Babylonian furnace (Dan. 3) and the parable of Dives and Lazarus (Luke 16:19–31): the fire of penitential tears willingly endured during the Fast consumes carnal passions and delivers the faithful from the unquenchable fire of Gehenna.

[^309]: The Heirmos of the Tri-ode for Cheesefare Friday (`По зако́нꙋ ѻ҆́тчꙋ`, Greek: `Νόμοις πατρῴοις`) was composed by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer (c. 816–886), one of the principal Byzantine melodists of the Triodion.

[^310]: Scriptural citation of Ephesians 2:14: `граде́жа средостѣ́нїе разорн́выи` ("having broken down the middle wall of partition between us"), uniting heaven and earth and inaugurating the spiritual peace of the Fast.

[^311]: Strict Cappadocian Trinitarian theology articulates the personal (hypostatic) incommunicability within the consubstantial Trinity: `ни Ѻ҆ц҃ꙋ̀ и҆зстꙋпи́вшꙋ въ Сн҃а, нижѐ Сн҃ꙋ премѣ́ншивсѧ въ и҆схожде́нїе` ("the Father not having passed over into the Son, nor the Son having changed into the procession [proper to the Holy Spirit]"), excluding both Sabellian modalism and subordinationism.

[^312]: The second Heirmos of Ode 9 (`Недоꙋмѣ́етъ всѧ́къ ꙗ҆зы́къ`, Greek: `Ἀπορεῖ πᾶσα γλῶσσα`), drawn from the Ninth Ode of the Theotokos Canon, acknowledging the inadequacy of all created human and angelic language to articulate the mystery of the Incarnation.

[^313]: Patristic typological parallelism: the wound in Christ's side opened by the soldier's spear upon the Cross (John 19:34) heals the primal defeat and mortality that issued from Adam's rib with the creation and transgression of Eve (Gen. 2:21–22, 3:6).

[^314]: On the aliturgical weekdays of Cheesefare Week (Wednesday and Friday), the Divine Liturgy is not celebrated; instead, the Lenten Hours (Third, Sixth, and Ninth) are chanted with the penitential prostrations and the Prayer of Saint Ephrem the Syrian, serving as a pedagogical bridge and vestibule into Great Lent.

[^315]: The Sixth Hour reading from Zechariah 8:7–17 prophesies the eschatological gathering and restoration of Jerusalem from the east and the west, demanding righteous judgment, truth, peace, and the forsaking of deceit in preparation for the spiritual fast.

[^316]: The Ninth Hour is joined immediately to the Sixth Hour and Typika on Cheesefare Friday, chanted with deep bows and the Prayer of Saint Ephrem the Syrian in accordance with the Studite-monastic rule codified in Metropolitan Petro Mohyla's 1640 edition.

[^317]: Internal cross-reference in body rubric: `зри ли́стъ, л҃. и҆ р҃д.` (see fol. 30 and 104), referencing folio 30 (physical leaf p44) and folio 104 (physical leaf p118) of the 1640 Kyiv Triodion, which govern the rubrics for combining the services of the Triodion with commemorations of Saints from the Menaion during Cheesefare Week.

[^318]: Internal cross-reference in body rubric: `Ра́звѣ Срѣ́тенїѧ прⷣз: и҆ прⷭ҇тн. и҆ли С҃: В҃: є҆́же є҆ хрⷱ҇а, зрⷱ҇, л҃. і҃.` (see fol. 30 and 10), referencing folio 30 (physical leaf p44) and folio 10 (physical leaf p24) of the 1640 Kyiv Triodion, regulating the feast of the Meeting of the Lord (*Hypapante*, February 2) or the temple patronal feast when coinciding with Cheesefare Friday.

[^319]: The full-page horizontal woodcut illustration heading Cheesefare Saturday in the 1640 Kyiv Triodion depicts the founders of monasticism: Saint Paul of Thebes, Saint Anthony the Great, Saint Agathon, and Saint Pachomius the Great, alongside the local Ukrainian-Rus' monastic patrons: Saint Nicetas of the Caves, Saint Anthony of the Caves, and Saint Theodosius of the Caves holding the architectural model of the Kyiv-Pechersk Dormition Lavra.

[^320]: The universal Commemoration of all the Holy Ascetic Monks and Nuns (*Synaxis of All Venerable Fathers in Asceticism*) on Cheesefare Saturday was instituted to inspire the faithful by displaying the multitude of saints who conquered passions through fasting before entering the arena of Great Lent.

[^321]: At Friday Vespers of Cheesefare Saturday, the first Kathisma of the Psalter ("Blessed is the man", Ps. 1–8) is appointed, marking the festal transition into the Saturday commemoration.

[^322]: Commemoration of the primordial geographical centers of early Christian anchoritic and coenobitic monasticism in the 4th century: Egypt, Libya, and the Upper Egyptian desert of the Thebaid.

[^323]: The theological phrase `Є҆́же по ѡ҆́бразꙋ, съблю́дше невре́димо... въ є҆́же по подо́бїю` (Greek: `Τὸ κατ' εἰκόνα τηρήσαντες ἀλώβητον... εἰς τὸ καθ' ὁμοίωσιν`) embodies the patristic doctrine of Genesis 1:26: man receives the divine image (*imago Dei*) by creation as an ontological endowment, but achieves the divine likeness (*similitudo Dei*) through the free ascetic struggle of virtue and deification (*theosis*).
