## Epigram upon the Ancient Crest of the Noble Lords Proskura-Sushchansky

*[Woodcut engraving of the ancestral coat of arms of the noble house of Proskura-Sushchansky, flanked by the armorial initials: (Ѳ Т / П / Σ / П / Z / K), signifying: Theodor Proskura Sushchansky, Land Scribe of Kyiv.[^4] The escutcheon, framed by Baroque foliate mantling and crested with a knight's helm, noble coronet, and three ostrich plumes, bears the ancestral charge of an upward-pointing arrow crossed with a transverse bar in the likeness of a cross, rooted in a split base.[^5]]*

Upon the Ancient Crest of Their Graces,
the Noble Lords Proskura-Sushchansky:

The ancient House of the Proskury-Sushchansky on both sides,
Ever glorious through the valiant, and renowned from of old,
Fittingly boasts of such an armorial jewel,
Which bears both the Arrow from the Earth and the Cross from Heaven.
For Valor and Humility have here built their nest,
That with them the Sushchanski might ever shield themselves.
They shield themselves, for Heaven itself acknowledged their virtue,
And with that same emblem endowed its own Archer.[^6]
O most fortunate House, by such a portent,
Even the Zodiac rejoices in the Sushchansky Jewel!

## Dedicatory Epistle and Theological Foreword of Metropolitan Petro Mohyla

PETRO MOHYLA,
By the Mercy of God, Archbishop,
METROPOLITAN OF KYIV,
Halych, and all Rus', Exarch
of the Holy Apostolic Throne of Constantinople,[^7]
Archimandrite of the Caves.
To the Noble and Pious, His Grace
PAN TEODOR PROSKURA-
SUSHCHANSKY, Land Scribe
of the Kyiv Voivodeship:[^8]
The grace of God, good health, prosperous success,
and the blessing of the Most Holy Metropolitan Throne of Kyiv
and of our humility from the Lord God, we cordially wish.

The people of Israel, following their leader Moses, while on pilgrimage from the Egyptian bondage unto the Promised Land, experienced the heavenly food of delicious manna with varied and marvelous flavors. We also experience this, gracious Sir, in the New Grace: for having been delivered from that ancient injurer of our souls, the devil, through the voluntary death undergone for us by Christ the Savior, and having departed from that Egypt onto the road that leads unto the Kingdom of Heaven, our promised land, we journey on pilgrimage, led invisibly by our Savior. Truly, as we journey toward the Jerusalem above, we experience in this manna varied flavors through diligent prayer *The Foreword.*

and through varied divine grace. For the Lord Himself recalled: *It is written, That man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word of God.*[^9] Who does not see clearly that our Teacher calls prayer food, since food serves for the preservation of our life? Who cannot easily consider that man, being composed of body and soul, requires one food for the body and another for the soul? There is therefore bread for the body, and there is for the soul, like heavenly manna, holy prayer; for by it the soul is ineffably nourished, which is truly called the voice of God. The Savior says: *Watch therefore, and pray always, that you may be accounted worthy to escape all these things that shall come to pass, and to stand before the Son of Man.*[^10] Thus He taught that man cannot lead his life in true holy faith, guide it spiritually, and enliven it with diverse divine gifts in any other way than through holy prayer. And just as through the manna, whichever food any of the people of Israel desired, he straightway perceived that flavor: so each of us, whenever he desires any grace of God, will perceive it within himself through prayer; for no one can be closer to receiving the grace of the Lord than he who is ever with God, who speaks with God, and God with Him. But to be with God and to speak with Him is accomplished by prayer, as St. Isidore *The Foreword.*

testifies, saying: *If anyone desires to be always with the Lord at every hour, let him pray and read: for when we pray, we speak with the Lord, and when we read, the Lord speaks with us.*[^11] Therefore the chosen vessel, Holy Paul, rightly urges us unto prayer, saying: *Pray without ceasing.*[^12] The Euripus straits bring wonder when the current changes course and flows backward at set hours; man marvels how such artful and precious ore-deposits can be formed in the bowels of the earth, or how the magnet draws iron unto itself; but even more must everyone marvel as soon as he begins to consider the powers and effects of holy prayer. That very holy teacher of the universal Church acknowledges and declares that the power of prayer surpasses all nature.[^13] What opened the Red Sea and parted it in two, that the people of Israel might pass through sound and without stumbling? Prayer! What transformed the furnace flame in Babylon into dew for the Three Youths? Prayer! What shut and opened heaven that it might sprinkle the earth? Prayer![^14] Indeed, whatever man shall ask, he will receive it all through holy prayer, according to those words of Christ: *Ask, and it shall be given you.* Therefore, as St. Augustine says: Holy prayer is the enlivenment of the soul, comfort to the good Angel, sorrow to the evil one, service unto the Lord,[^15] the support of all *The Foreword.*

virtues, a ladder to God, close to the Angels, the foundation of faith: with every God-inspired man prayer has its pious commendation and praise. Your Grace yourself, our gracious Sir, many a time out of your piety, while visiting the Caves of our Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra, have seen with your own eyes the incorrupt bodies of so many, indeed countless, holy ones of God;[^16] which they could obtain for themselves from the Lord God in no other way than from the holy Orthodox faith (which the Eastern Catholic Church, our holy Mother, teaches) through prayer. Together with it they endured monastic struggles and sleepless vigils, being zealous for the accomplishment of every spiritual virtue; for when anyone tastes fervent prayer, all pious virtues are straightway pleasing unto him. For these reasons the holy Church has instituted daily prayers according to the season and according to need: for she has prayers unto the Lord God, and she has prayers unto His saints, whom we beseech for their intercession before our Creator and Redeemer. And therefore, in this land of Rus', by the inspiration of the Holy Spirit in the heart of His Grace the King, WŁADYSŁAW THE FOURTH,[^17] happily reigning over us, and by the will of the entire Rus' nation, having received the Rus' hierarchic office with the blessing of the Most Holy Apostolic Throne of Constantinople, I am bound by my pastoral duty, while presiding over all who journey unto the Kingdom of Heaven, to ensure that through a lack *The Foreword.*

of copies of these prayers, the sheep entrusted unto me should not starve without spiritual pasture and without the spiritual nourishment of prayer. Looking diligently to this, as soon as I perceived that they began to complain of the scarcity of the Triodion or Trichristian Canon-Book, I commanded straightway, amid my great labors, that the previously printed Triodion from the local printing press of the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra should be renewed;[^18] which contains within itself prayers and canons moving unto great contrition, fitting for these days, that they who ought especially to meditate upon repentance during the Forty Days may have a contrite heart. For if the ancient Egyptians during their banquets and revels were accustomed to bring a human skull to the table and then hand it to each guest, that he might see what he shall become after death, so that he might be moved, renounce worldly pleasures, and while living in the world not neglect to serve God; and among others coffins were set out in the midst of the public square, that those passing by might remember death:[^19] how much more ought we, being Christians, ever to meditate at every hour upon death, for it will unfailingly wipe us from this world! And with a more fervent and diligent heart, we ought to take before us during the days of the Holy Great Fast these Canons, which serve for the remembrance of our mortality: therein each will see a human skull, will perceive the coffins set out and appointed for man, *The Foreword.*

and will easily understand that man is earth, and unto earth he shall return again. As the Psalmist says: In these days the voice of John the Baptist is renewed: *Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.*[^20] And whoever desires to obtain weapons for his rescue to overcome the enemy of his soul will obtain them from these Lenten prayers: as David with three stones from his sling conquered Goliath, so everyone who prays in truth will cast down our enemy under his feet with the three stones of contrition, confession of sins, and satisfaction for them.[^21] Whoever desires to eradicate his sins will destroy them through these Lenten prayers; for holy prayer, together with this Fast of the Forty Days, will accomplish this, so that even as the rod of Moses destroyed the serpents of Egypt, so it will root out and destroy our inner serpents, our infectious sins. Having therefore reviewed this most necessary and exceedingly profitable Book following its edition by my predecessor who fell asleep in God, Kyr Zacharias Kopystensky, Archimandrite of the Caves,[^22] and having made additions in several places for spiritual profit, I resolved to embellish it with the coat of arms of the ancient House of Your Grace, our gracious Sir, and to offer it unto the Orthodox sons for the performance of pious prayers in devout days; for it is fitting to dedicate it unto no one else than unto Your Grace, my gracious Sir, who from your ancestors of many ages *The Foreword.*

renowned, walking in their footsteps both in piety toward the Holy Church and toward our beloved Fatherland, strive that the fame of the most excellent House of Your Grace, our gracious Sir, should not be covered with dust, but should flourish ever more brightly. The hope which the citizens of this Voivodeship placed in the youthful years of Your Grace, our gracious Sir, has not proved false, when you trained yourself in nothing other than noble virtues: when, together with other champions, courageously standing up for our beloved Fatherland against the enemies of the Fatherland in national expeditions, you entrusted your life unto doubtful Mars;[^23] whereby you renewed the glory of your house, which for its valor received distinguished praise from the Princes of Rus' and the Kings of Poland. You have shown yourself to be a true Sushchansky Proskura, for in this manner your ancestors ever trained their youth. Nor do you cease now, when, holding yourself in reward for your valor the office of Land Scribe of Kyiv and attending unto it, you do not neglect to guide your beloved offspring in his youth in those same footsteps in which the ancestors of the most excellent House of Your Grace, our gracious Sir, were accustomed to walk. For while attending unto these duties, you do not abandon piety, nor do you quench the zeal in your heart toward the holy Eastern Church. Therefore the holy Church of Rus', being grateful, never ceases to proclaim from her lips the services rendered by the diligent care of Your Grace, our gracious Sir, *The Foreword.*

and considers more than once how she might show her gratitude and reward you. Yet she knows that this cannot fittingly be rewarded by us, but only by the Lord God Himself with the eternal Kingdom of Heaven, for there all zealous sons of the Holy Church receive their perfect reward; nevertheless, so far as she can testify her love in honor, she testifies it. Therefore, in ministering unto the holy Church of Rus' and preparing books for the celebration of divine worship, I dedicate this Book, the Triodion, that is named the Trichristian Canon-Book, unto Your Grace, our gracious Sir: knowing well that you greatly delight in the Forty Days, ever remembering the words of the Lord: *The kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and the violent take it by force.*[^24] For it was not necessary for our Savior to bear such great labors for Himself, but He bore them for us, that we, believing in Him, might know how to conduct ourselves in similar labors, for the attainment of that Heavenly Paradise; which He, desiring in His mercy to reopen—having been shut through the sin of our Forefather—and to prepare the way for all, Himself after other labors, as man, fasted forty days, prayed in the garden even unto bloody sweat, suffered diverse intolerable torments, and at last was crucified upon the Cross. All this it behooves every Christian, a son of the holy Eastern Church, to fulfill according to his strength: as she has *Foreword.*

...not a few of her sons, over whom the Holy Church rejoices when keeping their memory, counting so great a multitude of Fasters, Hermits, and Martyrs, who poured out their blood for the true faith, praying willingly unto the Lord: I do not mention in detail those holy Fathers who, drawing themselves unto labors, employed and applied diverse methods; there are even now those so devout that they bind their bodies with iron,[^25] so that they may not fall asleep during prayer, but may continually be found in nocturnal vigilance. But for the people of secular station, these Forty Days of prayers have been instituted, so that at least in these days they may be engaged in spiritual labors and be constant in prayer. Therefore, delivering this most necessary Book unto Your Grace, our gracious Lord, I pray that it may be favorably received, promising on our part that in further occasions that present themselves we will not neglect to testify our love toward Your Grace, our gracious Lord: striving together that choice proofs of our goodwill and devotion in the most noble house of Your Grace, our gracious Lord, may remain for future times. And offering this Book, I commend myself, with my archiepiscopal prayers and devotion, diligently unto the favor of His Grace, my gracious Lord.

In the Holy, Great, Orthodox, and Wonderworking Caves Lavra of Kyiv. In the year from the creation of the world 7148, and from the Nativity of Christ 1640, on the 25th day of May.[^26]

*[Woodcut Headpiece: The New Testament Holy Trinity—the Lord Pantokrator, God the Father, and the Holy Spirit in the form of a Dove, surrounded by Seraphim, Cherubim, and foliate ornament.]*

*FOREWORD TO THE BENEVOLENT READER.*

If each of us, remembering so great a divine benevolence, would always reflect that God created man above other creatures, having imprinted in him His own Image, an inestimable jewel, as is made known from these words: *Let Us make man in Our image and likeness;*[^27] and having created him for greater dignity, He brought him into a prepared house of delight, into that joyous Paradise, and gave him dominion;[^28] without doubt, he would immediately and ineffably be stirred up to render praise unto the Lord, recognizing that he is all the more obligated the more noble the Image of the Lord is that he possesses within himself. Thus once, having understood this through reflection, the Psalmist-King David confessed, declaring man to be but little lower than the Angels, saying: *Thou hast made him a little lower than the Angels.*[^29] But through our sluggishness and the vain distractions of this world, it does not often occur to us to consider this; and thereby our enemy, who walks about like a roaring lion seeking whom he may devour, takes advantage. Warning of this, holy Peter says: *Be sober, be watchful; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walks about, seeking whom he may devour.*[^30] Therefore our Mother the Church, sorrowing that her sons fall into forgetfulness of the beneficence of God, and thereby fall through grievous sins into the devil's snares, by the inspiration of the Most Holy Spirit has established that during this Holy Forty Days these Canons should be celebrated, which are composed for standing before the Lord God and for stirring up man to reflect upon the grace of God: for the holy Great Fast shows us Christ the Savior Himself, Who, having become man, fasted forty days for us,[^31] desiring to teach how we must conquer ourselves and accustom ourselves unto spiritual labors...

*Foreword.*

...unto spiritual labors, and reconcile offended God. Whoever wishes to celebrate these Lenten Canons with spiritual profit (as is incumbent upon everyone), ought beforehand, when approaching this, to set before his mental eyes his own conscience, and look closely into it, considering well in what things he is guilty before the Lord: knowing that some sins are venial and others are mortal,[^32] and on account of mortal sins it is impossible to have access unto the Kingdom of Heaven, but rather one must be where there is weeping, wailing, and gnashing of teeth forever.[^33] And considering oneself truly guilty before the Lord, whenever one comes to remember it, one is bound from the depths of the heart to sigh with sorrow for having offended the Lord; and proceeding thus, it is fitting for such a one to prepare for holy confession with all diligence. To keep the Fast also, not only by abstaining from improper food forbidden by the holy Church, but also mentally, and not only bodily, we are bound to fast, guarding ourselves so that we neither think anything that would be an offense unto God, nor speak anything resembling it, putting away from ourselves all passions, abandoning all occasions leading to our temporal pleasure: but rather we ought to fix this in our mind, that the Lord Jesus Christ, the Son of the eternal God, being with the Father and the Holy Spirit our Creator, willed not the death of the sinner, but came Himself out of His love to save him: *For God so loved the world that He gave His only-begotten Son.*[^34] When thou, benevolent reader, shalt celebrate these offices, observing all spiritual virtues, thou wilt easily be led unto the contemplation of God's benefits, wilt easily come unto repentance, and wilt easily be spiritually strengthened, so that others also will more zealously care for holy penance and for watchfulness over thy conscience, keeping all in mind that this holy Great Fast shows us.

And as to the order in which these holy prayers are to be celebrated in this Fast, thou hast them orderly arranged, so that it is shown for each day which are to be read; and when thou fulfillest this, without doubt thou shalt obtain spiritual profit, none other than the grace of God; and thereafter, when thou, O reader, dost not lose it through sin, thou shalt receive the Kingdom of Heaven. Wishing this faithfully unto thy love, we send forth the blessing of the most holy Throne of the Metropolia of Kyiv, and of our humility.

From the Holy, Great, and Wonderworking Caves Lavra of Kyiv, in the year 1640, in the month of May, on the 25th day, Indiction 8.[^35]

### Notes

[^1]: Collated from the Greek (*Ѡ Еллинскагѡ изслѣдованъ*): Demonstrates the systematic textual collation undertaken by the scholarly circle of Metropolitan Petro Mohyla and the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra brotherhood directly against Byzantine Greek Triodia (Venice printed editions and Byzantine manuscript witnesses). This collation was intended to rectify scribal errors, corruptions, and divergent redactions that had accumulated in East Slavic liturgical manuscripts, establishing a philologically robust pre-Nikonian Kyivan recension.
[^2]: Now printed the second time (*Второе Тѵпомъ издано*): The first Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra folio edition of the Lenten Triodion was published in 1627 under Archimandrite Zacharias Kopystensky and proto-printer Pamvo Berynda. This 1640 imprint represents the revised second Lavra edition produced under the direct archpastoral supervision of Metropolitan Petro Mohyla.
[^3]: Dating formula: The imprint records the date according to both the Byzantine Creation Era (*Anno Mundi* 7148) and the Christian Era of the Incarnation (*Anno Domini* 1640), on March 23, in the 8th Indiction (March 23, 1640 O.S.).
[^4]: Armorial initials (*Ѳ Т / П / Σ / П / Z / K*): The Greek and Cyrillic initials flanking the heraldic crest stand for: *Ѳеодор Проскура Сущанский, Писарь Земский Киевский* (Theodor Proskura-Sushchansky, Land Scribe [Pisarz Ziemski] of the Kyiv Voivodeship), a prominent Orthodox nobleman and magistrate of the Commonwealth, and major patron of the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra.
[^5]: The Sushchansky-Proskura coat of arms (*Клейнотъ*): An armorial variation belonging to the Polish-Ruthenian heraldic tradition (associated with the *Kozieł* / *Proskura* arms). The escutcheon bears an upward-pointing arrow crossed by a transverse bar resembling the Christian Cross, issuing from a bifurcated base; it is ensigned with a nobleman's crowned helmet and three ostrich feathers.
[^6]: Endowed its own Archer (*своего Стрѣлца даровало*): A learned Baroque conceit (*conceptus*) linking the family's martial symbol of the arrow with the celestial constellation Sagittarius (the Archer of the Zodiac), asserting that the heavens themselves reflect and honor the virtues of the House of Sushchansky.
[^7]: Exarch of the Holy Apostolic Throne of Constantinople: A canonical title borne by the Metropolitans of Kyiv, reflecting their direct canonical subordination and jurisdictional tie to the Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople, as well as the stauropegial dignity enjoyed by the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra.
[^8]: Land Scribe of the Kyiv Voivodeship (*Писарь Земский Киевский* / *Pisarz Ziemski Kijowski*): A high-ranking judicial and administrative magistrate elected by the nobility of the Kyiv Voivodeship to serve in the land court (*sąd ziemski*) and provincial assembly (*sejmik*), responsible for maintaining the official territorial registers and acts.
[^9]: Luke 4:4 (citing Deuteronomy 8:3): "And Jesus answered him, saying, It is written, That man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word of God."
[^10]: Luke 21:36: "Watch therefore, and pray always, that you may be accounted worthy to escape all these things that shall come to pass, and to stand before the Son of Man."
[^11]: St. Isidore of Seville (*Sententiarum libri tres*, Book III, chapter 50: *De oratione*): "Prayer and reading are the two ways by which we converse with God. If anyone wishes to remain always with God, let him pray and read without ceasing; for when we pray, we speak with God, and when we read, God speaks with us."
[^12]: 1 Thessalonians 5:17: "Pray without ceasing."
[^13]: St. John Chrysostom, *Homilies on the Epistle to the Ephesians*, Homily 13: St. John extols the omnipotent efficacy of pure prayer, asserting that its spiritual force transcends all physical laws and the powers of created nature.
[^14]: Scriptural paradigms of the power of prayer: Exodus 14 (the miraculous division of the Red Sea by Moses); Daniel 3 (the preservation of the Three Holy Children Ananias, Azarias, and Misael in the fiery furnace of Babylon); 3 Kings 18 [1 Kings 18 MT] (the Prophet Elijah shutting heaven and praying for rain to moisten the earth); and Matthew 7:7 ("Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and you shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you").
[^15]: St. Augustine on prayer: The holy Father defines prayer as the spiritual breath and enlivenment of the soul, a joy to the holy Angels, a scourge to demons, a sacred offering unto God, and the indispensable foundation of every virtue.
[^16]: Incorrupt bodies of the Pechersk saints: Metropolitan Petro Mohyla directs the reader's attention to the tangible, empirical reality of the hundreds of incorrupt relics of the monastic saints resting in the Near (*St. Anthony's*) and Far (*St. Theodosius's*) Caves of the Kyiv Lavra as living proof of divine grace and the truth of the Orthodox faith.
[^17]: King Władysław IV Vasa (*Władysław IV Waza*, reigned 1632–1648): King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania, who in the "Articles of Pacification" (1632) and royal diplomas formally legalized and re-established the Orthodox hierarchy of Kyiv, recognizing Petro Mohyla as legitimate Metropolitan of Kyiv, Halych, and all Rus'.
[^18]: The 1627 Kyiv Triodion edition: Archimandrite Zacharias Kopystensky produced the editio princeps of the Pechersk Lenten Triodion in 1627. Due to the high demand across Ruthenian parishes and monasteries, copies quickly became scarce (*na skupost'*), necessitating this comprehensive 1640 edition.
[^19]: The Memento Mori tradition: Refers to the well-documented classical and early Christian practice—cited by Herodotus (*Histories* II.78) and Christian moralists—of displaying skulls or coffins at banquets to admonish participants of the vanity of earthly delights and the certainty of divine judgment.
[^20]: Matthew 3:2: "Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand."
[^21]: The three stones of repentance: Petro Mohyla allegorizes the three stones from David's sling (1 Kings 17:40 [1 Sam 17:40 MT]) into the three traditional sacramental components of genuine repentance: contrition of the heart (*skrusheniye* / *contritio cordis*), oral confession (*spovidaniye hrikhov* / *confessio oris*), and satisfaction/acts of penance (*dosyt' chynennya* / *satisfactio operis*).
[^22]: Archimandrite Zacharias Kopystensky (d. 1627): Renowned Kyivan churchman, polemicist (author of the *Palinodiya*), and Archimandrite of the Kyiv-Pechersk Caves Lavra from 1624 until his repose in 1627; spiritual predecessor (*antecessor*) of Petro Mohyla.
[^23]: Doubtful Mars (*vontplyvomu Marsowi*): A typical Baroque humanist mythological allusion to Mars (the god of war), evoking Teodor Proskura's youthful military expeditions defending the southeastern borders of the Commonwealth against Tatar and Ottoman incursions.
[^24]: Matthew 11:12: "And from the days of John the Baptist until now the kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and the violent take it by force."
[^25]: Physical asceticism with iron chains (*желѣзом тѣла ѡковываютъ*): An allusion to the traditional East Slavic and Athonite ascetical practice of wearing heavy iron chains or penitential crosses (*verigi* / *żelaza*), exemplified among the monastic fathers of the Kyiv-Pechersk Caves (such as St. Theodosius of the Caves and subsequent cave-dwelling recluses) to mortify bodily passions and prevent physical sleep during nocturnal vigilance.
[^26]: Dating formula of the Mohyla Foreword: The dedicatory preface concludes on May 25, 7148 AM (1640 AD), precisely two months after the imprint date recorded on the architectural master title page (March 23, 1640 O.S.), demonstrating the chronological window between typesetting and final archpastoral imprimatur.
[^27]: Genesis 1:26: "And God said, Let Us make man in Our image, after Our likeness."
[^28]: Genesis 2:8, 15: God placing man in the Garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it.
[^29]: Psalm 8:5–6 (LXX Ps 8:6): "Thou hast made him a little lower than the angels, and hast crowned him with glory and honor."
[^30]: 1 Peter 5:8: "Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walks about, seeking whom he may devour."
[^31]: Matthew 4:2; Luke 4:1–2: The forty-day fast of Christ our Savior in the wilderness, establishing the archetype and spiritual pattern for the Holy Great Forty Days.
[^32]: Venial and mortal sins (*повше́хныи* vs. *смерте́лныи* / *peccata venialia* et *mortalia*): Metropolitan Petro Mohyla employs the precise moral-theological distinction between venial and mortal sins formulated in the Latin and Eastern theological syntheses of the 17th-century Mohylan circle in Kyiv.
[^33]: Matthew 22:13; Matthew 25:30: The cast outer darkness where there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth.
[^34]: John 3:16: "For God so loved the world, that He gave His only begotten Son, that whosoever believs in Him should not perish, but have everlasting life."
[^35]: The 8th Indiction of 1640: The Byzantine 15-year indiction cycle corresponds exactly: 7148 AM divided by 15 leaves a remainder of 8, confirming the technical accuracy of the Pechersk printing house chronogram.
[^36]: Full-page woodcut engraving of the Crucifixion: Produced by the printshop of the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra, this plate synthesizes Byzantine iconographic conventions (the solemn, standing postures of the Theotokos and St. John, the Golgotha cavern containing Adam's skull) with Western Baroque naturalism in the anatomical rendering and the urban panorama of Jerusalem.
[^37]: 1 Corinthians 2:2 (Slavonic Pericope 129): "For I determined not to know anything among you, save Jesus Christ, and Him crucified."
