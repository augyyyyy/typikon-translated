# PART IV: THE LIFE OF OUR VENERABLE MOTHER MARY OF EGYPT BY ST. SOPHRONIUS OF JERUSALEM AND CONCLUDING COLOPHON

## On Thursday of the Great Canon

*The Life and Conduct of Our Venerable Mother Mary of Egypt, Written by the Most Holy Sophronius, Patriarch of Jerusalem.*[^3253]

It is good to keep close the secret of a king, but it is honorable to reveal the works of the Lord openly. For so said the Angel unto Tobit, after the marvelous restoration of the sight of his blinded eyes, and after those tribulations through which he passed, and from which he was delivered by grace. For not to keep the secret of a king is perilous and ruinous; but to keep silence concerning the marvelous works of God brings peril unto the soul. Therefore, seized with fear of keeping silence concerning His divine proclamations, and fearing the threatened peril of the servant who received a talent from the Master and buried it in the earth, hiding without profit that which was given him for trading, I shall in no wise keep silence concerning the sacred narrative that has reached me.[^3254]

Therefore let no one disbelieve me, for I write that which I have heard; nor let any marvel at me, as though I were contriving something beyond all others. Far be it from me to tell falsehoods concerning divine things, or to make merchandise of the word wherein God is commemorated! Rather than meditating upon lesser things that are unworthy of the majesty of God the Word Incarnate, or disbelieving them that speak thus, it does not seem unto me to be praiseworthy.[^3255]

If any, receiving such writings and marveling at the sublime wonder of the account, should be reluctant to believe, may the Lord be merciful also unto them; for they, considering the infirmity of human nature, deem it impossible for marvelous things to be spoken concerning men.[^3256]

I shall proceed therefore to the very subject of the narrative, that is to say, to that which took place in our generation, which a sacred man, accustomed from infancy to speak and do divine things, has related. But let not even this draw unto disbelief those who find such a wonder in our generation...[^3257]

...nor let them deem this to be impossible; for the grace of the Spirit entering from generation to generation into holy souls makes them friends of God and Prophets, as the divine Solomon taught. But it is time to begin that sacred narrative.[^3258]

There was a certain man in the monasteries of Palestine, adorned in life and word, who from his very swaddling clothes had remained in monastic customs and practices. Zosimas was the name of this elder; and let no one, because of the common name of Zosimas, suppose me to speak of that Zosimas who was once cast out of the dogmas as heretical. For the one is different from the other, and there is a great distinction between them both, even though both have the same name. For that one was Zosimas the heretic; but this was Zosimas the Orthodox, who lived as a monk from the beginning in one of the sacred monasteries in Palestine, having traversed every form of ascetical struggle, and having become zealous for every word of self-restraint. For every rule handed down in the arena wherein he walked he preserved intact. He also devised many things of his own, in order to subdue the flesh unto the spirit. Therefore he did not fail of this purpose; for the elder was so eminent in practical virtue that many from the neighboring monasteries (and sometimes even from afar) used to come unto him, to be trained and guided toward self-restraint by his teaching.[^3259]

And so, having such vision in active deed, the elder never neglected the study of the divine words; but both lying down and rising up, he held handiwork in his hands, and food (if indeed it is meet to call it food) he tasted, partaking of it according to custom, having one unceasing and never-ending work: to chant continually and ever to meditate upon the sacred words. Many times, indeed, it was said that the elder was accounted worthy of divine vision, illumination having come unto him from God; and this is neither marvelous nor incredible. For if, as the Lord said, *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*, they that manifestly purify their flesh and are ever vigilant in sobriety behold with the eye of their soul the vision of divine illumination, receiving from thence the pledge of those abiding good things.[^3260]

The blessed Zosimas himself used to relate that from his very swaddling clothes, so to speak, he had been given to the monastery, and had traversed the course of ascetical struggle therein until his fifty-third year. But after this he was troubled (as he said) by certain thoughts, as though he were perfected in all things and in no wise needed the instruction of others. For he thought within himself, saying...[^3261]

"Can there be any monk capable of delivering unto me something strange, or able to profit me, or to show me a form of asceticism which I know not and have not accomplished? Can there be found among the philosophers who dwell in the wilderness one who is superior to me in action or contemplation?" While the elder was meditating in this manner, someone stood before him and said: "O Zosimas, well indeed hast thou contended, as far as it is possible for a man; well hast thou run the course of asceticism. Yet there is no one among men who possesses perfection, but the contest that lies ahead is greater than that which is already past, of which thou knowest not. That thou mayest know also thyself how many other ways there are unto salvation: go forth out of thy land, and from thy kindred, and from thy father's house, even as that renowned Abraham among the Patriarchs, and come unto one of the monasteries situated near the river Jordan."[^3262]

Straightway the elder, following the one who commanded him, went forth from the monastery wherein he had lived as a monk from infancy; and having reached the Jordan (which is the holiest among rivers), he was guided by the one who called him unto that monastery wherein God had commanded him to be. Having knocked with his hand upon the gate of the courtyard, he met the monk who guarded the gate, and the latter announced him unto the Abbot, who received him. And the Abbot, beholding his reverent appearance and manner, and seeing that he made the customary monastic metania and received a blessing, questioned him in this manner:[^3263]

"Whence art thou, brother, and why hast thou come unto us humble elders?"[^3264]

Zosimas answered: "Whence I come it is not necessary to say, but for the sake of profit have I come, O father; for I have heard great and praiseworthy things concerning you, which are able to strengthen the soul unto union with Christ our God."[^3265]

The Abbot said unto him: "God alone, brother, is He Who heals human infirmity; may He teach both thee and us His divine will, and guide all to do what is profitable. For a man cannot profit a man unless each gives heed unto himself continually, and is sober in mind, doing what is necessary, having acquired God as a fellow-worker in his deeds. Yet since, as thou hast said, the love of Christ has moved thee to see us, humble elders, abide with us if for this cause thou hast come; and the Good Shepherd, Who gave His life as a ransom for us and calls His own sheep by name, will feed all of us with spiritual grace." When the Abbot had spoken these things unto Zosimas, the latter straightway made a metania, asked for a prayer, and having said "Amen," remained in that monastery.[^3266]

And seeing the elders shining in action and contemplation, burning in spirit, and serving the Lord—for their chanting was unceasing, and their standing was all-night; in their hands was constant handiwork, and the Psalms were in their mouths; no idle word had any dwelling among them, and there was no care for material things in their midst; uncounted years of revenues and worldly anxieties cursed by toil were not even known among them by name; but their single, first, and urgent care was that each of them should be dead in the body, as having once for all died and ceased to be unto the world and all things in the world. They had as inexhaustible food the divinely-inspired words; and they nourished their body only with what was strictly necessary, bread and water, according as each had the burning of the love of God. Seeing these things, Zosimas (as He said) was greatly edified, pressing forward unto what lay ahead, ever increasing his course, and He was found to be a good fellow-laborer with them that cultivate the divine Paradise.[^3267]

When sufficient days had passed, the time drew near when it is handed down for Christians to keep the holy Fast; for they purify themselves beforehand unto the veneration of the divine Passions and the Resurrection of the Lord. The gates of the monastery were never opened, but were always shut; and the monks performed their ascetical struggle without distraction; nor was it ever opened, unless somewhere one of them went forth for some urgent necessity. For the place was desolate, and to the neighboring dwellers and monks it was not only inaccessible, but even unknown. Now such a rule had been preserved in the monastery from the beginning, for the sake of which God, through a revelation, had brought Zosimas unto that monastery. And what that rule was and how it was kept, I shall relate here.[^3268]

Now on the Sunday which is customarily called the first of the fasting weeks, the Divine Liturgy was celebrated according to custom, and each became a partaker of the most pure and life-creating Mysteries, and tasted of a little food according to custom. Then all gathered into the church; and when fervent prayer had been made and sufficient knee-bowings, the elders kissed one another, and each, embracing the Abbot, made a metania, asking to receive his blessing and prayer, to have it as an aid and companion in the struggle that lay before him. When this was done, the monastery gates were opened; and chanting in unison: *The Lord is my light and my Savior, whom shall I fear? The Lord is the defender of my life, of whom shall I be afraid?* and the rest of the Psalm, all went forth, leaving one or two elders as guardians of the monastery—not to guard the property within, for there was nothing in their possession to be stolen by thieves, but that the church might not be without divine service.[^3269]

...might not be without divine service.

For each nourished himself as he was able and as he wished: one indeed carried bread measured according to bodily necessity; another carried twice-baked bread, another dried dates, another lentils soaked in water; and another carried nothing at all, save only his body and the rags wherewith he was clothed; and he nourished himself whenever nature constrained him upon the herbs growing in the wilderness.[^3270]

Now there was a rule for each of them, and an inviolable law among them, that none should see how another lived in self-restraint, or how he passed his time. For having straightway crossed the Jordan, they dispersed far apart from one another, and made the wilderness their city; nor did any come unto another. But if one should see from afar another walking toward him, he immediately turned aside to go in the opposite direction and departed unto another place. And each lived unto himself and unto God, chanting unceasingly and partaking of food at its proper time.[^3271]

In this manner, therefore, they passed all the days of the Fast, and returned unto the monastery on the Sunday that precedes the Life-creating Resurrection of the Savior, the Forefeast of which celebration the Church has received to celebrate with the Palm Branches.[^3272] Each returned having the cultivation of his own labor, his own conscience bearing witness how he had labored and what manner of seeds of toil he had sown. And none among them asked another how or in what manner he had traversed the contest set before him. Such indeed was the monastic rule, and thus well was it accomplished. For each of them, being in the wilderness for the sake of God the Author of the contest, contended for himself, in order not to seek to please men or to practice self-restraint for display before men, being completely free from this on every side. For deeds done for the sake of men, and wickedly performed to please men, it is not even meet to say that they bring no profit unto him that performs them, but rather they become the cause of much loss unto the doer.[^3273]

At that time, therefore, Zosimas also,[^3274] according to the customary rule of the monastery, crossed the Jordan, carrying a few small things for bodily necessity on the way, and the very rags wherewith he was clothed. And he fulfilled his rule walking through the wilderness, and at the time of food he satisfied natural necessity; and he tasted of a little sleep at night, reclining upon the earth wherever the evening hour overtook him. In the deep of the morning he would go forth again upon his journey, having ever the continuous strength of ascetical endeavor. And he came into the desire (as he said) to enter into the inner wilderness, hoping to find one of the fathers dwelling in the wilderness and fasting,[^3275] who might be able to help him unto what he desired; and so he courageously held fast unto his journey...

...holding fast unto his journey, pressing forward as though toward some bright and manifest dwelling place. Having traversed a journey of twenty days, when the time of the sixth hour arrived, he halted toward the East and performed his customary prayer. For he was accustomed, having interrupted his traveling course at the appointed hours of the day, to pray and rest a little from toil; and standing, he chanted, bowed down, and thus made his prayer.[^3276]

While he was standing, chanting, and gazing toward heaven with an unswerving eye, he beheld on his right hand (where he stood chanting the sixth hour) a shadow appearing like unto a human body. At first he was seized with fear, supposing that he beheld some demonic illusion, and he began to tremble. But having signed himself with the sign of the Cross and cast off fear (for his prayer had now come to an end), he turned his eyes and saw someone in truth walking toward the south. The figure seen was unclothed, the body black, as though darkened by the heat of the sun; and the hair was white as wool, and that also short, reaching no further than down to the neck.[^3277]

Zosimas, having beheld this and being filled as with sweetness with joy at so marvelous a sight, began to run toward that side whither the figure was hastening. For he rejoiced with unspeakable joy; for in all the distance of that region he had not seen a human form, nor any form of any living creature. And when he beheld the shadow, he sought to understand what and of what manner the sight might be, hoping that he would become an eyewitness of some great marvel.[^3278]

Now when that figure saw Zosimas approaching from afar, it began to run and flee toward the inner wilderness. But Zosimas, as though forgetting his old age and accounting the toil of the way as nothing, pressed onward to overtake the fleeing figure. Thus he pursued, and that figure fled. But the running of Zosimas was swifter, and after a little while he drew near unto the one who fled. When he was near enough for his voice to be heard, Zosimas began to cry aloud, and with tears he uttered such words as these:[^3279]

**Zosimas:** "Why dost thou flee from me, an aged and sinful man, O servant of the true God? Wait for me, whosoever thou art, for the sake of God for Whose sake thou dwellest in this wilderness! Wait for me, weak and unworthy, for the sake of the hope that thou hast of recompense for thy toils! Halt and grant a prayer unto an elder, for the sake of God Who disdains no one!"[^3280]

While Zosimas was speaking these words with tears, they came running unto a certain place that resembled a dry watercourse, where, as I suppose, a stream had once flowed. Now because of the nature of such a place, when the one of whom I have spoken reached the place, that figure ran down into it and ascended again unto the other side. But Zosimas was exhausted and unable to run any further...

...unable to run any further, he stood on the other side of the dry stream-bed. And he added tears unto tears and weeping unto weeping, so that the sound of his lamentation could be heard even further. Then that fleeing body uttered these words:[^3281]

**Mary:** "Father Zosimas, forgive me for the Lord's sake, for I cannot turn about and show myself unto thee face to face in this manner; for I am a woman, and unclothed as thou seest, having the shame of the flesh uncovered. But since thou hast desired to grant a single prayer unto a sinful woman, cast unto me the cloak wherewith thou art clothed, that therewith I may cover female weakness, and turning unto thee, receive thy prayers."[^3282]

Then terror and fear seized Zosimas (as he said, and trembling), hearing that she called him Zosimas by name. For being a man of swift understanding and wise in divine things, he perceived that she would never have called him by name, whom she had never seen and of whom she had never heard, had she not been manifestly illumined by the gift of clairvoyance. Therefore he made haste to do what was commanded; and stripping off the worn and torn garment that he wore, he cast it toward her, and turned his back and stood.[^3283]

And taking it, she girded herself as best she could, covering those parts of her body that it was most necessary to cover. Then turning unto Zosimas, she said unto him:[^3284]

**Mary:** "Why did it please thee, O father, to see a sinful woman? What seekest thou to see from me or to learn, that thou hast not been sluggish to undergo so great a labor?"

He, however, fell upon his knees upon the earth, asking to receive a blessing according to custom. She likewise, seeing this, made a prostration. And both lay upon the earth, each begging to be blessed by the other; and nothing could be heard from either of them, save only: "Bless!" After a long time had passed, the woman said unto Zosimas:[^3285]

**Mary:** "Father Zosimas, it is fitting for thee to bless and to pray; for thou art honored with the rank of the priesthood, and for many years hast stood before the holy Altar, and hast many times been a minister of the divine Mysteries."

These words cast Zosimas into greater fear and awe; and trembling, the elder was bathed in tears, groaning, and his voice failed him. Then with weary and breathless speech, terrified and pale of countenance, he said unto her:[^3286]

**Zosimas:** "O spiritual Mother, that thou hast drawn near unto God, having departed from the world, and hast for the most part died unto the world, the gift granted unto thee makes manifest, in that thou hast called me by name and named me a presbyter, whom thou hast never seen! But since grace is recognized not by dignities, but is accustomed to be shown forth by the disposition of the soul, do thou bless for the Lord's sake, and grant a prayer unto me who am lesser than thy perfection!"

Having received this resistance from the elder...

...having received this resistance from the elder, the woman said:

**Mary:** "Blessed is God, Who wills the salvation of human souls!"

And Zosimas having said: "Amen," both arose from kneeling. And the woman said unto the elder:[^3287]

**Mary:** "For what cause hast thou come unto me, a sinful woman, O man? Why hast thou come to see a woman stripped of every virtue? Yet the grace of the Holy Spirit has guided thee, that thou mightest perform some service for me in an appointed time. Tell me, how does the Christian realm fare today? How are the Emperors? How are the affairs of the Church governed?"[^3288]

**Zosimas:** "There is but one word for me, O Mother: Christ has granted firm peace unto all through your venerable prayers! But accept the supplication of an unworthy elder, and pray for the Lord's sake for the whole world and for me, a sinner, that my labor in this wilderness may not be fruitless for me!"[^3289]

She answered him:

**Mary:** "It is indeed fitting for thee, Abba Zosimas, who hast the rank of the priesthood (as I have said), to pray for me and for all, for unto this very thing hast thou been ordained. Nevertheless, since we are commanded to practice obedience, I shall zealously do that which is commanded by thee."[^3290]

And having said this, she turned toward the East; and lifting up her eyes unto the height and raising her hands, she began to pray in a whisper. Her voice was not heard at all, and Zosimas was able to discern nothing of her prayer. He stood (as He said) terrified, looking down upon the earth, and speaking nothing at all. He swore, calling upon God the Word as His witness, that He saw her during her prayer elevated about a cubit above the earth and standing in the air, praying.[^3291]

When he saw this, seized with still greater fear, he cast himself upon the earth, bathed in tears, and being in great awe, he dared not utter anything at all, save only that he repeated within himself for a long time: "Lord, have mercy!" As he lay upon the earth, the elder was troubled in his thoughts, lest she might be a phantom spirit pretending to pray.[^3292]

Turning about, the woman raised the elder, saying these words:

**Mary:** "Why do thy thoughts trouble thee, Abba, causing thee to stumble concerning me, as though I were a spirit and feigning prayer? Be it known unto thee, O man, that I am a sinful woman, yet I have received holy Baptism, and I am not a spirit, but earth, ash, and entirely flesh, having never meditated upon anything spiritual!"[^3293]

And immediately having said these things, she signed herself with the honorable Cross upon her forehead and eyes, her lips and her breast, saying thus: "May God deliver us, O Father Zosimas, from the evil one and from his snares, for great is his power against us!" Hearing and seeing these things, the elder fell down and prostrated himself at her feet, saying with tears:[^3294]

**Zosimas:** "I adjure thee by the Name of Christ our God, Who was born of the Holy Virgin...

...born of the Holy Virgin, for Whose sake Thou hast covered thy bareness with this raiment, and for Whose sake Thou hast thus subdued thy flesh: hide nothing from thy servant! Who art Thou, and whence, and when, and in what manner dost Thou dwell in this wilderness? Conceal not from me all that concerns thee, but tell all, that Thou mayest make known the marvelous works of God! For wisdom that is hid, and a treasure that is unseen, what profit is there in them both, as it is written?[^3295] Tell me all for the Lord's sake, that Thou mayest inform me, sinful and unworthy though I be! For I believe in God, for Whom Thou livest and in Whom Thou hast thy hope, that for this cause was I guided into this wilderness, that God might make manifest the things concerning thee. For it is not within our power to resist the judgments of God! For if it were not pleasing unto Christ our God that Thou shouldest be known, and how Thou hast struggled, He would not have permitted thee to be seen by anyone, nor would He have granted me to traverse so great a way, who never desired nor was able to go forth from my cell!"[^3296]

When Father Zosimas had spoken these and many other things, raising him up, the woman said unto him:[^3297]

**Mary:** "I am ashamed, my father—forgive me—to speak unto thee of the shame of my deeds! Yet since thou hast seen my body unclothed, I will uncover also my deeds, that thou mayest know with how much shame and dishonor my soul is filled. For it was not, as thou hast supposed, through any wicked vanity concerning myself that I was unwilling to confess. For wherein have I to boast, who became a chosen vessel of the devil? For I know that if I begin to tell the tale concerning myself, thou wilt flee from me even as one flees from a serpent, unable to endure hearing with thine ears the shameful things that I have done. Yet I shall speak, concealing nothing at all, having first adjured thee not to cease praying for me, that I may find mercy in the Day of Judgment."

While the elder was deeply moved and weeping without restraint, the woman began the narrative concerning herself, speaking thus:[^3298]

**Mary:** "My brother, I had Egypt as my fatherland. While my parents were yet living, when I had reached twelve years of age, I cast off their love and departed unto Alexandria.[^3299] And how I ruined the beginning of my maidenhood, and how I had an unrestrained and insatiable passion for wantonness, I am ashamed even to think upon! It is more necessary to speak of this now, yet I shall tell it in brief, that my passion and love of pleasure may be made known. For seventeen years and more—forgive me—I spent living openly for the gratification of wanton desires. Nor was it for the sake of any gifts, as truth is my witness! For I would not accept anything from those who offered it, having resolved upon this, that I might cause many to run eagerly unto me, that they might satisfy the desire that was in me...

...that they might satisfy the desire that was in me. Nor shouldest thou suppose that I was wealthy and for that reason did not take money; for I was begging and often remained hungry, but I had an insatiable flame and an unrestrained desire to wallow continually in the mire of wantonness. And this was my life and my thought: continually and unceasingly to commit dishonor of nature.[^3300]

While I was living in this manner, I beheld at a certain harvest time a great crowd of Libyan and Egyptian men running as though toward the sea. Then I asked a certain man who was near me: 'Whither are these men hastening, running thus?' He answered me: 'They are all going to Jerusalem for the Exaltation of the Precious Cross, which is accustomed to take place in a few days.'[^3301] I said unto him: 'Will they take me with them, if I should wish to follow them?' He answered me: 'If thou hast the passage-money and food, no one will hinder Thee.' I said unto him: 'In truth, brother, I have neither passage-money nor food; but I will go unhindered and board one of the ships that they have hired, and they will feed me even if they do not wish to, for I have a body, and they will take that in place of fare.' For this reason, my father, I wished to go—forgive me—that I might have many companions ready for my passion! I told Thee, Abba Zosimas, do not constrain me to tell my shame! For I tremble—God is my Lord—for I defile both Thee and the air with my words!"[^3302]

Zosimas, wetting the earth with his tears, answered her, saying: "Speak, for the Lord's sake, O my Mother, speak, and do not break off the discourse of so profitable a narrative!" She, beginning again, added unto her former words:[^3303]

**Mary:** "Now that youth, having heard the shamelessness of my foul words, departed laughing. But I, having cast away the spinning-wheel that I carried—for I happened to carry it at that time—ran toward the sea whither I had seen them running. And I saw certain young men standing by the sea, about ten in number or more, young in body and vigorous in movement, and they seemed unto me sufficient for what I sought. In truth, other fellow-voyagers were waiting, for some had already gone ahead unto the ship. Shamelessly, then, as was my custom, leaping into their midst, I said: 'Take me also whithersoever ye go, for ye will not find me unpleasing unto you!' Having spoken also other foul words, I stirred them all to laughter. And they, seeing my shameless disposition, took me and led me into the ship that they had ready; for those whom they were awaiting had by that time arrived, and thence we began to sail.[^3304]

And what happened thereafter, how shall I tell thee, O man? What tongue can utter, or what ear can receive, the things that took place in the ship and on the voyage, and how I, wretched one, compelled even those who were unwilling...

...compelled even those who were unwilling, wretched that I was! There is no form of impurity, spoken or unspoken, whereof I was not the wretched teacher! I in truth—be it known unto thee, O man—am struck with awe at how the sea bore my wantonness, and how the earth did not open its mouth and bring me down alive into hades, who had ensnared so many souls! But, as it seems unto me, God was seeking my repentance, not desiring the death of a sinner, but awaiting with longsuffering, hoping for conversion.[^3305]

In this manner, therefore, and with such speed did we go up unto Jerusalem. And as many days as I spent in the city, I did things similar unto the former, and even worse. For I was not content with those alone whom I had on the sea, who were defiled with me on the journey, but I also gathered many others, collecting both citizens and strangers for this purpose.[^3306]

When the Feast of the Holy Exaltation of the Precious Cross arrived, I was still going about as before, ensnaring the souls of others. Beholding early in the morning that all were running together unto the church, I also set out, running along with them that ran. So I arrived with them at the vestibule of the Temple. And when the hour of the holy Exaltation arrived, pressing and being pressed, I was crowded among the gates, striving to enter with the people. When I reached the gates through which one had to enter into the church itself wherein the Life-creating Tree was shown forth, I, wretched one, drew near with great toil and constraint.[^3307]

But when I stepped upon the threshold of the door, all the others entered unhindered, but a certain divine power hindered me, not suffering me to enter! Again I pressed forward and was thrust back, and found myself standing alone once more in the vestibule. Supposing that this had happened unto me because of womanly weakness, I mingled again with the others and strove, pushing my way forward, but I labored in vain. For again, as soon as my wretched foot stepped upon the threshold, the Temple received the others without hindering them in any way, but me alone, wretched one, it would not receive! But even as a host of soldiers arrayed for this purpose to hinder my entrance, so did a sudden power bar me, and again I stood in the vestibule.[^3308]

Having suffered this three and four times, and accomplished nothing, I became exhausted, and could no longer push forward or be pushed, and my body was exceedingly fatigued by the crowd. Filled with shame, I stepped aside and stood in a certain corner of the church court; and scarcely then did I come to a sense of the cause that hindered me from seeing the Life-creating Tree. For the word of salvation touched the eyes of my heart, showing me that the mire of my deeds was that which shut against me the divine entrance. Therefore I began to weep and lament, beating my breast and uttering groans from the midst of my heart.[^3309]

While I was weeping, I beheld, in the place where I stood, above me an Icon of the Holy Mother of God, and gazing steadfastly upon it, I said:[^3310]

**Mary:** "O Virgin Lady, who didst bear God the Word in the flesh, I know, yea, I know that it is neither fitting nor acceptable for me, so defiled and utterly wanton, to look upon thine Icon, the Icon of thee, the Ever-Virgin, who art pure, having body and soul chaste and unblemished! For it is right that I, a wanton, should be hated and loathed by thy purity. Yet since, as I have heard, for this cause God, Whom Thou didst bear, became man, that He might call sinners unto repentance: help me, who am alone and have no help from anyone! Command that the entrance into the church be granted unto me, and deprive me not of seeing the Tree whereon God, Whom Thou didst bear, was nailed in the flesh and gave His own Blood as a ransom for me! Command, O Lady, that the doors be opened unto me for the veneration of the Divine Cross, and grant me unto God, Who was born of thee, as a trustworthy surety that I will no longer defile this body with any shameful impurity! But when I behold the Tree of the Cross of Thy Son, I will immediately renounce the world and all things in the world, and straightway go forth whithersoever Thou thyself, as the surety of my salvation, shalt teach and guide me!"[^3311]

Having spoken these things, and having received as it were some pledge of unwavering faith, trusting in the loving-kindness of the Mother of God, I moved from that place where I had stood praying. And coming again and mingling with them that were entering, there was no longer anyone who pushed me or thrust me away, none who hindered me from being near the doors through which they entered into the church. Fear and awe seized me, and I was entirely trembling and shaking. Then reaching the doors which until then had been shut against me, the Divine Power which had previously hindered me now made my entrance easy. Thus I entered without toil, and thus I came within the holy places, and was accounted worthy to see the Life-creating Cross! And I beheld the Divine Mysteries, and how God is ready to receive them that repent.[^3312]

Casting myself upon the earth and having venerated that holy ground, I kissed the Precious Tree, and straightway ran forth, hastening to return unto my Surety. And when I was in that place where the pledge had been written with my Surety, kneeling before the Ever-Virgin and Mother of God, I uttered these words:[^3313]

**Mary:** "Thou indeed, O Lady, Lover of mankind, hast shown forth thy love of mankind toward me, and didst not disdain the prayer of an unworthy one! For I have seen the glory...

...the glory which in truth it was not worthy for me, a wanton, to behold! Glory to God, Who for thy sake receives the repentance of sinners! For what more have I, a sinner, to think or say? It is time, O Lady, to fulfill the remaining pledge of my covenant! Now guide me whither thou commandest; now be thou henceforth my teacher of salvation, guiding me by the hand along the way that leads unto repentance!"[^3314]

While I was saying these things, I heard someone crying from afar: 'If thou crossest the Jordan, thou shalt find good rest!' Having heard this voice, and believing that it had come for my sake, I cried out with tears: 'O Lady, Lady, do not abandon me!' Having thus cried aloud, I went forth from the church court and journeyed with haste. As I was going forth, someone who saw me gave me three coins, saying: 'Accept these, my mother.' And having received that which was given me, I bought three loaves of bread with them, and took these as a blessing for the journey.[^3315]

I asked the bread-seller: 'Whither and which way, O man, is the road that leads unto the Jordan?' Having learned the gate of the city leading out toward that side, I went forth running, and set out upon my journey weeping, adding inquiry unto inquiry; and thus I journeyed the whole day. For it was the third hour of the day when I beheld the Cross (as I think), and when the sun was now inclining toward the west, I reached the Church of St. John the Baptist, which is near the Jordan. Having first prayed in the church, I immediately went down unto the Jordan, and washed my face and hands in that holy water. Then I partook of the Most Pure and Life-creating Mysteries in the Church of the Forerunner, and ate half of one of the loaves of bread; and having drunk of the Jordan water, I lay down upon the earth.[^3316]

On the following morning, having found a small boat there, I crossed unto the other side. And again I prayed unto my Guide to direct me whithersoever it should be well-pleasing unto her. And so I came into this wilderness, from that time even unto this day; but I withdrew, fleeing far away, and took up my dwelling here, awaiting my God Who saves from faint-heartedness and tempest them that turn unto Him."[^3317]

Zosimas said unto her:

**Zosimas:** "How many years are there, O my lady, since thou didst settle in this wilderness?"

The woman answered:

**Mary:** "Forty-seven years, as I suppose, it is since I went forth from the holy city."

Zosimas said:

**Zosimas:** "And what hast thou had, or what findest thou for food, my lady?"

The woman said:

**Mary:** "Having crossed the Jordan, I brought two and a half loaves of bread, which gradually dried up and became like stones; and eating of them little by little over the years, I finished them."

Zosimas said:

**Zosimas:** "And how didst thou pass so great a span of time without distress, no contrary change troubling thee?"

The woman answered...

...the woman answered:

**Mary:** "Thou hast asked of me a matter, Father Zosimas, concerning which I tremble to speak! For if I bring to remembrance so many afflictions which I endured, and the cruel thoughts that troubled me, I fear lest I be seized by them again!"[^3318]

Zosimas said:

**Zosimas:** "Leave nothing untold, O my lady, of that which thou canst inform me; for once and for all have I asked thee concerning this, that thou mightest instruct me in every detail."

She said unto him:

**Mary:** "Believe me, Abba Zosimas: seventeen years did I spend in this wilderness contending with wild beasts, that is, with irrational desires! For whenever I began to partake of food, I longed for the meat and fish which Egypt had; I longed for wine, which was so desirable unto me, for I drank much wine while living in the world; but here, not having even water to taste, I was fiercely parched and suffered great distress. There came upon me also a demonic desire for wanton songs, fiercely troubling me continually and compelling me to sing the demonic songs which I had learned. But immediately weeping and beating my breast with my hand, I recalled the vows which I had promised when going forth into the wilderness. And in thought I stood before the Icon of the Most Pure Mother of God, my Surety, and wept before her, praying her to drive away the thoughts that so troubled my wretched soul. And when I had wept sufficiently and beaten my breast according to my strength, I saw a light that shone upon me from every side, and thenceforth a deep calm from the tempest came upon me.[^3319]

And how shall I tell thee, Abba, of the thoughts that urged me on unto wantonness? For a fire was kindled within my passionate heart, and burned me entirely on every side, urging me unto the desire of carnal union. But immediately, whenever such a thought came upon me, I cast myself upon the earth and bathed it with tears, imagining that my Surety herself stood before me, judging my transgression and threatening the torment of transgression. Nor did I arise from kneeling upon the earth—even if night and day should pass—until that sweet light had shone upon me and driven away the thoughts that troubled me. Therefore, having the eyes of my heart ever turned toward my Surety, I uttered an unceasing cry, asking help for one who was in peril in the desert deep; for in truth I had her as a helper and as a coworker in repentance. And having thus passed the span of seventeen years, I endured countless afflictions. From that time even unto this day, my Helper has been with me, guiding me in all things and in everything..."

...having consumed them (as I said) during seventeen years, I lived eating herbs and whatever else is found in this wilderness. As for the garments that I had when I crossed the Jordan, they gradually rotted and were torn away. Many afflictions indeed did I endure from the winter, and again from the heat of the harvest season: burned by the scorching heat, and frozen by the winter, and shivering, so that many times I fell to the ground breathless and lay altogether motionless, besieged by many and varied afflictions, temptations, and boundless thoughts. From that time forth even unto this day, the multiform power of God has preserved my sinful soul and humble body. For reflecting only upon what evils the Lord has delivered me from, I acquired as inexhaustible food the hope of my salvation. For I am nourished and clothed by the Word of God Which upholds all things: "Man shall not live by bread alone."[^3320] And: "They that have no covering embrace the rocks,"[^3321] inasmuch as I stripped off the garment of sin.

When Zosimas heard that she also cited words from the Scriptures—from Moses, the Prophets, and from the Books of the Psalms—he said unto her:[^3322]

**Zosimas:** "Hast thou studied the Psalms, O my lady, or other Books?"

She, hearing this, smiled and said unto him:[^3323]

**Mary:** "Believe me, O man: I have not seen another human being since I crossed the Jordan, save only thy form today; nor have I seen a beast or any other living creature since I saw this wilderness. I have never learned the Scriptures anywhere, nor have I heard anyone chanting or reading; but the Word of God is living and active,[^3324] and It teaches the understanding of man. Unto this point is the end of the narrative concerning me. And now I adjure thee by the Incarnation of God the Word to pray for me, a wanton woman, for the Lord's sake!"

When she had said these things and finished her discourse, the elder hastened to make a prostration again, and cried aloud with tears:[^3325]

**Zosimas:** "Blessed is God, Who does great and marvelous things, glorious and marvelous things without number![^3326] Blessed is God, Who has shown unto me what He grants unto them that fear Him! For in truth Thou hast not forsaken them that seek Thee, O Lord!"[^3327]

She, however, prevented the elder from completing a full prostration, and said unto him:[^3328]

**Mary:** "All these things that thou hast heard, O man, I adjure thee by Christ the Savior, our God, that thou tell no one until God takes me away from the earth! But now depart in peace with quietness; and in the coming year thou shalt see me again, and shalt appear unto me preserved by the grace of God. Yet do for the Lord's sake that which I now command thee: during the Holy Fast of the coming year, do not cross the Jordan as ye are accustomed to do in the monastery!"

Zosimas marveled, hearing that she also...

...she declared, and uttered nothing else save only: "Glory to Thee, O God, Who grantest great things unto them that love Thee!"

She said unto him:[^3329]

**Mary:** "Abide therefore in the monastery (as I have said), Abba Zosimas; for even if thou shouldest wish to go forth, it will not be possible for thee. But on the evening of the Mystical Supper, take the Life-creating Body and Blood of our Lord Jesus Christ and God in a holy vessel worthy of such Mysteries, and bring It and wait for me on the bank of the Jordan that borders upon the inhabited world, that having come, I may partake of the Life-creating Gifts. For since the time when I communed in the Church of the Forerunner before crossing the Jordan, I have never until now obtained this sanctification; and now I long for It with an uncontrollable desire. Therefore I beseech thee, do not reject my supplication, but by all means bring me these Life-creating and Divine Mysteries at that hour wherein the Lord made His Disciples partakers of the Divine Supper.[^3330] And unto Father John, the Abbot of the Monastery wherein thou dwellest, say these words: 'Take heed unto thyself, brother, and unto thy flock; for certain things are done there that need correction.'[^3331] Yet I do not wish thee to speak unto him now, but when the Lord shall command thee."

Having said these things, and having spoken unto the elder: "Pray for me," she departed again into the inner wilderness. But Zosimas, having bowed his knees, prostrated himself upon the earth where the soles of her feet had stood, giving glory to God; and he returned, rejoicing in soul and spirit, praising and glorifying Christ our God. Straightway having crossed that wilderness, he reached the monastery on the very day whereon those who dwelt therein were accustomed to return. And during that year he kept silence concerning all things, daring to tell no one anything of what he had seen. But within himself he prayed God to show him again that desired countenance; and he grieved and sorrowed as he contemplated the yearly cycle, wishing that year to be but a single day, if that were possible.[^3332]

When the Sunday that begins the Holy Fasts arrived,[^3333] straightway after the customary prayer all the others went forth chanting; but a feverish sickness seized him and constrained him to remain within. Then Zosimas recalled what the Venerable one had spoken, that even if he wished to go forth from the monastery, he would not be able to do so. A few days having passed, he arose from the sickness and remained in the monastery. When the monks returned once again, and the evening of the Mystical Supper drew near,[^3334] he performed that which had been commanded him: and having placed the Most Pure Body and Precious Blood of Christ our God into a small Chalice,[^3335] and having placed also into another vessel a few dried dates and figs, and a little lentils soaked in water,[^3336] he departed late in the evening; and he sat...

...upon the bank of the Jordan, awaiting the arrival of the Venerable one. When the holy woman delayed, Zosimas did not slumber; but he looked unswervingly into the wilderness, awaiting to see what he ever fervently desired to see. And the elder sat saying within himself:[^3337]

**Zosimas:** "Has my unworthiness somehow hindered her from coming? Or having come and not found me, has she straightway returned?"

And saying these things, he wept; and weeping, he sighed, and lifting up his eyes unto heaven, he prayed God, saying:[^3338]

**Zosimas:** "Deprive me not, O Master, of seeing again that which Thou didst grant me to see, lest I depart empty, bearing my sins for my condemnation!"

Having prayed these things with tears, he came into another thought, for he said within himself:

**Zosimas:** "And what shall happen if she comes? For there is no boat; how therefore will she cross the Jordan and come unto me who am unworthy? Alas for my unworthiness! Woe is me, who has caused me to be deprived of so great a good!"

While the elder was meditating upon these things, behold, the Venerable woman arrived, and stood on the other side of the river whence she had come. Zosimas arose rejoicing and making glad, glorifying God. But again the thought wrestled within him: how would she be able to cross the Jordan? Then he beheld her signing the Jordan with the Precious Cross—for the moon was shining brightly all that night (as he related)—and immediately with that sign she stepped upon the water, and walking upon the surface of the water, she came toward him.[^3339] When he wished to make a prostration, she forbade him while yet walking upon the waters, crying aloud:[^3340]

**Mary:** "What art thou doing, Abba, being a Priest and bearing the Divine Mysteries?"

He obeyed her words. Stepping off the water, she said unto the elder:[^3341]

**Mary:** "Bless, father, bless!"

He answered her trembling, for terror seized him at the most glorious vision:[^3342]

**Zosimas:** "Truly God is without deceit, Who promised that they who cleanse themselves as far as is possible shall become like unto God![^3343] Glory to Thee, Christ our God, Who hast shown unto me through this Thy servant how far I stand from the measure of perfection!"

And speaking these things together, the holy woman asked him to recite the Symbol of Faith [the Creed], and likewise to recite beforehand the "Our Father Who art in the Heavens."[^3344] When this had been done, and the prayer had reached its conclusion, according to custom she gave the elder the kiss of love upon the mouth [that is, the liturgical kiss of peace];[^3345] and having thus partaken of the Life-creating Mysteries, she lifted up her hands unto heaven, sighed, wept, and cried aloud in this manner:[^3346]

**Mary:** "Now lettest Thou Thy servant depart in peace, O Master, according to Thy word: for mine eyes have seen Thy salvation!"[^3347]

Then she said unto the elder:[^3348]

**Mary:** "Forgive me, father, and fulfill another desire of mine: go now unto thy monastery, preserved in the peace of God; but in the coming year come again unto that stream-bed where I first conversed with thee. Come..."

**Mary:** "...by all means for the Lord's sake, and thou shalt see me again as the Lord wills."

He answered her:[^3349]

**Zosimas:** "I would have wished (if it were possible) to follow thee from here, and ever to behold thy venerable face! But grant a single petition of an elder: partake of the little food that I have brought here!"

And straightway, having said this, he showed her what offering he had. But she, touching the lentils with the tips of her fingers, and taking three grains, brought them unto her mouth, saying:[^3350]

**Mary:** "Sufficient is the grace of the Holy Spirit to preserve the spiritual substance undefiled!"

And having spoken these things, she said again unto the elder:[^3351]

**Mary:** "Pray for the Lord's sake, my father, pray for me, and ever remember my wretchedness!"

He fell down at the feet of the Venerable one, and having asked her with tears to pray for the Church, and for the Sovereigns, and for himself, he let her depart, groaning and weeping; for he dared not detain any longer her who could not be detained. And she, having again signed the Jordan, stepped upon the water and crossed walking as before. But the elder returned held fast by great joy and fear, reproaching himself that he had taken no thought to learn the name of the Venerable one; yet he hoped to obtain this in the coming year.[^3352]

When the year had passed, having completed all things according to custom, he went forth again into the wilderness, hastening toward that most glorious vision. Having traversed the length of the wilderness, and arrived at certain landmarks that showed the finding of the place, he looked to the right and gazed with his eyes to the left, even as a most skillful hunter seeking if anywhere he might catch a most sweet prey. When he looked and saw that nothing moved anywhere, he began again to bathe himself in tears, and lifting up his eyes unto heaven, he prayed, saying:[^3353]

**Zosimas:** "Show me, O Master, Thy undefiled treasure which Thou hast hidden in this wilderness! Show me, I pray Thee, her who is an Angel in the flesh, of whom the world is not worthy!"[^3354]

And while he was thus praying, he reached the place which the dry stream-bed marked out. Standing upon its edge, he beheld on the side that looks toward the sunrise the Venerable one lying dead, having her hands crossed as was fitting, lying and facing toward the East. Running up, he bathed the feet of the Blessed one with tears, for he dared not touch any other part of her body. Having wept for a long time, and recited the Psalms fitting for the occasion, he performed the funeral prayer, and said within himself:[^3355]

**Zosimas:** "Shall I undertake to bury the body of the Venerable one? Might the Blessed one be displeased at this being done?"

And saying these words, he beheld at her head an inscription traced upon the earth, which contained in itself these words:[^3356]

...contained:

"Bury, Abba Zosimas, in this place the body of humble Mary; yield earth to earth, praying for me for the Lord's sake, who reposed in the month of Pharmouthi according to the Egyptian tongue, which is April according to the Greek, on the very night of the saving Passion, after partaking of the Divine Mystical Supper."[^3357]

When the elder had read this writing, he first meditated: who was he that wrote it? For she (as she had said) knew not how to write. But he rejoiced that he had learned the name of the Venerable one; and he understood that as soon as she had partaken of the Divine Mysteries at the Jordan, she was straightway at the place wherein she also reposed. And the journey which Zosimas had traversed laboring for twenty days, Mary crossed in a single hour, and straightway departed unto God.[^3358]

Glorifying God and bathing the body of the Venerable one with tears, he said:[^3359]

**Zosimas:** "It is time, humble Zosimas, to accomplish that which was commanded thee! But how wilt thou dig, wretched one, having nothing at all in thy hands?"

And having said this, he beheld at a small distance a small piece of wood cast down in the wilderness. Taking it up, he began straightway to dig; but the earth being dry, it did not obey the laboring elder in any way. He labored, bathed in sweat, but was able to accomplish nothing. Sighing deeply from the bottom of his spirit, he beheld a great lion standing beside the body of the Venerable one and licking the soles of her feet.[^3360] Seeing the beast, he trembled in his flesh; but recalling the words of the Blessed one, who said that she had never seen a beast, he signed himself with the sign of the Cross, believing that the power of her who lay there would preserve him unharmed. And the lion began to approach the elder, fawning upon him with its movements. Zosimas said unto the lion:[^3361]

**Zosimas:** "Since, O beast, the great one commanded me to bury her body, but I am old and cannot dig a grave, nor have I a tool fitting for the need, and the distance being so great, I cannot return to bring a suitable implement: do thou therefore perform the work with thy claws, that we two may commit the body of the Venerable one to the earth!"

Immediately at his word, the lion dug a pit with its forepaws, as much as was sufficient to cover that which was to be buried. Again washing the feet of the Venerable one with tears, and having prayed much, he covered the body with earth while the lion stood by—the body being naked as before, and having nothing else save only that cast-down cloak which Zosimas had thrown, wherewith Mary had formerly covered certain parts of her body. Then both departed: the lion indeed withdrew into the inner wilderness like a sheep, while Zosimas...

...returned, blessing and praising Christ our God. And having come again unto the cenobitic monastery, he related unto all the monks, concealing nothing of what he had heard and seen, recounting everything word for word from the beginning unto them, so that all who heard were amazed at the great works of God, and celebrated the memory of the Venerable one with fear and longing.[^3362]

And Abbot John found certain things in the monastery that required correction, so that not even this word of the Venerable one might be idle or unfulfilled.[^3363] And Zosimas also reposed in that monastery, being near a hundred years old, as they related. Now the monks continued, receiving the tale through oral transmission and teaching it without writing, offering the profitable narrative in common unto all who wished to hear. But I have heard of no one who had committed this narrative unto writing even unto this day; but I, having received it unwritten, have set it forth in a written narrative.[^3364] And if indeed others also have written the Life of the Venerable one, and that in a manner more majestic and elevated, yet no such work has come unto my knowledge; nevertheless I also have written according to my ability, preferring nothing before the true narrative.[^3365]

May God, Who performs great wonders and grants recompense with great gifts unto them that flee unto Him, grant reward unto them that derive profit from this narrative, and unto him who commanded that this present narrative be committed unto writing![^3366] And may He account us worthy of the portion, standing, and rank of this Blessed Mary concerning whom is this narrative, and of all who have pleased Him [from the ages,] by contemplation and ascetical action![^3367]

Let us also therefore give glory unto God, the King of all ages, that He may account us also worthy to obtain mercy in the fearful Day of Judgment: in Christ Jesus our Lord, unto Whom belongs all glory, honor, and worship, dominion, majesty, and power, with the Father and the Most Holy and Life-creating Spirit, always, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.[^3368]

AMEN.[^3369]
