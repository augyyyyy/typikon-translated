*Sunday of Meatfare.*

*Synaxarion (continued):*

On this day, we celebrate the commemoration of the Second and Impartial Coming of Christ, which the most divine Fathers appointed after those two Parables [of the Publican and the Prodigal Son]: to the end that no one, having learned from those parables of God's benevolence toward man, should begin to live carelessly, saying: "God is the Lover of mankind, and whenever we turn away from sin, He is ready and willing to advance us in His goodness." They ordered this dreadful day here, so that through the contemplation of death and the expectation of those fearsome woes, having struck awe into those lying in negligence, they might convert and bring them unto virtue, and teach them not only to place their trust in God's loving-kindness, but also to consider that He is a righteous Judge, Who renders unto each according to his works (Matt. 16:27; Rom. 2:6). Furthermore, after those days that preceded, it was needful that the Judge should also appear. And in a certain manner, this present feast is set forth today as it were the end of all feasts, since it shall indeed be the very last and final one of all concerning us.

It is proper, moreover, to see and consider that on the coming Sunday next at hand, they place the beginning of the world and Adam's very expulsion from Paradise.

This present Sunday, however, they have established on Meatfare as the end of all our affairs and of that world itself—curbing, as I deem, and restraining luxury, gluttony, and insatiable lust through the dread of the Feast, and likewise calling us unto fellow-suffering and showing mercy unto our neighbors. And in another way: since through living luxuriously we were cast out from Eden and fell under condemnation and a curse, in accordance therewith this present Feast is placed here, seeing that on the second Sunday, by way of prefiguration—that is, representation—we are to be cast out from Eden through Adam, until Christ, having come, shall again gather us and lead us into Paradise!

Moreover, this present day is named the Second Coming of Christ, because the first time He also came bodily unto us, yet exceedingly quietly and without glory.

Now, however, He shall come with supernatural wonders and with pre-eminent radiance from Heaven, and with His Body, so that He shall be recognized by all: that this is He Who came formerly and redeemed the human race, and Who is to come again to judge it and to examine whether it kept well the things given and entrusted unto it!

When, however, that Coming shall be, no one knows. For this indeed...

...the Lord concealed and kept hidden from the Apostles (Mark 13:32),[^222] although He revealed that certain signs are to precede it, which signs some of the Saints have explained at greater length in their writings.

They say also that after seven thousand years, the Coming shall take place.[^223] And before His Coming, Antichrist shall come, and shall be born, as Saint Hippolytus of Rome says,[^224] of an impure woman and a supposed virgin, being of the Jews, of the tribe of Dan, who was a son of Jacob (Gen. 49:17). And he shall walk as if living according to Christ, and shall work wonders which Christ also performed, and shall raise the dead; yet according to phantasm—that is, of delusive apparition—he shall perform all things: both his birth, and flesh, and all other things, as says the Apostle.

"And then," he says, "shall be revealed the son of perdition in all power and signs and lying wonders" (2 Thess. 2:3, 9).[^225] And yet the devil himself shall not be clothed in a body nor transformed, as John of Damascus says,[^226] but a man born of fornication shall receive all the operation of Satan, and shall suddenly emerge and arise.

Thereafter he shall be deemed by all to be benevolent and modest. And there shall be a great famine then, and in that famine he shall as it were help and rescue people; and he shall study the Divine Scriptures, and shall exercise himself in fasting; and he shall be compelled by the people, and they shall proclaim him king. And he shall greatly love the Jewish race, and shall settle in Jerusalem, and shall rebuild their temple.

And seven years before, as Daniel says,[^227] Enoch and Elijah shall come, preaching unto the people that they should not receive him; but he, having seized them, shall torment them, and thereafter shall cut off their heads (Rev. 11:3, 7). Those, however, who are called pious shall flee far away, and having found them among the mountains, he shall tempt and seduce them through demons.

Yet those seven years shall be shortened for the sake of the elect (Matt. 24:22).[^228] And there shall be a great famine, when all the elements shall be overturned, so that all shall almost vanish and perish.

Thereafter, suddenly, as it were lightning from heaven, shall be the Coming of the Lord (Matt. 24:27, 29–30; Mark 13:24–26; Luke 21:25–27; 1 Cor. 15:52; 1 Thess. 4:16; Rev. 20:10),[^229] when His Precious Cross shall go before Him, and a river of fire terribly foaming shall flow before Him, cleansing all the earth from filth and defilement! At that hour Antichrist shall immediately be seized, and his ministers, and he shall be given over unto eternal fire. And when the Angels sound the trumpet, there shall come gathered together into one heap from the ends of the earth and from all...

...elements, the whole human race unto Jerusalem, since it is the center of the world.[^230] And there the Thrones are prepared for judgment; and people stand solely with their bodies and souls, when all shall be transformed into incorruption (1 Cor. 15:52) and shall have one form, and the very elements shall receive a change for the better. It is necessary to know also this: that at that hour Christ will not seek in us fasting, nor nakedness, nor any wonders—though these things also are good—but virtues far more noble than these: that is to say, almsgiving and mercy!

For He shall say unto the righteous six certain things: "For I was hungry, and ye gave Me to eat; I was thirsty, and ye gave Me drink; I was a stranger, and ye took Me in; naked, and ye clothed Me; I was sick, and ye visited Me; I was in prison, and ye came unto Me. For inasmuch as ye did it unto one of these least, ye did it unto Me" (Matt. 25:35–40). Which virtues everyone can fulfill according to his strength.

At that hour, then, every tongue shall confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father (Phil. 2:11). The torments, moreover, which the Holy Gospel has handed down, are these: "There shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth" (Matt. 8:12); "Their worm shall not die, and their fire shall not be quenched" (Mark 9:44); and that other: "Cast him into the outer darkness" (Matt. 22:13). Which things all, having received clearly and distinctly, the Church of God understands: that delight and the Kingdom of Heaven is for the Saints a co-dwelling with God, and that for those Saints there is to be continual illumination and exaltation. In like manner also, that torment, and darkness, and the like, is estrangement from God, and the loss and ruin of souls before conscience, as those who for the sake of negligence and temporal luxury have forfeited the Divine radiance. By Thine ineffable love for mankind, O Christ God, make us worthy of Thy longed-for voice, and number us among those standing at Thy right hand, and have mercy upon us! Amen.[^231]
