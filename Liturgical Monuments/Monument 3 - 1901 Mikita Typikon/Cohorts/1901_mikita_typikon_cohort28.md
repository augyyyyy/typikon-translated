V. The Consecration of a Bishop.[^287]

It is celebrated in the episcopal cathedral. In the church there is prepared a faldstool before the Royal Doors with a seat, and in the church eagle-rugs [orletzy][^288] are spread out. On this day they ring all the bells, and the choir singeth spiritual hymns.

When the Hierarch (Archbishop) cometh into the church, and is vested in all the hierarchal vestments, then two Bishops-consecrators take the one newly elected unto a Bishop, vested in priestly vestments without the phelonion, and lead him before the Royal Doors upon the first eagle-rug.

§. 182.

The Order of the Holy Mystery of Matrimony.[^289]

I. The Order of the Blessing of the Matrimony of the First-Married.

Before the priest proceedeth unto the blessing of the marriage, he must assure himself concerning the free status of the bridegroom and the bride, concerning their mutual consent, concerning the completion of the lawful banns, and whether there be any canonical impediment (*impedimentum*) unto the marriage. Likewise the bride and bridegroom must approach unto the Holy Mystery of Repentance and Holy Communion.

On the appointed day and hour the bridegroom and bride, accompanied by witnesses (groomsmen and bridesmaids) and parents, come into the church, and take their stand before the tetrapod: the bridegroom on the right hand, and the bride on the left hand. Upon the tetrapod are laid the holy cross, the holy Gospel-Book, two crowns, and two candles.

The priest, vested in epitrachelion and phelonion, cometh out before the Royal Doors or unto the tetrapod, and questioneth the bridegroom: “Hast thou a good and unconstrained desire, and a firm purpose, to take unto thyself as wife this woman (*Name*)?” The bridegroom answereth: “I have, reverend Father.” Further he questioneth: “Hast thou not promised thyself unto another bride?” He answereth: “I have not promised myself, reverend Father.” In like manner he questioneth the bride also.

(p. 252)

After this the priest giveth into their hands lighted candles, and beginneth the betrothal, saying: “Blessed is our God...” Cantor: “Amen.” There followeth the Great Litany with special petitions for those being betrothed: “That this betrothal may be blessed...” After the litany the priest readeth two prayers, blesseth the rings, and layeth them upon the Altar-Table or upon the tetrapod. After this he taketh the ring of the bridegroom, maketh with it a cross over the head of the bridegroom, saying three times: “The servant of God (*Name*) is betrothed unto the handmaid of God (*Name*), in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.” And he placeth the ring upon a finger of his right hand. In like manner he maketh also for the bride. After this the groomsmen exchange the rings three times. The priest readeth the prayer: “O Lord our God, Who didst accompany the servant of the Patriarch Abraham...” And there followeth the Augmented Litany and the dismissal of the betrothal.

After this followeth the crowning itself.

The priest goeth before them with the censer unto the analoj [tetrapod], while the cantors sing Psalm 127 LXX [Psalm 128 MT]: “Blessed are all they that fear the Lord...” with the refrain: “Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee.”

Having arrived at the tetrapod, the bridegroom and bride lay their right hands upon the holy Gospel-Book, while the priest bindeth their hands with the epitrachelion, blesseth them with the cross, and they pronounce the marital oath of mutual fidelity, love, and the indissolubility of the union even unto death.

After this the priest beginneth: “Blessed is the Kingdom...” The Great Litany. Three long prayers. After the prayers the priest taketh the crown of the bridegroom, signeth the bridegroom cruciformly with it, and crowneth him, saying three times: “The servant of God (*Name*) is crowned unto the handmaid of God (*Name*), in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.” In like manner he crowneth the bride also. After this he blesseth both three times, saying: “O Lord our God, crown them with glory and honor!”

There followeth the Prokimenon, the Epistle (Eph. § 230), the Gospel (John § 6), the Augmented Litany and the Litany of Supplication. “Our Father.”

II. The Order of the Blessing of the Matrimony of the Second-Married.

If both (the bridegroom and bride) are second-married (widowed), it is celebrated according to a special order having a penitential character. The betrothal is not celebrated separately, but together with the crowning.

The priest vesteth only in epitrachelion and phelonion. Beginning: “Blessed is our God...” Trisagion. “O Most Holy Trinity...” “Our Father...” Troparion of the day. There followeth the litany: “In peace let us pray unto the Lord,” with the addition of petitions for the remission of sins and cleansing of the iniquities of the bridegroom and bride.

After the litany the priest readeth two prayers with penitential meaning: “O Eternal God, Who hast gathered together the dispersed into union...” and the second prayer: “O Lord our God, Who desirest that all men be saved...”

After this he placeth the rings without special prayers, but directly with the words: “The servant of God is betrothed...”

After this the common cup is offered, the procession around the tetrapod with the singing of: “Rejoice, O Isaiah...”, the removal of crowns with the corresponding prayers.

§. 183.

The Order of the Holy Mystery of Holy Anointing [Eleosvyashcheniye].

This holy Mystery is celebrated over the severely ill by a synod of seven priests (whence also the name “Soborovanie”), but in case of need it can be celebrated also by a smaller number of priests, even by a single priest.

In the house of the sick person there is prepared a table covered with a white cloth. Upon the table is set a vessel with wheat (or rye), and upon the wheat is placed a lamp (or cup) with oil mixed with wine. Around the vessel are set 7 small wands wrapped with cotton [struptsy] for the anointing, and 7 lighted candles.

The priests vest in epitrachelia and phelonia (if, however, there be one priest, then in epitrachelion and phelonion).

(p. 255)

The usual beginning: “Blessed is our God...” Trisagion. “Our Father.” Psalm 142 LXX [Psalm 143 MT]: “O Lord, hear my prayer...” The Little Litany. Penitential troparia: “Have mercy on us, O Lord, have mercy on us...” Psalm 50 LXX [Psalm 51 MT]: “Have mercy upon me, O God...” The Canon of Holy Anointing, the composition of Patriarch Arsenius, in Tone 4: “O bountiful and merciful Lord...” After the 9th Ode, the stichera and the troparion: “O Christ, Who alone art swift to help...”

There followeth the Great Litany of Holy Anointing: “In peace let us pray unto the Lord... That this oil may be blessed...” After the litany the presiding priest readeth the prayer over the oil: “O Lord, Who by Thy mercy and bounties healest the contrite of our souls and bodies, do Thou Thyself bless this oil also...”

After this followeth the 7-fold reading of the Epistle, the Gospel, the prayer, and the 7-fold anointing of the sick person.[^291]

The 1st Epistle from James (§ 57: “Is any sick among you...”). The Gospel from Luke (§ 51: concerning the Good Samaritan). The 1st Prayer: “O Father without beginning, eternal, Holy of Holies...” After the prayer the priest taketh a wand, dippeth it into the holy oil, and anointeth the sick person cruciformly upon the forehead, the nostrils, the cheeks, the lips, the breast, and upon both hands,[^292] saying the form of the mystery: “O Holy Father, Physician of souls and bodies... [Forma Sacramenti] heal Thy servant (*Name*) from the bodily and spiritual infirmity which holdeth him...”[^293]

In like manner are celebrated also the second, third, fourth, fifth, sixth, and seventh anointings with the corresponding Epistles, Gospels, and prayers.

There followeth the Augmented Litany: “Have mercy on us, O God...” with the petition for the healing of the sick person, and the dismissal. The sick person asketh forgiveness from the Priests and from all his kinsfolk and relatives, saying three times: “Bless me, holy fathers, and forgive me, a sinner.” And the Priests answer: “May God forgive and heal thee.”
