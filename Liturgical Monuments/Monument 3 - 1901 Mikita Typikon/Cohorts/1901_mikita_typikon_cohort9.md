§. 23.

What Is Further Necessary to Note Concerning These Designations?

(α) The evening chant “Gladsome Light” is called the “Lucernarium Hymn” (*hymnos epilychnios* [ὕμνος ἐπιλύχνιος] == *hymnus lucernarius*). This hymn, as St. Basil saith,[^67] the Holy Martyr Athenogenes († 169) composed; nevertheless it is customarily attributed unto St. Sophronius, Patriarch of Jerusalem, probably for this reason, that he introduced it into church use in his province around the year 634.
(β) A twofold doxology (*doxologia* [δοξολογία]) do we have: little and great. The Little Doxology is: “Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit,” etc.; the Great, however, is “Glory to God in the highest.” This hymn is called also the Angelic Hymn (*hymnus angelicus*). — Here, nevertheless, it is also necessary to note that in church books, when the hymn “Glory to God in the highest” is only to be read, then this is called the “Little Doxology,” whereas the singing of this hymn is called the “Great Doxology.”
(γ) The hymn “Holy God” is called the “Trisagion” (*hymnos trisagios* [ὕμνος τρισάγιος]). It came into church use during the time of

<!-- LEAF: p82 -->
St. Proclus, Patriarch of Constantinople († 446), and Emperor Theodosius II (408–450).[^68]
(δ) The hymn “Let us who mystically represent the Cherubim” is called the “Cherubic Hymn” (*hymnus Cherubicus*).[^69]
(ε) “Holy, Holy, Holy” is called the “Triumphal Hymn” (*hymnos epinikios* [ὕμνος ἐπινίκιος] == *hymnus victoriae et triumphi*).
(ϛ) Antidoron (in Greek: *antidoron* [ἀντίδωρον], from *anti* and *hagia dora* [ἀντὶ et ἅγια δῶρα]) are particles of the blessed prosphora wherefrom the Lamb was taken for the sacred action, which instead of the holy gifts, instead of Holy Communion, it is necessary to give unto those who did not participate in the Holy Mysteries.[^70] Pope St. Pius I, Universal Pontiff, instituted the distribution of the antidoron.[^71]

## Part II
### Concerning Church Services in Particular

Since public services, as was said above, are divided into three parts: (α) into pre-liturgical services, (β) into liturgical services, and (γ) into services following the Divine Liturgy; therefore, conformably unto this division, we divide this Part II into three sections: and in Section I we will speak in particular concerning pre-liturgical services; in Section II concerning liturgical services; and in Section III concerning the celebration of the Holy Mysteries and other sacred actions.

<!-- LEAF: p83 -->
### Section I

§. 24.

Concerning Pre-Liturgical Services

The pre-liturgical services are the following: (α) Vespers;[^72] (β) Compline; (γ) Midnight Office; (δ) Matins; (ε) The First, Third, Sixth, and Ninth Hours;[^73] and (ϛ) Typika.[^74] The aggregation of all these services is called the “Church Rule” (*pensum canonicum*). According to present practice, each service among the adduced divine services is not celebrated separately, but they are rather combined, so that the entire church rule is celebrated at three intervals; and in that manner each day hath a threefold service: evening, morning, and daytime. (α) The evening service consisteth of the Ninth Hour, Vespers, and Compline. (β) The morning service consisteth of the Midnight Office, Matins, and the First Hour. (γ) The daytime service consisteth of the Third and Sixth Hours, the Divine Liturgy, or the Typika. In this section we will speak first concerning the individual parts of the church rule, and secondly concerning the church rule according to diverse occasions.

Parts of the Church Rule

##### 1) Ordinary Vespers

§. 26.

Order of Little Vespers

The Priest, having come into the church and taken upon himself the epitrachelion, bowing before the royal doors maketh the usual beginning, that is: “Blessed is our God,” “Heavenly King,” the Trisagion, “Most Holy Trinity,” “Our Father” with the Exclamation, “Lord, have mercy” (12 times), “Come, let us worship” (thrice). (Thus far the usual beginning). And immediately the reader in a quiet voice readeth the evening psalm: “Bless the Lord, O my soul.” After the psalm: “Glory,” and “Now and,” “Alleluia” (thrice) without chanting. There is no Litany of Peace, but the Priest entereth into the sanctuary, and the choir instead of it saith: “Lord, have mercy” (12 times), and immediately chanteth: “Lord, I have cried.” Stichera on 4, that is, from the verse: “From the morning watch.” At the conclusion of the stichera: “Glory,” and “Now and,” Theotokion. After this the Priest at his place in the sanctuary proclaimeth: “Wisdom! Stand upright!” and the choir singeth: “Gladsome Light.” At “Let us attend! Peace be to all! Wisdom, let us attend!” the choir singeth the Daily Prokimenon, after which the reader readeth: “Vouchsafe, O Lord.” After “Vouchsafe” there is no litany “Let us complete our evening prayer,” but instead of this the choir saith: “Lord, have mercy” (12 times), and immediately singeth the aposticha stichera (3). After “Glory,” and “Now and” followeth “Now lettest Thou,” then: Trisagion, “Our Father” with the Exclamation, Troparion of the feast or troparion unto the Saint, “Glory,” and “Now and,” Theotokion. After these the Priest cometh forth from the sanctuary and chanteth the litany: “Have mercy on us, O God,” with the Least Dismissal: from “Glory to Thee, O Christ God, our hope, glory to Thee.”

Little Vespers is celebrated on Feasts of the Lord (except the Nativity and Theophany), Feasts of the Mother of God, and of Solemn Saints always in the evening, namely, when on the morrow Great Vespers with Litiya and the blessing of loaves taketh place.

§. 27.

Order of Daily Vespers without Entrance

The Priest cometh forth in the epitrachelion before the royal doors, and having made bows, maketh the usual beginning as at Little Vespers, that is: “Blessed is our God,” “Heavenly King,” the Trisagion, “Most Holy Trinity,” “Our Father” with the Exclamation, “Lord, have mercy” (12 times), and “Come, let us worship” (thrice). After this, Psalm 103: “Bless the Lord, O my soul.” Upon the conclusion of this, “Glory,” and “Now and,” “Alleluia” (thrice), “Glory to Thee, O God” with singing; and immediately the Priest loudly proclaimeth the Litany of Peace. After this litany the Priest goeth into the sanctuary, and the chanter readeth the kathisma of the day with three antiphons, but before it: “Lord, have mercy” (thrice), “Glory,” and “Now and.” Upon the conclusion of the kathisma: “Lord, have mercy” (twice), “To Thee, O Lord,” “Amen.” And immediately from Psalm 140 the first verse “Lord, I have cried” and the second verse “Let my prayer be set forth” are sung, while the remaining verses of the psalm are read. Psalm 141, “With my voice unto the Lord,” is also read, likewise also Psalm 129, “Out of the depths,” even unto the verse “If Thou, Lord, shouldest mark iniquities”; for here we set 6 verses and chant them unto the stichera appointed at “Lord, I have cried.” After “Glory,” and “Now and,” the Priest loudly proclaimeth: “Wisdom! Stand upright!” and the choir singeth: “Gladsome Light.” After this the Priest proclaimeth: “Let us attend! Peace be to all! Wisdom, let us attend!” whereunto the choir singeth the Daily Prokimenon which is in the Horologion, and afterwards there is read: “Vouchsafe, O Lord.” Thereafter the Priest cometh forth from the sanctuary before the royal doors and loudly proclaimeth the litany: “Let us complete.” Having finished this litany and with the second exclamation: “Blessed and glorified be the majesty,” he goeth into the holy sanctuary. After that, the aposticha stichera (3), then “Now lettest Thou.” Trisagion. “Our Father” with the Exclamation. Troparion unto the Saint who falleth on the morning of the day. “Glory,” and “Now and,” Dismissal Theotokion according to the tone and day. During the singing of the troparia the Priest cometh forth, and after the singing saith the litany: “Have mercy on us, O God,” and the Exclamation. After this the Small Dismissal from “Wisdom.”

This Vespers is celebrated on all weekdays, if on the morning of the day there be no Great Saint.

§. 30.

(α) On Sunday[^83] evening such a Vespers is celebrated which up to the prokimenon: “Turn not away Thy face”[^84] or “Thou hast given an inheritance,”
