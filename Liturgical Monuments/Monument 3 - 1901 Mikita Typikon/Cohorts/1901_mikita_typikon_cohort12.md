there follow (the Trisagion, “Our Father,” “Lord, have mercy” [12 times] are omitted) the prayers: “O undefiled, unblemished,” and “And grant unto us,” “Our hope,” but without kneeling. (The prayer “O most merciful Master” is omitted.) The smallest dismissal, commemorations, and the blessing.

§. 42.

Order of Great Compline with Litiya.

The Priest in phelonion and epitrachelion censeth the holy sanctuary, openeth the royal doors, censeth the whole church, standeth before the royal doors, and when the chanter saith: “Command!” he beginneth: “O Lord, bless!” And he proclaimeth: “Glory to the holy...” — and thus followeth: “Come, let us worship.” The Priest, having entered after this into the holy sanctuary, shutteth the royal doors, and having divested of the phelonion, in the epitrachelion cometh forth before the royal doors. The chanter meanwhile beginneth Part I of Great Compline, saying after “Come, let us worship” 3 psalms. The Priest goeth into the holy sanctuary, and the chanter readeth Psalms: 4, 6, 12 [Pss. 4, 6, 12]. “Glory,” and “Now and,” “Alleluia” (thrice), “Lord, have mercy” (thrice), “Glory,” and “Now and.” And immediately Psalms: 24, 30, and the 90th [Pss. 24, 30, 90]. “Glory,” and “Now and,” “Alleluia” (thrice), “Lord, have mercy” (thrice), “Glory,” and “Now and.” And immediately the Priest cometh forth and singeth once: “God is with us.” The choir repeateth this 1st verse twice. After this the Priest, standing before the royal doors, readeth the verses: “Unto the ends of the earth,” etc., and the choir after each verse singeth: “God is with us,” etc. At the end the Priest again singeth this first verse once, and the choir repeateth it twice. After this canticle of the Prophet Isaiah there followeth: “The day being past,” etc., “The bodiless nature,” “O Most Holy Virgin,” “I believe,” “O Most Holy Trinity,” each verse twice. After these: The Trisagion. “Our Father” with the exclamation. The troparion of the feast (for instance, of the Nativity of Christ, the Theophany, or the Annunciation), and that once. “Lord, have mercy” (40 times), “Glory,” and “Now and,” “More honorable,” “In the name of the Lord,” etc. And the Priest proclaimeth: “Through the prayers of our holy Fathers,” etc., and with the prayer: “O Lord, Lord of hosts,” concludeth Part I, having entered into the sanctuary. Part II beginneth from “Come, let us worship” (thrice), after which follow Psalms: 50 and the 101st [Pss. 50, 101]. The prayer of King Manasses, which the Priest readeth before the royal doors. The Trisagion. “Our Father” with the exclamation. The kontakion of the feast. “Lord, have mercy” (40 times), “Glory,” and “Now and,” “More honorable,” “In the name of the Lord,” etc., and the Priest proclaimeth: “O Lord Jesus Christ,” etc., and with the prayer: “O Master, God the Father,” etc., concludeth Part II, having entered into

the holy sanctuary. Part III beginneth from “Come, let us worship” (thrice). After this Psalms: 69 and the 142nd [Pss. 69, 142]. “Glory to God in the highest” is read. — Meanwhile the Priest vesteth in the phelonion, openeth the royal doors, blesseth the censer, and in the usual manner, as at the entrance, goeth forth by the northern door, censeth the entire church, and after the censing goeth unto the table or *Tetrapod* in the midst of the church, where there are already prepared 5 loaves or prosphora, wheat, wine, and oil. The arrangement of the *Tetrapod* is thus: on the right side is placed the wine, on the left the oil, in the middle the wheat, and upon it 5 loaves (prosphora), and that four prosphora are placed cruciformly, and the fifth lieth upon them touching all four. After this the choir after “Glory to God in the highest,” etc., singeth the stichera at the Litiya (and that 5 with “Glory,” and “Now and”), and upon the conclusion of these the Priest chanteth the litany at the Litiya: “Save, O God, Thy people,” etc. After this, the stichera at the aposticha. Next: “Now Thou lettest depart.” The Trisagion. “Our Father” with the exclamation. The troparion of the feast (thrice). (When the Priest singeth the troparion, he at the same time also censeth.) After the troparion the Priest proclaimeth: “Let us pray to the Lord,” and readeth the prayer over the blessing of the loaves: “O Lord Jesus Christ,” etc., taking first the prosphora lying on top, and having then set it back in its place, with outstretched fingers he signeth the entire oblation at the words: “Bless these loaves, the wheat, the wine, and the oil” with a single sign of the cross. After this prayer followeth: “Blessed be the name of the Lord” (thrice), while the Priest censeth the oblation, — and after this Psalm 33 [Ps. 33] is read up to “Shall not want any good thing.” The Priest goeth unto the royal doors, and the chanter upon the conclusion of the psalm saith: “Lord, have mercy” (thrice), “Glory,” and “Now and,” “In the name of the Lord bless, Father”; and the Priest standing before the royal doors, at the exclamation of the choir: “In the name of the Lord bless, Father,” beginneth Matins, saying: “Glory to the holy...” etc.[^113]

It is necessary to note that if Compline is combined with Litiya, then from Compline everything beginning from the canon is omitted.

### ARTICLE III.

§. 43.

On the Midnight Offices.

The ordinary Midnight Office is threefold: (α) daily, (β) Saturday, and (γ) Sunday.

<!-- LEAF: p113 -->
The daily Midnight Office is read on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday. — The Saturday one, however, only on Saturday; and the Sunday one on Sundays of the whole year whereon there is no vigil.

Besides the ordinary Midnight Offices there are also extraordinary ones. Such are: (α) in the Great Fast; (β) on feasts; (γ) on Holy and Great Saturday.

### 1) Ordinary Midnight Offices.

§. 44.

Order of the Daily Midnight Office.

On weekdays it is usually read in private, but sometimes it is also sung in church before Matins. — If we sing this Midnight Office in church, the Priest in the epitrachelion before the royal doors maketh the usual beginning, proclaiming: “Blessed is our God,” etc. After this: “O Heavenly King,” The Trisagion. “Our Father” with the exclamation. “Lord, have mercy” (12 times), “Come, let us worship” (thrice). And the Priest entereth into the holy sanctuary. Psalm 50 [Ps. 50]. The 17th Kathisma, which hath 3 antiphons. Before the 2nd and 3rd antiphons: “Alleluia” (thrice), “Lord, have mercy” (thrice), “Glory,” and “Now and.” After this: “I believe.” The Trisagion. “Our Father” with the exclamation. Troparia: “Behold, the Bridegroom,” “Glory,” “That day,” and “Now and,” “Thee, the invincible rampart”; and immediately “Lord, have mercy” (40 times). After this the Priest cometh forth and readeth the prayer: “Thou Who at all times.” After this: “Lord, have mercy” (thrice), “Glory,” and “Now and,” “More honorable,” “In the name of the Lord,” etc., and the Priest immediately proclaimeth: “God be merciful unto us,” etc., whereunto the choir answereth: “Amen.” After this the Priest with the prayer: “O Master, God the Father,” etc., concludeth Part I.

Part II beginneth from: “Come, let us worship” (thrice), and immediately Psalms: 120 and the 133rd [Pss. 120, 133]. “Glory,” and “Now and.” The Trisagion. “Our Father” with the exclamation. After the exclamation follow the troparia for the departed: “Remember, O Lord,” “With profound wisdom,” “Glory,” “With the Saints give rest,” “Now and,” “All generations bless thee,” etc. After this the chanter saith: “Lord, have mercy” (12 times), whereunto the Priest readeth the prayer: “Remember, O Lord.” There are added also the troparia: “Have mercy on us, O Lord,” etc. Litany: “Have mercy on us, O God,” etc. (*Molitvoslov*, p. 47). After these, the smallest dismissal. Commemorations: “Let us pray for our most holy Hierarch,” etc. Furthermore after the commemorations the Priest proclaimeth aloud the prayers: “Remit, forgive,” “Those who hate,” etc., and giveth the blessing.

<!-- LEAF: p114 -->
Note: In our *Molitvoslov* there are after the prayer “For the departed” also the troparia: “Have mercy on us, O Lord,” etc., and the litany: “Have mercy on us, O God,” further “Remit, forgive,” etc., and “Those who hate and wrong us,” etc., but all this is not contained in the Horologia.[^114]

§. 45.

Order of the Saturday Midnight Office.

The Saturday Midnight Office is celebrated in the same manner as the daily one, except that: (α) instead of the 17th Kathisma the 9th Kathisma is taken, that is to say, Psalms: 64, 65, 66, 67, 68, and the 69th [Pss. 64, 65, 66, 67, 68, 69]; (β) instead of the troparia: “Behold, the Bridegroom,” etc., there are read the troparia in honor of the Most Holy Trinity: “Uncreated nature”; finally (γ) instead of the prayer: “Those who hate,” etc., the prayer of St. Eustratius is taken: “Magnifying, I magnify Thee,” etc.

§. 46.

Order of the Sunday Midnight Office.

If it be celebrated in church, then: The Priest, having put on the epitrachelion, maketh before the royal doors the usual beginning: “Blessed is our God,” etc. The choir: “Amen,” and immediately: “O Heavenly King,” The Trisagion. “Our Father” with the exclamation. “Lord, have mercy” (12 times), “Come, let us worship” (thrice). During this the Priest entereth into the holy sanctuary, and the chanter beginneth to read Psalm 50 [Ps. 50]. After this (instead of the kathisma), the canon to the Most Holy Trinity, which is found in the Octoechos under each tone. After the canon, first the Triadicon Megalynarion, consisting of several troparia (“It is truly meet to praise Thee, Who art God,” “The Chief of the Cherubim,” “Unto the unoriginate Father and God,” “Let us all sing,” “With radiant,” “Before Whom tremble,” “It is truly meet reverently,” “Receive tears,” “Glory,” “O Father Almighty,” and at the end “Now and,” “From the dead”). After this is sung the Megalynarion of the Theotokos: “It is truly right,” and thus: The Trisagion. “Our Father” with the exclamation and (among us) the Troparia:[^115] “Have mercy on us, O Lord, have mercy on us,” and the litany: “Have mercy on us, O God,” etc., with the exclamation: “Hear us, O God.” After the litany the smallest dismissal, and that

<!-- LEAF: p115 -->
the resurrectional one (“Christ, our true God, Who rose from the dead,” etc.). Commemorations. (The commemoration for the departed is omitted.) The prayer: “Remit, forgive,” etc., and the prayer to the Most Holy Trinity: “All-powerful and life-creating,” and thus the blessing.

If, however, the Sunday Midnight Office is read in private, then there is read the general Triadicon canon, recorded in the *Molitvoslov*, and at the end after the troparia “Have mercy on us, O Lord” the litany: “Have mercy on us, O God,” etc., is not said, but instead of it: “Lord, have mercy” (40 times), “Glory,” and “Now and,” “Lord, have mercy” (thrice), “Lord, bless,” and the Dismissal. And thus the prayer: “All-powerful and life-creating,” etc.

### 2) Extraordinary Midnight Offices.

§. 47.

Order of the Daily Midnight Office in the Holy Great Fast.

It is celebrated in the same manner as the daily Midnight Office at other times, except that it is necessary to note that at the end of Part I, after “God be merciful unto us,” etc., and before the prayer: “O Master, God the Father,” there are prostrations with the prayer of St. Ephrem: “O Lord and Master,” etc. And that is: (α) three great prostrations with the prayer of St. Ephrem. (A great prostration is when a man falleth upon his knees and boweth his head unto the earth as far as he can.) (β) 12 lesser prostrations (with the verses: “O God, be merciful unto me a sinner”; “O God, cleanse my sins and have mercy on me”; and “Without number have I sinned, O Lord, forgive me”). After the prayer: “O Master, God the Father,” again one great prostration. The rest in the usual manner.

§. 48.

Order of the Festal Midnight Office.

If a certain feast of the Lord, or a feast of the Mother of God, or a feast of a great Saint, or the Afterfeast of the Nativity of Christ and the Theophany fall upon weekdays whereon Litiya is not celebrated: then the Midnight Office is celebrated in the same manner as the daily Midnight Office, except that: (α) the troparion: “Behold, the Bridegroom,” etc., is omitted, and instead of these the troparion of the feast is sung; (β) the troparion and prayer for the departed are omitted, and

<!-- LEAF: p116 -->
instead of them the kontakion of the feast is sung. Upon the conclusion of the kontakion there are sung among us the Troparia: “Have mercy on us, O Lord,” etc., and the litany: “Have mercy on us, O God,” etc., with the exclamation: “Hear us, O God.”[^116] Dismissal. Commemorations. (The commemoration for the departed is omitted.) Blessing. (The last two prayers are omitted.)

§. 49.

Order of the Midnight Office on Holy and Great Saturday.

Before the Resurrection of Christ, likewise before midnight, there is to be celebrated the Midnight Office, or the so-called “Burial Service” (*Nadhrobne*). And that in the following order: The Priest, vested in a black epitrachelion, before the royal doors beginneth the Midnight Office with: “Blessed is our God.” After this the choir: “Amen,” “O Heavenly King,” “Our Father,” “Come, let us worship” (thrice). Psalm 50 [Ps. 50]. And there followeth the Matins canon of Great Saturday: “By a wave of the sea.” After the canon, instead of “It is truly right,” the Heirmos: “Weep not for Me, O Mother.” And immediately: The Trisagion. “Our Father” with the exclamation. Troparia: “When Thou didst descend,” “The noble Joseph,” and “Unto the Myrrhbearers.” After these the Priest goeth from the royal doors unto the Tomb, and having bowed, censeth the Shroud (*Plashchanytsia*), and after this transferreth It unto the holy table, where he spreadeth It out and again censeth It. After this he saith the litany: “Have mercy on us, O God,” etc., as at the Sunday Midnight Office. Dismissal.

### ARTICLE IV.

§. 50.

On Matins.

Ordinary Matins is divided into: (α) Small, that is to say, daily without the Great Doxology; and (β) Great, with the Great Doxology.

Daily Matins is celebrated on weekdays if there occur on them one or more minor Saints.

<!-- LEAF: p117 -->
Great Matins, however, is celebrated on feasts and on Sundays. And in accordance with this it is: Festal and Sunday. But since feasts are of two kinds: those with the Doxology and those with the Polyeleos, so also festal Matins services are of two kinds, that is to say: (α) with the Doxology, but without the Polyeleos; (β) with both the Doxology and the Polyeleos.

Besides these ordinary Matins services there are also extraordinary ones, whereof the most notable are the following: (α) Matins for the departed; (β) Matins in the Great Fast; (γ) Matins with the Akathist; (δ) Matins in Passion Week; (ε) Matins with the Stases on Holy and Great Saturday; (ζ) Matins in Bright Week; (η) Matins on the Exaltation of the Precious Cross.
