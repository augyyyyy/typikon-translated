§. 196.

Order of the Blessing of Palms.

The Blessing of Palms taketh place on Palm Sunday at Matins. The cantor after the Gospel beginneth to read Psalm 50, and the Priest, without saying “Having beheld the Resurrection of Christ,” immediately goeth forth unto the branches and censeth them cruciformly. When the choir hath finished Psalm 50, the Priest proclaimeth: “Let us pray to the Lord,” and in a loud voice saith the prayer: “O Lord our God, Who sittest upon the Cherubim,” etc. After this: “Peace be unto all.” “Bow your heads,” etc., and the exclamation: “O Lord God Almighty,” etc. At the conclusion of this the Priest sprinkleth the branches with holy water, saying: “These branches and palms are blessed and sanctified by the sprinkling of this holy water, in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, Amen,” etc. At the conclusion of this the Priest giveth the Gospel to kiss and distributeth the branches; and Matins continueth as prescribed.

§. 197.

Order of the Blessing of Foods.

The Blessing of Foods taketh place on Pascha Sunday.

At the Liturgy after the Ambo Prayer and after “Christ is risen” hath been sung three times, etc., the Priest proclaimeth: “Glory to the Holy, Consubstantial,” etc., unto which the choir singeth: “Amen,” etc. After this “Christ is risen” three times, during which singing the Priest, having come unto the place where the artos and the foods have been prepared, censeth them. After this he proclaimeth: “Let us pray to the Lord,” and reciteth three prayers, namely: a) for the blessing of the artos, b) for the blessing of the lamb and other meat foods, and c) for the blessing of dairy and other foods. And having finished these prayers, he sprinkleth the foods with holy water, saying: “These foods are blessed and sanctified by the sprinkling of this holy water, in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit,” etc., and the Dismissal.

§. 198.

Order of the Blessing of a Sown Field.

The blessing of a sown field taketh place around the feast of Saint Mark, and that in the following manner:

When the Priest at the Liturgy after “Blessed be the name of the Lord” hath pronounced the words: “Let us depart in peace,” etc., the procession, having gone forth from the church, with the cross-bearer preceding, goeth unto the field. On the way various songs are sung, especially: “Our Father” and “O Theotokos Virgin.”[^311]

Having arrived at the field at the designated place, the Priest first of all turneth with his face toward the east (First Stasis), and the cantor beginneth to read Psalm 84: “Lord, Thou hast been favorable unto Thy land.” After this: “Glory, and Now. Alleluia” (three times), and the Priest proclaimeth: “Let us pray to the Lord,” and in a loud voice reciteth the prayer: “O Lord God from before all ages,” etc., and so follow the Gospel and the second prayer: “O Almighty God.” After this the Priest first conjureth with the words: “I conjure you, aerial waves,” etc., afterwards sprinkleth the field with holy water and blesseth the seeds, saying: “May God bless,” etc., and with this he concludeth the stasis.

After this the Priest turneth toward the south (Second Stasis), and the choir readeth Psalm 125: “When the Lord turned again the captivity of Zion”; after this the Priest in a loud voice reciteth the prayer: “O Holy Master,” then the Gospel, and again saith the prayer: “Multiply, we pray Thee, O Master”; after the prayer he first conjureth, saying: “I conjure you, clouds,” etc., and so blesseth the field with holy water, saying: “Bless, O Lord, the earth,” etc.

At the Third Stasis the Priest turneth toward the west; the cantor readeth Psalm 146: “Praise the Lord”; after which the Priest in a loud voice reciteth the prayer: “O Master Jesus Christ,” readeth the Gospel, and again the second prayer: “O Almighty,” etc., after which he first conjureth with the words: “I conjure you, waves and winds,” etc., and only so blesseth, saying: “May He bless you, the seeds,” etc.

At the Fourth Stasis he turneth toward the north, and the choir readeth Psalm 147: “Praise Jerusalem,” after which the Priest in a loud voice reciteth the prayer: “O Creator of all,” readeth the Gospel, and the second prayer in a loud voice: “O Holy Master,” etc. And he conjureth with the words: “I conjure you, devils,” etc.; afterwards he blesseth the field, sprinkling with holy water, saying as before: “May He bless,” and with this is concluded also the Fourth Stasis, after which they return unto the church.

§. 199.

Order of the Blessing of Herbs.

Among us for the most part herbs are blessed on the day of the Nativity of Saint John the Baptist, in some places however on the Dormition of the Most Holy Virgin.

After the Liturgy after “Blessed be the name,” the Priest proclaimeth: “Blessed is our God,” and beginneth to cense the herbs, which the people hold standing on both sides from the Royal Doors even unto the church gates. The cantor, having sung “Amen,” saith: “Heavenly King,” “Trisagion,” “Our Father,” “Lord, have mercy” (12 times), “Come, let us worship” (three times), and immediately readeth Psalm 64 (“Praise is meet for Thee”). After the Psalm he singeth the troparion and kontakion of the feast, unto which the Priest: “Let us pray to the Lord,” and in a loud voice reciteth the prayer: “O Almighty and Pre-Eternal God.” After this prayer he in a loud voice reciteth the second prayer: “O God, Who through Moses,” and so: “Peace be unto all,” “Bow your heads,” etc., with the exclamation. After the exclamation he sprinkleth the herbs with holy water, walking in the midst of the people and saying: “May the blessing of God Almighty, the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit, descend upon this creation and abide with it always. Amen,” etc. Dismissal.

§. 200.

What Other Blessings We Have.

a) The Blessing of Candles: it is twofold: on the feast of the Meeting of the Lord before the Holy Liturgy solemnly;[^312] on other feast days, however, it is performed in brief, and that in such a manner, that after “Blessed be the name of the Lord” the Priest proclaimeth: “Let us pray to the Lord,” and readeth the prayer: “O God Almighty,” etc. After the prayer he sprinkleth the candles with holy water, saying: “These lights are sanctified by the sprinkling of this holy water, in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, Amen.”

b) Grapes or vine berries and grain seeds are blessed on the feast of the Transfiguration of Christ, and that in token of thanksgiving for the receiving of these fruits and in test-

<!-- LEAF: p294 -->

(p. 270)

-imony that the wine and bread from these fruits must be brought for the sacred action.

c) On the feast of the Nativity of the Most Holy Virgin the field seeds that have already been sown are blessed.

d) On the day of the Falling Asleep of Saint John the Theologian taketh place the blessing of wine.

Besides these, yet other blessings of this kind are traced in the Trebnik, but concerning these we do not speak.

§. 201.

Order of the Blessing and Laying of the First Stone for the Foundation of a Church.

The Priest, having received from the Bishop the authority for the foundation of a church and for the blessing of the first stone, performeth this rite in this manner: On the preceding day the Priest erecteth a wooden cross upon that place where the Altar-Table is to be; and by the workmen on that place where the foundation is to be, trenches are excavated, and stones are prepared, of which one four-cornered stone is to be with the representation of the Holy Cross.

On the following day, on the day of the blessing, the Priest vesteth in bright vestments and goeth with the whole clergy, the deacons preceding him with censers, and the church servers with holy water and with other necessary things, unto this place. As they go, the cantors sing the litiya stichera of the Saint unto whom the temple will be dedicated.

Having arrived at the place, they place upon the prepared Tetrapod candles, the Gospel, and the Holy Cross.[^313] And the Priest, having censed the Tetrapod cruciformly, proclaimeth: “Blessed is our God,” etc. After this followeth: “Heavenly King,” “Trisagion,” “Our Father,” “Come, let us worship” (three times), etc. Psalm 83: (“How lovely are Thy tabernacles, O Lord.”) The Litany of Peace and the troparia: “Thou Who hast founded it, O Christ,” etc.

After the troparia the Priest sprinkleth with holy water the place where the Holy Cross is to be — or hath already been erected — and saith the prayer: “Deign, O Christ our God,” etc.; and having taken the Holy Cross, if it is not yet erected, he erecteth it, while the choir singeth the troparion: “The Cross having been erected on the earth.” Afterwards the Priest

before the erected cross readeth this prayer: “O Lord, Incomprehensible God,” etc.

After the prayer follow the Prokeimenon, the Epistle, the Gospel.

After this the Priest goeth unto the trench where the foundation is to be laid, and with all the clergy singeth in Tone 6: “Arise, as tomorrow,” etc., and there is first read Psalm 126: “Except the Lord build the house.” After this the Priest in a loud voice reciteth the prayers: “O Lord Jesus Christ,” and “Bless, O Lord, this creation.”

After the prayers the Priest sprinkleth the stone with holy water and with his own hands layeth it upon the foundation of the sanctuary, saying: “In the faith of Jesus Christ, this first stone, being laid into this foundation, I bless and establish: In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, Amen,” etc.

And the stone laid and strengthened he sprinkleth with holy water, saying: “How awesome is this place,” etc.[^314]

After this the choir readeth Psalm 86: “His foundation is in the holy mountains,” at the conclusion of which the Priest encompasseth the whole place of the sanctuary, sprinkling it with holy water, going first toward the north, and after that toward the west.

Finally, having returned unto the place where he had begun to sprinkle, he standeth before the erected cross, saying: “Again and again, having bowed our knees, let us pray to the Lord,” and there follow two prayers: “O God Almighty,” and “O Lord our God.” At the end, when they have stood up, the Litany: “Have mercy on us, O God,” the Dismissal, and Many Years.

§. 202.

Order of the Blessing of a New Church.[^315]

The Priest, having permission from the Bishop, wishing to bless a new church, after the singing of Matins, vested in all the priestly vestments, goeth with the other invited Priests unto the doors of the church, one holy cross preceding them, where first of all he saith secretly the prayer of intention: “O God of our action.” After this he beginneth in a loud voice: “Blessed is our God,” etc. “Heavenly King,” “Trisagion,” “Our Father,” “Come, let us worship” (three times). §. 203.

Order of the Blessing of the Altar-Table Itself.

The Altar-Table is twofold: one immovable, and the other movable, that is: the Antimension. Both are consecrated by the Bishop, but for the blessing of an immovable Altar-Table a Priest also can have authority from the Bishop.

The Priest, wishing to bless an Altar-Table, vesteth in all the priestly vestments and, having come before the Altar-Table that is to be blessed, standeth there before the placed Tetrapod. Upon this Tetrapod are placed the Cross, lighted candles, holy water, a sprinkler, and likewise also the Antimension, which must be placed upon the new Altar-Table.

After this he performeth this rite thus: First, standing before the Tetrapod, he proclaimeth: “Blessed is our God,” etc. “Heavenly King,” etc., “Trisagion,” etc., “Our Father.” “Come, let us worship” (three times). Psalm 83: (“How lovely are Thy dwellings, O Lord,” etc.) During the Psalm the Priest sprinkleth the Altar-Table with holy water, saying: “Thou shalt sprinkle me with hyssop, and I shall be cleansed; Thou shalt wash me, and I shall be made whiter than snow.” The cantor, after the Psalm, singeth the troparion of the Saint whose image is above the Altar-Table.

The Priest, after the sprinkling, placeth the Antimension upon the Altar-Table, and censeth the table. After this he proclaimeth: “With compunction, having bowed our knees, let us pray to the Lord,” and the Priest, having bowed his knees, in a loud voice readeth the prayer: “O Lord God of hosts,” etc. At the end the usual dismissal of the day, and the Divine Liturgy is celebrated upon the newly-blessed Altar-Table.

§. 205.

Order of the Blessing and Sanctification of Sacred Vessels, that is: the Diskos, Chalice, Star, and Spoon.

To bless and sanctify the Chalice, Diskos, Star, Spoon, and Ciborium pertaineth properly unto the Bishop alone; but if a Priest receiveth authority for this from the Bishop, he performeth this in this manner:

Before the Royal Doors a Tetrapod is placed, and upon it the vessels. The Priest, vested in epitrachelion and phelonion, cometh hither and first censeth them cruciformly, and after this proclaimeth: “Blessed is our God.” Heavenly King. Trisagion. Our Father. Lord, have mercy (12 times). Come, let us worship (12 times). And thus is read Psalm 22: “The Lord is my shepherd.”

After the Psalm the Priest proclaimeth: “Let us pray to the Lord,” and in a loud voice reciteth the prayer: “O Master Almighty,” etc.

After this prayer he first sanctifieth the chalice and cup, proclaiming: “Let us pray to the Lord,” and saying the prayer: “O Master Almighty, unto Thee we pray,” etc., and immediately: “Peace be unto all.” “Bow your heads,” and the exclamation: “For Thou art the fountain,” etc. And upon this he sprinkleth the vessels with holy water, saying: “These vessels are sanctified by the grace of the All-Holy Spirit, by the sprinkling of this holy water, in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, Amen.”

After this, wishing to sanctify the Star, he again proclaimeth: “Let us pray to the Lord,” and readeth the prayer: “O Lord Jesus Christ our God,” etc. After the prayer he sprinkleth the Star with holy water, saying: “This Star is sanctified by the grace of the All-Holy Spirit, by the sprinkling of this holy water: in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, Amen.”

Finally he sanctifieth the Spoon, proclaiming: “Let us pray to the Lord,” and readeth the prayer: “O Lord God Almighty, with the coal taken with tongs by the Seraph,” etc. Then he sprinkleth the Spoon with holy water, saying: “This Spoon is sanctified by the grace

§. 206.

Order of the Blessing of New Priestly Vestments.[^317]

Before the Royal Doors upon the prepared Tetrapod are placed the new vestments brought for blessing. The Priest, vested in epitrachelion and phelonion, first censeth them, saying as usual: “Blessed is our God,” etc. After this: “Amen.” “Heavenly King,” etc. Trisagion. Our Father. §. 207.

Order of the Blessing of Church Banners.

The Priest, vested in epitrachelion and phelonion, having censed the banners, beginneth: “Blessed is our God,” etc., unto which the choir answereth: “Amen,” and saith: “Trisagion,” and “Our Father.” After the exclamation the Priest saith: “Let us pray to the Lord,” and in a loud voice reciteth the prayer: “O Eternal God without beginning.” And he sprinkleth the banners with holy water, saying: “These banners are sanctified by the grace of the All-Holy Spirit, by the sprinkling of this holy water: in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, Amen,” and the Dismissal.

§. 208.

Order of the Blessing of the Iconostasis.

An Iconostasis can be erected either in a blessed church or in an unblessed church.

<!-- LEAF: p300 -->

(p. 276)

a) If the Iconostasis is erected in a blessed church, then it is blessed in this manner: The Priest, vested in epitrachelion and phelonion, beginneth before the Royal Doors, saying: “Blessed is our God.” Heavenly King. Trisagion. Our Father. Come, let us worship (three times). And immediately the cantor readeth Psalm 131: (“Lord, remember David”), during which the Priest censeth, and after this, having said “Let us pray to the Lord,” in a loud voice reciteth the prayer: “O Lord our God, God of gods,” etc., after the prayer: “Peace be unto all,” “Bow your heads,” and the exclamation: “For Thou art the God of mercy.” After this he sprinkleth with holy water the whole Iconostasis from the bottom even unto the top, saying: “These Icons are sanctified by the sprinkling of this holy water in honor and glory of the great God and our Savior Jesus Christ, and in memory of His Saints: in the name of the Father, † and of the Son, † and of the Holy Spirit, † Amen.” — After this the cantor readeth the troparia: “Unto the Most Pure Image.” Glory: “Apostles, Prophets,” etc. And Now: “With love, O Pure Virgin,” etc. And thus he maketh the usual dismissal.

b) But if the Iconostasis is erected in an unblessed church, and it is to be blessed by a Priest, then first the church is blessed, and so before the dismissal the Iconostasis is blessed. That is: the Priest, having stood before the Royal Doors, after he hath first said: “Let us pray to the Lord,” readeth the prayer: “O Lord our God, God of gods,” etc., and after the prayer continueth as was said above, and only thus maketh the dismissal.

§. 209.

Order of the Blessing of Bells (Cymbals).

The bell that is to be blessed is suspended in a prepared place, and that in such a manner that it may be sprinkled both from without and from within. The Priest, having taken upon himself the epitrachelion and phelonion, beginneth: “Glory to the Holy,” etc. And immediately: “Trisagion,” and “Our Father.” After the exclamation the choir singeth the troparion: “Blessed art Thou, O Christ our God,” with the kontakion: “When He came down and confused the tongues.”

After this the Priest with his hand blesseth the bell three times, saying: “In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit,” etc., and in a loud voice proclaimeth the Litany: “In peace let us pray to the Lord.”

After the Litany the Priest giveth a name unto the bell, saying: “In the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, let this bell be named (N.), according to the grace given unto us from the Life-Creating Spirit, Amen,” and having taken the censer, he censeth the bell from four sides from without and from within. The choir unto this readeth Psalm 28.
