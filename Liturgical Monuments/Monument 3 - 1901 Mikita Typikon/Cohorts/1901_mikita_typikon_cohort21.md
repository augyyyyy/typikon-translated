(b) The Order of the Liturgy of the Catechumens.

Upon the conclusion of the Proskomedia, the priest, having censed the church, standeth before the holy Altar-Table with the deacons, and having bowed reverently and signed themselves with their right hand, they say secretly: “Blessed is our God.” After this, having joined hands at their breast, they say: “Heavenly King,” etc. And signing themselves again, they say: “Glory to God in the highest,” and that twice; after this: “O Lord, open Thou my lips,” once; and they kiss the holy Altar-Table and the Cross. The deacon, after this having received a blessing from the priest, goeth forth from the holy Sanctuary by the northern door, holding the Orarion with three fingers of his hand, and standing upon the ambo directly before the holy Royal Doors, and having bowed with reverence, beginneth with a loud voice: “Bless, Master.” And the priest, having taken

the holy Gospel Book into his hands, raiseth it crosswise, saying: “Blessed is the kingdom...”[^187] etc., unto which the choir answereth: “Amen.” After this the priest kisseth the Gospel Book and placeth it upon its customary place; the deacon then beginneth to chant the Litany of Peace aloud: “In peace let us pray to the Lord.” (If there is no deacon, the priest himself saith this litany.) Unto each petition the choir answereth: “Lord, have mercy.” It is to be noted here that in this litany, after the petition: “For them that travel by sea or by land...” etc., if the Liturgy is celebrated for a special intention, the appropriate prescribed litanies are added, which are found in the *Sluzhebnik* on page 642; if, however, for the departed, this one: “For the servant of God...” etc., or “For the souls of all.” (This litany for the departed, however, is not added on Sunday, nor on any feast day.) After this: “That we may be delivered...”; this Litany of Peace endeth with the exclamation: “For unto Thee is due...” etc., unto which the choir saith: “Amen.”[^188]

After the Litany of Peace the choir beginneth to sing the Psalms for the Antiphon, namely, when the 1st Antiphon (daily or festal) is sung, the priest saith the 1st prayer secretly: “O Lord our God...” etc. Upon the conclusion of this prayer he saith the Little Litany: “Again and again in peace let us pray to the Lord.”

When the choir singeth the second Antiphon, the priest saith the second prayer secretly: “O Lord our God, save Thy people...”; but when the choir beginneth to sing: “Glory to the Father... Only-begotten Son...” etc., the priest, having signed himself with the cross, with outstretched hands saith secretly likewise this hymn: “Glory to the Father... Only-begotten Son...” It is to be noted that at the word “to become incarnate” the priest boweth low. After this prayer the priest saith the Little Litany: “Again and again,” as before. — If there is a deacon, then standing before the Royal Doors during the 1st Antiphon he goeth unto the icon of the Savior; thence, when the choir beginneth to sing the 2nd Antiphon, he goeth unto the icon of the Theotokos; and at “Glory... After this, the troparia and kontakia are sung according to custom; the priest, standing before the holy Altar-Table, readeth the prayer of the Trisagion hymn, having his hands joined at his breast: “O holy God, Who restest in the saints...” etc. Having concluded this prayer, he exclaimeth: “For holy art Thou, our God, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, always, now and forever.” At the words “Now and forever” the priest turneth toward the people, blessing them, and the deacon concludeth the exclamation, saying: “And unto the ages of ages.” Unto this the choir, having sung “Amen,” beginneth to sing the “Trisagion.” The priest with the deacons say also themselves the “Trisagion,” making together three bows before the holy Altar-Table.
