then we take already the 14th day following after this month. For since this rule commandeth to celebrate Pascha after the vernal equinox, therefore the vernal full moon can be only between March 21 and April 18; and since the first Sunday after this full moon can be already on the following day, but can also be after 7 days, Pascha can occur at the earliest on March 22, and at the latest on April 25 (that is, between April 4 and May 8 according to the new calendar). From this it is evident that Pascha is celebrated within a span of 35 days, which days from March 22 unto April 25 inclusively are called the “paschal days.”

For calculating the day of Pascha and of all movable feasts and seasons, the so-called “Paschalion” serveth, which was composed on the basis of the following three calculations:

**1.** Calculation according to the moon. It is known that the lunar (monthly) year (12 lunar months of 28 days each) hath only 354 days and about 9 hours, and is consequently 11 days shorter than the solar year. These 11 days are called the “Epact” (*Epactae*). Since this difference is multiplied annually by 11 days, after 19 years a difference of 209 days will result. These 209 days make exactly 7 lunar months (7 × 28 = 209 days, 19 hours), after which the lunar year coincideth again with the solar year on one and the same day. Hence it is that every 19 years the phases of the moon fall upon the very same days of the solar year. This 19-year period is called the “Lunar Cycle.”[^340]

**2.** Calculation according to the sun. In the church order the 7 days of the week are designated by 7 letters (А. В. Г. Д. Є. Ѕ. З.), which letters are called *vrucilito*. Since a common year hath 365 days, that is, 52 weeks and 1 day, the days of the week advance by one day to the following day, and after 4 years, when a leap year hath 366 days, by 2 days. Hence it followeth that the order of the days of the week can repeat itself only after 28 years (4 × 7 = 28). This 28-year period is called the “Solar Cycle.”[^341]

**3.** Calculation according to the Indiction. Since the period of the moon containeth within itself 19 years, and the period of the sun 28 years, both these periods will coincide in one and the same order after 532 years (19 × 28 = 532). This 532-year period is called the “Indiction” or the “Great Cycle.”[^342]

<!-- LEAF: p312 -->

(p. 288)

Since the last 532-year period (Indiction) began in the year 1408, it will end in the year 1940 (1408 + 532 = 1940), and in the year 1941 a new 532-year period will begin.[^343]

Strictly speaking, the “Perpetual Paschalion”[^344] was composed on the basis of these three calculations. It presenteth a table which hath 28 rows (according to the number of the solar cycle) and 19 columns (according to the number of the lunar cycle); consequently, 532 cells are contained therein (28 × 19 = 532), in each of which is found one boundary letter. (See Table III.)

In order to find from this table the boundary letter for any given year, the following rule is used:

From that year for which we wish to find the boundary letter, we subtract 1408 (the beginning of the present Indiction). The remainder we divide by 19 (the lunar cycle). The quotient resulting from the division (*osztalék*) showeth in which row (*sor*) from top to bottom it is necessary to seek the letter; while the remainder from the division (*maradék*) showeth in which column (across) the sought-for boundary letter (*betü*) is found.[^345]

But if in the division nothing remaineth, then in the row that hath been found the last letter is the paschal letter.

Now if we have found the “paschal letter,” then we shall find also the day of Pascha. — We have already said[^346] that the “boundary letters” signify numbers; here let us only recall that if the number of the paschal letter is 10, or less than 10, 21 is added, and the day of Pascha in March will be obtained; but if that number is greater than 10, 10 is subtracted from it, and the day of Pascha in April is obtained. — For example: for the year 1874 the paschal letter is “и” = 10; 10 + 21 = 31 is added; on this account Pascha of that year fell on March 31. For the year 1872 the paschal letter was “ч” = 26; from this number is subtracted: 26 − 10 = 16; Pascha was on April 16.

The festal Sundays are the following: a) Sunday of the Holy Forefathers; b) Sunday of the Holy Fathers of the Old Testament; c) Sunday of the Holy God-Bearers; d) Sunday before the Theophany; e) Sunday after the Theophany; f) Sunday before the Exaltation of the Precious Cross; g) Sunday after the Exaltation of the Precious Cross; h) Sunday of the Holy Fathers of the Six Councils; i) Sunday of the Holy Fathers of the 7th Council.

**2.** The Polyeleos from September 22 unto December 20 is chanted on Sundays for this reason: because this period is a “period of preparation” for the feast of the Nativity of Jesus Christ; likewise also on the Sunday of the Publican and the Pharisee, of the Prodigal Son, Meatfare, and Cheesefare it is chanted for this reason: because these Sundays are “preparatory” (= pre-festal) unto the Great Fast.

**3.** In the first centuries of Christianity the faithful on Sunday did not bend their knees, but prayed standing in token of joy and

### Note on Dominical and Ferial Letters in the Menologion

Note: In our Prayer Books in the Menologion, for every single day these letters are found:

$$egin{array}{ccccccc}
	ext{А} & 	ext{В} & 	ext{Г} & 	ext{Д} & 	ext{Є} & 	ext{Ѕ} & 	ext{З} \
	ext{И} & 	ext{П} & 	ext{В} & 	ext{Р} & 	ext{Ч} & 	ext{Т} & 	ext{С}
\end{array}$$

Concerning these letters we appoint the following: In the first row stand the dominical letters, in the second row the ferial letters. The dominical letters everywhere, for every single day, are inscribed uniformly, while the ferial letters vary. These letters serve for this purpose: that if I know the dominical letter, I immediately say upon which day one or another feast will occur, if only I look at that ferial letter which standeth beneath the dominical letter. For example, if the dominical letter is А, then the day of the Indiction will occur on Sunday; but if the dominical letter is З, then on Saturday, since those letters stood beneath the dominical letters.
