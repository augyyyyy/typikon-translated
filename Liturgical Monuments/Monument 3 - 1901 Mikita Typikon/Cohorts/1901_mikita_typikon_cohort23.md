<!-- LEAF: p221 -->

Upon the conclusion of the litany, the priest, having taken the holy Gospel Book from the right side, maketh therewith the sign of the holy cross over the Iliton, saying the exclamation: “For Thou art our sanctification, and unto Thee we ascribe glory: to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages.” The choir singeth: “Amen.” The priest: “Let us go forth in peace.” The choir: “In the name of the Lord.” The deacon: “Let us pray to the Lord.” The choir: “Lord, have mercy.” After this the priest goeth forth through the Royal Doors, and standing in the midst of the church (below the ambo)[^214] saith the prayer (the Prayer Behind the Ambo): “O Lord, Who blessest them that bless Thee, and sanctifiest them that put their trust in Thee...”[^215] etc. Unto such prayer the choir singeth: “Amen.” After this: “Blessed be the name of the Lord from this time forth, and unto the ages” (thrice), and Psalm 33: “I will bless the Lord at all times.” During the singing of this psalm the priest distributeth unto the faithful the antidoron[^216] (blessed bread), in memory of that which the Christians of the first centuries

<!-- LEAF: p222 -->

left the holy bread unto the poor. After the distribution of the antidoron the priest entereth through the Royal Doors into the holy Sanctuary [Altar], and standing before the holy Altar-Table quietly saith the prayer: “Thou Who art Thyself the fulfillment of the Law and the Prophets, O Christ our God...” etc. After this: “Let us pray to the Lord.” The choir: “Lord, have mercy.” The priest: “The blessing of the Lord be upon you, through His grace and love for mankind, always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages.” The choir: “Amen.” The priest turneth unto the holy Altar-Table and saith: “Glory to Thee, O Christ God, our hope, glory to Thee!” The choir: “Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen. Lord, have mercy (thrice). Bless.” After this the priest turneth toward the people and pronounceth the dismissal: “May Christ our true God, through the prayers of His most pure Mother...” etc. After the dismissal the choir singeth: “Many years.” And the priest, having bowed thrice before the holy Altar-Table, saith the prayers of thanksgiving: “Glory to Thee, O God; glory to Thee, O God; glory to Thee, O God,” and the prayer of St. Simeon: “Now lettest Thou Thy servant depart in peace, O Master...” etc., the Trisagion through the Our Father, the Troparion of St. John Chrysostom: “The grace of thy mouth shining forth...” etc., the Kontakion: “From heaven hast thou received divine grace...” etc., “Lord, have mercy” (12 times), “More honorable than the Cherubim,” “Glory be to the Father... now and forever,” and maketh the small dismissal. After this he divesteth himself of the sacred vestments, saying: “Now lettest Thou Thy servant depart,” and kisseth the holy Altar-Table.

Note 1. The hymn: “Only-begotten Son”[^217] is customarily sung at the Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom and St. Basil the Great after the second litany. But if this Liturgy is served on a Saturday, and a Feast of the Mother of God should occur, then instead of “Only-begotten Son” the Dogmatikon of the Feast of the Mother of God is sung.

Note 2. If several priests concelebrate, then at the Great Entrance the serving priest saith: “The Most Holy Universal... our God-loving Bishop, and all the priestly order...”[^218] And all the concelebrants say: “May the Lord God remember thy priesthood in His kingdom, always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages.”[^219]

#### ARTICLE II. Concerning the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great.

§. 142. The Order of the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great without Vespers.

The Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great is celebrated 10 times in the year: 1. On the day of Saint Basil the Great (January 1); 2. On the first five Sundays of Great Lent; 3. On Great Thursday; 4. On Great Saturday; 5. On the Eve of the Nativity of Christ; 6. On the Eve of the Theophany of the Lord.

The order of this Liturgy is entirely the same as that of the Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom, with this distinction only: 1. that the priestly prayers in the Liturgy of Saint Basil are longer, on which account the singers sing the chants more slowly and at greater length, that the priest during that time may quietly read his prayers; 2. the priest at the words of consecration

<!-- LEAF: p223 -->

doth not say: “Take, eat,” but first saith: “He gave it unto His holy disciples and Apostles, saying: Take, eat: this is My Body...” Likewise also over the holy chalice he first saith: “He gave it unto His holy disciples and Apostles, saying: Drink of it, all of you: this is My Blood...”[^220] 3. Instead of “It is truly right” there is sung: “All of creation rejoiceth in thee, O full of grace, the assembly of Angels and the human race: sanctified temple and spiritual paradise, virgin boast, from whom God was incarnate and became a child, Who is our God before the ages; for He made thy womb a throne, and shaped thy body more spacious than the heavens. All of creation rejoiceth in thee, O full of grace, glory to thee!”[^221] 4. The Prayer Behind the Ambo is different: “O Master rich in mercy...”

§. 143. The Order of the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great with Vespers.

With Vespers the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great is celebrated: 1. On the Eve of the Nativity of Christ; 2. On the Eve of the Theophany; 3. On Great Thursday; 4. On Great Saturday. If the Eve of the Nativity of Christ or of the Theophany should fall on a Saturday or Sunday, then on the Eve the Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom is celebrated, and on the feast day itself that of Saint Basil the Great.

The order of this Liturgy with Vespers is as follows:

After the initial exclamation: “Blessed is the kingdom...” there is read: “Come, let us worship,” and the Introductory Psalm 103: “Bless the Lord, O my soul.” The deacon readeth the prayers of the Lucernarium. After the psalm there is the Litany of Peace. After the litany: “Lord, I have cried” on 8, at the end the Theotokion or Dogmatikon, during which the entrance is made with the censer (and if the Gospel Book is to be read, then with the Gospel Book). After the entrance: “O Joyful Light.” After this the deacon: “Let us attend!” The priest: “Peace be unto all.” The deacon: “Wisdom! Let us attend!” and the Prokimenon of the day. After the Prokimenon the Paremias are read (on the Eve of the Nativity 8, of the Theophany 13, on Great Thursday 3, on Great Saturday 15). After the Paremias

<!-- LEAF: p224 -->

there is the small litany, and the Trisagion (but on Great Thursday and Great Saturday, instead of the Trisagion: “All ye who have been baptized into Christ”). After the Trisagion the Prokimenon, Epistle, Alleluia, and Gospel. After the Gospel, the customary order of the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great.

Note. The Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom is likewise celebrated with Vespers on the day of the Annunciation,[^222] if it falleth upon any weekday of Great Lent, except Saturday and Sunday.

#### ARTICLE III. The Order of the Divine Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts.

The Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts is a divine service wherein the unbloody sacrifice is not accomplished, that is, there is no consecration of the gifts (bread and wine), but communion taketh place with the Gifts presanctified at a full Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom or Saint Basil the Great. A full Liturgy is not served[^223] on all days of Great Lent, except Saturdays and Sundays, and the Feast of the Annunciation; but that the faithful might not be deprived of Holy Communion during this holy time of repentance, it was enacted by the Synod of Laodicea[^224] and the Synod in Trullo to celebrate on Wednesdays and Fridays of Great Lent the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts.

The order of this Liturgy consisteth of 4 parts: (a) The Preparation of the Holy Lamb, (b) Vespers, (c) The Liturgy of the Catechumens, (d) The Liturgy of the Faithful.

§. 144. (a) The Preparation of the Holy Lamb.

The Holy Lamb[^225] for the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts is prepared at a full Liturgy on the preceding Sunday (or Saturday), when there is prepared not one Lamb alone, but two or three

<!-- LEAF: p225 -->

(according to the number of Presanctified Liturgies to be celebrated). At the Proskomedia over each Lamb the priest doth the same as over the first Lamb. At the consecration of the gifts, however, the priest pronounceth the words of consecration once only in the plural number: “Make these breads the precious Body of Thy Christ...” And at the fraction of the Lamb the priest taketh the first Lamb and inciseth it and placeth a particle into the chalice; the other Lambs he doth not divide, but having dipped the spoon into the Holy Blood, he anointeth them crosswise on the side of the incision with the Holy Blood of Christ and placeth them into the Tabernacle [Kyvot] upon the holy Altar-Table[^226] until the day of celebration of the Presanctified Liturgy.

§. 145. (b) Vespers, as a Part of the Holy Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts.

The Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts is celebrated on Wednesday and Friday of all weeks of Great Lent, on Thursday of the Great Canon (in the 5th week), on Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday of Passion Week, and if the parish feast or the day of the 40 Martyrs should fall on a weekday of the fast.[^227]

On the appointed day the priest vests in all the sacred vestments, of bright or dark color, praying only: “Let us pray to the Lord. Lord, have mercy” over each vestment, and kisseth the vestments.

Having opened the curtain (the Royal Doors, however, remaining closed), the priest maketh a bow before the holy Altar-Table, kisseth the Gospel Book and the Altar-Table, taketh the Gospel Book with both hands, and having signed the Altar-Table crosswise, exclaimeth: “Blessed is the kingdom of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages.” The choir: “Amen.”

The reader: “Come, let us worship” (thrice), and the Introductory Psalm 103: “Bless the Lord, O my soul.” The priest, having gone forth through the northern door, standing before the Royal Doors readeth the prayers of the Lucernarium (beginning from the 4th, because the first three prayers are read later at the litanies of the faithful). After the psalm the priest entereth into the holy Sanctuary [Altar], and exclaimeth the Great Litany: “In peace let us pray to the Lord.” After the litany the reader readeth the 18th Kathisma: “Unto the Lord in mine affliction have I cried...” divided into three Stases [Slavas]

<!-- LEAF: p226 -->

At the first Stasis [Slava] the priest taketh the Holy Lamb from the Tabernacle [Kyvot], placeth it upon the Diskos, and censeth the holy Altar-Table round about and the Holy Lamb.

At the second Stasis [Slava] the priest, having censed the Altar-Table, taketh the Diskos with the Holy Lamb and, having placed it upon his head, transferreth it through the northern door unto the Table of Preparation [Prothesis], a candle-bearer preceding him with a candle and censer. Having arrived at the Table of Preparation [Prothesis], he setteth down the holy Diskos, poureth wine and water into the chalice, censeth the Asterisk [Star] and the veils, covereth the Diskos and the chalice each with its veil separately, and thereafter both together with the Aer, praying: “Let us pray to the Lord” over each, and censeth the oblation, saying nothing.

At the third Stasis [Slava] the priest returneth unto the holy Altar-Table, foldeth the Iliton, and censeth the Altar-Table round about.

After the Kathisma there is the small litany. After the litany: “Lord, I have cried” is sung with stichera on 10. At the last sticheron the Royal Doors are opened and the entrance is made with the censer (and if the Gospel Book is to be read, then with the holy Gospel Book). The priest kisseth the holy Altar-Table, goeth forth through the northern door before the Royal Doors, and exclaimeth: “Wisdom! Stand aright!” The choir singeth: “O Joyful Light.”

After “O Joyful Light” the priest entereth into the holy Sanctuary [Altar], and standing before the holy Altar-Table saith: “Let us attend!” “Peace be unto all.” “Wisdom! Let us attend!” The reader exclaimeth the first Prokimenon of the day. The priest: “Wisdom!” The reader: the title of the first Paremia (from the Book of Genesis). The priest: “Let us attend!” The reader readeth the Paremia. The Royal Doors are closed.

After the first Paremia the Royal Doors are opened. The priest exclaimeth: “Let us attend! Wisdom!” The reader exclaimeth the second Prokimenon of the day. After the Prokimenon the reader saith: “Command!” And the priest, having taken with both hands the candlestick with the lighted candle and the censer, standing before the holy Altar-Table and turning toward the east, signeth crosswise and exclaimeth: “Wisdom! Stand aright!” After this, turning toward the west unto the people, he exclaimeth: “The light of Christ enlighteneth all!” And the people prostrate themselves unto the ground. The priest, having returned unto the holy Altar-Table, setteth down the candlestick and the censer. The Royal Doors are closed. The reader readeth the second Paremia (from the Book of Proverbs). After the Paremia the priest exclaimeth: “Peace be unto thee.” The reader: “And unto thy spirit.”

And immediately the Royal Doors are opened. The priest, having taken the censer and standing before the holy Altar-Table, singeth with a contrite voice:[^228]

<!-- LEAF: p227 -->

“Let my prayer arise like incense before Thee, the lifting up of my hands as an evening sacrifice.” The choir singeth the very same, and all the people pray kneeling. And the priest censeth the holy Altar-Table on the right side.

Then the priest singeth the 1st verse: “Lord, I have cried unto Thee, hear me; attend to the voice of my supplication, when I cry unto Thee.” The choir: “Let my prayer arise...” And the priest censeth the High Place.

Then the priest singeth the 2nd verse: “Set a guard, O Lord, upon my mouth, and a door of enclosure about my lips.” The choir: “Let my prayer arise...” The priest censeth the holy Altar-Table on the left side.

Then the priest singeth the 3rd verse: “Incline not my heart unto words of malice, to devise excuses for sins.” The choir: “Let my prayer arise...” The priest censeth the holy Altar-Table on the front side.

Then the priest singeth the 4th verse: “With men that work iniquity, and I will not join with their chosen ones.” The choir: “Let my prayer arise...” The priest departeth unto the Table of Preparation [Prothesis] and censeth the offered Holy Gifts.

Then the priest singeth: “The righteous man shall correct me in mercy and reprove me, but let not the oil of the sinner anoint my head.” The choir: “Let my prayer arise...”

Then the priest, having returned before the holy Altar-Table, singeth the first half of the verse: “Let my prayer arise like incense before Thee.” And having handed over the censer, he prostrateth himself unto the ground. And the choir finisheth singing: “The lifting up of my hands as an evening sacrifice.” The priest maketh 3 prostrations with the prayer of St. Ephrem: “O Lord and Master of my life...”[^229] The Royal Doors are closed.

<!-- LEAF: p228 -->

(If it is a feast day, then after “Let my prayer arise” the Epistle and Gospel of the feast are read.)

And hereby Vespers as a part of the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts is concluded.

§. 146. (c) The Liturgy of the Catechumens.

Thereafter the Royal Doors are opened, and the priest readeth the Litany of Fervent Supplication: “Let us all say with our whole soul and with our whole mind, let us say...” The choir singeth: “Lord, have mercy” (thrice each time). After the litany the priest exclaimeth: “For a merciful and man-befriending God art Thou, and unto Thee we ascribe glory: to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages.” The choir: “Amen.”

After this the deacon or priest exclaimeth: “Pray unto the Lord, ye catechumens.” The choir: “Lord, have mercy.” “Ye faithful, let us pray for the catechumens...” and the priest prayeth quietly the prayer for the catechumens. After the litany the exclamation: “That they also with us may glorify Thine all-honorable and majestic name...”

From Wednesday of the 4th week of Great Lent (Mid-Pentecost of the fast), after the Litany of Fervent Supplication there is added also the Litany for Those Preparing for Illumination (for those catechumens who prepare for Holy Baptism on Great Saturday, on which account they were called the illuminated—*illuminandi*): “Pray, ye that are preparing for illumination, unto the Lord...” And the priest quietly prayeth the prayer for those preparing for illumination: “Manifest, O Lord, Thy countenance...” After the litany the exclamation: “For Thou art our illumination, and unto Thee we ascribe glory: to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages.”

After this he exclaimeth: “All ye that are preparing for illumination, depart... All ye who are catechumens, depart; let none of the catechumens remain; as many as are of the faithful, again and again in peace let us pray to the Lord.”

And hereby the Liturgy of the Catechumens is concluded.

§. 147. (d) The Liturgy of the Faithful.

The Liturgy of the Faithful beginneth with two small litanies: 1. “Again and again in peace let us pray to the Lord”; the priest readeth the 1st Prayer of the Faithful: “O God, great and praiseworthy...” and exclaimeth: “For unto Thee is due all glory, honor, and worship...” The choir: “Amen.” 2. “Again and again...”; the priest readeth the 2nd Prayer of the Faithful: “O holy Master, exceedingly good...” and the exclamation: “According to the gift of Thy Christ...” The choir: “Amen.”

After these litanies the Royal Doors are opened, and the priest censeth the holy Altar-Table round about, reciting Psalm 50. The choir then beginneth to sing the magnificent hymn:

“Now the heavenly hosts minister invisibly with us: for behold, the King of Glory entereth; behold, the mystical sacrifice, fully accomplished, is ushered in with spears. In faith and love let us draw near, that we may become partakers of life everlasting. Alleluia, alleluia, alleluia.”

During this hymn the priest prayeth before the holy Altar-Table, saying thrice with bows: “Now the heavenly hosts...” After this, having kissed the Altar-Table, he departeth unto the Table of Preparation [Prothesis], a candle-bearer preceding him with a candle and censer. Having censed the gifts, the priest taketh the Aer and placeth it upon his left shoulder, taketh the Diskos into his right hand and the chalice into his left hand before his breast, and saying nothing goeth forth through the northern door amidst profound silence. The people and the singers fall prostrate upon the earth with fear and reverence, because the Holy Gifts are being transferred, already consecrated and transubstantiated into the true Body and true Blood of Christ the Savior.

The priest, having passed through the church, entereth through the Royal Doors into the holy Sanctuary [Altar], setteth the holy Diskos and the holy chalice upon the antimension (iliton), removeth the veils, and covereth them with the Aer, and censeth them thrice, saying nothing.

The choir then singeth the second half of the hymn: “In faith and love let us draw near...” Upon the conclusion of this singing, all arise from the earth; the priest maketh 3 prostrations with the prayer of St. Ephrem: “O Lord and Master of my life...” The Royal Doors are closed, and the curtain is drawn halfway.

<!-- LEAF: p230 -->

After this the priest exclaimeth the Litany of Supplication: “Let us complete our evening prayer unto the Lord.” The choir: “Lord, have mercy.” “For the precious Gifts set forth and presanctified...” And the priest prayeth quietly: “O God of ineffable and invisible mysteries...” After the litany the exclamation: “And account us worthy, O Master, with boldness...” The choir singeth: “Our Father.” The priest: “For Thine is the kingdom...” The choir: “Amen.” The priest: “Peace be unto all.” The choir: “And unto thy spirit.” The priest: “Bow your heads unto the Lord.” The choir: “To Thee, O Lord.” The priest quietly saith the prayer of the bowing of heads: “O God, Who alone art good and compassionate...” and the exclamation: “Through the grace and compassions...” The choir: “Amen.”

The priest prayeth quietly: “Attend, O Lord Jesus Christ our God...” After the prayer he boweth thrice, saying: “O God, be merciful unto me a sinner!” and having inserted his hand beneath the Aer, toucheth the Holy Bread with reverence. And immediately the curtain is drawn completely, and the priest elevateth the Holy Lamb, saying: “The Presanctified holy things for the holy!” The choir singeth: “One is holy, one is Lord, Jesus Christ, to the glory of God the Father. Amen.”

And it singeth the Communion Hymn of the day: “O taste and see that the Lord is good. Alleluia” (thrice).

The priest breaketh the Holy Lamb into 4 parts in the customary manner, and placeth the particle “IC” into the holy chalice, saying nothing. The server poureth in the zeon [warm water]. The priest receiveth communion of the particles of the Body and Blood of Christ, just as at the full Liturgy, but from the chalice he drinketh saying nothing.[^230]

After communion the curtain and the Royal Doors are opened, and the priest showeth the holy chalice unto the people, exclaiming: “With the fear of God and with faith draw near!” The choir: “I will bless the Lord at all times; His praise shall continually be in my mouth.” The priest blesseth the people, saying: “O God, save Thy people, and bless Thine inheritance.” The choir: “Taste of the heavenly bread and the cup of life, and see that the Lord is good. Alleluia” (thrice).

The priest carrieth the Holy Gifts unto the Table of Preparation [Prothesis]. Having returned unto the Altar-Table, he exclaimeth: “Always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages.” The choir: “May our mouths be filled with Thy praise...” After this the thanksgiving litany: “Stand aright! Having partaken...” The priest foldeth the antimension, taketh the holy Gospel Book, and maketh therewith a cross over the antimension, exclaiming: “For Thou art our sanctification...” The choir: “Amen.” The priest: “Let us go forth in peace.” The choir: “In the name of the Lord.” The deacon: “Let us pray to the Lord.” The choir: “Lord, have mercy.”

The priest goeth forth from the Sanctuary [Altar], and standing before the ambo readeth the Prayer Behind the Ambo of the Presanctified Liturgy: “O Master Almighty, Who hast fashioned all creation in wisdom...” After the prayer the choir singeth: “Amen,” and “Blessed be the name of the Lord from this time forth, and unto the ages” (thrice); and the priest, having returned into the holy Sanctuary [Altar], readeth the prayer at the consumption of the Holy Gifts: “O Lord our God, Who hast led us into these all-honorable days...” He consumeth the Holy Gifts, washeth his hands, divesteth himself of the vestments, readeth the prayers of thanksgiving, and maketh the dismissal.
