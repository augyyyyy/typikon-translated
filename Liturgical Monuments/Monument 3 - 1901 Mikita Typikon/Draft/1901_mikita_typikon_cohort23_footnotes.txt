[^214]: The Prayer Behind the Ambo signifies: a prayer read behind the ambo, because in ancient times the ambo stood in the midst of the church, and the priest read this prayer behind the ambo.
[^215]: The prayer: “O Lord, Who blessest them that bless Thee...” ought to be read with great contrition of heart and not rapidly.
[^216]: Antidoron (ἀντίδωρον) signifies a reciprocal gift. This bread was distributed among the ancient Christians after the common banquet (agape = feast of love) unto the poor faithful. For in ancient times those gifts which remained from the loaves of oblation at the holy Proskomedia were distributed unto the faithful *in memoriam agaparum* [in memory of the love-feasts], so that even those who did not receive communion might be partakers of the blessing.
[^217]: The hymn: “Only-begotten Son” was composed by Emperor Justinian in the 6th century (about the year 528).
[^218]: If several priests concelebrate, then the serving priest at the words: “and all the priestly order” turns toward the concelebrating priests and bows unto them.
[^219]: For these words pertain unto all priests, both the superior and the concelebrants.
[^220]: The words of consecration in the Liturgy of St. Basil the Great are these: “He gave it unto His holy disciples and Apostles, saying: Take, eat: this is My Body, which is broken for you for the remission of sins.” The choir: “Amen.” “He gave it unto His holy disciples and Apostles, saying: Drink of it, all of you: this is My Blood of the New Testament, which is shed for you and for many for the remission of sins.” The choir: “Amen.”
[^221]: On the day of Great Thursday, because at this Liturgy the institution of the Most Holy Eucharist is celebrated, there is sung: “Of Thy mystical supper, O Son of God, receive me today as a communicant; for I will not speak of the mystery unto Thine enemies, nor give Thee a kiss like Judas, but like the thief do I confess Thee: Remember me, O Lord, in Thy kingdom.”
[^222]: When the Feast of the Annunciation falls upon Great Thursday or Great Saturday, the Liturgy of St. Basil the Great is celebrated with Vespers.
[^223]: A full Liturgy is not served on weekdays of Great Lent, because the full Liturgy is a solemn and joyful commemoration of the entire mystery of human salvation, while the days of Great Lent are days of weeping, contrition, and repentance.
[^224]: The Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts is attributed to St. Gregory the Dialogist (Pope of Rome), but the order of this Liturgy already existed earlier, and St. Gregory only gathered and arranged it. The Synod of Laodicea (in the 4th century) by Canon 49 decreed: “It is not fitting during the Forty Days to offer bread, except on Saturday and Sunday alone”; and the Synod in Trullo (in the 7th century) by Canon 52 determined: “On all days of the fast of the holy Forty Days, except Saturday and Sunday and the holy day of the Annunciation, let no other Liturgy take place but that of the Presanctified.”
[^225]: As many Lambs are prepared as there are Presanctified Liturgies to be celebrated during the week.
[^226]: Some priests, before the immersion into the holy chalice of the particle from the first Lamb, dip the particles of the other Lambs into the Holy Blood, and then place them upon the Diskos, and set them into the Tabernacle [Kyvot].
[^227]: On Wednesday and Friday of the first week of Great Lent the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts is not celebrated according to the custom of our eparchy, but Vespers alone with prostrations; on other days, however, it is celebrated.
[^228]: On weekdays of Great Lent the dismissal of Vespers is as follows: “May Christ our true God, through the prayers of His most pure Mother, of the holy, glorious, and all-praised Apostles, of our holy Father Nicholas, Archbishop of Myra in Lycia, the Wonderworker (and of the saint whose temple it is, and whose day it is), of the holy and righteous Ancestors of God Joachim and Anna, and of all the saints, have mercy on us and save us, for He is good and loves mankind.”
[^229]: In Galicia these verses are sung thus: 1. The choir sings: “Let my prayer arise like incense before Thee, the lifting up of my hands as an evening sacrifice.” 2. The reader in the midst of the church (or the deacon upon the ambo): “Lord, I have cried unto Thee, hear me; attend to the voice of my supplication, when I cry unto Thee.” The choir: “Let my prayer arise...” 3. The reader: “Set a guard, O Lord, upon my mouth, and a door of enclosure about my lips.” The choir: “Let my prayer arise...” 4. The reader: “Incline not my heart unto words of malice, to devise excuses for sins.” The choir: “Let my prayer arise...” 5. The reader: “Let my prayer arise.” The choir finishes singing: “Like incense before Thee, the lifting up of my hands as an evening sacrifice.” During this singing the priest, standing before the holy Altar-Table, censes it, and thereafter the Table of Preparation [Prothesis], while the people and the singers kneel; after this the priest makes 3 prostrations with the prayer of St. Ephrem.
[^230]: If at the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts there should be people who wish to receive communion of the Holy Mysteries, the priest then puts particles of the Body of Christ into the holy chalice, and imparts communion unto them with the holy spoon in the customary manner. (See the Sluzhebnik [Liturgikon] at the Presanctified Liturgy.)
