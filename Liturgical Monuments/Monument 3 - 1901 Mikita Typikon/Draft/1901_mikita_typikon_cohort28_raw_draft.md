V. The Consecration of a Bishop.[^287]

It is celebrated in the episcopal cathedral. In the church there is prepared a faldstool before the Royal Doors with a seat, and in the church eagle-rugs [orletzy][^288] are spread out. On this day they ring all the bells, and the choir sings spiritual hymns.

When the Hierarch (Archbishop) comes into the church, and is vested in all the hierarchal vestments, then two Bishops-consecrators take the one newly elected unto a Bishop, vested in priestly vestments without the phelonion, and lead him before the Royal Doors upon the first eagle-rug.

§. 182.

The Order of the Holy Mystery of Matrimony.[^289]

I. The Order of the Blessing of the Matrimony of the First-Married.

Before the priest proceeds unto the blessing of the marriage, he must assure himself concerning the free status of the bridegroom and the bride, concerning their mutual consent, concerning the completion of the lawful banns, and whether there be any canonical impediment (*impedimentum*) unto the marriage. Likewise the bride and bridegroom must approach unto the Holy Mystery of Repentance and Holy Communion.

On the appointed day and hour the bridegroom and bride, accompanied by witnesses (groomsmen and bridesmaids) and parents, come into the church, and take their stand before the tetrapod: the bridegroom on the right hand, and the bride on the left hand. Upon the tetrapod are laid the holy cross, the holy Gospel-Book, two crowns, and two candles.

The priest, vested in epitrachelion and phelonion, comes out before the Royal Doors or unto the tetrapod, and questions the bridegroom: “Hast thou a good and unconstrained desire, and a firm purpose, to take unto thyself as wife this woman (*Name*)?” The bridegroom answers: “I have, reverend Father.” Further he questions: “Hast thou not promised thyself unto another bride?” He answers: “I have not promised myself, reverend Father.” In like manner he questions the bride also.

[Book Page 252]

(p. 252)

After this the priest gives into their hands lighted candles, and begins the betrothal, saying: “Blessed is our God...” Cantor: “Amen.” There follows the Great Litany with special petitions for those being betrothed: “That this betrothal may be blessed...” After the litany the priest reads two prayers, blesses the rings, and lays them upon the Altar-Table or upon the tetrapod. After this he takes the ring of the bridegroom, makes with it a cross over the head of the bridegroom, saying three times: “The servant of God (*Name*) is betrothed unto the handmaid of God (*Name*), in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.” And he places the ring upon a finger of his right hand. In like manner he makes also for the bride. After this the groomsmen exchange the rings three times. The priest reads the prayer: “O Lord our God, Who didst accompany the servant of the Patriarch Abraham...” And there follows the Augmented Litany and the dismissal of the betrothal.

After this follows the crowning itself.

The priest goes before them with the censer unto the analoj [tetrapod], while the cantors sing Psalm 127 LXX [Psalm 128 MT]: “Blessed are all they that fear the Lord...” with the refrain: “Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee.”

Having arrived at the tetrapod, the bridegroom and bride lay their right hands upon the holy Gospel-Book, while the priest binds their hands with the epitrachelion, blesses them with the cross, and they pronounce the marital oath of mutual fidelity, love, and the indissolubility of the union even unto death.

After this the priest begins: “Blessed is the Kingdom...” The Great Litany. Three long prayers. After the prayers the priest takes the crown of the bridegroom, signs the bridegroom cruciformly with it, and crowns him, saying three times: “The servant of God (*Name*) is crowned unto the handmaid of God (*Name*), in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.” In like manner he crowns the bride also. After this he blesses both three times, saying: “O Lord our God, crown them with glory and honor!”

There follows the Prokimenon, the Epistle (Eph. § 230), the Gospel (John § 6), the Augmented Litany and the Litany of Supplication. “Our Father.”

II. The Order of the Blessing of the Matrimony of the Second-Married.

If both (the bridegroom and bride) are second-married (widowed), it is celebrated according to a special order having a penitential character. The betrothal is not celebrated separately, but together with the crowning.

The priest vests only in epitrachelion and phelonion. Beginning: “Blessed is our God...” Trisagion. “O Most Holy Trinity...” “Our Father...” Troparion of the day. There follows the litany: “In peace let us pray unto the Lord,” with the addition of petitions for the remission of sins and cleansing of the iniquities of the bridegroom and bride.

After the litany the priest reads two prayers with penitential meaning: “O Eternal God, Who hast gathered together the dispersed into union...” and the second prayer: “O Lord our God, Who desirest that all men be saved...”

After this he places the rings without special prayers, but directly with the words: “The servant of God is betrothed...”

§. 183.

The Order of the Holy Mystery of Holy Anointing [Eleosvyashcheniye].

This holy Mystery is celebrated over the severely ill by a synod of seven priests (whence also the name “Soborovanie”), but in case of need it can be celebrated also by a smaller number of priests, even by a single priest.

In the house of the sick person there is prepared a table covered with a white cloth. Upon the table is set a vessel with wheat (or rye), and upon the wheat is placed a lamp (or cup) with oil mixed with wine. Around the vessel are set 7 small wands wrapped with cotton [struptsy] for the anointing, and 7 lighted candles.

The priests vest in epitrachelia and phelonia (if, however, there be one priest, then in epitrachelion and phelonion).

Penitential troparia: “Have mercy on us, O Lord, have mercy on us...” Psalm 50 LXX [Psalm 51 MT]: “Have mercy upon me, O God...” The Canon of Holy Anointing, the composition of Patriarch Arsenius, in Tone 4: “O bountiful and merciful Lord...” After the 9th Ode, the stichera and the troparion: “O Christ, Who alone art swift to help...”

There follows the Great Litany of Holy Anointing: “In peace let us pray unto the Lord... That this oil may be blessed...” After the litany the presiding priest reads the prayer over the oil: “O Lord, Who by Thy mercy and bounties healest the contrite of our souls and bodies, do Thou Thyself bless this oil also...”

[Forma Sacramenti] heal Thy servant (*Name*) from the bodily and spiritual infirmity which holds him...”[^293]
