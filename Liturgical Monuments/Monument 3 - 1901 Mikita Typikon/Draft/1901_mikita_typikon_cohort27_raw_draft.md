§. 178.

The Order of the Holy Mystery of Chrismation.

If a child of the Greek Rite was baptized by a priest of the Latin Rite, then the priest, vested in epitrachelion and phelonion, comes unto the tetrapod, upon which are laid the cross, the Gospel-Book, and burning candles, and administers Holy Chrismation unto him, saying thus: “Blessed is the Kingdom,” etc., whereunto the cantor speaks the sticheron in Tone 6: “O Heavenly King,” and so follows the Litany of Chrismation: “In peace let us pray unto the Lord.” After the Litany the priest loudly proclaims: “Let us pray unto the Lord,” and reads with the greatest attentiveness loudly the prayer: “Blessed art Thou, O Lord God Almighty,” etc. After the prayer, having dipped his finger into the holy Myron,[^280] he cruciformly anoints the newly-baptized one upon the forehead, upon the eyes, upon the nostrils, upon the lips, upon the ears, upon the breast, upon the hands, and upon the feet, together with the anointing saying:[^281] “The seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit. §. 179.

The Order of the Holy Mystery of the Eucharist.

The priest administers this Mystery either unto the healthy, or unto the sick.

I. If the administration of the Holy Eucharist is unto healthy people, it is celebrated in the Holy Liturgy, and that in the following manner. After “With the fear of God,” the priest places the spoon into the holy Chalice, and covers it with the communion-cloth [lention], and after this takes the Chalice into his left hand, and between two fingers of the left hand takes the diskos, and having turned toward the people signs himself with the holy cross and loudly speaks the prayer: “I believe, O Lord, and I confess, that Thou art truly the Christ, the Son of the living God, Who didst come into the world to save sinners, of whom I am the first.

Of Thy Mystical Supper today, O Son of God, receive me as a communicant: for I will not speak of the Mystery unto Thine enemies, neither will I give Thee a kiss as did Judas, but as the thief confessing Thee I cry unto Thee:

Remember me, O Lord, when Thou comest into Thy Kingdom.

Remember me, O Master, when Thou comest into Thy Kingdom.

Remember me, O Holy One, when Thou comest into Thy Kingdom.

Let not the communion of Thy holy Mysteries be unto me for judgment or for condemnation, O Lord, but for the healing of soul and body.

For I believe and confess that that which I receive is the true, most pure Body of Thine and the very most pure and life-creating Blood of Thine, which I pray make me worthy to receive worthily unto the remission of all my sins and unto life everlasting. Amen.”

After the prayer, for the communicating of the laity, the priest descends unto the ambo, and administers Holy Communion in such a manner that, holding the holy Chalice in the left hand, and between two fingers, between the second and middle fingers, the diskos, which he places under the mouth of the communicant, and with his right hand from the Chalice with the spoon he takes the Body and Blood of the Savior, and placing it into the mouth of the communicant, say-

Amen.” After this, wiping the mouth of the communicant with the communion-cloth [lention], he saith: “Lo, this hath touched thy lips, and shall take away thine iniquities, and shall purge all thy sins.”[^282] And when in this manner all the faithful have become partakers of Holy Communion, the priest continues the Holy Liturgy, saying: “O God, save Thy people,” and the rest.

II. If, however, the Holy Eucharist is administered unto the sick, this takes place thus. — The priest, especially the pastor, — since he must particularly care also concerning this, that not one of his faithful at the departure from his life should be deprived of Divine Communion of the Holy Mysteries, — first must keep and carefully preserve the Holy Mysteries in the church upon the holy table in the tabernacle [ciborium]; furthermore, secondly, he must admonish his faithful also unto this, that if sickness should fall upon any of them, they should make this known unto him as quickly as possible. — If, then, it is necessary for the priest to go unto the sick person, then he comes into the church and there takes upon himself the epitrachelion, then having devoutly taken forth from the tabernacle a particle of the Holy Eucharist, places it into a pyx specially prepared for this, which he carefully shuts, and after this places the pyx into a burse [pira] made of silk cloth, in which in a separate compartment he puts an eileton, a spoon, and a communion-cloth [lention]. Having then carefully tied this burse, he hangs it upon his neck, so that the Divine Mysteries lie upon his breast. §. 180.

The Order of the Holy Mystery of Repentance.

If a priest is called unto the hearing of holy confession, let him show himself ready for this work without any murmuring. For the hearing of confessions, however, if he hath time for this, let him prepare himself by fervent prayer and the stirring up of good intention. In the church, and not in private houses, the priest is to receive confessions, except for a necessary cause. When he receives holy confession, let him be vested in the epitrachelion, and sitting and seeing that the sinner draws near unto him, let him speak the prayer: “O Master, Lord our God, receive this Thy servant who repents of his sins, and give unto him contrition of heart, and direct his conscience, that with good hope he may confess all his transgressions before the glory of Thy dread throne and my unworthiness, and that there may be glorified in him thereafter Thy most honorable and magnificent name, of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.” After this, when the approaching sinner bows his knees and makes upon himself the sign of the cross, the priest likewise signs him with the sign of the cross, having laid upon his head the epitrachelion, saying unto him: “May our Lord Jesus Christ be in thy heart and upon thy lips, that thou mayest worthily confess all thy transgressions: in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.”

And immediately the sinner himself or with the priest speaks the prayer: “I confess unto the Lord God...” etc. — After this the priest inquires of the penitent: “When didst thou confess the last time? Hast thou concealed in thy last confession any sin? Hast thou performed the works of penance [pokuta] appointed in thy former confession? What sins dost thou remember since thy last confession?”

[Book Page 242]

(p. 242)

Unto these and similar questions the sinner must answer sincerely. Especially the priest must inquire of the sinner concerning his dominant sin (*peccatum dominans* [*dominant sin*]), or that which most of all gnaws his conscience. After a sincere, full, and accurate confession and acknowledgment of sins, the priest once more inquires of the sinner: “Dost thou not yet remember any other sin?” and after this saith unto him: “Dost thou repent for the sins committed by thee?” whereunto in answer the sinner speaks the prayer: “For these and all my sins, conscious and unconscious...” etc.

After such an expression of sorrow the priest inquires of the sinner: “Hast thou hope in the mercy of God that He will forgive thee thy sins?” And again: “Hast thou a good will to amend and not to sin?” and after this follows the spiritual instruction of the priest as teacher, judge, and physician, as Pastoral Theology teaches.[^284] Finally the priest imposes upon the sinner a penance [pokuta] corresponding unto his sins, looking upon the station, life, strength, etc., of the sinner.

Absolution is administered with these words: “Let us pray unto the Lord.” (“Lord, have mercy.”) “May our Lord and God Jesus Christ, through the grace and bounties of His love for mankind, forgive thee, child [Name], all thy transgressions; and I, an unworthy priest [Name], by His power given unto me, forgive and absolve thee from all thy sins. In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.” And dismissing the sinner he saith: “Go in peace, and sin no more.”[^285]
