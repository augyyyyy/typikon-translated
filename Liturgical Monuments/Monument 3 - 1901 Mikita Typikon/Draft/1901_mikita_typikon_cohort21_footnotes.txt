[^182]: That one may not err as to which side is right and which is left, according to ecclesiastical custom it is necessary to picture Christ seated upon the throne with His face turned toward the people.
[^183]: According to ancient custom, 5 prosphoras were used for each Liturgy, that is to say: from the first prosphora they cut out the “Lamb,” from the second the particle in honor of the Theotokos, from the third the particle in honor of the Saints, from the fourth in remembrance of the living, and from the fifth in remembrance of the departed. Now only one prosphora is used, from which are cut out the “Lamb” and the other particles, and the remainder is distributed as “Antidoron.”

[^186]: In the Proskomedia there is symbolically represented the quiet and secret Incarnation and Nativity of Jesus Christ in this world for all men, Who is placed here upon the Diskos in the form of the Lamb; this Diskos representeth the manger in which the Child Jesus was laid, and the Diskos resteth upon the Table of Preparation, which here figureth the cave of Bethlehem where Christ was born. After His Nativity the star appeared unto the Magi in the East, and showing them the way, stood over the cave in which Jesus was born; therefore the priest taketh the Asterisk and setteth it over the Diskos, saying: “And the star came and stood over where the young Child was” (Matt. 2:9), and covereth the Diskos and Chalice with veils, figuring those swaddling cloths with which Jesus Christ was wrapped. — Finally, the priest boweth down before the Child and with the Magi offereth gifts unto Him: for gold, the Chalice; for myrrh, the censer; and for frankincense, the incense with which he censeth the prepared gifts.
[^187]: Vespers begins with “Blessed is our God,” etc., Matins with “Glory to the holy, consubstantial, and life-creating Trinity,” etc., and the Liturgy with “Blessed is the kingdom of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit,” etc.; and that because Vespers and Matins symbolically figure the Old Testament, at the beginning of which the mystery of the Holy Trinity was not revealed, while toward the end of this Old Testament the people already knew something concerning It, but not clearly; clearly this mystery is revealed only in the New Testament, which the Liturgy figures.
