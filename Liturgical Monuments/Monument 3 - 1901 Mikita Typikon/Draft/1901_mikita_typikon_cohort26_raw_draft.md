## SECTION III.

§. 176.

Concerning Celebrations Following the Divine Liturgy in General.

Unto these celebrations belong: (α) The Rites of the Holy Mysteries, and (β) The Rites of Ecclesiastical Sanctifications and Blessings.

The Holy Mysteries of Christ, by means whereof, as through conduits, the power issuing from the sufferings of the Savior, that is to say, the grace merited upon the altar of the Cross, is to pass over unto us, are seven in number.

Ecclesiastical sanctifications and blessings, however, through which a sinner is either prepared for justification, or if he is already justified through the grace of God, this grace is increased in that person, in regard of number are not limited.

Nevertheless, between the Holy Mysteries and ecclesiastical sanctifications and blessings there is such a distinction, that: (a) The Holy Mysteries are instituted by Jesus Christ (*institutionis divinae* [*of divine institution*]), and are visible signs of invisible grace, which they have annexed unto them (*habent adnexam gratiam* [*they have grace annexed*]); whereas ecclesiastical blessings and sanctifications are instituted by the Church (*institutionis ecclesiasticae* [*of ecclesiastical institution*]), and although they too are visible signs, nevertheless they do not have grace annexed unto themselves (*non habent adnexam gratiam* [*they do not have grace annexed*]), but only prepare for it or increase it in men. — (b) Through the Holy Mysteries we receive the grace of God unfailingly “by the power of the completed action” (*ex opere operato* [*by the work worked*]), whereas through ecclesiastical sanctifications and blessings “by way of intercession” (*ex opere operantis* [*by the work of the worker*]), in consideration of the prayers and merits of the Church and of the interior preparation of our soul.

The rite of Holy Baptism is divided into 3 parts: (α) Actions performed before approaching the water of Holy Baptism; (β) Actions performed at the water of Holy Baptism; (γ) Actions performed after Holy Baptism.[^276]

The first part of the rite of Holy Baptism corresponds unto the ancient catechumenate, and in brief is an aggregate of those actions of Holy Bap-

That is to say: the priest comes into the narthex vested in epitrachelion and phelonion, holding in his hands the holy Cross, where those who brought the child already await him. Here the priest first with great diligence inquires concerning the parents and concerning their faith; after this: “Who are the sponsors that receive the child from the holy font?”[^277] After this, bestowing a name upon the child, he signs his head with the sign of the holy Cross 3 times, saying: “In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit,” etc.; and having laid his right hand upon the head of the child, he recites the prayer: “In Thy name, O Lord...” etc. After this prayer follow the 3 exorcisms, — and immediately the 2nd prayer: “O Master, Lord, Who hast created man...” in which, when the priest speaks the words: “Drive out from him (or: from her) every evil and unclean spirit,” etc., he blows upon the forehead, the mouth, and the breast of the child (these words the priest speaks 3 times, and likewise blows 3 times); upon the conclusion of this, the prayer is concluded with the words: “...now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.” — After this prayer the priest turns the one to be baptized with his face toward the west, and commands the sponsors to do likewise, of whom he inquires 3 times in the present tense: “Dost thou renounce Satan, and all his works, and all his angels, and all his service, and all his pride?” etc., whereunto the catechumen or the sponsors answer: “I renounce him.” — After this the priest again inquires of them in the past tense: “Hast thou renounced Satan?” etc., and they answer: “I have renounced him.” And immediately the priest bids them to blow and spit upon the ground (that is, upon Satan); and when they have done this, again at the command of the priest they turn toward the east. — After this the priest inquires of them: “Dost thou unite thyself unto Christ?” whereunto they answer 3 times: “I do unite myself.” — This he inquires of them also in the past tense, saying: “Hast thou united thyself unto Christ?” whereunto they answer: “I have united myself.” Finally the priest inquires of them: “Dost thou believe in Christ?” and they answer: “I believe”; and entering into the nave, they recite the Creed. During this the priest, having laid the epitrachelion upon the one to be baptized, saith: “He shall enter into Thy house, he shall worship toward Thy holy temple,” etc., — and immediately — when he hath already entered into the nave — he saith: “Blessed is our God, Who desires that all men should be saved,” etc. And herewith the 1st Part is concluded.
