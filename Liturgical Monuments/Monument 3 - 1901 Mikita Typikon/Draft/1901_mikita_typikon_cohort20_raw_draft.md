§. 130.

When Is the Polyeleos Sung?

The Polyeleos is sung: (a) On all feasts of the Lord, of the Theotokos, and of Great (Designated) Saints throughout the entire year, on whatever day they fall, unto which is also appended the refrain of the feast (the Magnification) [*Velichaniye*] with the chosen verses from the Psalms. Likewise the Polyeleos is sung: (b) At Sunday Matins, beginning from the Sunday after the Exaltation of the Precious Cross unto December 20. But from December 20 unto January 14 (unto the Apodosis of the Feast of Theophany), it is not sung, except for a Great Saint having a Polyeleos. After the Apodosis of the Feast of Theophany (from January 14), the Polyeleos is sung on all Sundays, even unto Cheesefare Sunday. It is also necessary to note this further: that on the Sunday of the Prodigal Son, Meatfare Sunday, and Cheesefare Sunday, to the Polyeleos (that is, unto the two Psalms 134 and 135 [Pss. 134 and 135], of the 19th Kathisma), we also chant in addition the 136th, that is, the third Psalm: “By the rivers of Babylon” (*На рѣкахъ Вавvлонскихъ*) [Ps. 136] with the “beautiful Alleluia.” On other Sundays, however, the Polyeleos is not sung, but the 17th Kathisma is read, except only for Feasts of the Lord, of the Theotokos, and of Great Saints, if they fall on a Sunday.

§. 131.

On Which Sundays Is the Resurrection Gospel Not Read, and When Is “Having Beheld the Resurrection of Christ” Omitted?

The Resurrection Gospel is not read on Sundays on which a feast of the Lord or of the Theotokos occurs, for then the festal Gospel is taken.[^175] Likewise “Having Beheld the Resurrection of Christ” is omitted on feasts of the Lord, but on feasts of the Theotokos it is taken. Furthermore, the Resurrection Gospel is not read on Palm Sunday, when also “Having Beheld the Resurrection of Christ” is not said; for “Having Beheld the Resurrection of Christ” is said on Lazarus Saturday. From Pascha Sunday unto the Ascension, “Having Beheld the Resurrection of Christ” occurs also at daily Matins. At the Ascension, the 3rd Resurrection Gospel is read at Matins and “Having Beheld the Resurrection of Christ”

## SECTION II.

§. 137.

Concerning Liturgical Celebrations.

Liturgy (from the Greek word *leitón* [λειτόν] = public, common, and *érgon* [ἔργον] = work) in the broad sense (*sensu lato*) [*in the broad sense*] comprehends within itself all divine services, but in the strict sense (*sensu stricto*) [*in the strict sense*] by it is understood solely the unbloody sacrifice of the New Testament. And although the unbloody sacrifice of the New Testament in essence (*in essentia*) [*in essence*] is only one, nevertheless the manner of offering this sacrifice (*modus vel forma offerendi*) [*the manner or form of offering*] both in the East and in the West is not one, and that because both in the East and in the West many Liturgies exist. Among us the Liturgy of the Church of Constantinople [Tsarhorod] is used, and it is threefold, that is to say: (a) The Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom, (b) the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great, and (c) the Liturgy of Saint Gregory the Dialogist, or the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts.

=== LEAF p198 ===
[Book Page 174]

Of these Liturgies, the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts is celebrated in the Great Fast on Wednesday and Friday; and in Passion Week on Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday.

The Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great is sung: (a) Without Vespers on the Feast of Saint Basil the Great, January 1, thereafter on the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, and 5th Sunday of the Holy Great Fast. (b) Together with Vespers on the Paramony [Eve] of the Nativity of Christ and of Theophany, if the Paramony be on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, or Friday; if, however, the Paramony of both of these feasts fall on Saturday or Sunday, then on the Paramony the Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom is celebrated, and on the very feast day of the Nativity or of Theophany the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great without Vespers. Likewise on Holy and Great Thursday and on Great Saturday, the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great is sung together with Vespers.

The Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom is sung throughout the entire year, except on the days on which it is commanded to sing the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great or of the Presanctified Gifts.

The Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom is not prescribed: (a) In the Great Fast, except on Saturdays and Sundays.[^179] (b) It is forbidden to celebrate the Liturgy on Holy and Great Friday.[^180]

It is necessary also to note this further: that the Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom is celebrated together with Vespers on the Feast of the Annunciation, if this feast fall on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, or Friday of the holy Forty Days.

We shall speak of these Liturgies here: (a) In general, (b) according to various occasions.

§. 139.

(a) Concerning the Proskomedia.

The Proskomedia (from the Greek word *proskomizō* [προσκομίζω] = I bring forward, I offer) is the preparatory part of the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great and of the Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom. — The order of the Proskomedia is as follows:

The priest, wishing to celebrate the Liturgy, upon entering the temple, recites the prayer: “I will enter into Thy house, I will worship toward Thy holy temple in Thy fear. Guide me, O Lord, in Thy righteousness; because of mine enemies, make straight my way before Thee” [Ps. 5:8–9]. After this, standing upon the ambo before the Holy Doors, he makes three bows and forms the intention before celebrating thus: “I, [N.], an unworthy priest, intend to celebrate the Liturgy and to perform the Mystery of the Body and Blood of our Lord Jesus Christ according to the order of the Holy Eastern Church, unto the glory of Almighty God, and of all the Church Triumphant, and for the benefit of myself and of all the Church Militant, for all who have commended themselves unto my prayers in common and in particular (for the servant of God, [N.], or for the servants of God, [NN.]), and for the welfare of the holy Catholic Church. Moreover, for all the living and the departed, concerning whom Thou, my God, knowest me to be bound to pray and to celebrate the Liturgy unto Thee. May the Almighty and merciful Lord grant unto us joy and peace, amendment of life, time for true repentance, the grace and consolation of the Holy Spirit, and perseverance in good works. Amen.”

Upon the completion of this prayer, the priest, standing there, begins the usual prayers before the Proskomedia, saying: “Blessed is our God,” etc. “Amen.” “Heavenly King,” “Trisagion,” “Our Father.” After this the troparia: “Have mercy on us, O Lord,” etc. “Glory” (*Сла́ва*): “Lord, have mercy on us,” etc. “Now and forever”: “Open unto us the doors of thy loving-kindness,” etc.
