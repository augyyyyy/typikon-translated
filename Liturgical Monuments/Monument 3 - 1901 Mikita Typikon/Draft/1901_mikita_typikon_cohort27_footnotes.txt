[^279]: If an infant on account of necessity was baptized by a lay person: then the Priest, if he findeth any doubt concerning the form or concerning the matter at the baptism, must repeat the form conditionally, and perform all the rites; — but when there is no doubt concerning the matter and concerning the form: then he performeth only the rites themselves from the beginning unto the end. — On the contrary, however, Bishop Papp-Szilágyi of blessed memory stateth that the exorcisms are omitted and only the concluding prayers after baptism are performed: *“Dum in necessitate collatus est Baptismus absque exorcismis, si infans mortuus non est, exorcismi suppleri non debent, sed solum, quae post Baptismum juxta Rituale praescripta sunt, absolvantur”* [*“When in necessity Baptism hath been conferred without exorcisms, if the infant is not dead, the exorcisms ought not to be supplied, but only those things which are prescribed after Baptism according to the Ritual are to be fulfilled”*]. *Enchir. Juris Eccl. Orient.*, M. Varad. [Nagyvárad / Oradea], 1862, p. 86.
[^280]: The Bishop consecrates the Holy Myron [Chrism] on Great Thursday, and it is not permitted to use it for more than one year, and for a second year only by permission of the Ecumenical Roman Pontiff. When the pastor receives the Myron from the Bishop through the dean, he must immediately burn the remains of the old Myron upon a clean stone, and throw the ashes into the place designated for that purpose. (*Syn. Zamosc.* tit. III, §. 2, p. 83.)
[^281]: *“Ex hisce unctionibus solummodo unctio in fronte est essentialis, ceterae solummodo ad integritatem Sacramenti pertinent”* [*“Of these anointings only the anointing on the forehead is essential, the rest pertain only unto the integrity of the Sacrament”*]. Cf. Papp-Szilágyi, *op. cit.*, p. 383.
[^282]: If there are many communicants and the Blood of our Lord Jesus Christ is not sufficient: then among us the Chalice on the holy Altar-Table is replenished with wine and thus communion is increased. — This wine is not transubstantiated into the Blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, but is sanctified only through contact; for thus we read concerning this question: *“Contactu tamen illo vinum sanctitatem acquirit, et in alium superiorem statum sustollitur; — ut separatum a Corpore Christi, si id fieret, veneratione omni, non tamen ea, quae Corpus Christi colitur, dignum sit, quousque species illas vini, quibuscum in Corpus Christi infusum est, conservaverit. — Quod si quis in vinum majorem molem immiserit, et copia alterius vini species illae singulares evanuerint, sanctitas quoque illa et veneratio evanescet”* [*“Yet by that contact the wine acquires holiness, and is raised unto another higher state; — so that if it were separated from the Body of Christ, it would be worthy of all veneration, yet not of that wherewith the Body of Christ is worshiped, as long as it hath preserved those species of wine with which it was poured upon the Body of Christ. — But if anyone hath poured a greater volume into the wine, and by the abundance of the other wine those particular species have disappeared, that holiness also and veneration will disappear”*]. Leo Allatius, *Dissertatio de Missa Praesanctificatorum apud Graecos*, Cologne [Coloniae Agrippinae], 1548, ch. XVIII, p. 1590.
[^283]: The sick who are not in mortal danger must be communicated before all eating and drinking, and it is not permitted for them even to take medicine before eating; but for those who are in danger of death it is fitting to communicate them even after eating. — Unto those, however, who on account of continual or frequent vomiting are unable to retain anything of what is consumed, it is not possible to impart the Holy Eucharist lest dishonor occur; for such who are unable, — if they have truly cleansed their soul by holy confession, — a heartfelt desire for Holy Communion is sufficient. — Likewise it is not permitted to impart Holy Communion unto the insane, lest any indignity be brought upon the Holy Mysteries.
[^284]: From unfamiliar and strange persons one ought not to inquire thus: “Hast thou a husband, or a wife?” but thus: “Hast thou a sworn wife or husband?” For those living in adultery do not even consider this sin as a sin, — and will conceal it.
[^285]: During a jubilee, absolution is plenary, for at that time one may also absolve from reserved sins (*casus reservati* [*reserved cases*]).
