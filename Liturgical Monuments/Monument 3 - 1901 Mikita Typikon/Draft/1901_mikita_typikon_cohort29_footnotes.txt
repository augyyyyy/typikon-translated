[^305]: Between a “Panikhida” and a “Parastas” there is such a difference, that a Panikhida is a “night office of the departed” (*officium nocturnum Defunctorum* [*night office of the Dead*]), while a Parastas is a “commemorative office of the departed” (*officium commemorativum Defunctorum* [*commemorative office of the Dead*]). In the early centuries of the Church, Christians held vigils either in church, awaiting a great feast, or by the remains of the dead. And corresponding unto this, all these services were called *παννυχίδες* == *vigiliae* == *officium pernox* [*all-night service*]. Now only the vigil by the departed is called by the Greek word “*παννυχίς*,” while the vigil in church on great feasts is called by the simple word “All-Night Vigil”; among the Greeks, however, it is called: “*παννυχὶς ἀγρυπνητή*” or “*ἀγρυпνία ὁλονύκτιος*” [*all-night vigil*].
