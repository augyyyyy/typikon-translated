§. 185.

The Order of the Churching of a Bride after Marriage.

When the newly-married bride comes unto the church, she stands outside the church doors; the Priest, however, vested in the epitrachelion, comes unto her and speaks: “Enter, child, into the courts of the Lord,” etc.

And immediately the Priest enters into the church with the bride and with those present. And having entered, the Priest, standing before the Icon of the Mother of God — the bride standing near him — begins usually: “Blessed is our God...” etc., the Trisagion, “Our Father,” “Lord, have mercy” (*Господи помилуй*) (12 times), “Come, let us worship” (*Прїиди́те поклони́мся*) (3 times), etc., and Psalm 66: “May God be merciful unto us...” etc.

After this the Priest, before the bride who hath bowed her head, proclaims: “Let us pray to the Lord,” and speaks the prayer: “O God, our God,” and blesses her head with his right hand, saying: “May the Lord cover thee under the shelter of His wings,” etc. And immediately he sprinkles her with holy water, saying: “By the sprinkling of this sanctified water, may blessing descend and abide upon thee,” etc.

And after this he makes the Dismissal; and he concludes the rite with this blessing: “Go in peace, and diligently observe all the commandments of the Lord,” etc.

§. 187.

The Order of the Burial of Infants.

Infants in the Church are called those children who know not yet how to speak; when, however, they begin to speak, even unto the seventh year, they are called children [*otroky*]. — The burial of infants as well as of children is celebrated in bright, that is, white vestments.

When the Priest comes into the house where the deceased infant lies, being vested in epitrachelion and phelonion, he puts incense into the censer, and goes into the room in which the deceased lies; during this the choir sings: “When Thou didst descend...”

After this, in the room, the Panikhida begins, namely: “Blessed is our God...” etc., the Trisagion, “Our Father,” “With the spirits of the righteous...”[^297] etc., and the litany: “Have mercy on us, O God,” with the exclamation. After the exclamation there is a special dismissal ending with: “In blessed repose grant eternal rest, O Lord...” etc., unto which the choir sings: “Memory eternal.” — And the Priest, having sprinkled the body of the deceased with holy water, goes out with the cantor from the room into the courtyard, where the burial itself begins.

First the Priest speaks: “Blessed is our God...” etc.; the choir: “Amen”; and among us[^298] there follow: Psalms 112, 102, and 23 (“Praise the Lord, O ye servants...” etc., “O Lord, my heart is not exalted...” etc., “The earth is the Lord’s...” etc.).

After the Psalms the Priest proclaims: “For holy art Thou, O God...” etc., and the Trisagion is sung. — After this follow: the Prokeimenon, the Epis-

§. 188.

The Order of the Burial of Lay Persons.

The Priest, having come into the house where the remains of the deceased lie, puts upon himself the epitrachelion and phelonion, and having put incense into the censer, goes into the room, while the cantor sings: “When Thou didst descend” (*Егда снизшелъ еси*); in the room he begins the Panikhida: “Blessed is our God...” etc. After this the Trisagion, and the rest as usual. After the Dismissal the Priest speaks: “In blessed repose...” etc., the choir sings: “Memory eternal,” during which singing the Priest sprinkles the body of the deceased with holy water, and with the cantor goes out of the room.

When the body is laid upon the prepared place in the courtyard, the Priest begins: “Blessed is our God...” etc., and immediately among us[^299] the choir sings “Alleluia” 3 times, and the troparia: “With depth of wisdom,” Glory, and Now: “We have thee as a wall and a haven...” etc.

After the troparia there are sung first the verses from the first stasis of Kathisma 17: “Blessed are the undefiled,” with the verse: “Blessed art Thou, O Lord, teach us Thy statutes,” etc.[^300] After this are sung verses from the second stasis of Kathisma 17: “I am Thine...” etc., with the verse: “O Savior, save the soul of Thy servant,” concluding with the sticheron: “My soul shall live...” etc. Finally the stichera “The choir of the Saints,” with the verse: “Blessed art Thou, O Lord,” and we conclude with “Alleluia” (3 times).

After this the Little Litany, and the Priest censes the body of the deceased from the four sides.

§. 191.

The Order of Parastas.[^305]

The presiding Priest and all the Priests present there, having entered into the Sanctuary, clothe themselves in epitrachelion and phelonion, and having gone out from the Sanctuary, stand by the tetrapod, upon which is placed a dish with kolyvo.

The presiding Priest, having taken the censer with incense, censing, begins: “Blessed is our God...” etc. (The censing from the 4 sides of the tetrapod takes place cruciformly.) At this they sing the “Trisagion.” After this: “Our Father,” “Come, let us worship” (*Прїиди́те поклони́мся*) (3 times), and Psalm 90 (“He that dwells in the aid...”); and thus the Litany of Peace.

After the Litany of Peace the cantors sing first “Alleluia” 3 times, and then the troparia: “With depth of wisdom.”

After the troparia are sung the verses from Kathisma 17, and that: first from the 1st stasis: “Blessed are the undefiled,” with the verse: “Blessed art Thou, O Lord, teach us Thy statutes.” After this from the second stasis: “I am Thine,” with the verse: “O Savior, save the soul of Thy servant,” concluding with the sticheron: “My soul shall live.” After this is sung: “The choir of the Saints,” and it concludes with “Alleluia” (3 times). After this the Little Litany, when the Priest censes, and the Sessional hymn; and thus follows Psalm 50.

After this is sung the canon: “Having traversed the water.” After the 3rd ode of the canon takes place the Sessional hymn, after the 6th the Kontakion and Ikos, and after the 9th “It is truly meet,” when the Priests stand.

After this the Priests sing: “Holy God,” and the Little Panikhida begins with the litany for the departed and with the Dismissal. At the conclusion the Priest proclaims: “In blessed repose...” etc., and the cantor sings: “Memory eternal.”

§. 193.

The Order of the Sanctification of Water on the Feast of Theophany.[^308]

After the Ambo Prayer[^309] the Priest, with candle-bearers preceding him, goes unto the place where in a certain vessel (in the font) water hath been prepared, while the choir sings the Troparia: “The voice of the Lord upon the waters,” during which singing the Priest censes the water.
