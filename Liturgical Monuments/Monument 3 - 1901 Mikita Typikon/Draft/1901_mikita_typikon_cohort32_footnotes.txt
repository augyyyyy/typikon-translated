[^340]: "The Lunar Cycle" (*cyclus lunae*) is a revolution of 19 years, after the passage of which the new moons and full moons return unto their original days; that number which shows which year a given year is in the number of a single cycle is called the "golden number" (*numerus aureus*). For example, the year 1891 will have the golden number 8 (и), which signifies only that it is the 8th year in this lunar cycle; the year 1902 will have 19, and 1903 anew 1; — that is, here a second lunar cycle will begin anew.
[^341]: It is customary in the Church to designate the 7 days of the week by 7 letters (А. В. Г. Д. Є. Ѕ. З.), — and this designation proceeds from year to year, so that in one year the turn comes unto one letter, and in another year unto another letter, that by it the Sunday day may be designated. — These letters are called "Sunday letters = *litterae dominicales* = *vrucilito*." If the year had 365 days, this revolution would conclude in seven years. But since after every 3 common years one day is added in a leap year, the order of Sunday letters cannot conclude except after 28 years (4 × 7 = 28). This cycle is called the "solar cycle." The Sunday letter begins from the 1st day in March.
