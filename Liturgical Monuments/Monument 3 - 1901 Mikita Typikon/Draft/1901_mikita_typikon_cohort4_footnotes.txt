[^8]: Sundays are twofold: ordinary (*riadovi*) and festal. Ordinary Sundays follow one after another in order from the Sunday after the Descent of the Holy Spirit unto the Sunday “of Zacchaeus.” The first ordinary Sunday is the “Sunday of All Saints”; the last, the 32nd, is “of Zacchaeus.” Sunday is the first day of the week, and it is dedicated to the Resurrection of Jesus Christ; Monday to the Angels; Tuesday to the Forerunner; Wednesday and Friday to the Precious Cross; Thursday to the Apostles and St. Nicholas; Saturday to All Saints. The period comprising eight full weeks, or 8 Tones, is called a pillar (*stolp*). There are six pillars in a year, which always begin with Sunday and Tone 1. Each pillar indicates which Tone and which Matins Gospel will fall on which Sunday. In the Paschalia, moreover, under the boundary key, it is indicated when the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, etc., pillar begins. Festal Sundays, however, which have their own proper Epistles, Gospels, Prokimena, and Communion Hymns, are 7 in number, namely: (α) The “Sunday of the Holy Forefathers,” the second before the Nativity of Christ; (β) The “Sunday of the Holy Fathers,” that is, the first before the Nativity of Christ; (γ) The “Sunday of the Holy Ancestors of God,” the first after the Nativity of Christ; (δ) The Sunday before Theophany; (ε) The Sunday after Theophany; (ϛ) The Sunday before the Exaltation of the Precious Cross; (η) The Sunday after the Exaltation of the Precious Cross.
[^9]: If the 11th falls on a Sunday, the service of the holy Fathers is chanted on that same day; if it falls on a Monday, Tuesday, or Wednesday, on the preceding Sunday; but if on a Thursday, Friday, or Saturday, on the following Sunday.
[^10]: Between the Sunday of All Saints and the Sunday of Zacchaeus there are at times more and at times fewer than 32 Sundays, but the ordinary liturgical Gospels are constantly only 32 in number; whence it follows that at times the liturgical Gospels do not suffice, and at times they are left over. — If there are more Sundays than ordinary Gospels, then those Gospels are interrupted and are not taken on festal Sundays, but are taken only from the Sunday after Theophany; and if even so they do not suffice, they are then repeated after the festal Sundays. — But if there are fewer Sundays than ordinary Gospels, and Gospels are left over, then they must be read together on festal Sundays with the festal Gospels, and some are omitted altogether.
