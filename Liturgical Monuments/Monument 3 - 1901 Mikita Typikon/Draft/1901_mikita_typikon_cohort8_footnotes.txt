[^58]: The 8 verses are these: (α) “Praise ye the name of the Lord”; (β) “Ye that stand in the temple”; (γ) “O Lord, Thy name endures forever”; (δ) “Blessed be the Lord out of Zion”; (ε) “O give thanks unto the Lord”; (ϛ) “To Him that established the earth”; (η) “With a strong hand”; (ϑ) “O give thanks unto the God of heaven.” And there is appended unto each verse: “Alleluia.” It is sung 9 times in token of the chanting of the nine ranks of Angels.
[^59]: In Galicia even now, when on great feasts at Matins they chant the Polyeleos: a tetrapod [analogion] is placed in the midst of the church, and upon it is set the festal icon of the Lord, or of the Mother of God, or of the Saint of the day, and all candles are lighted and distributed unto the people. The Priest then vests in phelonion, opens the holy doors, and comes out before the tetrapod [analogion], where he censes the icon of the feast placed upon the tetrapod [analogion], and thereafter the altar and the people. Upon the conclusion of the Polyeleos, standing before the tetrapod [analogion], he begins the magnification there, singing the first verse, and the people continue. Upon the completion of the magnification, the Priest enters into the altar, and the people continue Matins. Pelesz, *Pastoral Theology* [Богослов. Пастырское], p. 617; *Typikon*, p. 19.

[^62]: Of Theotokia-troparia the most renowned are: (α) The dismissal Sunday Theotokia in the 8 tones sung after the Sunday troparia at Vespers and at Matins. [^63]: Among the stichera sung at “Lord, I have cried,” the sticheron at “Glory” is called the “Doxastikon” [Slavonic: *slavnyk*]; for it hath as its subject the magnification of the occurring feast or Saint.
[^64]: Of great bows the most renowned are those 15 bows which it is necessary to use at the daily Midnight Office in the Great Fast. Those bows formerly (according to the Typikon of Irene Augusta [*Juxta Typikon Irene Augustae*]) they performed thus: The first 3 bows the sinful man made in such wise that when standing, he outstretched his arms and said three times these words: “O God, be merciful unto me a sinner”; thereafter bending the knees and head, he said again three times: “Countless times have I sinned, O Lord, forgive me!” — The other 12 he performed likewise in this manner, but these prayers, both when he stood and when he knelt, he said only once. Cf. Nilles, *Kalendarium Manuale Utriusque Ecclesiae*, Innsbruck [Oeniponte], 1879, Vol. I, p. 119.
