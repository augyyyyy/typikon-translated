§. 168.

XX. How is the Liturgy Celebrated on Weekdays if a Great Saint Occurs Within a Feast?

If the Saint hath a Polyeleos, then it is taken thus: The typical antiphons. At the Beatitudes, Ode 3 of the feast on 4 and Ode 6 of the Saint on 4. After the entrance: the troparion of the feast and the troparion of the Saint; “Glory” (*Сла́ва*): the kontakion of the Saint; “Now and forever”: the kontakion of the feast. The Prokimenon of the feast and of the Saint. The Epistle and Gospel of the Saint (while that in course of the day is read on the day before). In place of “It is truly right,” the Heirmos of the feast. The Communion Hymn of the feast and of the Saint.[^248]
If there is also a service of various intention, then after the entrance: the troparion of the feast, the troparion of the Saint, and the troparion of the intention; the kontakion of the Saint; “Glory” (*Сла́ва*): the kontakion of the intention; “Now and forever”: the kontakion of the feast. The Prokimenon of the feast, of the Saint, and of the intention. The Prokimenon, Epistle, Gospel, and Communion Hymn of the Saint and of the intention.[^249]

§. 169.

XXI. How is the Liturgy Celebrated on Weekdays if the Apodosis of a Feast Occurs?

The typical antiphons; at the Beatitudes, Ode 9 from both canons on 8. After the entrance: the troparion of the feast; “Glory, now and forever”: the kontakion of the feast. The Prokimenon and Communion Hymn of the feast. The Epistle and Gospel of the day (in course). In place of “It is truly right,” the Heirmos of the ode from the festal canon. The festal dismissal.

§. 170.

XXII. When is it Permitted to Celebrate the Liturgy of the Departed in Black Vestments?

There is a distinction between the service for the departed (*pro defunctis*) and the “service of the departed” (*missa defunctorum*).[^250]

=== LEAF p242 ===
[Book Page 218]

(p. 218)

I. As regards the Liturgy for the departed: the priest is permitted to celebrate it every day, that is, he takes and completes the service of the feast, or of the Sunday, or of the day, or of the Saint in white vestments without any litany for the departed, yet nevertheless applies the special fruits of the Liturgy (*fructus speciales*) unto the departed. This Liturgy also in its external aspect is dedicated unto the Sunday, Feast, or Saint, and only in its internal ministerial application (*in applicatione*) is for the departed. It is celebrated without any regard unto the feast day.
II. The “service of the departed” is celebrated specifically for the departed in black vestments. It hath proper troparia (“With profound wisdom,” etc.), a proper: Prokimenon, Epistle, Gospel,[^251] Communion Hymn, and Dismissal. In place of “It is truly right,” there is sung: “Save those who hope,” etc. Into the Litany of Peace are inserted 2 petitions for the departed; and after the litany “Let us all say,” the litany “Have mercy on us, O God” (*Помилуй насъ Боже*) is sung; and at the conclusion of the holy Liturgy a Pannychis[^252] for the departed is sung.[^253]
To celebrate the Liturgy for the departed in black vestments, according to the decree of His Excellency, our Bishop Julius, given under No. 4227:1897, is not permitted in the Eparchy of Mukachevo on the following days: 1) On Sundays and on decreetal feasts. 2) On Polyeleos feasts (yet on Doxology feasts it is permitted). 3) On the Paramon [Eve] of the Nativity of Christ and of Theophany, when the Royal Hours are celebrated. 4) From Lazarus Saturday unto Thomas Sunday. 5) Throughout the entire Week of the Exaltation of the Precious Cross and in the Week of the Sunday of the Veneration of the Cross, because the precious Cross is exposed for the sake of the Passion of Christ. 6) In Great Lent, in the Cathedral Church at the main Altar-Table, the celebration of the holy Liturgy for the departed in black vestments is permitted only on Saturdays, yet at the side Altar-Tables and in other

=== LEAF p243 ===
[Book Page 219]

(p. 219)

churches it is not permitted to celebrate the holy Liturgy for the departed in black vestments only on such a day when a feast is celebrated. If anyone on prohibited days should wish to serve a Liturgy for the departed, the Liturgy is served not of the departed (in black vestments), but for the departed (in white vestments). The Liturgy of the departed in black vestments is celebrated as is written in the Sluzhebnik (p. 497). In the Sluzhebnik on pp. 488–496 are set forth the services of the departed for every day of the week. The service of the departed *praesente cadavere* [in the presence of the body of the deceased] may be served on any day.

#### ARTICLE II.

Concerning Liturgical Extraordinary Occurrences.

§. 171.

Which are the Most Notable Extraordinary Occurrences in Holy Liturgies?

1. The holy Liturgy hath its proper beginning (*initium proprium*),[^254] namely: “Blessed is the kingdom,” etc., and thus the Litany of Peace; but if the holy Liturgy is combined with Vespers, then “Blessed is the kingdom” is taken, “Come, let us worship” (*Прїиди́те поклони́мся*) 3 times, etc.
2. “Heavenly King” (*Царю̀ небе́сный*) before the beginning of the holy Liturgy with the Royal Doors opened is sung 3 times: (α) On the Sunday of the Descent of the Holy Spirit; (β) At the first holy Liturgy of a newly ordained presbyter; and (γ) If a holy Liturgy is celebrated for invoking the help of the Holy Spirit. We do not say “Heavenly King” (*Царю̀ небе́сный*) from the Sunday of Pascha until the Descent of the Holy Spirit, but instead of it we take from the Sunday of Pascha until the Ascension “Christ is risen,” and from the Ascension until the Descent of the Holy Spirit “Thou Who didst ascend into the heavens, O God.”

=== LEAF p244 ===
[Book Page 220]

(p. 220)

3. From the Sunday of Pascha until the Ascension, after “Blessed is the kingdom,” we sing “Christ is risen” (*Христосъ воскресе*) 3 times, and only then follows the Litany of Peace.
4. Festal antiphons are appointed for: Pascha, the Ascension, the Descent of the Holy Spirit, the Transfiguration, the Exaltation, the Nativity of Christ, Theophany, and Palm Sunday. Festal antiphons are sung on all those Master Feasts which have their own antiphons, and that—as was said—only on the very first day of the feast.[^255] Daily antiphons[^256] are taken at weekday Liturgies when a Minor or Middle Saint occurs on a weekday. The typical antiphons (“Bless the Lord, O my soul” (*Благослови душе моя Господа*) and “Praise the Lord, O my soul” (*Хвалѝ, дꙋшѐ моя̀, Го́спода...*)) and the Beatitudes (“In Thy kingdom when Thou comest”) are taken: a) On all Sundays of the entire year (except Palm Sunday, Bright Week, the Wednesday before Ascension, and the Sunday of the Descent of the Holy Spirit, as well as the Master Feasts having proper antiphons). b) On all forefeasts and afterfeasts. c) On all Marian feasts. d) And on all Polyeleos and Doxology feasts.[^257] As regards the Beatitudes, in the Horologion we have only the refrains, and between these refrains are placed the troparia corresponding unto the feast or tone. For the Sundays of the entire year, the troparia at the Beatitudes are set forth in the Oktoechos. On feasts, forefeasts, and afterfeasts, as well as on feasts of Saints, the troparia at the Beatitudes are taken from the canon of those feasts and are sung in the tone of the troparia. It is necessary to note here that on a Polyeleos feast there are taken at the Beatitudes usually Ode 3 and Ode 6 from the canon of the Saint on 8, and on afterfeasts the ode in course on 8. But what is an ode in course? There are 9 odes in a canon (Ode 2 doth not exist here), and if, for example, the Nativity of the Theotokos should be on Monday, then at the weekday Liturgy on Tuesday we take at the Beatitudes Ode 1, on Wednesday Ode 3, on Thursday Ode 4, on Friday Ode 5, etc.
5. In place of the Trisagion, “As many as have been baptized into Christ” is sung on the Nativity of Christ, Theophany, on Lazarus Saturday, on Great Saturday, on the Sunday of Pascha, throughout the entire Bright Week, and on the

=== LEAF p245 ===
[Book Page 221]

(p. 221)

Descent of the Holy Spirit (on the Saturday before Pentecost and on the very feast of Pentecost itself).
6. “Before Thy Cross we bow down in worship, O Master” is sung: (α) On the Exaltation of the Precious Cross[^258] and throughout the entire afterfeast of this feast; (β) On the Sunday of the Veneration of the Cross and throughout its entire week;[^259] (γ) On the feast of the “Procession of the Precious Wood” (August 1); and (δ) On the very apodosis of the feast of the Exaltation of the Precious Cross.[^260] On these feasts the holy Cross is brought out into the middle of the temple upon the Tetrapod for the veneration and kissing of the faithful; and the bringing forth of this Cross takes place after the Great Doxology at Matins.[^261]
7. On Great Saturday after the Epistle, in place of “Alleluia,” there is sung: “Arise, O God, judge the earth, for Thou shalt inherit among all the nations” with its verses. And when they sing “Arise, O God,” then the priest and deacons strip off the black vestments and vest in white, and thus the Gospel is read.
8. On the Sunday of Pascha the holy Gospel according to John (“In the beginning was the Word,” etc.) is read in 12 sections in various languages

=== LEAF p246 ===
[Book Page 222]

(p. 222)

by multiple priests; and at each section of the Gospel they strike the bells.[^262]
9. Because of Sundays in course—from the Sunday of All Saints unto the Sunday of Zacchaeus—there are 32, which sometimes are lacking, and sometimes are in excess, therefore concerning the Epistles and Gospels—they are taken in the following manner: That is, if they are lacking: then the 28th, 29th, and 30th are repeated; yet if they are in excess: then the 28th, 29th, and 30th are omitted, but in such wise that the 32nd (of Zacchaeus) is always taken. Sometimes these Sundays in course are also supplemented by festal Sundays (that is, by the Sunday of the Holy Forefathers, the Sunday before the Nativity, the Sunday after the Nativity, the Sunday before the Enlightenment [Theophany], and the Sunday after the Enlightenment).
10. Litanies of various intentions are inserted into the Litany of Peace after the petition “For those who travel by sea, land, and air” and before the petition: “That we may be delivered.” And in the Triple Litany [Litany of Fervent Supplication] after the petition: “Again let us pray for our God-loving Bishop” and before the petition: “For the people here present.” But if a Liturgy for the departed is celebrated—the litany of the departed: “Have mercy on us, O God” (*Помилуй насъ Боже*) is inserted after the exclamation of the Triple Litany and before the litany of the catechumens (“Pray, ye catechumens”).
11. In place of the Cherubic Hymn (“Let us who mystically represent the Cherubim”), on Great Thursday there is sung: “Of Thy Mystical Supper,” etc.; on Great Saturday: “Let all mortal flesh keep silence” (*Да молчитъ*); and at the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts: “Now the Heavenly Powers.”
12. In place of “It is truly right,” there is taken on Great Feasts the Heirmos of the 9th Ode with the festal refrain, and that is called the *zadostoinyk* (that is, in place of the *dostoinyk* [hymn to the Theotokos]). In Great Lent at the Liturgy of St. Basil the Great, the Theotokion of Tone 8: “All creation rejoices in thee.” On Great Saturday: “Weep not for Me, O Mother.” At the Liturgy of the departed: “Save those who hope in thee, O Mother,” etc.
13. From the Sunday of Pascha until the Ascension, the priest at the holy Liturgy exclaims “With the fear of God” and “Save, O God, Thy people” (*Спаси Боже*) thus: “With the fear of God and with faith draw near: Christ is risen,” and again: “Save, O God, Thy people, and bless Thine inheritance: Christ is risen,” unto which the cantor, both in place of “Blessed is He that comes” and in place of “Many years, O Master,” sings “Christ is risen.” From the Ascension until its Apodosis, in place of “We have seen the true light,” there is sung: “Be Thou exalted above the heavens, O God.”

14. At the Liturgy of St. Basil the Great, in place of “All creation rejoices in thee,” there is sung on Great Thursday “Come, O ye faithful, let us enjoy the Master’s hospitality,” and on Great Saturday “Weep not for Me, O Mother.”
15. In place of “Blessed be the name of the Lord,” from the Sunday of Pascha until its Apodosis, “Christ is risen” (*Христосъ воскресе*) is sung.

On Great Saturday straightway after the Prayer Behind the Ambo, the priest comes unto the Tetrapod and blesses the 5 loaves, wheat, wine, and oil, exclaiming: “Let us pray to the Lord,” and reads the litany prayer: “O Lord Jesus Christ our God, Who didst bless,” etc., and only then censes and sings: “Blessed be the name of the Lord” (*Буди имя Господне*) 3 times. At the conclusion of the singing, the priest exclaims “Let us pray to the Lord,” and saith the prayer over the kolyvo (“O Lord, Who hast completed all things by Thy word”); after this he sprinkles the offering with holy water, saying: “These gifts are blessed and sanctified by the sprinkling of this holy water, in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. the litiya. During the distribution of the “antidoron” and the Myrovanije, the priest greets the faithful thus: “Christ is among us!”—and from the Sunday of Pascha until the Apodosis: “Christ is risen!” From the Sunday of Pascha until the Ascension, when the antidoron is distributed, at the same time there takes place also the kissing of the Cross.

Amen.” Or: “I lay my hands upon the servant (or handmaid) of God, that he (or she) may be whole, in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.”
19. On feasts at the dismissals, in place of “More honorable than the Cherubim,” there is said the refrain of the feast and the Heirmos of the 9th Ode. Besides this we note that the festal dismissal is taken not only at the Liturgy, but also at public services, and besides this also at such sanctifications and blessings as do not have a mystical festal event. But at the Holy Mysteries administered on Master Feasts and afterfeasts, the festal dismissal is not taken. A burial hath its own proper dismissal. In these latter cases neither is the Heirmos taken at the dismissal, for it is taken only at public sacred actions. In Bright Week (through Saturday inclusive) there is no other dismissal except the Dismissal of Pascha, and that in all cases without exception, even at the Holy Mysteries.[^266]
20. In Bright Week from Sunday Matins even unto the end of the Saturday Liturgy, the Royal Doors, the Deacon’s Doors, and the servers’ doors remain open as a sign that Christ by His resurrection hath opened unto us the doors of the heavenly kingdom. Finally we also note that the priest on Great Friday after Vespers, when the laying of the Plashchanytsia into the tomb hath already been completed, exposes the Holy Gifts from the tabernacle upon the holy Altar-Table, where they shall remain exposed even unto Sunday Matins.[^267]

### APPENDIX.

§. 172.

What is Necessary to Note Concerning the Eves [Paramons] of the Nativity of Christ and of Theophany?

I. If the Paramon [Eve][^268] of the Nativity of Christ occurs on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, or Friday, then on that very day there is a fast, and the service: that is, the Royal Hours and Vespers with the Liturgy of St. Basil the Great are celebrated on the Paramon itself in the morning at the 10th hour. But if the Paramon of the Nativity of Christ occurs on Saturday or Sunday, then the service is divided: that is, the fast is transferred unto Friday, the Royal Hours we sing on Friday, and on the Paramon itself there is the Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom without Vespers. Vespers we sing on the Paramon itself in the afternoon at the 3rd hour, while the Liturgy of St. Basil the Great without Vespers is celebrated on the Nativity of Christ itself.[^269]
II. If the Paramon[^270] of Theophany occurs on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, or Friday, then on the Paramon itself there is a fast, in the morning are celebrated the Royal Hours, and Vespers with the Liturgy of St. Basil the Great; and the Great Blessing of Water takes place after the Liturgy of Basil. But if the Paramon of Theophany occurs on Saturday or Sunday, then the service is divided, and that in this wise: that on the Paramon itself there is not a strict fast, but only abstinence from meat and dairy products, and the fast of the Paramon is not transferred unto Friday, but on this Friday on account of the compact week the eating of meat is permitted. The Royal Hours nevertheless are celebrated on Friday. On the Paramon itself in the morning is celebrated the Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom without Vespers, while Vespers takes place on the Paramon in the afternoon at the 3rd hour, at which time also the blessing of water takes place. And the Liturgy of St. Basil the Great without Vespers is celebrated on the very feast of Theophany itself.

§. 173.

What is Necessary to Know Concerning the Extraordinary Occurrences of the Remaining Feasts?

I. If the feast of the “Three Holy Hierarchs” occurs on Meatfare Saturday, that is, of All Souls: we sing their service on Friday in all things without fail. But if the temple is dedicated unto them: then the service of the departed is sung on the preceding Saturday; and on Saturday we sing the service of the Saints only.
II. If the feast of the Meeting of the Lord occurs on Meatfare Saturday, that is, of All Souls, the service of the departed is sung on the preceding Saturday or on Meatfare Thursday. And on Saturday the entire service of the feast alone is sung without fail. If the Meeting of the Lord occurs on the Monday of the first week of Lent, the service of the feast is sung on Cheesefare Sunday.
III. If the Finding of the Precious Head of St. John the Baptist occurs on Meatfare Saturday, that is, of All Souls, the entire service of the Forerunner is sung on Meatfare Friday. But if it occurs on the Monday of the first week of holy Lent, it is sung on Cheesefare Sunday. If it occurs on any day of the 1st week of Lent, we sing the service of the feast either on Cheesefare Sunday or on the Saturday of the 1st week of Lent.
IV. If the 40 Martyrs occurs on the Thursday of the Great Canon, we sing their service on the Tuesday before that same week. If this feast occurs on the Saturday of the Akathist, we sing their service on the 5th Sunday of holy Lent.
V. If the feast of the holy Great-Martyr George occurs on Great Friday, or on Great Saturday, or on the Sunday of Pascha itself, we sing his service on the Monday of Bright Week together with the service of the day.

§. 174.

What is Necessary to Note Concerning the Annunciation?

The feast of the Annunciation of the Most-Holy Theotokos begins from the Thursday of the 3rd week of holy Lent, and extends unto the Wednesday of Bright Week. An extensive instruction concerning this feast is found in the Triodia and in the Triodion or Menaion; here however in brief we set down only the following notes:
I. It is fitting to know that if the Annunciation of the Most-Holy Theotokos occurs on the 3rd, 4th, or 5th Sunday of holy Lent, Small Vespers is sung on Saturday according to the instruction set down in the Triodion.
