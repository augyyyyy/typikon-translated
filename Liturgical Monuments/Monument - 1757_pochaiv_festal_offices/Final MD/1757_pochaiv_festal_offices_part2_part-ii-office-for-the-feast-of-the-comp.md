# PART II: OFFICE FOR THE FEAST OF THE COMPASSION OF THE MOST HOLY BIRTHGIVER OF GOD

*Celebrated on the Friday of the Second Week after the Sunday of All Saints.[^36]*

*On Thursday evening, at "Lord, I have cried", Stichera to the Birthgiver of God, on 6. Tone 2, Podoben: "When from the Tree" (Є҆гда̀ ѿ дре́ва):*

*Sticheron 1:* Showing thee unto all men as a protectress, unto all a great refuge, unto all Christians a champion, He Who from thee compassionately clothed Himself in our mortal clay, and endured the Cross and death for our sake: Him do thou entreat unceasingly, O Bride of God, to send down unto all forgiveness of transgressions.

*Sticheron 2:* Of old the Virgin and Ewe-lamb, beholding Him Who sprang from her womb hanging upon the Tree as one condemned, between two condemned men, crying aloud, said: O my Son and my God, strange spectacle, marvelous mystery! Yet the abyss of Thy wisdom no man can search out: I sing of Thy longsuffering.

*Sticheron 3:* Where is the comeliness of Thy countenance? Where is hidden the beauty of Thy goodness, O Son? How is it that He Who made all things ineffably good, O God fair in goodness, for the sake of all men hangest upon the Tree inglorious and dishonored, having neither form nor beauty, O Child? cried the most good Virgin, groaning and weeping.

*Sticheron 4:* Beholding Thee, O Jesus, lifted up upon the Tree of the Cross, the unwedded Maiden wept and said: O sweet Child, why hast Thou left me alone who gave Thee birth? O unapproachable Light of the pre-eternal and unoriginate Father, hasten and be glorified, that they may obtain divine glory who glorify Thy divine passions.

*Sticheron 5:* When upon the Tree thou didst behold the Life dying, O Virgin, and painfully pierced with a spear, weeping thou didst cry: O my Son and my God, what hath the ungrateful assembly rendered unto Thee? Woe is me, unable to endure the agony, I am consumed in my womb, seeing Thee suffer these things, O Master!

*Sticheron 6:* When she saw Him taken down from the Tree, the unwedded Maiden, stretched out and laid upon the earth without breath like a mortal man, embracing Him in her bosom and kissing His lips and eyes, cried unto Him: Marvelous it is, how I now behold Thee, Who vivifiest all things, speechless and motionless! Truly a great wonder!

*Glory... Now and ever... Stavrotheotokion:* Having endured many sorrows at the crucifixion of Thy Son and thy God, O all-pure one, groaning and shedding tears thou didst cry out: Woe is me, O sweet Child! How dost Thou suffer without guilt, willing to deliver mortals descended from Adam: therefore, O all-pure Virgin, we entreat thee in faith, make Him merciful unto us.

*Entrance. And the Prokeimenon of the day. Then the Readings:[^37]*

*Reading from the Prophecy of Jeremiah [Lamentations 1:1–2, 3, 6, 9, 12, 13, 16, 17, 20]:*

In those days, Jeremiah wept over Jerusalem, and said: How does the city sit solitary, that was full of people! She is become as a widow; she wept sore in the night, and her tears are on her cheeks, and she hath none to comfort her among all them that love her. All her friends have dealt treacherously with her, they are become her enemies. She dwells among the nations, she finds no rest. And from the daughter of Zion all her beauty is departed. Behold, O Lord, my affliction, for the enemy hath magnified himself. All ye that pass by, turn and see if there be any sorrow like unto my sorrow, for the Lord hath cropped me as a vineyard, even so spoke the Lord, in the day of His fierce anger. He called a time against me. And from above He hath sent fire into my bones, and instructed me. My strength is weakened within me, for the Lord hath delivered affliction into my hands. For these things I weep; mine eyes have poured down water, because the comforter that should relieve my soul is far from me, and my children are desolate, because the enemy prevailed. Zion spread forth her hands, and there is none to comfort her. The Lord hath commanded concerning Jacob, that his enemies should be round about him. Behold, O Lord, that I am in distress; my bowels are troubled; mine heart is turned within me, for I have been filled with bitterness.

*Reading from the Prophecy of Jeremiah [Lamentations 2:13, 15, 17, 18, 19, 20; 3:7, 12]:*

In those days, Jeremiah wept over Jerusalem, and said: What thing shall I take to witness for thee, or what shall I liken to thee, O daughter of Jerusalem? Who shall save thee, or who shall comfort thee, O virgin daughter of Zion? For all that pass by clap their hands at thee; they hiss and wag their head at the daughter of Jerusalem, saying: Is this the city, the crown of glory, the joy of the whole earth? The Lord hath done that which He had purposed; He hath fulfilled His word that He had commanded in the days of old. Give thyself no rest, and let not the apple of thine eye cease. Arise, cry out in the night, in the beginning of the watches; and pour out thine heart like water before the face of the Lord: lift up thy hands toward Him for the life of thy young children that faint. Behold, O Lord, and consider whom Thou hast thus pruned. He hath hedged me about, and encompassed my head, and I labored. He hath bent His bow, and set me as a mark for the arrow.

He hath caused the arrows of His quiver to enter into my reins. He hath filled me with bitterness, He hath made me drunken with wormwood. Remember my poverty, my bitterness and my gall. These things I recalled in my heart, therefore will I hope in the Lord. It is of the Lord's mercies that we are not consumed, because His compassions fail not. And I knew in the morning that great is Thy faithfulness. The Lord is my portion, said my soul; therefore will I wait for Him. The Lord is good unto them that wait for Him, to the soul that seeks Him. It is good to hope in silence for the salvation of God.

*Reading from the Prophecy of Jeremiah [Lamentations 3:40–41, 55–58, 64; 4:1–2, 7, 11, 20; 5:19–21]:*

In those days, Jeremiah wept over Jerusalem, and said: Let us search out our ways, and try them, and let us turn again to the Lord. Let us lift up our hearts unto the high God. I called upon Thy name, O Lord, out of the low dungeon; Thou hast heard my voice. Hide not Thine ear at my supplication; draw near for my help in the day when I called upon Thee; He said unto me: Fear not. O Lord, plead the cause of my soul, and redeem my life. Thou hast seen, O Lord, their wrong done unto me; judge Thou my cause. Thou hast seen all their vengeance, and all their imaginations against me. Render unto them a recompense, O Lord, according to the work of their hands. How is the gold become dim! How is the good silver changed! The stones of the sanctuary are poured out in the top of every street. The honorable sons of Zion, clothed in first gold. Her Nazirites were purer than snow, they were whiter than milk, their old bones were more ruddy than coral, their polishing was of sapphire. The Lord hath accomplished His fury. The breath of our nostrils, God, the Lord was taken in our pits, of Whom we said: Under His shadow we shall live among the nations. Thou, O Lord, remainest unto ages; Thy throne is from generation to generation. Turn Thou us unto Thee, O Lord, and we shall be turned; renew our days as of old.

*At the Aposticha, Stichera, Tone 4. Podoben: "As noble among martyrs" (Прихва́льнїи мꙋ́ченицы):[^38]*

*Sticheron 1:* Standing by the Cross of her Son and her God, and beholding His longsuffering, weeping sore the pure Mother cried: Woe is me, O sweetest Child! Why dost Thou suffer these things unjustly, O Word of God, that Thou mayest save mankind?

*Verse: O God, my God, attend unto me; why hast Thou forsaken me? [LXX Ps. 21:2a].[^39]*

*Sticheron 2:* Standing by the Cross, the unwedded Bride, receiving wounds from sorrows, cried aloud: Leave me not childless, O my Child, who gave Thee birth, O Word of God; forsake me not, O Only-begotten, Who art untouched by corruption.

...Who art untouched by corruption.

*Verse: The troubles of my heart are enlarged; O bring Thou me out of my distresses [LXX Ps. 24:17].[^40]*

*Sticheron 3:* When I gave Thee birth, I knew no travail; how is it then that now I suffer sorrows, as Thou, O Word, art unjustly crucified? cried bitterly the Virgin shedding tears: Woe is me! I cannot bear to behold Thee lifted up naked upon the Cross, O Lover of mankind.

*Glory... Now and ever... Stavrotheotokion:* When the undefiled Ewe-lamb beheld her Lamb willingly dragged to slaughter as a mortal man, weeping she said: Dost Thou now hasten to leave me childless, Who didst grant me good courage, O Christ? What is this that Thou hast done, O Redeemer of all? Yet I hymn that which is beyond mind and word, and I glorify Thine extreme goodness, O Lover of mankind.

*Troparion, Tone 2:* With the virgin disciple, the Virgin Mother, standing by the Cross of Him Who came forth from her, (unable to endure seeing Him lifted up upon the Cross,) cried aloud with weeping and lamentation: How dost Thou suffer, Who art impassible in Thy nature? I glorify Thine ineffable goodness, O my Son.

*At Matins, at "God is the Lord", Troparion, thrice.*

*After the 1st Kathisma, Sessional Hymn, Tone 8. Podoben: "That which was mystically commanded" (Пове́лѣнное та́йнѡ):[^41]* Beholding Him Who was incarnate of thee, O Birthgiver of God, of ineffable goodness: for having endured the Cross and death as the Lover of mankind, that He might save the world which He created: Him do thou entreat, to deliver also me the wretched from multifaceted torments, and to establish me where the unwaning light shines.

*Glory... Now and ever... The same.*

*After the 2nd Kathisma, Sessional Hymn, Tone and Podoben: The same (Гла́съ и҆ подо́бенъ то́йже).* What is this spectacle that is visible to mine eyes, O Master! He Who holds all creation is lifted up upon the Tree, and Thou diest, Who givest life unto all! cried weeping the Birthgiver of God, when she beheld Him Who ineffably shone forth from her, God and Man, lifted up upon the Tree. (Twice).

*Megalynarion:* We magnify thee, O all-blameless Virgin, Mother of Christ our God, and we honor thy most venerable sorrows, which thou didst endure in the passion of thy Son.

*Then Psalm 21 [LXX Ps. 21]:*

*Verse 1: O God, my God, attend unto me; why hast Thou forsaken me, and art far from my salvation? [LXX Ps. 21:2a].*

*Verse 2: The troubles of my heart are enlarged; O bring Thou me out of my distresses [LXX Ps. 24:17].*

*Verse 3: Awake, my glory; awake, psaltery and harp; I myself will awake early [LXX Ps. 56:9].*

*Verse 4: I will remember thy name throughout all generations [LXX Ps. 44:18a].*

*Verse 5: For it is good before Thy saints [LXX Ps. 51:11b].*

*Verse 6: In Him my heart trusted, and I was helped, and my flesh flourished again, and willingly will I praise Him [LXX Ps. 27:7b].*

*Glory... Now and ever... Alleluia, thrice.*

*After the Polyeleos, Sessional Hymn, Tone 1:* The undefiled Ewe-lamb, beholding the Lamb and Shepherd hanging dead upon the Tree, weeping spoke, maternally crying aloud: How shall I endure Thine ineffable condescension, O my Son, and Thy voluntary passions, O most good God?

*Glory... Now and ever... The same.*

*Then 1st Antiphon of the 4th Tone. Then the Prokeimenon, Tone 4: I will remember thy name throughout all generations. Verse: Give ear, O daughter, and see, and incline thine ear. Gospel from John, pericope 61 [John 19:25–27].[^42]*

*After the Gospel, Sticheron, Tone 6:* O only undefiled Ewe-lamb, Virgin Birthgiver of God, beholding Him Who was incarnate of thy pure blood hanging upon the Cross of His own will, the Maiden cried aloud with bitter tears: O my guileless Son, I hymn Thy passionless dispensation.

*Canon to the Birthgiver of God, Tone 8. Ode 1. Heirmos: Tracing a cross, Moses with his rod: Refrain: Most Holy Birthgiver of God, save us.*

When she saw her Son and Lord nailed upon the Cross, the pure Virgin, tearing herself, cried out bitterly, groaning with the other women, and said:

Seeing Thee now, my beloved and desired One, hanging upon the Cross, I am bitterly wounded in my heart, said the pure one: but grant a word unto Thy handmaid, O good One. *Glory:*

Willingly, O my Son and Creator, Thou endurest a cruel death upon the Tree, said the Virgin, standing by the Cross with the beloved disciple. *Now and ever:*

Now am I bereft of my hope, my joy and gladness, my Son and Lord! Woe is me! with sorrow of heart, the pure one weeping said.

*Katavasia: I shall open my mouth:*

*Ode 3. Heirmos: As a figure of the mystery:*

For fear of the Jews Peter hid himself, and all the faithful fled, forsaking Christ, the Virgin lamenting said.

At Thy strange birth and Thy marvelous dispensation, O my Son, above all mothers was I magnified; but woe is me! now seeing Thee upon the Tree, I am consumed in my womb. *Glory:*

Seeing my womb, and upon my hands wherein I held Thee as an infant, to receive Thee from the Tree, spoke the pure one: but no one, (woe is me!) gave Him. *Now and ever:*

Behold, my sweet light, my good hope and life, my God is extinguished upon the Cross! I am consumed in my womb, the Virgin groaning said.

*Katavasia: O Mother of God, thou living fountain:*

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 4:* Deliver that which Thy Son and God acquired, O all-pure one, having sprinkled it with His Blood, through thy precious prayers: driving away visible and invisible enemies like beasts, and fill their faces with dishonor, as David the psalmist of old proclaimed, O pure one.

*Glory... Now and ever... The same.*

*Ode 4. Heirmos: I have heard, O Lord, the mystery of Thy dispensation:*

O unwaning Sun, pre-eternal God and Creator of all creatures, O Lord, how dost Thou endure suffering upon the Cross? the pure one weeping said.

Weeping, the unwedded one said unto the noble counselor: Make haste, Joseph, go unto Pilate, and beg to take down from the Tree thy Teacher. *Glory:*

Seeing the all-pure one weeping bitterly, Joseph was troubled, and weeping went unto Pilate: Give me, he cried with tears, the Body of my God. *Now and ever:*

Seeing Thee wounded and without glory, naked upon the Tree, O my Child, I am consumed in my womb, lamenting as a Mother the Virgin proclaimed.

*Katavasia: He Who sits in glory:*

*Ode 5. Heirmos: O thrice-blessed Tree:*

Joseph was torn with grief and wept, and wondering together with Nicodemus took Him down, and having kissed the all-pure Body, wept and groaned, and sang unto Him as God.

Receiving Him with weeping, the unwedded Mother laid Him upon her knees, beseeching Him with tears, and kissing Him, bitterly lamenting and crying aloud. *Glory:*

One hope and life, O Master, my Son and God, the light of mine eyes had I, Thy handmaid: but now am I bereft of Thee, my sweet and beloved Child. *Now and ever:*

Sorrows, afflictions, and groanings have overtaken me, woe is me! the pure one bitterly lamenting said: seeing beholding Thee, my beloved Child, naked, wounded, and anointed with spices as a corpse.

*Katavasia: All creation was astonished:*

*Ode 6. Heirmos: In the belly of the aquatic beast:*

I behold Thee as a corpse, O Lover of mankind, Who didst vivify the dead; sorely grieving, I am wounded in my womb: I would fain die with Thee, the all-pure one said: for I cannot bear in my soul to see Thee dead.

Today beholding Thee, O most good God, and having come, O Lord, without glory, without breath, and without form: I weep holding Thee, as I did not expect, woe is me! to see Thee, my Son and my God. *Glory:*

Wilt Thou not speak a word unto Thy handmaid, O Word of God? Wilt Thou have compassion, O Master, upon her who gave Thee birth? said the pure one, lamenting, weeping, and kissing the Body of her Lord. *Now and ever:*

I consider, O Master, that no more shall I hear Thy sweet voice, nor see the beauty of Thy face, as before, I Thy handmaid: for Thou hast set from mine eyes, O my Son.

*Katavasia: Celebrating this divine and all-venerable feast:*

*Kontakion, Tone 8:* Beholding Thee nailed upon the Cross, O Jesus, and willingly accepting wounds, O Master, the Virgin Thy Mother cried: Woe is me, O sweet Child! How dost Thou endure wounds unjustly, O Physician and Healer of human infirmity, Who by Thy compassion hast delivered all from corruption?

*Ikos:* Beholding her Lamb dragged unto slaughter, Mary followed with disheveled hair with the other women, crying thus: Whither goest Thou, Child? For what cause dost Thou accomplish this swift course? Is there perhaps another wedding again in Cana of Galilee, and dost Thou now make haste thither, to make wine for them from water? Shall I go with Thee, Child, or shall I rather wait for Thee? Give me a word, O Word, pass me not by in silence, Who didst keep me pure: for Thou art my Son and my God.

*Ode 7. Heirmos: The mindless decree of the ungodly tyrant:*

Where, O my Son and God, is the former annunciation, which Gabriel spoke unto me, naming Thee King and Son of the Most High God? But now I behold Thee, my sweet light, naked and wounded as a corpse.

Now receive me with Thee, my Son and God, as deliverance from sorrows, that I also may go down into Hades with Thee: leave me not alone, for I can no longer bear to live, not seeing Thee, my sweet light. *Glory:*

With the other myrrh-bearing women, the blameless one bitterly lamented, and seeing Christ carried, said: Woe is me, what do I see! Whither goest Thou now, my Son, and leavest me alone? *Now and ever:*

Fainting and lamenting, the blameless one said unto the myrrh-bearing women: Lament with me and weep bitterly! For behold, my sweet light and your Teacher is delivered unto the tomb.

*Katavasia: The godly-minded worshiped not creation:*

*Ode 8. Heirmos: Bless the Lord, ye children equal in number to the Trinity:*

Seeing the Virgin weeping, Joseph was wholly torn with grief, and cried out bitterly: How shall I, Thy servant, now bury Thee, my God! In what manner of winding-sheets shall I wrap Thy Body?

Thy strange spectacle transcended all mind, carrying the Lord Who upholds all creation:

therefore Joseph also bears Thee as dead in his hands, but with Nicodemus carries and buries Thee. *Glory:*

I behold a strange and most glorious mystery, (the Virgin cried unto the Son and Lord,) how Thou art laid in a poor tomb, Who by Thy command dost raise the dead in the tombs! *Now and ever:*

I will not depart from Thy tomb, my Child, nor will I, Thy handmaid, cease shedding tears, until I also go down into Hades: for I cannot bear separation from Thee, my Son.

*Katavasia: The godly youth in the furnace:*

*Ode 9. Before the Heirmos we sing the Refrain:*

*Magnify, O my soul, the Virgin Mary who suffered together with the Lord at His Cross.*

*Heirmos: A mystery art thou, O Birthgiver of God:*

*Another Refrain: The Angels seeing the sorrow of the all-pure one were astonished, how the Virgin suffers in tears.*

*Another Heirmos: Because of the eating from the tree:*

Joy shall never henceforth touch me, (the blameless one said weeping:) my light and my joy hath set into the tomb; but I will not leave Him alone, here will I die and be buried with Him.

Heal now the wound of my soul, my Child, (the all-pure one cried shedding tears,) rise and assuage my sorrow and grief: for Thou canst do whatsoever Thou wilt, O Master, even though Thou art buried of Thine own will. *Glory:*

O how hath the abyss of mercies been hidden from thee? (the Lord spoke in secret unto the Mother:) for desiring to save My creation, I willed to die; but I will rise again, and will magnify thee, as God of heaven and earth. *Now and ever:*

I hymn Thy compassion, O Lover of mankind, and I worship the wealth of Thy mercy, O Master. For desiring to save Thy creation, Thou didst submit unto death, (said the all-pure one:) but by Thy Resurrection, O Savior, have mercy on us all.

*Katavasia: Let every earthborn mortal leap for joy:*

*Exapostilarion:* O holy all-pure Virgin, glory of the Angels, when thou didst stand by the Cross of thy Son and thy God, unable to endure seeing the reproach of enemies, thou didst cry out, weeping maternally: How dost Thou endure contradictory words, O Lover of mankind? I glorify Thy longsuffering.

*Glory... Now and ever... The same.*

*At the Praises, Stichera, on 4, Tone 2. Podoben: "Ye angelic powers, go before" (А҆́гг҃льскїѧ пред̾иди́те си́лы):*

*Sticheron 1:* Vivify my soul, slain by many passions, O Sovereign Lady, who didst bear God the Word impassibly, Who suffered passions being impassible, and Who put death and Hades to death: by His mortification slay the sin that lives within me, and direct my turbulent thoughts unto divine things. (Twice).

*Sticheron 2:* When Thou didst will to be lifted up upon the Cross, O Christ, then seeing the disciple standing by whom Thou lovedst, and Thy Mother, Thou didst say unto them: Mother, behold thy son; behold, this youth is thy son, O pure Virgin Mother, whom I loved, my virgin and disciple.

*Sticheron 3:* From that time forth, the virgin disciple took the Virgin into his own home. For having been named a son by the all-bounteous Son, he showed filial service unto her as a mother, and fervent filial sorrow evermore. Therefore by their prayers, O Christ God, save our souls.

*Glory... Now and ever... Stavrotheotokion:* When thou sawest Him nailed upon the Cross, O ewe-lamb, Who was born of thee, O ewe-lamb, thou didst cry aloud tearing thy hair:

...tearing thy hair: Woe is me, O sweetest Child! Without travail formerly I gave Thee birth, but now I am in agony of heart, and I cannot bear to behold Thee crucified, O Christ, my most merciful Son.

*Then the Great Doxology. After the Trisagion, Troparion of the Feast: "With the virgin disciple": see on Thursday at Vespers. After the Litany, Dismissal.*

***************************************************

*AT THE DIVINE LITURGY.*

*Antiphons customary of the Birthgiver of God: 1st. "It is good to give praise": 2nd. "The Lord is King": 3rd. "O come, let us sing unto the Lord: Save us, O Son of God, through the prayers of the Birthgiver of God, who sing unto Thee: Alleluia." After the Entrance, Troparion of the Feast, Tone 2: "With the virgin disciple": Glory... Now and ever... Kontakion: "Beholding Thee nailed upon the Cross": Then after the Trisagion:*

*Prokeimenon, Tone 4: She wept sore in the night, and her tears are on her cheeks [Lam. 1:2a].[^43]*

*Verse: And she hath none to comfort her among all them that love her [Lam. 1:2b]. In place of the Epistle:*

*Reading from the Book of Judith [Judith 13:18–20]:[^44]*

The Lord God hath blessed thee in His Son, Who through thee hath brought our enemies to nought. Blessed art thou, O daughter, by the Most High Lord God, above all the women upon the earth; and blessed be the Lord God, Who hath created the heaven and the earth, Who hath directed thee to the cutting off of the head of the chief of our enemies, and Who this day hath so magnified thy name, that thy praise shall not depart from the mouths of men, that they may remember the power of the Lord unto ages, seeing that thou hast not spared thy life, by reason of the affliction and sorrow of thy nation, but hast helped our fall before the face of the Lord our God.

*Alleluia, Tone 4: My strength is weakened within me, for the Lord hath delivered affliction into my hands [Lam. 1:14c].[^45]*

*Verse: The comforter that should relieve my soul is far from me [Lam. 1:16b].*

*Gospel from John, pericope 61 [John 19:25–27].[^46]*

At that time, there stood by the Cross of Jesus His Mother, and His Mother's sister, Mary the wife of Cleopas, and Mary Magdalene. When Jesus therefore saw His Mother, and the disciple standing by, whom He loved, He said unto His Mother: Woman, behold thy son! Then said He to the disciple: Behold thy mother! And from that hour that disciple took her unto his own home.

*Communion Verse: If there be any sorrow like unto my sorrow, therefore my bowels are troubled within me [Lam. 1:12b, 20b].[^47] Alleluia, thrice.*

*At the trapeza, on account of the labor of the vigil, there is great consolation for the brethren: we eat fish with olive oil, and drink wine, unto the glory of God.[^48]*

### Notes

[^36]: The Feast of the Compassion of the Most Holy Birthgiver of God (Сострада́нїе Пресвяты́ѧ Богоро́дицы / *Compassio Beatae Mariae Virginis* or *Our Lady of Sorrows*), celebrated on the Friday after the Octave of Corpus Christi (the Friday of the Second Week after All Saints), was prescribed for the Ruthenian Uniate Church by the Provincial Synod of Zamość (1720, Title III, §4).
[^37]: The three Old Testament readings (Paremias) for the Feast of the Compassion are drawn from the Lamentations of Jeremiah (Threni): Reading 1 (Lam. 1:1–2, 3, 6, 9, 12, 13, 16, 17, 20); Reading 2 (Lam. 2:13, 15, 17, 18, 19, 20; 3:7, 9, 12); Reading 3 (Lam. 3:13, 15, 19–26, 40–41, 55–58, 64; 4:1–2, 7, 11, 20; 5:19–21).
[^38]: Podoben incipit: *Прихва́льнїи мꙋ́ченицы* (*As noble among martyrs*).
[^39]: LXX Ps. 21:2a.
[^40]: LXX Ps. 24:17.
[^41]: Podoben incipit: *Пове́лѣнное та́йнѡ* (*That which was mystically commanded*).
[^42]: Matins Gospel lection: John 19:25–27 (pericope 61).
[^43]: Lamentations 1:2a.
[^44]: Judith 13:18–20. In the Byzantine-Slavonic liturgical tradition, lections from Judith and Proverbs are frequently appointed for festal Liturgies of the Theotokos in place of the standard New Testament Epistle.
[^45]: Lamentations 1:14c, 16b.
[^46]: Gospel lection: John 19:25–27 (pericope 61).
[^47]: Communion verse drawn from Lamentations 1:12b, 20b.
[^48]: Rubric permitting fish, oil, and wine (*razreshenie na rybu, elei i vino*) at the common refectory (trapeza) on Friday in honor of the Feast of the Compassion.
