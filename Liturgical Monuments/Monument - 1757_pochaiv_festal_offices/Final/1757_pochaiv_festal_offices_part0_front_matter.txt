*OFFICES FOR THE FEASTS:*
*Of the Most Holy Mystery of the Eucharist,*
*The Compassion of the Most Holy Birthgiver of God,*
*And the Blessed Hieromartyr Josaphat,[^3]*
*Prescribed by the Holy Council of Zamość.[^4]*
****************************
*Printed in the Monastery of Pochaiv,*
*Of the Order of Saint Basil the Great,*
*In the Year of the Lord, 1757.[^5]*

*Here is set the singing which takes place on the present Feast of the Most Holy Eucharist before the early or brief Liturgy.[^6]*

*The Priest after the Proskomide, having opened the Ark, makes a bow; and having taken the Ciborium with the Most Pure Mysteries, turns toward the people and sings:*

> **Priest:** "☩ O saving Victim!"[^7]

*Then the* **Choir:** Gate opening unto heaven! Hostile battles stir up against us: grant us strength and help, to escape their enmity.

> **Priest:** "☩ Unto the one Lord in Trinity."

*And he places the Ciborium upon the Altar, in the prepared place.*

*Then the* **Choir:** Be eternal glory, Who grants endless life unto us in His Kingdom. Amen.

*After this he begins the Liturgy in the usual manner.*

***************************************************

*After Vespers.*

*The Priest, vested in all the priestly vestments, having approached before the high altar, bows low before the Holy Mysteries, and having taken Them, turns toward the people and begins:*

> **Priest:** "☩ Unto Thy most great Mystery,"[^8]

*Then the* **Choir:** We bow our necks: the Old Testament has passed away, glorious grace we behold; faith likewise supplies that which our senses cannot comprehend.

> **Priest:** "☩ Unto the Father, and unto the Begotten,"

*Then the* **Choir:** Be sung praise and jubilation: honor, glory, power, dominion, and adoration; unto Him Who proceeds from both, equal praise.[^9]

***************************************************

*Then is joined the Trisagion and the hour of supplications, or Supplications, and the other chants.*

> **Priest:** "Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, have mercy on us."

*Then the* **Choir:** Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal.

**Priest again:** Holy God, Holy Mighty. **Choir:** Holy God, Holy Mighty.

> **Priest:** "☩ From plague, famine, fire, and sword, deliver us, O Lord."

*Then the* **Choir:** From plague, famine, fire, and sword, deliver us, O Lord.

*(Know: Thou mayest employ the following three petitions when there is distress from murrain of cattle, or excessive rain, or fierce solar heat:*

*1st Petition:* **Priest:** ☩ From the murrain of cattle:

> **Choir:** "Deliver us, O Lord."

*2nd Petition:* **Priest:** ☩ From immeasurable and unseasonable downpour of rain:

> **Choir:** "Deliver us, O Lord."

*3rd Petition:* **Priest:** ☩ From fierce solar heat.

> **Choir:** "Deliver us, O Lord.*)*"

> **Priest:** "☩ From sudden and unexpected death, preserve us, O Lord."

> **Choir:** "From sudden and unexpected death."

> **Priest:** "We sinners beseech Thee, O God: give ear unto us, O Lord."

> **Choir:** "We sinners [beseech] Thee, O God."

> **Priest:** "Deliver us from every evil, by the intercessions of the Birthgiver of God."

> **Choir:** "Deliver us from every evil."

### Notes

[^1]: Archival manuscript provenance inscription on the preliminary flyleaf referencing the Pochaiv Dormition Lavra ('Książka Ławry z Poczaj[owa]'), accompanied by the central pencil title shorthand 'Posliduy' (for Вослѣдованіа).
[^2]: Archival shelfmark of the Vasyl Stefanyk National Scientific Library of Ukraine in Lviv (LNB): ЛНБ У 32621 СФ-460.
[^3]: Holy Hieromartyr Josaphat Kuntsevych (c. 1580–1623), Archbishop of Polotsk, beatified by Pope Urban VIII in 1643.
[^4]: The Provincial Synod of Zamość (1720), Title III, §4, officially promulgated the Feast of the Most Holy Eucharist (Corpus Christi), the Feast of the Compassion of the Mother of God, and the Feast of Blessed Josaphat throughout the Ruthenian Uniate Church.
[^5]: Inked with Cyrillic alphanumeric date ҂аѱн҃з (1757) with thousands titlo.
[^6]: Church Slavonic: 'коткою' (variant of 'короткою') indicates a low or brief private Divine Liturgy.
[^7]: Church Slavonic metrical translation of the Latin Eucharistic hymn *O Salutaris Hostia* ('O Saving Victim'), traditionally sung at Eucharistic exposition and Benediction.
[^8]: Church Slavonic metrical translation of Saint Thomas Aquinas' Eucharistic hymn *Tantum Ergo Sacramentum* ('Unto Thy most great Mystery').
[^9]: The Church Slavonic text ('Исходѧщꙋ ѿ ѻбоихъ, равное хваленїе') renders the Latin *Procedenti ab utroque Compar sit laudatio*, reflecting the post-Zamość Uniate liturgical assimilation of the *Filioque* in hymns translated directly from Latin rites.
