# PART III: OFFICE FOR THE FEAST OF THE BLESSED HIEROMARTYR JOSAPHAT[^49]

*Celebrated on the 16th day of the Month of September.[^50]*

*If it fall on a Sunday, on Saturday at Vespers we chant the kathisma: "Blessed is the man": All of the Resurrection. At "Lord, I have cried", Resurrection Stichera on 4, and to the Saint on 6. *At the Praises, Stichera, Tone 6:*

*Sticheron 1:* Wholly from youth dedicating thyself together unto Christ, being above the flesh and worldly love, a sacred monk didst thou become, O most sacred Father Josaphat; and a vessel of the Holy Spirit, thou madest thy soul and body pure: living on earth as an angel among youths, in obedience and poverty, in love toward God and neighbor, in prayers and all-night vigils, thou becamest a pattern in time for monastics, and a mirror of every virtue: thou wingedst thy mind unto heavenly things, to behold the ineffable beauty of Christ the King and God, Whom having loved with all thy heart, and strengthened by His power, thou enduredst many sorrows and torments: Whom with boldness entreat for our souls.

*Sticheron 2:* Sitting upon the hierarchic throne, O blessed one, thou adornedst thyself with all virtues, being a guide unto repentance and salvation by teaching and deeds: therefore armed with purity and fervent prayers, with fasting and all-night vigils, and drawing near like the Master Christ unto suffering and the death of the Cross, lying upon the earth crosswise making prayer, thou preparedst thyself for suffering, O Hieromartyr Josaphat: therefore ungodly enemies, falling upon thee as merciless beasts, dragging thee as an innocent lamb, unjustly tore thee in pieces, who sufferedst valiantly and with boldness prayest for our souls.

**[Right Page — Fol. 18r]**

*Sticheron 3:* The ungodly people, thine enemies, as David the psalmist said in the Psalms, they that hate thee made a tumult, they lifted up the head against thee, O blessed Josaphat, by the devil's will, and consulting an ungodly counsel they said: Come, let us destroy him from among men, and his name shall be remembered no more [cf. LXX Ps. 82:3–5].[^65] And having consulted together with one mind, the mighty fell upon thee, and severed thy precious head, beating thee mercilessly and cruelly with stones, and poured out thy blood like water throughout the whole city: likewise having tied stones to thy neck and holy feet, they cast thee down from the hill into the swift current of the river. Behold thy courage and longsuffering! Therefore thou hast received, O much-suffering Josaphat, an unfading crown.

*Glory... Tone 7:* Having loved Christ God from infancy, O all-blessed Josaphat, having forsaken the world for the sake of His love, and all the fair things in the world, in obedience, purity, and monastic poverty thou followedst all the days of thy life: having made slumber of works for our sake unto Christ God, monastically thou pleasedst Him: light as a good bird, thou madest thyself ready for suffering like a martyr; and for the sake of the Union of the Church of God, thou enduredst patiently many sorrows from ungodly people: being beaten and struck, and wounded throughout thy whole body the entire day with many wounds, and having thy head severed, thou wast vouchsafed crowns from Christ God: Whom entreat, O all-blessed one, for those who with love celebrate thine ever-honorable memory, that we may be delivered from visible and invisible enemies, from passions and assaults of the devil, and from the future judgment, and that our souls may be saved.

*Now and ever... of the Feast, Tone the same [Tone 7]:* Today the Cross of the Lord comes forth, and the faithful receive it with longing, and take healings of soul and body and of every disease. This let us kiss with joy and fear: with fear, on account of sin, being unworthy; and with joy, on account of the salvation which Christ God, Who was nailed upon it, grants unto the world, having great mercy.

*Great Doxology. And the Dismissal.*
