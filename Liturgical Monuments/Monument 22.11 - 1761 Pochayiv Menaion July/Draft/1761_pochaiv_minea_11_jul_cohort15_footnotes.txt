[^346]: *Bearing the Light that shone forth from Light*: alluding to the vision of Zechariah (Zech. 4:2) and the Nicene Creed (*Light of Light*), applied to the Mother of God bearing the pre-eternal Word.
[^347]: *Sweet-briar* translates *шипо́къ* (wild rose / sweet-briar / eglantine, Greek *ῥόδον* / *κυνόροδον*), paired with *крї́нъ* (lily) as imagery of martyric purity and sweet aroma.
[^348]: *Co-unoriginate, without division in hypostases* (*собезнача́льнѣ... неразлѣ́чнѡ въ ᲂу҆поста́сѣхъ*): precise Trinitarian and Christological terminology confessing the Son as co-eternal with the Father, distinct in hypostasis yet undivided in divine essence.
[^349]: *The author of evil* translates *безначя́льнаго врага̀*, literally *the enemy from the beginning* or *the arch-enemy* (Greek *τὸν ἀρχέκακον ἐχθρόν*); Church Slavonic *безначальный* here reflects *ἀρχέκακος* rather than unoriginate.
[^350]: *A mature understanding* (*сѣ́дъ ра́зꙋмъ*, literally *gray-haired intellect*, Greek *πολιὰν φρόνησιν*): a classical patristic trope describing youthful martyrs who possessed the wisdom of venerable old age.
[^351]: *Born of the Varangians* (*ѿ варѧ̑гъ роди́сѧ*): referring to the Scandinavian/Norse (Varangian, Rurikid) lineage of Grand Prince Volodymyr the Great.
[^352]: *Overshadowed by the Mosaic law* translates *мѡѷсе́йскимъ ѡ҆тне́вшасѧ [ѡ҆тѣне́на бы́вши] зако́номъ*, referring to the giving of the Law on Mount Sinai amidst clouds and thick darkness (Ex. 19:16–18), contrasted here with the bright light of Christian illumination in Kyiv.
[^353]: *A father spiritually and a ruler physically* (*ѻ҆ц҃ъ дх҃о́внѡ, цр҃ь же чꙋ́вственнѡ*): contrasting the spiritual fatherhood of the baptizer with the temporal, sensory (*чꙋ́вственнѡ*, Greek *αἰσθητῶς*) rule of the monarch.
[^354]: *With his foremother Helen*: Princess Olha of Kyiv, who took the Christian name Helen (*Олена* / *Елена*) at her baptism in Constantinople.
[^355]: *Roman and David*: the baptismal Christian names of the Holy Passion-bearers Boris and Gleb.
[^356]: *Unto our king victories over heathen enemies* (*коро́лю же на́шемꙋ пода́ти побѣ̑ды на пога́ныѧ врагѝ*): preserving the diplomatic Ruthenian ink witness of the 1761 Pochaiv Menaion printed in the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth under the reign of King Augustus III of Poland, contrasting with Russian Synodal editions which read *царю нашему* or *императору*.
[^357]: *The wild branches cut off* (*вѣ́тви сѣкꙋ́щыѧ вседи́чныѧ*): alluding to Rom. 11:17–24, the cutting down and grafting of wild olive branches, signifying the pruning away of pagan idolatry.
[^358]: *Unto our king... and victories over the heathen*: repeating the official royal petition for the reigning Polish monarch in the Pochaiv edition.
[^359]: *The Christ-loving king, and the multitude of his subjects* (*хрⷭ҇толюби́вомꙋ королю̀, и҆ мно́жествꙋ владо́мыхъ*): the Pochaiv Troparion concluding petition adapted for the Polish-Lithuanian Crown and its subjects.
[^360]: *Praising thee as his forefather*: referring to the traditional claim of the Kings of Poland and Grand Dukes of Lithuania of dynastic descent or titular inheritance connecting to the ancient Kyivan Rurikid grand princes.
[^361]: *Cast off the disease of the eyes*: alluding to the Kyivan Primary Chronicle account of Prince Volodymyr’s miraculous recovery from sudden blindness upon entering the baptismal font in Chersonesus (Korsun), mirroring the Apostle Paul’s restoration of sight at his baptism by Ananias (Acts 9:18).
[^362]: *Tying him to a horse's tail*: recording the physical destruction of the chief pagan idol Perun in Kyiv, who was dragged at horse-tail down the Borychiv descent to the Dnipro River, as recorded in the Primary Chronicle.
[^363]: *Raised up a divine church in her name*: referring to the famous Church of the Tithes (*Десятинна церква*) in Kyiv, dedicated to the Dormition of the Mother of God, built by Grand Prince Volodymyr.
