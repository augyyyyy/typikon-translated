[^365]: Refers to Holy Princess Olga (baptized Helen, feast July 11), grandmother of St. Vladimir, who received baptism in Constantinople.
[^366]: Slavonic: *рѡссі́йскїй цр҃ю васі́лїе* (Rus' king/tsar Basil). In East Slavic and Byzantine liturgical panegyric, *tsar* (caesar/basileus) was frequently applied to sovereign Christian monarchs, and Basil was Vladimir's baptismal name, received from his sponsor, Byzantine Emperor Basil II.
[^367]: Isaiah 2:2 / Micah 4:1 (LXX): "In the last days the mountain of the Lord shall be manifest, and the house upon the tops of the mountains."
[^368]: Refers to the Church of the Tithes (Desyatynna) erected by St. Vladimir on the Starokyivska Hill in Kyiv, consecrated in honor of the Dormition of the Mother of God in 996.
[^369]: Allusion to the messianic prophecy of Isaiah 7:14.
[^370]: Slavonic: *хрⷭ҇толюби́вагѡ королѧ̀* (the Christ-loving king). The Pochaiv 1761 edition, printed by the Basilian Fathers within the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth during the reign of Augustus III, retains the Ruthenian Greek Catholic petition for the King of Poland.
[^371]: Slavonic: *въ ко́рсꙋнѣ* (in Cherson/Korsun). The text explicitly names the Crimean city where Prince Vladimir was instructed and baptized.
[^372]: Psalm 111:2 (LXX): "The generation of the upright shall be blessed."
[^373]: Refers to Vladimir's sons, the holy martyrs Boris and Gleb (baptized Roman and David).
[^374]: Refers to Kyiv as the "Mother of Rus' cities" (*Мати градомъ русьскимъ*, Primary Chronicle s.a. 882) and the Church of the Tithes (*Desyatynna*), consecrated to the Dormition of the Theotokos, where the relics of St. Vladimir and St. Olga were translated and enshrined.
[^375]: Psalm 67:16 (LXX): "The mountain of God is a fertile mountain, a curdled mountain."
[^376]: The Church of the Tithes (*Desyatynna*) in Kyiv, founded by St. Vladimir in 989 and consecrated in 996.
[^377]: Roman and David are the Christian baptismal names of the holy passion-bearers Boris and Gleb.
[^378]: Luke 2:35 ("A sword shall pierce through thine own soul also").
[^379]: Refers to the hagiographical tradition of the Hieromartyr Athenogenes, bishop of Heracleopolis (or Sebaste in Armenia), whose hermitage was frequented by wild beasts, notably a hind which brought her fawn to receive the saint's blessing before his martyrdom under Diocletian.
