[^327]: The Synaxis of the Holy Archangel Gabriel is celebrated on July 13 (as well as on March 26, the day following the Annunciation). Commemorated with him is our Venerable Father Stephen the Sabaite (725–794/807), hymnographer and monk of the Great Lavra of Saint Sabas.
[^328]: Church Slavonic *къ первоенно́вномꙋ* represents a typographer's metathesis for *первовино́вномꙋ* (unto the First Cause / Author of all).
[^329]: Church Slavonic *мольбꙋ̀ ѿтря́съ*: the Slavonic *мольба* translates Greek τύρβη / θόρυβος (worldly turmoil, cares, or anxieties), hence "shaking off worldly turmoil".
[^330]: The Greek acrostic reads Ὡς παμμέγιστον Γαβριὴλ μέλπω τάχα ("I sing of Gabriel, the truly greatest"). The Slavonic text reflects this as *Ꙗ҆́кѡ всечⷭтна гаврїи́ла хвалю̀* ("As all-honorable I praise Gabriel").
[^331]: The phrase "activity... and vision" translates the classical ascetical dichotomy of *πρᾶξις* (ascetical labor) and *θεωρία* (spiritual contemplation / divine vision).
[^332]: The Kontakion closes with a liturgical adaptation of Romans 8:31: *са́мъ бꙋ́ди помо́щникъ, и҆ никто́же на ны̀* ("Be thou our helper, and none shall be against us!").
[^333]: Church Slavonic *свѣтонаро́чникꙋ* represents a typographical slip for *свѣтонача́льникꙋ* (Author / Source of Light, Greek φῶς ἀρχηγόν).
[^334]: Scriptural allusion to Luke 15:7, 10: "joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repents".
[^335]: A hymnographic wordplay upon the name *Stephen* (Greek Στέφανος, *Stephanos*, meaning "crown" or "wreath"): "Christ the Life-Giver hath placed a goodly crown upon thy head, O namesake Stephen (*тезоимени́те стефа́не*)".
[^336]: Church Slavonic *Королю́ же да́рꙋй побѣди́тєлнаѧ* ("And unto the King grant victories"): The Pochaiv Lavra edition explicitly preserves the supplication for the Polish-Lithuanian monarch (*rex* / *король*), reflecting the historical civic and ecclesiastical context of Western Ruthenia prior to the partitions of Poland.
