# APPENDIX: RESURRECTIONAL THEOTOKIA FOR THE EIGHT TONES

*Resurrectional Theotokia for the Eight Tones.*[^657]

*Tone 1. Theotokion [Dogmatikon at "Lord, I have cried"]:*
Let us hymn Mary the Virgin, the glory of the whole world, who blossomed forth from mankind and gave birth unto the Master, the gate of heaven, the song of the bodiless hosts, and the adornment of the faithful! For she was shown forth as heaven and the temple of the Godhead; she, having broken down the middle wall of enmity, brought in peace and opened the Kingdom. Having her, therefore, as the confirmation of faith, we possess as champion the Lord Who was born of her. Be of good courage, therefore, be of good courage, O people of God; for He shall vanquish the enemies, as Almighty!

*At the Aposticha, Theotokion:*
Lo, the prophecy of Isaiah is fulfilled;[^658] for thou, a virgin, didst give birth, and after childbirth didst remain as before childbirth, for God was He Who was born; therefore nature is newly transformed. But O Mother of God, despise not the entreaties of thy servants offered unto thee in thy temple; but as thou bearest the Merciful One in thine arms, have compassion upon thy servants, and entreat that our souls may be saved.

*Tone 2. Theotokion [Dogmatikon at "Lord, I have cried"]:*
The shadow of the law has passed away, now that grace is come; for as the bush burned not when enveloped in fire,[^659] so as a virgin thou didst give birth, and a virgin didst remain. In place of the pillar of fire, the Sun of righteousness has shone forth; in place of Moses, Christ, the salvation of our souls!

*At the Aposticha, Theotokion:*
O new wonder, greater than all ancient wonders! For who has known a mother who gave birth without a husband, and carried in her arms Him Who upholds all creation? It is the counsel of God that was born. Whom as an Infant having borne in thine arms, O most pure one, and possessing maternal boldness toward Him, cease not to entreat for those who honor thee, that He may have mercy and save our souls.

*Tone 3. Theotokion [Dogmatikon at "Lord, I have cried"]:*
How shall we not marvel at thy God-man childbirth, O most pure one? For without having received the experience of man, O all-undefiled one, thou didst give birth in the flesh unto the Son without a father, Who was born of the Father without a mother before the ages; Who in no wise underwent change, or confusion, or division[^660] *[Fol. 159v]*
...nature, but preserved whole the proper character of each nature.[^661] Therefore, O Virgin Mother, Sovereign Lady Birthgiver of God, entreat Him that our souls may be saved, who in an orthodox manner confess thee to be the Birthgiver of God.

*At the Aposticha, Theotokion:*
Without seed, of the Divine Spirit and by the will of the Father, thou didst conceive the Son of God, Who before the ages was of the Father without a mother; and for our sake, having come to be without a father from thee, thou didst bear Him in the flesh and didst nourish Him as an Infant with milk. Therefore cease not to entreat that our souls may be delivered from tribulations.

*Tone 4. Theotokion [Dogmatikon at "Lord, I have cried"]:*
David, the forefather of God and prophet, for thy sake melodiously proclaimed concerning thee unto Him Who did great things for thee: "The Queen stood at Thy right hand."[^662] For God, Who was well-pleased to become man of thee without a father, showed thee forth as the Mother and Mediatress of Life, that He might renew His own image that had been corrupted by passions; and having found the sheep that had gone astray upon the mountains, taking it upon His shoulders,[^663] He might bring it unto the Father, and according to His own will might unite it with the heavenly powers, and Christ, Who possesses great and rich mercy, might save the world, O Birthgiver of God.

*At the Aposticha, Theotokion:*
Look down upon the entreaties of thy servants, O all-undefiled one, putting down the cruel risings against us, delivering us from every affliction; for thee alone do we have as a firm and sure confirmation, and we have acquired thy protection, that we may not be put to shame, O Birthgiver of God, who call upon thee. Make haste unto the supplication of those who faithfully cry unto thee: Rejoice, O Sovereign Lady, help of all, joy, protection, and salvation of our souls!

*Tone 5. Theotokion [Dogmatikon at "Lord, I have cried"]:*
In the Red Sea the image of the unwedded Bride was depicted of old: there Moses was the divider of the water, here Gabriel was the minister of the wonder; then Israel walked through the deep dryshod, now the Virgin gave birth unto Christ without seed; the sea after the passage of Israel remained impassable, the undefiled one after the childbirth of Emmanuel remained incorrupt. Thou Who art, and Who wast before, and hast appeared as man: O God, have mercy on us!

*At the Aposticha, Theotokion:*
Thou art a temple and a gate, a palace and a royal throne, O all-honored Virgin, through whom my Deliverer, Christ the Lord, shone forth unto those sleeping in darkness, being the Sun of righteousness, desiring to enlighten those whom He fashioned according to His own image with His hand. Therefore, O all-hymned one, as having acquired maternal boldness toward Him *[Fol. 160r]*
...cease not to entreat that our souls may be saved.

*Tone 6. Theotokion [Dogmatikon at "Lord, I have cried"]:*
Who shall not call thee blessed, O all-holy Virgin? Or who shall not sing of thy most pure childbirth? For the only-begotten Son, Who shone timelessly from the Father, the same came forth from thee, pure one, ineffably incarnate; being God by nature, and having become man by nature for our sake; not divided into two persons, but recognized in two natures without confusion. Entreat Him, O pure and all-blessed one, that our souls may have mercy.

*At the Aposticha, Theotokion:*
My Creator and Deliverer, O most pure one, Christ the Lord, having come forth from thy womb, clothed Himself in me, and set Adam free from the first curse. Therefore unto thee, O all-pure one, as the Mother and Virgin of God in truth, we cry unceasingly with the angel's "Rejoice!": Rejoice, O Sovereign Lady, protection, shelter, and salvation of our souls!

*Tone 7. Theotokion [Dogmatikon at "Lord, I have cried"]:*
Thou wast known as a mother beyond nature, O Birthgiver of God, and didst remain a virgin beyond word and understanding; and the tongue cannot tell the wonder of thy childbirth; for since the conception was most glorious, O pure one, the manner of the childbirth is incomprehensible; for where God wills, the order of nature is overcome. Therefore, knowing thee as Mother of God, all of us diligently entreat thee: pray that our souls may be saved.

*At the Aposticha, Theotokion:*
Fleeing beneath thy protection, O Sovereign Lady, all we earth-born cry unto thee: O Birthgiver of God, our hope, deliver us from our measureless transgressions, and save our souls!

*Tone 8. Theotokion [Dogmatikon at "Lord, I have cried"]:*
The King of heaven through His love for mankind appeared upon earth, and lived among men; for taking flesh of the pure Virgin, and having come forth from her with the assumed nature, the Son is One, twofold in nature, but not in hypostasis. Therefore, proclaiming Him truly as perfect God and perfect man, we confess Christ our God: Whom entreat, O unwedded Mother, that our souls may have mercy.

*At the Aposticha, Theotokion:*
O unwedded Virgin, who ineffably didst conceive God in the flesh, Mother of God Most High: accept the entreaties of thy servants, O all-undefiled one, granting unto all the cleansing of transgressions; receiving now our supplications, pray that all of us may be saved.

*[Fol. 160v]*

### Notes

[^657]: The Resurrectional Theotokia (Dogmatika) of the Eight Tones (*Dogmatiki*), traditionally attributed to St. John of Damascus, sung at Saturday Great Vespers at "Lord, I have cried" and the Aposticha.
[^658]: Isa. 7:14; "Behold, a virgin shall conceive in the womb, and shall bring forth a Son, and thou shalt call His name Emmanuel."
[^659]: Ex. 3:2; the burning bush that was not consumed on Mount Horeb, prefiguring the virginity of the Mother of God before, during, and after childbirth.
[^660]: The Chalcedonian Christological definition (451 AD): Christ recognized in two natures, "without confusion, without change, without division, without separation" (*inconfuse, immutabiliter, indivise, inseparabiliter* / `непреложно, несмѣсно, нераздѣлно`).
[^661]: Preserving the proper character of each nature intact refers directly to the Fourth Ecumenical Council (Chalcedon, 451 AD), affirming that the union of the divine and human natures in Christ occurs without division and without confusion.
[^662]: Ps. 44:10 (LXX: Ps. 44:9); "The queen stood at thy right hand, clothed in vesture wrought with gold."
[^663]: Luke 15:4–5; the Parable of the Lost Sheep, where the Good Shepherd seeks the lost sheep in the mountains and lays it upon his shoulders.
