[^124]: At the conclusion of the Sunday Praises (Lauds) stichera Tone 5, the text transitions directly into the Sunday Beatitudes (*Makarizmoi*). The rubric 'Сла́, стїра є҆ѵ . И҆ нн҃ѣ , Пребл҃гве́нна' cues the Morning Gospel Stichera (*Eothinon*) and the standard Sunday Theotokion 'Преблагословенна еси, Богородице Дево' before the Great Doxology, followed by the Beatitudes ('Внеде́ли бл҃женны').

[^125]: The Sunday evening office at Vespers ('Внеде́лю ве́черъ , на Гдⷭ҇и возва́хъ') initiates the weekday cycle of Tone 5 with Penitential Stichera ('стихи̑ры покаѧ́нны') sung to the prosomoion model ('Подо́бенъ') 'Ра́дꙋйсѧ по́стническїи' (*Rejoice, thou bearer of the ascetic life*). The running header on Fol. 119v exhibits the printer's erratum 'Поклѧ́ны' for 'Покаѧ́нны'.

[^126]: On Monday liturgical services (commencing at Sunday Vespers), the Octoechos pairs penitential hymns with stichera dedicated to the Bodiless Powers (Holy Angels), reflecting the weekly liturgical allocation of Monday to the angelic hosts. Both sets of stichera share the prosomoion model 'Ра́дꙋйсѧ по́стническїи'.

[^127]: The Sunday evening Aposticha ('На̀ стихо́внѣ') features two penitential stichera followed by a Martyrion ('Мч҃нченъ') and a Theotokion ('Бг҃ородиченъ'), marking the traditional evening transition into the Monday liturgical remembrance of both repentance and the holy martyrs alongside the angelic powers.

[^128]: Folio 123r opens Tone 6 ('Гласъ Шестый') with a monumental Renaissance woodcut headpiece and ornamental calligraphic *vyaz* heading 'ВЪ КНИГИ ВОСКРЕСНЫ' (*In the Resurrection Book*), initiating the Sunday cycle of Lord, I have cried stichera (*Stichera Anastasima*). Folio 122v concludes Tone 5 with an elaborate woodcut cartouche tailpiece depicting a cherubic mascaron, below which 17th-century cursive ink marginalia records an early liturgical reader's inscription.

[^129]: The Anatolian or Eastern Stichera ('Стїхи̑ры восто́чны') of Tone 6 follow the primary Resurrection Stichera at Sunday Vespers (*Lord, I have cried*). Folio 124r culminates in the famous Dogmatic Theotokion (*Dogmatikon*) of Tone 6 attributed to St. John of Damascus ('Кто̀ тебѐ не бл҃жи́тъ'), which articulates the Chalcedonian Christological dogma of the two unconfused natures in the single Person of Christ.

[^130]: The Sunday Aposticha of Tone 6 opens with the venerable Paschal hymn 'Воскресе́нїе твоѐ хр҃тѐ сп҃се' (*Thy Resurrection, O Christ Savior, the angels hymn in the heavens*), which also serves universally across Byzantine and Ruthenian rites as the processional troparion sung during the Paschal midnight procession before Matins.

[^131]: The Resurrection Dismissal Troparion of Tone 6 ('А҆́ггелскїѧ си̑лы на гро́бѣ твое́мъ') encapsulates the Easter morning Gospel events (the soldiers fainting, Mary Magdalene seeking Christ's body, and Hades overthrown). This is followed on Fol. 125v by the Sunday Midnight Office Canon to the Holy Trinity ('На полꙋ́нощницн , Кано́нъ прест҃ѣ́й живонача́льнѣй Тр҃ци'), highlighting the trinitarian theology of St. Paul citing Romans 11:36.

[^132]: Folio 126v contains the Trinity Sessional Hymn (*Троичен Седален*) of Tone 6 modeled on the prosomoion 'Еже о нас исполнив смотрение' (*Having fulfilled the dispensation for our sake*), borrowing the scriptural assurance of Romans 8:31: *If God be for us, who can be against us?* ('буди с нами, и никтоже на ны'). The text attributes this same consolation to the Theotokos in the corresponding Theotokion.

[^133]: The Trinity Canon of Sunday Midnight Office continues through Odes 5 and 6 on Fol. 127v–128r. The running header shifts on Fol. 127v to 'Тро́йчны' (*Triadika / Trinitarian hymns*), denoting the dedicated midnight trinitarian genre, before reverting to 'Гла́съ , ѕ҃ .' on Fol. 128r.
