In the third Sunday Praises sticheron on Fol. 68v, the dramatic Easter proclamation of the angel incorporates the synoptic resurrection narratives (Luke 24:5–6; Matthew 28:7), balancing epigraphic fidelity with the vivacity of the liturgical dialogue (`нѣ́сть здѣ̀, но воста̀ ꙗ҆́кѡ речѐ`).
[^75]: The sticheron on Fol. 69v employs the rare dual/collective expression `сꙋпрꙋ́гъ же живоно́сныхъ а҆́гг҃лъ` ("a pair of life-bearing angels", reflecting Luke 24:4 and John 20:12). The subsequent sticheron contrasts the enclosing of Christ in the tomb by the Jews with the penitent thief opening paradise with his confession (`разбо́йникъ же ѿве́рзе ꙗ҆зы́комъ ра́й`), affirming Christ's simultaneous suffering on the Cross and co-enthronement with the Father.
[^76]: A major codicological error occurs in the printed foliation at quire S (gathering signature `С`): the recto folio printed mark jumps from `ѯ҃є` (65) on Fol. 70r to `о҃а` (71) on Fol. 71r, skipping folios 66–70 in error, while the hymnographic text and quire continuity are completely intact. On Fol. 71r, the Sunday evening transition to Tone 3 Penitential Stichera is marked by a typographic band ornament, opening the penitential cycle of the week.
[^77]: In the stichera to the Holy Bodiless Angels on Fol. 72r (appointed for Sunday evening Vespers anticipating Monday, dedicated to the angelic hosts), the angels are designated as "secondary lights" (`вторы́ѧ свѣ́тлости`), directly echoing the Areopagitic celestial hierarchy wherein the angelic orders reflect the primary, uncreated light of the Holy Trinity unto the created cosmos.
[^78]: Tone 4 opens on Fol. 73r (printed `о҃г` [73], recte 68) beneath a monumental foliate woodcut headpiece across the full width of the text block and a large ornamental initial `Ж`, preceded by the calligraphic title inscription `ВЪ КНИГИ ВОСКРЕСНЫ, Гла́съ , Четве́ртый` ("In the Books of the Resurrection, Tone 4"). The first sticheron celebrates the restoration of fallen human nature and the reopened ascent to heaven accomplished by the Cross.
[^79]: In the sticheron upon Fol. 74r, the typesetter set `ве́рєѧ стертерла́зъ є҆сѝ`, an apparent dittographic typographical slip for `ве́рєѧ стерлъ є҆сѝ` ("Thou hast crushed the bars [of Hades]", Psalm 106:16 / LXX Ps. 106:16), celebrating Christ's shattering of the brazen gates and iron bars of the netherworld.
[^80]: The Dogmatic Theotokion of Tone 4 on Fol. 74v (`Иже тебѐ ра́ди бг҃оѻ҆тц҃ъ пррⷦ҇къ Дв҃дъ`) articulates the royal Davidic typology (Psalm 44:10 / LXX Ps. 44:10: "The queen stood at Thy right hand") alongside the Parable of the Lost Sheep (Luke 15:4–6), depicting Christ lifting the fallen, wolf-ravaged sheep upon His shoulders to present it unto the Father.
[^81]: Fol. 76r opens the Sunday Midnight Office with the Canon to the Holy and Life-Originating Trinity in Tone 4, attributed to Metrophanes of Smyrna. The hymnography is densely dogmatic, employing precise Trinitarian terminology (`Тр҃цꙋ бг҃онача́льнꙋ дапросла́вимъ соста́вы . є҆ди́нственною же є҆стество́мъ тріе́хъ , соприсносꙋ́щнꙋ сопресто́льнꙋ`), asserting the co-eternity and consubstantiality of the three Hypostases.
[^82]: In Ode 3 of the Trinity Canon (Fol. 76v), the Hospitality of Abraham (Genesis 18) is interpreted as a manifestation of the Trinitarian Unity (`Патрїа́рхꙋ А҆враа́мꙋ є҆гда̀ ꙗ҆ви́сѧ воѡ́бразѣ мꙋ́жествѣ , тро́ичнаѧ є҆ди́нице`), affirming the patristic reading of the three angelic visitors as an epiphany of the tri-personal God.
[^83]: In Ode 5 of the Trinity Canon (Fol. 77v–78r), the poet invokes the seraphic contemplation of the divine majesty (`Разꙋмѣва́ше ѡ҆́бѣкры вседѣ́тельнаго бж҃тва̀`), employing the radiant invocation `Свѣ́те трисли́чне` ("O thrice-radiant Light"), beseeching illumination from the uncreated Fountain of light to behold the ineffable beauty of God.
