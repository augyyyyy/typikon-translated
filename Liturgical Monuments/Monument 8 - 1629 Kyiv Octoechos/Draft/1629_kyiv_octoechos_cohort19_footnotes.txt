[^185]: In Fol. 179v–180r (Pages 182–183), the Sunday Midnight Office Canon to the Holy Trinity reaches Ode 9, concluding with a petition for ecclesiastical unity and peace ('и҆ цр҃ква́мъ соединенїе'). The rubrics then transfer directly to Sunday Matins, referencing the Troparion from Saturday Great Vespers.
[^186]: In Fol. 180v–181r (Pages 183–184), the Sessional hymn Theotokion is the renowned megalynarion 'All creation rejoices in thee' ('Ѡ҆ тебѣ̀ ра́дꙋетсѧ...'), attributed to St. John of Damascus and sung in the Liturgy of St. Basil the Great. This is immediately followed by the Tone 8 Sunday Hypakoe ('Мѵроно́сица живода́вномꙋ...') and Antiphon 1 of the Anabathmoi (Ascents/Degrees).
[^187]: In Fol. 181v–182r (Pages 184–185), Antiphons 2, 3, and 4 of the Tone 8 Anabathmoi (Ascents) feature the distinctive Ruthenian Church Slavonic pneumatological strophes, confessing the Holy Spirit as co-enthroned ('сопресто́ленъ') and the peace-bestowing Lord consubstantial with the Father and the Son ('миропода́теленъ бг҃ъ бо, є҆́сть ѻ҆ц҃ꙋ̀ и҆ сн҃ꙋ є҆диносꙋ́щенъ гдⷭ҇ь'). The Prokeimenon follows Psalm 145:10 (LXX).
[^188]: In Fol. 182v–183r (Pages 185–186), the Matins canons for Tone 8 unfold in three distinct poetic streams: the Resurrection Canon ('Воѡрꙋже́нна фараѡ́на...'), the Cross-Resurrection Canon ('Во́дꙋ проше́...'), and the Canon to the Theotokos ('Пое́мъ гв҃и...'). At the bottom of Fol. 183r, the typographer marks a new gathering with signature 'Ѕ ѕ' (Cyrillic Dzelo = 6).
[^189]: In Fol. 183v–184r (Pages 186–187), the Sunday Canon Ode 3 draws on Matthew 12:29 and Luke 1:79 in depicting Christ binding the 'strong one' (Hades/Satan) and plundering his vessels ('свои́мъ бж҃твомъ крѣ́пкаго свѧза̀, и҆ совꙋ́ды є҆го̀ расхи́тилъ є҆́стъ'). The Cross-Resurrection ode then explores the Christological condescension of the Logos taking on Adam's form in obedience.
[^190]: In Fol. 184v–185r (Pages 187–188), Ode 4 of the Tone 8 Sunday Canon incorporates the Habakkuk canticle theme (Habakkuk 3:2 LXX) and the kenotic theology of Philippians 2:7 ('и҆стоща́нїемъ стра́ннымъ сни́де на зе́млю'). The rubric at the foot of Fol. 185r ('И҆́нъ І҆рмо́. То́.') indicates that the accompanying Theotokos canon shares the same heirmic melody.
[^191]: In Fol. 185v (Page 188, left), the text prints 'Бꙋ́рѧ тѧ̀ всечтⷭ҇аѧ... сл҃нце бо родила̀ є҆сѝ пра́вды' where 'Бꙋ́рѧ' represents an editorial or typesetting corruption for 'Зарѧ̀' (dawn/aurora), corresponding to the Greek 'Ὄρθрон σε πανύμνητε...' herald of the Sun of Righteousness (Malachi 4:2). Fol. 186r continues with the Isaiah canticle theme (Isaiah 26:9 LXX) in Ode 5.
[^192]: In Fol. 186v–187r (Pages 189–190), Ode 6 embodies the Jonah canticle archetype (Jonah 2:2–9 LXX). At the foot of Fol. 187r, the gathering transitions to signature 'З з' (Cyrillic Zemlya = 7). The Cross-Resurrection troparion contrasts Christ stretching His hands upon the Cross with Adam's outstretched hand reaching intemperately for the forbidden tree in Eden.
[^193]: In Fol. 187v–188r (Pages 190–191), Ode 7 of the Sunday Matins Canon opens with the Three Holy Children in the Babylonian Furnace (Daniel 3 LXX). The typographer sets 'Пѣ́нь Се́маѧ, І҆рмо́съ' on Fol. 187v, exhibiting haplography for 'Пѣ́снь Седма́ѧ'. The leaf closes mid-sentence on the Triadikon strophe on Fol. 188r with signature 'З з в҃' and catchword 'юще' (for 'вѣ́дающе').
