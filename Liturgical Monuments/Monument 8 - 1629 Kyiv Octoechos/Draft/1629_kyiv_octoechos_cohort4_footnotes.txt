[^34]: Typology of the Exodus in the Dogmatikon of Tone 2 (*Preide sen' zakonnaya*): The hymn explicitly contrasts the shadow of the Mosaic Law with the grace of the Gospel, drawing upon the pillar of fire (Exodus 13:21–22) that led the Israelites through the wilderness, which is fulfilled and superseded by Christ, the spiritual Sun of righteousness (Malachi 4:2 [LXX 3:20]). In place of Moses the lawgiver stands Christ the Savior, effecting the redemption of mankind.
[^35]: The Aposticha Stichera of the Resurrection, Tone 2 (*Voskreseniye Tvoye, Khriste Spase*): These four hymns, traditionally attributed to St. John of Damascus, celebrate the cosmic victory of the Cross over the tree of the Garden of Eden (Genesis 3:1–6), the harrowing of Hades, the piercing of the Lord's side by the soldier's spear (John 19:34), and the empty tomb sealed by the Judean authorities (Matthew 27:62–66).
[^36]: Theotokion of the Aposticha (*O chudese novago vsekho drevnikho chudes* / "O wonder new beyond all wonders of old!"): Composed by St. John of Damascus, this venerable hymn ponders the mystery of the Virgin Mother holding in her maternal embrace the uncircumscribed God Who sustains all creation in the palm of His hand. It affirms the orthodox dogma of the Theotokos as defined at the Council of Ephesus (431 AD) and the Council of Chalcedon (451 AD).
[^37]: Sunday Midnight Office (*Mesonyktikon*), Canon to the Most Holy and Undivided Trinity (*Kanon Troichen*), Tone 2: Composed by Metrophanes, Metropolitan of Smyrna (c. 820–880; author of the Triadikos Canons of the Octoechos). The canon provides an exhaustive patristic and scholastic synthesis of Trinitarian theology, systematically articulating the consubstantiality (*homoousion*), co-eternity, and unconfused hypostatic properties of the Father (the unbegotten Source and Cause), the Son (begotten before all ages), and the Holy Spirit (eternally proceeding from the Father).
[^38]: Sessional Hymn to the Holy Trinity, Tone 2, Ode 3 (*Svyataya Troitse, pomilui ikhzhe sozda*): Chanted according to the melodic model (*prosomoion*) *Vyshnikh ishcha* ("Seeking the things on high"), entreating the consubstantial Trinity to deliver creation from fire and condemnation and grant forgiveness of sins.
[^39]: Canon to the Holy Trinity, Ode 5, Troparion 2: Moses and the vision in the Burning Bush (Exodus 3:2–14). The hymn incorporates the traditional patristic christology (patristic consensus of St. Justin Martyr, St. Irenaeus of Lyons, and St. Athanasius the Great) identifying the "Angel of the Lord" who spoke from the flame as the pre-incarnate Logos, the "Angel of Great Counsel" (LXX Isaiah 9:6), revealing the tri-hypostatic nature of the one divine Principle.
[^40]: Sunday Matins Sessional Hymns of the Resurrection, Tone 2, after the First Kathisma: *Blagoobraznyi Iosif* ("The noble Joseph..."), the classic troparion of Great and Holy Saturday (commemorating Joseph of Arimathea taking down Christ's Body, wrapping it in a clean winding sheet, and placing it in a new rock-hewn sepulcher; Matthew 27:57–60, Mark 15:43–46), used here in the Sunday Resurrection cycle to proclaim the Lord's third-day rising.
[^41]: Hypakoe of Tone 2 (*Po strasti shedshe na grob* / "After the Passion, the women having gone unto the tomb"): The Hypakoe (from the Greek *hypakoe*, "obedience" or "listening attentively") recounts the holy Myrrh-bearing women who went at early dawn to anoint the Lord's sacred Body with aromatic spices, beholding the angelic messengers who declared that Christ is not among the dead (Mark 16:1–8, Luke 24:1–10).
[^42]: The Gradual Hymns (*Anavathmoi*) of Tone 2: Structured into three antiphons inspired by the Old Testament Psalms of Ascents (Psalms 119–133 [LXX 118–132]) sung by Hebrew pilgrims journeying up to Jerusalem. Each antiphon concludes with a pneumatological troparion (*Svyatomu Dukhu...*) extolling the Holy Spirit as the sovereign Principle of life, wisdom, and sanctification alongside the Father and the Son.
[^43]: Canons of Sunday Matins, Tone 2: The classic Byzantine-Ruthenian tri-canon structure prescribed for the Resurrection Matins in the Octoechos: (1) The Canon of the Resurrection by St. John of Damascus (*Vo glubine potopi drevle* / "Of old, the all-armed power drowned..."); (2) The Canon of the Cross (*Netrennuyu neobychuyu* / "Traversing an untrodden, unaccustomed path..."); and (3) The Canon of the Most Holy Theotokos to the same heirmos model.
