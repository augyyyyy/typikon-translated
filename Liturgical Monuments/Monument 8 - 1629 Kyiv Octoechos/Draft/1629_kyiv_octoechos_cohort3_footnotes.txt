[^23]: Canon of the Cross, Ode 7, Heirmos: *Sotrotsi vo blagochestii vospitani* ("The youths reared in piety..."), rooted in the biblical canticle of the Three Holy Children in the Babylonian furnace (Daniel 3:19–24 [LXX Daniel 3:51–90]). The first troparion explicitly contrasts the earth cursed through the innocent blood of Abel shed by Cain (Genesis 4:10–11) with the earth blessed through sprinkling by Christ's God-flowing Blood (Hebrews 12:24).
[^24]: Canon of the Most Holy Theotokos, Ode 7: *v Adamovu kozhu obleksya* ("clothed Himself in the skin of Adam"). This draws upon the classic patristic interpretation of the "tunics of skins" (Genesis 3:21) bestowed upon the fallen protoplasts, signifying mortal and passible human nature assumed without sin by the Incarnate Logos (cf. St. Gregory the Theologian, *Oration 38 on the Theophany*, 12; St. John of Damascus, *Exposition of the Orthodox Faith*, III.1).
[^25]: Epigraphic witness on Fol. 20r: The lower margin contains an identical 19th-century cursive ownership inscription to that found on Fol. 10r (Cohort 2): *Принадлежитъ библїотекѣ Троицкой Сергїевой Лавры. 1859 года* ("Belongs to the library of the Trinity-St. Sergius Lavra, year 1859"), corroborating the provenance of this copy in the collection of the Trinity-St. Sergius Lavra.
[^26]: Canon of the Cross, Ode 8: Typological imagery of Aaron's rod that budded without cultivation (Numbers 17:8 [LXX 17:23]), prefiguring the virgin birth of Christ without male seed.
[^27]: Canon of the Resurrection, Ode 9: The Burning Bush (*he batos* / *neopalimaya kupina*) seen by the Prophet Moses on Mount Horeb (Exodus 3:2–4) as the pre-eminent Old Testament type of the Virgin Mary, whose womb contained the uncreated fire of Divinity without being consumed.
[^28]: Rubric `Нахвали́те. Стїхи̑ры. Гла́съ, а҃.` (At the Praises / *Lauds*, Tone 1): The praises of Sunday Matins in Tone 1 comprise four Resurrection Stichera, followed by three Anatolian (*vostochny*) Stichera, and conclude with an apologetic Sticheron (*Lyubomyatezhnyi rode evreiskii* / "Give ear, O riot-loving Hebrew race...") confronting the guards' testimony and the empty tomb (Matthew 27:62–66, 28:11–15).
[^29]: Rubric `Внедѣ́л[ю]. Налїтꙋргі́и, Бл҃же́ны. Гла́съ, а҃.` (On Sunday at the Divine Liturgy: The Beatitudes, Tone 1): In the Ruthenian and Byzantine liturgical synthesis, the Makarismoi at the Divine Liturgy are interspersed with eight troparia (six Resurrection troparia followed by a Triadikon and a Theotokion), each punctuated by the refrains of the Beatitudes (Matthew 5:3–12) and the Good Thief's petition (Luke 23:42).
[^30]: Rubric `Внеⷣ вече́ръ. Стїхи̑ры Покаѧ́нны. Кѵръ, І҆ѡ́сифа` (Sunday Evening at Vespers: Penitential Stichera, by Kyr Joseph): Sunday evening Vespers inaugurates the liturgical week with penitential (*katanyctic*) hymns alongside commemorations of the Bodiless Heavenly Hosts (traditionally assigned to Monday). The stichera are authored by St. Joseph the Hymnographer (c. 816–886). Note also the typographical error on Fol. 25r: `Чл҃коче́нче` for `Чл҃колю́бче`.
[^31]: Stichera to the Bodiless Powers, Tone 1: The hymnography systematically incorporates the nine orders of the celestial hierarchy derived from the corpus of Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite (*De Coelesti Hierarchia*), invoking Angels, Archangels, Principalities, Thrones, Dominions, Seraphim, Cherubim, Powers, and Authorities.
[^32]: Primary Landmark: Tone 2 (*Гласъ Второй*) opens on Fol. 27r with a monumental full-page ornamental woodcut headpiece bearing the inscription `ВЪ КНИГИ ОКТѠИХЪ ВСКРⷭ҇НЪ` and the explicit attribution `Творе́нїе І҆ѡа́нна . Дамаски́на` (Composed by John Damascene). It contains the first cycle of Saturday evening Great Vespers Stichera at *Lord, I have cried* (*Pervovechnomu ot Otsa* / "Come, let us worship God the Word...").
[^33]: Theotokion / Dogmatikon of Tone 2 at Saturday Great Vespers (*Preide sen' zakonnaya, blagodati prishedshi* / "The shadow of the Law passed away when grace arrived..."): St. John of Damascus's classic theological hymn expounding the transition from Old Testament types and shadows to New Testament grace and the reality of the Incarnation.
