[^174]: Folios 168v–169r conclude the Sunday Praises/Lauds in Tone 7 and open the Sunday Beatitudes (*Блаженны*). Fol. 168v articulates the Christological Stone typology, conflating Psalm 117:22 LXX ("The stone which the builders rejected, the same is become the head of the corner") and Isaiah 28:16 with the apocalyptic imagery of Daniel 2:34–45 (the stone cut out of the mountain without human hand prefiguring the virginal conception) and Daniel 7:13–14 (the Son of Man coming on the clouds of heaven unto the Ancient of Days). Fol. 169r introduces the Sunday Beatitudes in Tone 7, contrasting the fatal tree of knowledge in Eden with Christ as the Tree of Life whose fruit brings immortality, and invoking the penitent thief (Luke 23:42).
[^175]: Folios 169v–170r conclude the Sunday Beatitudes in Tone 7 and open Sunday Evening Vespers (for Monday). Fol. 169v features a dogmatic Triadikon emphasizing the identity of essence and distinction of hypostases ("Light is the Father, Light is the Son the Word, Light is the Holy Spirit; yet one Light is the Trinity, for God is one in three Persons"), followed by a Theotokion celebrating human deification through the Incarnation (*обожившеся тебе ради*). Fol. 170r begins Sunday Evening Vespers with compunctious Penitential Stichera at "Lord, I have cried" to the Podoben *Днесь бдитъ Іюда* (*Today Judas watches*), urging the soul to marvel at God's cosmic condescension in descending to earth to raise mankind from sinful misery.
[^176]: Folios 170v–171r encompass the Sunday Evening Vespers (Monday cycle) of Tone 7, featuring stichera to the Holy Archangels and Bodiless Hosts (as Monday is liturgically dedicated to the angelic orders). Following the Areopagitic hierarchy (*On the Celestial Hierarchy*), the Archangels are venerated as "secondary lights" (*свѣти вторіи*) shining by participation in the Primary Uncreated Light of the Trinity. At the Aposticha, the service transitions to the Lukan parables of repentance: the Prodigal Son (Luke 15:11–32) begging to be received as a hired servant, and the traveler beaten by thieves (Luke 10:30–37) seeking Christ the Physician of souls.
[^177]: Folios 171v–172r mark the transition between Tones 7 and 8. Fol. 171v concludes the weekly cycle of Tone 7 with a Marian Theotokion synthesizing major Old Testament types: the Bridal Chamber, the Closed Gate (Ezekiel 44:2), the Fiery Throne (Daniel 7:9), the Uncut Mountain (Daniel 2:34), and the Unburnt Bush (Exodus 3:2), sealed with an ornamental printer's palmette tailpiece. Fol. 172r opens Tone 8 with a primary Lavra woodcut headpiece across the page width, monumental Cyrillic ligatured vyaz lettering (*ВЪ КНИГИ ѠКТѠИХЪ ВСКРСНЪ / Гласъ О́смый*), and an ornate historiated initial 'В' introducing Saturday Great Vespers at "Lord, I have cried" (*Вечернюю пѣснь и словесную службу* / *An evening song and reasonable service* / Romans 12:1).
[^178]: Folios 172v–173r encompass the Anatolian (*Восточны*) Resurrection stichera at "Lord, I have cried" in Tone 8 and the monumental Saturday evening Dogmatikon (*Царь небесный* / *The King of heaven* / Baruch 3:38 LXX), traditionally attributed to St. John of Damascus. This Dogmatikon presents a definitive formula of Chalcedonian Christology, confessing Christ as "one Son, twofold in nature but not in hypostasis" (*единъ есть сынъ, сугубъ естествомъ а не составомъ*), truly perfect God and perfect man, born of the unwedded Virgin without human seed.
[^179]: Folios 173v–174r conclude Tone 8 Saturday Great Vespers with the Resurrection Aposticha and the Sunday Apolytikion/Troparion in Tone 8 (*С вышних сниде милосерде* / *Thou didst descend from the heights, O Compassionate One*). Fol. 173v articulates the patristic doctrine of Christ's triduum in death: Christ's divine hypostasis remained inseparably united to both His soul and His body upon the Cross, so that His rational human soul descended into Hades to despoil death while His flesh lay incorrupt in the tomb (cf. Psalm 15:10 LXX; Acts 2:27–31).
[^180]: Folios 174v–175r introduce the Sunday Midnight Office / Matins Canon to the Holy and Life-Originating Trinity in Tone 8 (Heirmos 1: *Вооруженна* / *Armed was Pharaoh* / Exodus 15:1–19). Fol. 175r incorporates the patristic interpretation of the Hospitality of Abraham at Mamre (Genesis 18:1–15), where the Patriarch receives the three mysterious visitors as an epiphany of the one Godhead in three divine Hypostases (*в трех поставех державу единственну разуме*). Ode 3 (Heirmos: *Утверждей небеса* / *Thou Who didst establish the heavens*) recalls Isaiah's vision of the Thrice-Holy Lord of Hosts upon His throne on high (Isaiah 6:1–3).
[^181]: Folios 175v–176r present the conclusion of Ode 3, the Sessional Hymn (*Седаленъ*) to the Holy Trinity (to the Podoben *Повеленное таинство* / *That which was commanded secretly*), and the opening of Ode 4 (Heirmos: *Ты ми Христе Господь* / *Thou art my Lord, O Christ* / Habakkuk 3:2 LXX). Fol. 175v articulates precise Nicene-Constantinopolitan Trinitarian dogma: the unbegotten Father (*нерожденный Отецъ*) eternally and incorruptibly begetting the Son as the radiance of His essence (*своего существа сіяніе*, Hebrews 1:3) and bringing forth the Holy Spirit as Light proceeding from Light (*Светъ от Света исходна*), consubstantial (*соестественъ*) and co-equal in honor.
[^182]: Folios 176v–177r encompass Ode 4 and Ode 5 of the Sunday Midnight Office / Matins Trinity Canon in Tone 8 (Heirmos 5: *Вскую мя отринул еси* / *Why hast Thou cast me off from Thy presence* / Psalm 87:14 LXX). Fol. 176v refers to the Virgin Mary as the "beauty of Jacob" (*Іаковлю доброту*, quoting Psalm 46:4 LXX) chosen by God from all ages. Fol. 177r expresses the classic Cappadocian dialectic of Trinitarian theology: distinguishing the Persons simply and uniting the Nature without confusion (*раздѣляюще просто, и совокупляюще*), followed by a heart-wrenching penitential stanza lamenting the soul's involuntary susceptibility to passion and spiritual alteration.
[^183]: Folios 177v–178r encompass Ode 6 (Heirmos: *Очисти мя Спасе* / *Cleanse me, O Savior* / Jonah 2:2–10) and the post-Ode 6 Sessional Hymns (*Седальны*) of the Sunday Midnight Office / Matins Trinity Canon in Tone 8. Fol. 178r presents a solemn dogmatic confession of the consubstantial Trinity, applying the negative adverbs of Chalcedon (*ἀσυγχύτως, ἀτρέπτως, ἀδιайρέτως* / *несліяннѣ, непревратнѣ, и нераздѣльнѣ*) to the divine Persons: confessing the unoriginate Father, the co-unoriginate Son, and the divine Spirit as one simple, holy, and co-equal Godhead worshipped alongside the heavenly hosts.
