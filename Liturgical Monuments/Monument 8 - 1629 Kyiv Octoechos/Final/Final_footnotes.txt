

## Cohort 1 Footnotes

[^1]: The Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra preface to the reader is composed in 17th-century vernacular Chancery Ruthenian (*prosta mova*), marked by Polish syntactic borrowings (*абысь*, *кождый*, *зачимъ*) and Ukrainian phonetic developments (*буди*, *матки*, *живаго*), serving as a didactic pastoral address distinct from the archaic Church Slavonic of the Octoechos hymnody.
[^2]: Ruthenian *v"yezdě* (въездѣ): denotes pastoral itinerary, travel, or episcopal/pastoral visitation across the parishes of the Kyivan metropolitanate, or alternately one's local parish settlement and liturgical station.
[^3]: Full-page woodcut frontispiece portraying St. John of Damascus (c. 676–749), hymnographer, Father of the Church, and compiler of the Octoechos. The inscribed text is Psalm 48:4 [LXX 48:3]: "My mouth shall speak wisdom, and the meditation of my heart shall be of understanding." The initials "K D" belong to the renowned Kyivan woodcut master active at the Pechersk Lavra press in the 1620s.
[^4]: The Saturday Great Vespers Dogmatikon in Tone 1 (*Vsemirnuyu slavu* / "The glory of the whole world") is traditionally attributed to St. John of Damascus, celebrating the mystery of the Incarnation and the Virgin as the Ark and gate of heaven.
[^5]: Epigraphic witness: The Kyiv 1629 imprint reads *iz" istlěniya* (изъ истлѣ́нїа / "out of corruption"), reflecting Greek ἐκ φθορᾶς, rather than the variant reading *ot" tleniya*.
[^6]: The Sunday Midnight Office (*Polunoshchnitsa*) features a dedicated Canon to the Most Holy and Undivided Trinity for each of the Eight Tones, chanted in monastic and cathedral practice prior to Sunday Matins.
[^7]: Melodic model (*podoben* / automelon): *Grob" tvoy* (Гро́бъ тво́й / "Thy Tomb, O Savior, the soldiers guarding..."), Tone 1, providing the rhythmic and strophic template for the Sessional Hymn (Kathisma / Sedalen).
[^8]: The Sessional Theotokion highlights the maternal bold intercession (*parrhesia*) of the Theotokos before the Son, urging believers to invoke her day and night to dispel despondency and spiritual lethargy.
[^9]: The Irmos of Ode 6 draws directly from the biblical prayer of Jonah in the belly of the sea monster (Jonah 2:3–7 [LXX Jonah 2:4–8]), an archetype of descent into Hades and divine deliverance.
[^10]: The phrase *na stado yezhe okradoste* (на ста́до є́же ѡкра́досте) in the Sedalen addresses the Trinity on behalf of the faithful flock that has been raided, scattered, or despoiled by spiritual adversaries (the demons).


## Cohort 2 Footnotes

[^11]: The Sunday Midnight Office (*Polunoshchnitsa*) dedicated Canon to the Holy and Undivided Trinity in Tone 1 concludes here with the completion of Ode 8 and the entirety of Ode 9, ending with a final Theotokion before the transition to Sunday Matins.
[^12]: Heading `НАЧА́ЛО ОУ́ТРЕННї` (Beginning of Matins): In early 17th-century Ruthenian printed Octoechoi, Sunday Matins is printed continuously following the Midnight Office. It opens with "God is the Lord" and the Resurrection Apolytikion of the Tone (*Kameni znamenanu* / "The stone being sealed..."), accompanied by a cross-reference rubric to Saturday Great Vespers where the full text is printed.
[^13]: Cursive 19th-century ownership inscription along the lower margin of Fol. 10r: *Принадлежитъ библїотекѣ Тр. Сергїевой Лавры 1859 года* ("Belongs to the library of the Trinity-Sergius Lavra, year 1859"), witnessing to the codex's historical preservation in the library of the Trinity-St. Sergius Lavra.
[^14]: The Sunday Hypakoe (*hypakoe* / у̀пакоѝ): *Razboinicheye pokayaniye* ("The repentance of the thief opened Paradise..."), chanted after the second Kathisma and Sedalen, reflecting early Jerusalem liturgical practice.
[^15]: The Hymns of Ascent (Anavathmoi / *Stepénna*), Tone 1, consisting of three antiphons based on the Gradual Psalms (Psalms 119–133 [LXX 118–132]), composed by St. Theodore the Studite (759–826). The second antiphon opens with an allusion to Psalm 120:1 [LXX 119:1]: *V hory tvoyikh voznesé mya zakon dobrodětelei* ("Unto the mountains of Thine ordinances did the law of virtues elevate me...").
[^16]: Trinitarian theology in the third antiphon: *yedinstvo bo yest' Tritsa yestestvom", a ne litsy* ("for the Trinity is one in essence, yet not in persons"), an exact Slavonic rendering of the orthodox Cappadocian formula (μία οὐσία, τρεῖς ὑποστάσεις / one essence, three hypostases).
[^17]: Sunday Canon of the Resurrection, Tone 1, traditionally attributed to St. John of Damascus. The Irmos of Ode 1 (*Tvoyá pobědítelnaya desnítsa* / "Thy victorious right hand...") is based on the First Biblical Canticle, the Song of Moses at the Red Sea (Exodus 15:1–19 [LXX 15:1–19]).
[^18]: The Kyiv 1629 Octoechos includes alongside the Resurrection Canon two complementary Sunday canons: the Canon to the Precious and Life-Giving Cross (*In" Kanon" Krestu*) and the Canon to the Most Holy Theotokos (*In" Kanon" Presvyatěi Bogoroditsě*), chanted conjointly at Sunday Matins.
[^19]: Dogmatic Theotokion of the Canon to the Cross: *Boga dshche zachalá yesi... v" dvoyu sushchestvu, znayemago sna* ("Thou didst conceive God, O daughter... the Only-begotten Son known in two natures"), expressing the Christological dogma of the Council of Chalcedon (451 AD).
[^20]: Epigraphic reading: *u Piláta isprosíste umobrědno* [Slavonic: оумобрѣ́дно / оумнобрѣ́дно], translating Greek ἐкφρόνως ("madly, frantically, out of your mind"), describing the irrational clamor of the crowd demanding Christ's crucifixion before Pontius Pilate.
[^21]: Canon to the Cross, Ode 4: *Kto yesí krasen" iz" Edéma... ot" vinograda vosoríka* ("Who art Thou that comest fair from Edom, and this redness of garments from the vineyard of Bozrah?"), drawing directly upon the messianic and prophetic vision of Isaiah 63:1–3 [LXX 63:1–3].
[^22]: Canon to the Cross, Ode 6: *Um" syi bezstrasten" i neshchestven", priměshayetsya Khs" Bg" umu chlcheskomu* ("Being the impassible and immaterial Mind, Christ God unites Himself to the human mind"), directly echoing St. Gregory the Theologian's soteriological doctrine (Ep. 101): "For that which He has not assumed He has not healed" (τὸ γὰρ ἀπρόσληπτον, ἀθεράпеυτον).


## Cohort 3 Footnotes

[^23]: Canon of the Cross, Ode 7, Heirmos: *Sotrotsi vo blagochestii vospitani* ("The youths reared in piety..."), rooted in the biblical canticle of the Three Holy Children in the Babylonian furnace (Daniel 3:19–24 [LXX Daniel 3:51–90]). The first troparion explicitly contrasts the earth cursed through the innocent blood of Abel shed by Cain (Genesis 4:10–11) with the earth blessed through sprinkling by Christ's God-flowing Blood (Hebrews 12:24).
[^24]: Canon of the Most Holy Theotokos, Ode 7: *v Adamovu kozhu obleksya* ("clothed Himself in the skin of Adam"). This draws upon the classic patristic interpretation of the "tunics of skins" (Genesis 3:21) bestowed upon the fallen protoplasts, signifying mortal and passible human nature assumed without sin by the Incarnate Logos (cf. St. Gregory the Theologian, *Oration 38 on the Theophany*, 12; St. John of Damascus, *Exposition of the Orthodox Faith*, III.1).
[^25]: Epigraphic witness on Fol. 20r: The lower margin contains an identical 19th-century cursive ownership inscription to that found on Fol. 10r (Cohort 2): *Принадлежитъ библїотекѣ Троицкой Сергїевой Лавры. 1859 года* ("Belongs to the library of the Trinity-St. Sergius Lavra, year 1859"), corroborating the provenance of this copy in the collection of the Trinity-St. Sergius Lavra.
[^26]: Canon of the Cross, Ode 8: Typological imagery of Aaron's rod that budded without cultivation (Numbers 17:8 [LXX 17:23]), prefiguring the virgin birth of Christ without male seed.
[^27]: Canon of the Resurrection, Ode 9: The Burning Bush (*he batos* / *neopalimaya kupina*) seen by the Prophet Moses on Mount Horeb (Exodus 3:2–4) as the pre-eminent Old Testament type of the Virgin Mary, whose womb contained the uncreated fire of Divinity without being consumed.
[^28]: Rubric `Нахвали́те. Стїхи̑ры. Гла́съ, а҃.` (At the Praises / *Lauds*, Tone 1): The praises of Sunday Matins in Tone 1 comprise four Resurrection Stichera, followed by three Anatolian (*vostochny*) Stichera, and conclude with an apologetic Sticheron (*Lyubomyatezhnyi rode evreiskii* / "Give ear, O riot-loving Hebrew race...") confronting the guards' testimony and the empty tomb (Matthew 27:62–66, 28:11–15).
[^29]: Rubric `Внедѣ́л[ю]. Налїтꙋргі́и, Бл҃же́ны. Гла́съ, а҃.` (On Sunday at the Divine Liturgy: The Beatitudes, Tone 1): In the Ruthenian and Byzantine liturgical synthesis, the Makarismoi at the Divine Liturgy are interspersed with eight troparia (six Resurrection troparia followed by a Triadikon and a Theotokion), each punctuated by the refrains of the Beatitudes (Matthew 5:3–12) and the Good Thief's petition (Luke 23:42).
[^30]: Rubric `Внеⷣ вече́ръ. Стїхи̑ры Покаѧ́нны. Кѵръ, І҆ѡ́сифа` (Sunday Evening at Vespers: Penitential Stichera, by Kyr Joseph): Sunday evening Vespers inaugurates the liturgical week with penitential (*katanyctic*) hymns alongside commemorations of the Bodiless Heavenly Hosts (traditionally assigned to Monday). The stichera are authored by St. Joseph the Hymnographer (c. 816–886). Note also the typographical error on Fol. 25r: `Чл҃коче́нче` for `Чл҃колю́бче`.
[^31]: Stichera to the Bodiless Powers, Tone 1: The hymnography systematically incorporates the nine orders of the celestial hierarchy derived from the corpus of Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite (*De Coelesti Hierarchia*), invoking Angels, Archangels, Principalities, Thrones, Dominions, Seraphim, Cherubim, Powers, and Authorities.
[^32]: Primary Landmark: Tone 2 (*Гласъ Второй*) opens on Fol. 27r with a monumental full-page ornamental woodcut headpiece bearing the inscription `ВЪ КНИГИ ОКТѠИХЪ ВСКРⷭ҇НЪ` and the explicit attribution `Творе́нїе І҆ѡа́нна . Дамаски́на` (Composed by John Damascene). It contains the first cycle of Saturday evening Great Vespers Stichera at *Lord, I have cried* (*Pervovechnomu ot Otsa* / "Come, let us worship God the Word...").
[^33]: Theotokion / Dogmatikon of Tone 2 at Saturday Great Vespers (*Preide sen' zakonnaya, blagodati prishedshi* / "The shadow of the Law passed away when grace arrived..."): St. John of Damascus's classic theological hymn expounding the transition from Old Testament types and shadows to New Testament grace and the reality of the Incarnation.


## Cohort 4 Footnotes

[^34]: Typology of the Exodus in the Dogmatikon of Tone 2 (*Preide sen' zakonnaya*): The hymn explicitly contrasts the shadow of the Mosaic Law with the grace of the Gospel, drawing upon the pillar of fire (Exodus 13:21–22) that led the Israelites through the wilderness, which is fulfilled and superseded by Christ, the spiritual Sun of righteousness (Malachi 4:2 [LXX 3:20]). In place of Moses the lawgiver stands Christ the Savior, effecting the redemption of mankind.
[^35]: The Aposticha Stichera of the Resurrection, Tone 2 (*Voskreseniye Tvoye, Khriste Spase*): These four hymns, traditionally attributed to St. John of Damascus, celebrate the cosmic victory of the Cross over the tree of the Garden of Eden (Genesis 3:1–6), the harrowing of Hades, the piercing of the Lord's side by the soldier's spear (John 19:34), and the empty tomb sealed by the Judean authorities (Matthew 27:62–66).
[^36]: Theotokion of the Aposticha (*O chudese novago vsekho drevnikho chudes* / "O wonder new beyond all wonders of old!"): Composed by St. John of Damascus, this venerable hymn ponders the mystery of the Virgin Mother holding in her maternal embrace the uncircumscribed God Who sustains all creation in the palm of His hand. It affirms the orthodox dogma of the Theotokos as defined at the Council of Ephesus (431 AD) and the Council of Chalcedon (451 AD).
[^37]: Sunday Midnight Office (*Mesonyktikon*), Canon to the Most Holy and Undivided Trinity (*Kanon Troichen*), Tone 2: Composed by Metrophanes, Metropolitan of Smyrna (c. 820–880; author of the Triadikos Canons of the Octoechos). The canon provides an exhaustive patristic and scholastic synthesis of Trinitarian theology, systematically articulating the consubstantiality (*homoousion*), co-eternity, and unconfused hypostatic properties of the Father (the unbegotten Source and Cause), the Son (begotten before all ages), and the Holy Spirit (eternally proceeding from the Father).
[^38]: Sessional Hymn to the Holy Trinity, Tone 2, Ode 3 (*Svyataya Troitse, pomilui ikhzhe sozda*): Chanted according to the melodic model (*prosomoion*) *Vyshnikh ishcha* ("Seeking the things on high"), entreating the consubstantial Trinity to deliver creation from fire and condemnation and grant forgiveness of sins.
[^39]: Canon to the Holy Trinity, Ode 5, Troparion 2: Moses and the vision in the Burning Bush (Exodus 3:2–14). The hymn incorporates the traditional patristic christology (patristic consensus of St. Justin Martyr, St. Irenaeus of Lyons, and St. Athanasius the Great) identifying the "Angel of the Lord" who spoke from the flame as the pre-incarnate Logos, the "Angel of Great Counsel" (LXX Isaiah 9:6), revealing the tri-hypostatic nature of the one divine Principle.
[^40]: Sunday Matins Sessional Hymns of the Resurrection, Tone 2, after the First Kathisma: *Blagoobraznyi Iosif* ("The noble Joseph..."), the classic troparion of Great and Holy Saturday (commemorating Joseph of Arimathea taking down Christ's Body, wrapping it in a clean winding sheet, and placing it in a new rock-hewn sepulcher; Matthew 27:57–60, Mark 15:43–46), used here in the Sunday Resurrection cycle to proclaim the Lord's third-day rising.
[^41]: Hypakoe of Tone 2 (*Po strasti shedshe na grob* / "After the Passion, the women having gone unto the tomb"): The Hypakoe (from the Greek *hypakoe*, "obedience" or "listening attentively") recounts the holy Myrrh-bearing women who went at early dawn to anoint the Lord's sacred Body with aromatic spices, beholding the angelic messengers who declared that Christ is not among the dead (Mark 16:1–8, Luke 24:1–10).
[^42]: The Gradual Hymns (*Anavathmoi*) of Tone 2: Structured into three antiphons inspired by the Old Testament Psalms of Ascents (Psalms 119–133 [LXX 118–132]) sung by Hebrew pilgrims journeying up to Jerusalem. Each antiphon concludes with a pneumatological troparion (*Svyatomu Dukhu...*) extolling the Holy Spirit as the sovereign Principle of life, wisdom, and sanctification alongside the Father and the Son.
[^43]: Canons of Sunday Matins, Tone 2: The classic Byzantine-Ruthenian tri-canon structure prescribed for the Resurrection Matins in the Octoechos: (1) The Canon of the Resurrection by St. John of Damascus (*Vo glubine potopi drevle* / "Of old, the all-armed power drowned..."); (2) The Canon of the Cross (*Netrennuyu neobychuyu* / "Traversing an untrodden, unaccustomed path..."); and (3) The Canon of the Most Holy Theotokos to the same heirmos model.
