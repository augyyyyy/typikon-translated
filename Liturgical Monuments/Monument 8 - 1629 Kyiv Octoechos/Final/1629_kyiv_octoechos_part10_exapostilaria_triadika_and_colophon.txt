# EXAPOSTILARIA, TRIADIKA (HYMNS TO THE TRINITY), PROVENANCE MARGINALIA, AND REAR BINDING

## RESURRECTIONAL EXAPOSTILARIA AND ELEVEN EVANGELICAL STICHERA (EOTHINA)

[f. 279rA] *The Resurrectional Exapostilaria and Evangelical Stichera. The work of Emperor Leo.* [^285]

*First Exapostilarion:*

With the disciples let us ascend the mountain of Galilee in faith, to behold Christ saying that He received authority over the things above and below: let us learn how to teach and to baptize all the nations in the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, and to abide with the disciples, as He promised, unto the end of the age.

*Theotokion:*

He Who is divinely glorified in the highest by the angels, not departing ineffably from the bosom of the Father, O all-holy Virgin, and [f. 279vA] lived: and thou, having received Him as the Mediator of salvation, from thy pure blood didst lend flesh beyond understanding unto the Redeemer: pray Him to grant deliverance from transgressions unto thy servants.

*Evangelical Sticheron 1, Tone 1:*

When the disciples were going up to the mountain for the earthly ascension, the Lord stood before them; and they worshipped Him, and they are sent forth throughout the universe under heaven to preach the resurrection from the dead and the ascension into the heavens; with whom Christ God, the Savior of our souls, promised without falsehood to abide.

*Second Exapostilarion:*

Seeing the stone rolled away, the myrrh-bearing women rejoiced; for they beheld a young man sitting in the tomb, and he said unto them: "Behold, Christ is risen! Say therefore unto Peter and to the disciples:

[f. 280rA] Hasten to the mountain of Galilee; there shall He appear unto you, as He foretold unto His friends."

*Theotokion:*

The Sun shone forth from the tomb today as a bridegroom from his chamber, and led Hades captive, and abolished death: by the prayers of her who bare Thee, O Christ, send forth light upon us, light enlightening hearts and souls, light leading all of us inward and guiding us unto the paths of Thy commandments and unto the way of humility.

*Evangelical Sticheron 2, Tone 2:*

Unto the women who came with myrrh together with Mary, and were in perplexity how they might attain their desire, the stone appeared rolled away; and a divine young man assuaged the turmoil of their souls, for he said: "The Lord is risen; therefore hasten to Galilee unto His preachers, the disciples, and behold Him [^286] [f. 280vA] raised from the dead, as Giver of life and Lord."

*Third Exapostilarion:*

Let no one disbelieve that Christ is risen! For He appeared unto Mary, and then was seen by two walking into the country; and He appeared again unto the eleven as they sat at meat, whom He sent forth to baptize, and ascended into heaven whence He had descended, confirming their preaching with many signs.

*Theotokion:*

By essential properties, O Virgin, Thy Son awoke from sleep as one dead, and rose as God; for He saw no corruption. Entreat Him unceasingly, O pure Lady, to send down light upon us, Thy people and Thy flock: save from daytime and nighttime stumbling us who worship Him together with the Father and the Spirit, one God.

*Evangelical Sticheron 3, Tone 3:*

[f. 281rA] When Mary Magdalene announced the Savior's resurrection from the dead and His appearance, the disciples, disbelieving, were reproached for their hardness of heart; yet, being filled with signs and wonders, they were sent forth to preach. But Thou, O Lord, didst ascend unto the Father, the primal Light; and they proclaimed the Word everywhere, confirmed by wonders. And having been enlightened through them, we glorify Thy resurrection from the dead, O Lord, Lover of mankind.

*Fourth Exapostilarion:*

Having shone forth in virtues, let us behold standing in the life-bearing tomb men in gleaming raiment; and as the myrrh-bearing women bowed their faces to the earth before Him Who rules the heavens, let us learn of the resurrection, and run with Peter to the life-bearing tomb [^287] [f. 281vA] and marveling at what was done, let us remain to behold Christ.

*Theotokion:*

Having become incarnate in thy womb, O Lord, willing to call back from corruption a fallen world, as He alone knows, He dwelt in thee, O Mother of God; and having all found salvation, we cry unto thee like the angel: Rejoice, only pure Lady, blessed among all women, for thou didst bear salvation unto the whole universe!

*Evangelical Sticheron 4, Tone 4:*

It was early morning, and the women came unto Thy tomb, O Christ, but the body desired by them was not found. Therefore, as they were in perplexity, two men stood by them in gleaming raiment, saying: "Why seek ye the living among the dead? He is risen, as He said before! Why remember ye not His words?" And believing them, they proclaimed what they had seen; but their glad tidings seemed an idle tale:

[f. 282rA] so slumbering were the disciples still! But Peter ran and beheld, and glorified within himself Thy wonders.

*Fifth Exapostilarion:*

Christ, the Life and the Way, rose from the dead: journeying on the road with Cleopas and Luke, He was made known unto them in Emmaus in the breaking of bread; whose hearts and souls were burning when He spoke unto them on the way and expounded from the Scriptures what He had suffered; with them we also cry: "He hath appeared also unto Peter!"

*Theotokion:*

The Creator and Lord and God of all things, taking earthly flesh from thy pure blood, O all-praised Mother of God, made my corrupted nature new, leaving thee after childbearing again even as thou wast before childbirth: therefore we all praise thee in faith, crying: Rejoice, glory of the world!

*Evangelical Sticheron 5, Tone 5:* [^288]

[f. 282vA] O how wise are Thy judgments, O Christ! How unto Peter indeed by the graveclothes alone Thou gavest to understand Thy resurrection; yet walking on the way with Luke and Cleopas, Thou didst converse with them, and speaking, didst not quickly reveal Thyself. Therefore Thou wast reproached as one journeying alone in Jerusalem, not knowing her counsel unto the end. Yet disposing all things for the benefit of the creation, Thou didst unfold the prophecies concerning Thyself, and in the blessing of the bread wast made known unto them, whose hearts even before that were kindled unto the knowledge of Thee; and they openly proclaimed Thy resurrection unto the assembled disciples: through whom have mercy upon us!

*Sixth Exapostilarion:*

Thou didst show Thyself, O Savior, as man by nature, standing in their midst when Thou didst rise from the tomb; and partaking of food, Thou didst teach the baptism of repentance:

[f. 283rA] and straightway Thou didst ascend unto the heavenly Father, and didst promise to send the Comforter unto the disciples: O supremely divine Lover of mankind, glory to Thy rising!

*Theotokion:*

Beholding her Son risen from the dead, the all-holy Virgin was filled with joy, and praised His ineffable lovingkindness; for, not having departed from the Father's bosom, and born of thee, by His passion on the Cross He released all from corruption, and led back to life His own infant creature.

*Evangelical Sticheron 6, Tone 6:*

Truly Thou art peace, O Christ, granting Thy peace of God unto men; after the resurrection showing Thyself unto the disciples, Thou didst find them terrified, supposing that they beheld a spirit; but Thou didst quell the turmoil of their souls, showing them Thy hands and Thy feet [^289] [f. 283vA] yet while they still disbelieved, by the partaking of food and the recalling of teachings Thou didst open their mind to understand the Scriptures; and having promised them the Father's promise, and blessed them, Thou didst ascend into heaven: therefore with them we worship Thee: O Lord, glory to Thee!

*Seventh Exapostilarion:*

Saying that they had taken away the Lord, Mary ran to the tomb, and Simon Peter and the other disciple of Christ whom He loved ran with her: and both ran together, and found only the linen clothes lying within, and the napkin for the head was apart from them. Therefore they held their peace again until they beheld Christ.

*Theotokion:*

Sing of Thy countless mercy, O my Savior, for Thou didst pour Thyself out to bear and to save [f. 284rA] the fallen nature of earthly beings: being God, Thou wast pleased to become like me from the pure Virgin Maiden of God, and to descend into Hades, and to lead me forth thence: by her prayers, O Lord, save our souls!

*Evangelical Sticheron 7, Tone 7:*

Lo, it is dark and early: why standest thou at the tomb, O Mary, having great darkness in thy mind, asking where Jesus was laid? But look upon the disciples running together: how by the linen clothes and the napkin they came to know of the resurrection, and remembered the Scriptures concerning them! With whom and for whose sake having believed, we hymn Thee, O Christ Giver of life.

*Eighth Exapostilarion:*

Beholding two angels within the tomb, Mary was amazed; and not knowing Christ, she questioned Him as the gardener: "Sir, where hast thou laid the body of my Jesus?" But having recognized Him by His call [^290] [f. 284vA] within herself, she heard the Savior say: "Touch Me not, for I ascend unto My Father: say unto My brethren!"

*Theotokion:*

Not having left the Father's bosom, though He became incarnate of the Virgin, the Lord came down, bowing the heavens, to save me who was fallen, and desiring to snatch me from the mouth of the intelligible beast, held fast in his bonds: therefore, having been delivered, O Savior, I glorify Thy power.

*Evangelical Sticheron 8, Tone 8:*

Not without reason were Mary's warm tears poured forth: for lo, she was accounted worthy of the instruction of angels and the vision of Jesus Himself! Yet still she thought earthly thoughts, being frail: therefore she was sent away not to touch Christ. But nevertheless she was sent as a herald unto Thy disciples, bearing the glad tidings unto them, [f. 285rA] announcing Thine ascent unto the Father's portion: and by these things vouchsafe Thine appearance unto us also, O Master and Lord!

*Ninth Exapostilarion:*

When Thou camest in, O Master, the doors being shut, Thou didst fill the apostles with the All-Holy Spirit, breathing peace upon them, unto whom Thou saidst to bind and to loose sins; and after eight days Thou didst show Thy side and hands unto Thomas, with whom we also cry: "Thou art my Lord and my God!"

*Theotokion:*

O Birthgiver of God, pure and all-holy Virgin, Joy of the afflicted, Protection of the world, Intercessor for sinners: by thy prayers console the hearts of the sorrowful; for we the faithful have no other hope after God besides thee, O pure Mother, our hope loving mankind, and thou savest the world, O Maiden.

*Evangelical Sticheron 9, Tone 5:* [^291]

[f. 285vA] As in the latter times, on the evening of the Sabbath, Thou didst appear unto Thy friends, O Christ, and by a wonder didst confirm a wonder: entering through shut doors, Thou didst confirm Thy resurrection from the dead! But Thou didst fill the disciples with joy, and didst bestow upon them the Holy Spirit, and gavest authority for the remission of sins; and Thou didst not leave Thomas to drown in the tempest of unbelief: therefore grant unto us true understanding and remission of transgressions, O tender-hearted Lord!

*Tenth Exapostilarion:*

At the Sea of Tiberias of old, with the sons of Zebedee, Nathanael was with Peter, and two other disciples, engaged in fishing; who at Christ's bidding cast the net on the right side, and drew out a multitude of fishes:

[f. 286rA] whom Peter recognizing, waded toward Him. Behold, this was the third time He appeared, showing bread and fish upon the coals.

*Theotokion:*

Venerating thine honorable icon, O all-holy Lady, show us to be sons of light, and dwellers of Paradise, and partakers of the Kingdom: for we are thy suppliants and thy people, O all-pure one; let us not fall from thy protection, O Good Lady, and account us worthy of the portion of all the saved, O all-immaculate Virgin.

*Evangelical Sticheron 10, Tone 6:*

After the descent into Hades and the resurrection from the dead, grieving as was meet over parting from Thee, O Christ, the disciples returned to work, and again took to the boat and nets; and of catch there was nothing! But Thou, O Savior, didst appear as Master of all, commanding to cast the net on the right side; and immediately the word became deed [^292] [f. 286vA] swiftly, and a great multitude of fishes, and a strange supper prepared on the earth! Of which Thy disciples then partook: vouchsafe us also now to be spiritually filled, O Lord Lover of mankind!

*Eleventh Exapostilarion:*

After the divine rising, thrice the Lord said unto Peter: "Lovest thou Me?" proposing him as chief shepherd over His sheep. When Peter beheld following him him whom Jesus loved, he questioned the Master: "And what shall this man do?" "If I will," He said, "that he tarry till I come again, what is that to thee, friend Peter?"

*Theotokion:*

Rejoice! we cry unto thee, O all-holy Mother of God, for thou didst announce joy unto Eve, turning sorrow into joy, O Virgin: and fill us with thy joy, and account us worthy of the radiant glory of thy Son and the portion of all the elect, [f. 287rA] O all-pure one, that we all may cry unto thee: Rejoice, pure Maiden!

*Evangelical Sticheron 11, Tone 8:*

Manifesting Himself unto the disciples after the resurrection, the Savior gave unto thee, Simon, the pasturing of His sheep as a recompense for love, requiring care of the flock; therefore He said unto thee: "If thou lovest Me, Peter, feed My lambs, feed My sheep." Whereupon, straightway showing his brotherly love, he questioned concerning the other disciple: by whose prayers, O Christ, preserve Thy flock from the wolves that destroy it!

## WEEKDAY TRIADIKA (HYMNS TO THE TRINITY) AND PHOTOGOGIKA (TONES 1–8)

*Triadika of the days, which are chanted in the Holy Forty Days and other fasts when Alleluia is chanted; then Triadikon, Tone 1:*

In the bodily configurations of the bodiless hosts, being led up in mind to the intelligible and immaterial [^293] [f. 287vA] mind, and receiving the illumination of the thrice-holy hymn of the tri-hypostatic Godhead, cherubically we cry: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God: through the intercession of Thy bodiless hosts, have mercy on us!

*Glory:*

With all the heavenly powers, cherubically sending up thrice-holy praise unto Him Who is in the highest, we cry: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God: by the prayers of all Thy saints, have mercy on us!

*Both now:*

Having risen from sleep, we fall down before Thee, O Good One, and we cry unto Thee the angelic hymn, O Almighty One: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God: for the sake of the Mother of God, have mercy on us!

*Exapostilarion, Tone 1:*

Thou Who hast shone forth the light, O Lord, cleanse my soul from every sin, and through the intercession of Thy bodiless hosts, have mercy on us.

*Triadika, Tone 2:*

O uncreated Nature, Creator of all, open our lips that we may proclaim Thy praise, [f. 288rA] crying: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God!

*Glory:*

Imitating the powers on high on earth, we offer Thee the triumphal hymn, O Good One: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God: by the prayers of all Thy saints, have mercy on us!

*Both now:*

From the bed and sleep Thou hast raised me up, O Lord; enlighten my mind and heart, and open my lips to hymn Thee, O Holy Trinity: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God: for the sake of the Mother of God, have mercy on us!

*Exapostilarion, Tone 2:*

Send forth Thy light everlasting, O Lord, and enlighten the secret eyes of my heart: through the intercession of Thy bodiless hosts, have mercy on us.

*Triadika, Tone 3:*

The unoriginate Father, and the co-unoriginate Son, and the co-eternal Spirit, one Godhead, daring to glorify in cherubic praise, we say: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God!

*Glory:* [^294]

[f. 288vA] O Trinity consubstantial and undivided, Monad tri-hypostatic and co-eternal: unto Thee as God we cry the angelic hymn: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God: by the prayers of all Thy saints, have mercy on us!

*Both now:*

Suddenly the Judge shall come, and each one's deeds shall be laid bare; but with fear cry out in the midnight, O my soul: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God: by the prayers of the Mother of God, have mercy on us!

*Exapostilarion, Tone 3:*

Send forth Thy light, O Christ God, and enlighten my heart: through the intercession of Thy bodiless hosts, save me.

*Triadika, Tone 4:*

Thine unoriginate Father, and Thee, O Christ God, and Thy Most-Holy Spirit, daring cherubically to glorify, we say: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God!

*Glory:*

[f. 289rA] As now the angelic ranks in the heavens, and human assemblies with fear upon the earth, we offer Thee the triumphal hymn, O Good One: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God: by the prayers of all Thy saints, have mercy on us!

*Both now:*

Unto Thy noetic servants, we mortal ones offer Thee hymnody, daring to say: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God: for the sake of the Mother of God, have mercy on us!

*Exapostilarion, Tone 4:*

Thou Who hast shone forth the light unto Thy world, cleanse my soul held fast in darkness from every sin: through the intercession of Thy bodiless hosts, have mercy on us.

*Triadika, Tone 5:* [^295]

It is the time of singing, and the hour for prayer: fervently let us cry unto the one God: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God!

*Glory:*

Daring to emulate Thy noetic armies [f. 289vA] O unoriginate Trinity, with unworthy lips we cry unto Thee: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God: by the prayers of all Thy saints, have mercy on us!

*Both now:*

Contained in the virgin womb, and not parted from the Father's bosom, with the angels receive also us, O Christ God, who cry unto Thee: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God: for the sake of the Mother of God, have mercy on us!

*Exapostilarion, Tone 5:*

O Giver of light, Lord, send forth Thy light, and enlighten my heart: by the prayers of Thy bodiless hosts, save me.

*Triadika, Tone 6:*

With bodiless lips, with unceasing doxologies, the six-winged ones sing unto Thee the thrice-holy hymn, O our God; and we upon the earth with unworthy lips send up praise unto Thee: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God!

*Glory:*

[f. 290rA] Standing before Thee with fear the Cherubim, and dreading with trembling the Seraphim, offer the thrice-holy hymn with unceasing voice; with whom also we sinners cry: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God: by the prayers of all Thy saints, have mercy on us!

*Both now:*

The Trinity, a Monad in Godhead, the unconfused Unity let us glorify, and let us cry aloud the angelic hymn: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God: for the sake of the Mother of God, have mercy on us!

*Exapostilarion, Tone 6:*

Through the intercession of the bodiless hosts, O Lord, send down upon our souls Thy light everlasting.

*Triadika, Tone 7:* [^296]

Unapproachable in Godhead, Who art in the Unity of the Trinity, sending up the Seraphic thrice-holy praise, with fear we cry: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God!

*Glory:*

Having cast off sluggish sleep, O my soul, and [f. 290vA] amendment toward standing show forth unto the Judge, and with fear cry out: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God: by the prayers of all Thy saints, have mercy on us!

*Both now:*

Thou Who by power on high art praised cherubically, and in divine glory receivest worship from angels: receive also us sinners who cry unto Thee with compunction: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God: for the sake of the Mother of God, have mercy on us!

*Exapostilarion, Tone 7:*

Through the intercession of the bodiless hosts, O Lord, send down light upon our souls, and great mercy.

*Triadika, Tone 8:*

Having our hearts in the heavens, let us emulate the angelic rank, and with fear let us fall down before the Judge, sending up the triumphal praise: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God!

*Glory:*

Not daring to gaze upon Thee, the Cherubim, flying, cry with acclamation the divine hymn of the thrice-holy voice; with whom also we cry: [f. 291rA] we sinners: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God: by the prayers of all Thy saints, have mercy on us!

*Both now:*

Constrained by the multitude of our transgressions, and not daring to look up to the height of heaven, bowing soul and body, with the angels we cry unto Thee: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God: for the sake of the Mother of God, have mercy on us!

*Exapostilarion, Tone 8:*

Being the Light, O Christ, enlighten me through Thyself: through the intercession of the bodiless hosts, save me.

### Notes

[^285]: Fol. 278v–279r concludes the Saturday Beatitudes in Tone 6 and opens the concluding section of the Octoechos: the Resurrectional Exapostilaria (*Svetilna voskresny*) and the Eleven Evangelical Stichera (*Stikhiry yevangelskiya*) attributed in the rubrics to "Leo the Despot" (*tvoreniye Leva despota*, Byzantine Emperor Leo VI the Wise, 866–912). Fol. 279r features an elaborate ornamental woodcut headpiece framed by typographical vignettes, marking this major division of the codex. The first Exapostilarion reflects Matthew 28:16–20 (the Great Commission on the mount in Galilee).
[^286]: Fol. 279v–280r sets forth Evangelical Sticheron 1 (Tone 1, corresponding to the 1st Matins Gospel, Matthew 28:16–20), Exapostilarion 2, and the opening of Evangelical Sticheron 2 (Tone 2, based on the 2nd Matins Gospel, Mark 16:1–8). The hymnography emphasizes the cosmic transition from burial to resurrection, depicting Christ shining from the sepulchre "as a bridegroom from his chamber" (*yako zhenikh ot chertoga*, Psalm 18:6 LXX) and dispelling the sorrow of the myrrh-bearing women.
[^287]: Fol. 280v–281r contains Exapostilarion 3 (based on Mark 16:9–20), Evangelical Sticheron 3 (Tone 3), and Exapostilarion 4 (based on Luke 24:1–12). The hymnographer captures the narrative drama of the eleven apostles rebuked for their initial unbelief and hardness of heart (*zhestoserdie*, Mark 16:14) before receiving power through miraculous signs to preach throughout the world.
[^288]: Fol. 281v–282r features Evangelical Sticheron 4 (Tone 4, corresponding to Luke 24:1–12) and Exapostilarion 5 (based on the Emmaus journey, Luke 24:13–35). The hymn describes how the apostles initially regarded the myrrh-bearers' announcement as foolishness (*lzha* / idle talk, Luke 24:11), until Peter ran to the sepulchre and marveled at the empty graveclothes. Exapostilarion 5 recalls the burning hearts of Luke and Cleopas and echoes Luke 24:34: "The Lord is risen indeed, and hath appeared to Simon."
[^289]: Fol. 282v–283r contains the conclusion of Evangelical Sticheron 5 (Tone 5), Exapostilarion 6, and the opening of Evangelical Sticheron 6 (Tone 6, based on Luke 24:36–53). The hymnographer marvels at Christ's pedagogical dispensation: opening the mystery to Peter through empty graveclothes alone, yet walking incognito with the disciples to Emmaus before revealing Himself in the Eucharistic fraction of bread (*vo blagoslovenii khleba poznasya ima*).
[^290]: Fol. 283v–284r presents the conclusion of Evangelical Sticheron 6, Exapostilarion 7 (Tone 7, based on John 20:1–10: Peter and the Beloved Disciple running to the tomb and finding the sudarium folded apart), Evangelical Sticheron 7 (Tone 7), and Exapostilarion 8 (based on John 20:11–18). The hymnography highlights Mary Magdalene's initial sorrowful confusion at daybreak (*Se tma i rano*) and her mistaking the risen Savior for the gardener (*vertogradar*).
[^291]: Fol. 284v–285r contains the conclusion of Exapostilarion 8, Evangelical Sticheron 8 (Tone 8, detailing Mary Magdalene's encounter with Christ and the *Noli me tangere* command of John 20:17), Exapostilarion 9, and the opening of Evangelical Sticheron 9 (Tone 5 / Plagal of Tone 1, based on John 20:19–31). Exapostilarion 9 commemorates the bestowal of the Holy Spirit behind closed doors (*Zatvorennym dverem*) and the confession of St. Thomas eight days later (*Gospod i Bog moy ty yesi*).
[^292]: Fol. 285v–286r contains Evangelical Sticheron 9 (commemorating Christ's appearance on Sunday evening and the Thomas narrative, John 20:19–29), Exapostilarion 10, and Evangelical Sticheron 10 (Tone 6, based on the miraculous draught of fishes at the Sea of Tiberias, John 21:1–14). The hymn vividly portrays the disciples' return to their mundane fishing boat in their sorrow until Christ manifests His sovereign mastery by commanding the cast on the right side.
[^293]: Fol. 286v–287r concludes the Eleven Evangelical Stichera with Exapostilarion 11 and Evangelical Sticheron 11 (Tone 8, John 21:15–25, Christ's threefold rehabilitation of Peter and pastoral commission: *Pasi agntsa moya, pasi ovtsa moya*), followed immediately by the codex's final liturgical collection: the Triadika (*Troichni dnyevi*) chanted at weekday Matins during Great Lent (*vo Svyatuyu Chetyredesyatnitsu*) and the other fasting seasons when Alleluia replaces *God is the Lord*. The opening Triadikon in Tone 1 urges the mind to transcend bodily symbols to contemplate the immaterial angelic powers.
[^294]: Fol. 287v–288r presents the Triadika (Hymns to the Trinity) and Photogogika (Lenten Exapostilaria) for Tone 1 and Tone 2, and the opening of Tone 3. Each tone's set consists of three Triadika punctuated by the Thrice-Holy Hymn (*Svyat, Svyat, Svyat yesi Bozhe*), supplicating God respectively through the intercession of the bodiless angelic hosts (*zastupleniyem bezplotnykh*), the prayers of all the saints (*molitvami vsekh svyatykh*), and the intercession of the Mother of God (*Bogoroditsy radi*), followed by a Photogogikon petitioning for inward spiritual illumination.
[^295]: Fol. 288v–289r presents the continuation of the weekday Triadika (Hymns to the Holy Trinity) and Photogogika (Lenten Exapostilaria) for Tone 3, Tone 4, and the opening of Tone 5. In accordance with the Studite and Sabaite Horologia, these hymns are sung at weekday Lenten Matins when "Alleluia" replaces "God is the Lord." Each tone comprises three Triadika: the first glorifying the divine hypostases, the second (at *Glory:*) invoking the prayers of the bodiless heavenly powers and all saints, and the third (at *Both now:*) seeking the intercession of the Theotokos, followed by a concise Exapostilarion imploring spiritual illumination and remission of sins.
[^296]: Fol. 289v–290r provides the Triadika and Photogogika for Tone 5, Tone 6, and Tone 7. The hymnography emphasizes the angelic liturgy: the six-winged Seraphim and many-eyed Cherubim offering unceasing doxology (*nemolchnymi slavoslovlenii*) before the transcendent Trinity, contrasting with humanity's mortal frailty. The Tone 7 Triadikon on *Glory:* features the striking epigraphic abbreviation `сонъ лѣ́ностный ѿложи́вши` ("having cast off sluggish sleep"), summoning the soul to stand in vigilance before the impending judgment of Christ.
[^297]: Fol. 290v–291r contains the conclusion of the Tone 7 Triadika and the complete set of Triadika and Photogogikon for Tone 8, followed by a cruciform typographical vignette of four rosettes and the major display colophon: *Konets Svetilnam i Troichnam* ("The End of the Exapostilaria and Triadika"). This formula formally closes the printed liturgical body of the 1629 Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra Octoechos, marking the completion of all eight tones and their supplementary nocturnal and Lenten hymnographic cycles.
[^298]: Fol. 291v and the rear endleaf (Fol. [292r]) preserve invaluable codicological and bibliographic evidence. Pasted on Fol. 291v is a 19th-century bibliographic note citing Pavel Stroev's *Dopolnenie k opisaniiu staropechatnykh knig* (No. 39), which recorded 297 leaves in the Lavra edition. The note observes that the present copy contains 295 leaves, suggesting that the final two leaves—traditionally bearing typographer's epigrams or supplementary verses—were missing or omitted in this binding. The adjacent flyleaf exhibits 17th-century cursive accounting memoranda ("December 13th day. Unto Petr Sergeevich given 25; took"), secular pen trials, and calligraphic flourishing typical of monastic scriptoria and administrative chancelleries of the Cossack Hetmanate era.
[^299]: Physical leaf 295 constitutes the lower (rear) outer binding board of the codex. The binding is an authentic contemporary monastic binding of the first third of the 17th century, executed in dark brown calfskin over bevelled wooden boards with blind-tooled Renaissance roll borders. The brass catches on the fore-edge correspond to the clasps on the upper board (Leaf p1), demonstrating that the codex has survived in its original Lavra or Trinity-Sergius monastic binding. This physical board seals the closed mathematical leaf sequence of the monument (Leaves 1–295).
