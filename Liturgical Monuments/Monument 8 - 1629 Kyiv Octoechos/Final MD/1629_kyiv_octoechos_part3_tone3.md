# TONE 3: RESURRECTIONAL AND WEEKDAY HYMNOGRAPHY

## TONE 3

*On Saturday Evening, at "Lord, I have cried," Tone 3:*

*Stichera of the Resurrection, Tone 3.*

By Thy Cross, O Christ Savior, the dominion of death has been shattered, and the deceit of the devil has been abolished; and the human race, being saved by faith, ever offers a hymn unto Thee.[^55]

All creation has been enlightened by Thy Resurrection,

**[Left Page — Fol. 50v]**

O Lord, and Paradise has been opened again; and all creation, praising Thee, ever offers a hymn unto Thee.

I glorify the power of the Father and of the Son, and I praise the dominion of the Holy Spirit: undivided, uncreated Godhead, consubstantial Trinity, reigning unto ages of ages.

*Other Stichera, Anatolian:*

We worship Thy precious Cross, O Christ, and we praise and glorify Thy Resurrection; for by Thy wound we have all been healed.

We hymn the Savior Who was incarnate of the Virgin; for for our sake He was crucified, and on the third day He rose, granting unto us great mercy.

Having descended into Hades, Christ proclaimed the good tidings unto those there, saying: Take courage, now have I conquered; I am the Resurrection, I will lead you forth, having shattered the gates of

**[Right Page — Fol. 51r]**

death.

Standing unworthily in Thy most honorable house, we send up an evening hymn, crying out from the depths: O Christ God, Who hast enlightened the world by Thy third-day Resurrection, deliver Thy people from the hand of Thine enemies, O Lover of mankind!

*Glory, Both now:*

*Theotokion [Dogmatikon]:*

How shall we not marvel at thy God-and-human childbearing, O most pure one? For without receiving trial of man, O all-blameless one, thou didst bring forth in the flesh a Son without a father, Who before the ages was begotten of the Father without a mother, undergoing neither change, nor mingling, nor division, but preserving intact the specific properties of each nature. Therefore, O Lady Virgin Mother, entreat Him that the souls of those who in the Orthodox faith confess thee to be the Mother of God may be saved![^56]

**[Left Page — Fol. 51v]**

may be saved!

*At the Aposticha:*

*Stichera of the Resurrection, Tone 3.*

By Thy Passion, O Christ, Thou didst darken the sun, and by the light of Thy Resurrection Thou didst enlighten all things; accept our evening hymn, O Lover of mankind.

*Other Stichera:*

Thy life-giving rising, O Lord, has enlightened the whole universe, and has called back His fallen creation; therefore, delivered from the curse of Adam, we all cry aloud: O almighty Lord, glory to Thee!

Being changeless God, Thou didst change by suffering in the flesh; and creation, unable to bear the sight, bowed down in fear, and groaning, it hymns Thy long-suffering. And descending into Hades, Thou didst rise on the third day, granting life unto the world, and great mercy.

**[Right Page — Fol. 52r]**

That Thou mightest deliver our race from death, O Christ, Thou didst endure death, and didst rise from the dead as the firstborn; Thou didst raise up with Thyself those who acknowledged Thee to be God, and Thou didst enlighten the world. O Lord, glory to Thee!

*Glory, Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

Without seed, from the Holy Spirit, by the will of the Father, thou didst conceive the Son of God, Who from the Father is without mother before the ages, and for our sake was born without father from thee; thou didst bear Him in the flesh, and didst nourish the Infant with milk. Therefore cease not to pray, that our souls may be delivered from tribulations.

*Dismissal Troparion:*

*Resurrection Troparion, Tone 3.*

Let the heavens be glad, and let the earth rejoice; for the Lord has wrought strength with His arm, He has trampled down death by death, He has become the firstborn from the dead; He has delivered us from the belly of Hades, and grants unto the world[^57]

**[Left Page — Fol. 52v]**

great mercy.

*Glory, Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

We hymn thee as the mediatress of the salvation of our race, O Virgin Mother of God; for having accepted suffering on the Cross in the flesh which He took from thee, Thy Son and our God has delivered us from corruption, as the Lover of mankind.

*Canon to the Holy and Life-Creating Trinity:*

*A composition of Metrophanes. Tone 3.*

*Ode 1:*

*Heirmos: He Who gathered the waters...*

O incomprehensible, sole sovereign Godhead, and one Trinitarian source of Divinity: make me worthy of Thy thrice-radiant light, that with angelic lips I may ceaselessly sing unto Thee, the thrice-holy Power.

The immaterial ranks holily sing unto Thee, the premier Light, as the creative Cause, the sole thrice-radiant One; and in unison with them, we also, the multitudes of mankind,

**[Right Page — Fol. 53r]**

faithfully glorify Thee with clay lips.

*Glory:*

Theologians ever symbolically name Thee Mind, Word, and Spirit: denoting the passionless generation of the Son from the unbegotten Father, and together with it the procession of the Divine Spirit, O God sole-ruler over all.

*Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

Having assumed human substance as Lover of mankind by nature, O Divine Word, Thou didst shine forth the Trinitarian, most sovereign Light of the one Godhead, showing forth the most pure Virgin who bore Thee to be glorified by all.[^58]

*Ode 3:*

*Heirmos: He Who brought all things out of nothing...*

Commanding of old that water be poured three times upon the wood, Elijah figuratively proclaimed the Trinitarian hypostasis of the sole Divine Sovereignty.

The corruptible nature of the earthborn hymns Thee, the one thrice-radiant Creator, the un-

**[Left Page — Fol. 53v]**

changeable One; and I cry unto Thee, O Master: Deliver me from every sudden destruction, and save me!

*Glory:*

Echoing in harmony the words of the prophets, the most wise apostles, and the preachers of the faith, we the faithful glorify Thee as the equally operative Trinity, O God of all.

*Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

Descending from the high throne, taking man upon Himself as Lover of mankind through thee, O most pure one, the Word shone forth the thrice-personal Light upon all.

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 3:*

*Podoben: "Beholding the beauty of thy virginity" (Красотѣ̀ дѣ́вства твоегѡ̀):*

O undivided and inseparable Trinity, have mercy on us who worship Thee, who piously hymn Thee, and who ceaselessly cry unto Thee: Save those whom Thou hast gathered in Thy holy Church, O Mother of compassions, to glorify Thy dominion; for as merciful, Thou knowest the weakness of our heart.

*Glory, Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

**[Right Page — Fol. 54r]**

I am wholly changed through mine intemperance and senseless lack of fear; insensible, wretched, I do not tremble before the day at which all things visible and invisible tremble. O Sovereign Lady, help me and dispel the perplexity of my heart; for thou art the hope and protectress of thy servants.

*Ode 4:*

*Heirmos: Thou hast set firm love toward us: the...*

A two-fold branch from the Father, as from a root, sprang forth the Son and the upright Spirit: co-natural shoots, planted by God, and co-unoriginate flowers; so that there are three luminaries of the Godhead.[^59]

*Glory:*

The assemblies of intellectual beings ceaselessly hymn Thee, the unsearchable God; and with them we also glorify Thee, saying: O supra-essential Trinity, preserve Thy servants, as Thou art compassionate!

*Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

**[Left Page — Fol. 54v]**

Thou hast inflamed us with love for Thee, O most merciful Word of God, Who for our sake wast incarnate without change, and mystically didst reveal the thrice-radiant, one Godhead; therefore we glorify Thee.

*Ode 5:*

*Heirmos: As Isaiah saw...*

As Isaiah figuratively beheld God, the one Sovereign Lord, glorified in three Persons with most pure voices, a Seraph was sent straightway to proclaim the thrice-radiant Essence and the thrice-personal Unity.

*Glory:*

O thrice-personal Unity, Who didst formerly compose the nature of things invisible and visible out of non-being: deliver from all temptations those who faithfully hymn Thee as the one God, and make them worthy of Thy glory!

*Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

Having become a radiant and pure bridal chamber for God, O Virgin,

**[Right Page — Fol. 55r]**

we hymn thee with love and bless thee; for from thee Christ was born in dual substances and wills, Who is the only-begotten of the Father, and the Lord existing in glory.

*Ode 6:*

*Heirmos: The lowest abyss...*

Abraham, being a sojourner, was accounted worthy figuratively to receive the supra-essential Lord in three Persons, under human appearances.[^60]

*Glory:*

Direct the hearts of Thy servants unto the unapproachable Light, O thrice-personal Lord; and grant unto our souls the radiance of Thy glory, that we may be illumined by Thine ineffable beauty.

*Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

Open unto me the gates of the Light, Who was born from thy most pure womb; that beholding the radiant beam of the Godhead, I may glorify thee, the

**[Left Page — Fol. 55v]**

all-radiant Sovereign Lady.

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 3:*

*Podoben: "Today the Virgin" (Дѣ́ва дне́сь):*

When Thou shalt come upon the earth, O Master, to judge all whom Thou didst fashion from the earth according to Thine own image: show pity as Thou art merciful, and deliver all from Thy bitter judgment, and send us not into the fire of Gehenna, as Thou alone art abundantly merciful, O Christ.

*Glory, Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

Grant me sighing, O Virgin, as to the publican, and tears as to the harlot; that offering these unto thee, I may cry: Have mercy on me, O all-merciful one, and crush my perplexity by thy grace, pouring forth upon me a shower of compunction; for unto thee have I fled, O most pure one.

*Ode 7:*

*Heirmos: As of old Thou didst bedew the three...*

Show me forth, O Master, as a temple of Thy Godhead, of Thy thrice-radiant light, having been darkened by grievous sin and passions;

**[Right Page — Fol. 56r]**

by Thy light-giving splendors, O God of our fathers, blessed art Thou!

*Glory:*

We proclaim one aspect of the Godhead in three hypostatic and distinct properties: of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Spirit; crying: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers![^61]

*Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

The Creator of all appeared on earth, having become man in divine manner from thy virginal, most pure womb, and He deified us. Blessed art thou, all-pure one, most pure Mother of God!

*Ode 8:*

*Heirmos: In the unstable fire...*

The Son and the Spirit, existing co-unoriginate with the unoriginate Father as from a root, like an offshoot of the supra-essential Divinity, have shown forth the one glory and power of the Trinity, which all of us, the faithful, hymn unto the ages.

*Glory:*

Thou dost direct the heavenly ranks by Thy splendors,

**[Left Page — Fol. 56v]**

to sing ceaselessly with thrice-holy divine hymns unto the Father, the Word of one form, and the Spirit: a thrice-radiant and equal-powered dominion; therefore we hymn Thee unto all ages.

*Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

Beholding thy childbearing from afar, the prophetic proclamations praised it, O Mother of God; for without seed, and beyond nature, the Master was born; and in unison we sing unto Him as Lord, and we honor thee who didst bear Him, O Virgin, unto all ages.

*Ode 9:*

*Heirmos: A new wonder and God-befitting: for through the...*

Desiring to behold the thrice-radiant glory, the God-beholding ranks of the bodiless hosts manifestly desire to ascend on high with immaterial wings; yet they stand in deep awe before the unapproachable Light, and ceaselessly sing a hymn; and in unison with them, we glorify Thee, O single Trinity!

**[Right Page — Fol. 57r]**

*Glory:*

With insatiable love, we who are upon the earth, having received from Thee an intellectual and rational soul, hymn Thee from the whole heart, O Master, God Almighty: one Nature in truth and three Persons. Therefore, O compassionate One, preserve us as Thou art almighty!

*Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

Show me forth, O Beholder of light, as a temple of Thy sovereign and thrice-radiant Godhead, that I may purely serve Thee, the Creator of all, and intellectually behold Thine ineffable glory, through the prayers of the only Mother of God, whom we worthily magnify as most glorious.

### Notes

[^55]: Codicological Transition to Tone 3 on Fol. 50r: The concluding Sunday evening office of Tone 2 ends on Fol. 49v with a decorative floral tailpiece and the catchword *Vsubotu* ("On Saturday..."). On Fol. 50r, Tone 3 opens with a magnificent woodcut headpiece and monumental vyaz lettering *ВЪ КНИГИ ВОСКРЕСНЫ* ("Into the Resurrection Books"). The text immediately commences the first Saturday Vespers sticheron on *Lord, I have cried* (*Tvoim krestom Khriste Spase*), containing the early printed typographical haplography *rozhe* for *rod zhe*.
[^56]: The Dogmatikon of Tone 3 (*Kako ne divimsya*): Attributed to St. John of Damascus, this classic Sunday Vespers Theotokion articulates the Chalcedonian Christology of the Incarnation. The Son is begotten of the Father without a mother before all ages, and born in the flesh of the Virgin Mother without a human father, undergoing neither change (*izmeneniya* / Greek *alloiōsin*), confusion (*smesheniya* / *synchysin*), nor division (*razdeleniya* / *diairesin*), but preserving intact the specific properties (*svoystvo tselo sokhransha* / *ten idiotēta sōsan*) of each nature in one divine Hypostasis.
[^57]: The Resurrection Troparion of Tone 3 (*Da veselyatsya nebesnaya*): "Let the heavens be glad and let the earth rejoice..." (Psalm 95:11 LXX). The troparion summarizes the Paschal victory through the Magnificat typology of the arm of the Lord (*sotvori derzhavu myshtseyu svoyeyu*, Luke 1:51), trampling death by death and becoming the firstborn from the dead (Colossians 1:18), delivering humanity from the belly of Hades.
[^58]: Sunday Midnight Office Canon to the Trinity by Metrophanes (Ode 1 & 3, Tone 3): Metrophanes of Smyrna (9th c.) composed the Sunday Midnight Office canons to the Holy Trinity found across the Octoechos. In Ode 1, he invokes the classical psychological analogy of St. Gregory of Nazianzus: Mind, Word, and Spirit (*Um, Slovo, i Dukh*), imaging the unoriginate begetting of the Son and the procession of the Holy Spirit. In Ode 3, he interprets Elijah commanding water to be poured three times over the wood of the sacrifice on Mount Carmel (3 Kings / 1 Kings 18:33–34) as an Old Testament type of the Thrice-Radiant Sovereignty of God.
[^59]: Botanical Trinitarian Imagery in Ode 4 (*Otrasl sogub ot Otsa*): Metrophanes develops the patristic metaphor of the Root and the Offshoots (first articulated by Tertullian and refined by the Cappadocian Fathers): the Father is the eternal Root (*koren*), from Whom spring forth the two uncreated, consubstantial shoots (*otrasli*)—the Son by begetting and the Holy Spirit by procession—blooming as co-eternal, divine flowers sharing one essence and three sovereign hypostases.
[^60]: The Hospitable Theophany to Abraham in Ode 6 (*Preselnik syi Avraam*): Allusion to Genesis 18:1–15, where Abraham the stranger/sojourner welcomed three travelers beneath the oak of Mamre. Patristic tradition, codified liturgically and iconographically (as in the classic Byzantine *Philoxenia*), views this event as an Old Testament typological revelation of the Holy Trinity—the supra-essential Godhead foreshadowed in three distinct persons under human forms.
[^61]: Trinitarian Terminology in Ode 7 (*Bozhestva zrak yedin vozveshchayem*): The troparion balances unity and distinction: "One Form/Aspect of Godhead (*zrak yedin* / Greek *morphe*) in three hypostatic and distinct properties (*vo triyekh sostavnykh i razdelnykh svoystvekh* / *en trisi hypostatikois kai diakekrimenois idiotēsi*), Father, Son, and Holy Spirit." The response echoes the Song of the Three Youths: "Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!" (Daniel 3:52).
[^62]: Sunday Matins Transition and First Kathisma Sessional Hymn (*Khristos ot mertvykh vosta*): Following the conclusion of the Midnight Office Triadikon canon, the text transitions directly to Sunday Matins. The Troparion at *God is the Lord* (*Da veselyatsya*) and its Theotokion are cross-referenced to Saturday Vespers (*pisa vo subotu na vecherni*). The Sessional Hymn following the first Kathisma of the Psalter celebrates Christ risen as the firstfruits of those that sleep and the firstborn of creation (1 Corinthians 15:20; Colossians 1:15).
[^63]: The Hypakoe and Anabathmoi (Antiphon 1) of Tone 3: The Hypakoe (*Udivlyaya videniyem*) presents the shining angel at the tomb telling the Myrrh-bearers: "Why seek ye the Living One in the tomb? He is risen, emptying the graves!" quoting Psalm 65:3 LXX. Antiphon 1 of the Songs of Ascent (*Stepennа*) interprets the historical return of the captivity of Zion from Babylon (Psalm 125:1 LXX) as a mystical figure of the soul's liberation from passions into life by the Divine Logos, sowing tears in the south wind to reap sheaves of joy (Psalm 125:5–6 LXX).
[^64]: The printed foliation in the Kyiv 1629 edition diverges from the physical facsimile leaf index due to unnumbered front matter: the printed recto folio number at the upper right of Page_0061 (Fol. 59r) is `н҃д` (54). In Antiphon 1, the rare Church Slavonic verb `соѡблне́таетъ` (co-illumines / shines forth together) translates Greek συμφωτίζει, expressing the consubstantial co-radiance of the Holy Spirit with the Father and the Son.
[^65]: The Sunday Matins canon complex in the 1629 Kyiv Octoechos follows the classical Byzantine tri-ode system: the Resurrection Canon by St. John of Damascus, the Canon of the Cross and Resurrection, and the Canon of the Most Holy Mother of God. Here, the Theotokion of Ode 1 articulates the classical hypostatic union formula (`бг҃ъ и҆ чл҃къ и҆стебѐ роди́сѧ`: "God and Man was born of thee").
[^66]: In Ode 3 of the Canon of the Cross and Resurrection (Fol. 61r / printed `н҃ѕ`), the typesetter printed `и҆долекны́мъ`, a typographic metathesis for `и҆дольскымъ` ("idolatrous"), contrasting the purifying wood of the Cross with heathen defilement.
[^67]: In Ode 4, Golgotha is designated by the Greek calque `на крані́евѣ` (ἐν τῷ Κρανίῳ, Luke 23:33, "the Place of the Skull"). The troparion vividly balances Christ's voluntary endurance of the spitting and blows (`ѿплева́ніе носѧ̀`, `доса́дꙋ оу҆даре́ніа терпѧ̀`) with His divine majesty as Giver of life.
[^68]: In Ode 5 of the Theotokos Canon (Fol. 63r / printed `н҃и`), the poet provides an explicit Chalcedonian dyophysite definition: `сло́во бж҃іе соста́въ соверше́нъ , водво́ю є҆стествꙋ̀ , гс҃ гдⷭ҇ь : бг҃ъ соверше́нъ , и҆ чл҃къ` ("the Word of God, a perfect Hypostasis, in two natures, Jesus the Lord: perfect God and Man").
[^69]: In Ode 6 of the Canon of the Cross and Resurrection (Fol. 63v), the reading `Се́ніи І҆ѡ́на` corresponds to the Greek ἐν τύπῳ Ἰωνᾶς ("In type Jonah..."), where `сѣ́нь` (shadow, figure, type) represents the prophetic prefiguration of the three-day resurrection.
[^70]: The Heirmos of Ode 7 invokes the Three Holy Youths in the furnace of Babylon, interpreting the miracle through the light-bearing fire of Divinity (`свѣтоно́снымъ бж҃тва̀ ѻ҆гне́мъ`). The subsequent troparion references the tearing of the Temple veil (`Раздра́сѧ цр҃ко́внаѧ свѣ́тлаѧ ѡ҆по́на`, Matthew 27:51) as the unveiling of hidden prophecy to the faithful.
[^71]: In Ode 8 of the Canon of the Resurrection, the troparion upon Fol. 65v employs the theological compound `бг҃ососта́вна пло́ть` (flesh constituted/united with God), affirming the indissoluble divine-human unity accomplished at the Incarnation.
[^72]: In Ode 8 of the Theotokos Canon, the phrase `Чи́ны мысле́ныѧ... преминꙋ́ла є҆ѝ` translates the Greek phrase ὑπερβᾶσα τὰς νοερὰς τάξεις, emphasizing that the Theotokos surpassed all ranks of the bodiless heavenly powers by bearing the Creator in her womb.
[^73]: In the concluding troparion of Ode 9 of the Theotokos Canon (Fol. 68r / printed `ѯ҃г`), the Pneumatological clause `ѿ ѿц҃а тобо́ю происходѧ̀` ("Who proceedeth from the Father through Thee") reflects the Eastern Patristic formula *per Filium* (*δι' Υἱοῦ*), prominent in the writings of St. John of Damascus (*De Fide Orthodoxa* I.8) and frequently discussed in 17th-century Kyiv theological academies.
[^74]: The printed foliation in the Kyiv 1629 edition diverges from the physical facsimile leaf index by 5 leaves: the printed recto folio number at the upper right of Page_0071 (Fol. 69r) is `ѯ҃д` (64). In the third Sunday Praises sticheron on Fol. 68v, the dramatic Easter proclamation of the angel incorporates the synoptic resurrection narratives (Luke 24:5–6; Matthew 28:7), balancing epigraphic fidelity with the vivacity of the liturgical dialogue (`нѣ́сть здѣ̀, но воста̀ ꙗ҆́кѡ речѐ`).
[^75]: The sticheron on Fol. 69v employs the rare dual/collective expression `сꙋпрꙋ́гъ же живоно́сныхъ а҆́гг҃лъ` ("a pair of life-bearing angels", reflecting Luke 24:4 and John 20:12). The subsequent sticheron contrasts the enclosing of Christ in the tomb by the Jews with the penitent thief opening paradise with his confession (`разбо́йникъ же ѿве́рзе ꙗ҆зы́комъ ра́й`), affirming Christ's simultaneous suffering on the Cross and co-enthronement with the Father.
[^76]: A major codicological error occurs in the printed foliation at quire S (gathering signature `С`): the recto folio printed mark jumps from `ѯ҃є` (65) on Fol. 70r to `о҃а` (71) on Fol. 71r, skipping folios 66–70 in error, while the hymnographic text and quire continuity are completely intact. On Fol. 71r, the Sunday evening transition to Tone 3 Penitential Stichera is marked by a typographic band ornament, opening the penitential cycle of the week.
[^77]: In the stichera to the Holy Bodiless Angels on Fol. 72r (appointed for Sunday evening Vespers anticipating Monday, dedicated to the angelic hosts), the angels are designated as "secondary lights" (`вторы́ѧ свѣ́тлости`), directly echoing the Areopagitic celestial hierarchy wherein the angelic orders reflect the primary, uncreated light of the Holy Trinity unto the created cosmos.
