# TONE 4: RESURRECTIONAL AND WEEKDAY HYMNOGRAPHY

## TONE 4

*On Saturday evening, at "Lord, I have cried," Tone 4:*

*Stichera of the Resurrection, Tone 4:*

Unto Thy life-creating Cross do we unceasingly bow, O Christ, and Thy third-day Resurrection do we glorify; for thereby hast Thou renewed the decayed nature of mankind, O Almighty One, and renewed for us the ascent unto the heavens, as the only Good One and Lover of mankind.[^78]

Thou hast annulled the condemnation of the tree of disobedience, O Savior, by being willingly nailed unto the tree of the Cross;

**[Left Page — Fol. 73v]**

and having descended into Hades, O Mighty One, Thou hast burst asunder the bonds of death as God; therefore we worship Thy Resurrection from the dead, crying out with joy: O Almighty Lord, glory to Thee!

Thou hast shattered the gates of Hades, O Lord, and by Thy death hast Thou abolished the kingdom of death; and having freed the human race from corruption, Thou hast granted life and incorruption unto the world, and great mercy.

*Other Stichera, Anatolian:*

Come, ye peoples, let us sing the third-day rising of the Savior, whereby we have been delivered from the unbreakable bonds of Hades, and have received incorruption and life, crying out: O Thou Who wast crucified and buried, and didst rise: save us by Thy Resurrection, O only Lover of mankind!

**[Right Page — Fol. 74r]**

Angels and men, O Savior, sing of Thy third-day rising, whereby the ends of the universe have been enlightened, and we all have been delivered from the servitude of the enemy, crying out: O Life-Creator, Almighty Savior, save us by Thy Resurrection, O only Lover of mankind!

Thou hast shattered the gates of Hades, and crushed the bars, O Christ God, and raised the fallen human race; therefore with one accord we cry unto Thee: Risen from the dead, O Lord, glory to Thee![^79]

O Lord, Thy generation from the Father is timeless and co-eternal, while Thine incarnation from the Virgin is ineffable and unutterable unto men; and Thy descent into Hades was fearful unto the devil and his angels; for having trampled down death, Thou didst rise on the third day, granting incorruption unto men, and great mercy.

**[Left Page — Fol. 74v]**

*Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

*Theotokion:*

The Prophet David, who through thee was the forefather of God, melodiously proclaimed concerning thee unto Him that hath done great things for thee: The queen stood at Thy right hand. For God, Who was pleased without father to become man from thee, revealed thee as mother and mediatress of life, that He might recreate His own image decayed by passions, and having found the lost sheep caught by the wolf, and taken it upon His shoulders, He might bring it unto the Father, and according to His own will unite it with the heavenly powers, and save the world, O Mother of God, Christ Who possesses great and abundant mercy.[^80]

*At the Aposticha, Stichera of the Resurrection, Tone 4:*

Ascending the Cross, O Lord, Thou hast wiped out our ancestral curse; and descending into Hades, Thou hast freed the eternal prisoners, granting incorruption unto the human race;

**[Right Page — Fol. 75r]**

therefore singing, we glorify Thy life-creating and saving Resurrection.

*Other Stichera:*

Suspended upon the tree, O only Mighty One, Thou didst shake all creation; and laid in the tomb, Thou didst raise those dwelling in the tombs, granting incorruption and life unto the human race; therefore singing, we glorify Thy third-day rising.

Lawless people, O Christ, having delivered Thee unto Pilate, condemned Thee to be crucified, showing themselves ungrateful for Thy benefits; but willingly having endured burial, by Thine own authority Thou didst rise on the third day as God, granting us unending life and great mercy.

With tears the women, having reached the tomb, sought Thee; and finding Thee not, lamenting with weeping they cried aloud, saying:

**[Left Page — Fol. 75v]**

"Woe is us, our Savior, King of all! How hast Thou been stolen? And what place holds Thy life-bearing body?" But the angel answered them, saying: "Weep not, but go and proclaim that the Lord is risen, granting us joy, as the only Merciful One."

*Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

*Theotokion:*

Grant consolation unto Thy servants, O all-blameless one, calming the fierce assaults against us, and delivering us from every sorrow; for thee alone do we hold as a firm and unshakable foundation, and we have acquired thy protection; let us not be put to shame, O Sovereign Lady, who call upon thee; hasten unto the prayer of those that cry unto thee: Rejoice, O Sovereign Lady, helper of all, the joy and protection of our souls!

**[Right Page — Fol. 76r]**

*Canon to the Holy and Life-Originating Trinity, Tone 4:*

*Ode 1. Heirmos: The Red Sea:*

Let us glorify the God-originating Trinity in hypostases, unique in nature, three, co-eternal, sharing one throne; and praying, let us say: Save by faith those that honor Thee.[^81]

Anointed by the Father with the Spirit of gladness, with the God-working oil, as Son and having become Man, He hath taught the tri-hypostatic unity of the one Godhead.

Beholding the beauty of Thine unapproachable glory, O tri-hypostatic Unity, the Seraphim, unable to gaze thereon, cover themselves with their wings, and with thrice-holy hymns unceasingly glorify Thee.

*Theotokion:*

Ineffably hast thou given birth unto the Creator of all, O most pure one, Who delivers men from the ancient curse and from mortal corruption; and through thee have we come to know the one tri-hypostatic God.

**[Left Page — Fol. 76v]**

*Ode 3. Heirmos: Neither by wisdom, nor by power, nor by riches:*

Power from on high didst Thou give unto Thy holy apostles, O Christ, when Thou didst send the Comforter from the Father: Thou hast revealed the one tri-hypostatic nature.

When Thou didst appear unto Patriarch Abraham in human form, O Trinitarian Unity, Thou didst show forth the immutable dominion of Thy goodness.

O Thou Who in three Persons art believed in as one God, visibly uncircumscribed, incomprehensible unto all: deliver our souls from every affliction.

*Theotokion:*

Instructed visibly by the teachings of thy Son, we glorify the single and thrice-radiant divine Principle; and we ever bless thee, O Virgin.

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 4.*

*Podoben: "Speedily anticipate" (Ско́рѡ предварѝ):*

He Who alone by a beckoning and by the most pure counsel of Thy Father and the Spirit didst create all things, O most merciful Christ, didst accept crucifixion for the human race; therefore giving thanks, we hymn Thee, crying: Have mercy on Thy flock, O only Lover of mankind![^82]

**[Right Page — Fol. 77r]**

*Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

*Theotokion:*

We hymn thee, O Bride of God, Mother of Christ God, glorifying thine incomprehensible childbearing, whereby we have been delivered from the deceit of the devil and from all perils, O Sovereign Lady, Mother of God; therefore in faith we cry out: Have mercy on thy flock, O only all-hymned one!

*Ode 4. Heirmos: Sitting in glory:*

O super-essential Trinity, in the unity of the Godhead and Lordship, with the Seraphim we glorify Thee as an indivisible nature, as unconfused, as equally powerful: O God incomprehensible in glory!

Divided ineffably in the Persons of the Godhead, and united by the dominion of one Lordship, boundless, one, uncircumscribed, we hymn Thee, the Creator of all creation.

**[Left Page — Fol. 77v]**

The unoriginate Mind ineffably begat the Word, and the Divine Spirit proceeded with equal power; and because of them we proclaim the consubstantial Trinity, and God, the Master of all.

*Theotokion:*

Seen of old typologically, the incarnation of the Word from thee was foretold, O Virgin; but later appearing unto men, He truly manifested the tri-hypostatic monarchy.

*Ode 5. Heirmos: All things were amazed:*

The wings of the all-effecting Godhead understood the one unapproachable essence; and the three life-originating Hypostases we hymn as equals, the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit: a co-eternal existence.

O thrice-radiant Light of Thine essential light, shine upon me, O unique Godhead, uncreated nature, and Fountain that gives light, with every light-giving ray, that I may contemplate Thine ineffable beauty.[^83]

**[Right Page — Fol. 78r]**

As the one true Creator of all, Upholder, all-wise Pilot, and Giver of life: we the faithful fall down before Thee, O tri-hypostatic Master, and pray: Guard those that hymn Thee!

*Theotokion:*

Wishing to deify man who of old had decayed, whom for His goodness He had created, Thy Son, O Virgin, showing forth the image of the divine likeness, became Man from thee, and proclaimed the one tri-hypostatic divine Principle.

*Ode 6. Heirmos: The abyss:*

The Father revealed sonship by voice, and the Spirit was seen when Christ was baptized; therefore we glorify the one and threefold divine Principle.

**[Left Page — Fol. 78v]**

When Isaiah beheld Thee seated upon a high throne, hymned with thrice-holy voices, he recognized the three Hypostases of the one divine Principle.[^84]

Show forth an exalted heart also in us Thy servants, O exalted, tri-hypostatic King, that we may clearly behold the radiant brightness of Thy glory.

*Theotokion:*

I have sinned, yet have I not departed from Thee, wretch that I am; have compassion on me, O tri-hypostatic, one venerable Godhead, and deliver me from grievous condemnation, through the supplications of the Maiden of God.

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 4.*

*Podoben: "Thou Who wast lifted up upon the Cross" (Вознесы́йся на Крⷭ҇тъ во́лею):*

Incomprehensible and unapproachable God, Who art alone by nature good and merciful, Father, Son, and all-holy Spirit: preserve and sanctify those who in faith cry aloud in Thy holy church by night and by day; and deliver us not over, O Master, unto enemies who seek our destruction. *Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

**[Right Page — Fol. 79r]**

*Theotokion:*

We Thy servants shall never cease, O Virgin, thankfully to hymn Thine acts of mercy from the heart, O Sovereign Lady, crying out and saying: O most holy Virgin, anticipate and deliver us from invisible enemies, and from perils and every threat; for thou art our protection.

*Ode 7. Heirmos: In the fiery furnace the Persian children of...*

Imitating the ordered heavenly natures and the noetic ranks in right belief, all we earthborn glorify the one Godhead in three equally-active Persons.

The utterances of the holy prophets of old typologically showed Thee forth as the sole Creator of all ages, the ineffable God and Lord, in three God-originating Persons.

*Theotokion:*

The Word, Who by essence is invisible and all-effecting, was revealed unto men as Man from the pure Mother of God, calling man to communion with Thy Godhead, O Christ.

**[Left Page — Fol. 79v]**

*Ode 8. Heirmos: Spreading out his hands, Daniel:*

O Light unique and thrice-radiant, unoriginate Essence, unfathomable Beauty: take up Thine abode in my soul, and show me forth as a luminous and pure temple of Thy Godhead, as I cry aloud: Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord; hymn and exalt Him above all unto all ages![^85]

From diverse passions, O indivisible Trinity, unconfused Unity, deliver me from the darkening of transgressions, and illumine me with Thy God-working rays, that I may behold Thy glory and hymn Thee unto the glory of the Lord.

The super-essential Mind, the Father; and the con-formable Word; and the co-enthroned Spirit: one essence, power, and being, super-essential, ineffable, majestic Trinity in Unity: preserve Thy flock through the prayers of the Mother of God, as the only Lover of mankind.

**[Right Page — Fol. 80r]**

*Ode 9. Heirmos: Let every earthborn mortal:*

Unto Thee I direct all my heart and mind, and all love of both soul and body, unto my Creator and Deliverer, O Sovereign Three-Sun Light; and I cry unto Thee: Save me Thy servant from tribulations and all afflictions!

Exalt our mind and thought unto Thee on high, and illumine us with Thy most pure radiance, O Father, Word, and Comforter, Who dwellest in unapproachable light, O Sun and light-holding Lord of glory: that we may ever glorify Thee, the one Principle, the tri-hypostatic God.

One Light in three Hypostases, distinguished in Persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, by Whom all creation subsists; Who descending renewed it, and rose on the third day.

*Theotokion:*

Save those that believe in Thee, O Lord, and proclaim Thine unoriginate, eternal, single essence, and the three God-originating and con-formable Persons of Thy Lordship; and make them worthy of divine glory, through the supplications of the pure Mother of God.

**[Left Page — Fol. 80v]**

*At Matins, at "God is the Lord", the Resurrection Troparion, Tone 4:*

Having learned the radiant proclamation of the Resurrection from the angel, and having cast off the ancestral condemnation, the women disciples of the Lord boasting said unto the apostles: Death is overthrown, and Christ God is risen, granting great mercy unto the world![^86]

*Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

*Theotokion:*

The mystery hidden from the ages and unknown unto angels, through thee, O Mother of God, was manifested unto those on earth: God incarnate in unconfused union, Who voluntarily accepted the Cross for our sake; and having raised up the first-fashioned man, He saved our souls from death.

**[Right Page — Fol. 81r]**

*After the First Kathisma, Resurrection Sessional Hymn, Tone 4:*

Beholding the entrance of the tomb and unable to endure the angelic flame, the myrrh-bearing women marvelled in trembling, saying: Was He stolen away, Who opened paradise unto the thief? Or is He risen, Who before His passion proclaimed resurrection? Truly Christ is risen, granting life and resurrection unto those in Hades!

*Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

*Theotokion:*

Thee we magnify, O Mother of God, crying aloud: Thou art the mountain from which the stone was cut without hands, and which crushed the gates of Hades.

*After the Second Kathisma, Sessional Hymn, Tone 4:*

Of Thine own will didst Thou endure crucifixion, O Savior, and mortal men laid Thee in a new tomb, Who by Word didst establish the ends of the earth; therefore was the stranger bound, and death was grievously despoiled; and those in Hades all cried out unto Thy life-bearing resurrection: Christ the Giver of life is risen, abiding unto all ages!

**[Left Page — Fol. 81v]**

*Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

*Theotokion:*

Joseph was amazed, beholding that which was beyond nature, and considered in mind the dew upon the fleece in thy seedless conception, O Mother of God: the bush unburnt by fire, the rod of Aaron that budded; and bearing witness as thy betrothed and guardian, he cried aloud unto the priests: A Virgin gives birth, and after childbirth remains Virgin again![^87]

*After the Amomos, the Hypakoe:*

Forestalling the dawn of Thy most glorious resurrection, the myrrh-bearing women proclaimed unto the apostles that Christ is risen as God, granting great mercy unto the world.

*The Gradual Hymns (Anabathmoi), Tone 4. Antiphon 1:*

From my youth many passions war against me; but do Thou Thyself defend and save me, O my Savior.

**[Right Page — Fol. 82r]**

Ye that hate Zion, be put to shame before the Lord; for as grass by fire shall ye be dried up.

By the Holy Spirit every soul is quickened, and by purity exalted, illuminated by the Trinitarian Unity in sacred mystery.

*Antiphon 2:*

I have cried unto Thee fervently, O Lord, from the depths of my soul; let Thy divine ears be attentive unto me.

Whosoever has acquired hope in the Lord is higher than all who grieve.

By the Holy Spirit stream forth the waters of grace, watering all creation unto revitalization.

*Antiphon 3:*

Let my heart be lifted up unto Thee, O Word, and let none of the world's allurements beguile me unto weakness.

**[Left Page — Fol. 82v]**

As one has love toward his mother, with warmer affection are we indebted to love the Lord.[^88]

By the Holy Spirit is the wealth of divine knowledge, contemplation, and wisdom; for in Him the Word reveals all the Father's decrees.

*Prokeimenon, Tone 4:* Arise, O Lord, help us, and redeem us for Thy name's sake. *Verse:* O God, with our ears have we heard. *Let every breath praise the Lord. Verse:* Praise God in His saints. *The Gospel.*

*Canon of the Resurrection, Tone 4. Ode 1. Heirmos:*

Traversing the depth of the Red Sea of old with dry feet, Israel, by the cross-wise stretched hands of Moses, overcame the power of Amalek in the wilderness.

*Troparia:*

Thou wast lifted up upon the most pure wood of the Cross, setting right our fall, healing the ancient murder, O Master, as good and all-powerful.

**[Right Page — Fol. 83r]**

In the tomb bodily, in Hades with Thy soul as God, in Paradise with the thief, and on the throne wast Thou, O Christ, with the Father and the Spirit, filling all things, Thyself uncircumscribed.

*Theotokion:*

Without seed, by the Father's will, from the divine Spirit of God thou didst conceive the Son, and in the flesh gavest birth unto Him Who from the Father was without mother, and for our sake from thee without father.

*Another Canon, of the Cross and Resurrection, Tone 4. Ode 1. Heirmos: I shall open my mouth:*

Healing the brokenness of mankind, O Lord, by this Thy divine blood Thou didst renew it, and didst shatter the one strong in might who of old had shattered Thy creation.

Resurrection came unto the dead by reason of His putting to death; for power was taken away from death, which became food unto eternal life, when God, Who rules over all, became incarnate.

**[Left Page — Fol. 83v]**

*Theotokion:*

Fair and exalted above the heavenly hosts was thy divine ensouled temple, O Virgin, when in thy womb thou didst carry the holy mountain of our God.[^89]

*Another Canon to the Most Holy Mother of God, the same Tone. Heirmos: The captains:*

The peoples were shaken, the nations were troubled, and mighty kingdoms yielded, O pure one, from awe at thy childbearing; for my King came, cast down the tormentor, and delivered the world from corruption.

Descending unto men, O Christ, Who dwellest on high, Thou didst sanctify Thine abode and showed it forth unshaken; for after childbirth the only Virgin vessel preserved the Creator Whom she bare.

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*

Thy church rejoices in Thee, O Christ, crying aloud: Thou art my strength, O Lord, my refuge and my firm support!

*Troparia:*

The living tree, the true noetic vine, hangs upon the wood, pouring forth incorruption unto all.

**[Right Page — Fol. 84r]**

How great, how awesome! He cast down the arrogance of Hades, and as incorruptible God is now risen bodily from the tomb.

*Theotokion:*

Thou alone upon earth wast made the mediatress of blessings beyond nature, Mother of God; therefore do we cry unto thee: Rejoice!

*Another Canon (of the Cross and Resurrection). Ode 3. Heirmos: Thy singers:*

The serpent plunged his venom-filled fangs into me, O Savior; but Thou, O Almighty Lord, didst shatter them with the nails of Thy hands; for there is none holy among the saints save Thee, O Lord.

Thou wast seen voluntarily in the tomb, O Lover of mankind, dead yet life-giving, and didst burst open the gates of Hades for the souls bound from the ages; for there is none holy among the saints save Thee, O Lord.

*Theotokion:*

Thou wast shown forth as the unplowed furrow, having brought forth the living ear of wheat, the mediator of immortality unto all who partake of Him Who rests as Holy among the saints.

**[Left Page — Fol. 84v]**

*Another Canon (to the Theotokos). Ode 3. Heirmos: He Who is on high:*

The essence of earthly mortals is purified, having conversed with the unendurable divine fire; for mystically, O all-pure Virgin, within thee was the Bread baked by Him Who preserved thee unharmed.[^90]

Who is this who truly is close unto God, having surpassed the angelic ranks, shining alone through the beauty of maidenhood as the Mother of the Almighty?

*Canon of the Resurrection. Ode 4. Heirmos:*

Beholding Thee, the Sun of righteousness, lifted up upon the Cross, the church stood in her order, worthily crying aloud: Glory to Thy power, O Lord!

*Troparia:*

Having tasted of the sinless and life-creating body of Thine, death was worthily put to death, O Master; and we cry aloud unto Thee: Glory to Thy power, O Lord!

**[Right Page — Fol. 85r]**

Thou didst come healing my passions, when Thy most pure flesh, wherein Thou wast willingly clothed, suffered upon the Cross; therefore we cry unto Thee: Glory to Thy power, O Lord!

*Theotokion:*

Without knowing wedlock didst thou give birth, O Virgin, and after childbirth wast shown forth still a Virgin; therefore with frail voices, with undoubting faith, we cry unto thee: Rejoice, O Sovereign Lady!

*Another Canon (of the Cross and Resurrection). Ode 4. Heirmos: Unto the inscrutable divine counsel:*

The lawful Israel did not recognize Thee, O Christ, the God Who gave the Law; but as lawless, transgressing the law, unworthy of the legislation, nailed Thee to the Cross.

Thy deified soul, O Savior, despoiled the treasuries of Hades and raised the souls bound from the ages; and Thy life-bearing body poured forth incorruption unto all.

**[Left Page — Fol. 85v]**

*Theotokion:*

Thee, the truly ever-virgin, the only Mother of God, do we glorify: whom Moses prefigured in the bush that was mingled with fire unconsumed, O most pure one.[^91]

*Another Canon (to the Theotokos). Ode 4. The same Heirmos:*

Bearing our corruption, the Invisible One became visible, being in the fullness of invisible Godhead, and taking form from thee, O Virgin, to save from the alien one those who know thee as the pure Mother of God.

Materially the Virgin received the Immaterial One in the midst of matter, become an Infant from her; therefore is He now known in two natures: God bearing flesh, and an earthly mortal above nature.

He Who took up His abode in thee, a virgin, after childbirth remained Word and God, born without seed; and in childbirth He preserved thee a Virgin, as Master and Creator.

**[Right Page — Fol. 86r]**

*Canon of the Resurrection. Ode 5. Heirmos:*

Thou, my Lord, art a light come into the world: a holy light turning those that hymn Thee in faith from the darkness of ignorance.

*Troparia:*

Thou, O Lord, didst mercifully descend unto the earth; Thou didst raise up fallen human nature when Thou wast lifted up upon the tree.

Thou didst take away from me, O Christ, the condemnation of transgressions; Thou didst dissolve the pangs of death, O Compassionate One, by Thy divine resurrection.

*Theotokion:*

Thee we set forth as an invincible weapon against enemies; thee have we acquired as the confirmation and hope of our salvation, O Bride of God.

**[Left Page — Fol. 86v]**

*Another Canon (of the Cross and Resurrection). Ode 5. Heirmos: All things were amazed:*

Senseless Hades received Thee wholly into his mouth; for beholding Thee nailed to the Cross and pierced with a spear, lifeless, the living God, he deemed Thee mere man; yet he recognized Thee when he experienced the might of Thy Godhead.[^92]

The tomb and Hades shared the destroyed temple of Thy body, O Lover of mankind; unwillingly both were despoiled: the one sending back the souls of Thy saints, and the other their bodies, O Immortal One.

*Theotokion:*

Behold, now is fulfilled the prophetic utterance; for thou, unwedded Virgin, didst receive in thy womb God Who is over all, and gavest birth unto the timeless Son, Who grants peace unto all who hymn thee.

*Another Canon (to the Theotokos). Ode 5. Heirmos: Now will I arise prophetically:*

A house of glory and a holy mountain of God, O pure one; a bride, a bridal chamber, a temple of sanctification: in thee the Son of God made His dwelling, and made for us an everlasting paradise of delight.

**[Right Page — Fol. 87r]**

Flesh from virgin blood didst Thou receive, O Christ: seedless, most pure and substantial, endowed with reason, mind, and soul, active, willing, self-governing, and free.

The virginal womb put to shame the understanding of the tyrants; for as an Infant He explored with His hand the soul-destroying hole of the asp, and casting down the proud apostate, made him a footstool for the faithful.

*Canon of the Resurrection. Ode 6. Heirmos:*

I will sacrifice unto Thee with the voice of praise, O Lord, the church cries out unto Thee, having been cleansed from the blood of demons by the blood that flowed in mercy from Thy side.

*Troparia:*

Thou didst ascend upon the Cross, girded with power, and wrestling with the tormentor, as God Thou didst cast that adversary down from the heights; and by Thine unconquerable power Thou didst raise up Adam.

**[Left Page — Fol. 87v]**

Risen radiant and fair from the tomb, O Christ, Thou didst scatter all enemies by divine power, and as God filled all things with gladness.[^93]

*Theotokion:*

O wonder newest of all wonders! For the Virgin in her womb received without knowing man Him Who upholds all things by His word, and contained Him without constriction.

*Another Canon (of the Cross and Resurrection). Ode 6. Heirmos: We traversed the depths of the sea:*

Hades opened his gullet and dilated it, senseless one, and swallowed down me and my soul; but Thou, O Christ, descending, didst raise up my life, as the Lover of mankind.

Death perished by death; for He Who died is risen, granting me incorruption; and appearing unto the women, He proclaimed immortal joy.

**[Right Page — Fol. 88r]**

*Theotokion:*

A tranquil haven of unendurable Godhead was shown forth in thy pure womb, O Mother of God, which without fear the heavenly hosts cannot behold.

*Another Canon (to the Theotokos). Ode 6. The same Heirmos:*

Of old the serpent beguiled and slew me because of my foremother Eve; but now, O pure one, He Who created me recalled me from incorruption for thy sake.

The abyss of ineffable loving-kindness, O Maiden, showed thee forth as an abyss chosen for miracles; for from thee, with the lightning of the Godhead, shone forth the pearl, Christ.

*Canon of the Resurrection. Ode 7. Heirmos:*

In the Persian furnace, the Abrahamic children, rather burned by love of true piety than by the flame, cried aloud: Blessed art Thou in the temple of Thy glory, O Lord!

**[Left Page — Fol. 88v]**

Mankind was led back unto incorruption, washed by the divine Blood of Christ, as we thankfully hymn: Blessed art Thou in the temple of Thy glory, O Lord.

As life-bearing, as truly fairer than paradise, and brighter than any royal bridal chamber, was Thy tomb revealed, O Christ, the fountain of our resurrection.

*Theotokion:*

Rejoice, O sanctified, divine dwelling-place of the Most High! For through thee joy is given unto those who cry unto thee, O Theotokos: Blessed art thou among women, O all-immaculate Sovereign Lady.

*Canon of the Cross and Resurrection. Another Heirmos: Disobedient...:*

Being lifted up upon the Wood, Thou didst humble the lofty eye, casting down exalted pride unto the earth; and Thou didst save man, O praised God of our fathers: Blessed art Thou!

**[Right Page — Fol. 89r]**

By Thy power, O Christ, Who didst rise from the dead, exalt the horn of us who serve Thee, and exhaust the wealth of the deceiver of many men, O praised God of our fathers: Blessed art Thou!

*Triadikon:*

Following the divine utterances, we glorify the one Godhead as in three lights, unmingled, indivisible, and as one flame: O praised God of our fathers: Blessed art Thou!

*Canon to the Theotokos. Another Heirmos: The three youths...:*

Virginal love draws me unto hymning, and the fire has stirred me up to cry aloud unto the Mother and Virgin: Blessed art thou, the Lord of hosts is with thee!

Thou art higher than all creatures, since thou didst bear the Creator and Lord; therefore do I cry unto thee, O Theotokos: Blessed art thou, the Lord of hosts is with thee!

*Triadikon:*

I revere one Lordship in three hypostases, indivisible, and I sing the tri-hypostatic Nature:

**[Left Page — Fol. 89v]**

Blessed art Thou, O Lord God of our fathers![^95]

*Ode 8. Heirmos: Spreading out his hands, Daniel:*

Spreading out his hands, Daniel shut the lions' gaping mouths in the pit; and, girded with virtue, the youths, lovers of piety, quenched the power of the fire, crying aloud: Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord!

Appearing white-robed unto the women, radiant with unapproachable light, the Angel of the Resurrection cried aloud: Why seek ye the living among the dead? In truth Christ is risen! Unto Whom we cry: Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord; sing and exalt Him above all unto all ages!

*Canon of the Cross and Resurrection. Another Heirmos: The pious youths in the furnace...:*

Beholding Thine unjust slaughter, creation was darkened and wept; for while the earth was shaken, the sun was clothed in darkness as in a dark mantle; yet we hymn Thee, O Christ, and exalt Thee above all unto all ages.

Having descended with me even unto Hades, Thou didst make the Resurrection a pathway for all; and Thou didst ascend again, bearing me upon Thy shoulders, and didst bring me unto the Father. Therefore I cry unto Thee: Hymn the Lord, all ye works, and exalt Him above all unto all ages!

**[Left Page — Fol. 90v]**

*Triadikon:*

Proclaiming Thee as the primal Mind and the Cause of all, the only uncaused God of all, the unoriginate Begetter of the Word, and the Projector of the Spirit, and the Creator of all: we bow down before the harmonious Trinity and exalt Thee above all unto all ages.[^96]

*Canon to the Theotokos. Another Heirmos: Redeemer of all...:*

He Who fashioned thee from the rib of Adam, the Cause of all, is incarnate of thy virginity; hymning Whom, we cry aloud: Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord; sing and exalt Him above all unto all ages!

**[Right Page — Fol. 91r]**

*Ode 9. Heirmos: A stone cut without hands:*

A cornerstone was cut without hands from an uncut mountain, even from thee, O Virgin: Christ, Who joined together the parted natures; therefore rejoicing, we magnify thee, O Theotokos.

*Troparia:*

**[Left Page — Fol. 91v]**

*Triadikon:*

All we faithful praise the unity of the divine essence, yet the Trinity in Hypostases, in unconfused Persons: the Father supremely unoriginate, the Son, and the all-holy Spirit.[^97]

*Canon of the Cross and Resurrection. Another Heirmos: Let every earthborn mortal leap for joy...:*

Creeping deceitfully, the serpent took me captive from Eden; yet the Lord Almighty, having dashed that serpent against the hard rock on the Place of the Skull like an infant, opened unto me once again the entrance of delight, by reason of the Wood of the Cross.

Thou didst lay waste the strong bulwarks of the enemy, and with Thine almighty hand Thou didst plunder his riches, O Christ, and didst raise Adam up with Thyself; and him who of old boasted immeasurably, Thou didst show forth as mocked with derision.

**[Right Page — Fol. 92r]**

Come, visiting the affliction of Thy poor people; and with Thy merciful and mighty hand strengthen the cross-bearing king against blasphemous enemies, to deliver Thine elect inheritance, as the Lover of mankind.

*Canon to the Theotokos. Another Heirmos: O most pure mystery of God...:*

We behold thee, O pure one, clothed like a lily, O all-immaculate one, shining in the midst of thorns through the Divine Spirit, and filling with fragrance those who sincerely magnify thee.

Taking corruptible earthly nature from thine all-immaculate womb, the Incorruptible One showed it forth in Himself as entirely incorruptible through the bowels of His compassion; therefore do we magnify thee as the Theotokos.

O Sovereign Lady Who rulest over all creatures, grant unto Thy people victorious overcome; subdue the disobedient under the feet of the king, that we may magnify thee as the Theotokos.

**[Left Page — Fol. 92v]**

...magnify thee.[^98]

Thou Who didst endure the Cross and death, and didst rise from the dead: almighty Lord, we glorify Thy Resurrection.

By Thy Cross, O Christ, Thou didst deliver us from the ancient curse; and by Thy death Thou didst abolish the devil that tormented our nature; and by Thy rising Thou didst fill all things with joy; therefore we cry unto Thee: O Thou Who didst rise from the dead, Lord, glory to Thee!

By Thy Cross, O Christ Savior, guide us unto Thy truth, and deliver us from the snares of the enemy; O Thou Who didst rise from the dead, raise us who are fallen through sin, stretching forth Thy hand, O Lord, Lover of mankind, through the prayers of Thy saints.

Without departing from Thy Father's bosom, O only-begotten Word of God, Thou camest down to earth for love of mankind, becoming man without undergoing change; and Thou didst endure crucifixion and death in the flesh, Who art impassible in Thy Godhead; and rising from the dead, Thou didst grant immortality unto the race of men, as the only Almighty One.

*Other Stichera, Anatolian:*

Thou didst accept death in the flesh, dispensing immortality unto us, O Savior, and didst take up Thy dwelling in the tomb, that Thou mightest free us from Hades, raising us up together with Thyself: Thou didst suffer indeed as man, but Thou didst rise as God; therefore do we cry: Glory to Thee, O Giver of life, Lord, only Lover of mankind!

The rocks were rent, O Savior, when Thy Cross was set up on the Place of the Skull; the gatekeepers of Hades were terrified when Thou wast laid in the tomb as dead; for abolishing the power of death, Thou didst grant incorruption unto all the dead by Thy Resurrection, O Christ: Giver of life, Lord, glory to Thee!

**[Left Page — Fol. 93v]**

Where is Jesus Whom ye thought to guard? Tell us, O Jews! Where is He Whom ye laid in the tomb, sealing the stone? Give up the dead man, ye that have rejected Life! Give up the Buried One, or else believe in Him Who is risen! Even though ye keep silence concerning the Lord's rising, the stones shall cry out, and most of all the one rolled away from the tomb! Great is Thy mercy, great is the mystery of Thy dispensation: our Savior, glory to Thee!

Having the Cross of Christ in our thoughts, let us all strive to mortify worldly pleasures, making our souls a temple of the Holy Spirit, sending up the voice of the wise thief in faith unto Him: Remember us also in Thy Kingdom!

Unto Him Who is risen from the dead and plundered the power of Hades, and appeared unto the myrrh-bearing women, crying unto them: Rejoice!

**[Left Page — Fol. 94v]**

Truly Christ is risen, and the tomb bears witness unto it, O ye transgressors of the Law! *Glory:*

With one accord let all of us the faithful pray worthily to doxologize the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit: the Unity of the Godhead abiding unconfused in three Hypostases, simple, undivided, and unapproachable, which delivers us from the torment of the fire.

*Both now:*

Thy Mother, O Christ, who bore Thee in the flesh without seed, and truly remained an incorrupt Virgin after childbearing: her do we offer unto Thee in prayer, O exceedingly merciful Master, to grant forgiveness of sins unto those who ever cry in faith:

Rejoice, in truth, O treasury of life![^101]

*Podoben: "Thou hast given a sign" (Да́лъ є҆сѝ зна́менїе):*

I have sinned against Thee, O Lover of mankind, not according to human nature that I might seek forgiveness, but beyond man and beyond nature, and beyond forgiveness; yet Thou Who hast mouths beyond nature and beyond comprehension of intellect, having become man, O my Savior, and possessing a love for mankind that passes understanding: have mercy on me who turn unto Thee!

Thou didst appoint repentance for sinners and not for the righteous; for having as patterns the thief, the prodigal, Manasseh, the harlot, the persecutor, the publican, and the blasphemer: not easily do we despair, knowing Thy love for mankind and Thy supreme goodness, O Christ my Savior; turning unto Thee and pouring forth tears, I am of good hope that Thou wilt receive me.

Grant me compunction, estrangement from evils, and perfect amendment, unto me who am now plunged in bodily passions, far removed from Thee, O God, King of all, and possessing no hope whatever: save me, the prodigal, for the sake of Thy great goodness, O all-powerful Jesus, Savior of our souls!

*Podoben: "As one valiant among martyrs" (Я҆́кѡ до́бля):*

### Notes

[^78]: Tone 4 opens on Fol. 73r (printed `о҃г` [73], recte 68) beneath a monumental foliate woodcut headpiece across the full width of the text block and a large ornamental initial `Ж`, preceded by the calligraphic title inscription `ВЪ КНИГИ ВОСКРЕСНЫ, Гла́съ , Четве́ртый` ("In the Books of the Resurrection, Tone 4"). The first sticheron celebrates the restoration of fallen human nature and the reopened ascent to heaven accomplished by the Cross.
[^79]: In the sticheron upon Fol. 74r, the typesetter set `ве́рєѧ стертерла́зъ є҆сѝ`, an apparent dittographic typographical slip for `ве́рєѧ стерлъ є҆сѝ` ("Thou hast crushed the bars [of Hades]", Psalm 106:16 / LXX Ps. 106:16), celebrating Christ's shattering of the brazen gates and iron bars of the netherworld.
[^80]: The Dogmatic Theotokion of Tone 4 on Fol. 74v (`Иже тебѐ ра́ди бг҃оѻ҆тц҃ъ пррⷦ҇къ Дв҃дъ`) articulates the royal Davidic typology (Psalm 44:10 / LXX Ps. 44:10: "The queen stood at Thy right hand") alongside the Parable of the Lost Sheep (Luke 15:4–6), depicting Christ lifting the fallen, wolf-ravaged sheep upon His shoulders to present it unto the Father.
[^81]: Fol. 76r opens the Sunday Midnight Office with the Canon to the Holy and Life-Originating Trinity in Tone 4, attributed to Metrophanes of Smyrna. The hymnography is densely dogmatic, employing precise Trinitarian terminology (`Тр҃цꙋ бг҃онача́льнꙋ дапросла́вимъ соста́вы . є҆ди́нственною же є҆стество́мъ тріе́хъ , соприсносꙋ́щнꙋ сопресто́льнꙋ`), asserting the co-eternity and consubstantiality of the three Hypostases.
[^82]: In Ode 3 of the Trinity Canon (Fol. 76v), the Hospitality of Abraham (Genesis 18) is interpreted as a manifestation of the Trinitarian Unity (`Патрїа́рхꙋ А҆враа́мꙋ є҆гда̀ ꙗ҆ви́сѧ воѡ́бразѣ мꙋ́жествѣ , тро́ичнаѧ є҆ди́нице`), affirming the patristic reading of the three angelic visitors as an epiphany of the tri-personal God.
[^83]: In Ode 5 of the Trinity Canon (Fol. 77v–78r), the poet invokes the seraphic contemplation of the divine majesty (`Разꙋмѣва́ше ѡ҆́бѣкры вседѣ́тельнаго бж҃тва̀`), employing the radiant invocation `Свѣ́те трисли́чне` ("O thrice-radiant Light"), beseeching illumination from the uncreated Fountain of light to behold the ineffable beauty of God.
[^84]: Fol. 78v–79r (Leaf p81). Sunday Midnight Office, Canon to the Holy Trinity, Tone 4 (Odes 6–7). The troparion on Fol. 78v invokes Isaiah 6:1–3 ("I saw the Lord sitting upon a throne, high and lifted up... Holy, holy, holy"), interpreting the Seraphic trisagion as a prophetic revelation of the tri-hypostatic monarchy of God. The Sessional Hymn on Fol. 78v follows the model *Вознесы́йсѧ на крⷭ҇тъ* (*Thou Who wast lifted up upon the Cross*), standard for Tone 4 sessional hymns.
[^85]: Fol. 79v–80r (Leaf p82). Sunday Midnight Office, Canon to the Holy Trinity, Tone 4 (Odes 8–9). Fol. 79v line 1 completes the cross-page clause from Fol. 79r (*прича́стїю х҃е твое́го бж҃тва* — "unto communion with Thy Godhead, O Christ"). The heirmos of Ode 8 references Daniel 6:10 / 6:22 (*Рꙋ́цѣ распросте́ръ Данїи́лъ*), and Ode 9 references the Magnificat and Incarnation (*Всѧ́къ земноро́дный*).
[^86]: Fol. 80v–81r (Leaf p83). Transition from the Sunday Midnight Office to Sunday Matins (*На ѻ҆́ꙋтрни, на Бг҃ъ Гдⷭ҇ь*). The canonical Tone 4 Resurrection Troparion (*Свѣ́тлꙋю воскрⷭ҇нїѧ проповѣ́дь* / *Having learned the radiant proclamation of the Resurrection*) is paired with the universal Sunday Tone 4 Theotokion (*Є҆́же ѿвѣ́ка оу҆тае́нное* / *The mystery hidden from the ages*). Fol. 81r records the Sessional Hymns after the First and Second Kathismata (*Воззрѣ́вше на гро́бный вхо́дъ* and *Свое́ю во́лею распѧ́тїе претерпѣ́лъ є҆сѝ*).
[^87]: Fol. 81v–82r (Leaf p84). Fol. 81v concludes the Second Kathisma with its Theotokion (*Оу҆диви́сѧ Ἰ҆ѡ́сифъ* / *Joseph was amazed*), which draws on Aaron's budding rod (Numbers 17:8) and Gideon's dew on the fleece (Judges 6:36–40) as prefigurations of the virginal conception. This is followed by the Tone 4 Hypakoe (*Па́же твое́го пресла́внаго воста́нїѧ*) and the Anabathmoi (Gradual Antiphons 1 and 2), grounded in Psalm 128 [LXX 127] and Psalm 129 [LXX 128].
[^88]: Fol. 82v–83r (Leaf p85). Fol. 82v concludes the Anabathmoi with Antiphon 3 and gives the Sunday Matins Prokeimenon (*Воскрⷭ҇нѝ Гдⷭ҇и помозѝ на́мъ*, Psalm 43:26, 1 [LXX]). The Matins Canons begin with the Resurrection Canon of Tone 4 (*Мо́рѧ чермна́го пꙋчи́нꙋ* / *Traversing the depth of the Red Sea*, referencing Exodus 14–15 and Moses' cross-figured outstretched hands defeating Amalek in Exodus 17:11–12), followed on Fol. 83r by the second Canon of the Cross and Resurrection (*Ѿве́рзꙋ оу҆ста̀ моѧ̑*).
[^89]: Fol. 83v–84r (Leaf p86). Fol. 83v introduces the third canon of Sunday Matins: the Canon to the Most Holy Theotokos in Tone 4 (*Триста́тыщѧ* / *The captains*). Ode 3 opens with the Resurrection heirmos (*Весели́тсѧ ѡ҆тебѣ̀, цр҃кви твоѧ̀ хрⷭ҇те* / 1 Kings [1 Samuel] 2:1–10). Fol. 84r provides Ode 3 of the Cross-Resurrection Canon (*Твоѧ̑ пѣ́вцы*) and the Theotokos Canon, developing the anti-demonic typology of Christ shattering the fangs of the serpent with the nails of the Cross.
[^90]: Fol. 84v–85r (Leaf p87). Fol. 84v gives Ode 3 of the Theotokos Canon (*Свы́шнихъ*), followed by Ode 4 of the Resurrection Canon (*Воздви́женна тѧ̀ ви́дѣвши цр҃кви*, based on Habakkuk 3:11). Fol. 85r contains Ode 4 of the Cross-Resurrection Canon (*Неизслѣ́дному бж҃їю совѣ́тꙋ*), presenting the theological paradox of the Law-giving God nailed to the Cross by those who had received the Law.
[^91]: Fol. 85v–86r (Leaf p88). Fol. 85v provides Ode 4 of the Theotokos Canon, affirming Christ's two distinct natures after the Incarnation (*во двѣ̀ сꙋ́щества̀, познава́етсѧ плотоно́сецъ бг҃ъ, и҆ зе́менъ превы́шше сꙋщества̀* — "known in two natures: God bearing flesh, and an earthly mortal above nature"). Fol. 86r opens Ode 5 of the Resurrection Canon (*Ты̀ ми гдⷭ҇и свѣ́тъ*, referencing Isaiah 26:9).
[^92]: Fol. 86v–87r (Leaf p89). Fol. 86v contains Ode 5 of the Cross-Resurrection Canon (*Оу҆диви́шасѧ всѧ́ческаѧ*) and Ode 5 of the Theotokos Canon (*Нн҃ѣ воста́нꙋ пррⷦ҇чески*). Fol. 87r articulates the Chalcedonian and Dyothelite teaching on Christ's true human soul, reason, and will (*слове́снꙋ и҆ ѻ҆у҆́мнꙋ, ѡ҆дꙋшевленнꙋ . дѣ́йственꙋ, хоти́телнꙋ, самовла́стнꙋ*), followed by Ode 6 of the Resurrection Canon (*Пожрꙋ́ ти со гла́сомъ хвале́нїа*, based on Jonah 2:10 [LXX]).
[^93]: Fol. 87v–88r (Leaf p90). Fol. 87v contains Ode 6 of the Cross-Resurrection Canon (*Прейдо́хомъ вогльбенны морскіѧ*). Fol. 88r completes Ode 6 and opens Ode 7 with the Heirmos *Во пе́щи а҆враа́мстїи сі́и дѣ́ти перси́дстѣй* (Daniel 3 / Song of the Three Holy Children), praising the steadfastness of the three youths in the furnace.
[^94]: Fol. 88v–89r (Leaf p91). Sunday Matins, Canons of Tone 4 (Ode 7 concluded). The Resurrection Canon completes the heirmos *Во пе́щи а҆враа́мстїи* based on Daniel 3 / Song of the Three Holy Children. In the Triadikon of the Theotokos Canon on Fol. 89r, the phrase *во трїе́хъ свѣ́твахъ* represents a rare morphological variant in Ruthenian Cyrillic typography designating the three hypostatic lights or santities of the single divine essence (*Гдⷭ҇ьство є҆ди́но чтꙋ̀*).
[^95]: Fol. 89v–90r (Leaf p92). Sunday Matins, Canons of Tone 4 (Ode 8). The Resurrection Heirmos *Рꙋ́цѣ распросте́ръ Данїи́лъ* draws upon Daniel 6:10–22 (Daniel in the lions' den) and the Song of the Three Holy Children. The troparion on Fol. 89v parallels Daniel's outstretched hands with Christ stretching out His hands upon the Cross to unite earthly and heavenly beings into one Church. Fol. 90r presents the second canon's heirmos *Ѻ҆тро́ки бл҃гочести́выѧ вопе́щи* and the imagery of the cosmic darkening at the Crucifixion (Matthew 27:45).
[^96]: Fol. 90v–91r (Leaf p93). Sunday Matins, Canons of Tone 4 (Ode 8 concluded, Ode 9 begun). Fol. 90v features the Triadikon professing the Father as the unoriginate Source (*Ѻ҆у҆ма̀ пе́рваго и҆ вино́внаго всѣ̑мъ*) and Begetter of the Word and Projector of the Spirit (*преложи́телѧ дх҃ꙋ*). Ode 9 opens on Fol. 91r with the Heirmos *Ка́мень нерꙋкосѣ́чныѧ горы̀*, drawing upon Daniel 2:34–35, 45 (the unhewn stone cut from the mountain) as the classical Marian type of the virginal conception of Christ without human seed. Fol. 91r line 16 explicitly witnesses the liturgical realia loanword *плащени́ца* (*бг҃оносныѧ плащени́ца непра́жнены*), referring to the empty grave-linens/burial shroud of the risen Lord.
[^97]: Fol. 91v–92r (Leaf p94). Sunday Matins, Canons of Tone 4 (Ode 9 concluded). The troparion on Fol. 91v interprets Golgotha typologically (*накра́нїевѣ же на тве́рдѣмъ ка́мени* — "upon the hard rock of the Place of the Skull"), contrasting the serpent's deceitful seduction of Adam in Eden with Christ dashing the ancient enemy against the rock of the Cross. Fol. 92r contains supplications for the cross-bearing ruler (*крⷭ҇тоно́снаго цр҃ѧ*) against blasphemous enemies, reflecting the standard Byzantine-Slavic imperial and regal petitions preserved in the 1629 Lavra imprint.
[^98]: Fol. 92v–93r (Leaf p95). Transition to the Praises (Lauds / *На хвали́техъ*) of Sunday Matins, Tone 4. Fol. 92v records the first four Resurrection Stichera (*Ст҃ры воскр҃ . гла́ д҃*), beginning with *И҆́же распѧ́тїе претерпѣ́въ* and celebrating Christ's liberation of human nature from the demonic tyrant and the ancient curse. Fol. 93r introduces the Anatolian Stichera (*И҆́ны Стїхи̑ры, Восто́чны*), developing the Dyophysite paradox of Christ suffering and dying as man while rising and shattering Hades as God.
[^99]: Fol. 93v–94r (Leaf p96). Conclusion of Matins and opening of the Sunday Divine Liturgy Beatitudes (*В недѣ́лю на бл҃же́нна, Гла́съ д҃*). Fol. 93v contains the dramatic apologetic apostrophe against the unbelieving guards and authorities (*Гдѣ̀ є҆́стъ і҆́съ є҆го́же вомнѣ́нїсте стрещѝ; рцѣ́те і҆ꙋде́е*), asserting that even if human witnesses kept silence, the rolled-away stone of the sepulchre would cry aloud. Fol. 94r opens the Beatitude troparia with the classic liturgical antithesis between the tree of the Fall and the tree of the Cross (*Дре́ва ра́ди А҆да́мъ ра́ѧ бы́сть и҆зселе́нъ, дре́ва ра́ди крⷭ҇тнаго разбо́йникъ вра́й всели́сѧ*).
[^100]: Fol. 94v–95r (Leaf p97). Sunday Beatitudes of Tone 4 concluded. Fol. 94v continues the troparia commemorating the holy Myrrh-bearing women arriving at the tomb with tears and departing with joy (*ра́дꙋйтесѧ*). Fol. 95r commemorates the post-Resurrection appearance of Christ unto the Apostles and the tactile verification of the wounds by the Apostle Thomas (*невѣ́рꙋющаго ѳѡмꙋ̀, ꙗ҆́звъ показа́нїемъ оу҆вѣ́рѧѧ*). The Beatitudes conclude with a Trinitarian doxology and Marian theotokion.
[^101]: Fol. 95v–96r (Leaf p98). Transition to Sunday Vespers (Sunday Evening / *В недѣ́лю ве́черъ*). Fol. 95v concludes the Sunday Beatitude Theotokion and introduces the Penitential Stichera at "Lord, I have cried" (*на Гдⷭ҇и возва́хъ, Стїхи̑ры покаѧ́нны*) in Tone 4, following the prosomoion model *Да́лъ є҆сѝ зна́менїе*. The penitent invokes biblical archetypes of repentance: the thief, the prodigal son, King Manasseh, the sinful woman/harlot, the persecutor Saul/Paul, the publican, and the blasphemer/apostate (*ѡ҆ме́тникъ*). Fol. 96r introduces the Stichera to the Bodiless Powers (*И҆́ны стїхи̑ры Безпло́тнымъ а҆́ггеломъ*), modeled on *Ꙗ҆́кѡ до́блѧ*.
[^102]: Fol. 96v–97r (Leaf p99). Sunday Evening, Stichera to the Bodiless Powers and Penitential Aposticha in Tone 4. Fol. 96v outlines the Pseudo-Dionysian celestial hierarchy: the Seraphim, Cherubim, and Thrones partaking directly of the divine radiance, followed by Dominions, Principalities, Powers, Archangels, and Angels. Fol. 97r opens the Aposticha (*На стихо́внѣ, Стїхи̑ры покаѧ́нны*) with the poignant penitential prayer *Хотѣ́хъ слеза́ми мои́ми ѡ҆мы́ти мои́хъ прегрѣше́нїй гдⷭ҇и рꙋкописа́нїе*, invoking Christ as the Harbor for the storm-tossed and the Physician of souls.
[^103]: Fol. 97v–98r (Leaf p100). Conclusion of Tone 4 and formal opening of Tone 5 (*Гла́съ Пѧ́тый*). Fol. 97v concludes the Sunday Evening office with a Martyrikon and an extensive Marian Akathist-style salutation hymn (*Ра́дꙋйсѧ свѣ́та ѡ҆́блаче...*). Fol. 98r marks a major structural division of the codex with a monumental ornamental woodcut headpiece, the vyaz title banner *ВЪ КНИГИ ВОСКРⷭ҇НЫ [ОКТѠИХЪ]*, the heading *Гла́съ Пѧ́тый*, a decorated woodcut initial *Ч*, and the opening Lord, I have cried Resurrection Stichera of Tone 5 (*Че́стнымъ твои́мъ крⷭ҇то́мъ х҃е дїа́вола посрами́лъ є҆сѝ*).
