# PREFACE: TO THE READER

Be it known unto every pious reader,[^1] that each one of us is a temple of the living God, wherein necessity showeth at every hour that we should offer up unto Him godly chants. Procure therefore for thyself this little book, that in thine own church during visitation,[^2] by celebrating the canons therefrom, thou mayest praise the living God: so that, having instructed thyself therein from such a beginning of virtues as the fear of God, thou mayest advance into love, the perfect mother of virtues, which is God Himself. Unto Whom be glory, honor, and worship, unto ages of ages: Amen.

# TONE 1

*[In the Book of the Resurrectional Octoechos]*

*Tone 1.*

### On Saturday Evening at Great Vespers

*At "Lord, I have cried," the Resurrectional Stichera:*

Receive our evening prayers, O holy Lord, and grant us remission of sins, for Thou alone art He Who hath revealed the Resurrection unto the world.

=== LEAF p4 ===

**[Left Page — Fol. 1v]**

Encompass Zion, O ye people, and surround her, and give glory therein unto Him Who is risen from the dead; for He is our God, Who hath delivered us from our iniquities.

Come, O ye people, let us chant and bow down before Christ, glorifying His Resurrection from the dead; for He is our God, Who hath delivered the world from the deception of the enemy.

Be glad, O ye heavens; sound the trumpet, ye foundations of the earth; cry aloud with rejoicing, O ye mountains; for behold, Emmanuel hath nailed our sins unto the Cross, and hath granted us life, having slain death, and hath resurrected Adam with all his race, for He is the Lover of mankind.

Him Who was crucified in the flesh willingly for our sake, Who suffered, and was buried, and rose from the dead, let us hymn, saying: Confirm Thy Church in Orthodoxy, O Christ, and make peaceful our life, as Lover of mankind.

**[Right Page — Fol. 2r]**

Standing before Thy tomb which received not corruption, we, the unworthy, offer up doxology unto Thine ineffable compassion, O Christ our God; for Thou, the Sinless One, didst accept crucifixion and death, that Thou mightest grant resurrection unto the world, as Lover of mankind.

The Word, Who is co-unoriginate and co-eternal with the Father, Who came forth ineffably from the virginal womb, and willingly accepted crucifixion and death for our sake, and rose in glory: let us hymn, saying: O Giver of life, Lord, glory to Thee, O Savior of our souls.

*Glory, and now. Theotokion (Dogmatikon), Tone 1:*[^4]

Let us hymn the Virgin Mary, the glory of the whole world, who sprang forth from mankind and gave birth to the Master, the portal of heaven, the song of the bodiless hosts and the adornment of the faithful; for she hath been revealed as heaven and the temple of the Godhead: she, having broken down the barrier of enmity, hath brought in peace, and opened the kingdom.

**[Left Page — Fol. 2v]**

Having her therefore as a steadfast confirmation of our faith, we have as our defender the Lord Who was born of her. Be bold therefore, be bold, O people of God; for He shall conquer the enemies, for He is the Lover of mankind.

### At the Aposticha

*Resurrectional Stichera. Tone 1:*

By Thy Passion, O Christ, we have been set free from passions, and by Thy Resurrection we have been delivered out of corruption,[^5] O Lord, glory to Thee.

*Other Stichera:*

Let creation rejoice, let the heavens be glad, let the nations clap their hands with gladness; for Christ our God hath nailed our sins unto the Cross, having put death to death, and hath granted us life, and hath raised fallen Adam with all his race, as Lover of mankind.

**[Right Page — Fol. 3r]**

Being King of heaven and earth, O Incomprehensible One, Thou wast crucified willingly in Thy love for mankind; whom Hades, meeting below, was embittered, while the souls of the righteous, receiving Thee, rejoiced; and Adam, beholding Thee, the Creator, in the nethermost regions, arose. O marvel: how hath He Who is the Life of all tasted death? Yet as He willed to illumine the world, we cry aloud and say: O Thou Who art risen from the dead, Lord, glory to Thee.

The Myrrh-bearing women, bearing sweet spices with haste and weeping, reached Thy tomb, and not finding Thine immaculate Body, yet learning from the Angel of the new and most glorious miracle, said unto the Apostles: The Lord is risen, granting great mercy unto the world.

*Glory, and now. Theotokion:*

Behold, the prophecy of Isaiah is fulfilled, for thou, being a Virgin, hast given birth, and after giving birth thou didst remain even as thou wast before childbirth.

**[Left Page — Fol. 3v]**

For God was He Who was born, and therefore nature also was renewed. But, O Mother of God, despise not the supplications of thy servants offered unto thee in thy church. But as thou didst bear the Compassionate One in thine arms, have mercy on thy servants, and pray that our souls may be saved.

*Resurrectional Troparion, Tone 1:*

When the stone was sealed by the Jews, and the soldiers were guarding Thine immaculate Body, Thou didst rise on the third day, O Savior, granting life unto the world. *Theotokion:*

**[Right Page — Fol. 4r]**

Thou hast been shown forth more spacious than the heavens, having borne thy Creator. Glory to Him Who dwelt within thee; glory to Him Who came forth from thee; glory to Him Who hath set us free through thy childbearing.

*Ode 1. Tone 1. Heirmos: Thy victorious right hand.*

*Refrain: Most Holy Trinity, our God, glory to Thee.*

The one Tri-hypostatic Origin, unoriginate, co-eternal, Creator of all, incomprehensible, the Seraphim glorify unceasingly, and every tongue faithfully laudeth in hymns.

That Thou mightest reveal unto mankind Thy singular, thrice-shining Godhead: having fashioned man, Thou didst form him according to Thine image, giving him intellect, word, and spirit, as Lover of mankind.

From on high having revealed the singular dominion in the three divine hypostases, O Father:

*Ode 3. Heirmos: Who alone knowest.*

Thou of old didst manifestly appear unto Abraham as three hypostases, yet singular in the essence of the Godhead, figuratively showing forth the truest theology. And faithfully we hymn Thee, the one-originating and thrice-radiant God.

The Trinity, a singular Monad transcending nature, ineffably beyond understanding, is glorified by the spiritual intelligences, who unceasingly cry aloud praise with thrice-holy voices. In harmony with whom, the Tri-hypostatic Lord is chanted also by us.

*Theotokion:*

From thee, in time and without seed, came forth He Who is beyond time, the Invisible One, having made Himself like unto us. And He hath taught us the single nature and lordship of the Father, the Son, and the Spirit, O Theotokos; therefore we glorify thee.

## At Sunday Matins[^12]

*At "God is the Lord," Troparion, Tone 1:* **"The stone being sealed,"** *twice; and its Theotokion, written at Saturday Vespers. Thereafter, the Sessional Hymns (Kathismata).*

*Resurrection Sessional Hymn. At the First Stichology (Kathisma), Tone 1:*

While the soldiers were guarding Thy tomb, O Savior, they became as dead from the lightning brilliance of the appearing angel, who proclaimed the Resurrection unto the women. Thee we glorify, the Destroyer of corruption; before Thee we fall down, Who didst rise from the tomb, our only God.

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*

We all know thee to be the Mother of God, who truly appeared as a virgin even after childbirth. Fleeing with love unto thy goodness, we sinners have thee as an intercessor; thee have we acquired as salvation in tribulations, the only all-blameless one.

**[Right Page — Fol. 10r]**

*At the Second Stichology (Kathisma). Sessional Hymns, Tone 1:*

The women came early in the morning unto Thy tomb, and seeing the angelic vision they trembled; the tomb flashed forth life, and the miracle astonished them. Therefore going their way unto the disciples, they proclaimed the Resurrection: Christ has overthrown Hades, as the only strong and mighty One, and He has raised up all the dead together with Himself, destroying the fear of condemnation by the power of the Cross.

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*

Having conceived the divine Fire unburnt, and having given birth without seed unto the Lord, the Fountain of life, O full of grace, Theotokos, save those who magnify thee.

## Cohort 4 Footnotes

[^34]: Typology of the Exodus in the Dogmatikon of Tone 2 (*Preide sen' zakonnaya*): The hymn explicitly contrasts the shadow of the Mosaic Law with the grace of the Gospel, drawing upon the pillar of fire (Exodus 13:21–22) that led the Israelites through the wilderness, which is fulfilled and superseded by Christ, the spiritual Sun of righteousness (Malachi 4:2 [LXX 3:20]). In place of Moses the lawgiver stands Christ the Savior, effecting the redemption of mankind.
[^35]: The Aposticha Stichera of the Resurrection, Tone 2 (*Voskreseniye Tvoye, Khriste Spase*): These four hymns, traditionally attributed to St. John of Damascus, celebrate the cosmic victory of the Cross over the tree of the Garden of Eden (Genesis 3:1–6), the harrowing of Hades, the piercing of the Lord's side by the soldier's spear (John 19:34), and the empty tomb sealed by the Judean authorities (Matthew 27:62–66).
[^36]: Theotokion of the Aposticha (*O chudese novago vsekho drevnikho chudes* / "O wonder new beyond all wonders of old!"): Composed by St. John of Damascus, this venerable hymn ponders the mystery of the Virgin Mother holding in her maternal embrace the uncircumscribed God Who sustains all creation in the palm of His hand. It affirms the orthodox dogma of the Theotokos as defined at the Council of Ephesus (431 AD) and the Council of Chalcedon (451 AD).
[^37]: Sunday Midnight Office (*Mesonyktikon*), Canon to the Most Holy and Undivided Trinity (*Kanon Troichen*), Tone 2: Composed by Metrophanes, Metropolitan of Smyrna (c. 820–880; author of the Triadikos Canons of the Octoechos). The canon provides an exhaustive patristic and scholastic synthesis of Trinitarian theology, systematically articulating the consubstantiality (*homoousion*), co-eternity, and unconfused hypostatic properties of the Father (the unbegotten Source and Cause), the Son (begotten before all ages), and the Holy Spirit (eternally proceeding from the Father).
[^38]: Sessional Hymn to the Holy Trinity, Tone 2, Ode 3 (*Svyataya Troitse, pomilui ikhzhe sozda*): Chanted according to the melodic model (*prosomoion*) *Vyshnikh ishcha* ("Seeking the things on high"), entreating the consubstantial Trinity to deliver creation from fire and condemnation and grant forgiveness of sins.
[^39]: Canon to the Holy Trinity, Ode 5, Troparion 2: Moses and the vision in the Burning Bush (Exodus 3:2–14). The hymn incorporates the traditional patristic christology (patristic consensus of St. Justin Martyr, St. Irenaeus of Lyons, and St. Athanasius the Great) identifying the "Angel of the Lord" who spoke from the flame as the pre-incarnate Logos, the "Angel of Great Counsel" (LXX Isaiah 9:6), revealing the tri-hypostatic nature of the one divine Principle.
[^40]: Sunday Matins Sessional Hymns of the Resurrection, Tone 2, after the First Kathisma: *Blagoobraznyi Iosif* ("The noble Joseph..."), the classic troparion of Great and Holy Saturday (commemorating Joseph of Arimathea taking down Christ's Body, wrapping it in a clean winding sheet, and placing it in a new rock-hewn sepulcher; Matthew 27:57–60, Mark 15:43–46), used here in the Sunday Resurrection cycle to proclaim the Lord's third-day rising.
[^41]: Hypakoe of Tone 2 (*Po strasti shedshe na grob* / "After the Passion, the women having gone unto the tomb"): The Hypakoe (from the Greek *hypakoe*, "obedience" or "listening attentively") recounts the holy Myrrh-bearing women who went at early dawn to anoint the Lord's sacred Body with aromatic spices, beholding the angelic messengers who declared that Christ is not among the dead (Mark 16:1–8, Luke 24:1–10).
[^42]: The Gradual Hymns (*Anavathmoi*) of Tone 2: Structured into three antiphons inspired by the Old Testament Psalms of Ascents (Psalms 119–133 [LXX 118–132]) sung by Hebrew pilgrims journeying up to Jerusalem. Each antiphon concludes with a pneumatological troparion (*Svyatomu Dukhu...*) extolling the Holy Spirit as the sovereign Principle of life, wisdom, and sanctification alongside the Father and the Son.
[^43]: Canons of Sunday Matins, Tone 2: The classic Byzantine-Ruthenian tri-canon structure prescribed for the Resurrection Matins in the Octoechos: (1) The Canon of the Resurrection by St. John of Damascus (*Vo glubine potopi drevle* / "Of old, the all-armed power drowned..."); (2) The Canon of the Cross (*Netrennuyu neobychuyu* / "Traversing an untrodden, unaccustomed path..."); and (3) The Canon of the Most Holy Theotokos to the same heirmos model.
