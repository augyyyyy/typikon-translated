# TONE 7: RESURRECTIONAL AND WEEKDAY HYMNOGRAPHY

## TONE 7

*In the Book of the Resurrection: Tone 7.*

Come, let us rejoice in the Lord, Who shattered the dominion of death and enlightened the human race, crying with the bodiless hosts: O our Creator and Savior, glory to Thee!

Thou didst endure the Cross and burial for our sake, O Savior; and as God, by death Thou didst put death to death.

**[Left Page — Fol. 148v]**

...didst put to death: therefore we worship Thy third-day Resurrection, O Lord, glory to Thee!

The Apostles, seeing the Resurrection of the Creator, marveled, singing the angelic praise: This is the glory of the Church, this is the wealth of the Kingdom! O Thou Who didst suffer for our sake, O Lord, glory to Thee!

*Other Stichera, Anatolian:*

Though Christ was seized by lawless men, yet Thou art my God, and I am not ashamed; He was smitten upon the back, and I deny Thee not; He was nailed unto the Cross, and I conceal it not; in Thy Resurrection do I boast, for Thy death is my life: O Almighty One, Lover of mankind, O Lord, glory to Thee! [^154]

Fulfilling the prophecy of David, Christ revealed...

**[Right Page — Fol. 149r]**

...revealed His majesties in Zion unto His disciples, showing Himself praised and ever glorified with the Father and the Holy Spirit: first indeed bodiless as the Word, and afterwards for our sake incarnate and put to death as man, and risen with power as the Lover of mankind.

Thou didst descend into Hades, O Christ, as Thou didst will; Thou didst overthrow death as God and Master, and didst rise on the third day, raising Adam with Thyself from the bonds of Hades and from corruption, as he cried out and said: Glory to Thy Resurrection, O only Lover of mankind!

The Lord was laid in the tomb as one asleep, and He arose on the third day as One mighty in strength, raising Adam with Himself from the corruption of death, as Almighty God.

*Glory, and now. Theotokion:*

Thou wast known as Mother above nature, O Theotokos, yet remainedst...

**[Left Page — Fol. 149v]**

...remainedst a virgin above speech and understanding, and no tongue can declare the wonders of thy childbearing; for glorious being thy conception, O pure one, incomprehensible is the manner of thy giving birth: for where God so wills, the order of nature is overcome. Therefore, knowing thee as the Mother of God, we earnestly pray thee: Intercede that our souls may be saved! [^155]

*At the Aposticha, Resurrection Stichera, Tone 7:*

Risen from the tomb, O Savior of the world, Thou didst raise man with Thyself in Thy flesh: O Lord, glory to Thee!

*Other Stichera:*

Come, let us worship Him Who rose from the dead and enlightened all things; for He has freed us from the tyranny of Hades by His third-day Resurrection, granting us life and great mercy.

**[Right Page — Fol. 150r]**

Having descended into Hades, O Christ, Thou didst take death captive, and having risen on the third day, Thou didst raise us with Thyself as we cry: Glory to Thy Resurrection, O only Lover of mankind!

Thou didst appear fearful, O Lord, even as Thou layest in the tomb as one asleep; but having risen on the third day as One mighty, Thou didst raise Adam with Thyself, who glorifies Thy almighty uprising, O Lord, Lover of mankind.

*Glory, and now. Theotokion:*

Beneath thy shelter, O Sovereign Lady, all we who are born of earth flee, crying unto thee: O Theotokos, our hope, deliver us from our countless transgressions, and save our souls!

*On Sunday at the Midnight Office, Canon to the Holy and Life-Originating Trinity, Tone 7. Ode 1. Heirmos: By Thy beck:*

Open Thou the spiritual lips of my heart and these material lips to Thy praise, O Thrice-Radiant One, unique Godhead of all, that I may sing unto Thee...

**[Left Page — Fol. 150v]**

...a light-bestowing song of thanksgiving. [^156]

That Thou mightest show forth the co-eternal power of Thy goodness, O unfathomable Trinity, Thou didst fashion man, an earthen image of Thy primordial and sovereign lordship, O Creator.

O unoriginate Mind, Who didst beget the co-eternal Word, and didst cause the co-unoriginate Spirit to shine forth: make us worthy to worship the one God, connatural in essence and tri-hypostatic!

*Theotokion:*

Thou didst appear unto Moses at the bush, O Word of God, as a consuming fire, yet leaving it unconsumed, prefiguring Thine incarnation from the Virgin, whereby Thou didst refashion mankind.

*Ode 3. Heirmos: In the beginning, the heavens:*

Hymning Thee as the single, thrice-radiant, and all-creative Master, we ask deliverance from sins and...

**[Right Page — Fol. 151r]**

...and calamities: O Almighty God, despise not those who in faith glorify Thy goodness!

A shoot sprouted from the Father as from an unoriginate root, God the Word, equal in power with the connatural Divine Spirit; therefore, O faithful, in Trinity of persons we glorify the single Lordship.

Equal in glory and connatural is the tri-hypostatic Godhead, indivisible and inseparable; and as the monarchical Trinity, all we the faithful doxologize Thee, and bowing down, we ask forgiveness of transgressions.

*Theotokion:*

Unchangeably likened in all things unto men, O Word of God, Thou didst come forth visibly from the pure Virgin, and didst reveal unto all the thrice-radiant Godhead and single essence of the unchangeable...

**[Left Page — Fol. 151v]**

...hypostases. [^157]

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 7.*

*Podoben: "He Who for my sake" (И҆́же менѐ ра́ди):*

Have mercy, O Holy Trinity, upon Thy servants who have sinned; and accept those who repent unto Thee, O Compassionate One, and vouchsafe them forgiveness!

*Glory, and now. Theotokion:*

Comfort our souls afflicted by sins, O all-pure Theotokos, and deliver from transgressions those who hymn thee, O Bride of God!

*Ode 4. Heirmos: Leaving not the bosom of the Father:*

All-sustaining, thrice-radiant Unity, divine Principle and Savior of all: preserve and save Thy cantors now from sorrow, passions, and every constraint!

Through symbolic utterances knowing of Thy incomprehensible, thrice-radiant Godhead, we sing unto Thee, O Lord and Lover of mankind, and we glorify Thy power.

*Glory:*

**[Right Page — Fol. 152r]**

On earth as in heaven, together with the bodiless choirs, indivisibly we distinguish Thee, and in love we glorify Thee: O Unity Trinity, as reigning piously over all that exists!

*And now. Theotokion:*

Not departing from the Father's glory, the super-essential One willingly came down into our lowliness, became incarnate, and raised us unto divine glory, as He is merciful.

*Ode 5. Heirmos: Night is without light unto the unbelieving, O...*

O primordial Nature of Light, Trinity in images, Unity in lights, glory, and power: establish us in Thy love! *(Twice)*

*Glory:*

Mind, Word, and Spirit, glorifying the one divine Principle and tri-personal Nature, we pray to be delivered from temptations and all affliction.

*And now. Theotokion:*

Taking shape in human nature from the holy Virgin, O Word of God, Thou didst teach us to sing...

**[Left Page — Fol. 152v]**

...unto the Trinity in unity, connatural and sharing one throne. [^158]

*Ode 6. Heirmos: Sailing in the surge of the billows of...*

The dominion of equal glory, the monarchical divine Principle, being tri-hypostatic and immutable, one with another we glorify, having distinction only by the existential properties belonging to each. *(Twice)*

*Glory:*

The noetic ranks of angelic splendors praise the tri-personal divine Principle; and with them we too, with earthen lips, sing unto Thee, the Creator of all, as one, and faithfully glorify Thee.

*Theotokion:*

Having by an ineffable word begotten the Word from the Sun, the Father: another eternal Sun has shone forth from the Virgin in the last times, proclaiming the one tri-personal, unfathomable God.

*Then the Sessional Hymn, Tone 7.*

*Podoben: "Brighter than fire" (О҆гня̀ свѣтлѣ́е):*

O consubstantial Trinity, tri-hypostatic Unity...

**[Right Page — Fol. 153r]**

...have mercy on those whom Thou hast created, O Intangible One, burning away the wickedness of our souls and enlightening the hearts of those who hymn Thy compassion: O God, glory to Thee!

*Glory, and now. Theotokion:*

Brighter than fire and more active than flame, O Mother of goodness, our Sovereign Lady: burning away the sins of men and enlightening the hearts of those who hymn thy great deeds, O woman full of divine grace!

*Ode 7. Heirmos: The Children of old showed the fiery furnace...*

Ever shining with beam-radiant splendors, O Thrice-Radiant God, inaccessible and super-essential Unity: save those who piously believe in Thee, O Master, and who worship Thee!

Obedient to the voice of the divine prophets, Thee alone, the God of all, we glorify in three Persons, thus crying: Blessed...

**[Left Page — Fol. 153v]**

...art Thou, O Lord God of our fathers! [^159]

*Glory:*

With earthen lips, using the songs of the immaterial ranks, we sing unto Thee, O Holy Trinity, crying in unity of essence: Blessed art Thou, O Lord God of our fathers!

*Theotokion:*

That He Who fashioned man might renew him again, O all-pure one, having become visibly incarnate from thee, He deified mankind who thus cry out: Blessed art Thou, O Lord God of our fathers!

*Ode 8. Heirmos: Unconsumed by fire:*

Vouchsafe the hearts of Thy cantors to be illumined with light-bestowing rays of the thrice-radiant Sun, and grant now unto all, as far as it is possible, ever to behold Thy beauty, O Unity Trinity, who with fitting faith warmly and gloriously hymn Thy majesty.

Thou upholdest all things, O Triune and Single...

**[Right Page — Fol. 154r]**

...Lordship without beginning, and rulest over heaven and earth; therefore, drawn by Thy love, ever preserve me who sing: Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord, and exalt Him above all unto ages of ages!

Make me a temple of Thy thrice-radiant ray, O Benefactor, Lover of mankind; and by communion and Thy overshadowing, show me inaccessible to invisible foes and bodily passions, O Sovereign Master, my monarchical God, O Lord of glory, that I may warmly sing unto ages of ages!

*Theotokion:*

The primordial Light, having shone forth from thy womb, O all-pure Mother of God, illumined the whole world with tri-radiant light, and revealed the earth as another heaven, singing: Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord, and exalt Him above all unto ages of ages!

*Ode 9. Heirmos: Mother of God and Virgin:*

To exalt and hymn Thee worthily, Whom...

**[Left Page — Fol. 154v]**

...the Seraphim unceasingly hymn in the highest, we earthen ones are unable to do; nevertheless, taking courage, we sing unto Thee as the Master of all, and we magnify the God Who loves mankind. [^160]

Deliver Thy faithful cantors from bodily ailments and spiritual passions, O unified, indivisible Trinity, and vouchsafe that we be preserved unharmed from every worldly assault!

O co-equal, primordial, thrice-radiant, almighty Dominion, immutable Beauty, essential Goodness: grant remission of transgressions unto Thy servants, and deliverance from afflictions and passions.

*Theotokion:*

Taking a mind and a soul and the commingling of flesh from thy all-pure womb, O Mother of God, God the Word truly appeared as man, and...

**[Right Page — Fol. 155r]**

...clearly revealed man as a partaker of the divine nature.

*At "God is the Lord", Resurrection Troparion, Tone 7:*

Thou didst destroy death by Thy Cross, and didst open Paradise unto the thief; Thou didst transform the weeping of the myrrhbearers, and didst command the apostles to proclaim that Christ God is risen, granting great mercy unto the world.

*Glory, and now. Theotokion:*

As the treasure of our Resurrection, O all-hymned one, lead up from the pit and depth of transgressions those who place their hope in thee; for thou hast saved those guilty of sin, having given birth unto our Salvation: who wast a Virgin before childbearing, and in childbearing a Virgin, and after childbearing still a Virgin.

*After the 1st Kathisma, Sunday Sessional Hymn, Tone 7:*

While the tomb was sealed, O Life, Thou didst shine forth from the tomb, O Christ God; and while the doors were shut...

**[Left Page — Fol. 155v]**

...unto the disciples Thou didst appear, O Resurrection of all, renewing an upright spirit in them according to Thy great mercy. [^161]

*Glory, and now. Theotokion:*

Since thou possessest compassion for our weakness, and seest those on earth: have mercy upon the suffering people, O blessed Theotokos! Ever continue praying that we perish not grievously; pray to God as the Compassionate One that our souls may be saved, O all-pure Virgin!

*After the 2nd Kathisma, Sunday Sessional Hymn, Tone 7:*

Life lay in the tomb, and a seal lay upon the stone; soldiers guarded the King as one asleep, and angels glorified Him as immortal God; and the women cried: The Lord is risen, granting great mercy unto the world!

*Glory, and now. Theotokion:*

Him Who was crucified for our sake, Christ Who cast down the power of death, unceasingly entreat...

**[Right Page — Fol. 156r]**

...entreat, O Theotokos Virgin, that He may save our souls!

*Hypakoe:*

O Thou Who didst assume our likeness and endure crucifixion in the flesh: save us by Thy Resurrection, O Christ God, as the Lover of mankind!

*The Songs of Ascents, Tone 7. Antiphon 1:*

Turn back the captivity of Zion from delusion, and give me life, O Savior, lifting me up from slavish passions.

They that sow in sorrows in the south sing with tears: so shall they reap joyous sheaves of ever-living sustenance.

In the Holy Spirit is the fountain of divine treasures; from Him come wisdom, understanding, and fear: unto Him be praise and glory, honor and dominion!

*Antiphon 2:*

Unless the Lord build the spiritual house, in vain do we labor; for without Him neither deed nor word is accomplished.

**[Left Page — Fol. 156v]**

Moved by the Spirit, Thy saints sprout forth as fruit of the womb, fulfilling the Father's commandments of adoption. [^162]

In the Holy Spirit all things have existence: for He is God before all, Lordship over all, unapproachable Light, and the Life of all.

*Antiphon 3:*

They that fear the Lord have found the ways of life: now and ever are they beatified with incorruptible glory.

Seeing thy offspring around thy table like young shoots, rejoice and be glad, bringing them unto Christ, O Chief of Shepherds!

In the Holy Spirit is the depth of gifts, the riches of glory, and the great depth of judgments: equal in glory with the Father and the Son, and worshipped together as God.

*Then the Prokeimenon, Tone 7: Arise, O Lord my God! Verse: I will praise Thee, O Lord, with my whole heart. "Let every breath praise the Lord." Verse: Praise God in His saints. The Gospel.*

*Sunday Resurrection Canon, Tone 7. Ode 1. Heirmos:*

**[Right Page — Fol. 157r]**

By Thy beck, the watery element previously pouring forth was transformed into dry land, O Lord; therefore Israel, walking dryshod, sings unto Thee a song of victory.

The tyranny of death was condemned by the Tree, through the unrighteous death adjudged unto Thee, O Lord; therefore the prince of darkness, having not prevailed against Thee, was justly cast out.

Hades drew near unto Thee, but unable to crush Thy body with its teeth, its jaws were shattered; and Thou, O Savior, having destroyed the pangs of death, didst rise on the third day.

*Theotokion:*

The pangs of foremother Eve were loosed: escaping childbearing pangs, thou didst give birth without wedlock; therefore, knowing thee clearly as the all-pure Theotokos, all we glorify thee.

*Second Canon, Cross-Resurrection. Ode 1. Heirmos: Like the former:*

**[Left Page — Fol. 157v]**

Christ poured forth two life-bearing streams for me upon the Cross from His pierced side: let us sing unto Him, for He is glorified! [^163]

Dwelling in the tomb and rising on the third day, Christ granted incorruption unto the dead who awaited Him: let us sing unto Him, for He is glorified!

*Theotokion:*

Thou wast revealed as alone a virgin even after childbearing; for thou didst give birth to the Creator of the world: let us sing unto Him, for He is glorified!

*Another Canon, to the Most Holy Theotokos, Tone 7. Ode 1. Heirmos: By Thy beck, into an earthen:*

Having given birth to the abyss of compassion, O Virgin, illumine my soul with thy light-bearing radiance, that I may worthily hymn the abyss of thy wonders!

Seeing the sinful arrows wounding us, O Word, as the Benefactor Thou didst have mercy; therefore, ineffably united...

**[Right Page — Fol. 158r]**

...to flesh from thee, O all-pure one, is the super-divine One.

Corruptible and frail human nature was held fast by death; but thou, O Sovereign Lady, having conceived the Life, didst lead it up from incorruption unto life.

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*

In the beginning, O Lord and Savior, by Thine almighty Word Thou didst establish the heavens, and all their host by the all-creating divine Spirit: establish me upon the immovable rock of Thy confession!

Having mounted upon the Tree of Thine own will, O merciful Savior, Thou didst suffer for our sake, and didst endure the wound that mediated humility and salvation unto the faithful, through which we were reconciled unto Thy merciful Father.

Thou cleansest me from the wound, O Christ, who was wounded in soul by the serpent's bite, and Thou hast shown forth light unto those who of old...

**[Left Page — Fol. 158v]**

when I lay in corruption of old: for having descended into Hades, by the Cross Thou didst raise me up together with Thyself. *Theotokion:*

By the supplications of Thy Mother who knew not man, grant peace to the world, O Savior, and grant victory to the king against adversary enemies, and make worthy of Thine ineffable glory those who glorify Thee.

*Another Heirmos: The power from on high.*

Thou didst quench the flaming sword, O Lord, when the lawless people nailed Thee to the Cross; and to the thief Thou didst open the gates of Paradise. Thou hast saved, O Lover of mankind, those who unceasingly glorify Thee in faith.

Having risen from Thy tomb, O Christ, Thou didst first manifest Thyself to the women, and Thou didst dispel the grief of Eve, and didst raise up together with Thyself Adam who had fallen into corruption, as the compassionate God; therefore we all sing of Thy rising. *Theotokion:*

**[Right Page — Fol. 159r]**

Having received the Lord into thy virginal womb, O all-pure one, thou didst give birth to Him incarnate. Grant Him to be reconciled to us, His servants, through thy maternal supplications, as a haven wherein we are saved.[^164]

*Another Heirmos: In the beginning, the heavens with Thine all-powerful Word.*

The crawling serpent, having crept out from Eden and deceived me with the craving for divinity, cast me down to the earth; but as the merciful One, by nature compassionate, showing bountiful mercy, He deifies me, having made His abode in thy womb and become like unto me, O Mother and Virgin.

Blessed is the fruit of thy womb, O Virgin Theotokos, the Joy of all; for thou didst bear joy unto all the world, and true gladness, destroying sinful sorrow, O Bride of God.

Eternal life, and light, and peace, O Virgin Birth-giver of God, hast thou brought forth unto us, quieting mankind's ancient enmity toward the Father and God,

**[Left Page — Fol. 159v]**

who pacified it through faith and graceful confession.

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*

Without leaving the Father's bosom, having descended to earth, O Christ God, I heard the mystery of Thine economy, and I glorified Thee, O Lover of mankind.

Having given His shoulders over to scourging, He Who was incarnate of the Virgin; when the servant had sinned, the innocent Master is beaten, absolving my transgressions.

Standing before the judge, the Judge of judges receives judgment like a condemned man from lawless judges, and is buffeted by an earthen hand: He Who created man as God, and Who judges the earth righteously. *Theotokion:*

As the true Mother of God, pray to thy Creator and Son to guide me unto the saving haven of thy glorious will, O all-immaculate one.

**[Right Page — Fol. 160r]**

*Another Heirmos: Foreseeing.*

Knowing no sin, yet made sin for its sake, O Lord, that He might know it, accepting to be formed in what was foreign, that Thou mightest make manifest their madness; and Thou didst slay the tyrant of deceit.[^165]

Having risen from the dead and raised up together with Thyself Adam, who had fallen by his own will; for whose sake I heard of Thy power, O Lord, and that Thou didst come to save all Thy good servants. *Theotokion:*

Having come from the snowy part of the mountain, O Virgin Maiden, He was enrolled among servants, delivering all from servitude, He Who rules as Master unto ages; therefore we glorify thee who didst bear Him.

*Another Heirmos: Without leaving the Father's bosom.*

Having appeared all-chosen and good unto God before creation, illumine with thy radiance those who sing of thee.

Thou didst bear God for earthly mortals, O pure one, incarnate

**[Left Page — Fol. 160v]**

from thy pure blood, delivering from grievous sins those who honor and glorify thee with love, O all-pure one.

Rational nature ministers unto Him Who came forth from thee beyond understanding, O praised one, having now partaken of the ineffable mystery of thy childbearing, O all-blessed one.

*Ode 5. Heirmos:*

Night is unillumined to the faithless, but unto the faithful there is illumination in the sweetness of Thy words; and for this cause I rise early unto Thee, and I praise Thy Divinity.

For the sake of Thy servants Thou wast sold, O Christ, and Thou didst endure buffeting, procuring freedom for those who sing: I rise early unto Thee, and I praise Thy Divinity.

By Thy divine power, O Christ, through the weakness of the flesh,

**[Right Page — Fol. 161r]**

Thou didst cast down the strong one, and by Thy Resurrection, O Savior, Thou hast shown me to be victorious over death. *Theotokion:*

Thou didst give birth to God, O pure Mother, incarnate from thee, O lauded in godlike manner; for thou knewest not the male sex, but didst bring forth from the Holy Spirit.[^166]

*Another Heirmos: O Lord my God.*

When He was numbered with the lawless and lifted up upon the Place of the Skull, the luminaries hid themselves, and the earth was shaken, and the temple's splendor was rent in twain, making manifest the falling away of the Hebrews.

Thee, Who didst destroy the tyrant's power by the might of Thine incomprehensible Godhead, and didst raise the dead by Thy Resurrection, we glorify with hymns. *Theotokion:*

O Mother of the King and God, all-hymned Theotokos, unto those who in faith

**[Left Page — Fol. 161v]**

and love ever praise thee with hymns, send down cleansing from sins through thy supplications.

*Another Heirmos: Night is unillumined to the faithless.*

Jacob beheld a ladder established on high, and is taught the figure of thy virginity that knew not wedlock; for through thee God conversed with men, O all-pure Sovereign Lady.

Having now found eternal redemption through thee, O Virgin, we earnestly cry unto thee: Rejoice, O Sovereign Lady! And rejoicing in thy light, O all-pure one, we magnify thee with hymns.

Having found thee alone as a lily in the midst of thorns, O Virgin, shining with the brilliance of purity and with the light of maidenhood, the Bridegroom brought thee to Himself as a bride, O all-hymned one.

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*

As I float in the surge of worldly vanities,

**[Right Page — Fol. 162r]**

and am shipwrecked and sinking through sins, and cast out to the soul-destroying beast, yet like Jonah, O Christ, I cry unto Thee: Bring me up from the depth of death![^167]

The fainting souls of the righteous imprisoned in Hades remembered Thee, and prayed for salvation from Thee, which Thou, O Christ, didst grant by the Cross unto those in the nethermost regions, having arrived as the merciful One.

When Thine ensouled and not-made-by-hands temple was destroyed by sufferings, the company of the Apostles of old despaired of beholding it; but worshipping beyond expectation, they proclaimed Thee risen everywhere.

The Myrrh-bearing women, having first received the glad tidings of the joy of Thy rising, O Giver of life, exultingly told the Apostles: The curse of denial

**[Left Page — Fol. 162v]**

is dissolved, for Christ is risen from the dead!

Who among men can declare the manner of thine ineffable childbearing for our sake, O all-immaculate Virgin, Bride of God? For uncircumscribably, God the Word was united unto me and became flesh from thee.

*Another Heirmos: I cried unto the Lord in mine affliction.*

When the Master was crucified, He shook the whole earth, and as good, He healed its grievous fractures.

Death was abolished, and Hades was despoiled; for Christ is risen, preparing a path for men by His Resurrection.

O all-pure Sovereign Lady, sure Hope, preserve those who sing of thee in love as the true Theotokos.

*Another Heirmos: As I float.*

The figures of the Law and the prophecies of the prophets clearly prefigured thee as the one who would give birth

**[Right Page — Fol. 163r]**

to the Benefactor of all creation, O pure one, Who in many parts and in diverse manners has bestowed benefits upon those who faithfully magnify thee.[^168]

Adam the first-formed, alienated of old from divine Paradise food by the plotting of the manslayer, hast thou led in again, O Bride of God, having given birth to Him Who delivered us from transgression.

He Who by divine counsel and effective power composed all things out of non-existence came forth from thy womb, O pure one, and shone with divinely-originating rays upon those sitting in the shadow of death.

*Ode 7. Heirmos:*

Of old the youths showed the burning furnace to be cooling, singing to the one God and saying: Supremely exalted God of our fathers, blessed art Thou.

**[Left Page — Fol. 163v]**

By the tree was Adam put to death, having wrought disobedience by his own will, but by Christ's obedience is he quickened; for for my sake is the supremely glorified Son of God crucified.

All creation sings of Thee risen from the tomb, O Christ; for Thou, the Life, didst blossom forth in Hades, the Resurrection to the dead, and the supremely glorified Light to those in darkness.

Rejoice, daughter of corruptible Adam! Rejoice, only Bride of God! Rejoice, for whose sake corruption was driven away, because thou didst give birth to God: Whom implore, O pure Virgin, that we all may be saved.

*Another Heirmos: He Who into the fiery furnace.*

Thou Who didst blunt the sting of sin on the Tree of the Cross, and didst tear up the handwriting of Adam's transgression by the spear of Thy side: Blessed art Thou, O Lord God of our fathers.

**[Right Page — Fol. 164r]**

Thou Who by the piercing of Thy side and the sprinkling of Thy divine blood didst cleanse the earth defiled by the blood of idol sacrifices: Blessed art Thou, O Lord God of our fathers. *Triadikon:*

The undivided Nature we orthodoxly theologize: God the unoriginate Father, and the Word and the Spirit of the same dominion, crying: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers.[^169]

*Another Heirmos: The furnace for the youths.*

Having acquired for thyself a variegated, gilded adornment, thy Creator and Lord loved thee: Supremely exalted God of our fathers, blessed art Thou.

Isaiah is cleansed, receiving a live coal of old, beholding typically thy childbearing, O Maiden: Supremely exalted God of our fathers, blessed art Thou.

**[Left Page — Fol. 164v]**

All the divine prophets, beholding the figures of thy divine childbearing, joyfully sang and cried: Supremely exalted God of our fathers, blessed art Thou.

*Ode 8. Heirmos:*

Unconsumed by fire as it partook of it on Sinai, the bush revealed God to slow-tongued, stammering Moses; and the children cast into the fire through zeal for God were shown to be singers: All ye works of the Lord, sing ye the Lord, and supremely exalt Him unto ages.

The all-pure, rational Lamb, having been slain for the world, taking away the ordinances according to the Law, cleanses all its transgressions as God, for those who ever cry: All ye works of the Lord, sing ye the Lord, and supremely exalt Him unto ages.

Our flesh, received from the Creator, which was not incorruptible before the Passion,

**[Right Page — Fol. 165r]**

was made untouched by corruption after the Passion and Resurrection, and it renews mortals who cry: All ye works of the Lord, sing ye the Lord, and supremely exalt Him unto ages.[^170]

Thy pure and all-immaculate childbearing, O Virgin, cleansed the foul and abominable of the universe, and became the cause of our reconciliation with God, O all-pure one; therefore, O Theotokos, all the works of the Lord hymn thee unto ages.

*Another Heirmos: The only unoriginate.*

Him Who endured sufferings voluntarily, and was nailed to the Cross willingly, and destroyed the power of Hades: praise, ye priests, and supremely exalt Him, ye peoples, unto ages.

Him Who abolished the dominion of death, and rose from the tomb with glory, and saved the human race: praise, ye priests, and supremely exalt Him, ye peoples, unto ages.

**[Left Page — Fol. 165v]**

### Notes

[^154]: Folios 148v–149r contain the concluding Resurrection Stichera and the Anatolian (*Восточны*) stichera of Sunday Great Vespers in Tone 7 at "Lord, I have cried", culminating in the opening of the famous Dogmatic Theotokion (*Мати убо познася паче естества, Богородице*). The hymnography emphasizes the paradoxical twofold nature of Christ (first bodiless as the Word, then incarnate and put to death as man, rising in divine power), and David's prophecies in Zion (Psalms 49:2, 109:2 LXX).
[^155]: Folios 149v–150r conclude the Sunday Great Vespers of Tone 7 (with the completion of the Dogmatikon and the Aposticha stichera) and transition on Fol. 150r to the Sunday Midnight Office (*В неделю на полунощници*) with the opening of the Canon to the Holy and Life-Originating Trinity (*Канѡнъ святей живоначальней Троице*). Note the famous theological aphorism in the Dogmatikon: *идеже бо хощетъ Богъ, побеждается естества чинъ* ("for where God so wills, the order of nature is overcome"). The Midnight Trinity Canon is composed to the Heirmos *Маниемъ ти* (*By Thy beck*).
[^156]: Folios 150v–151r contain Odes 1 and 3 of the Sunday Midnight Office Trinity Canon in Tone 7. The hymnographer articulates high Trinitarian theology: the Father as the unoriginate Mind (*Оумъ безначалный*) begetting the co-eternal Word and causing the co-unoriginate Spirit to shine forth, and as the unoriginate root (*ѿ корене безначална*) from which the Word sprouts like a shoot (*стебль*). Christological typology is prominent in the Ode 1 Theotokion, where the Burning Bush (*купина*) seen by Moses prefigures the virginal conception without corruption.
[^157]: Folios 151v–152r span Odes 4 and 5 of the Sunday Midnight Office Trinity Canon, including the Sessional Hymn (*Сѣдаленъ: Помилуй, Троице святая*) after Ode 3. Note the antinomic Trinitarian phrasing on Fol. 152r: *нераздѣлно тя раздѣляемъ* ("indivisibly we distinguish Thee"), reflecting the classical Cappadocian synthesis of the undivided divine essence (*ousia*) existing in three distinct hypostases.
[^158]: Folios 152v–153r contain Odes 6 and 7 of the Sunday Midnight Office Trinity Canon, divided by the mid-canon Sessional Hymn (*Тажъ Сѣдална: Троице единосущная*). Fol. 152v displays notable technical theological vocabulary from St. John of Damascus's *De Fide Orthodoxa*: distinguishing the Persons solely by their hypostatic/existential properties (*различіе имуще точію, бытными коимиждо свойствы* — Greek ἰδιώμασι ὑποστατικοῖς), namely unbegottenness in the Father, generation in the Son, and procession in the Holy Spirit.
[^159]: Folios 153v–154r span Odes 8 and 9 of the Sunday Midnight Office Trinity Canon. In Ode 8, the refrains draw from the Song of the Three Holy Children (Daniel 3:57–88: *Вся дѣла Господня Господа пойте* / "Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord"). The hymnographer's personal petition on Fol. 154r asks the Lord to make the worshiper a "temple of Thy thrice-radiant ray" (*Храмъ мя твоея тріесіянныя сотвори луча*), safe from unseen spiritual demons and fleshly passions.
[^160]: Folios 154v–155r mark the completion of the Sunday Midnight Office Trinity Canon and the transition to Sunday Matins in Tone 7 at "God is the Lord" (*На Богъ Господь*). Fol. 155r features the seminal Resurrection Troparion of Tone 7 (*Разруши крестомъ своимъ смерть* / *Κατέλυσας τῷ σταυρῷ σου τὸν θάνατον*), accompanied by its Resurrection Theotokion celebrating Mary's perpetual virginity (*яже прежде рождества дѣва, и въ рождествѣ дѣва, и по рождествѣ паки дѣва*), followed by the first Kathisma Sessional Hymn (*Знаменану гробу* / *Τῆς ζωῆς ἐν тῷ τάφῳ*).
[^161]: Folios 155v–156r contain the second Kathisma Sessional Hymn, the Sunday Hypakoe (*Иже нашъ зракъ воспріемый* / *Ὁ τὴν ἡμετέραν μορφὴν ἀναλαβών*), and the opening Antiphons of the Tone 7 Sunday Songs of Ascents (*Степенна* / *Ἀναβαθμοί*). The text paraphrases Psalm 125:1–6 LXX ("When the Lord turned back the captivity of Zion... They that sow in tears shall reap in joy") and Psalm 126:1 LXX ("Except the Lord build the house, they labor in vain that build it"), reinterpreting the house allegorically as the "spiritual house" (*домъ душевный*).
[^162]: Folios 156v–157r conclude the Sunday Songs of Ascents (Antiphons 2 and 3), give the Sunday Matins Prokeimenon (*Воскресни, Господи Боже мой* / Psalm 9:33 LXX) and Gospel rubric, and open the Sunday Matins Canons of Tone 7. Antiphon 3 draws directly from Psalm 127:3 LXX ("Thy children like young olive plants round about thy table"). The opening Resurrection Canon is set to the Heirmos *Маніемъ ти на земны образъ* (*Νεύσει σου πρὸς γεώδη*), recounting the drying of the Red Sea (Exodus 14).
[^163]: Folios 157v–158r present the concluding troparia of Ode 1 for both the Cross-Resurrection and Theotokos Canons, followed by the opening of Ode 3 of the Sunday Matins Resurrection Canon in Tone 7. On Fol. 157v, the Cross-Resurrection troparion meditates on the "two life-bearing streams" (*Два источника живоносна*) flowing from Christ's pierced side (John 19:34: blood and water, typifying the Holy Mysteries of Baptism and the Eucharist). The Ode 3 Heirmos on Fol. 158r (*В начатцѣхъ небеса*) paraphrases Psalm 32:6 LXX: "By the word of the Lord the heavens were established, and all the host of them by the breath of His mouth."
[^164]: Folios 158v–159r conclude Ode 3 of the Sunday Matins Resurrection Canon in Tone 7 and feature Ode 3 of the Cross-Resurrection Canon (Heirmos: *Свышше силу* / *The power from on high*) and Theotokos Canon (Heirmos: *В начатцех небеса* / *In the beginning the heavens*). Notice the Christological imagery on Fol. 158v where Christ by His Cross quenches the flaming sword that guarded Eden (Genesis 3:24) and opens Paradise to the penitent thief (Luke 23:43). Fol. 159r meditates on the serpent's deceit in Eden, countered by the Son of God taking human flesh in the Virgin's womb to deify mankind through His tender mercy.
[^165]: Folios 159v–160r encompass Ode 4 of the Sunday Matins Canons in Tone 7. Heirmos 1 (*Очя недра не оставль* / *Not leaving the Father's bosom*) expresses the classical patristic paradox of the Incarnation: the divine Logos descends to earth without departing from the Father's bosom. The troparia depict the innocent Master submitting to scourging and standing before unjust human judges to free mankind from the condemnation of sin. The Cross-Resurrection Heirmos on Fol. 160r (*Смотряй* / *Foreseeing*) echoes the Prophet Habakkuk (Habakkuk 3:2 LXX), celebrating Christ's voluntary assumption of human form to slay the tyrant of deception.
[^166]: Folios 160v–161r present the conclusion of Ode 4 and the entirety of Ode 5 across the Resurrection and Cross-Resurrection Canons in Tone 7. Heirmos 5 (*Нощь несветла неверным* / *Night is unillumined to the faithless*) echoes Isaiah 26:9 LXX: "Out of the night my spirit awakes early unto Thee, O God, for Thy commandments are a light upon the earth." On Fol. 161r, the crucifixion narrative highlights cosmic convulsions at Christ's elevation upon Golgotha—the darkening of the luminaries, the earthquake, and the rending of the temple veil (Matthew 27:51), signifying the passing of the Old Covenant.
[^167]: Folios 161v–162r span Ode 5 of the Theotokos Canon and Ode 6 of the Resurrection Canon in Tone 7. Fol. 161v employs rich Marian typology: Jacob's ladder extending to heaven (Genesis 28:12) and the lily among thorns (Song of Songs 2:2). Ode 6 opens with the Jonah-typology Heirmos (*Плавающего во молве* / *Floating in the surge of worldly vanities* / Jonah 2:1–10), prefiguring the three-day burial and Resurrection. Fol. 162r portrays the souls of the righteous imprisoned in Hades awaiting Christ's descent, and the Apostles' astonishment transformed into proclamation when the Myrrh-bearing women announce the Resurrection.
[^168]: Folios 162v–163r conclude Ode 6 with the Cross-Resurrection and Theotokos troparia, and open Ode 7 of the Sunday Matins Resurrection Canon. Fol. 162v articulates Chalcedonian Christology: the uncircumscribed God the Word united Himself to humanity and became flesh from the Virgin. Ode 7 opens with the Heirmos of the Three Holy Youths in the Babylonian furnace (*Пещь отроцы* / *The furnace for the youths* / Daniel 3), singing praise to the God of our fathers. Fol. 163r notes how the Creator, Who brought all things out of non-existence by His divine counsel and effective power, shone forth from Mary's womb as the radiant dawn upon those sitting in the shadow of death.
[^169]: Folios 163v–164r contain the troparia of Ode 7 for both the Resurrection and Cross-Resurrection Canons, along with a solemn Trinitarian stanza (*Троична*). On Fol. 163v, the Pauline Adam-Christ typology (Romans 5:12–19; 1 Corinthians 15:21–22) is set forth: Adam suffered death through voluntary disobedience at the tree, but mankind is quickened through Christ's obedience on the Tree of the Cross. Fol. 164r celebrates the divine blood from Christ's pierced side cleansing the earth stained by the blood of pagan idolatry, followed by an explicit anti-Sabellian and anti-Arian Trinitarian confession (*Нераздельное естество* / *The undivided Nature*). The Theotokos troparion invokes Isaiah's vision of the live coal taken from the altar by the seraph (Isaiah 6:6–7) as a type of Christ borne in the Virgin's womb.
[^170]: Folios 164v–165r present the transition from Ode 7 to Ode 8 of the Sunday Matins Canons in Tone 7. Heirmos 8 (*Неопална огнем* / *Unconsumed by fire*) weaves together the Burning Bush of Mount Sinai (Exodus 3:2)—which revealed God to Moses of halting tongue—with the Three Holy Children preserved amidst the flames of Babylon (Daniel 3:57–88). On Fol. 165r, the hymnographer emphasizes that human flesh, which before the Passion was subject to corruption, was rendered incorruptible and made new through Christ's Passion and Resurrection, calling all created works of the Lord to bless and highly exalt Him unto all ages.
[^171]: Folios 165v–166r span the conclusion of Ode 8 and the opening of Ode 9 (the Megalynarion ode). Fol. 165v extols the pre-eternal Word born of the Virgin in the last times to save mankind. Ode 9 opens on Fol. 166r with the Heirmos *Нетления искушения* (*Giving flesh in exchange to the all-craftsman Word*), addressing the Virgin as the "vessel of the Incomprehensible, domain of the Uncontainable." A sharp anti-Monophysite and anti-Theopaschite polemic follows: let all alien-minded heretics be put to shame who ascribe suffering to the divine Nature; for the Lord of glory was crucified in the flesh, but remained impassible in His divine nature, being one Christ in two natures.
[^172]: Folios 166v–167r continue Ode 9 across the Resurrection, Cross-Resurrection, and Theotokos Canons, featuring a dogmatic Triadikon (*Троиченъ*). Fol. 166v warns against both Sabellian modalism ("we divide not the Trinity in essence, but confess It in Hypostases") and Arian subordinationism ("we sever not the Unity into distinct natures, but worship one Godhead"). It also confounds those who imagine a single composite nature in Christ (*единосложное естество*), demonstrating that He died as man, but as Creator of all rose from the dead. Fol. 167r recalls King David naming Mary the Ark of divine holiness (Psalm 131:8 LXX), carrying within her God Who sits in the Father's bosom.
[^173]: Folios 167v–168r conclude the Sunday Matins Canons and introduce the Sunday Praises / Lauds (*На хвалитех: Стихиры Воскресны Гласъ, з*). The initial stichera on Fol. 167v–168r celebrate the cosmic triumph of the Resurrection: "Christ is risen from the dead, having broken the bonds of death; proclaim good tidings of great joy, O earth; sing, O heavens, the glory of God!" Stichera 2 and 3 paraphrase Psalm 115:3 LXX ("What shall I render unto the Lord for all that He has rendered unto us?"), recounting how the Word became flesh and dwelt among us—the Benefactor to the ungrateful, the Deliverer to the captives, the Sun of Righteousness to those sitting in darkness.
[^174]: Folios 168v–169r conclude the Sunday Praises/Lauds in Tone 7 and open the Sunday Beatitudes (*Блаженны*). Fol. 168v articulates the Christological Stone typology, conflating Psalm 117:22 LXX ("The stone which the builders rejected, the same is become the head of the corner") and Isaiah 28:16 with the apocalyptic imagery of Daniel 2:34–45 (the stone cut out of the mountain without human hand prefiguring the virginal conception) and Daniel 7:13–14 (the Son of Man coming on the clouds of heaven unto the Ancient of Days). Fol. 169r introduces the Sunday Beatitudes in Tone 7, contrasting the fatal tree of knowledge in Eden with Christ as the Tree of Life whose fruit brings immortality, and invoking the penitent thief (Luke 23:42).
[^175]: Folios 169v–170r conclude the Sunday Beatitudes in Tone 7 and open Sunday Evening Vespers (for Monday). Fol. 169v features a dogmatic Triadikon emphasizing the identity of essence and distinction of hypostases ("Light is the Father, Light is the Son the Word, Light is the Holy Spirit; yet one Light is the Trinity, for God is one in three Persons"), followed by a Theotokion celebrating human deification through the Incarnation (*обожившеся тебе ради*). Fol. 170r begins Sunday Evening Vespers with compunctious Penitential Stichera at "Lord, I have cried" to the Podoben *Днесь бдитъ Іюда* (*Today Judas watches*), urging the soul to marvel at God's cosmic condescension in descending to earth to raise mankind from sinful misery.
[^176]: Folios 170v–171r encompass the Sunday Evening Vespers (Monday cycle) of Tone 7, featuring stichera to the Holy Archangels and Bodiless Hosts (as Monday is liturgically dedicated to the angelic orders). Following the Areopagitic hierarchy (*On the Celestial Hierarchy*), the Archangels are venerated as "secondary lights" (*свѣти вторіи*) shining by participation in the Primary Uncreated Light of the Trinity. At the Aposticha, the service transitions to the Lukan parables of repentance: the Prodigal Son (Luke 15:11–32) begging to be received as a hired servant, and the traveler beaten by thieves (Luke 10:30–37) seeking Christ the Physician of souls.
[^177]: Folios 171v–172r mark the transition between Tones 7 and 8. Fol. 171v concludes the weekly cycle of Tone 7 with a Marian Theotokion synthesizing major Old Testament types: the Bridal Chamber, the Closed Gate (Ezekiel 44:2), the Fiery Throne (Daniel 7:9), the Uncut Mountain (Daniel 2:34), and the Unburnt Bush (Exodus 3:2), sealed with an ornamental printer's palmette tailpiece. Fol. 172r opens Tone 8 with a primary Lavra woodcut headpiece across the page width, monumental Cyrillic ligatured vyaz lettering (*ВЪ КНИГИ ѠКТѠИХЪ ВСКРСНЪ / Гласъ О́смый*), and an ornate historiated initial 'В' introducing Saturday Great Vespers at "Lord, I have cried" (*Вечернюю пѣснь и словесную службу* / *An evening song and reasonable service* / Romans 12:1).
