# TONE 2: SUNDAY RESURRECTIONAL HYMNOGRAPHY

## SATURDAY EVENING: VESPERS (TONE 2)

[f. 27rA] *In the Book of the Octoechos, of the Resurrection. Composition of John of Damascus. Tone 2:*

*At "Lord, I have cried," Stichera of the Resurrection. Tone 2.*[^32]

*Composed by John Damascene.*

Come, let us worship God the Word, begotten of the Father before all ages, Who was incarnate of the Virgin Mary; for having endured the Crucifixion, He was committed unto burial as He Himself willed, and having risen from the dead, He saved me, the straying man.

[f. 27vA] Christ our Savior, having nailed the handwriting that was against us to the Cross, tore it up, and abolished the dominion of death: we worship His third-day rising.

With the Archangels let us hymn the Resurrection of Christ; for He is the Redeemer and Savior of our souls, and in fearful glory and mighty power He comes again to judge the world which He created.

*Other Stichera, Anatolian:*

The Angel proclaimed Thee, the crucified and buried Master, and said unto the women: Come and see where the Lord lay; for He has risen as He said, as Almighty: therefore we worship Thee, the only Immortal One; O Life-giving Christ, have mercy on us!

By Thy Cross Thou didst abolish the curse of the tree; by Thy burial Thou didst slay the dominion of death, [f. 28rA] and by Thy rising Thou didst enlighten the human race: therefore we cry unto Thee: O Benefactor, Christ our God, glory to Thee!

The gates of death opened unto Thee in fear, O Lord, and the gatekeepers of Hades seeing Thee were afraid; for Thou didst shatter the gates of brass and crushed the iron bars, and didst deliver us from darkness and the shadow of death, and broke our bonds asunder.

Singing a saving hymn with our lips, come, let us all sing in the house of the Lord, falling down and saying: O Thou Who wast crucified upon the tree, and didst rise from the dead, and art in the bosom of the Father, cleanse our sins!

*Glory, both now: Theotokion (Dogmatikon).*[^33]

The shadow of the Law passed away when grace arrived; for as the bush was not consumed [f. 28vA] so being a Virgin didst thou bring forth, and a Virgin didst thou remain. In place of the pillar of fire, the Sun of righteousness shone forth; in place of Moses, Christ, the salvation of our souls.[^34]

*Other Stichera, at the Aposticha, of the Resurrection, Tone 2:*[^35]

Thy Resurrection, O Christ Savior, hath enlightened the whole universe, and Thou didst recall Thine own creation: O almighty Lord, glory to Thee!

By the tree, O Savior, Thou didst abolish the curse of the tree; and by Thy burial Thou didst slay the dominion of death, and by Thy rising Thou didst enlighten our race; therefore we cry unto Thee: O Life-giver, Christ our God, glory to Thee!

Appearing upon the Cross, O Christ, nailed fast, Thou didst change the beauty of creation; and the soldiers, showing inhumanity, pierced Thy side with a spear, while the Hebrews asked that the sepulcher [f. 29rA] be sealed, not knowing Thy power. But Thou, Who in the tender mercy of Thy heart didst accept burial and didst rise on the third day: O Lord, glory to Thee!

O Life-giving Christ, Who willingly didst endure the Passion for the sake of mortals, and didst descend into Hades as the Mighty One, snatching away those who awaited Thy coming as from the mouth of a fierce beast: Thou didst grant them to dwell in paradise instead of Hades; therefore grant also unto us, who glorify Thy third-day rising, cleansing of sins and great mercy.

*Glory, both now: Theotokion:*[^36]

O wonder new beyond all wonders of old! For who hath known a mother bearing without a husband, and carrying in her arms Him Who upholds all creation? It is the will of God that was brought forth: Whom as an Infant having borne in thine arms, O all-pure one, and possessing maternal boldness toward Him, [f. 29vA] cease not to pray for those who honor thee: that He have compassion and save our souls.

*Then the Resurrection Troparion, Tone 2:*

When Thou didst descend unto death, O Life...

*Theotokion:*

All beyond thought, all all-glorious are thy...

## SUNDAY MIDNIGHT OFFICE (TONE 2)

*Canon to the Most Holy Trinity, Tone 2:*

*Ode 1:*

*Heirmos:* Of old, the all-armed power drowned the whole host of Pharaoh in the deep; and the Incarnate Word consumed all-wicked sin: the all-glorious Lord, for gloriously hath He been glorified.[^37]

*Troparia:*

Let us sing hymns unto the Triune and unique beginning nature of the Godhead, crying: O abyss of mercy, [f. 30rA] possessing it unconsumed and essential: have compassion and save those who worship Thee, as Lover of mankind!

Being the source and root, the Father is the Cause: in the Son and Thy Holy Spirit of consubstantial Godhead, shine into my heart the three-sunned Light, and illuminate me by communion with Thy divine radiance!

O three-radiant Unity and divine Principle: completely disperse the darkness of my sins and passions by Thy bright rays and sweet communions, and make me an animate church and tabernacle of Thine unapproachable glory!

*Theotokion:*

The ancient flow of our nature, having suffered unworthily and slid into corruption, O all-pure one: being incarnate from thy womb, God the Word [f. 30vA] shone forth in love for mankind, and mystically taught us the tri-radiant divine Principle.

*Ode 3:*

*Heirmos:* Establish me upon the rock of faith...

*Troparia:*

By the equality of the divinely sovereign nature, I glorify the equally honored Persons of the Trinity: for proceeding as Life from Life, Thou art sinless, our only God; and there is none holy besides Thee, O Lord!

Thou didst establish the immaterial heavenly ranks as mirrors of Thy goodness, O Trinity, undivided and sole Sovereignty, to sing unto Thee ceaselessly; but accept even now our praise from earthy lips!

Establish upon the rock of faith, and broaden by the abyss of Thy love, the hearts and thoughts of Thy servants, O three-sunned Unity: for Thou art our God, trusting in Whom we shall not be put to shame!

He Who formed every substance beforehand [f. 31rA] united with the creation in thy womb, through unmeasured goodness, O Theotokos; and the three-sunned Light shone forth unto all, of one Godhead and Lordship.

*Sessional Hymn to the Trinity, Tone 2:*

*Podoben: "Seeking the highest" (Вы́шнихъ и҆щѧ̀):* [^38]

Holy Trinity, have mercy on those whom Thou didst create, and deliver them from fire and all condemnation, for Thou canst do all things, as compassionate and most merciful God; therefore falling down before Thee, we cry: Forgive our sins, O Lover of mankind!

*Glory, both now: Theotokion:*

Filled with many wounds, Thy servants, by the invisible arrows of the destroyer, and lying upon the bed of despair, O Lady, we cry: Despise not our supplication, who flee unto thee in faith!

*Ode 4:*

*Heirmos:* Covered by the report...

*Troparia:*

Not even the immaterial angelic ranks can comprehend Thee, O Unity, Trinity without beginning; [f. 31vA] yet with an earthy tongue, we hymn Thy essential goodness with fear and glorify it.

Being Creator of creation and Almighty Ruler of human nature, Thou seest all my infirmity now, as All-seeing One; therefore have compassion on Thy servant, and lead me up unto a better life!

We hymn the unconfused Trinity of the primordial Unity, as possessing properties and distinction of Hypostases, yet united and undivided in Light, glory, and Godhead.

*Theotokion:*

A pure and all-pure temple did the Creator find thee alone clearly from the ages, O Virgin Theotokos: having dwelt wherein, He reformed human nature, as Lover of mankind.

*Ode 5:*

*Heirmos:* Thou Who art the Enlightenment...

*Troparia:*

As Thou extendest in every way upon all beings the peace-giving and saving rays of Thy providence, [f. 32rA] O King of humility: preserve me in Thy peace, for Thou art the Life and peace of all things.

When Thou didst appear unto Moses in the bush in a fiery vision, Thou, O Word from the Father, wast called an Angel, revealing unto us Thy coming,[^39] whereby Thou didst clearly proclaim unto all the tri-hypostatic dominion of the one Godhead.

O holy Trinity, sole Sovereignty, Who hast set forth natural and co-eternal glory: vouchsafe those who hymn Thee in Orthodox faith to behold Thy glory, the unoriginate and unique three-sunned radiance!

*Theotokion:*

Being by essence the Upholder of all ages, O Virgin Mother, God the Word was ineffably contained within thy womb, calling mankind unto union with the one Lordship.

[f. 32vA] *Ode 6:*

*Heirmos:* Held fast in the abyss of sins...

*Troparia:*

O Thou Who willest mercy, have compassion on those who believe in Thee, O three-sunned God; deliver us from transgressions, and save us from passions and afflictions!

By Thine ineffable abyss of goodness, brightly grant me the Light-giving and unsearchable radiance of Thy brilliance and three-radiant Godhead.

*Theotokion:*

Ineffably, O Virgin, the Most High, having become man from thee, clothed Himself in man in every way; and illuminated me with the three-sunned Light.

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 2:*

*Podoben: "Seeking the highest" (Вы́шнихъ и҆щѧ̀):*

O Christ God, only all-good One, Who didst pour out Thy Blood for our sake when suspended upon the Cross by the counsel of Thy Father and of the Spirit: therefore taking courage we cry: Have mercy on Thy flock, O Lover of mankind!

*Glory, both now: Theotokion:*

O pure Virgin, have mercy on thy servants, and deliver from fire and all torment those who night and day cry [f. 33rA] unto thy Son, O all-pure one; despise not Thy flock, O Christ, through the prayers of her who bare Thee!

*Ode 7:*

*Heirmos:* From the golden image...

*Troparia:*

Thou ever appointest the angelic hosts unto immutability, O Lord Who alone art immutable and tri-hypostatic: show forth also my heart to be ever unswerving, that I may fervently glorify Thee and piously hymn Thee!

The noetic ranks of immaterial essences are illuminated by Thy rays, O God, sole Ruler and Three-sunned One, becoming secondary lights by placement: by their radiance and communion show me also forth as light, as the three-radiant Light-giver!

Illuminated by the three-radiant beams, O all-holy Trinity, in one Dominion, preserve Thy hymn-singers from adversary assaults, that Thee [f. 33vA] we may ceaselessly glorify: Father and Son, and Holy Spirit, in one Godhead.

*Theotokion:*

Guiding us and lifting us up unto the heavens unerringly who love thee, He Who for His ineffable love for mankind became man in the Virgin's womb, and deified man, and sits upon the throne of glory with the Father.

*Ode 8:*

*Heirmos:* Of the image...

*Troparia:*

Unapproachable Trinity, co-eternal, unoriginate, divinely sovereign, immutable unto the ages except in Light-bearing properties: bring to naught all evil counsel of adversaries and turmoil of demons, keeping me ever unharmed, O Lord of all!

All-wisely and almighty, uncircumscribed, three-sunned sole Sovereignty, Who didst establish the world and dost preserve it by Thy power in all-perfect order: dwell within my heart, to sing and glorify Thee [f. 34rA] unflinchingly, together with the angelic choirs unto all ages!

O Wisdom of the Father, incomprehensible, ineffable Word of God: without changing Thine immutable and divine essence, Thou didst assume human nature, and didst teach all to honor the unique Trinity as the Sovereign Principle unto all ages.

*Theotokion:*

Righteousness and Wisdom, guiding all things, loved thee worthily, O all-honored and light-bearing Virgin: ineffably did He dwell within thee, O all-pure one; and hymn-glorifying Him, we sing, crying: Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord; sing and supreme in glory exalt Him unto all ages!

*Ode 9:*

*Heirmos:* Him Who before the sun...

*Troparia:*

From the unoriginate Light, the co-unoriginate Son shone forth as Light, and the consubstantial Light, the Holy Spirit, [f. 34vA] proceeded, ineffably and in a manner befitting God: being assured of the incorrupt begetting, and likewise of the ineffable procession.

Shine into the hearts of those who hymn Thee, O three-sunned Godhead, with Thy three-radiant Light; and grant understanding in all things to perceive and see Thy good and perfect will, and to magnify and glorify Thee!

Being unreckoned in essence as God, and having an unreckoned abyss of compassions, Thou didst show compassion of old, O Trinity: so also have compassion upon us, Thy servants, and deliver us from transgressions, and assaults, and tribulations!

Save me, O my Savior, from every temptation and affliction, Who art hymned in three Persons, ineffably unique and almighty God; and preserve Thy flock, through the Theotokos' [f. 35rA] prayers.

## SUNDAY MATINS (TONE 2)

*At "God is the Lord," Troparion, Tone 2, chanted as on Saturday evening:* "When Thou didst descend..." *and its Theotokion.*

*Sessional Hymns of the Resurrection, Tone 2:*

*After the First Kathisma Reading:*[^40]

The noble Joseph, having taken down Thine all-pure Body from the tree, wrapped it in a clean shroud with sweet spices, and laying it in a new tomb, covered it; but on the third day Thou didst rise, O Lord, granting great mercy unto the world.

*Glory, both now: Theotokion:*

Most glorified art thou, O Theotokos, we hymn thee: for by the Cross of thy Son, Hades was overthrown and death was slain; we who were dead have risen, and have been deemed worthy of life; we have received paradise, the ancient delight. Therefore giving thanks we cry unto thee, O Virgin: Rejoice, thou who art full of grace, the Lord is with thee!

*After the Second Kathisma Reading:*

Unto the myrrh-bearing women standing by the sepulcher the Angel cried aloud: Myrrh is fitting for the dead, [f. 35vA] but Christ hath shown Himself a stranger to corruption: but cry aloud, singing: The Lord has risen, granting great mercy unto the world!

*Glory, both now: Theotokion:*

Having concealed thyself from the laws of nature, in thy divine childbirth thou didst unite virginity: for thou alone didst bring forth Him Who was begotten timelessly before thee; therefore, O Theotokos, we magnify thee!

*Hypakoe, Tone 2:*

After the Passion, the women having gone unto the tomb to anoint Thy Body, O Christ God, saw Angels in the tomb and were terrified; for they heard a voice from them: The Lord has risen, granting great mercy unto the world![^41]

*The Gradual Hymns (Anavathmoi), Tone 2:*

*Antiphon 1:*

Unto heaven I send up the eyes of my heart, unto Thee, O Savior: save me by Thine illumination![^42]

Have mercy on us who sin against Thee greatly, at every hour, O my Christ; give me a manner of repenting unto Thee [f. 36rA] before the end!

To the Holy Spirit it belongs to reign, to sanctify and to move creation: for He is God, consubstantial with the Father and the Word.

*Antiphon 2:*

Unless the Lord were among us, who could be kept whole from the enemy and manslayer together?

Unto their teeth deliver not Thy servant, O Savior; for like lions mine enemies move against me.

To the Holy Spirit belongs the origin of life and honor: for as God He is able to preserve all created things, from the Father and with the Son.

*Antiphon 3:*

They who trust in the Lord are like unto the holy mountain: who shall in nowise be moved by the assaults of the enemy.

In iniquities let not those who live divinely stretch forth their hands:

[f. 36vA] for Christ shall not leave the rod of sinners upon His lot.

Through the Holy Spirit gushes forth all wisdom, thence grace unto the Apostles; and through sufferings the Martyrs are crowned, and the Prophets behold mysteries.

*Prokimenon, Tone 2:* Arise, O Lord my God, in the commandment which Thou hast commanded, and the congregation of the peoples shall surround Thee.

*Verse: O Lord my God, in Thee have I put my hope, save me.*

*Then:* Let every breath praise the Lord. *Verse:* Praise God in His saints.

*Theotokion:*

Singing in orthodox manner, we the faithful glorify the Unity in three Hypostases, the consubstantial Trinity, the indivisible divine nature: the thrice-radiant, [f. 45rA] unwaning dawn, the one incorruptible light that has shone upon us.

## SUNDAY EVENING VESPERS (TONE 2)

*On Sunday Evening, at "Lord, I have cried," Penitential Stichera, Tone 2:[^53]*

*Podoben: "When from the Tree" (Е҆гда̀ ѿ дре́ва):*

Having the fountain of loving-kindness, and pouring forth the abysses of mercies and streams of compassions, O most good Father, and O Son, Word of the Father, and O Holy Spirit, uncreated Nature: accept our supplication and prayer, granting forgiveness unto all who abide in transgressions, as a compassionate God and Lover of mankind.

[f. 48rA] Thou hast by nature, as God, a womb of compassions and of mercies and goodness; therefore we pray Thee, O Christ our Savior, and falling down we call upon Thee, ever crying out: Grant unto Thy servants absolution of their many transgressions, and forgiveness for all wherein we have all sinned, as a compassionate God and Lover of mankind.

Desiring to save all as God, O Savior, for whose sake Thou didst become incarnate and appeared as man: save us who bow down before Thy commandments; for Thou camest not, O Lover of mankind, to save the righteous, but to loose by the grace of holy baptism those of us bound in transgressions by many sins, as compassionate and Lover of mankind.

*Other Stichera, to the Holy Angels, Tone 2:*

*Podoben: "The same" (то́йже):*

Thrones, Cherubim, and Seraphim, Dominions and Powers, and honorable Authorities, Angels, [f. 48vA] Archangels, and Principalities with them, who together ceaselessly sing in unison unto Him Who composed their incorruptible nature, teaching all to honor the one Essence of the Trinity, united, equally honorable, and enthroned together.[^54]

The foremost choirs of the bodiless hosts, receiving the rays of the Godhead in unmediated splendors, transmit the divine brightnesses in their turn unto the rest; and by the law of love they convey these unto us also according to worthiness, by communion in each one's hierarchical purity.

Having the soul lifted on high, on high the eye of the heart, and having our intellectual strivings directed by divine love, let us ever stretch forth in our souls; so that, illuminated by the rays from thence, we may escape the darkness of the passions, expecting [f. 49rA] to stand with the angels before the awesome throne of the Creator, being transformed from light to light.

*Glory, Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

The multitude of angels, O most pure one, hymn Thy Son with thrice-holy voices; for Thou art unto Him a fiery throne and an animate palace, a divine bridge ever leading from earth unto Him. Rejoice, full of grace! we cry unto thee in unison with the Archangel Gabriel, for thou hast borne the Fountain of joy.

*At the Aposticha:*

*Penitential Stichera, Tone 2.*

I have sinned against Thee, O Savior, like the Prodigal Son; receive me, O Father, as I repent, and have mercy on me, O God.

I cry unto Thee, O Christ Savior, with the voice of the Publican: Cleanse me as Thou didst cleanse him, and have mercy on me, O God.

Not loving the pleasures of earth, the passionbearers [f. 49vA] were accounted worthy of heavenly good things, and became fellow citizens with the angels. By their prayers, O Lord, have mercy on us and save us.

*Glory, Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

Rejoice, O Mary, Mother of God, impregnable temple, yet more holy, even as the prophet cries: Holy is Thy temple, wonderful in righteousness!

### Notes

[^32]: Primary Landmark: Tone 2 (*Гласъ Второй*) opens on Fol. 27r with a monumental full-page ornamental woodcut headpiece bearing the inscription `ВЪ КНИГИ ОКТѠИХЪ ВСКРⷭ҇НЪ` and the explicit attribution `Творе́нїе І҆ѡа́нна . Дамаски́на` (Composed by John Damascene). It contains the first cycle of Saturday evening Great Vespers Stichera at *Lord, I have cried* (*Pervovechnomu ot Otsa* / "Come, let us worship God the Word...").
[^33]: Theotokion / Dogmatikon of Tone 2 at Saturday Great Vespers (*Preide sen' zakonnaya, blagodati prishedshi* / "The shadow of the Law passed away when grace arrived..."): St. John of Damascus's classic theological hymn expounding the transition from Old Testament types and shadows to New Testament grace and the reality of the Incarnation.
[^34]: Typology of the Exodus in the Dogmatikon of Tone 2 (*Preide sen' zakonnaya*): The hymn explicitly contrasts the shadow of the Mosaic Law with the grace of the Gospel, drawing upon the pillar of fire (Exodus 13:21–22) that led the Israelites through the wilderness, which is fulfilled and superseded by Christ, the spiritual Sun of righteousness (Malachi 4:2 [LXX 3:20]). In place of Moses the lawgiver stands Christ the Savior, effecting the redemption of mankind.
[^35]: The Aposticha Stichera of the Resurrection, Tone 2 (*Voskreseniye Tvoye, Khriste Spase*): These four hymns, traditionally attributed to St. John of Damascus, celebrate the cosmic victory of the Cross over the tree of the Garden of Eden (Genesis 3:1–6), the harrowing of Hades, the piercing of the Lord's side by the soldier's spear (John 19:34), and the empty tomb sealed by the Judean authorities (Matthew 27:62–66).
[^36]: Theotokion of the Aposticha (*O chudese novago vsekho drevnikho chudes* / "O wonder new beyond all wonders of old!"): Composed by St. John of Damascus, this venerable hymn ponders the mystery of the Virgin Mother holding in her maternal embrace the uncircumscribed God Who sustains all creation in the palm of His hand. It affirms the orthodox dogma of the Theotokos as defined at the Council of Ephesus (431 AD) and the Council of Chalcedon (451 AD).
[^37]: Sunday Midnight Office (*Mesonyktikon*), Canon to the Most Holy and Undivided Trinity (*Kanon Troichen*), Tone 2: Composed by Metrophanes, Metropolitan of Smyrna (c. 820–880; author of the Triadikos Canons of the Octoechos). The canon provides an exhaustive patristic and scholastic synthesis of Trinitarian theology, systematically articulating the consubstantiality (*homoousion*), co-eternity, and unconfused hypostatic properties of the Father (the unbegotten Source and Cause), the Son (begotten before all ages), and the Holy Spirit (eternally proceeding from the Father).
[^38]: Sessional Hymn to the Holy Trinity, Tone 2, Ode 3 (*Svyataya Troitse, pomilui ikhzhe sozda*): Chanted according to the melodic model (*prosomoion*) *Vyshnikh ishcha* ("Seeking the things on high"), entreating the consubstantial Trinity to deliver creation from fire and condemnation and grant forgiveness of sins.
[^39]: Canon to the Holy Trinity, Ode 5, Troparion 2: Moses and the vision in the Burning Bush (Exodus 3:2–14). The hymn incorporates the traditional patristic christology (patristic consensus of St. Justin Martyr, St. Irenaeus of Lyons, and St. Athanasius the Great) identifying the "Angel of the Lord" who spoke from the flame as the pre-incarnate Logos, the "Angel of Great Counsel" (LXX Isaiah 9:6), revealing the tri-hypostatic nature of the one divine Principle.
[^40]: Sunday Matins Sessional Hymns of the Resurrection, Tone 2, after the First Kathisma: *Blagoobraznyi Iosif* ("The noble Joseph..."), the classic troparion of Great and Holy Saturday (commemorating Joseph of Arimathea taking down Christ's Body, wrapping it in a clean winding sheet, and placing it in a new rock-hewn sepulcher; Matthew 27:57–60, Mark 15:43–46), used here in the Sunday Resurrection cycle to proclaim the Lord's third-day rising.
[^41]: Hypakoe of Tone 2 (*Po strasti shedshe na grob* / "After the Passion, the women having gone unto the tomb"): The Hypakoe (from the Greek *hypakoe*, "obedience" or "listening attentively") recounts the holy Myrrh-bearing women who went at early dawn to anoint the Lord's sacred Body with aromatic spices, beholding the angelic messengers who declared that Christ is not among the dead (Mark 16:1–8, Luke 24:1–10).
[^42]: The Gradual Hymns (*Anavathmoi*) of Tone 2: Structured into three antiphons inspired by the Old Testament Psalms of Ascents (Psalms 119–133 [LXX 118–132]) sung by Hebrew pilgrims journeying up to Jerusalem. Each antiphon concludes with a pneumatological troparion (*Svyatomu Dukhu...*) extolling the Holy Spirit as the sovereign Principle of life, wisdom, and sanctification alongside the Father and the Son.
[^43]: Canons of Sunday Matins, Tone 2: The classic Byzantine-Ruthenian tri-canon structure prescribed for the Resurrection Matins in the Octoechos: (1) The Canon of the Resurrection by St. John of Damascus (*Vo glubine potopi drevle* / "Of old, the all-armed power drowned..."); (2) The Canon of the Cross (*Netrennuyu neobychuyu* / "Traversing an untrodden, unaccustomed path..."); and (3) The Canon of the Most Holy Theotokos to the same heirmos model.
[^44]: Codicological Rubric Anomaly in the Canon of the Cross, Tone 2, Ode 3: The rubric printed in red cinnabar reads *Бгородиченъ* (*Theotokion*), but the liturgical hymn text itself (*Mertv sobezakonnymi vmenisya, siyaya zhenam krasen dobrotoyu, Khriste yavisya voskreseniyem*) is a Christological Resurrectional/Stavro-resurrectional troparion celebrating Christ being numbered among the lawless (Isaiah 53:12) and appearing in transcendent beauty to the holy Myrrh-bearing women upon His third-day rising.
[^45]: The Resurrection Heirmos of Ode 4, Tone 2 (*Prishestvoval ot Devy, ni khodatai ni angel*): Composed by St. John of Damascus, grounded in the Septuagint of Isaiah 63:9 ("Not an ambassador, nor an angel, but the Lord Himself saved them...") and the prophetic vision of Habakkuk (Habakkuk 3:2–3). The hymn exalts the unmediated, personal condescension of the Divine Logos in taking upon Himself true human nature from the Virgin Mother of God.
[^46]: Typology of the Tripartite Wood of the Holy Cross in Ode 5 (*Yako kedry Khriste*): The troparion evokes Christ's voluntary crucifixion upon the cypress, the pine, and the cedar (*na kyparise, i na pevge i kedre*). This draws directly from the Septuagint prophecy of Isaiah 60:13 ("The glory of Lebanon shall come unto thee, with the cypress tree, the pine tree, and the cedar together, to beautify the place of My sanctuary"), a primary patristic and hymnographic type for the construction of the Venerable Cross.
[^47]: The Cherubim and the Reopening of Paradise in Ode 6 (*Blistitelya polozhil yesi padshemu kheruvim*): Direct scriptural allusion to Genesis 3:24, where the Cherubim and the turning, flashing sword of fire guarded the way to the tree of life after Adam's expulsion. Upon the Lord's crucifixion and triumph over death, the gates of Eden swung open to welcome the repentant thief (Luke 23:43), manifesting the royal highway to eternal communion.
[^48]: The Heirmos of Ode 7, Tone 2 (*Bogoprotivnoye veleniye, bezakonuyushchago muchitelya*): Typology of the Three Holy Youths in the Babylonian furnace (Daniel 3:19–28). While the tyrannical decree of King Nebuchadnezzar raised the flame forty-nine cubits high, Christ manifested spiritual dew (*khlad dukhovnyi*) amidst the inferno to preserve His confessors, prefiguring His victorious descent into Hades to deliver the righteous souls held captive from the beginning of the world.
[^49]: Heirmos of the Canon of the Cross, Ode 8 (*Telu zlatomu nepoklonishasya tribogatyya yunosha*): Commemorating the steadfast refusal of the three holy youths (Ananias, Azarias, and Misael) to bow down before the golden colossus on the plain of Dura (Daniel 3:1–18). Gazing upon the true, living image of God, they prefigured the mystery of the Incarnation. The technical theological term *osushchestvivyisya* ("having taken substance" / Greek *ousiotheis*) confirms the orthodox dogma of the true hypostatic enfleshment of the Divine Logos.
[^50]: Typology of the Thrice-Rich Tree of the Cross on Golgotha in Ode 9 (*Yako vrai nasazhdenno vo kraniyevi Spase*): The Venerable Cross planted upon Golgotha (*Kranion* / the Place of the Skull) is hailed as the new tree of life, far surpassing the tree of life in the Garden of Eden (Genesis 2:9). Irrigated by the water and the blood that flowed from the pierced side of the Savior (John 19:34), it buds forth incorruptible life for the whole world.
[^51]: Polemical Paschal Apologetic at the Praises (*Da rekut iyudeye*): This celebrated Resurrection sticheron at the Praises directly refutes the guard deception concocted by the chief priests (Matthew 27:62–66, 28:11–15). Employing dramatic rhetorical irony, the hymn demands of the guards: if they were asleep, how could they know the disciples stole Him; and why would grave-robbers take a naked corpse while leaving the linen cloths and face veil carefully rolled up in place (John 20:5–7)?
[^52]: The Sunday Beatitudes (*Blazheny* / *Makarismoi*) of Tone 2 at the Divine Liturgy: In the Byzantine-Ruthenian liturgical tradition, the Beatitudes from the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5:3–12) are sung at the Little Entrance, interpolated with troparia from the Octoechos commemorating the penitent thief, the harrowing of Hades, the empty sepulcher, and the angelic greeting to the Myrrh-bearing women.
[^53]: Sunday Evening Vespers (Entering Monday): Liturgically, Sunday evening marks the transition from the festive Lord's Day of the Resurrection into the penitential rhythm of the weekly cycle. Because Monday is dedicated to the Bodiless Powers of Heaven (the holy Angels), Vespers commences with penitential stichera (*pokayanny*) imploring the mercy of the Holy Trinity, followed by stichera in honor of the Nine Angelic Choirs to the melodic model (*podoben*) *Yegda ot dreva Tya* ("When from the Tree...").
[^54]: On the Dionysian Angelology of the Stichera to the Bodiless Powers on Sunday evening (entering Monday). The hymn directly reflects the *Celestial Hierarchy* of Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite, depicting the first hierarchy (Thrones, Cherubim, Seraphim) receiving the unmediated rays of the Godhead (*nekhodotaystvennymi siyanii*) and transmitting these divine illuminations down through the subordinate ranks according to their capacity and hierarchical purity (*k yerarkhichney komuzhdo chistote*).
