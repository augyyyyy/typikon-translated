## Service of the Holy Apostle, First-Martyr, and Archdeacon Stephen
*And of our venerable Father and Confessor Theodore the Branded, and his brother Theophanes.*[^399]
*At Vespers.*
*On "Lord, I have cried," Stichera of the Apostle, 3; and of the Venerable, 3.*
*Stichera of the Apostle, Tone 4. Podoben: "As one noble...":*
Having enlightened thy mind by the grace of the Spirit, thou didst appear as an angel in form, O Stephen, thy body shining with inward brightness, showing the radiance of thy soul unto those that beheld thee, through which thou didst also obtain the vision of light, the heavens being most gloriously opened unto thee, O beginning and boast of martyrs.
As steps and a ladder for ascent into heaven were the hurling of stones unto thee,

=== LEAF p153 ===
mounting whereon, thou didst behold the Lord standing at the right hand of the Father, stretching forth unto thee a crown of thy same name with His life-bearing right hand; near Whom thou standest as a noble victor, and the prince of martyrs.[^400]
Shining with signs and wonders, thou didst extinguish the council of the lawless with thy teachings; and being slain by them, and pelted with stones, thou didst pray for forgiveness for those who slew thee, following the voice of the Savior, into Whose hands thou didst commit thy spirit, O consecrated Stephen.[^401]
*Other Stichera of the Venerable, Tone and model melody the same:*[^402]
Unto the wounds of exile were added wounds unto thee, most grievous banishments, O venerable one, when he who lawlessly held the lawful throne fiercely agitated the laws of faith, casting down the most pure icon of the Savior; whom by divine grace, O servant of Christ, thou didst put to shame.[^403]
Neither prison nor affliction, nor gloomy darkness, nor wounds nor beatings, O father, were able to separate thee from the love of God, O Theodore; neither grievous, lingering, and intolerable sickness, nor scarcity of food, O divinely-speaking one; but unswervingly thou didst endure, foreseeing the recompense of thy labors.[^404]
Instead of sorrow, relief; instead of sickness, delight; instead of darkness, illumination has the Umpire of the contest granted unto thee, O venerable one; and instead of the writings upon thy face, O marvelous one, thou hast been written in the book of life,

and thou hast been enrolled in the church of the firstborn on high, and hast come with exceeding joy unto the festival of the angels.[^405]

But come, O ye lovers of the martyrs, taking flowers of hymns, let us crown our heads, and singing hymns let us say: O thou who didst enlighten thy soul with wisdom and grace, first-martyr of Christ God, ask peace for us, and great mercy.
