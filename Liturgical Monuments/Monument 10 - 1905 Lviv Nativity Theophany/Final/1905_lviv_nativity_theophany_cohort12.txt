*First Canon, Tone 1. Ode 4. Heirmos:*[^172]
Rod of the stem of Jesse, and blossom from it, O Christ, Thou hast budded forth from the Virgin; from the praised, overshadowed mountain, from the dense forest, Thou hast come, having become incarnate from the unwedded Maiden, Thou immaterial and God: Glory to Thy power, O Lord!
Thou Whom of old Jacob foretold, the Expectation of the nations, O Christ, hast shone forth from the tribe of Judah; and Thou hast come to overthrow the power of Damascus and the spoils of Samaria, changing deceit into faith worthy of God: Glory to Thy power, O Lord!
By the words of the diviner Balaam of old, Thou hast filled with joy the wise stargazers, his disciples; having shone forth as a star out of Jacob, O Master, and taking the firstfruits of the nations led in, Thou didst manifestly receive them: Glory to Thy power, O Lord!
As rain upon the fleece, O Christ, Thou didst come down into the Virgin’s womb, and as drops falling upon the earth; and Ethiopia, and Tarshish, and the Arabian isles, Sheba, and they that hold the whole land of the Medes fell down before Thee, O Savior: Glory to Thy power, O Lord!
*Another Canon. Ode 4. Heirmos:*[^173]
Singing of old the renewal of the human race, the Prophet Habakkuk foretold it, having been accounted worthy to behold the ineffable type: for as a young Babe from the mountain, the Virgin, came forth the Word, unto the renewal of the peoples.
Equal to men came forth the Most High, willingly taking flesh from the Virgin, to purge the poison of the serpent's head, bringing all from sunless gates unto the life-bearing light, being God.

=== LEAF p112 ===
The nations that of old were sunken in corruption, having wholly escaped the ruin of the enemy, lift up their hands with songs of praise, honoring Christ alone, as One Who has come in mercy.
Having sprung from the root of Jesse, O Virgin, thou hast passed beyond the bounds of human nature, having borne the Father's eternal Word, as He Himself was pleased to pass through a sealed womb by a strange emptying.
*Ode 5. Heirmos:*[^174]
Being the God of peace and Father of compassions, Thou hast sent unto us the Angel of Thy Great Counsel, Who grants peace; therefore, guided unto the light of divine knowledge, waking early out of the night, we glorify Thee, O Lover of mankind.
Submitting to be enrolled among servants by the decree of Caesar, Thou hast set us free who were the slaves of the enemy and of sin, O Christ; and becoming wholly poor according to our condition, from the very union and communion Thou hast made earthly man divine.
Behold, as it was said of old, the Virgin, having conceived in her womb, has given birth to God incarnate, and remains a Virgin; reconciled through her to God, let us sinners, the faithful, praise her as truly the Mother of God.
*Another Canon. Ode 5. Heirmos:*[^175]
Out of the night of the dark works of deceit, as cleansing unto us who now watchfully offer song unto Thee as Benefactor, come, O Christ, granting an easy path, ascending along which we shall find glory.

=== LEAF p113 ===
Cutting off again the cruel enmity against us by Thy coming in the flesh, O Master, that Thou mightest destroy the soul-destroying ruler, uniting the world with the immaterial essences, Thou hast made the Parent accessible to creation.
The people that of old were darkened have seen after days the light of the brightness from on high; and as an inheritance unto God the Son brings the Gentiles, granting ineffable grace there where sin abounded most.
*Ode 6. Heirmos:*[^176]
The sea beast spewed forth Jonah from its belly like a babe, just as it had received him; and the Word, having dwelt within the Virgin and taken flesh, came forth, preserving her incorrupt; for suffering no corruption Himself, He kept her who bore Him unharmed.
Incarnate came Christ our God, Whom the Father begat from the womb before the morning star; and holding the governance of the all-pure hosts, He lies in a manger of dumb beasts and is wrapped in swaddling bands, yet He looses the tangled bonds of transgressions.
As a young Child from Adam, a Son is born of our mingled nature and given unto the faithful; He is the Father and Ruler of the age to come, and is called the Angel of Great Counsel; He is the mighty God, holding dominion over all creation.
*Another Canon. Ode 6. Heirmos:*[^177]
Dwelling in the depths of the sea, Jonah prayed that Thou wouldest come and calm the tempest; and pierced by the dart of the tormentor, I cry aloud to Christ, the Destroyer of evils,

=== LEAF p114 ===
that Thou wouldest come swiftly unto my slothfulness.
God the Word, Who in the beginning was with God, now strengthens what was feeble of old, having purposed to preserve the nature that belongs to us, wherein by a second communion He straightway makes Himself manifest, free from passions.
For our sake He comes from Abraham's loins to raise up as sons those who had fallen darkly in the gloom of sins, bowed down to the earth; He Who dwells in light is now well pleased, through His condescension, to be laid in a manger for the salvation of mankind.
*Kontakion, Tone 3. Automelon (Самоподо́бенъ):*[^178]
Today the Virgin gives birth to the Transcendent One, and the earth offers a cave to the Inaccessible; angels with shepherds give glory, and Magi journey with a star: for unto us is born a young Child, God before the ages.
*Ikos:*[^179]
Bethlehem has opened Eden, come, let us behold; we have found delight in secret: come, let us receive the things of paradise within the cave; there an unwatered root has appeared, budding forth remission; there an undug well is found, whereof David of old longed to drink; there the Virgin, having given birth to an Infant, straightway quenched the thirst of Adam and David; therefore let us go unto Him where He is born: a young Child, God before the ages.
*Ode 7. Heirmos:*[^180]
The Children, brought up together in piety, despising the impious command, were not afraid of the threat of fire;

=== LEAF p115 ===
but standing in the midst of the flame, they sang: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!
The shepherds playing on their pipes received an awe-inspiring manifestation of light; for the glory of the Lord shone round about them, and the angel cried out: Sing, for Christ is born, the blessed God of our fathers!
Suddenly with the word of the angel, the heavenly hosts cried aloud: Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will among men! Christ has shone forth, the blessed God of our fathers!
What is this saying? said the shepherds: Having gone, let us see that which is come to pass, the divine Christ; and having reached Bethlehem, with her who bore Him they bowed down, singing: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!
*Another Canon. Ode 7. Heirmos:*[^181]
Caught by love for the King of all, the Children reproached the impious, wicked-tongued raving of the tyrant that raged without measure; to them the raging fire yielded as they said to the Master: Blessed art Thou unto the ages!
Fiercely it consumed the servants, but preserved the youths with reverence, the all-devouring furnace heightened by a sevenfold burning; whom the flame crowned, the Lord bounteously granting dew for their piety's sake.
O Christ, Helper of men, possessing an ineffable incarnation, Thou hast put to shame the adversary's boast; bearing the riches of deification, Thou hast now taken form, therefore, through hope from on high, we have come into the nether darkness.
The sin which was wickedly and unrestrainedly exalted, raging impiously through the perversion of the world, which drew us down of old, Thou hast almighty overthrown;

=== LEAF p116 ===
and today from the snares Thou savest us, incarnate of Thine own will, O Benefactor.
*Ode 8. Heirmos:*[^182]
The dew-bearing furnace portrayed the type of a supernatural marvel; for it burned not the youths whom it had received, just as the fire of the Godhead burned not the Virgin's womb into which it entered; therefore, singing, let us sing: Let all creation bless the Lord, and exalt Him above all unto all ages!
The daughter of Babylon draws unto herself the captive children of David from Zion; but she sends the Magi youth bearing gifts, entreating the God-receiving daughter of David; therefore, singing, let us sing: Let all creation bless the Lord, and exalt Him above all unto all ages!
Instruments turned aside mournful songs, for the sons of Zion sang not in a strange land; but Christ, having shone forth in Bethlehem, looses all the deceit of Babylon and musical harmonies; therefore, singing, let us sing: Let all creation bless the Lord, and exalt Him above all unto all ages!
Babylon received the spoils of the kingdom of Zion and its captive wealth; but Christ draws unto Zion the treasures thereof, and its kings, the stargazers, guiding them by a star; therefore, singing, let us sing: Let all creation bless the Lord, and exalt Him above all unto all ages!
*Another Canon. Ode 8. Heirmos:*[^183]
The youths burned in the Old Testament prefigure without burning the womb of the Maiden who supernaturally

=== LEAF p117 ===
gives birth sealed; grace, accomplishing both as a single miracle, rouses the people to song.
Having escaped the destruction of seeking deification through deceit, all creation unceasingly praises the Word Who emptied Himself, and in youthlike manner with trembling, in fear offers an inglorious praise, being corruptible, even though wisely enduring.
Thou comest turning the strayed unto the flower-bearing pasture from the desert hills, to quell the violent power of the manslayer over the uprising of the nations, human nature; having appeared as man and God by providence.
*At Ode 9, we do not sing "More honorable than the Cherubim", but we sing the Refrains of the Feast.*[^184]

*And the Heirmos:*[^185]
A strange and most glorious mystery do I behold: the cave is heaven; the Virgin, the Cherubic Throne; the manger, the receptacle in which Christ God, Whom nothing can contain, has reclined; Whom singing, we magnify.

*Psalm 148:*[^191]
Let everything that has breath praise the Lord. Praise the Lord from the heavens, praise Him in the highest; to Thee is due a hymn, O God.
Praise Him, all His angels, praise Him, all His powers; to Thee is due a hymn, O God.
Praise Him, sun and moon; praise Him, all you stars and light.
Praise Him, you heavens of heavens, and you waters that are above the heavens; let them praise the name of the Lord.
For He spoke, and they were made; He commanded, and they were created.
He established them unto the age, and unto the age of the age; He made a decree, and it shall not pass away.
Praise the Lord from the earth, you dragons and all deeps:
Fire, hail, snow, ice, stormy wind, fulfilling His word;
Mountains and all hills, fruitful trees and all cedars;
Beasts and all cattle, creeping things and winged birds;
Kings of the earth and all peoples, princes and all judges of the earth.
