[^372]: Discourse of Saint John Chrysostom: the preacher invokes the austere fasting of the Prophet Elijah on Mount Horeb (3 Kings / 1 Kings 19:8) and the ascetic detachment of the Prophet Daniel in Babylon (Daniel 1:8–16; 6:16–22), who spurned royal riches, precious stones, and morse ivory / walrus tusks (*рыбе зоубны*, a characteristic medieval East Slavic trade luxury) and was preserved unharmed in the lions' den; followed by an urgent rebuke against human pride, gluttony, and spiritual blindness.

[^373]: Discourse of Saint John Chrysostom: contrasting present temporal deprivation with future eternal reward (paraphrasing the Gospel Beatitudes and the parable of the Rich Man and Lazarus, Luke 16:25); exhorting the faithful to guard the tongue, eyes, and ears; lamenting the soul trapped within a pampered body; and appealing to Christ the Physician, Who raised Lazarus four days dead (John 11:39–44), urging repentance before the final accounting.

[^374]: Conclusion of the discourse of Saint John Chrysostom: citing the divine challenge from the Book of Job (Job 38:4); comparing fleeting youth to the morning dew; warning of the sudden coming of death like a thief in the night (Matthew 24:43; 1 Thessalonians 5:2); providing a model prayer to the Lord Jesus Christ and the Most Holy Theotokos; and concluding with the solemn Trinitarian doxology.
