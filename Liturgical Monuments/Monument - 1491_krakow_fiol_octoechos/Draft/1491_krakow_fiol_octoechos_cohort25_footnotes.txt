[^279]: Tuesday Liturgy Beatitudes concluding Theotokion, followed by the Prokeimenon in Tone 7 (LXX Ps. 63:11, 2), Epistle reading from Acts 13:25–33 (St. Paul in Antioch of Pisidia citing John the Baptist and the Resurrection of Christ), Alleluia in Tone 4 (LXX Ps. 91:13–14), and the opening of the Gospel according to John 1:29–34 for the Holy Forerunner on Tuesday. Note Fiol's printed text omits by haplography the clause "therefore came I baptizing with water" (John 1:31) before John's testimony.

[^280]: Tuesday Liturgy Gospel conclusion (John 1:32–34), followed by the Tuesday Dismissal Troparion to the Holy Forerunner in Tone 2 (*Память праведника съ похвалами...*), Theotokion citing 2 Pet. 1:4 (*Божественнаго быхомъ причастницы естества...*), and Kinonikon (LXX Ps. 111:6). Concludes with the opening of Wednesday Vespers Stichera of the Cross in Tone 3 on *Lord, I have cried*, with automelon *Прѣтекше языцы, клвенному дрѣву поклонимся...* contrasting Adam's deception by the tree of knowledge with the devil's defeat upon the Tree of the Cross.

[^281]: Wednesday Vespers Stichera of the Cross in Tone 3 continued: the second and third stichera commemorating the vision of the Cross to Emperor Constantine ("in this sign conquer") and Old Testament types of Moses and Elijah foreshadowing the Cross and the Second Coming of Christ, followed by the Stichera of the Theotokos to the melodic model (*Podoben*) *Great Is the Power of Thy Cross* (*Велия креста Твоего*).

[^282]: Wednesday Vespers Stichera of the Theotokos concluded, followed by the Staurotheotokion in Tone 3 depicting the sorrowful lament of the Virgin at the foot of the Cross (*Къ древу крестному Тя, Иисусе, Спасе нашъ...*), the Wednesday Vespers Prokeimenon in Tone 4 (LXX Ps. 22:6, 1), and the Aposticha Stichera of the Cross in Tone 3 (*Кресту Твоему покланяемся, Христе...* and the typological victory of Moses over Amalek, Ex. 17:11–12, and the sign given to flee from the bow, LXX Ps. 59:6).

[^283]: Wednesday Matins: Dismissal Troparion of the Holy Cross in Tone 1 (*Спаси, Господи, люди Твоя...*) with its pre-Nikonian Ruthenian princely reading (*побѣды княземъ нашимъ на варвары даруй*), followed by Sessional Hymns at the 1st and 2nd Stichologia in Tone 3 meditating on the cosmic Tree of the Cross bridging earth to heaven and Christ's voluntary suffering in the flesh.

[^284]: Wednesday Matins Canons in Tone 3: the Sessional Hymns concluded, followed by Ode 1 Heirmos (*Сушу глубокую родительную землю...* / Greek: *Χέρσον ἀβυσσοτόκον πέδον ἥλιος...* / Ex. 14:21–22) and troparia of the Cross meditating on the passage through the Red Sea as a type of the Cross, and the Creator hanging the earth upon the waters while Himself hanging upon the Tree of Golgotha.

[^285]: Wednesday Matins Canons in Tone 3: concluding Ode 1 of the Canon of the Cross with the Staurotheotokion, followed by the Canon of the Mother of God, Katavasia (*Предѣлаему морю жезломъ древле...* / Israel crossing the parted sea as a type of the Cross), and the opening of Ode 3 Heirmos (*Утвержение на Тя надѣющимся...* / Greek: *Τὸ στερέωμα τῶν ἐπὶ σοὶ πεποιθότων...* / 1 Sam. 2:2).

[^286]: Wednesday Matins Canons in Tone 3, concluding Ode 3 with troparia of the Cross (citing Christ bearing the curse to pour forth renewal, Gal. 3:13), the Theotokos, the Staurotheotokion (*How hath the Sun of glory set from mine eyes?*), the Katavasia of the dispensation foreseen by Habakkuk, and the opening of Ode 4 Heirmos (*Покрыла есть небеса добродѣтель Твоя, Христе...* / Hab. 3:3).

[^287]: Wednesday Matins Canons in Tone 3: Ode 4 concluded with troparia of the Cross citing Isaiah 53:7 (*He is brought as a sheep to the slaughter...*), followed by the Canon of the Mother of God invoking her protection with the Ruthenian petition for Orthodox princes (*побѣды даруй православнымъ княземъ нашимъ*), the scroll of Isaiah 8:1 and the tearing up of the handwriting of Adam's debt (Col. 2:14), and the Katavasia of Ode 5.

[^288]: Wednesday Matins Canons in Tone 3: Ode 5 of the Cross, Heirmos (*Яко видѣ Исаиа образно на престолѣ превысоцѣ Бога...* / Isa. 6:1–5) and troparia comparing Christ's flesh on the Cross to a lamp lit to find the lost drachma (Luke 15:8–9) and mourning the piercing of the side of Him Who fashioned Eve (Gen. 2:21–22; John 19:34); followed by Ode 5 troparia of the Mother of God, invoking the parable of the Good Samaritan (fallen among robbers, Luke 10:30) and seeking her surety for the amendment of life.
