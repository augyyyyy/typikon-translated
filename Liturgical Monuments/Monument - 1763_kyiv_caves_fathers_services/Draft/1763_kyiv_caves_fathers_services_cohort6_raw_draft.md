A temple of the All-Holy Spirit wast thou, which first having pre-cleansed with the water of humility, and anointed with the myrrh of grace and spiritual purity, thou didst enlighten with all virtues; and the Father with the Son having come, made an abode in thee: therefore we pray thee, O most sacred father: pray thou unto Christ God that we also may receive grace through thee, and great mercy.

As a teacher of piety, and a faithful laborer in the vineyard of Christ, and a most honorable hierarch, assembling today on thine annual memory, ye people, let us bless him with odes and hymns, saying: O venerable father Ephraim, forget not us that honor with love thy falling asleep, but pray thou unto Christ God to grant us great mercy.

*Glory: Tone 2:*
The God-bearing hierarch of Christ, our father Ephraim, come today, ye assemblies of feast-lovers, let us sing with hymnographic praises, saying: Rejoice, pastor and most excellent guide of Christ's rational sheep; rejoice, honorable adornment of hierarchs; rejoice, all-radiant luminary and guardian of the church; rejoice, fervent intercessor for all that faithfully flee unto thee: deliver us from afflictions by thy prayers, who now celebrate thy memory with love and fall down with joyful soul before the shrine of thy relics, and pray thou unto Christ God to give peace unto the world and save our souls.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Save from perils thy servants...

# MONTH OF FEBRUARY

*On the 12th Day: Service to our Venerable Father Prokhor the Wonderworker of the Caves.*[^127]

*At Vespers, on "Lord, I have cried," Stichera, Tone 4. Podoben: "Thou hast given a sign" (Да́лъ є҆сѝ ꙁна́менїе):*[^128]
O marvelous Prokhor, passing through the angelic life, thou didst flee all worldly pleasures, being nourished rather by the word of God than by meats. Therefore thou wast truly an edifice with them, with whom Jesus the Lover of mankind and Savior of our souls granted thee an habitation in the heavenly mansions.

**[Right Page — Fol. 54r]**
*To Prokhor the Wonderworker of the Caves*

O glorious Prokhor, fleeing human glory, like a soaring bird thou didst hasten unto the hill of the Caves, and didst fly across, being enriched with vision and the doing of the law of the Lord, whence having ascended unto the summit of virtues, thou didst receive the gift of wonderworking from Jesus the Lover of mankind and Savior of our souls.

O most blessed Prokhor, following the footsteps of the blessed fathers, thou didst acquire a narrow way of life in this world, so that thou didst not even taste of ordinary bread, but wast satisfied with wild-growing herbs; therefore now dwelling in the blessed life, thou art nourished with the food of heavenly joy, which Jesus the Lover of mankind and Savior of our souls gave thee from His goodness.

*Glory: Tone 6:*
Who will not marvel at thy life, or who will not recount thy patience, O most blessed father Prokhor? For passing through life in great abstinence, thou didst neglect the body, and didst crush the pleasure-loving serpent unto the end, having established thy feet upon the steps of fasting virtues. Therefore on this present day, honoring thy memory, we confess thy labors, and we pray, falling down before the shrine of thy relics: as thou hast boldness toward Christ God, ask peace for the world, and great mercy for our souls.

*Both now: Theotokion, or Stavrotheotokion.*
*At the Aposticha, all from the Octoechos.*
*Glory: To the Venerable, Tone 6:*
The multitudes of monastics in thy memory, O venerable one, having formed a choir of praises, we honor thy labors and ascetic feats, and marvel how, thyself leading a narrow and poor life, thou didst preserve others living in abundance in the time of necessity: for them that were fainting with hunger thou didst feed with bread made from weeds, and unto them that needed salt thou didst give from ashes: and now strengthen us that faint with spiritual hunger by thy prayers, and ask great mercy from Christ God.
*Both now: Theotokion, or Stavrotheotokion.*

*Troparion, Tone 1:*
O venerable and wonderworking father Prokhor, who in famine didst feed the hungry with bread from weeds, and with salt from ashes: feed us also with spiritual food, praying unto Christ God for our souls.

*Canon, Tone 8. Ode 1. Heirmos:*[^129]
He who threw down the chariot-driving Pharaoh, the wonderworking staff of Moses of old, striking crosswise and dividing the sea, saved Israel a fugitive, walking on foot, chanting a song unto God.

*Service to the Venerable Father*

...Gird us, O Master Christ, with the spiritual weapon against invisible enemies, and teach us how to contend and overcome their power, that we may offer unto Thee our God a festive song in the memory of Thy servant, sending it forth with love.

Having their loins girded with vigilance, the ranks of monastics remember thee, O father, and celebrate thine annual memory with praises, sending forth a song unto God.

Remembering eternal blessedness, O blessed one, thou didst leave the corruptible things of this world, and that thou mightest be a co-dweller with angels, thou didst clothe thyself in the angelic habit; therefore we honor thy fasting life, sending forth a song unto God.

*Glory:*
Taking upon thyself the good yoke of abstinence, thou didst acquire the hard life of fasting deeds, O God-wise one, wherein having wrestled with the invisible enemy, thou didst conquer him: therefore we glorify thee, sending forth a song unto God.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Unto us that walk in the broad way and flow by pleasure into the abyss of hades, guide us, O Mother of God, unto the narrow way leading unto the blessed life: that with thy chosen servants on this present day we may fall down before thee, sending forth supplications.

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*[^130]
Thou Who in the beginning didst establish the heavens with understanding, and founded the earth upon the waters, establish me, O Christ, upon the rock of Thy commandments: for there is none holy beside Thee, O only Lover of mankind.

*Troparia:*
Having established thy feet upon the rock of Christ's commandments, O father, thou didst remain unshaken by the snares of demons, ever offering praise unto God: There is none holy beside Thee, O Lover of mankind.

The kindling of carnal passions thou didst extinguish by the dew of abstinence: for not even eating bread, but wild orache, thou didst continue, having the word of God rather as food, and this strengthened thee, O venerable one.

Famine thou didst account as nothing, O blessed one, hoping upon Him that feeds the birds; and He gave thee to eat bread not from sown grain, which was sweeter unto thee than honey and the honeycomb.

*Glory:*
We marvel at thy marvelous possession, O father, that even for orache bread thou didst condemn thyself, making thyself unworthy even of such food, wherewith lightly thou didst traverse the sorrowful fasting way.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Our enemies who exalt themselves with pride bend down unto humility, O pure Virgin: for thou alone didst say: Behold the handmaid of the Lord, and thou reignest unto the ages over all generations that bless thee.

*To Prokhor the Wonderworker of the Caves*

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 1:*
Emulating the great fathers, extreme fasting thou didst earnestly desire: for being nourished with weed alone and water, thou didst not faint from fasting, but didst remain stronger than adamant; and now receiving the fruits of thine abstinence, recompense from God, pray thou ever for us, that through thee we also may receive a reward, as we bless thy memory with love, O one worthy of wonder.
*Glory, both now: Theotokion:*
With choirs of them that bless thee, the human race, and having surpassed every creature with glory, in hymns we honor thy seedless conception and thine ineffable childbirth, O Mary, pure Virgin Mother.

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*[^131]
Thou art my strength, O Lord, Thou art my power, Thou art my God, Thou art my rejoicing, Who didst not leave the bosom of the Father, and didst visit our poverty: therefore with the Prophet Habakkuk I cry unto Thee: Glory to Thy power, O Lover of mankind!

*Troparia:*
Having from God the gift of wonderworking, O father, thou didst feed many with orache bread in the time of famine, and making salt from ashes, thou didst give it freely unto them that came: and glory was unto the Lover of mankind Who became a defender there.

By thy prayer, O venerable one, thou didst give sweetness unto the orache bread: for it was sweet for food, and radiant and pure like wheat bread unto them that received it with a blessing. From the blessing of thine herbs grant unto us also, O most sacred father, as we have thee as an intercessor toward Christ the Lover of mankind.

Thy weed-bread, O most blessed father, was sweet and radiant unto them that received it with a blessing; but unto them that stole it secretly, it appeared black like soot and bitter. With thy blessing bless us, and grant of thy sweetness, as thou sittest at the heavenly table, O glorious Prokhor.

*Glory:*
Not sated, but leading a choir of good praises, fainting with spiritual hunger, we come unto thee, O father, on this present day of thy memory, and we pray: grant us of thy grace, that in thee we may glorify Christ the Lover of mankind.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Who that flees unto thee departs from thee confounded, O pure Virgin! Or who that asks receives not of thy grace, O Lady! Which we also believing and confessing, offer supplication unto thee, O pure one: grant us thy bounties, as the good Mother of the Good Son.

*Ode 5. Heirmos:*[^132]
Why hast Thou cast me off from Thy countenance, O unwaning Light, and a strange darkness has covered me, the wretched one? But convert me, and direct my ways unto the light of Thy commandments, I pray.

*Troparia:*
O glorious Prokhor, delay not to come, and vouchsafe us thy blessing, even as of old in time of need thou didst give it unhindered; and direct the ways of them that praise thee with love unto the commandments of the Lord, as our fervent intercessor.

He who troubles the earth and shakes the people brought war upon us, the adversary enemy, and brought spiritual famine upon all our entrance; but do thou, O most blessed one, appear quickly, and by thy prayer feed us with noetic food, and direct our ways, as our intercessor.

Seeing thy compassions, O father, many came unto thee in the time of famine, unto whom the bread made from herbs and the salt made from ashes by thy hands thou didst give without fail; look down therefore now upon us, and by thy prayer grant every abundance unto our monastery, we pray thee, O venerable father.

*Glory:*
Many that hate us, enemies have risen up against us, striving to deprive us of spiritual and bodily food; but come thou unto us and rebuke them, and by thy prayer grant unto our monastery all that is unto profit, we pray thee, O venerable Prokhor.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
The adversary enemy, envying thy flock, continually stirs up war against us; but do thou, O all-pure one, as the Mother of the mighty King, turn him back, and direct our ways unto the commandments of the Lord, as our intercessor.

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*[^133]
Cleanse me, O Savior, for many are mine iniquities, and lead me up from the depths of evils, I pray: for unto Thee have I cried, and give ear unto me, O God of my salvation.

*Troparia:*
Cleanse the defilement of my soul by thy prayer, O glorious Prokhor, and heal the fiery darts of my heart with the plaster of thy grace: for unto thee on this present day do I cry aloud, as unto an intercessor for my salvation.

Overcome by the thought of extortion, the prince commanded all the salt made from ashes by thy prayer to be plundered, which being turned into ashes again, was found bitter in their mouths; from such malice and passion deliver me, O father, as a helper of my salvation.

Shame covered the faces of them that slandered the food of the hungry: for seeing the transformation of thy salt into ashes, they cast it out from the house; but thou didst command the cast-out ashes to be gathered up, and it became pure salt again by thy prayer, even as before. Grant unto me also now thy prayer, O prayerful helper of my salvation.

*Glory:*
Thinking to hide thy wonder, they commanded the salt that had turned into ashes to be poured out by night outside; but the night witnessed all the more unto thy wonders: for the ashes having again been changed into salt, by thy command the needy gathered it, blessing thee, O venerable one, as their defender.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Night holds me fast in slothfulness, an intercessor of sleepiness, not drawing near unto the light of spiritual vigilance; O Mother of God, enlighten my mind, guiding me unto doxology, that I may ever sing unto thee: Rejoice, candle kindled with the divine flame!

*Kontakion, Tone 2:*
Having mortified thy flesh with extreme abstinence, O father, being deprived of human food, herbs and grass were in place of bread, and with ashes like salt thou wast nourished, O wonderworking Prokhor; which seeing, the Lord changed the herbs into bread, and the ashes into salt, wherewith thou didst feed the multitude of the hungry: therefore we honor and bless thee.

*To Prokhor the Wonderworker of the Caves*

*Ode 7. Heirmos:*[^134]
The fire was ashamed before the condescension of God in Babylon of old; therefore the children, dancing in the furnace with joyful step, as in a meadow, sang: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!

*Troparia:*
Remembering the condescension of God and the dread judgment, the prince was afraid, seeing how he had surrounded thee, O venerable Prokhor, not knowing the grace that was wrought in thee; but coming to himself, he cried aloud: Blessed is the God of our fathers!

Recognizing thee, O father, as a servant of God, the ruler afterward blessed thy benefactions, and having received warmer love toward the most marvelous monastery, he repented of his extortion, and asking forgiveness for the evil done, sang: Blessed is the God of our fathers!

As a true minister and servant of the innocent Lamb, the Son of God, having shown thy guiltlessness, thou gavest forgiveness unto him that wronged thee, O blessed one, and confirmed a covenant with him, that the one should lay the other in the grave, when he should depart from the body, as the God of our fathers should command one of you.

*Glory:*
Having lived in holiness and directed thy life, thou didst attain unto a blessed end, O father; therefore rejoicing unto the angelic choirs, crying aloud, thou didst sing: Blessed is the God of our fathers!
