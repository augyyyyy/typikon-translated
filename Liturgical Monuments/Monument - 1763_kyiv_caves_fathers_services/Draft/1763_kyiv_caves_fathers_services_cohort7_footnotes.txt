A wealthy Kievan who gave away his wealth and took monastic tonsure at the Caves, Eustratius was taken captive during the Polovtsian raid on Kyiv in 1096 and sold into slavery with fifty other Christians to a Jewish merchant in Chersonesus (Korsun). When the master attempted to force the captives to renounce Christ, Eustratius exhorted them to endure starvation rather than apostatize; all fifty captives died of hunger over fourteen days, while Eustratius survived through prayer. Enraged by the loss of his captives and money, the master crucified St. Eustratius on Pascha, mocking him as his ancestors mocked Christ. Pierced through the ribs with a spear from which his soul ascended to heaven in a fiery chariot, his holy relics were cast into the Black Sea, miraculously washed ashore, and translated to the Near Caves of the Lavra.
