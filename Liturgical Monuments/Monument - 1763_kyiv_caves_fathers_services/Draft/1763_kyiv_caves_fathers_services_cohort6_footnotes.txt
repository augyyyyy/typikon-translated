[^109]: Woodcut engraving of Saint Ephraim, Bishop of Pereyaslavl (*Ѡ С͠тъмъ Е҆фремѣ Е҆пⷭ҇копѣ Переꙗславскомъ*). Disciple of Saint Anthony of the Caves and former boyar and treasurer to Grand Prince Izyaslav Yaroslavich of Kyiv. After being tonsured by St. Anthony, he undertook a pilgrimage to Constantinople and the holy places, whence he brought back copies of the Studite Rule (*Typikon*) that governed monastic life at the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra. Consecrated Bishop of Pereyaslavl in 1089, he adorned the city with monumental stone churches (including the Cathedral of St. Michael) and built the first recorded public infirmaries and bathhouses in Kievan Rus', where the poor and sick were treated without fee.

[^126]: Woodcut engraving of Saint Prokhor the Weed-Eater of the Caves (*Прпⷣ: Про́хоръ Ло́бодникъ*), Discourse 31 of the Kyiv-Pechersk Paterikon. A native of Smolensk, Prokhor received tonsure at the Kyiv-Pechersk Monastery and adopted an extreme rule of ascetic fasting, gathering wild orache (chenopodium) during the summer, grinding it into flour, and baking bread without ever tasting normal wheat or rye bread. During the severe famine in Rus' under Grand Prince Svyatopolk Izyaslavich (1093–1113), St. Prokhor baked orache bread for all the starving citizens, which by his blessing tasted sweeter than honey. When the merchant guild created an artificial salt monopoly and salt became unavailable, he gathered ashes from the cells and distributed them to the poor, where they miraculously turned into pure white salt. Prince Svyatopolk, deeply moved by these miracles, repented of his extortions and vowed never to harm the monastery; upon Prokhor's repose in 1107, the prince himself carried the saint's body to the caves for burial.
