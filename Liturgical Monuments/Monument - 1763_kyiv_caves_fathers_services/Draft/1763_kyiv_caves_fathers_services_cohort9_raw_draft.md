*To John the Much-Suffering*

Unceasingly remembering the voice of perpetual rejoicing and the sound of them that keep festival, O father John, thou didst leave worldly pleasures that call unto themselves; and taking upon thee Christ's yoke, thou didst bear that light burden in hope of speedy transformation, singing unto God: For He is glorified!

*Glory:*
Having departed far from the passionate world, O blessed one, thou didst run like a hart unto the Caves hill, where being watered by the arrows of the spiritual protection of the blessed fathers, thou didst sing unto God: For He is glorified!

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Being overwhelmed with the ship of soul in the sea of passions, I cry unto thee, O pure one: Help me by thy mercy, and deliver me from sinking; for thou art the ship for them that desire to be saved, and the calm haven of worldly voyagings.

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*[^265]
Thou art the confirmation of them that run unto Thee, O Lord; Thou art the light of the darkened, and my spirit sings of Thee.

*Troparia:*
Having escaped the worldly abysses, thou didst run unto the calm haven of monastics, O father, living with whom thou didst love the one God, crying unto Him: O my Christ, confirm me in Thy love!

Desiring to follow the pure and blameless Lamb in purity, O blessed one, the foul enemy seeing thee attacked thee with fleshly burnings, against whom thou didst stand, subduing thy flesh by fasting and thirst, and in prayer singing: O my Christ, confirm me!

Remembering the fall of him that brings light unto fornicators, O venerable one, thou didst dwell in a dark cave, and didst keep silence beside the tomb of the great Anthony, ever remembering thine own departure; and thus thou didst find peace in thy bones.[^266]

*Glory:*
Death is not death, but a translation unto a better life for the saints of God: for though they are laid in tombs, yet they ever live unto God and utter a voice, even as unto thee, O father John, a voice came from the tomb of the great Anthony, calling thee unto silence.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
I remember the hour of judgment, yet I have no change from passions unto a dispassionate life, O pure Mother of God: for the sake of the venerable virgin John, wash me from the love of passions, and confirm me in the love of thy Son.

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 8:*[^267]
Emulating the beatitude of them that have not defiled themselves with women, that thou mightest follow the Lamb upon Mount Sion, thou didst resolve to keep thy virginity undefiled, and didst preserve it whole, unharmed by many demonic temptations; therefore thou singest a new song, O venerable John, and thy spirit rejoices with the angels.

*Glory, both now: Theotokion:*
Not having emulated the beatitude of virgins, wretch that I am, having defiled the robe of my baptism with fornicating corruptions, I have departed far from Thy grace, O immaculate Lamb: O Son of God, for the sake of the sufferings of Thy saints and of the all-pure Mother of God, grant me correction and have mercy on me.

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*[^268]
Thou hast set toward us a steadfast love, O Lord: for Thou didst give Thine Only-begotten Son unto death for our sake; therefore giving thanks we cry unto Thee: Glory to Thy power, O Lord!

*Troparia:*
Thou didst establish thy life in a dark cave without departing, O father, until the passionate gloom was changed into light through silence and unseenness of sensible light.

Thou didst put on many chains, O father, contending against passions as with fierce beasts, firmly bridling their harsh attacks in thy bodily frame by fasting and vigilance.

Unable to cool thy flesh burned with the fire of Ethiopian assaults, O blessed one, thou didst choose to live naked in a narrow place, struggling there through freezing cold unto spiritual warmth.[^269]

*Glory:*
Desiring to subdue thy fierce and untamed body as a wild beast, thou didst bind it with heavy irons, O venerable one, that it might not lust against the spirit, but rather be subject unto the spirit.[^270]

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Unable to quiet the passions of much intemperance that rise up against me, unto thee do I flee, O Lady: do thou, O all-pure one, subdue their impurity, and save me from their assault.

*Ode 5. Heirmos:*[^271]
Thou didst appear invisible upon the earth, and didst willingly dwell with men, O Incomprehensible One; and rising early unto Thee, we sing of Thee, O Lover of mankind.

*Troparia:*
Who among all that live upon earth will not marvel at thy patient endurance, O father? For having dug a pit for thyself, and entering naked, thou didst bury thyself up to thy shoulders, subduing with earth the fleshly surge moved by the enemy adversary.[^272]

Thy whole body oppressed with earth was growing faint, yet the enemy was vigilant...

*To John the Much-Suffering*

...yet the enemy was vigilant, rising up fiercely in warfare and assailing thee with terrors, wishing thus to turn thee away, lest having escaped he should fall before thee, O venerable one.

The devil seeing thy mind soaring, O John, burning toward God, with crafty flame burned thy members and entrails from below; yet thou, neglecting this pain, didst choose to be burned up for the Lord's sake, rather than be mocked by the demon.

*Glory:*
Satan prepared to strike thee with the venom of asps in weakness, O blessed one; yet he transformed himself into a fire-breathing serpent, that at least by terrifying thee he might overcome thee, an unconquerable warrior of Christ.[^273]

*Both now: Theotokion:*
The ashen venom of passionate assaults, wherewith the enemy wounds my heart: heal with the medicine of thy mercy, O all-immaculate Virgin, that being saved from them, I may desire to bring forth fruit of repentance.

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*[^274]
The lowest abyss of sins has surrounded me, and my spirit fails; but stretch forth Thy high arm, O Master, and save me as Thou didst Peter, O Guide!

*Troparia:*
The lowest abyss opened its maw: for the devil transforming himself into an unclean serpent, armed himself breathing with fiery gaping, wishing to swallow thee; but thou, O father, wast like an immovable pillar, singing: O my God, my Guide, save me!

Standing upon the rock of firm faith, O venerable one, thou didst cover thyself with the shield of hope, and accounting the crafty enemy as naught by faith, thou didst direct thyself against his head; and being in his maw, thou wast not terrified, crying unto Christ: O my Guide, save me!

The noetic beast thrust thy head into his maw, O blessed one, and thy hair was burned; yet thy heart was not burned with faint-heartedness, O father, therefore thou didst cry aloud; and the Lord heard thee, as a good Helmsman and Savior Who accepts all without regard to persons.[^275]

*Glory:*
Thy prayer, O John, was as lightning arrows against the power of the adversary: for thou didst say: My God, why hast Thou forsaken me? And straightway the fierce serpent vanished, and no longer approached thee, being driven away by the Divine Light.

*Both now: Theotokion:*

*Kontakion, Tone 8:*[^276]
Being an unconquerable pillar of chastity in battles of contention, as a vigilant pillar of much suffering thou hast left us thy body, O all-honorable John: bowing down before which in sicknesses of soul and body, we pray thee, O venerable one: Pray unceasingly unto Christ God for us all.

*Ikos:*[^277]
Having loved the divine commandments of Christ, O venerable one, and hated the pleasures of this world, thou didst come eagerly unto Him, and wast a radiant beacon, enlightening the ends of the earth with spiritual radiance; therefore falling down I pray thee: Enlighten my spiritual eyes to sing of thy ascetic struggles, thy fasting, vigilance, tears, sicknesses, and the exhaustion of thy body, for the sake of the blessed future life, which thou now enjoyest: pray unceasingly for us all.

*Ode 7. Heirmos:*[^278]
Even as of old Thou didst bedew the three pious youths in the Chaldean flame with the bright fire of the Godhead: illuminate us also who cry: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!

*Troparia:*
Even as dust before the wind and as smoke from a thick cloud perishes, so also when the Divine Light arrived the evil one vanished; and thou, O father, didst bow down before the Lord, singing: Blessed art Thou, O God!

The Lord was near when thou didst call Him unto help, O father; whose grace having perceived, with joyful soul from the depth of thy heart thou didst cry aloud: Blessed art Thou, O God!

*Glory:*
Even as a wise master lays a great task upon a strong servant, so also the Lord permitted thee to endure the day and night heat of fleshly burnings; and thou didst bear it with patient endurance, singing: Blessed art Thou, O God!

*Both now: Theotokion:*
As a most wise Master, Thy Son, O Virgin, permits me who am slothful to be constrained by light passions; yet before they even arrived, I grew faint; therefore unto thee do I cry: Help me, O Mother of the Word: for thou art blessed.

*Ode 8. Heirmos:*[^279]
United with the unbearable fire, the youths standing for piety, unharmed by the flame, sang a divine song: Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord, and supremely exalt Him unto all ages!

*Troparia:*
In the unbearable fire of fleshly burnings, a voice from on high commanded thee, O blessed one, to pray unto a dead man who was able to help them that are in miserable passions and others, that they may sing of the Lord unto ages.[^280]

He that did the same as Joseph of old, when being held captive he obeyed not the defilement of the woman: he also helped thee, O father, by intercession unto God, whereby having heard him of corrupt passions, thou now singest of the Lord unto ages.[^281]

Seeing thy magnanimity, O father, ten thousand times brighter than the sun, the eye of God sent down the light of grace, and the gloom of ruinous fornication was utterly driven away from thy bones: and having lived the rest of thy life in stillness, thou now singest of the Lord unto ages.

*We bless the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit: the Lord.*
The Master Who grants noetic gladness unto the dispassionate granted it unto thee also, O venerable John, after thy many labors; which having received from the Divine Light, thou didst rejoice, and now rejoicest in heaven with them that sing of the Lord unto ages.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
O Virgin who without passions didst conceive, and without fleshly lust didst bear the Word of God: destroy the assaults that begin in the darkness of the evil one, that having escaped foulness, I may be vouchsafed to sing of the Lord unto ages.

*Ode 9. Heirmos:*[^282]
A new wonder and divinely-befitting: for through the shut door of the virgin the Lord plainly passes, naked in His entrance, and God was shown forth flesh-bearing in His departure, and the door remains shut: her ineffably do we magnify as the Mother of God.

*Troparia:*
A new life didst thou begin, when after much suffering the tempest of passions grew calm within thee, O father, and thou didst live the rest of thy time in stillness, magnifying the power of God.

Standing upon the firm rock of patient endurance, thou wast as a strong pillar against the face of the enemy, whose arrows striking against thee were shattered; therefore thee, O John, do we magnify.

Having lived pure in soul and body upon earth, thou didst depart unto the heavenly ones, with the pure in heart to behold the Face of God; enjoying the vision of Whom, pray for us, O venerable one, that with thee we may magnify the Trinity.

*Glory:*
Blessed is thy life, O John, blessed is thy dormition, but most blessed is thy life in the unwaning light, for the sake of which thou didst endure many afflictions; now rejoicing, pray for us that honor thy memory, O all-wise one.
