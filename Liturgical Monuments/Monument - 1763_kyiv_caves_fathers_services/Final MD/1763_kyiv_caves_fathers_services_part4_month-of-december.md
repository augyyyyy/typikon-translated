# MONTH OF DECEMBER

*On the 2nd Day: Service to our Venerable Father Athanasius the Recluse of the Caves.*[^63]

*At Vespers, on "Lord, I have cried," Stichera on 6, Tone 4. Podoben: "As one valiant" (Ꙗ҆́кѡ до́блѧ):*[^64]
Desiring to mortify thy flesh, thou didst enter into narrow seclusion, O father, and didst bury thy body by unslackening ascetic labors; but before the general resurrection, showing forth a certain resurrection of the dead, after two days thou didst rise again; and now living in immortality *Service to the Venerable Father*

...living in immortality, pray thou that we also may escape eternal death, and obtain great mercy from Christ God.

He that believes in Me, as the Scripture hath said, though he die, yet shall he live unto ages; in thee the word was fulfilled in deed, O venerable Athanasius: for thou didst die unto the world and the flesh, but knowing thee hereafter by good deeds, He raised thee up unto a better life, and thou wast an awe-inspiring wonder unto all, and rather unto profitable correction: which grant also unto us by thy prayers, as we prayerfully celebrate thy memory.

He that showed the paths of eternal life, did not suffer His venerable one to see corruption, raising him up after two days, like four-days-dead Lazarus, having loosed the bonds of death by the call of the divine voice: by this raise us up also unto God, and raise us up from dead works, O intercessor, Giver of life, and Savior of our souls.

At Thy resurrection, O Christ Savior, the things of death were shaken, and the faithful people are not held in dominion by it, as Athanasius, marvelous among the venerable, showed unto us, having learned by his own resurrection, and providing profitable correction unto monastics by three virtues: confirm this also in our hearts, O Savior, by his prayers, as we honor the yearly memory of Thine ever-memorable servant.

*Both now: Theotokion, or Stavrotheotokion. At the Aposticha, in the Oktoechos.*

*Glory, Tone 3:*
For thou didst drown the death that relaxes the limbs, and wast likened unto the Life of all that spent three days in the tomb, rising from the dead after two days, thou didst choose the narrowest path unto eternal life: having attained which in the immortal land, look down upon us from thence without delay, that thou mayest drive away the passions that mortify us, and make us partakers of thine immortality, on this day as we celebrate thy memory, O father and most blessed Athanasius.

*Both now: Theotokion, or Stavrotheotokion. Troparion, Tone 3:*
Desiring to obtain the beatitude of them that weep, day and night thou didst weep unceasingly, having in mind the hour of judgment; therefore after thy falling asleep thou didst find consolation in heaven, O venerable Athanasius. Grant also unto us to weep here for our sins, so that having escaped endless weeping, we may receive eternal consolation there.

*Canon*

*Tone 4. Ode 1. Heirmos:*[^65]
Let us all sing a song of victory unto God, Who wrought marvelous wonders with a high arm, and saved Israel, for He hath been glorified.

*To Athanasius the Recluse of the Caves*

*Troparia:*
Unto them that now desire to sing thy fitting falling asleep with blessed mouths, O father, not a song, but compunction do thou grant, as they pray and cry out now in the day of thy solemnity, wherein thy memory is glorified.

Understanding that human life in this world is cut down by the axe of death, O venerable one, that thou mightest crush the passions by thyself, thou didst run unto the firm rock; and by most marvelous virtues escaping the assault of death, thou singest unto God, for He hath been glorified.

*Glory:*
Having first died unto the world by the renunciation of its many-passioned snares, thou didst direct thy goings from thence, O father, unto the unapproachable life; therefore being now a son of life, look down upon us that sing unto God in thy memory: for He hath been glorified.

*Both now:*
My soul, mortified by voluntary passions, hasten to quicken, O pure Virgin; for thou art the Mother of life, and givest resurrection unto the dead.

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*[^66]
The stone which the builders rejected, the same is become the head of the corner: this is the stone whereon Christ established the Church, which He redeemed from the nations.

*Troparia:*
Bound by the faith of a God-pleasing life, O most blessed one, death was taken captive, having no dominion over thee; for though it snatched thee away, yet He commanded to return thee again for the profit of many, Who by His own death delivered all from death.

Lo, thou didst lie dead upon the bed, and the providence of the Most High was diligent to work wonders through thee; for after two days He returned thy spirit, and thou wast found alive, unto the wonder of all that beheld, who had seen thy falling asleep.

*Glory:*
Thy sickness, O venerable one, was not unto death, but unto the glory of God; for having died, thou didst receive death as sleep in the departure of thy sickness, and after two days, as if awaking from sleep, thou didst wash thy face with streams of tears.

*Both now:*
Having cut off the sickness of death by thy holy childbirth, and given life unto all the world, I alone have remained mortified by defiled deeds: O Mother of God, lay thy hand upon me, and I shall turn unto a better life.

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 8:*
Looking upon immortality, that thou mightest escape eternal death, thou didst choose a narrow life, O most blessed Athanasius, in thy mortified body; therefore marveling at the daily providence over thee, we glorify the only Lover of mankind, Who gave life and incorruption unto all.

*Service to the Venerable Father*

*Glory, both now. Theotokion:*
Them that were subject unto deathly bondage hast thou delivered by thy childbirth, O Virgin Bride of God; therefore the race of Christians glorifies thee with songs, for thou hast given unto them life and incorruption.

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*[^67]
The sun was taken up, and the moon stood in its order; Thou wast lifted up upon the Tree, O longsuffering One, and didst establish Thy Church upon it.

*Troparia:*
Lying breathless, thou didst rise up, and having received breath, thou camest again into being, O father; which they that stood by beholding, glorified the divine power with fear.

Seeing how fearful is the hour of death, O blessed one, thou didst bitterly weep, and didst deliver thyself by silence and sighing; and thy tears became for many an inscription of the fearful judgment.

*Glory:*
Ever mindful of thine exodus, O father, thou didst weep in this sorrowful life; and past mournings were added unto thee, having sensibly beheld what manner of fear and trembling they have that depart: which, that we also may escape, pray thou, O venerable father.

*Both now:*
I fear not the fearful judgment, nor dread Gehenna, for my heart is hardened by many passions: soften it by thy gentle breeze, O pure Virgin Mother, and pierce it, that it may be made whole by the remembrance of fearful torments.

*Ode 5. Heirmos:*[^68]
Grant us Thy peace, O Son of God, for beside Thee we know no other God; we name Thy name, for Thou art the God of the living and of the dead.

*Troparia:*
Beholding thy weeping and inconsolable lamentation, they that stood by, terrified, asked that thou wouldest tell them a word for profit: and thou didst tell them three profitable things: that they maintain obedience, repentance, and prayer, as higher than other virtues: in which that we also may be established, pray thou, O father Athanasius.

Being thyself obedient unto the commandments of Christ, thou didst tell the brethren that obedience is higher than all virtues, O venerable one; for it dies not even after death, but preserves life with God, Who is the fearful Judge of the living and the dead.

*Glory:*
Repentance and prayer are two fiery wings, wherewith thou didst learn that passage through the aerial toll-houses is unrestrained, O blessed one; thou didst find the paths of silence, whereon thou didst hasten unto higher things, leaving instruction unto them that asked:

*To Athanasius the Recluse of the Caves*

...and grant us correction, as we celebrate the day of thy departure.

*Both now:*
Speedy intercessor for the Christian race, O Mary Virgin, having received the prayers of thy singers into thy most pure hands, bring them unto thy Son, and ask for us forgiveness of sins, and grant stillness unto them that are in passions.

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*[^69]
As Thou didst swallow me into the deep of the heart of the sea, and didst save me, O Savior, from the bondage of death, and didst loose the bonds of mine iniquities.

*Troparia:*
Having utterly renounced life and the seeing of sensible light, thou didst enter into the dark seclusion, exchanging sensible sight for the vision of the noetic light: which that we also may obtain by thy prayers, O father, having cast off our iniquities.

That thou mightest quicken the flesh mortified by ascetic labors, O father, thou didst continually water it with the streams of thy tears, remaining twelve years buried alive in the cave, quickened by the hope of immortal life.

*Glory:*
Thy flesh, O venerable Athanasius, a living tree, brought forth the fruits of repentance unto all; whereof grant me also to taste by thy love, that I may be strengthened to loose the bonds of mine iniquities.

*Both now:*
Thou art an ever-flowering tree, O All-Pure Virgin, covering with thy shadow them that are burned by the swelter of iniquities: with this cover also us that are scorched by passions, and we will sing: Rejoice, well-shaded leafy tree, beneath which many are sheltered.

*Kontakion, Tone 5:*
As namesake of immortality, after thy mortification thou didst rise alive; and showing that death is exceedingly bitter, thou didst weep bitterly: now having found unending joy, pray for us, that we may escape eternal weeping.

*Ode 7. Heirmos:*[^70]
Sevenfold in the furnace unto Thy youths, O Savior, the fire was untouched, neither did it scorch them; then the three as with one mouth sang and blessed, saying: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!

*Troparia:*
Thou didst spend thy life in weeping and sighing, and having whitened the robe of the angelic habit with the streams of thy tears, thou didst depart unto the regions free of tears, rejoicing and singing: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!

The angelic ranks having come on the day of thy falling asleep, and peacefully receiving thy spirit, guided it into the dwellings of paradise, where living, remember us, O venerable father, that sing: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!

*Service to the Venerable Father*

...O venerable father, that sing: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!

*Glory:*
Twice wast thou a dead man in this life, O father, and first indeed all saw thee returning again into bodily members, from which having been loosed a second time, thou now livest with the angelic ranks: where also forget not us that sing: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!

*Both now:*
Hasten to quicken me who am dead through evil deeds, O Sovereign Lady; for without doubting have I placed my hope in thee, as having given birth unto the immortal King and Giver of life unto all the world.

*Ode 8. Heirmos:*[^71]
All ye works, and all creation as much as is, bless, sing unto the Lord, and supremely exalt Him unto all ages!

*Troparia:*
The heavens rejoice, having received thy spirit into their bosom, and we make merry as we bow down before thy relics, O venerable one, from which grant grace unto them that are in need, and vouchsafe us to sing unto the Lord unto ages.

The angels celebrate having thee as fellow-citizen, and the Caves Mountain makes merry, possessing the shrine of thy relics as a most radiant pearl: by this enlighten us that are held by the darkness of passions, and grant us to sing unto the Lord unto ages.

*We bless the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit: the Lord.*

Thou didst well please thy Master in this life, O father, and didst receive the gift to heal infirmities: visit us that are held by passions, as once thou didst Babylas, and vouchsafe us to sing unto the Lord unto ages.

*Both now:*
I am in sorrow and grief, reflecting upon my defiled life, and I have no hope: unto thee alone, the worldwide joy, do I flee, O pure Virgin, and I pray that joy may shine upon my soul unto ages.

*Ode 9. Heirmos:*
He hath wrought strength with His arm; for He hath put down the mighty from their thrones, and exalted the humble, the God of Israel. Wherein the Dayspring from on high hath visited us, and hath guided us into the way of peace.

*Troparia:*
Show love unto us that pray unto thee, O father, and by thy prayer grant healing unto them that suffer soul-infirmities; and especially guide them that are assailed by enemy snares into the way of peace.

The Great Lavra sings thy labors and ascetic feats, O venerable one, the cave glorifies thee, preserving thy wonder-working relics; whereunto we also bowing down, beseech thee: guide our steps into the way of peace.

*To Athanasius the Recluse of the Caves*

Arise quickly and come, healing them that entreat thee, O most blessed servant Athanasius; for lo, from many a soul-infirmity I cry unto thee, having no relief; rather I pray: guide me into the peaceful path of salvation.

*Glory:*
I glorify Thee, O Most Holy Trinity, unoriginate Father, with the co-eternal Son, and I hymn Thy Holy Spirit; Whom grant unto my heart through the intercession of Thine agreeable servant, that I may find a peaceful path.

*Both now:*
If any glorify not thy pure conception, and sing not thine ineffable childbirth, O Mary, Bride of God, let him fall away from eternal glory, and let him not be a partaker of the light, nor a son of the everlasting peace.

*Exapostilarion:*
Let us the people crown with hymns of God Athanasius, marvelous among the venerable; for he while living died, and after two days rose again, and again departed; he lives no longer held in dominion by death, but began an everlasting life, being a son of life and heir of the resurrection of the righteous.

*Glory, both now. Theotokion:*
Today who shall recount thy mysteries, O pure one? Or who shall proclaim the coming to pass of ineffable things in thee? For in time thou gavest birth unto the Son Who before ages was begotten of the Father, the Firstborn of life and resurrection; entreat Him therefore, that I also may be an heir of the resurrection and of life.

*On the Praises, Stichera, Tone 2. Podoben: "When from the Tree" (Є҆гда̀ ѿ дре́ва):*
Come, ye people, let us sing of marvelous Athanasius, who is sung in the heavens by angels, the beauty of fasters, and radiant luster of singers; who having zeal for the monastic life, traversed the narrow path without slackness, having overcome all passions by abstinence, having an unrestrained desire toward God, before Whom he now stands with the angels, and prays for our souls.

Come, ye brethren and assemblies of feast-lovers, every choir of God-bearing fathers, let us bring crowns of songful flowers unto the tomb of the most blessed father, and let us fall down, saying: O venerable, most blessed fellow-citizen of angels, forget not thine intercession unto God the Master of all, that we may obtain eternal life and mercy in the day of judgment.

Come, ye choirs of monastics, unto the shrine of the many-miraculous relics of ever-memorable Athanasius, and let us honor the God-speaking father, who instructed us unto obedience, and unto continual repentance and prayer, and ascending unto God by other virtues as by fiery wings, let us find grace and great mercy.

*Service to the Venerable Father*

*Glory, Tone 8:*
Ever most blessed, father of the holy, Athanasius! For thine instruction became for many a correction and a rising up from falling through the remembrance of the future judgment; expecting which thyself also after thy resurrection, thou didst pour forth inconsolable tears from thine eyes; now standing before Christ with rejoicing, pray thou for them that sing thy honorable falling asleep, that they may receive forgiveness of sins and great mercy in the day of judgment.

*Both now. Theotokion:*
O Sovereign Lady, accept the supplications of thy servants, and deliver us from every distress and sorrow.

*On the 30th Day of the Month of December: Service to our Venerable Father Nikon the Dry.*[^73]

*At Vespers, on "Lord, I have cried," Stichera, Tone 4. Podoben: "Thou hast given a sign" (Да́лъ є҆сѝ зна́менїе):*[^74]
Pillar of endurance, glorious and God-bearing Nikon, established in the love of God, wherein thou didst abide immovable through many temptations; therefore do we glorify thee, O father, as we celebrate thy memory on this day, as one glorified in the heavens by Jesus the Lover of mankind and Savior of our souls.

Unharmed by fire, and mistreated in every way *Service to the Venerable Father*

...thanksgiving didst thou send up, O blessed one, unto the Creator and God; therefore, delivered from the hand of the ungodly, thou didst stand in the holy church, and now standing before Christ in the church of them that triumph, grant that we also may receive repentance from the Lover of mankind and Savior of our souls.

The fire of thy love toward God quenched the sensible fire, and the streams of thy blood drowned the hosts of the noetic enemy, and thou didst conquer them that tormented thee, O venerable father; therefore Jesus the Lover of mankind and Savior of our souls gave thee the reward of the highest ranks.

*Glory, Tone 6:*
The multitudes of monastics marvel at thy patience, how being mistreated by the unfaithful, baked with fire, and cut with knives, enduring heat and swelter, hunger and thirst, and having hope in God for thy salvation, thou didst easily traverse the narrow path and attain the highest beatitude: blessed art thou having so worked for Christ, and thou hast received as the reward of thy labor a dwelling with the angels and participation in the divine light: therefore we beseech thee, O most blessed Nikon, as having boldness before Christ God, ask peace for the world and great mercy for our souls.

*Both now: Theotokion, or Stavrotheotokion. At the Aposticha, in the Oktoechos.*

*Glory, Tone 6:*
Come, ye Christ-loving assemblies, clapping your hands, and gatherings of the choirs of monastics: come piously unto the holy cave, let us wisely bring praiseworthy hymns as a gift, and bow down before the relics of glorious Nikon, that by his holy prayers we may receive the crown of unfading glory from Christ God and the Savior of our souls.

*Troparion, Tone 8:*
Having loved the good God, O stout-hearted father Nikon, thou didst receive the monastic life, and having fought well in it, thou wast taken captive by the Polovtsi: by whom being much tormented, by the grace of Christ thou wast freed from them invisibly; and now abiding in unapproachable light, pray thou that we who honor thy memory may be saved.

*Canon to the Saint*

*Tone 4. Ode 1. Heirmos:*[^75]
He was my helper and protector unto salvation; this is my God, and I will glorify Him, the God of my father, and I will exalt Him, for gloriously hath He been glorified.

*Troparia:*
Help me, O Lord my God, and to honor glorious Nikon in song, send down the All-Holy Paraclete, that girded with vigil, I may bring a song unto Thee, Who givest strength unto all.

Who shall worthily bless thee, O most blessed father, who from youth didst love thy Lord, and didst follow Him with voluntary poverty even unto death? Therefore now living with the angels, pray that strength unto endurance be sent unto us also.

*Glory:*
Having traversed without stumbling the darkness and gloom of the lustful world by burning love toward Christ, and being reckoned unto the brightness of the angelic habit, thou wast as it were outside the flesh; therefore thou singest unto Him that gave thee strength.

*Both now:*
Drive away, I pray thee, O pure Virgin, the darkness that hath blinded the mental eyes of my soul with passions; for thou art the divine light-receiving candle, kindled with immaterial fire, and thou dost guide unto divine understanding them that sing: Rejoice, luminary of the never-waning light!

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*[^76]
As the barren church from the nations hath brought forth, and the synagogue great in children hath grown weak, unto our marvelous God let us cry out: Holy art Thou, O Lord!

*Troparia:*
As the barren woman rejoices having received in the womb, so *To Nikon the Dry*

...so makes merry the holy Caves Mountain, having revealed thee as a luminary unto them that receive thee, and among monastic ranks it sings: Holy art Thou, O Lord!

Imitating the first fathers, having laid the cross upon thy shoulder with joy, O glorious Nikon, thou didst bear it with great courage even unto death, unto Whom thou now singest: Holy art Thou, O Lord!

*Glory:*
Armed with the shield of firm faith, thou didst go into captivity, O venerable one, and desiring to take captive the enemy there, in fortitude and magnanimity thou didst quench him, treading upon him with thy foot; and unto God Who strengthened thee, thou didst cry out: Holy art Thou, O Lord!

*Both now:*
Unable to overcome the enemy that rises up against me by the strength of passions, unto thee do I flee, O Mother of God, help me: for thou art a strong pillar, a weapon in battle, and an unconquerable leader.

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 8:*
Desiring to cultivate the field of thy soul, thou didst lay upon thyself the cross as the yoke of voluntary patience, and with this as with a plow thou didst cultivate the rot-bearing ground of passions, sowing with tears, casting the seed of fasting labors, from which thou now reapest joyful sheaves with thy hands *Service to the Venerable Father*

...reapest joyful sheaves with thy hands, standing before Christ God: Whom do thou entreat, O glorious Nikon, as being an ever-present intercessor, for them that bless thy holy memory with love.

*Glory, both now. Theotokion:*

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*[^77]
For the sake of the love of Thine image, O compassionate One, Thou didst stand upon Thy cross, and the nations melted away; for Thou, O Lover of mankind, art my strength and praise.

*Troparia:*
Thy love toward God was strong, O father, for Whose sake desiring to suffer from the unfaithful, thou wouldest by no means be ransomed: for having loved the Lord Who loved thee, and hoping in Him, thou didst cry: Holy art Thou, O Lord!

Having received the instruction of the Lord affectionately, sweetly didst thou enter into the trial of sicknesses, O blessed one, saying with David: Blessed is the man whom Thou chastenest, O Lord; therefore I cry unto Thee: Glory to Thy power, O Lord!

*Glory:*
Looking upon future things, thou didst account present daily afflictions as nothing, O marvelous Nikon, though fire, and frost, and iron assailed thee, yet thou didst endure all these, remaining like a firm adamant, crying: Holy art Thou, O Lord!

*Both now:*
Looking not upon future things, my soul hath cleaved unto the earth, and I was likened unto senseless beasts: but do thou, O pure Virgin, open my mental eyes, and make marvelous thy mercy upon me, that understanding I may cry: Holy art thou and marvelous in truth!

*Ode 5. Heirmos:*[^78]
Thy light, O my Lord, came into the world, a holy light, turning back from the gloom of ignorance them that praise Thee with faith.

*Troparia:*
Being mistreated in every way by the unfaithful, O father, thou didst not despair thereof, but sending up thanksgiving unto God, thou didst say: Quench me, O God, for the waters are come in even unto my soul; for Thou alone art He that frees the shackled with courage, who praise Thee with faith.

Thy patience, O most blessed one, confounded the Polovtsian who took thee captive; therefore three years he spent tormenting thee every day, afflicting thee with hunger, thirst, cold, and swelter: but thou didst have hope in God, that He delivers them that praise Him with faith.

*Glory:*

By the streams of thy help, O Virgin Bride of God, drown the invisible enemies that drag my soul into the abyss of Hades with the chains of passions, that being freed from them I may cry unto thee: Rejoice, sea that didst drown the mental Pharaoh!

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*[^79]
Prefiguring the three-day burial, the prophet Jonah cried out, praying in the sea-monster: Deliver me from corruption, O Jesus, King of hosts!

*Troparia:*
The iron of sorrows pierced thy soul, O glorious Nikon, until the martyr gave assurance in faith of thy deliverance sent unto thee quickly from God, unto Whom thou didst cry: In Thee, O Lord, have I put my trust; by Thy righteousness deliver me, O Jesus, King of hosts!

*Glory:*
As Habakkuk invisibly, the right hand of the Most High snatched thee away; and they that beheld marveled, hearing a voice from on high, and were put to shame, not knowing God, that He alone is the King of hosts.

*Both now:*
O mighty Deliverer, deliver me from the hands of adversaries, for lo, my head sinks down into the abyss, and I shall not escape the eternal bars of Hades, unless thou grant me help, who art the Mother of the King of hosts.

*Kontakion, Tone 6:*
Imitating the Protomartyr, thou didst pray for thy tormentor, and having delivered him from death, thou didst lead him unto Christ, O glorious venerable martyr Nikon; therefore, beholding Christ at the right hand of the Father with the first-crowned, pray for us, that we remain not outside the bridal chamber of Christ.

The Abrahamic youths of old in Babylon trampled the furnace flame, crying out with songs: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!

*Troparia:*
While the awesome service of the mystery of ordination was being celebrated in the church, snatched away from the shroud by the power of God, thou didst stand in the midst of the brethren, O glorious Nikon; and all beholding thy fetters, and the blood dripping from thy cruel wounds, lifted up their hands for thy deliverance, saying: Blessed art Thou, O God!

*Ode 8. Heirmos:*[^81]
O almighty Deliverer of all, descending in the midst of the flame unto them that practiced piety, Thou didst bedew them, and taught them to sing: Bless the Lord, all ye works, and sing unto Him!

*Troparia:*
Bearing in thy heart the Deliverer of all and thy Savior, thou didst not fear the tormentor who desired to crucify thee, O father; but rendering good for evil, thou didst heal him lying in sickness, that he also turning, might bless the Lord and supremely exalt Him unto ages.

[^96]: Woodcut engraving of Saint Gregory the Wonderworker of the Caves (*Прⷣ: Григо́рїй Чꙋдотво́рецъ Пече́рскїй*). According to Discourse 25 of the Kyiv-Pechersk Paterikon, St. Gregory was gifted with discernment and prophecy; when Prince Rostislav Vsevolodovich was marching on an expedition against the Polovtsi in 1093, his servants mocked the elder, who prophesied that the prince and his warriors would drown in the Stuhna River. Enraged, Rostislav had Gregory bound hand and foot with a heavy stone tied around his neck and cast into the Dnipro River. The prophecy was fulfilled shortly thereafter at the Battle of the Stuhna.
