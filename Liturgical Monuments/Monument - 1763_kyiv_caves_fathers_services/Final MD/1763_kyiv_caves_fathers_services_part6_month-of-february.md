# MONTH OF FEBRUARY

*On the 12th Day: Service to our Venerable Father Prokhor the Wonderworker of the Caves.*[^127]

*At Vespers, on "Lord, I have cried," Stichera, Tone 4. Podoben: "Thou hast given a sign" (Да́лъ є҆сѝ ꙁна́менїе):*[^128]
O marvelous Prokhor, passing through the angelic life, thou didst flee all worldly pleasures, being nourished rather by the word of God than by meats. Therefore thou wast truly an edifice with them, with whom Jesus the Lover of mankind and Savior of our souls granted thee an habitation in the heavenly mansions.

*To Prokhor the Wonderworker of the Caves*

O glorious Prokhor, fleeing human glory, like a soaring bird thou didst hasten unto the hill of the Caves, and didst fly across, being enriched with vision and the doing of the law of the Lord, whence having ascended unto the summit of virtues, thou didst receive the gift of wonderworking from Jesus the Lover of mankind and Savior of our souls.

O most blessed Prokhor, following the footsteps of the blessed fathers, thou didst acquire a narrow way of life in this world, so that thou didst not even taste of ordinary bread, but wast satisfied with wild-growing herbs; therefore now dwelling in the blessed life, thou art nourished with the food of heavenly joy, which Jesus the Lover of mankind and Savior of our souls gave thee from His goodness.

*Glory: Tone 6:*
Who will not marvel at thy life, or who will not recount thy patience, O most blessed father Prokhor? For passing through life in great abstinence, thou didst neglect the body, and didst crush the pleasure-loving serpent unto the end, having established thy feet upon the steps of fasting virtues. Therefore on this present day, honoring thy memory, we confess thy labors, and we pray, falling down before the shrine of thy relics: as thou hast boldness toward Christ God, ask peace for the world, and great mercy for our souls.

*Both now: Theotokion, or Stavrotheotokion.*
*At the Aposticha, all from the Octoechos.*
*Glory: To the Venerable, Tone 6:*
The multitudes of monastics in thy memory, O venerable one, having formed a choir of praises, we honor thy labors and ascetic feats, and marvel how, thyself leading a narrow and poor life, thou didst preserve others living in abundance in the time of necessity: for them that were fainting with hunger thou didst feed with bread made from weeds, and unto them that needed salt thou didst give from ashes: and now strengthen us that faint with spiritual hunger by thy prayers, and ask great mercy from Christ God.
*Both now: Theotokion, or Stavrotheotokion.*

*Troparion, Tone 1:*
O venerable and wonderworking father Prokhor, who in famine didst feed the hungry with bread from weeds, and with salt from ashes: feed us also with spiritual food, praying unto Christ God for our souls.

*Canon, Tone 8. Ode 1. Heirmos:*[^129]
He who threw down the chariot-driving Pharaoh, the wonderworking staff of Moses of old, striking crosswise and dividing the sea, saved Israel a fugitive, walking on foot, chanting a song unto God.

*Service to the Venerable Father*

...Gird us, O Master Christ, with the spiritual weapon against invisible enemies, and teach us how to contend and overcome their power, that we may offer unto Thee our God a festive song in the memory of Thy servant, sending it forth with love.

Having their loins girded with vigilance, the ranks of monastics remember thee, O father, and celebrate thine annual memory with praises, sending forth a song unto God.

Remembering eternal blessedness, O blessed one, thou didst leave the corruptible things of this world, and that thou mightest be a co-dweller with angels, thou didst clothe thyself in the angelic habit; therefore we honor thy fasting life, sending forth a song unto God.

*Glory:*
Taking upon thyself the good yoke of abstinence, thou didst acquire the hard life of fasting deeds, O God-wise one, wherein having wrestled with the invisible enemy, thou didst conquer him: therefore we glorify thee, sending forth a song unto God.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Unto us that walk in the broad way and flow by pleasure into the abyss of hades, guide us, O Mother of God, unto the narrow way leading unto the blessed life: that with thy chosen servants on this present day we may fall down before thee, sending forth supplications.

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*[^130]
Thou Who in the beginning didst establish the heavens with understanding, and founded the earth upon the waters, establish me, O Christ, upon the rock of Thy commandments: for there is none holy beside Thee, O only Lover of mankind.

*Troparia:*
Having established thy feet upon the rock of Christ's commandments, O father, thou didst remain unshaken by the snares of demons, ever offering praise unto God: There is none holy beside Thee, O Lover of mankind.

The kindling of carnal passions thou didst extinguish by the dew of abstinence: for not even eating bread, but wild orache, thou didst continue, having the word of God rather as food, and this strengthened thee, O venerable one.

Famine thou didst account as nothing, O blessed one, hoping upon Him that feeds the birds; and He gave thee to eat bread not from sown grain, which was sweeter unto thee than honey and the honeycomb.

*Glory:*
We marvel at thy marvelous possession, O father, that even for orache bread thou didst condemn thyself, making thyself unworthy even of such food, wherewith lightly thou didst traverse the sorrowful fasting way.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Our enemies who exalt themselves with pride bend down unto humility, O pure Virgin: for thou alone didst say: Behold the handmaid of the Lord, and thou reignest unto the ages over all generations that bless thee.

*To Prokhor the Wonderworker of the Caves*

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 1:*
Emulating the great fathers, extreme fasting thou didst earnestly desire: for being nourished with weed alone and water, thou didst not faint from fasting, but didst remain stronger than adamant; and now receiving the fruits of thine abstinence, recompense from God, pray thou ever for us, that through thee we also may receive a reward, as we bless thy memory with love, O one worthy of wonder.
*Glory, both now: Theotokion:*
With choirs of them that bless thee, the human race, and having surpassed every creature with glory, in hymns we honor thy seedless conception and thine ineffable childbirth, O Mary, pure Virgin Mother.

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*[^131]
Thou art my strength, O Lord, Thou art my power, Thou art my God, Thou art my rejoicing, Who didst not leave the bosom of the Father, and didst visit our poverty: therefore with the Prophet Habakkuk I cry unto Thee: Glory to Thy power, O Lover of mankind!

*Troparia:*
Having from God the gift of wonderworking, O father, thou didst feed many with orache bread in the time of famine, and making salt from ashes, thou didst give it freely unto them that came: and glory was unto the Lover of mankind Who became a defender there.

By thy prayer, O venerable one, thou didst give sweetness unto the orache bread: for it was sweet for food, and radiant and pure like wheat bread unto them that received it with a blessing. From the blessing of thine herbs grant unto us also, O most sacred father, as we have thee as an intercessor toward Christ the Lover of mankind.

Thy weed-bread, O most blessed father, was sweet and radiant unto them that received it with a blessing; but unto them that stole it secretly, it appeared black like soot and bitter. With thy blessing bless us, and grant of thy sweetness, as thou sittest at the heavenly table, O glorious Prokhor.

*Glory:*
Not sated, but leading a choir of good praises, fainting with spiritual hunger, we come unto thee, O father, on this present day of thy memory, and we pray: grant us of thy grace, that in thee we may glorify Christ the Lover of mankind.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Who that flees unto thee departs from thee confounded, O pure Virgin! Or who that asks receives not of thy grace, O Lady! Which we also believing and confessing, offer supplication unto thee, O pure one: grant us thy bounties, as the good Mother of the Good Son.

*Service to the Venerable Father*

*Ode 5. Heirmos:*[^132]
Why hast Thou cast me off from Thy countenance, O unwaning Light, and a strange darkness has covered me, the wretched one? But convert me, and direct my ways unto the light of Thy commandments, I pray.

*Troparia:*
O glorious Prokhor, delay not to come, and vouchsafe us thy blessing, even as of old in time of need thou didst give it unhindered; and direct the ways of them that praise thee with love unto the commandments of the Lord, as our fervent intercessor.

He who troubles the earth and shakes the people brought war upon us, the adversary enemy, and brought spiritual famine upon all our entrance; but do thou, O most blessed one, appear quickly, and by thy prayer feed us with noetic food, and direct our ways, as our intercessor.

Seeing thy compassions, O father, many came unto thee in the time of famine, unto whom the bread made from herbs and the salt made from ashes by thy hands thou didst give without fail; look down therefore now upon us, and by thy prayer grant every abundance unto our monastery, we pray thee, O venerable father.

*Glory:*
Many that hate us, enemies have risen up against us, striving to deprive us of spiritual and bodily food; but come thou unto us and rebuke them, and by thy prayer grant unto our monastery all that is unto profit, we pray thee, O venerable Prokhor.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
The adversary enemy, envying thy flock, continually stirs up war against us; but do thou, O all-pure one, as the Mother of the mighty King, turn him back, and direct our ways unto the commandments of the Lord, as our intercessor.

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*[^133]
Cleanse me, O Savior, for many are mine iniquities, and lead me up from the depths of evils, I pray: for unto Thee have I cried, and give ear unto me, O God of my salvation.

*Troparia:*
Cleanse the defilement of my soul by thy prayer, O glorious Prokhor, and heal the fiery darts of my heart with the plaster of thy grace: for unto thee on this present day do I cry aloud, as unto an intercessor for my salvation.

Overcome by the thought of extortion, the prince commanded all the salt made from ashes by thy prayer to be plundered, which being turned into ashes again, was found bitter in their mouths; from such malice and passion deliver me, O father, as a helper of my salvation.

Shame covered the faces of them that slandered the food of the hungry: for seeing the transformation of thy salt into ashes, they cast it out from the house; but thou didst command the cast-out ashes to be gathered up, and it became pure salt again by thy prayer, even as before. Grant unto me also now thy prayer, O prayerful helper of my salvation.

*Glory:*
Thinking to hide thy wonder, they commanded the salt that had turned into ashes to be poured out by night outside; but the night witnessed all the more unto thy wonders: for the ashes having again been changed into salt, by thy command the needy gathered it, blessing thee, O venerable one, as their defender.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Night holds me fast in slothfulness, an intercessor of sleepiness, not drawing near unto the light of spiritual vigilance; O Mother of God, enlighten my mind, guiding me unto doxology, that I may ever sing unto thee: Rejoice, candle kindled with the divine flame!

*Kontakion, Tone 2:*
Having mortified thy flesh with extreme abstinence, O father, being deprived of human food, herbs and grass were in place of bread, and with ashes like salt thou wast nourished, O wonderworking Prokhor; which seeing, the Lord changed the herbs into bread, and the ashes into salt, wherewith thou didst feed the multitude of the hungry: therefore we honor and bless thee.

*To Prokhor the Wonderworker of the Caves*

*Ode 7. Heirmos:*[^134]
The fire was ashamed before the condescension of God in Babylon of old; therefore the children, dancing in the furnace with joyful step, as in a meadow, sang: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!

*Troparia:*
Remembering the condescension of God and the dread judgment, the prince was afraid, seeing how he had surrounded thee, O venerable Prokhor, not knowing the grace that was wrought in thee; but coming to himself, he cried aloud: Blessed is the God of our fathers!

Recognizing thee, O father, as a servant of God, the ruler afterward blessed thy benefactions, and having received warmer love toward the most marvelous monastery, he repented of his extortion, and asking forgiveness for the evil done, sang: Blessed is the God of our fathers!

As a true minister and servant of the innocent Lamb, the Son of God, having shown thy guiltlessness, thou gavest forgiveness unto him that wronged thee, O blessed one, and confirmed a covenant with him, that the one should lay the other in the grave, when he should depart from the body, as the God of our fathers should command one of you.

*Glory:*
Having lived in holiness and directed thy life, thou didst attain unto a blessed end, O father; therefore rejoicing unto the angelic choirs, crying aloud, thou didst sing: Blessed is the God of our fathers!

*Both now: Theotokion:*
I have not emulated them that lived in holiness, but in slothfulness have spent my life, unto the end approaching the end, unto whom dost thou flee, O my soul? Having only the Mother of God as hope, cry aloud: O Full of grace, help, and deliver me from eternal condemnation.

*Ode 8. Heirmos:*[^135]
The Chaldean tyrant furiously kindled sevenfold the furnace for them that honored God; but seeing them saved by a higher power, he cried aloud unto the Creator and Deliverer: Ye youths, bless; ye priests, sing; ye people, supremely exalt Him unto all ages!

*Troparia:*
Having slain the seven-headed serpent with the spear of ascetic feats, and preparing thyself to receive the crown, with joy didst thou call aloud, O Prokhor: Ye people, sing unto the Lord Creator and Deliverer, and supremely exalt Him unto ages!

Having received word of thy blessed departure, hearing of it from thy sending, the prince left his warriors, and hastily ran unto thee, that he might be vouchsafed thy blessing; and he was not deprived of that which he desired: having received thy blessing, he sang: Ye people, supremely exalt Christ unto ages!

Even as aforetime in prayers thou didst stretch forth thy hands, O venerable one, so at thy departure, having given the kiss unto all, and having lifted up thy hands on high, thou didst yield up thy spirit; and all cried aloud: Ye people, glorify your protector unto ages!

*We bless the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit: the Lord.*
Thy sorrowless departure unto the heavenly habitations filled all with joyful weeping: for all lamented, the poor for their nourisher, the orphans for a father; but we on this present day glorifying thy falling asleep, O father, call aloud: Ye people, supremely exalt Christ unto ages!

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Thou that didst bear the Joy of all the venerable, eternal joy, by thine intercession vouchsafe me, O Mother of God, and vouchsafe me to glorify thy loving-kindness unto ages.

*Ode 9. Heirmos:*[^136]
Heaven was struck with awe at this, and the ends of the earth were amazed, that God appeared unto men in the flesh, and thy womb became more spacious than the heavens; therefore the ranks of angels and men magnify thee, O Mother of God.

*Troparia:*
Everyone who remembers thy life is amazed, O father, that being in the flesh, thou wast assimilated unto the angels by ascetic deeds; therefore all magnify thee, O Prokhor.

The wearer of the diadem, not loathing thee who wast poor in spirit, O holy and venerable one, laid thee into the grave with his own hands; and we now remembering thy falling asleep, call thee blessed, whom also the angelic choirs bless in the heavenly mansions.

The prince, not loathing thee who in this life didst bear the cross upon thy shoulders, carried thee upon his shoulders into the cave, and washed thy grave with warm tears, repenting that he had formerly vexed thee; but both things he set right by thy blessing, of which vouchsafe us also, as out of love we magnify thee with hymns.

*To Prokhor the Wonderworker of the Caves*

*Glory:*
Glory to Thee, O Most-Holy Trinity, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, one God: for by Thy grace Thou givest eternal glory unto them that love Thee from the heart, of which vouchsafe us also, through the supplications of venerable Prokhor and of all the venerable saints, where their choirs continually magnify Thee.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Glory to thee, O Mother of God, sole refuge of the faithful, sole reliance, sole hope and salvation of the Christian race; therefore as is due, all of us magnify thee.

*Exapostilarion:*
Having left the corruptible things of the world, thou didst attain unto the heavenly, honorable paths; and standing before the unoriginate Light with the ranks of the venerable, pray thou, as thou hast boldness to assist in necessities, for the pacification of the world, and for the empire of Christian sovereigns, who glorify thy memory.

*Glory, both now: Theotokion:*
O intercessor before the Holy Trinity for them that are tempest-tossed in the deep of worldly passions, stand forth quickly unto help, and by the calm waters of thy grace lead us into the blessed haven, as thou art a speedy helper unto them that glorify thy name.

*At the Aposticha, all from the Octoechos.*
*Then, to the Venerable:*
*Sticheron: Tone 4:*
*Verse:* The venerable ones shall exult in glory, and shall rejoice upon their beds.[^137]
Celebrating like angels, O ye people, let us bring a hymn unto God on the annual memory of the God-bearing father, exalting His honorable name, and calling aloud like the seraphim: Holy art Thou, O unoriginate Father; holy art Thou, O timeless Son; holy art Thou, O co-everlasting Spirit!

*Glory: Tone 4:*
Having formed an honorable choir, assimilating ourselves unto the choirs of the bodiless ones, come, ye people, lovingly now let us bring spiritual gifts unto the holy cave, and looking into the grave of the most blessed father, let us praise him, calling aloud: Rejoice, O Prokhor of glorious name, and intercessor for our souls!

*Both now: Theotokion, or Stavrotheotokion.*

*In the Month of February, on the 14th Day: Service to our Venerable Father Isaac the Recluse of the Caves.*[^139]

*At Vespers, on "Lord, I have cried," Stichera, Tone 8. Podoben: "O most marvelous wonder" (Ѡ҆ пресла́внагѡ чꙋдесѐ):*[^140]
O venerable father Isaac, though at first the dark enemy appeared in the form of light, and through ignorance deceived thee, and mocked thee with demonic delusions; yet afterward thou didst correct thyself, and wast shown forth terrible unto him: for thou didst receive from Christ God victory over all the power of the enemy, Whom *To Isaac the Recluse of the Caves*

...Whom cease not to pray that we also may be delivered from all the wiles of the enemy, and from grievous afflictions.

O God-wise father Isaac, though the enemy who appeared as light deceived thee, yet afterward through thy correction he that deceived thee fell: for having caught him by his own murderous scheme, thou didst cast him down, overturning him utterly; and having trampled him under thy feet, thou didst cry aloud: I will not fear your terror, for God is with me, and His strength is my confirmation against all your frightful devices.

O most blessed father Isaac, though in ignorance thou didst bow down unto a demon as unto the person of Christ, yet after this fall having corrected thyself again, thou didst spit upon the demonic legions, and didst fill their faces with dishonor: who, being put to shame, vanish like smoke and are driven away as by fire; for the fire of thy love toward God drives away their assaults, and helps all that call upon thy name.

*Glory: Tone 2:*
O father Isaac, by thyself thou hast given an example unto them that struggle, not to believe appearances that show themselves: for satan knows how to transform himself into an angel of light; but let them seal themselves with the sign of the mighty weapon, upon which the spirits of malice are unable to gaze, but even as dust is driven by the wind from the earth, so being scorched by its beams they vanish; therefore we also, holding fast unto this, will not fear the wiles of the enemy, but rather quenching their cunning designs, let us receive from Christ God peace in our bones, and great mercy.

*Both now: Theotokion, or Stavrotheotokion.*

*Troparion, Tone 2:*
Desiring to enjoy the radiance of the saints, thou didst settle in a dark cave, O all-praised one, and didst struggle well, O Isaac; and though deceived by the enemy, thou didst again firmly trample underfoot all his wiles; and now as victor standing in joy before Christ God, ask for us peace and great mercy.

*Glory, both now: Theotokion.*

*Canon to the Venerable, Tone 4. Ode 1. Heirmos:*[^141]
O Thou Who wast born of the Virgin, drown, I pray Thee, the three parts of my soul in the depth of dispassion, even as Thou didst drown the mighty captains; that unto Thee, as upon a timbrel, in the mortification of the body I may sing a song of victory.

*Troparia:*
As Thou art almighty, O Christ, in the depth of Thy compassions *Service to the Venerable Father*

...drown my sins like Pharaoh, I pray Thee, that with clay lips in the memory of Thy servant, glorifying Thee, I may worthily bring a victorious hymn.

The mighty ruler of the world stood by, seeking to cover all with the depth of passions; but he was not able to submerge thee, O father: for having recognized his manifold snares, thou didst flee the world like a bird from a snare, and didst bring a victorious hymn unto God.

A subtle merchant wast thou, O venerable one: for by corruptible things thou didst acquire the incorruptible, and for perishable things thou didst purchase the unfading pearl, wherewith thou didst adorn the crown of thy patience; and now rejoicing thou standest before Christ, bringing a victorious hymn.

*Glory:*
Thou didst distribute thy possessions unto the poor, O blessed one, and didst not spare even thine own will, coming unto that great Anthony, from whom having received the angelic habit in exchange, thou wast enriched with the heavenly treasure, and singest unceasing praise unto Christ.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Burdened with the yoke of sin, unto thee I come, O Mother of God, praying: take away my passionate torment, that at least from now on I may correct myself through repentance, and bring unto thee, O Lady, my fervent prayers, a contrite hymn.

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*[^142]
From on high Thou didst come down willingly upon the earth, O Thou Who art above every principality, and hast exalted the humble human nature, which was from the lowest hell: for there is none holy save Thee, O Lover of mankind.

*Troparia:*
Having received the calling from on high, by the exchange of the angelic habit thou didst set fire unto the world, which quenched all its lustful darts, having taught us by an honorable and austere life, O venerable one, having as helper God the only Lover of mankind.

That thou mightest not inherit a portion with the goats in the age to come, O father, in this life mortifying thy flesh, thou didst clothe thyself in a single rough hairshirt and in a goat-skin, wherein thou didst traverse the narrow monastic way, calling upon God for help, the only Lover of mankind.

Thou didst settle upon the hill, thou didst shut thyself in a narrow cave; and by the ladder of virtues wisely reaching forth unto the heavenly things, thou didst set ascents in thy heart, ever to hymn God the Lover of mankind.

*Glory:*
Thou didst traverse the narrow path of humility and poverty, O wise one, having dashed the crafty enemy like an infant against the rock of the love of God, which seeking, thou didst cleanse thy soul with fasting, and by all-night vigilance didst humble the body, that it might unslothfully stand before God the Lover of mankind.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Overcome by fierceness, I am unable to dash the passions that are born in me, like infants, against the rock of the commandments; thee I call upon, O Mother of God, for help: help me, as thou art the Mother of God Who loves mankind, and they shall fall under my feet, conquered by thy power.

*To Isaac the Recluse of the Caves*

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 8:*[^143]
Mortifying thy flesh by abstinence, thou didst neglect corruptible food, but satisfying thyself with a single prosphora every other day, thou didst abide in abstinence, and didst not rest upon thy ribs; thus contending against the invisible enemy, thou didst cry aloud: O Christ God, grant help unto them that are in battle!

*Glory, both now: Theotokion:*
Darkened by the sleep of slothfulness, my soul I stretch out unto thee, O pure one: by thy vigilant prayer and measureless goodness raise me up unto hymnody, and grant me help as I am in battle.

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*[^144]
For the sake of the love of Thine image, O Compassionate One, Thou didst stand upon Thy Cross, and the nations melted away: for Thou, O Lover of mankind, art my strength and my praise.

*Troparia:*
For the sake of the love of God, in many labors and fastings Thou didst continually abide until the seventh year, desiring her and having no departure from anywhere; and remembering thy dreadful departure, Thou didst call aloud unto Christ Who strengthens Thee: Thou art my strength, O God, and my praise!

Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together: a brother helped by a brother is like a strong city, but he that contends alone against the enemy falls; which also happened unto thee, O father, that thou shouldest not rely upon thyself, but upon the living God, for He alone is strength and praise unto all.

*Glory:*
The devil, desiring to darken thy mind, O father, came with a multitude of bright demons, and himself shone like the sun, but covered thee with deceitful darkness, from which being delivered, thou didst cry aloud unto God: Thou art my strength, O God, and my praise!

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Having my mind darkened with passions, I am held fast by many sins; but unto thee do I pray, who beyond mind didst bear the Light of the whole world: receive, O Mother of God, the weapon of thy power, and stand forth unto me for help, that I may behold the light of true divine understanding.

*Service to the Venerable Father*

*Ode 5. Heirmos:*[^145]
Shine forth upon me, O Lord, the light of Thy commandments, for unto Thee my spirit wakes early, and sings of Thee: for Thou art our God, and unto Thee do I flee, O King of peace.

*Troparia:*
They that shone forth in the likeness of light, the sons of eternal darkness, made a great tumult, calling thee their own, O father Isaac, and they surrounded thee like many dogs with their wiles, from which being delivered, thou didst flee unto God, the King of peace.

He who formerly commanded that at the tyrant's voice sound should be made before the golden-forged image, unto the deception of them that know not God: this same one commands pipes and timbrels to sound for thy deception; and though deceived by him, thou wast delivered by the help of God, the King of peace.

The devil commanded that thou who wast overwearied by ascetic labors shouldest be worn out by dancing; fulfilling whose command, the crafty spirits leaped with thee for a long time, and having mocked thee, left thee scarcely alive; but thou afterward having corrected thyself didst mock him, and trampled him underfoot unto the end, by the power of God, the King of peace.

*Glory:*
Seeing thee wounded by the enemy, speechless, only barely breathing, the venerable fathers carried thee out before the cave, and recognizing that this had happened from demonic working, they persevered in prayer, assisting thee; and the Lord, the King of peace, heard their prayer, and had mercy upon thee.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Having my heart wounded by the darkness of demonic assaults, unto thee I pray, O Lady: apply to it the good plaster of mercy, and save my darkened soul from falling; for thee alone do I have as a speedy visitation, O Queen of light.

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*[^146]
Submerged by the tempest of sin, as though held fast in the whale's belly, in holiness I call aloud unto Thee: Lead up my life from corruption, O Lord, and save me.

*Troparia:*
Distressed by the tempest of demonic temptations, O blessed one, thou wast weakened in mind and body, and knowing nothing about thyself, thou didst lie upon a bed for two years, neither eating bread nor drinking water; but being entreated through the prayer of venerable Theodosius, the Lord brought up thy life from corruption, and saved thee.

A strange wonder it is for human nature to be without food; yet this was accomplished in thee, O blessed one: for God, wheresoever He wills, overcomes the order of nature, for in His hand is the breath of every creature, and He does whatever He pleases.

Thy Cross, O Christ, is a mighty weapon against enemies, wherewith if we are not protected, we shall be tempted *To Isaac the Recluse of the Caves*

...we shall be tempted even as was Isaac; therefore being protected therewith, we pray Thee: even as Thou didst not permit Isaac to be tempted unto the end, so also permit not temptation beyond our strength to come upon us, and save us.

*Glory:*
Glory to Thee, O unoriginate Trinity, the Cause of goodness, Who by Thy love for mankind liftest up the fallen from all things: have compassion upon us Thy servants, and raise up the fallen, even as Thou didst save Thy servant Isaac, granting him greater strength against demons.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Glory to thee, O Mother of God, refuge of the faithful: for through thee the fallen are enriched, the dead are quickened, they receive strength against the devil, and are vouchsafed eternal life, in the glory of thy Son and God.

*Kontakion, Tone 5:*
Having overthrown the enemy's deception, and shone brightly in fasting, come, ye faithful, let us praise victorious Isaac, who passed through temptations like gold, and enlightens the hearts of them that faithfully run unto him, praying unceasingly unto the Lord for our souls.

*Ode 7. Heirmos:*[^147]
Deliver us not up unto the end for Thy name's sake, and break not Thy covenant, and take not Thy mercy away from us, O Lord, God of our fathers, supremely praised unto the ages.

*Troparia:*
Having passed through many temptations, O venerable one, thou didst abound all the more unto the glory of God, and standing firmly against the enemy, thou didst sing in psalmic words: Setting my feet in distress, Thou hast placed them in a broad place, O Christ God, all-praised unto the ages.

Closely didst thou bind thyself with haircloth garments, and having firmly bound the enemy also who had set thee in the kitchen, thou didst mock him who had mocked thee, driving him away by prayer, and anticipating all by obedience and ascetic labors.

Having raised up the fallen temple of thy soul through temptation, thou didst run first of all unto the temple of God's Mother, and therein, with thy feet freezing unto the stones from cold, thou didst stand until the completion of the rule, the fire of thy heart secretly kindling thee into love of God.

*Glory:*
Double grace before natural deception thou didst again attain, O father, which the bird above thee signified; which they that saw recognized the grace of God that was with thee, and all being amazed glorified Christ Who glorified thee, and honored thee as a servant of Christ.

*Both now: Theotokion:*

*Service to the Venerable Father*

...Darkened by holy folly through demonic delusions, and not having kept the commandments of my Lord, I transgress; but do thou, O pure Mother, grant me understanding, that I may perceive the right path, and having escaped the hand of Belial who hunts me, I may praise thee unto ages.

*Ode 8. Heirmos:*[^148]
Unto Him Who was nailed in the flesh upon the Cross, and showed us a weapon unto salvation, ye youths, supremely exalt Christ our God unto all ages.

*Troparia:*
Having ascended the cross of endurance, thou wast crucified unto the world, O most blessed Isaac: for thou didst nail thy members by the cutting off of passions; and with the spear of mockery, not knowing thy state for God's sake, they pierced thee, but thou didst endure all things joyfully, praising Christ unto ages.

Having stepped with bare feet upon the flame of a kindled furnace, thou didst remain standing unmoved until it was quenched, and this signified the quenching of demonic assaults, which thou didst quickly cool by the power of Christ's Cross, and therein didst praise Him unto ages.

*Glory:*
Many times the demons assaulted thee, O father, calling thee their own, because thou hadst bowed down unto their prince; but thou by the power of the Cross didst drive them away like flies, and in shame they fled from thee, unable to bear their humiliation; and thy latter victory over them became greater than the first.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Tried by the onslaught of grievous passions, unto thy protection do I flee, O all-immaculate Virgin: for the sake of thy venerable Isaac, cover me, and save me from these assaults, that in orthodox manner I may glorify thee with Christ unto ages.

*Ode 9. Heirmos:*[^149]
Thy childbirth was shown forth incorrupt: God came forth from thy side, and appeared upon earth as a bearer of flesh, and dwelt with men; therefore, O Mother of God, we all magnify thee.

*Troparia:*
We all magnify God the Lover of mankind, Who so strengthened His servant, that he turned all demonic terrors into flight; and deceit being conquered by no other weapon, he was like a diamond, firm, whole, and unbreakable.

Thine adversary was heard, O venerable one, though many times by night he brought many demons unto his help, with mattocks and spades, crying aloud: Let us dig up this cave, and bury him here, for already he overcomes us, calling upon the unconquerable Name of the Most High, and upon her that bore Him for help.

Recognizing the night-time darkness, the blest one drove away with the light-bearing sign of the Cross them that came by night, wherewith *To Isaac the Recluse of the Caves*

...wherewith being scorched, unable to endure, they turned unto flight, leaving unto thee the crown of victory, as unto a good soldier and invincible ascetic.

*Glory:*
The serpent and fierce ones contending with thee during the summer, O father, were transformed into four-footed beasts, wild animals, reptiles, and mice; but not overcoming thee by their phantoms, by constraint he confessed thee as master: for thou didst overthrow him by the Name of Christ, Whom with her that bore Him we magnify.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
In the time of my life, the enemy, transformed into diverse forms, overcomes me; unable to stand against him, thee do I call upon, O Mother of God: send me strength, as thou didst bear the Mighty One, Whom with thee I magnify.

*Exapostilarion:*
Having undertaken many labors in life, contending against enemies, thou didst attain unto a blessed end; thou wast translated unto the highest, unto heavenly rest, but thy memory abides among us, and the Church confesses thy labors: which remembering, we pray thee: visit us, and grant a blessing on this present day unto them that honor thy translation, O our father Isaac.

*Glory, both now: Theotokion:*
O speedy defense of Christians, make haste to stand forth unto them that are tormented by many afflictions, that hoping upon thee we may not fall away from hope; but delivered through thee from perils, we may glorify thy honorable childbirth, O all-immaculate one.

*At the Praises, Stichera, Tone 3:*
*Verse:* God is marvelous in His saints, the God of Israel.[^150]
Having ever tilled the field of thy soul in this corruptible life, thou didst water it with a stream of tears; and having sown labors like pure wheat, and grown the ears of monastic virtues, thou didst reap sheaves of unceasing prayers, which thou didst bring into the heavenly barn; and now receiving praise with the good servants, remember us, O blessed father, who lovingly honor thy memory.

Having walked by the narrow path in this temporal age, with sorrows and tears, and unceasing vigilance, with sighing and affliction, O long-praised one, thou didst turn away from the broad and ruinous way, and didst attain unto heavenly breadth, where having painless rest *Service to the Venerable Father*

...having painless rest, forget not us also in thy goodness, as an intercessor for our souls.

Having become poor for God's sake, despising all earthly possessions, thou didst follow Christ Who became poor for our sake, from Whom having received the perfection of virtues, thou wast enriched with an incorruptible treasure: forget not us who are impoverished in good deeds, but from thy fullness grant unto us, O venerable father, who on this present day honor thy precious falling asleep.

*Glory: Tone 2:*
Having fled from the world and all voluptuousness, O venerable one, taking thy cross upon thy shoulder, thou didst follow Christ: having lived in a narrow cave, by the extreme sharpness of monastic labors, thou didst conquer him who formerly conquered thee; and after thy demise thou wast shown forth so terrible unto him, that he trembles even at thy name, unable to bear thy rebuke: for living upon it, thou drivest away the spirits of malice under heaven; drive them away also from us, O blessed Isaac, and ask for us great mercy from Christ God.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Save from perils thy servants, O all-pure Mother of God.
*Great Doxology, and dismissal.*
