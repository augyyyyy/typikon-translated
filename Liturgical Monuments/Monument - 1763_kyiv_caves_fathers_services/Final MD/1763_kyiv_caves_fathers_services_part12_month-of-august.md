# MONTH OF AUGUST

*On the 7th Day: Service to our Venerable Father Pimen the Much-Ailing of the Caves.*[^363]

*At Vespers, "Blessed is the man," the 1st antiphon. On "Lord, I have cried," Stichera, Tone 4. Podoben: "Joy of the heavenly ranks" (Небе́сныхъ чинѡ́въ):*[^364]
He Who foreknew thee from thy mother's womb unto beatitude, by His providence gave an image of salvation, permitting upon thee the sickness of Job, that being tried thereby as gold in the furnace, thy body *Service to the Venerable One*

...thou didst exhaust in much-ailing sickness; and now being in painless rest, remember us who are sick with spiritual passions, O most blessed father Pimen![^365]

Having hated the world, O venerable one, and life that drags down by carnal delight, thou didst ask of the Lord to exchange it for prolonged illness, wherein thou didst ever utter thanksgiving unto God, saying psalm-like: My soul hath cleaved after Thee, that Thy right hand may uphold me, O Christ, guiding me unto the right path and eternal life.[^366]

Unto thee, O venerable father Pimen, after the multitude of painful temptations which thou didst painlessly offer up in the body, consolations in the unapproachable Light now gladden thy soul; for thou dost stand before the throne of the Master with the angelic ranks, where pray for us also, that we may obtain peace from on high and deliverance from passions.[^367]

*Glory: Tone 5:*
Come, ye choirs of monastics, let us praise the venerable Pimen, who became an emulator of the great Anthony; for as he received the monastic habit from angels, so this one also was vouchsafed to be clothed in the monastic habit by an angel's hand, with whom now wearing incorruptible garments, he rejoices with the righteous, keeps festival with the venerable, and prays with them unto Christ God for our souls.[^368]

*Both now: Of the Feast.*[^369]
*Entrance, Prokeimenon of the day, and three readings (see fol. 30 on the reverse).*[^370]

*At the Aposticha, Stichera, Tone 4. Automela:*[^371]
The candle given unto thee by the angels prefigured the brightness of thy soul, O venerable father; therefore thy blameless life shone forth brighter than the sun before men. Which venerating, we magnify, and glorify the Heavenly Father with the Son and the Holy Spirit.[^372]

*Verse: Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of His venerable ones.*[^373]
Beholding the desire of thy heart and thy love toward heavenly things, the Sovereign King on high sent His angels unto thee, O father, to clothe thee in the monastic habit and to sweeten thee with heavenly singing, that having completed the course of this painful life with patient endurance, thou mightest obtain painless rest through sickness.[^374]

*Verse: Blessed is the man that fears the Lord, in His commandments he shall greatly delight.*[^375]
Renunciation of the world and poverty thou didst choose, blameless virginity, and measureless patient endurance *To Pimen the Much-Ailing*

...patient endurance, and perfect love toward God, are truly great virtues, wherewith as upon a swift-coursing chariot thou didst ascend unto heavenly things, and stand before the Creator of all and God; unto Whom pray for us also, O venerable one, that we may complete our course without stumbling, and obtain mercy in the day of judgment.[^376]

*Glory: Tone 8:*
We, the multitudes of monastics, run unto thy reliquary, O father; we bow down before thine incorrupt relics, we draw healings for diseases, we receive strength against the enemies that war against us, and find victory over our adversaries; forgiveness of sins is granted us, and we are vouchsafed the mercy of the Lord through thy prayers.[^377]

*Both now: Of the Feast.*

*Troparion, Tone 5:*[^378]
Emulating the humble-mindedness of the God-bearing Anthony, and likened in patient endurance unto Job, thou didst not seek healing from sickness, but rather its prolongation for thyself, O patient-spirited Pimen, lest having become healthy and torn away by thy parents from this place, thou shouldest be deprived of the fellowship of the venerable ones and of their rank; therefore beyond hope thou wast tonsured by the very angels, with whom pray unto Christ God to save our souls.

*Glory, both now: Of the Feast.*

*At Matins, on "God is the Lord," Troparion of the Feast. Glory: of the Venerable One. Both now: of the Feast.*
*After Kathisma 1, Sessional Hymn, Tone 5. Podoben: "The Word co-unoriginate" (Собеꙁнача́лное):*[^379]
Exceeding great patient endurance didst thou possess, O father: for lying upon the bed of sickness for many years, covered with sores from head to foot, thou didst give thanks unto God, choosing in this life to rot entirely, that in that other life thou mightest be without corruption, and enduring the perception of stench, that thou mightest obtain the fragrance of Christ; make us also patient, therefore, by thy prayers!

*Glory, both now: Of the Feast, or this Theotokion:*
Lustful poisons have encompassed me, and I lie upon the bed of mine evil deeds; but do thou who didst bear without pain, wither away my diseases, and raise me up unto virtues, nailing me whole by thy prayers.[^380]

*After Kathisma 2, Sessional Hymn, Tone 3. Podoben: "Speedily anticipate" (Ско́рѡ предварѝ):*[^381]
Speedily did God give ear unto thee when thou didst pray, O blessed one, and fulfilled the desire of thy heart, sending His angels to clothe thee in the angelic habit; therefore be thou also speedy unto help for us, and fulfill the desire of our heart, entreating the angelic *Service to the Venerable One*

...Master of the angels, have mercy upon us from on high by His compassion!

*Glory, both now: Of the Feast, or this Theotokion:*
Speedily give ear unto the supplications of thy servants, O Sovereign Lady, and anticipate to cover us with the shelter of thy wings, lest we be enslaved unto sin unto the end; but that we may zealously serve thy Son, our Lord, Who grants us great mercy.

*After the Polyeleos, Sessional Hymn, Tone 8. Podoben: "Having reclined on Wisdom" (Премꙋ́дрости возле́гъ):*[^382]
Thy many sicknesses, O blessed Pimen, became the cause of health for many; for ailing thyself, thou didst heal the sick; lying thyself in disease, thou didst raise up the bedridden; by a word thou didst drive away every weakness. Therefore now running unto thine incorrupt relics, we pray thee: Pour forth health for bodies, and grant healing for souls; entreat Christ God to grant forgiveness of sins unto them that lovingly honor thy holy memory![^383]

*Glory, both now: Of the Feast.*

*Canon to the Venerable One, Tone 2. Ode 1. Heirmos:*[^387]
In the deep of old, the overwhelming power laid low the whole host of Pharaoh; and the incarnate Word destroyed all-wicked sin, the supremely glorified Lord, for He is gloriously glorified!

*Troparia:*
Held fast by the depth of perplexity, I pray Thee, the Depth of Wisdom, O Jesus: Raise up my mind, and move my tongue, to praise the great patient endurance of Thy favored one Pimen!

In the depth of dispassion, O venerable one, thou didst drown the passions, and didst cross the sea of the world with dry feet, so that not even thy mind touched sinful corruption; from which preserve me also by thy prayers.[^388]

*Glory:*
In the depth of thy tears thou didst drown the noetic Pharaoh, and now in the heavenly haven thou dost sing the song of victory, where pray for me also, O venerable one!

*Both now: Theotokion:*
When I am drowned in the depth of evils, O Sovereign Lady, reach forth *To Pimen the Much-Ailing*

...reach forth a hand of help, even as thy Son did unto Peter, and lead me unto the haven of salvation.[^389]

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*[^390]
The wilderness blossomed as a lily, O Lord, the barren church of the Gentiles, by Thy coming, wherein my heart was established!

*Troparia:*
The Caves monastery blossomed with venerable fathers as a garden with fragrant lilies, and in their midst thou also, O venerable one, didst blossom as a lily, by holiness in life, and after thy dormition by incorruption.[^391]

Thou didst blossom by thy God-pleasing life as a flower of the field, O venerable one; and now thy holy relics in the cave send forth sweet fragrance as a lily of the valleys, and pour forth grace unto them that run unto thee with faith.[^392]

*Glory:*
A lily among thorns, Pimen blossomed in purity through the patient endurance of sicknesses; who, transplanted into the gardens of Paradise, now makes us fragrant by his prayers.[^393]

*Both now: Theotokion:*
O fragrant Flower, All-Holy Virgin: drive away the stench of my sin, and show me forth as a sweet fragrance of Christ by thy prayers.

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 2:*[^394]
Bold was thy prayer unto God, for when the Caves fathers prayed for thy healing, thou didst ask of the Lord an increase of sicknesses for thyself, and the supplication of thee alone overcame the prayers of all. We believe, therefore, that now also thy holy prayers can do much![^395]

*Glory, both now: Theotokion:*
A mother's prayer avails much unto the winning over of the Master; pray therefore unto Him for us, O Virgin Mother, and make known our sorrow unto Him, that having sent thee, He may be merciful unto us, and turning our sorrow into joy, grant eternal life.[^396]

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*[^397]
I sing of Thee, for by hearing, O Lord, I have heard and was afraid; for Thou didst come unto me, seeking me who was gone astray; therefore do I glorify Thy great condescension toward me, O Greatly-merciful One!

*Troparia:*
Grant us worthily to sing and glorify God, O venerable father, by thy prayers, that we may ever call thee blessed!

The fathers that were near heard angelic singing at midnight, when they clothed thee in the garment of salvation; wherewith vouchsafe us also to enjoy angelic songs *Service to the Venerable One*

...vouchsafe us to enjoy, by thy intercession, O blessed one!

*Glory:*
I sing unto thee fervently, O father; I call blessed, O venerable one, thy great innocence and thy meekness; I praise thy patient endurance, I marvel at thine incorruption, and I bow down before thy healing relics.[^398]

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Save thy singers, O All-Hymned Mother, delivering them from eternal weeping; by thy mercy wipe away every tear from mine eyes, and vouchsafe unto eternal joys.[^399]

*Ode 5. Heirmos:*[^400]
Thou hast become a Mediator between God and men, O Christ God; for through Thee, O Master, we have introduction unto the Source of Light, Thy Father, out of the night of ignorance.

*Troparia:*
By thine intercession, O father, preserve from every assault of the enemy this monastery, wherein thou didst live in a God-pleasing manner, and be a helper unto them that live as monastics, for we put our trust in thy prayers.[^401]

Be an intercessor for me unto the righteous Judge, O blessed one; for behold, I have been negligent even as that schema-monk whom thou didst show unto the brethren at thy dormition, who had the great habit but small deeds. Therefore his schema is kept incorrupt, unto his reproof and condemnation.[^402]

*Glory:*
Intercede for salvation for me who am in despair, O blessed one; guide a sinner unto repentance, O venerable one; grant amendment unto the perverse, O father, that without shame I may hope in thy prayers.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
O Mediatress unto the Creator, All-Pure Virgin: cleanse me who am wholly defiled by sins, through thy prayers; for placing all my hope in thee, I shall not be put to shame forever.

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*[^403]
Wallowing in the abyss of sin, I invoke the unfathomable abyss of Thy compassion: Lead me up from corruption, O God!

*Troparia:*
In the abyss of evils lies the whole world, for which cause thou didst renounce it, that thou mightest please God in goodness; unto Whom vouchsafe me also to be well-pleasing, O favored one of God![^404]

Lying in the abyss of grievous sicknesses, neglected many times and abandoned by them that served thee, thou didst have God alone visiting thee; leave us not, O father, who are in sorrow, destitute of thy prayers, for we are continually ailing.[^405]

*To Pimen the Much-Ailing*

*Glory:*
Lest I be condemned unto the abyss of Hades by reason of the multitude of my sins, do thou, O father, stand before the righteous Judge, and entreat Him to deliver me from His righteous judgment.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
As thou hast borne the Abyss of mercy, exhaust the abysses of my measureless iniquities, O Mother of compassion, I pray thee.

*Kontakion, Tone 6:*[^406]
Come, having assembled together, let us praise Pimen the patient-enduring, who thankfully endured prolonged sickness; for he from whom men once fled because of the stench in his illness, now pours forth sweet fragrance from his relics unto them that approach, and drives away the foul stench of passions from them that cry: Come unto us, O Pimen, and the enemy shall accomplish nothing against us!

*Ikos:*[^407]
Ye that are held fast by diverse sicknesses and passions, having gathered together unto Pimen the much-ailing, marvel at his patient endurance! For remaining many times hungry and thirsty, he grieved not against them that served him, but with joy endured all things, giving thanks unto God; and having spent twenty years in his suffering, he was tried as gold in the furnace, and was found worthy before God, and received from Him the gift to heal infirmities and to drive away passions from them that run unto him with faith. Unto him, therefore, let us also run, and invoke him, crying: Come unto us, O Pimen, and the enemy shall accomplish nothing against us!

*Ode 7. Heirmos:*[^408]
The god-opposing command of the lawless tyrant raised up a high flame; but Christ spread forth spiritual dew unto the pious youths, He Who is blessed and supremely glorified!

*Troparia:*
Thou didst hate the god-opposing world, O father, knowing Him Who said: Whosoever would be a friend of the world becomes an enemy of God; grant us therefore also to flee the world and all its lusts.[^409]

The god-opposing law of mine evil custom forcibly draws me unto evil deeds, and what I would, that I do not, but what I would not, that do I commit; do thou therefore, O Pimen, deliver me from it by thy prayers.[^410]

*Glory:*
The god-opposing command of reigning sin in my members *Service to the Venerable One*

...in my members compels me often to anger God; unto thee do I flee, O father: deliver me from compulsive sins!

*Both now: Theotokion:*
O Virgin who didst bear God: grant us to fulfill the commandments of thy Son, and let us in nothing be found opposed unto Him, by thy help.

*Ode 8. Heirmos:*[^411]
The fiery furnace of old in Babylon divided its operation by the divine command, scorching the Chaldeans, but bedewing the faithful, who sang: Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord!

*Troparia:*
That thou mightest quench the furnace of passions, O father, thou didst ask of God an increase of sicknesses, wherein bodily refreshment was granted thee; which grant us also to obtain by thy prayers, that with thee we may sing unto the All-Pure God: Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord!

Though the furnace of many years of sickness scorched thy body, yet it could in no way harm the mind of thy soul; for in all these things that befell thee thou didst not sin, but remaining unconsumed in the midst of temptations, as in the midst of flame, thou didst sing with the children: Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord![^412]

*We bless the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit: the Lord!*
As in a furnace, I have kindled the fire of lusts in my flesh, and multiplied the flame of impurity by mine intemperance; O God Who sendest down dew, cool the flame, quench the fire, and bedew the furnace of my passions by the prayers of Thy favored one Pimen, that with him I may sing unto Thee, God glorified in the Trinity: Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord!

*Both now: Theotokion:*
The fiery furnace of old that did not scorch the youths prefigured thy childbirth, O All-Pure Lady; for having conceived the Divine Fire, thou didst remain unconsumed; and I therefore pray thee, O Sovereign Lady: preserve me unburnt by the adverse fire, that praying I may sing unto thee: Sing unto the Lord, all ye works of the Lord, and supremely exalt His all-holy name unto the ages!

*Ode 9. Heirmos:*[^413]
The Son of the unoriginate Begetter, God and Lord, incarnate of the Virgin, hath appeared unto us, to enlighten the darkened and to gather together the dispersed; therefore the All-Hymned Theotokos do we magnify!

*Troparia:*
O Son of the unoriginate Begetter: we bring unto Thee in prayer Thy favored one Pimen, who from the beginning of his monastic life loved Thee with all his soul *To Pimen the Much-Ailing*

...loved Thee with all his soul and with all his strength served Thee; look upon his ascetical struggles, and for their sake hear his supplication for us, and grant us a good end!

O Son, co-unoriginate with the Father and the Spirit: we present unto Thee in prayer Thy good and faithful servant Pimen; look upon his prolonged and grievous sicknesses, and for their sake have mercy upon us at his supplication, and be merciful unto us!

*Glory:*
O Holy Spirit, co-ever-existing with the Father and the Son: we offer unto Thee the fervent prayers of the blessed Pimen; give ear unto him, and for his sake have mercy upon us!

*Both now: Theotokion:*
O Mother of God, with the blessed Pimen and with all the venerable Caves fathers who pleased God: pray unto the All-Holy and Undivided Trinity to have mercy upon Thy servants who continually glorify thee.

*Exapostilarion:*[^414]
The bridal chamber of the Savior is open unto Thee, O venerable Pimen; for thou hast the wedding garment of the angelic habit laid upon Thee by angels, wherein having entered into the marriage supper of the Lamb, forget not us who honor Thee!

*Glory, both now: Of the Feast, or this Theotokion:*
O undefiled Bridal Chamber, All-Pure Virgin: cover us who have no wedding garment with thy precious robe, and lead us into the heavenly bridal chamber!

*At the Praises, Stichera, Tone 8. Podoben: "O marvelous wonder" (Ѽ пресла́внагѡ чꙋдесѐ):*[^415]
O marvelous wonder! O marvelous operation of the Holy Spirit upon thee, which wrought in thy infirm flesh, O venerable one! For while ailing thyself, thou didst heal others, and didst lead them unto amendment by thy patient endurance. Therefore show thy mercy unto them that praise thee, O father, and entreat of Christ God healing in our infirmities, for them that lovingly honor thy holy dormition!

O God-wise father Pimen: from childhood thou didst give thyself over unto sicknesses through prayer, and by grievous sufferings didst wear away thy body, that thou mightest preserve the purity of the flesh and present thy soul blameless unto God; standing before Whom in painless life, as one able to assist by thy prayer, pray continually for us who sing with faith of thy holy falling asleep!

*Service to the Venerable One*

O venerable father Pimen: let everyone that ails in this prolonged life learn to emulate thy patient endurance, and send up thanksgiving unto God, from Whom we receive trials as chastisement for the correction of spiritual sicknesses; but be an advocate for all that run unto the reliquary of thy relics, and entreat of Christ God deliverance from spiritual and bodily infirmity!

*Glory: Tone 2:*
A truly mystical myrrh, unto the healing of the sick, does the reliquary of thy holy relics pour forth, making fragrant them that approach with faith, granting them rest in necessities; grant therefore unto us also, O all-blessed father, from the ever-flowing fountain of thy love, deliverance from spiritual and bodily illnesses, as we praise thy holy falling asleep with the beauties of hymns!

*Month of August, on the 11th Day:*
*Service to our venerable fathers Theodore and Basil of the Caves.*
*At Vespers, "Blessed is the man," the 1st antiphon. On "Lord, I have cried," Stichera to the Venerable Theodore, Tone 6. Podoben: "Having laid all aside" (Всѐ ѿло́жше):*[^416]

...being enriched by the light of grace, thou didst shine forth in the world as the sun; and now standing with boldness before the Master, enlighten with the rays of thy radiant gifts them that sing of thy memory!

Desiring to receive noetic riches, thou didst disregard sensory wealth, and delivering it into the hands of the poor, thou didst choose in poverty to follow Christ, Who became poor for our sakes; and taking His cross upon thy shoulder, thou didst traverse the narrow way, and after a blessed end didst inherit spacious places, where dwelling with the angels, pray for them that praise thee, O most blessed Theodore![^417]

And now dwelling in unapproachable light, remember us also who celebrate thy honorable dormition![^418]

Bound together by a firm bond of love, O most blessed fathers, ye were found even in this life as a strong city, which the deceitful ministers of cruel Belial could not undermine; and feigning God-pleasing counsel, they fell wounded, while ye sang in praise: Our power and strength is Jesus, the Lover of mankind and Savior of our souls![^422]

...conquered the demonic hosts, and having cleansed your robes in the blood of your sufferings, ye entered into the heavenly bridal chamber; whence forget us not, but be compassionate toward us, asking for us from Christ God peace and great mercy![^423]
