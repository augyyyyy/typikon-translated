A native of Smolensk, Prokhor received tonsure at the Kyiv-Pechersk Monastery and adopted an extreme rule of ascetic fasting, gathering wild orache (chenopodium) during the summer, grinding it into flour, and baking bread without ever tasting normal wheat or rye bread. When the merchant guild created an artificial salt monopoly and salt became unavailable, he gathered ashes from the cells and distributed them to the poor, where they miraculously turned into pure white salt. Prince Svyatopolk, deeply moved by these miracles, repented of his extortions and vowed never to harm the monastery; upon Prokhor's repose in 1107, the prince himself carried the saint's body to the caves for burial.

A wealthy Kievan who gave away his wealth and took monastic tonsure at the Caves, Eustratius was taken captive during the Polovtsian raid on Kyiv in 1096 and sold into slavery with fifty other Christians to a Jewish merchant in Chersonesus (Korsun).
