# MONTH OF APRIL

*On the 8th Day: Service to our Father among the Saints, Niphont, Bishop of Novgorod.*[^174]

*At Vespers, on "Lord, I have cried," Stichera, Tone 4. Podoben: "As one valiant" (Ꙗ҆́кѡ до́блѧ):*[^175]
The adornment of hierarchs and zealot of piety, come, ye people, let us honor today with songs, weaving praiseful crowns with love *To Niphont, Bishop of Novgorod*

...and bowing before the shrine of the relics of the worthily-blessed father, with suppliant voices let us cry aloud: O holy hierarch of Christ, Niphont, as having boldness toward God, forget not, diligently praying, ever to intercede for thy flock.

The tongue of thy honey-flowing lips, O most blessed teacher Niphont, being the pen of the Holy Spirit, cast down the pride of them that hate thee: and now smite the enemies that rise up in pride against thy flock, and strengthening us in orthodox dogmas, save us by thine intercession, that with a pure conscience we may offer praise unto God concerning thee in love.

Illuminated by the light of God's grace, Thou didst shine forth like the sun in the world, and with the rays of thy virtues didst enlighten many lands; therefore by direction from on high, Thou wast chosen by all with one accord as shepherd unto the rational flock; for which also after thy departure diligently pray, that it may be preserved unassailed by enemies.

*Glory: Tone 2:*
Come, ye assemblies of the Orthodox of Novgorod, keeping festival today in the yearly memory of the most blessed father, having formed a choir with us in love, let us give glory unto God the Deliverer, for He gave unto us a shepherd and teacher and intercessor for our souls.

*Both now: Theotokion, or Stavrotheotokion.*
*At the Aposticha, in the Triodion.*
*Glory: Tone 4:*
Having made thy heart a temple for the Holy Spirit, thou didst erect houses of prayer for the increase of God's glory, which having splendidly adorned, thou didst gather thy rational sheep as into a heavenly sheepfold, and didst set before them the pasture of thy sweet and God-inspired teaching, instructing and guiding them unto the path of piety and salvation; whereon also direct us by thy prayers, O venerable father, that we may ever glorify the one God, and Savior of our souls.

*Both now: Theotokion, or Stavrotheotokion.*

*Troparion, Tone 3:*
As by disposition a true defender of the Orthodox traditions of the God-bearing Church, thou wast vouchsafed to see Theodosius the great among the fathers after his death, as alive, announcing unto thee the inheritance of the Heavenly Kingdom, whose ineffable goods thou now inseparably enjoyest with him; pray also for the salvation of thy children, O most praised Niphont.[^176]

*Glory, both now: Theotokion.*

*Service to the Hierarch*

*Canon to the Hierarch, Tone 1. Ode 1. Heirmos:*[^177]
Thy victorious right hand has been divinely glorified in power, O Immortal One: for being almighty, it shattered adversaries, fashioning a new path through the deep for the Israelites.

*Troparia:*
May Thy right hand, O my Christ, guide me on this present day to sing the memory of Thy servant, driving away the enemies that hinder me, and renewing the path grown old with passions within my heart.

Having sent the grace of Thy Comforter unto Thy servant, O Christ, Thou didst call him to run after Thee: who immediately renounced the world and loved Thy law, renewing the path unto Thy love.

Guided by Thine arm, O Lord, Thy venerable one came unto the untroubled haven, into the most honorable Caves Monastery of Thy Mother, and became a monk, living the monastic life in virtuous conduct.

*Glory:*
Far and wide did thy virtue shine forth, O father, seeing which many magnified thee, and glorified the Heavenly Father Who gave unto thee such grace; of which let not us also be deprived, through thy righteous deeds, O father.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Be thou my guide, O Virgin Bride of God, leading me upon the path of salvation, and renewing my soul that is grown old with passions, O Mary, by thy compassions.

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*[^178]
O Thou Who alone knowest the weakness of human nature, and didst mercifully take form therein: gird me with power from on high, that I may cry aloud unto Thee: Holy art Thou, O animate temple of Thine ineffable glory, O Lover of mankind!

*Troparia:*
Seeing the one right way, to take up Thy cross in this life and to follow Thee his Master, Thy faithful servant immediately hastened eagerly thereto: therefore now he is satisfied with Thine ineffable glory, O Lover of mankind.

Unceasingly sending up thy prayers unto the one God from a pure heart, thou didst break the wiles of the feeble enemy, striking him down with the spear of faith; therefore, O hierarch, thou hast received the crown of glory from Christ the Lover of mankind.

Soaring on high through vigilance and fasting, thou didst not choose to incline thy mind below unto fleeting things, and through such action didst acquire the vision of God's glory *To Niphont, Bishop of Novgorod*

...which that we also may obtain, pray thou unto Jesus the Lover of mankind.

*Glory:*
Thy life shone forth before men, so that seeing thy virtues, O father, they chose thee for the archiepiscopal throne of Novgorod, unto which thou wast also a shepherd, leading thy sheep unto Jesus the Chief Shepherd, the Lover of mankind.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
O pure Mother of the Chief Shepherd: guide me, a straying sheep, unto the right path, and direct my life, that guided by thee I may be vouchsafed to be filled with the glory of Jesus the Lover of mankind.

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 4:*[^179]
Having labored with great zeal for Orthodoxy, thou didst multiply the glory of God in thy flock; now therefore, being glorified where the voice of them that feast is unceasing, pray for them that honor thy memory, O Niphont, most sacred father.

*Glory, both now: Theotokion:*
As thou didst bear the Lord of glory Who promised to deliver us from inglorious standing: vouchsafe us to be partakers of the glory of the saints, and glorify them that honor thy childbirth, O pure one.

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*[^180]
Beholding with foresighted eyes thee as a mountain overshadowed by the grace of God, Habakkuk proclaimed that the Holy One of Israel should come forth from thee, for our salvation and renewal.

*Troparia:*
Them that walk upon the mountains of high-mindedness thou didst humble, O blessed one, not permitting them to wage internecine strife; but thou didst bless them with peace, bringing them unto salvation and the renewal of love.[^181]

Following the apostolic words, in season and out of season thou didst press urgently, O father, with all thy might striving that thy sheep should not depart from the commandments of the Lord, and from the canonical traditions of His Church, for these are for our salvation and renewal.

Who can recount thy boldness, O hierarch? For thou didst speak of the testimonies of the Lord before princes, not being ashamed before the diadem; therefore thou wast a second Forerunner, rebuking lawless deeds.[^182]

*Glory:*
The princes who gave over their body not unto Christ but unto fornication through an unlawful marriage, thou didst boldly rebuke, apostolically commanding thy church and all the clergy to turn away from such as walk disorderly.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Reclaim me who live lawlessly, and the perverted *Service to the Hierarch*

...passion-storms in me humble, O Mother of God, that being freed from them through thee, I may turn back: for thou art the only salvation and renewal of us all.

*Ode 5. Heirmos:*[^183]
O Christ Who didst enlighten the ends of the world with the radiance of Thy coming, and illuminated them with Thy Cross: enlighten with the light of Thy divine knowledge the hearts of them that sing of Thee in orthodox manner.

*Troparia:*
As a second Theodore the Studite,[^184] a hierarch zealot, the Church sings of thee, O blessed one, for thy boldness and confession, and calls unto spiritual rejoicing them that lovingly praise thee, O father.

Thou didst withstand them that walk uncanonically, O venerable one, in nowise consenting that without the blessing of the Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople a hierarch should be advanced to the throne, and an ungodly wolf be given as shepherd unto the sheep that piously glorify Christ.[^185]

Worthy of honor art thou, O father, for when they elevated Clement to the dignity of the hierarch's office by the head of Saint Clement,[^186] thou didst not consent, but rather, having rebuked the men-pleasers, didst condemn their council.

*Glory:*
Being a true son of the eastern ecumenical throne, thou didst not consent to abandon its primacy for destructive councils, but didst worthily maintain that consecration must be confirmed from the beginning, that through the guilelessness of its natural shepherd, the Patriarch of Constantinople, all things should be done.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
O thou who didst crown the human race with blessing: vouchsafe unto us the blessing of the chosen, and grant unto them that in right faith praise thee to behold the glory of thy most sweet Son.

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*[^187]
The lowest abyss has surrounded us, and there is none to deliver; we are accounted as sheep for the slaughter: save Thy people, O our God, for Thou art the strength of the weak and their restoration.

*Troparia:*
The hierarch who hated righteousness surrounded thee with malice, for thou didst not communicate with him, since he had unrighteously usurped the throne; but thou, O father, wast not terrified of him, having hope in God, Who is the strength of the weak and their restoration.

Scheming to restrain thee who couldst not be restrained, O blessed one, the hierarch plotted with the prince to send thee into exile; and he restrained thy person, but could not restrain thy tongue, for thou didst rebuke him for unlawfully usurping the throne.[^188]

Thy words were a sword unto the lawless, wherewith thou didst cut down their frenzy; for this reason they hated thee *To Niphont, Bishop of Novgorod*

...they hated thee, and separated thee from the throne; but thou didst not lose heart, O father, hoping for the beatitude of them that are persecuted for righteousness' sake.[^189]

*Glory:*
Thy rebuke, O hierarch Niphont, was a wound unto the ungodly, for it consumed them as transgressors of the law; and their hearts swelled, gnashing their teeth, wishing to tear thee to pieces.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Noetic beasts have surrounded us, gnashing their teeth and rejoicing at our ruin; but do thou, O Virgin mighty in strength, break their jaws, and strengthen us to stand against them: for thou art the strength of the weak and their restoration.

*Kontakion, Tone 5:*[^190]
Emulating the zeal of the ancient fathers in the steadfast keeping of true dogmas, O firm-minded Niphont, thou didst not consent to concelebrate with Clement who arbitrarily usurped the throne of the metropolis; but submitting to the one chosen for it by all,[^191] thou didst serve with him in sacred splendor, and having ended thy life, didst find the unending Kingdom of Christ.

*Ode 7. Heirmos:*[^192]
Thee, O Mother of God, we the faithful perceive as a noetic furnace: for as the Supremely Exalted One saved the three youths, so the Almighty One renewed the world in thy womb, the praised and supremely glorified God of our fathers.

*Troparia:*
Thou, O most blessed father, dwelling in the most marvelous monastery as in a heavenly bridal chamber, rejoicing, didst offer thanksgiving unto the King of all and unto her most sweet Son, singing: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!

Having stood boldly for Orthodoxy, O venerable one, thou wast deprived of thy throne by transgressors of the law; but a throne was prepared for thee in the heavenly sheepfold, as for a true witness of righteousness.

When the Chief Shepherd saw thy sheep troubled by thy quieting, He gave thee unto them again as shepherd; for he that worked evil, by the righteous judgment of God, himself received double evil, and he that removed thee from the throne himself fell from the throne.

*Glory:*
The pious Prince George,[^193] having righteously driven Izyaslav from the throne of the principality, released thee with great honor unto thy throne; whereon seeing thee, O venerable father, thy sheep cried aloud with joy: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!

*Both now: Theotokion:*
The human race which had righteously fallen from God's grace thou didst raise up by thy most pure childbirth, O Mother of God; therefore joyfully we sing unto thee who art blessed throughout all generations.

*Service to the Hierarch*

*Ode 8. Heirmos:*[^194]
In the furnace the Israelite youths, as in a smelter, shone purer than gold with the beauty of piety, saying: Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord; sing and supremely exalt Him unto all ages.

*Troparia:*
When the Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople heard that thou wast unburned in the furnace of temptations and in the flame of flattery of the transgressors of the law, he called thy courage blessed, O father, and numbered thee among the ancient confessors, with whom thou now singest of the Lord unto ages.[^195]

Seeing thy boldness written in the book of the living, the Patriarch of Constantinople sent his written epistle unto thee, wherein he called blessed thy zeal for Orthodoxy, which remembering, the sons of Rus' also supremely exalt the Lord unto ages.

Kindled with double desire, O hierarch, thou didst come in haste unto the city of Kyiv, desiring to bow before the Mother of God in the Caves Monastery and before the venerable fathers, and likewise to receive a blessing from the coming hierarch; therefore thou wast vouchsafed to see a marvelous vision, how Saint Theodosius said unto thee: From now on thou shalt be inseparably with us, singing of the Lord unto ages.

*Glory: We bless the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit: the Lord.*
Like a fledgling the swallow's nest built on the hill of the Caves by the appointed dove,[^196] lovingly didst thou desire to abide therein without departing; and though such desire drew thee, yet God's providence prepared Thy departure from temporal things unto eternal.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
A fledgling am I born in thy sheepfold, O my Lady, yet I have fallen down, ensnared by demonic assaults; yet I pray thee, bring me again into it: that where my beginning was, there also I may receive a good end, and with the holy fathers supremely exalt thy Son unto ages.

*Ode 9. Heirmos:*[^197]
The unburnt bush showed forth an image of thy pure childbirth: and now we pray thee to extinguish the furnace of temptations against us, that we may unceasingly magnify thee, O Mother of God.

*Troparia:*
By the image of a marvelous vision the Master of all assured thee, O father, that after thy labors thou shouldest depart unto peaceful places, where the angelic hosts unceasingly glorify the All-Holy Trinity, Whom we also with them magnify.

Brought down into a light sleep, O father, thou didst seem to stand in the Caves church, stretching forth supplication unto the Most Pure Mother of God, that thou mightest see the face of blessed Theodosius; and it came to pass even as thou didst desire.

According to thy desire, in the vision one of the blessed took thee, O venerable one, and brought thee unto blessed *To Niphont, Bishop of Novgorod*

...Theodosius; seeing whom joyfully, thou didst fall at his feet, and wast vouchsafed a blessing; held by desire of whom, we also magnify the All-Holy Trinity.

*Glory:*
O most sweet voice! O good greeting of our father! For he said: Henceforth thou shalt be with us; and he showed thee a scroll wherein was written: Behold, I and the children whom God has given me.[^198] That we also may hear this voice, we pray unto the Mother of God, and ever with thee, O father, we magnify Him.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Rejoice, O scroll, most pure Virgin, for in thee by the finger of God the Pre-eternal Word was inscribed; do thou write us also upon the scroll with thy servants: ever with the father we pray unto thee, and with songs magnify thee.

*Exapostilarion:*
Having lived angelically upon earth, thou didst appear as a companion of the angels; for having been translated from lower things, thou didst depart unto the highest, where standing together before the God of all, pray for them that honor thy memory, O blessed Niphont.

*Glory, both now: Theotokion:*
O thou that art higher than the heavenly ranks: depart not from us on earth, but ever be with us, delivering us from all assaults: for while thy help is steadfastly with us, no one shall rise up against us.

*At the Praises, Stichera, Tone 4:*
On the auspicious day of the memory of the God-bearing father, come, ye people named after Christ, let us leap for joy, offering praiseful songs, and regaling ourselves with spiritual sweetness, in love let us cry aloud: O Niphont, most sacred father, pray unto Christ God for the salvation of our souls.

We bring unto thee, O venerable father, on this present day songful gifts in love, remembering the achievements of thy virtues, and we pray unto Christ God, that having escaped harmful thoughts, we may direct our life in good deeds, and not be deprived of heavenly honors through thy prayers.

Who can worthily praise Thy holy deeds, O God? For being wounded with Thy love, they despised all earthly things, and being in the flesh, conquered the bodiless enemy, and now rejoice with the bodiless powers in heaven; of whose choir vouchsafe us also to be partakers, through the prayers of Thy teacher.

*Service to the Hierarch*

*Glory: Tone 6:*
The adornment of hierarchs and glory of fathers, the wellspring of wonders and speedy helper of the faithful, come, ye feast-loving people today, having assembled in the most honorable temple let us hymn with praises, saying: Rejoice, O good ascetic! Rejoice, O watchful shepherd of the rational sheep! Rejoice, thou that didst drive away the noetic wolf from thy flock by the rod of faith, and didst lead it wholly unto thy Chief Shepherd; unto Whom ever pray for us, that we may be preserved unharmed from all enemies, and may become heirs of eternal life.

*Both now: Theotokion: O Mother of God, thou art the true vine:*

*Great Doxology, and dismissal.*
*At the Liturgy, the common service to a Hierarch.*

*(Woodcut Engraving: Saint Simon, Bishop of Vladimir and Suzdal (*С: Сїмо́нъ Епⷭ҇кпъ Владимирескій и Сꙋждалскій*), depicted standing in episcopal vestments with sakkos, omophorion, and mitre, blessing with his right hand and holding a pastoral crosier in his left; a dove representing the Holy Spirit descends from heaven above; in the background on the left is the Dormition Cathedral of Vladimir and monastic dwellings, with clergy and people in procession on the right.)*[^199]

### Notes

[^174]: Feast of our Father among the Saints Niphont, Bishop of Novgorod, commemorated on April 8 (O.S.). Also commemorated on the Synaxis of the Near Caves (September 28) and the Synaxis of the Novgorod Hierarchs (February 10).
[^175]: Podoben model incipit: Slavonic 'Ꙗ҆́кѡ до́блѧ' (Tone 4), canonical Byzantine Greek 'Ὡς γενναῖον ἐν μάρτυσιν'.
[^176]: Paterikon Discourse 14 recounts that three days before his death in the Kyiv Caves, St. Niphont had a marvelous vision of St. Theodosius of the Caves, who appeared to him holding a scroll and welcoming him into the heavenly habitations.
[^177]: Heirmos of Ode 1 (Canon to St. Niphont): Exodus 15:6, 15:1–19 (The Song of Moses at the Red Sea).
[^178]: Heirmos of Ode 3 (Canon to St. Niphont): 1 Samuel 2:1–10 (1 Kings 2:1–10 LXX; Song of Hannah) and Habakkuk 3:3.
[^179]: Sessional Hymn to St. Niphont, Tone 4.
[^180]: Heirmos of Ode 4 (Canon to St. Niphont): Habakkuk 3:3, 3:1–19 (The Prayer of Habakkuk).
[^181]: St. Niphont repeatedly reconciled warring Rus' princes (including the princes of Kyiv, Chernihiv, and Novgorod in 1135 and 1141), preventing bloodshed and defending civil peace.
[^182]: Psalm 118:46 (Psalm 119:46 LXX). St. Niphont courageously rebuked Prince Svyatoslav Olgovich of Novgorod when the prince sought to contract an uncanonical marriage, refusing to crown him or allow his clergy to perform the ceremony.
[^183]: Heirmos of Ode 5 (Canon to St. Niphont): Isaiah 26:9.
[^184]: St. Theodore the Studite (759–826), renowned champion of ecclesiastical independence and defender of the sacred canons against imperial and uncanonical ecclesiastical usurpations.
[^185]: In 1147, Grand Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich convened a council of Rus' bishops in Kyiv to elevate Clement Smolyatich as Metropolitan of Kyiv without the confirmation or blessing of the Patriarch of Constantinople. St. Niphont firmly refused to participate or recognize Clement, defending the canonical order and unity of the Church.
[^186]: To bypass the requirement of patriarchal consecration, Onuphrius, Bishop of Chernihiv, proposed consecrating Clement Smolyatich using the relic of the head of St. Clement of Rome, preserved in Kyiv. St. Niphont protested that this was entirely uncanonical and contrary to apostolic tradition.
[^187]: Heirmos of Ode 6 (Canon to St. Niphont): Jonah 2:1–10; Psalm 43:23 (Psalm 44:22 LXX).
[^188]: Prince Izyaslav detained St. Niphont in Kyiv and confined him to the Kyiv Caves Monastery, forbidding him to return to his see in Novgorod until Prince Yuri Dolgoruky took Kyiv.
[^189]: Matthew 5:10.
[^190]: Kontakion to St. Niphont, Tone 5.
[^191]: Reference to Constantine I, consecrated Metropolitan of Kyiv by the Patriarch of Constantinople in 1156, whom St. Niphont was traveling to meet when he fell ill and reposed in the Kyiv Caves.
[^192]: Heirmos of Ode 7 (Canon to St. Niphont): Daniel 3:26–56 (The Three Holy Children in the Furnace).
[^193]: Prince Yuri Vladimirovich Dolgoruky, Grand Prince of Kyiv (1149–1151, 1155–1157), who drove out Prince Izyaslav Mstislavich and restored St. Niphont with great honor to the see of Novgorod.
[^194]: Heirmos of Ode 8 (Canon to St. Niphont): Daniel 3:57–88 (The Song of the Three Holy Children).
[^195]: Patriarch Michael II Kourkouas (or his successor Nicholas IV Mouzalon) sent an encyclical to Rus' praising Bishop Niphont's unbending fortitude, likening him to the ancient holy fathers.
[^196]: An allegorical allusion to the Great Church of the Kyiv Caves Monastery, revealed by the Mother of God (the mystical Dove) to St. Anthony and St. Theodosius.
[^197]: Heirmos of Ode 9 (Canon to St. Niphont): Exodus 3:2 (The Burning Bush).
[^198]: Isaiah 8:18; Hebrews 2:13; Paterikon Discourse 14.
[^199]: Woodcut engraving of Saint Simon, Bishop of Vladimir and Suzdal (*С: Сїмо́нъ Епⷭ҇кпъ Владимирескій и Сꙋждалскій*), principal author of the Kyiv-Pechersk Paterikon (Discourses 1–13, his Epistle to St. Polycarp). Tonsured at the Kyiv Caves, he was appointed igumen of the Nativity Monastery in Vladimir and in 1214 was consecrated the first Bishop of Vladimir and Suzdal. Despite his exalted episcopal rank, he maintained fervent devotion to the Kyiv Caves, declaring in his famous epistle: "I would rather be swept out as dung in the house of the Mother of God, and be trodden upon by men, or be one of the poor before the gates of that holy Great Church of the Caves, than possess this fleeting glory." He reposed in 1226; his holy relics rest in the Near Caves of the Lavra.
