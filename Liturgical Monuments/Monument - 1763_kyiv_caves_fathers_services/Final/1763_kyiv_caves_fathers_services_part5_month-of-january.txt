# MONTH OF JANUARY

*On the 8th Day: Service to our Venerable Father Gregory the Wonderworker of the Caves.*[^97]

*At Vespers, "Blessed is the man," 1st Antiphon. On "Lord, I have cried," Stichera on 6, Tone 4. Podoben: "O all-praised martyrs" (Прехвали́нїи мч҃нцы):*[^98]
O father Gregory, inflamed with the fire of love toward God, thou didst burn up the passions that extinguish the spirit; whence, living in infirm flesh, thou didst attain unto the angelic life, with whom now dwelling, pray thou unto Christ God to grant peace and great mercy unto our souls *To Gregory the Wonderworker of the Caves*

...to grant peace and great mercy unto our souls.

O father Gregory, attending unto the words of David, thou gavest no rest unto thy body, nor slumber unto thine eyelids, until thou wast cleansed from passions, and receiving the ladder of divine vision, didst ascend unto the purest light, where now living, pray thou to grant peace and great mercy unto our souls.

O father Gregory, the working of thy wonders traversed the whole land of Rus, like a loud-voiced trumpet crying out and calling together the feast-loving people to praise thine honorable memory: with whom we also bring praise, and bow down before the shrine of thy relics, praying to obtain through thee peace and great mercy.

*Glory, Tone 8:*
Honoring thy guided life, O host of monastics, we marvel, and glorify God Who has glorified thee: for He granted thee manifold wonderworking, wherewith thou didst drive away demons from men, and didst lead sinners unto correction. Whom also entreat for us that honor the day of thine honorable falling asleep, to grant mercy in the day of judgment.

*Both now: Of the Feast, Tone 1:*
The hand of the Baptist trembled when it touched Thy most pure head; the Jordan River turned back, not daring to minister unto Thee: for if it was awed by Joshua the son of Nun, how should it not be fearful of its Creator? But Thou didst fulfill all the dispensation, O our Savior, that Thou mightest save the world by Thy manifestation, O only Lover of mankind!

*Entrance: Prokeimenon of the day, and readings: (see fol. 30v).*[^99]

*At the Aposticha, Stichera, Tone 6. Podoben: "On the third day" (Трїдне́венъ):*
Our nature, decayed by passions, the Master renews, having descended into the waters: and thee, O father, who didst come unto the streams of water, the Lover of mankind renewed, having translated thee unto eternal life.
*Verse:* The righteous man shall be glad in the Lord, and shall hope in Him.
Emulating Christ Who renewed human nature by water and the Spirit, O venerable one, therefore for the sake of the truth thou wast drowned in the water; having finished which course, now thou rejoicest with Christ beside the waters of rest.
*Verse:* The righteous man shall be glad in the Lord, and shall hope in Him.
Standing again in the streams of Jordan, the Master looses the human race from bonds *Service to the Venerable Father*

...from the bonds of hades: Who also loosed thee with water, O blessed one, when He led forth thy spirit from the prison of the flesh.

*Glory, Tone 5:*
Thy life, adorned with the splendor of divine virtues, causes the land of Rus to marvel; and the shrine of thy holy relics emits beams of wonders unto all who approach with faith. Grant therefore unto us also a sign of thy love; for though thou hast departed, yet spiritually thou ever abidest, and beholding the warm supplication of all, by thy prayers thou grantest grace and great mercy from Christ God.

*Both now: Of the Feast, Tone 1:*
Beholding Thee coming unto him to cleanse the sin of the world in the river and with fire, the Baptist was struck with awe and trembled, crying aloud and saying: I dare not lay hold upon Thy most pure head: sanctify Thou me, O Master, by Thy divine manifestation, O only Lover of mankind!

*Troparion, Tone 3:*
O receiver of great gifts, and by the power of God worker of great wonders, O most marvelous father Gregory: as thou hast great boldness toward Christ God, unceasingly pray unto Him for us, that He may grant remission of sins unto our souls.
*Glory, both now: Of the Feast.*

*At Matins, on "God is the Lord": Troparion of the Feast, twice. Glory: Of the Saint. Both now: Of the Feast.*
*After the 1st Sticology, Sessional Hymn, Tone 1. Podoben: "Quickly anticipate" (Ско́ро предварѝ):*
Desiring to inherit the blessedness of them that fear the Lord, in deed Thou didst fulfill the commandments of God: all thy substance that Thou hadst, Thou didst disperse unto the glory of God, even unto them that deceitfully demanded it, O God-wise Gregory, most rich in virtues: therefore thy memory and thy righteousness abide unto ages.
*Glory, both now: Of the Feast.*

*After the 2nd Sticology, Sessional Hymn, Tone 3. Podoben: "At the beauty of virginity" (Красотѣ̀ дѣ́вства):*
When Thou, the Good One, camest unto Jordan, O Christ, the servant who faithfully fled unto Thee and served in righteousness, drowned by command of the prince, entered into unquenchable life: having whom as a swift intercessor before Thy majesty, Thee our God do we glorify in all things.
*Glory, both now: Of the Feast.*

*After the Polyeleos, Sessional Hymn, Tone 8. Podoben: "Wisdom" (Премꙋ́дрости):*
Desiring the blessedness of the righteous and choir-standing with the angelic ranks, despising all things in the world, thou didst take no thought even for life itself: therefore thou didst not feel ashamed before the soul of the prince, but *To Gregory the Wonderworker of the Caves*

...but didst admonish him as a father, leading him unto repentance: by whom innocently drowned in the water, through fire and water thou didst depart unto Him that clothes Himself with waters, and Thou, O God, didst lead him out into Thy rest: where now living, pray that we also who honor with love thy holy memory may not be deprived thereof, O Gregory worthy of wonder.

*Glory, both now: Of the Feast.*

*Canon of the Feast, with the heirmos, on 6; and of the Venerable, on 6, Tone 2.*
*Ode 1. Heirmos:*[^100]
He uncovered the bottom of the deep, and on dry land leads His own, having covered in it the adversaries, the Lord strong in battles: for He is glorified!

*Troparia:*
With the light of Thy grace, O Christ, Who didst illumine Thy righteous ones and with the beams of their deeds didst irradiate the ends of the world: illumine my mind darkened by ignorance, that honoring the memory of Thy servant, I may sing a hymn unto Thee: For He is glorified!

With the fire of love Thou didst inflame Thine ever-memorable father to run after Thee, and having found Him Whom he desired, now joyfully he sings: For He is glorified!

Desiring to emulate the life of the great fathers, thou didst come, O Gregory, into the monastery of the Mother of God, where taking upon thyself the angelic habit, thou didst accomplish in thyself the forms of virtue; and thy soul became a fair bride unto the immortal Bridegroom, Who has now appeared at the Jordan.

*Glory:*
For the sake of the many ascetic feats and labors wrought by thee, which through the count of time are innumerable, thou didst receive power from God over demons, O venerable one, even as they cried out: Unprepared art thou for us, Gregory, walled about by prayer and the terror of the Cross!

*Both now:*
For the sake of the prayers of thy servant, wall me about by thy power, O pure Virgin, and show me unapproachable unto enemies: for thou didst give birth unto the unapproachable God, Who in human nature for my sake now stands naked at the Jordan.

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*[^101]
The Lord Who gives strength unto our kings, and exalts the horn of His anointed, is born of the Virgin, and comes unto baptism: unto Him let us, the faithful, cry out: There is none holy as our God!

*Service to the Venerable Father*

*Troparia:*
Beholding the power of thy prayer, the spirits of wickedness under heaven bitterly cried out against thee, and pondered how they might do thee harm: but thou, O father, didst not fear their terror, but criedst out unto Him that strengthens thee: The Lord is my strength unto salvation!

The rulers of darkness and sons of Gehenna gnashed their teeth at thee who wast illumined with divine light, and set manifold snares for thy feet, fearing lest they might grieve thee even a little, but they could not: for unceasingly thou didst cry out: There is none, O father, as our God!

In the manner of thieves the fierce demons that assault the servants of God led evil men to rob thee, O blessed one; but what could they take from thee who hadst nothing, except books preserved for the praise of God?

*Glory:*
Darkened with the darkness of evil-doing, thieves coming in the night unto thy cell, O father, waited until thou shouldest go forth unto morning singing in the church, that then they might snatch thy goods: whose coming having perceived, thou didst pray, and they were weighed down with sleep for five days and five nights.

*Both now:*
The gloom of my soul, wherewith the demons darken me, drive away, O Mother of God: for thou didst give birth unto the Light-giver, Who by the radiance of His countenance enlightens every man that comes into the world.

*Kontakion and Ikos of the Feast.*
*Then, Sessional Hymn to the Venerable, Tone 4:*
The divine light shining around thee drove away its beams, and pursued them that walk in darkness, not permitting them to approach thee: for everyone that does evil deeds comes not to the light, lest he be reproved: but the enemy of God's yoke fell, and was put underfoot forever: from whom that we may be preserved, pray thou unto Christ God, O venerable father.

*Glory, both now: Of the Feast.*
Jesus born of the Virgin Mary, and baptized in Jordan by John: the Holy Spirit descended upon Him in the form of a dove: therefore the Prophet with the angels cried out: Glory to Thy coming, O Christ; glory to Thy kingdom; glory to Thy dispensation, O only Lover of mankind!

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*[^102]
I heard, O Lord, Thy voice which Thou didst speak, the voice of one crying in the wilderness, when Thou didst thunder over many waters, bearing witness unto Thy Son, wholly being filled with the Spirit that descended, he cried out: Thou art Christ, the wisdom and power of God!

*To Gregory the Wonderworker of the Caves*

*Troparia:*
Thou wast heard by God, O father, for the prayer of thy humility passed through the clouds: therefore the Most High, receiving thy prayers, preserved thee from every evil: for thou didst confess the power and wisdom of God to be one.

Having fallen asleep with their own sleep, and being unable to snatch anything, the first thieves, by thy prohibition, O blessed one, whom thou after five days and five nights didst awaken, saying: How long will ye suffer? sleep! but they could not, being held by an invisible divine power.

Beholding the thieves perish by spiritual and bodily famine, O father, thou didst not render evil for evil, but fulfilling that which is written, thou didst feed the hungry enemies with bread, and didst dismiss them unto their homes, that at least thus they might be brought to wisdom, and confess how in truth the wisdom and power of God work in thee.

*Glory:*
Unto God didst thou commit the judgment of them that grieved thee, Whose vengeance did not spare the thieves, but brought report unto the prince, who, seizing them, was about to deliver them unto torments: but thou, knowing human frailty, gavest thy books unto the judge for a ransom, and deliveredst them from bitter torments.

*Both now:*
A fierce adversary, conscience contending with me concerning sins, with which I did not come to agreement by correction when I was on the path of life, now has dragged me unto the Judge, that I may receive judgment according to deeds, and not go forth thence until I have reckoned unto the last farthing. Woe is me! Who shall deliver me from this evil, if thou, O pure one, grant me not help?

*Ode 5. Heirmos:*[^103]
Jesus the Author of life comes to loose the condemnation of Adam the first-formed; needing no purification as God, He cleanses the fallen in Jordan: wherein having lulled to sleep enmity, He grants the peace that surpasses all understanding.

*Troparia:*
Jesus the Author of life, guiding sinners unto the right path, gave the thieves an upright spirit, wherewith being softened they came unto repentance, and giving themselves unto the labor of the monastery of the Mother of God, were reconciled unto God, Who grants peace unto them that love His law.

Having loved Thee, the only Author of life, His word didst thou fulfill in deed, O father: for all that thou hadst thou didst sell, that no one might fall into distress again on their account, and the price thereof thou didst distribute unto the poor *Service to the Venerable Father*

...and by their hands thou didst carry it into the treasury where a thief does not approach, but it remains intact.

Again the enemy worked mischief against thee, O venerable one, through other thieves, who, having entered into thy garden, filled their vessels with fruit, but wishing to depart they were unable, being held by an invisible cord; and weighed down by their burdens, they remained standing for two days and two nights.

*Glory:*
They that expected to hide their evil-doing, having this plunder and the conviction of unrighteousness upon their shoulders, cried out, asking forgiveness from thee, O father, promising to spend the rest of their years in repentance.

*Both now:*
I desired to hide myself from the all-seeing God by mine evil deeds, but the conviction that exists in my inward parts, like a great burden, uncovers my shame: knowing not what else to do, I cry unto thee: Have mercy on me, O compassionate Mother, even though shame has covered my face.

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*[^104]
The voice of the Word, the candlestick of the Light, the dayspring of the Sun, the Forerunner in the wilderness cries out repentance unto all peoples, and says: Cleanse yourselves beforehand, for lo, Christ stands by, delivering the world from corruption!

*Troparia:*
The voice of thy words, O father, troubled the bones of them that stole, and their soul fainted from fear: for they were as though struck down by frost, motionless in one place, and there was none that could deliver them from so great an evil.

When the thieves heard the preaching voice calling unto repentance, they fell down before thee, O blessed one, with tears, asking for compunction: and thou, O father, having been moved with compunction, saidst with a gentle voice: Blessed is God Who strengthens you unto a good work, and may He loose you, Who delivers all from corruption!

When Christ appeared at the Jordan, publicans and all sinners came unto Him, and received the renewal of their life: so also the thieves, having labored under thine instruction, O father, began repentance in novelty of life, and completed it by labor.

*Glory:*
A good example didst thou give by thyself unto thy people, not to avenge oneself by Satan: therefore thy servant, being forced a third time by evil men, avenged not himself, but being a guileless son, gave what they demanded, committing unto perdition the one whom heaven delivered not.

*Both now:*
Drawn by mine own will, I fell into the pit of destruction, and I have no hope of being raised up: unto thee do I stretch forth my voice, O Sovereign Lady: pray for me unto thy Son, Who today appeared at the Jordan, delivering not only me, but the whole world from corruption.

*To Gregory the Wonderworker of the Caves*

*Kontakion, Tone 7:*
Forbidding the lawless to do lawlessness, thou wast cast by them into the streams of the river, O Gregory worthy of wonder; where receiving a blessed end, now with the angels thou standest before the Master's throne: pray thou for our souls.

*Ode 7. Heirmos:*[^105]
The godly youths that had communion with the burning fiery furnace, the whistling dew-bearing spirit preserved unharmed, and the descent of God's angel: therefore bedewed in the flame, thankfully they chanted: O supremely praised Lord and God of our fathers, blessed art Thou!

*Troparia:*
Unto the Hebrew youths of old in the furnace, who manfully trampled the flame and strongly opposed the tormentor, but not to the enemy's counselor: yielding themselves unto the passionate flame, they thought before thee, O father, to hide their guilt, which the blessed God revealed unto thee.

Beholding prophetically by the Spirit what was inside the hearts of the thieves, that they had an intention against righteousness, thou didst weep, O blessed one, foreseeing the wretchedness of him whose day of destruction had arrived, and unto the liar the blessed God gave no will to escape.

The Holy Spirit is able to open even unclean mouths, that they may speak the truth for the correction of others: therefore when thou didst ask, O venerable one, by what death the son of perdition should die, even without knowing it the thieves prophesied that he would be hanged upon a tree, which also came to pass in its own time.

*Glory:*
The sting of death is sin, which kills a man, and there is no healing for this until life is preserved by repentance: for though thou gavest thy books as a ransom for the thieves, O father, yet the guilty one escaped not destruction, but rather was his memory blotted out from the books of the living.

*Both now:*
Being the intelligible book, O Mary Bride of God, in which by the finger of the all-holy Spirit the Father's Word was written, vouchsafe all of us, O blessed one, to be written in the books of the living.

*Ode 8. Heirmos:*[^106]
A most glorious mystery did the Babylonian furnace show forth, pouring forth dew; even as Jordan was to receive with its streams the immaterial fire, and to embrace the Creator baptized in the flesh: Whom the peoples bless, and supremely exalt unto all ages!

*Troparia:*
Foreseeing thee, O father, discerning the mystery of their deceitful heart, the thieves shut thee in the cave, and opened their thought unto evil-doing *Service to the Venerable Father*

...unto evil-doing; but when they began to gather the fruits of others' labors, the judgment of God overtook Him concerning whom Thou didst prophesy: that he should not bless the Lord with the living unto all ages.

Desiring to devour thy labors, O father, like a wild boar, the thief stretched forth his hand to steal from the tree, but was not sustained: for the branch broke, and immediately by hanging the tree of his life withered, so that he could no longer yield the fruit of repentance unto all ages.

Let no one judge an unrighteous judgment, nor speak a lie: for God is not mocked. The mocking thieves, seeing one worthy of hanging upon a tree, and that what was spoken had come to pass: but thou, O most blessed one, supremely exaltest the Lord unto all ages.

*We bless the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit: the Lord.*

Seeing that nothing was hidden from thee, O father, falling down they asked forgiveness, that a similar destruction might not come upon them also: but thou, O father, didst admonish the thieves no longer to steal, but rather to labor for the brethren, and to eat blessed fruit, if they desire to supremely exalt the Lord unto all ages.

*Both now:*
Having eaten of the unblessed fruit, I faint with spiritual death: beholding thee therefore, the Mother of life, I stretch forth my voice: add unto me the life-giving weeping of repentance, that coming a little to myself, I may find life, and supremely exalt thee, O Mother of God, unto all ages.

*Ode 9. Heirmos:*[^107]
Every tongue is at a loss to praise thee as is meet, and an intellect transcendent is dazed to sing of thee, O Mother of God: but being good, accept our faith; for thou knowest our divine love: for thou art the protectress of Christians: thee do we magnify.

*Troparia:*
The enemy was at a loss how to ensnare thee, O father: he brought the foolish servants of the prince, who, when thou wast drawing water in the river, mocked thee, speaking wretched words: but thou, O venerable one, foreseeing by the Spirit their destruction, didst admonish them not to speak foul things, but rather with their tongue to magnify God.

The boastful men were not corrected by thine instruction, O father, but rather mocked thee, insulting thee: therefore again with compassion didst thou admonish them, that they had rather need of prayer, for soon they should be drowned with their prince Rostislav.

Reprove not a fool lest he hate thee, says the Scripture, which also was fulfilled upon thee, O most blessed one: for the prince, hearing the death prophesied concerning himself by thee, taking it as a reproach, was wroth against *To Gregory the Wonderworker of the Caves*

*Glory:*
Unto Christ Who is now glorified by waters, by water thou didst depart, O father, and death cut down the tree of thy temporal life, which now in diverse ways blossoms beside the outgoings of living waters, and the foliage of thy prayers gladdens the Church: therefore thee, O father, do we magnify.

*Both now:*
In the temple of thy glory, preserve in peace them that bring Gabriel's greeting unto thee, O pure Virgin, and establish the scepters of the kingdom, granting them the power of thy grace unto the driving away of all corrupting passions, that we all may magnify thee.

*Exapostilarion:*
Thou that didst assimilate thyself unto the angels in life, O blessed one, even after thy falling asleep wast not separated from them: for the King on high commanded His angels after three days to bring thy much-suffering body out of the water into thy cell, and the working thereof to be seen in the holy tabernacles: where now living, remember us that honor thy memory, O most sacred father.

*Glory, both now: Of the Feast.*
The Savior, grace and truth, has appeared in the streams of Jordan, and has enlightened them that slept in darkness and shadow: for the unapproachable Light has come and appeared.

*In the Month of January, on the 28th Day:*
*Service to our Venerable Father Ephraim of the Caves, formerly Eunuch of Prince Izyaslav of Kyiv, and afterward Bishop of Pereyaslavl.*[^110]

*At Vespers, "Blessed is the man," 1st Antiphon. On "Lord, I have cried," Stichera, Tone 4. Podoben: "Called from on high" (Зва́нный свы́ше):*
When by the good pleasure of Jesus, the Chief of pastors on high, Thou wast called, O venerable one, then reckoning the corruptible things of the world as nothing, and leaving all fair dominion, Thou didst go by an irreversible course into the life devoid of worldly care *Service to the Venerable Father*

...into the life devoid of worldly care, by whose assistance, having departed as a good soldier, thou rejoicest now in the heavenly courts, dwelling with the ranks of the venerable, where make haste to offer supplication unto Christ God, that He may give peace unto the world and save our souls.

When by the anointing of the Holy Spirit thou wast chosen, O venerable Ephraim, to shepherd the noetic flock of the heavenly King, then as a good shepherd and teacher, continually guiding with good instruction the sheep entrusted unto thee, warding off enemies and nourishing them with the word of comfort, thou didst confirm them, showing thyself as a pattern unto them in word and deed; and having well pastured them upon spiritual pastures, thou didst lead them whole unto the Chief of pastors, Christ God, Who has passed through the heavens, Whom entreat to preserve and save us also from invisible enemies.

When the good pleasure of God kindled the hearts of all into love toward thee, O most blessed father, then by the desire of him that wore the diadem, by the common counsel of all, thou wast appointed hierarch unto the glorious city, wherein, that faithfully as a faithful servant thou mightest serve with thy keys, thou hadst great diligence concerning the erection of temples in thy bishopric; and this before all things thou didst account unto thyself the most honorable and lovely work, to multiply praise unto the glory of Christ our God, Whom entreat to save and enlighten our souls.

*Glory: Tone 6:*
Adornment of hierarchs, most blessed and most sacred father, excellent disciple of Christ, good shepherd who didst pasture thy flock upon spiritual pastures, O Ephraim worthy of wonder: now standing with the hierarchs before the throne of Christ, cease not to pray for us that chant thy venerable falling asleep.

*The Reading from Proverbs. (Chapter 10):*[^111]
The memory of the just is with praises, and the blessing of the Lord is upon his head. Blessed is the man that has found wisdom, and the mortal that knows understanding. For it is better to purchase her than treasures of gold and silver. And she is more honorable than precious stones: and nothing honorable is worthy of her. For length of days and years of life are in her right hand: and in her left hand are riches and glory. Out of her mouth *To Ephraim, Bishop of Pereyaslavl*

...proceeds righteousness: and she bears law and mercy upon her tongue. Give ear therefore unto me, O children, for I speak honorable things: and blessed is the man that shall keep my ways: for my outgoings are the outgoings of life, and the will is prepared from the Lord. Therefore do I entreat you, and put forth my voice unto the sons of men: for I, wisdom, have set up; counsel, understanding, and knowledge have I called. Counsel is mine, and confirmation; understanding is mine, and strength is mine. I love them that love me, and they that seek me shall find grace. Understand craftiness, therefore, ye simple ones, and apply your hearts, ye uninstructed. Give ear unto me yet again, for I speak honorable things: and I will open right things from my lips; for my throat shall meditate truth, and lying lips are an abomination before me. All the words of my mouth are with righteousness: there is nothing crooked or perverse in them. They are all right to them that understand, and straight to them that find knowledge. For I teach you truth, that your hope may be in the Lord, and ye may be filled with the Spirit.

The mouth of the righteous drops wisdom, and the lips of wise men know grace. The mouth of the wise meditates wisdom: and righteousness delivers from death. When a righteous man dies, his hope perishes not. For a righteous son is born unto life, and in his good things he shall gather the fruit of righteousness. Light is ever unto the righteous, and from the Lord they shall find grace and glory. The tongue of the wise knows good things, and in their heart wisdom rests. For she anticipates them that desire her, before they know her: and she is easily seen of them that love her. Whoso rises early unto her shall have no travail: and he that watches for her sake shall quickly be without care. Malice shall never prevail against wisdom: for these things' sake I was an admirer of her beauty, and I loved her, and sought her out from my youth. And I sought to bring her to myself as a bride, for the Master of all loved her. For she is privy to the mysteries of the knowledge of God, and a chooser of His works. Her labors are virtues: for she teaches temperance and prudence, justice and fortitude, than which men can have nothing more profitable in life. And if *Service to the Venerable Father*

[^126]: Woodcut engraving of Saint Prokhor the Weed-Eater of the Caves (*Прпⷣ: Про́хоръ Ло́бодникъ*), Discourse 31 of the Kyiv-Pechersk Paterikon. A native of Smolensk, Prokhor received tonsure at the Kyiv-Pechersk Monastery and adopted an extreme rule of ascetic fasting, gathering wild orache (chenopodium) during the summer, grinding it into flour, and baking bread without ever tasting normal wheat or rye bread. During the severe famine in Rus' under Grand Prince Svyatopolk Izyaslavich (1093–1113), St. Prokhor baked orache bread for all the starving citizens, which by his blessing tasted sweeter than honey. When the merchant guild created an artificial salt monopoly and salt became unavailable, he gathered ashes from the cells and distributed them to the poor, where they miraculously turned into pure white salt. Prince Svyatopolk, deeply moved by these miracles, repented of his extortions and vowed never to harm the monastery; upon Prokhor's repose in 1107, the prince himself carried the saint's body to the caves for burial.
