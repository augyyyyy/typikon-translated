# MONTH OF JUNE

*On the 1st Day: Service to the Venerable Agapitus of the Caves, Unmercenary Physician.*[^233]

*At Vespers, on "Lord, I have cried," Stichera, Tone 4. Podoben: "As one valiant among martyrs" (Ꙗ҆́кѡ до́блѧ):*
Having abundantly received from on high the gift of spiritual grace, O venerable Agapitus, as with river streams thou dost water all, and by thy prayers thou grantest healing unto all that run unto thee with faith;[^234] therefore we also, having taken boldness *To the Caves Unmercenary Physician*

...having taken boldness, bow down before the reliquary of thy relics, and pray: Grant healing unto our souls and bodies.

Having by purity of mind and unceasing standing in prayers subdued irrational passions unto the spirit, O all-blessed one, thou didst receive the grace of healing, and dost grant it ungrudgingly unto all that ask help of thee. Therefore we also, honoring thy dormition, diligently pray thee: As an unmercenary physician, grant healing unto our souls and bodies.

The reliquary of thy relics, O venerable father Agapitus, is revealed as a radiant heaven, showing forth wonders like light-bearing stars, and granting gracious healing unto them that run unto it with hope. Therefore we also, possessing expectation, diligently pray thee: Grant help in our passions unto our souls and bodies.

*Glory: Tone 6:*
Having received the grace of healing from on high while yet living in this temporal life, O most blessed Agapitus, thou didst ungrudgingly grant healing unto all that asked it; and now vouchsafed greater grace, standing before the throne of the Highest, send down from thy gifts unto us that bow down before thy many-healing relics and honor thy memory with praises, that through thy prayers unto God, O father, we may receive healing for our sicknesses.

*Both now: Theotokion, or Stavrotheotokion.*

*At the Aposticha, Stichera:*
*Glory: Tone 3:*
O glorious one, who bringest forth the embers of spiritual gifts and healings unto them that are held fast by many passions and need thy defense: grant speedy help from on high, cooling and driving away with the streams of thy wonders the sicknesses brought on by the evil one; that having all alike drawn healing, we may glorify the Heavenly Father, and magnify His Co-eternal Son, with His Co-essential Spirit, and all may call thee blessed, O father Agapitus.

*Both now: Theotokion, or Stavrotheotokion.*

*Troparion, Tone 5:*
Emulating the humble-mindedness of the glorious Anthony, as with a medicine through edible herbs thou didst heal the sick, O venerable Agapitus; therefore having also convinced the unbelieving physician, thou didst guide him onto the path of salvation: heal our sicknesses also, and pray unto the Lord for them that sing of Thee.[^235]

*Service to the Venerable Agapitus*

*Canon to the Venerable One.*
*Tone 6. Ode 1. Heirmos:*[^236]
As on dry land did Israel walk through the abyss with their footsteps, and beholding Pharaoh the pursuer submerged, cried aloud: Let us sing a song of victory unto God!

*Troparia:*
As of old by a touch Thou didst make the mouth of the dumb to speak rightly, so also unto me, O my Christ, loosen my tongue and cleanse my mind, that I may bring praise in memory of Thy pleasing one, singing with love.

I cannot speak according to thy grace, O venerable one, unless thou by thy prayers grant me help, that I may glorify the Good One Who gave thee the grace of healings, singing in praise.

Called by a call, O father, thou wast summoned to walk after thy Master, and following Him, leaving all the fair things of the world, in love thou didst come unto the great Anthony, and with him didst live, singing unceasing praise unto God.

*Glory:*
Thou didst appear an angel unto the earthly and a heavenly man, and didst acquire the angelic life thyself, O venerable father. Therefore we honor thy achievements, and glorify the Master of all, singing a song concerning thee unto Him.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
O thou who art ever glorified by the earthly and the heavenly powers: on this present day preserve from all assaults them that make bold to bring praise unto thee, that we may sing of thee who art full of grace.

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*[^237]
There is none holy as Thou, O Lord my God, Who hast exalted the horn of Thy faithful, O Good One, and established us upon the rock of Thy confession.

*Troparia:*
Through many years thou didst pass through exceeding labors, O Agapitus, struggling in vigilance and fasting, until thou wast shown to be of like character with thy instructor, and wast established by him upon the rock of a steadfast life.

Possessing naught else save poverty, thou didst follow Christ Who became poor for our sake, O father, and keeping His commandments, didst serve the sick, visiting and giving them rest, and granting them health by thy prayer.

By conjoined life thou didst acquire venerableness, O laudable Agapitus, and wast vouchsafed the gifts of healings, for by prayer and edible herbs thou didst grant health unto the sick; therefore also thou wast called an unmercenary physician.

*Glory:*

*To the Caves Unmercenary Physician*

...Thou didst glorify God in thy members, O venerable one; therefore God also glorified thee with the grace of healing, wherewith heal our sicknesses both of soul and body, that we may be established upon the rock of the confession of God.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
O thou who didst bud forth the Tree of Life: by its fruit quicken us who are slain by sins, and grant unto us, O pure Mother, a firm confession of thy goodness.

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 1:*
Freely having received from God the gift of healing, freely dost thou grant healing unto them that suffer, who with faith run unto thee. Therefore we also, bowing down before the reliquary of thy relics, O venerable father, pray to receive from thee healing for our sicknesses.

*Glory, both now: Theotokion:*
Running unto thy loving-kindness in sorrows, we pray, O all-immaculate one: for the sake of thy eternal servant, grant speedy healing unto us that are held fast by sicknesses.

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*[^238]
Christ is my power, God and Lord, the honorable Church divinely sings, crying from a pure mind, keeping festival in the Lord.

*Troparia:*
Christ chose the foolish things of this world to put to shame the wise, which thing came to pass also in thee, O all-blessed father: for by grace and not by art didst thou put to shame the all-wise physician. Therefore celebrating in the Lord, we glorify thee.

He who formerly deceived the sick was wont to deceive, foretelling unto them the day and the hour of death: reproaching whom, O venerable one, thou didst heal the sick man with prayer and edible herbs, saying: Give glory unto God, Who has the key of life and death.[^239]

Wounded by the arrow of envy, the ungodly Armenian reproached thee, O father, and having sent a man condemned to death, commanded him to be slain before thee with a deadly potion. But thou, having healed him with prayer and herbs, didst put to shame the heretic who wished to put thee to shame.

*Glory:*
The exceeding great grace given unto thee became an occasion of stumbling, O most blessed one, for the ungodly Armenian arming himself yet more against thee, came upon thee to give thee poison; but even drinking deadly poison, thou wast unharmed, for the grace of God preserved thee unto greater wonders.[^240]

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Rejoice, O vessel that didst bear the Myrrh which healed the human race: quicken me who am slain by the poison of sin, that having lived the rest of my time whole, I may praise thee, O Virgin.

*Service to the Venerable Agapitus*

*Ode 5. Heirmos:*[^241]
Enlighten with the love of Thy divine light, O Good One, the souls of them that rise early unto Thee with love, I pray, that they may know Thee, O Word of God, as the true God, Who callest them forth from the darkness of sin.

*Troparia:*
Having received from God the grace of healing, Thou didst heal the prince, who though treated by the Armenian found no relief; but having tasted of thy herbs, he was delivered from his sickness.[^242]

Behold, ye monastics, and emulate the virtuous patient endurance of the God-bearing father, how with zeal he renounced the will of the world, and never went outside the gates of the monastery: which thing beholding, emulate him.

Possessing one love toward all and not regarding persons, whether prince or commoner came unto thee, O venerable one, unto all that came thou didst grant healing freely, seeking reward from God alone.

*Glory:*
Having received healing through thee, O all-blessed one, the prince understood that thou art a true servant of God, and desired to see thee; therefore he came unto thee with gifts, but having God alone, thou wouldest receive none of those gifts.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
We praise thee, O most pure Virgin, for thou openest unto us the doors of thy mercy, and grantest unto all an ungrudging wellspring of healings, delivering us from the venom of sin.

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*[^243]
Beholding the sea of life surging with the tempest of temptations, fleeing unto Thy calm haven, I cry unto Thee: Lead up my life from corruption, O Greatly-merciful One!

*Troparia:*
Leaving all the corruptible things of this world, thou didst cast the gold brought unto thee before the gates of thy cell, O blessed one, casting out together the grasping enemy and the shoot of his soul-harming head.[^244]

Having acquired such precious lack of envy, thou didst account all other things as refuse, O father, and thereby didst purchase for thyself a habitation in the heavens, which we also pray to attain by thy prayers, O father.

Fasting through many years, thou didst grow weak in body, O venerable one, yet in spirit thou didst remain firm as a pillar, enduring all things brought upon thee, and giving thanks unto God, unto Whom thou didst cry: Lead up my life from corruption, O Greatly-merciful One!

*Glory:*
Ailing in body, in spirit thou didst contend against the ungodly Armenian, who foretold death unto thee after three days; whom thou didst conquer, O blessed one, proclaiming thy departure unto God after three months.[^245]

*To the Caves Unmercenary Physician*

*Both now: Theotokion:*
O Word Who didst dwell in thy womb, O all-immaculate Virgin: Thou didst deify the human race in the heavens. Entreat Him to translate me from the corruption of this life, thither where the saints are nourished with the torrent of delight.

*Kontakion, Tone 6:*[^246]
Being an excellent physician, O marvelous one, thou wast shown to be ungrudging in thy faith, reproving the unbelief of the Armenian and bringing him unto piety; and being thyself near unto death, thou didst ask from God prolongation of life, and having convinced him by this wonder, didst bring him unto Christ; before Whom standing in gladness, pray for us, O venerable one.

*Ode 7. Heirmos:*[^247]
An angel made the furnace produce dew for the venerable youths, while the burning command of God consumed the Chaldeans, persuading the tyrant to cry aloud: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!

*Troparia:*
Possessing the gift from on high to heal sicknesses, O venerable one: heal me who am sick through neglect of good deeds, even as of old thou didst heal him that was brought in sickness, granting him health by prayer, and crying: Blessed is the God of our fathers!

He that made thee wrought a miracle: suddenly He set thee forth healthy before the ungodly one, who seeing it marveled at thy speedy change from sickness into health. By thy visitation from on high, O father, lead me who am tormented with wounds of shameful deeds unto the direction of correction, that I may sing: Blessed is the God of our fathers!

Having received thy sickness and edible herbs, the physician came to confess his ungodliness, whom thou didst rebuke, for he dared to condemn thy mouth, saying: Behold, I also make bold to approach thee, O father; but I pray thee, cast me not away in my needs, that I may sing: Blessed is the God of our fathers!

*Glory:*
Having gathered the fruits of thy labors with gladness, as a good husbandman, by a blessed end thou didst depart unto the highest, to receive reward from Christ God; and thou didst receive from Him unending joy, O much-marvelous Agapitus.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Unto thy haven do we flee, O Bride of God, Virgin, carried along by the waves of worldly passions, which do thou calm, that we may sing unto thee: Rejoice, haven of worldly voyagings.

*Ode 8. Heirmos:*[^248]
From the flame Thou didst pour forth dew for the venerable ones, and burned up the sacrifice of the righteous one with water: for Thou doest all things, O Christ, only by Thy willing: Thee do we supremely exalt unto all ages.

*Service to the Venerable Agapitus*

*Troparia:*
A most marvelous physician wast thou, O father, healing bodily sicknesses in this life while living in the flesh, and after thy dormition granting healing of soul, as thou didst show this in the Armenian, giving his soul whole and instruction unto piety.

Seeing thee in a marvelous vision, O father, the Armenian reported openly that thou art holy and venerable; and turning from unbelief unto faith, by thy direction he became a monk: with whom forget not us who sing of the Lord unto ages.

Possessing a great gift, thou didst practice the art of healing the sick; and now vouchsafed greater gifts, apply a plaster unto the wounds of my soul, that having received relief, I may supremely exalt the Lord unto ages.

*We bless the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit: the Lord.*
From the light resembling flame, grant rays of grace by the mouth, O Christ God; quench with the dew of Thy compassions the passions that burn me, dispersing the darkness of passions: whom the venerable one entreats with us, that we may supremely exalt Thee, the Lord, unto all ages.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
O thou who didst enlighten the world by thy most pure childbirth: disperse the gloom of my evil deeds, pouring forth upon me for the doing of good the rich mercy of thy compassions, that I may supremely exalt thee, the Mother of God, unto ages.

*Ode 9. Heirmos:*[^249]
It is impossible for men to see God, upon Whom the ranks of angels dare not gaze; yet through thee, O all-pure one, the Word was made flesh and appeared unto men: Whom magnifying, with the heavenly hosts we call thee blessed.

*Troparia:*
Possessing a God-given healer, let no people go unto vain physicians, who harm possessions and health; but let him that is in need come with faith unto the reliquary of the God-bearing father, and he shall straightway receive healing.

The noetic robbers having now wounded us, we possess incurable wounds; therefore without doubt we fall down before thy most marvelous reliquary, O venerable one, and pray: Grant healing unto them that need it, that we all may praise and magnify thee.

Having ascended in soul into the highest unto the all-radiant bridal chambers, thou didst leave the reliquary of thy relics in the cave, O all-blessed one: bowing down before which ever, grant healing unto our sicknesses, that we may magnify thee in songs.

*To the Caves Unmercenary Physician*

*Glory:*
Despise not them that ask help of thee in sicknesses, but by thy visitation grant healing of soul together with body, O most blessed Agapitus, as thou art an excellent physician, that we may ever magnify thee.

*Both now: Theotokion:*
Despise not them that need help from thee, O Virgin, but grant it unto them that run unto thee with faith: for thou art an inexhaustible wellspring of healings, that with the heavenly powers we may ever magnify thee.

*Exapostilarion:*[^250]
Having left the corruptible things of the world and striving to receive the incorruptible as an emulator, thou didst hasten to exchange the worse for the better, sanctifying by love-contempt of passions; and enriched with the gift of healings, grant this also unto us who today honor thy memory, O all-blessed Agapitus.

*Glory, both now: Theotokion:*
Being saved through thee, we confess thee as the pure Mother: for thou didst bear Him Who destroyed death by the Cross, and ascending on high, drew all things unto Himself, and granted us great mercy.

*At the Praises, Stichera, Tone 4. Podoben: "Thou hast given a sign" (Да́лъ є҆сѝ зна́менїе):*[^251]
Having withered the passionate stirrings of the flesh by ascetic labors, and having ascended unto the height of virtues, thou didst appear brighter than the sun, shining with the grace of healings, and enlightening with its rays them that come unto thee. Therefore we pray thee, O venerable one: Unto them that lovingly honor thy memory, grant healing for sicknesses.

Having received from the only Lover of mankind on high a rich gift, thou didst distribute it ungrudgingly unto them that needed it, granting them healing. And now also unto them that fall down before the reliquary of thy relics, send down healing in sicknesses, as a true physician, O most blessed one, and intercessor for our souls.

Gathered together in faith and love in memory of the worthy-of-blessedness father, let us the people praise him with songful voices, that we may be vouchsafed healings in our sicknesses from him *Service to the Venerable Agapitus*

...from him: for he is swift to incline, and grants healing as an unmercenary physician and speedy helper in sorrows.

*Glory: Tone 6:*[^252]
Endless is Thy grace, O Christ the King, and great is the gift which Thou hast given unto Thy venerable one: for not only while living in the body did he heal sicknesses, but even after death by Thy divine power the reliquary of his incorrupt relics grants healing unto them that come with faith. Therefore we pray Thee, O Good Master, bringing Thy holy one unto intercession: according to Thy manifold goodness, deliver us from sicknesses of soul and body, and save our souls, as Thou art the Lover of mankind.

*Both now: Theotokion.*

*(Woodcut Engraving: Saint John the Much-Suffering (*Іоаннъ многострадальный*), depicted buried in the ground up to his shoulders during Great Lent, enduring demonic assaults with a skull and cross beside him; an angel descends from heaven holding a crown (*Іоанъ благословен мощи Моисея приложи, змий опали ему главу*). Below, a secondary panel depicts the saint attacked by a fire-breathing demonic serpent in the cave.)*[^253]

### Notes

[^233]: Fol. 77v (p82): June 1 marks the commemoration of our Venerable Father Agapitus of the Caves, Unmercenary Physician (†ca. 1095), who healed the sick without fee using monastic herbs and prayers, famously curing Prince Vladimir Monomakh of Chernihiv.
[^234]: Fol. 77v (p82): The melodic model is the Tone 4 prosomoion "As one valiant among martyrs" (*Ꙗ҆́кѡ до́блѧ* / *Ὡς γενναῖον ἐν μάρτυσιν*).
[^235]: Fol. 78r (p82): The troparion highlights Agapitus's ascetic reliance on the vegetable diet of Saint Anthony of the Caves (*ꙁе́лїемъ снѣ́днымъ*) as a vehicle of miraculous healing, and his conversion of the Armenian physician who converted to Orthodoxy and was tonsured as a Caves monk after Agapitus's death.
[^236]: Fol. 78v (p83): Canon of Saint Agapitus, Tone 6, Ode 1 Heirmos: "Ꙗ҆́кѡ по сꙋ́хꙋ пѣшеше́ствовавъ І҆и҃ль..." (*Ὡς ἐν ἠπείρῳ πεζεύσας ὁ Ἰσραήλ*).
[^237]: Fol. 78v (p83): Ode 3 Heirmos: "Нѣ́сть ст҃ъ, ꙗ҆́коже ты̀ Гдⷭ҇и Бж҃е мо́й..." (*Οὐκ ἔστιν ἅγιος ὡς σύ, Κύριε ὁ Θεός μου*).
[^238]: Fol. 79r (p83): Ode 4 Heirmos: "Хрїсто́съ моѧ̀ си́ла, Бг҃ъ и҆ Гдⷭ҇ь..." (*Χριστός μου δύναμις, Θεὸς καὶ Κύριος*).
[^239]: Fol. 79r (p83): Reference to Discourse 27 of the Kyiv Caves Paterikon, where the Armenian physician predicted that a sick boyar would die within eight days; Agapitus healed the boyar with monastery herbs, rebuking the Armenian for presuming to know the divine boundary between life and death.
[^240]: Fol. 79r (p83): According to the Paterikon, the envious Armenian gave Agapitus a deadly poison, but the saint drank it and remained unharmed, fulfilling Christ's promise in Mark 16:18 ("if they drink any deadly thing, it shall not hurt them").
[^241]: Fol. 79v (p84): Canon of Saint Agapitus, Tone 6, Ode 5 Heirmos: "Бж҃їимъ свѣ́томъ твои́мъ Бл҃же..." (*Τῷ θείῳ φέγγει σου, Ἀγαθέ*).
[^242]: Fol. 79v (p84): Referring to the miraculous healing of Prince Vladimir Vsevolodovich Monomakh (†1125), Prince of Chernihiv (and later Grand Prince of Kyiv), who was dying of an illness that the Armenian physician could not cure. Saint Agapitus sent him herbs boiled in monastery soup through Igumen John, which instantly cured the prince.
[^243]: Fol. 79v (p84): Ode 6 Heirmos: "Жите́йское мо́ре воздвиза́емое зрѧ̀..." (*Τοῦ βίου τὴν θάλασσαν, ὑψουμένην καθορῶν*).
[^244]: Fol. 79v (p84): When Prince Vladimir Monomakh came in person to the Caves Monastery with rich gifts of gold and silver to reward Agapitus, the saint refused to receive them and threw the gold before the gate of his cell, telling the prince to distribute it to the poor.
[^245]: Fol. 79v (p84): When Saint Agapitus fell sick, the Armenian physician came to his cell, took his pulse, and announced that he would die within three days; Agapitus answered that God had revealed to him that he would depart only after three months, which came to pass exactly as prophesied.
[^246]: Fol. 80r (p84): Kontakion, Tone 6, emphasizing Agapitus's victorious contest against the Armenian physician and his post-mortem conversion of the Armenian to the Orthodox Christian faith and monastic habit.
[^247]: Fol. 80r (p84): Ode 7 Heirmos: "Росода́тельнꙋ ᲂу҆́бѡ пе́щь содѣ́ла а҆́гг҃лъ..." (*Δροσοβόλον μὲν τὴν κάμινον εἰργάσατο*).
[^248]: Fol. 80r (p84): Ode 8 Heirmos: "И҆зъ пла́мене прпⷣбнымъ ро́сꙋ и҆сточи́лъ є҆сѝ..." (*Ἐκ φλογὸς τοῖς ὁσίοις δρόσον ἐπήγασας*).
[^249]: Fol. 80v (p85): Ode 9 Heirmos: "Бг҃а человѣ́кѡмъ невозмо́жно ви́дѣти..." (*Θεὸν ἀνθρώποις ἰδεῖν ἀδύνατον*).
[^250]: Fol. 81r (p85): Exapostilarion to Saint Agapitus, summarizing his ascetic flight from worldly vanity to spiritual riches.
[^251]: Fol. 81r (p85): The melodic model is the Tone 4 prosomoion "Thou hast given a sign" (*Да́лъ є҆сѝ зна́менїе* / *Ἔδωκας σημείωσιν*).
[^252]: Fol. 81v (p86): The Doxastikon (*Сла́ва*) at the Praises highlights the ongoing bodily incorruption and wonderworking power of Saint Agapitus's relics in the Near Caves (Saint Anthony's Caves).
[^253]: Fol. 82r (p86): Visual description of the 1763 Lavra woodcut engraving for the feast of Saint John the Much-Suffering of the Caves (July 18), portraying his ascetic endurance buried up to his neck during the forty days of Great Lent and the fiery demonic serpent that assailed him in the cave.
