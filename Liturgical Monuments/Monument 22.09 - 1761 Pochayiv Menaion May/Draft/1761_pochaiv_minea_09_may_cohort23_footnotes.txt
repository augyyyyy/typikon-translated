[^411]: May 29 commemoration of the Holy Venerable Martyr Theodosia the Virgin of Constantinople (ca. 730), defender of the holy icon of Christ at the Bronze Gate (Chalke) under Emperor Leo III the Isaurian.
[^412]: "Copronymus" (dung-named) is the historical pejorative applied by iconodules to the Iconoclast Byzantine Emperor Constantine V (reigned 741–775), who ordered the removal and destruction of the icon of Christ at the Bronze Gate.
[^413]: Church Slavonic acrostic: *Ѳеодо́сїи мꙋ́нцы пою̀ сла́вꙋ* ("To the martyr Theodosia I sing praise").
[^414]: The Greek name Theodosia (Θεοδοσία) signifies "God-given" or "gift of God" (from θεός + δόσις).
[^415]: Cf. Song of Songs 1:3: "Thy name is as ointment [myrrh] poured forth."
[^416]: May 30 commemoration of Our Venerable Father Isaac (Isaacius, d. 383 or 396), founder and hegumen of the Dalmatian Monastery in Constantinople, who confronted Emperor Valens over Arianism and was later venerated as patron of the Romanov dynasty.
[^417]: Saint Isaac famously confronted the Arian Emperor Valens three times in 378 as the emperor was departing Constantinople for the Battle of Adrianople, admonishing him to reopen the Orthodox churches.
[^418]: Saint Isaac was appointed hegumen by Saint Dalmatius, founding the Dalmatian Monastery (the first attested monastery in Constantinople).
[^419]: Saint Isaac grasped the bridle of the horse of Emperor Valens as he rode out to battle, demanding that he reopen the Orthodox churches.
[^420]: Saint Isaac prophesied that Valens would be burned alive in a barn after fleeing from the battlefield, which was fulfilled after the Battle of Adrianople in 378.
[^421]: May 31 commemoration of the Holy Apostle Hermes of the Seventy (bishop of Philippopolis, mentioned in Rom. 16:14) and the Holy Martyr Hermias (Hermias of Comana, ca. 166).
