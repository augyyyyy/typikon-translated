[^24]: This sticheron contrasts the spiritual charisms of the two founders of the Kyiv Caves Lavra: St. Anthony, who like Gideon asked for dew and like Elijah brought down fire from heaven, and St. Theodosius, who boldly rebuked Prince Sviatoslav II Yaroslavich of Kyiv for driving out his brother Iziaslav I in 1073.
[^25]: The Kyiv Caves Patericon records that before the construction of the Dormition Cathedral, a miraculous pillar of light and fire extended from earth to heaven, revealing the exact site chosen by the Mother of God for the Lavra's great church.
[^26]: According to the Life of St. Theodosius by St. Nestor the Chronicler, when the saint was brought to church on the eighth day after his birth, the priest, seeing through the Spirit what he would become, named him Theodosius ('given by God') and dedicated him to God's service.
[^27]: In his youth at Kursk, St. Theodosius baked the prosphora (altar breads) with his own hands out of humility and love for the Divine Liturgy, enduring harsh rebuke and beatings from his mother, who deemed such menial manual labor shameful for a youth of noble boyar lineage.
[^28]: St. Theodosius twice attempted to make a pilgrimage to the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem, but was turned back each time by divine dispensation to fulfill his destiny as the founder of Rus' monasticism in Kyiv.
[^29]: St. Theodosius secretly wore heavy iron chains around his loins from his youth. When his mother discovered them clinging to his flesh and soaked in dried blood, she tore them off him in anger and beat him severely.
[^30]: After tracking down Theodosius at the Kyiv Caves, his mother was so moved by his spiritual exhortations and filial love that she renounced the world herself, took the monastic habit at the Monastery of St. Nicholas in Kyiv, and spent the remainder of her days in ascetic repentance.
[^31]: The Kyiv Caves Patericon relates that while St. Theodosius was travelling on monastery business, a nobleman's servant mistook the shabbily dressed hegumen for a common laborer, commanded him to get into the wagon and drive the horses while the servant slept, only for the servant to be terrified upon entering Kyiv to see princes and boyars dismounting to bow low before Theodosius.
[^32]: The Kyiv Caves Patericon relates that when the monastery's cellarer was despairing over having no gold to buy provisions for the brethren, an unknown handsome youth appeared in the courtyard, handed St. Theodosius a bag containing three bags of gold ducats, saying it was sent by God, and immediately vanished.
