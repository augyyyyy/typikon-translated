# PART XLVIII: THE ORDER OVER THE COMPOUNDING OF THE HOLY GREAT MYRON, CELEBRATED ON HOLY AND GREAT THURSDAY [^3837]

*When the Holy Gifts have been carried into the holy sanctuary and placed upon the holy table, the foremost of the priests brings in the glass phial with the myron, and places it near the diskos on the upper side. And after the ecphonesis: "May the mercies of [our great] God [and Savior Jesus Christ be with you all]"; the doors not being opened, and the deacon upon the ambo remaining silent, while the archdeacon in the altar says:*[^3838]

**Archdeacon:** Let us attend![^3839]

*The hierarch steps up onto the step; and having signed the myron with the cross three times, he steps down; and having inclined his head, he says this prayer:*[^3840]

**Bishop / Hierarch:** O Lord of mercy, Father of lights, from Whom every good giving and every perfect gift is given:[^3841] grant unto us, Thine unworthy servants, the grace and enjoyment of this great and life-creating mystery; even as Thou didst give unto Thy faithful pleasure-pleaser Moses,[^3842] and unto Thy servant Samuel,[^3843] and unto Thine holy Apostles:[^3844] and send down Thine all-holy Spirit upon this myron, and make it a royal anointing, a spiritual anointing, a guardian of life[^3845] succoring souls and bodies; the oil of gladness,[^3846] which came indeed under the Law, and hath shone forth also in the New Covenant; wherewith were anointed priests, high priests, prophets, and kings;[^3847] wherewith also Thou Thyself didst anoint Thine holy Apostles, and all those who through the laver of regeneration have been made bishops and presbyters from them in succession until this present day.[^3848] Yea, O Master Almighty, through the descent of Thine Holy and Adored Spirit show [it to be] a garment of incorruption,[^3849] a perfecting seal,[^3850] delineating Thy divine name, and of Thine Only-begotten Son, and of Thine Holy Spirit, upon those who receive Thy divine laver[^3851] that having been understood before Thee, they may be Thy citizens, Thy menservants and maidservants, sanctified in soul and body, changed from all malice, and delivered from every sin by the vesture of Thy most pure glory;[^3852] recognized on account of Thy holy sign by the holy Angels and Archangels and all the heavenly powers,[^3853] and fearful unto all unclean spirits;[^3854] that they may be unto Thee a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, a holy nation,[^3855] marked by this most pure myron; and having Thy Christ in their hearts for an habitation of Thee, the God and Father[^3856] with the Holy Spirit.[^3857]

*Ecphonesis:*

**Bishop / Hierarch:** For holy art Thou, our God, Who restest upon the saints, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.[^3858]

**People / Choir:** Amen.[^3859]

**Bishop / Hierarch:** Peace be unto all.[^3860]

**Deacon:** Let us bow our heads unto the Lord.[^3861]

*The hierarch says this prayer secretly:*[^3862]

**Bishop / Hierarch (secretly):** Unto Thee, O God, King of all, giving thanks, have we bowed the neck of our heart; for although we are unworthy, Thou hast accounted us worthy to be ministers of Thy divine mysteries; and we proclaim Thy mercy which Thou hast richly poured out upon us. To receive[^3863] **Bishop / Hierarch (secretly, continued):** ...consecration from Thee do we pray, as the myron descending upon the head;[^3864] for myron poured forth is the name of Thine Only-begotten Son, Christ our God,[^3865] through Whom the whole world is made fragrant, as much as is visible and as much as is invisible.[^3866]

*Ecphonesis:*

**Bishop / Hierarch:** For Thou art worshiped and glorified, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. [Amen.][^3867]

*After this is chanted in order what is upon the ambo; and the opening of the doors takes place; and the hierarch accomplishes the subsequent rites of the divine offering.*[^3868]

*[Headpiece: Decorative woven woodcut ribbon band of interlaced strapwork and stylized floral elements across the top.]*

### Notes

[^3837]: Headpiece, decorative woodcut engraving, and title marking the beginning of Part XLVIII: The Order Over the Compounding [Consecration] of the Holy Great Myron, Celebrated on Holy and Great Thursday (Въслѣдова́нїе на растворе́нїемъ ст҃го вели́каго мѵ́ра · быва́емо въ ст҃ы́й вели́кїй четверто́къ; Greek Τάξις γινομένη ἐπὶ καθιερώσει τοῦ ἁγίου Μύρου ἐν τῇ ἁγίᾳ καὶ μεγάλῃ Πέμπτῃ). In the Byzantine and Ruthenian tradition, the solemn consecration of Holy Chrism (Myron) is reserved to the primate or diocesan bishop and takes place during the Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great on Maundy Thursday. The Striatyn Trebnyk preserves this archaic pontifical rite, which Bishop Hedeon Balaban specifically noted in his preface (fols. 6v–7r, leaves p12–p13) as having been retrieved from ancient Slavonic codices to supply a vital liturgical office often omitted in smaller parish euchologia.
[^3838]: Rubric detailing the rubrical actions during the Divine Liturgy: after the Great Entrance, when the Holy Gifts of Bread and Wine have been placed upon the Holy Table, the principal archpriest brings in the glass phial or alabastron containing the newly blended ingredients of the Holy Myron and places it near the diskos on the upper side (towards the high place / back of the altar). Following the ecphonesis "May the mercies of our great God...", the royal doors remain closed and the deacon on the ambo keeps silence, while the archdeacon inside the sanctuary initiates the consecration sequence.
[^3839]: Deaconal admonition "Let us attend!" (Slavonic възи́млѣмъ, Greek Πρόσχωμεν) uttered by the archdeacon from within the holy sanctuary, signaling the commencement of the pontifical prayer of consecration.
[^3840]: Rubric directing the officiating bishop to ascend the step before the Holy Table, sign the vessel containing the Holy Myron three times with the sign of the cross, step down, and recite the solemn prayer of consecration with inclined head.
[^3841]: James 1:17: "Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and comes down from the Father of lights (ὁ Πατὴρ τῶν φώτων), with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning."
[^3842]: Exod. 30:22–33: God's command unto Moses on Mount Sinai to compound the sacred anointing oil (Greek ἔλαιον χρῖσμα ἅγιον) from pure myrrh, sweet cinnamon, calamus, cassia, and olive oil, strictly consecrated for the sanctuary, the ark of the testimony, and the priesthood.
[^3843]: 1 Sam. (1 Kings LXX) 10:1; 16:13: the Prophet Samuel anointing Saul and David with the horn of oil as the chosen kings over Israel, prefiguring the royal chrism of the New Covenant.
[^3844]: 1 John 2:20, 27: "But ye have an unction from the Holy One, and ye know all things... the anointing which ye have received of Him abides in you."
[^3845]: Petitions invoking the descent of the Holy Spirit upon the compounded oil, designating it as a "royal anointing" (χρῖσμα βασιλικόν) and a "spiritual anointing" (χρῖσμα πνευματικόν), serving as an invulnerable shield and preserver of life (φυлаκτήριον ζωῆς). The prayer continues onto fol. 624v (leaf p1264).
[^3846]: Ps. 44:8 LXX (Ps. 45:7 KJV); Heb. 1:9: "God, even Thy God, hath anointed Thee with the oil of gladness (ἔλαιον ἀγαλλιάσεως) above Thy fellows."
[^3847]: Typological continuity between the sacramental economy of the Old and New Covenants: the sacred anointing that consecrated Aaronite priests, high priests, prophets, and kings in the Old Law finds its fulfillment in Christ and the Christian royal priesthood.
[^3848]: Titus 3:5: "According to His mercy He saved us, by the washing [laver] of regeneration (διὰ λουτροῦ παλιγγενεσίας), and renewing of the Holy Ghost." The prayer explicitly articulates the doctrine of uninterrupted Apostolic Succession through the uninterrupted laying on of hands and chrismation from the Holy Apostles down to contemporary bishops and presbyters.
[^3849]: Gal. 3:27; 1 Cor. 15:53: the spiritual garment of incorruption bestowed in holy baptism and sealed in chrismation, clothing the neophyte in the divine radiance of Christ.
[^3850]: Eph. 1:13; 4:30; 2 Cor. 1:22: the "perfecting seal" (Greek σφραγὶς τελειωτική; Slavonic печа́ть съвръшеннотво́рнаѧ), denoting the indelible character and gift of the Holy Spirit imparted in the Sacrament of Chrismation ("The seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit").
[^3851]: The invocation of the Holy Trinity—Father, Only-begotten Son, and Holy Spirit—whose divine name is spiritually imprinted upon those receiving the holy laver of baptism. The prayer continues onto fol. 625r (leaf p1265).
[^3852]: Petitions for the spiritual transformation of the chrismated: that they may be recognized as citizens of the kingdom of God (Eph. 2:19), consecrated in soul and body, and delivered from demonic subjection.
[^3853]: Theological motif of the invisible character of the sacramental seal (σφραγίς): the holy sign of Chrismation is spiritually luminous and recognized by the celestial hierarchies (Angels, Archangels, and heavenly powers).
[^3854]: Mark 16:17; Luke 10:19; Eph. 6:11–12: the apotropaic efficacy of the Holy Myron, rendering the faithful formidable and terrifically unapproachable unto Satan and all impure demonic spirits.
[^3855]: 1 Peter 2:9 (echoing Exod. 19:6): "But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people (ὑμεῖς δὲ γένος ἐκλεκτόν, βασίλειον ἱεράτευμα, ἔθνος ἅγιον, λαὸς εἰς περιποίησιν)."
[^3856]: Eph. 2:22; 3:17: that Christ may dwell within the hearts of the faithful through faith, making them a living habitation and temple of God the Father through the Holy Spirit. The prayer continues onto fol. 625v (leaf p1266).
[^3857]: Concluding clause of the primary prayer of consecration of the Holy Myron: "...with the Holy Spirit" (Greek σὺν τῷ ἁγίῳ Πνεύματι).
[^3858]: Festive Trinitarian ecphonesis pronounced aloud by the bishop: "For holy art Thou, our God, Who restest upon the saints..." (Greek Ὅτι ἅγιος εἶ, ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν, ὁ ἐν ἁγίοις ἐпаναπαυόμενος).
[^3859]: Choral response confirming the episcopal ecphonesis.
[^3860]: Episcopal blessing of peace (Eirene pasin; Greek Εἰρήνη πᾶσι) imparted unto the clergy and congregation.
[^3861]: Deaconal bidding inviting the faithful to incline their heads in reverence before the solemn prayer of inclination.
[^3862]: Rubric directing the bishop to recite the prayer of head-bowing secretly (Greek μυστικῶς; Slavonic та́й) before the Holy Table.
[^3863]: Opening thanksgiving of the prayer of inclination: the bishop, acknowledging his personal unworthiness, offers thanks on behalf of the whole priesthood for being accounted worthy to minister at the divine mysteries and distribute the divine mercy richly poured forth upon the Church (Titus 3:6). The prayer continues onto fol. 626r (leaf p1267).
[^3864]: Ps. 132:2 LXX (Ps. 133:2 KJV): "It is like the precious ointment upon the head, that ran down upon the beard, even Aaron's beard: that went down to the skirts of his garments."
[^3865]: Canticles (Song of Songs) 1:2 LXX (1:3 KJV): "Thy name is as ointment [myron] poured forth (μύρον ἐκκενωθὲν ὄνομά σου), therefore do the virgins love thee." Patristic commentary universally interprets this verse as a prophecy of the Incarnation of Christ and the diffusion of the grace of the Holy Spirit throughout the world.
[^3866]: Cosmic scope of Christ's redemptive grace, diffusing spiritual sweetness and divine fragrance throughout all creation, both visible and invisible (cf. 2 Cor. 2:14–15; Col. 1:16).
[^3867]: Concluding Trinitarian ecphonesis of the prayer of head-bowing, praising God as the sole object of supreme adoration and worship.
[^3868]: Rubric indicating the resumption of the Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great: the deacon on the ambo resumes the litany, the holy royal doors are reopened, and the bishop proceeds with the Liturgy of the Faithful, consummating the Eucharistic sacrifice.
