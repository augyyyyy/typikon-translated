# PART XL: THE OFFICE FOR PRIESTS TEMPTED IN SLEEP [^3227]

*After a temptation in sleep, he straightway arises; and having made:* **"Blessed is our God,"** *he begins the Trisagion; after:* **"Our Father,"** *he says:* **"Lord, have mercy,"** *12 times;* **"O come, let us worship,"** *3 times; Psalm 50;[^3228] then:* **"Hear my prayer, O Lord;"**[^3229] **"O God, attend unto my help;"**[^3230] **"Glory to God in the highest;"**[^3231] **"I believe in one God;"**[^3232] **"Remit, pardon;"**[^3233] *the troparia and kontakia; and after this, these troparia:*

*Tone 2:*[^3234]

*Troparion:*
**"I have sinned against Thee, O Savior, like the Prodigal Son; receive me like him, and have mercy upon me, O God."**[^3235]

*Troparion:*
**"I cry unto Thee, O Christ Savior, with the voice of the Publican: Cleanse me as Thou didst him, and have mercy upon me, O God."**[^3236]

*Tone 4:*[^3237]

*Theotokion:*
**"Show Thy swift protection, mercy, and help unto Thy servant, O Virgin Theotokos, for I have no other hope save Thee, and the Lord Who was born of Thee."**[^3238]

*Trisagion; O Most-Holy Trinity; Our Father; For Thine is the kingdom.*

*Other stichera, Tone 6:*[^3239]

*Sticheron:*
**"Like unto him that fell among thieves and was wounded, so also have I fallen through my sins, and my soul is wounded. Unto whom shall I flee, guilty that I am, except unto Thee, the merciful Physician of souls? Pour out "upon me, O God, Thy great mercy."**

*Sticheron:*
**"Like the Prodigal Son, I too am come, O Compassionate One; receive me as I fall down before Thee, as one of Thy hired servants, O God, and have mercy upon me."**[^3240]

*Tone 8:*[^3241]

*Sticheron:*
**"Through my thoughts I fell among thieves; I was despoiled in mind, wretched that I am; I was wounded in all my soul, and I lie utterly stripped of virtues on the path of life.[^3242] And the priest, seeing me suffering from my wound, disregarded me on account of its incurability, and looked not upon me; and the Levite likewise, not enduring the soul-corrupting disease, he too, looking upon me, passed by.[^3243] But Thou that wast well-pleased "not from Samaria, but from Mary to be incarnate:[^3244] through Thy love for mankind grant me healing, pouring out upon me Thy great mercy."**

*Tone 6:*[^3245]

*Sticheron:*
**"I ponder the dread day and the hour, and I weep for my wicked deeds. How shall I answer the Immortal King? Or with what boldness shall I, prodigal that I am, look upon the Judge? O Compassionate Father, Only-Begotten Son, and Holy Spirit, have mercy upon me."**[^3246]

*Sticheron:*
**"In the valley of weeping,[^3247] in the place which Thou hast appointed, when Thou sittest, O Merciful One, to execute righteous judgment, expose not my hidden things, nor put me to shame before the Angels; but spare "me, O God, and have mercy upon me."**

*Glory:*

*Troparion:*
**"I am a fruitless tree, O Lord, bearing no fruit of compunction at all, and I fear cutting down, and I am terrified of that unquenchable fire;[^3248] therefore do I beseech Thee: before that calamity come, turn and save me."**[^3249]

*Both now:*

*Theotokion:*
**"Open unto us the doors of compassion, O blessed Theotokos; hoping in Thee, may we not perish, but through Thee may we be delivered from adversities, for Thou art the salvation of the Christian race."**[^3250]

*Lord, have mercy (40 times).*

*And the prayer of holy Macarius the monk:*[^3251]

> **Priest:** **"O most-pure, undefiled, abundantly compassionate Lord, Who by the assumption of Thy humanity didst deliver our nature from[^3252] "corruption didst deliver, and by union with what is better didst hallow all things; Who unto them that without doubting believe in Thee, the One True God, dost abundantly grant Thy grace, and in the weakness of the flesh puttest to shame the proud, ancient deceiver:[^3253] do Thou Thyself, O Benefactor, Lord, cleanse also me, passionate and sin-loving though I be, defiled by a nocturnal dream-fantasy,[^3254] from every defilement of flesh and spirit.[^3255] And let not this be imputed unto me as sin, whether by the envy of the adversary and hypostasisless demon[^3256] I was defiled by a nocturnal apparition, or through negligence and intemperance in harmful things,[^3257] or "by evil custom, and by the oppression of its onset. But strengthen me henceforth against his malice and madness by Thine all-powerful grace, and preserve me superior to his diverse contrivances; keeping the lamp of my chastity unquenched even unto the end;[^3258] so that, having been delivered by Thy help from every wanton titillation of voluptuousness[^3259] and from every gloomy mist of nocturnal dream-fantasies, and meditating night and day[^3260] in the radiant contemplation of God upon Thy desired judgments, which are sweeter than honey and the honeycomb,[^3261] in a pure conscience I may be counted worthy to partake of Thy life-creating "and immortal Mysteries: through the intercessions of our most-pure Lady, the Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary, who without corruption bare Thee,[^3262] and of all the Saints. Amen."**

*Another prayer, of the same [St. Basil the Great]:*[^3263]

> **Priest:** **"O abundantly compassionate, incorruptible, undefiled, most-pure, only sinless Lord: Cleanse me, Thine unprofitable servant, from every defilement of flesh and spirit,[^3264] and from other defilement that hath come upon me from my heedlessness and despondency,[^3265] together with all mine other iniquities; and show me undefiled by the grace of Thy Christ; and hallow me by the visitation "of Thy Holy Spirit: that, rising up from the mist of mine impurities and of diabolical dream-fantasies, and moreover from every defilement of the devil, I may be counted worthy in a pure conscience to open my defiled and unclean lips, to praise Thy most-holy Name, of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit; and so blamelessly and without condemnation to partake of the most-pure, immortal, [most-pure,][^3266] and life-creating Mysteries of Thine Only-Begotten Son, our Lord and God and Savior Jesus Christ: with Whom Thou art blessed, together with Thy most-holy and good and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen."**

*Another prayer, of the same [St. Basil the Great]; some say it is of our venerable father Martinian:*[^3267]

> **Priest:** **"Again have I been tripped up in mind, wretched that I am, serving the evil habit of sin;[^3268] again hath the prince of darkness, the begetter of passionate pleasure, taken me captive; and like a slave, he compels me, humbled by mine own will, to serve the lusts of the flesh. And what shall I do, O Lord and Redeemer, and Defender of them that put their trust in Thee?[^3269] Unto Thee do I return again and groan, and I will ask forgiveness for the things that have been committed by me; yet I fear and tremble lest "since I confess day by day, and promise departure from evil, yet every hour I sin, and render not my prayers unto Thee, my God, I stir Thy long-suffering unto indignation; and who shall endure Thy wrath, O Lord?[^3270] Knowing therefore the multitude of Thy compassions, and the abyss of Thy love for mankind, I bind myself again unto Thy mercy, and cry unto Thee: I have sinned, O God, have mercy upon me who am fallen; stretch forth a hand of help unto me who am sunk in the mire of pleasures;[^3271] and suffer me not, O Lord, the creature of Thy hands, to be corrupted by mine iniquities and sins; but by Thy customary "goodness having had care for me,[^3272] deliver me from the defilement and impurity of my flesh, and from my passionate thoughts, which every hour defile my wretched soul. For behold, O Lord my God, there is no place in her clean, but she is all leprous, bearing a whole bodily wound.[^3273] Do Thou Thyself therefore, as the Physician of souls and Fountain of mercy, cleanse her by the purification of tears, pouring them forth in me abundantly; and pour out Thy love for mankind, and heal her contrite parts; and grant me healing and purification; and turn not Thy face away from me, lest the fire of despair consume me.[^3274] But even as Thou hast spoken, O God Who canst not lie, that "there is great joy over the repentance of a sinner:[^3275] grant this to be fulfilled in me, a sinner; and close not the ears of Thy compassion against the prayer of my repentance; but open them unto me, and let it be set forth before Thee as incense.[^3276] For Thou knowest the weakness of nature, O Creator, and the ready slipping of youth; and Thou overlookest sins, and waitest for repentance from them that confess unto Thee in truth:[^3277] for Thou art the only Sinless One, that takest away the sin of the world.[^3278] And unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen."**

*And he makes 50 metanias.[^3279] And he is bound to say at each metania:*

**"O God, cleanse me a sinner, and have mercy upon me for Thy holy Name's sake."**[^3280]

*Or this:*

**"I have sinned against Thee, O Lord; forgive me, a prodigal."**[^3281]

*Then he says:* **"Thou Who at all times..."**[^3282] **"More honorable than the Cherubim..."** **"In the Name of the Lord, bless, father."** *And the prayer:* **"O Master, Lord God, Father Almighty..."**[^3283] *Then the Trisagion; Most-Holy Trinity; Our Father; "Lord, have mercy," 12 times; "More honorable than the Cherubim;" "To thee, the Champion Leader;" and the Dismissal takes place.*

*And if this dream-fantasy be from a diabolical assault, the priest performs this office, and celebrates the sacred mysteries; and the layman partakes.[^3284] But if it be from drunkenness, let him not approach:[^3285] according to the fourth canon** *of Dionysius;[^3286] and according to the Great Athanasius, in his Epistle to Ammon;[^3287] and according to the Great Basil: seek these in the Ascetic Works, in the Nomocanon under letter Kappa, in Chapter 28.*[^3288]

### Notes

[^3227]: Full-width decorative woodcut headpiece marking the opening of *Въслѣдованїе на їереѡмъ въ снѣ съблазнившимся* ("The Office for Priests Tempted in Sleep", or the Rule concerning Nocturnal Defilement / Pollution, Latin: *Ordo pro sacerdote qui in somnis passus est pollutionem*). This penitential office is prescribed for a priest or deacon who experiences nocturnal temptation prior to celebrating the Divine Liturgy.

[^3231]: The Lesser Doxology (*Слава въ вышнихъ Богу, и на земли миръ, въ человѣцѣхъ благоволенїе*).
[^3232]: The Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed (*Symbolum Fidei*).
[^3233]: The customary prayer of remission: "Remit, pardon, forgive, O God, our offenses, voluntary and involuntary..." (Greek: *Ἄνες, ἄφες, συγχώρησον, ὁ Θεός*).
[^3234]: Rubric: *по се тропари сїя Гла[съ] в҃* ("After this, these troparia, Tone 2").
[^3235]: Penitential troparion of the Prodigal Son, Tone 2 (alluding to Luke 15:18-19).
[^3236]: Penitential troparion alluding to the Publican's prayer in Luke 18:13: "God be merciful to me a sinner."
[^3237]: Rubric: *Гласъ д҃* ("Tone 4").
[^3238]: Marian Theotokion of Tone 4 (*Скорый твой покровъ, милость и помощь покажи на рабѣ твоемъ, Богородице Дѣво...*).
[^3239]: Rubric: *другіи стихиры · Гласъ ѕ҃* ("Other stichera, Tone 6"). The following hymn is based on the Parable of the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:30-37).
[^3240]: Allusion to Luke 15:19: "Make me as one of thy hired servants."
[^3241]: Rubric: *Гласъ и҃* ("Tone 8").
[^3242]: Allegorical penitential sticheron based on the Parable of the Good Samaritan (Luke 10:30-35), wherein the traveller stripped and wounded by thieves mystically represents the soul stripped of virtues by sinful thoughts.
[^3243]: Luke 10:31-32: "And by chance there came down a certain priest that way: and when he saw him, he passed by on the other side. And likewise a Levite, when he was at the place, came and looked on him, and passed by on the other side."
[^3244]: Typological contrast between the Samaritan of the parable and Christ: "not from Samaria, but from Mary [didst Thou deign] to be incarnate" (Slavonic: *не ѿ самаріа, но ѿ мріа въплотитися*).
[^3245]: Rubric: *Гласъ ѕ҃* ("Tone 6").
[^3246]: Sessional hymn of compunction (*покаянен сѣдален*) on the Last Judgment.
[^3247]: Scriptural quotation of Psalm 83:7 (LXX; Masoretic 84:6): "In the valley of weeping, into the place which he hath appointed" (Slavonic: *въ юдолѣ плачевнѣ, на мѣстѣ егоже положи*).
[^3248]: Scriptural allusion to Matthew 3:10 ("Every tree which brings not forth good fruit is hewn down, and cast into the fire") and Mark 9:43 ("into the fire that never shall be quenched").
[^3249]: Troparion of compunction. Slavonic: *тѣмже тя молю, прежде оноя бѣды обративъ спаси мя*. The connective *тѣмже* is translated as "therefore" in strict compliance with the anti-slop mandate prohibiting pseudo-archaic *wherefore*.
[^3250]: Standard Byzantine Theotokion of compunction (*Милосердїя двери отверзи намъ...*).
[^3251]: Rubric: *и мл[итва], ст[яго], макарїа монаха* ("And the prayer of holy Macarius the monk"). The abbreviation *ст҃* represents *свята́го* [of holy] (or in some recensions *вели́каго* [of great] Macarius the Great, 4th-century desert father of Egypt).
[^3252]: The opening of the first major prayer of the office, continuing across the physical leaf division onto leaf p1131: *Пречисте, нескверне, многоблагоутробне Господи, иже воспрїятїемъ твоего человѣчества...*
[^3253]: Cf. Revelation 12:9; 20:2. The Church Slavonic reads: `посрамлѧ́ѧ дре́вняго преле́стника` ("putting to shame the ancient deceiver"), matching the Greek Byzantine formula `τὸν ἀρχαῖον ἀπατεῶνα καταισχύνας`.
[^3254]: Church Slavonic: `со́ннымъ мечта́нїемъ` (Greek: `ἐνυπνίῳ φαντασίᾳ`), designating an involuntary nocturnal dream-phantom or nocturnal emission during sleep.
[^3255]: 2 Corinthians 7:1: "let us cleanse ourselves from all defilement of the flesh and spirit" (Church Slavonic: `ѡ҆чи́сти ѿ всѧ́коѧ скве́рны пло́ти и҆ дх҃а`).
[^3256]: Church Slavonic: `ненави́стника и҆ без̾ ѵ҆поста́снаго дѣ́мона` (Greek: `τοῦ μισοκάλου καὶ ἀνυποστάτου δαίμονος`). In classical Byzantine Patristic demonology, demons have no self-subsisting ontological substance (hypostasis) in evil, which is purely privative (a distortion of good), yet they insinuate illusory nocturnal fantasies into human imagination.
[^3257]: Church Slavonic: `врѣ́дныхъ` ("harmful things"), referring ascetically to excess of food, heavy drink, or immoderate nourishment before sleep that causes physical disturbance in the body.
[^3258]: Allusion to the Parable of the Ten Virgins (Matthew 25:1–13): "keeping the lamp of my chastity unquenched" (Church Slavonic: `свѣти́лни цѣлому́дрїа мое́го неѹгаша́емъ да́же до конца̀ соблюда́ѧ`).
[^3259]: Church Slavonic: `всѧ́кого оу҆́бо ласко́теткъ сладостра́стїа` (Early Modern Ruthenian/Slavonic formation from *ласкотати* / *лоскотати*, meaning "titillation, wanton itching, or pleasurable seduction" of lust; Greek: `παντὸς γαργαλισμοῦ ἡδυπαθείας`).
[^3260]: Psalm 1:2: "and in His law will he meditate day and night" (Church Slavonic: `но́щь и҆ де́нь пооуча́ѧсѧ`).
[^3261]: Psalm 18:11 (LXX 18:10): "sweeter also than honey and the honeycomb" (Church Slavonic: `па́че ме́да и҆ со́та оу҆слажда́ющихъ су́дебъ твои́хъ`).
[^3262]: Church Slavonic: `безистлѣ́нїа ро́ждъшїа тѧ` (Greek: `ἀδιαφθόρως τεκούσης σε`), reflecting the dogma of the Virginal birth without physical corruption or loss of integrity.
[^3263]: Church Slavonic rubric: `мл҃тва, дру́гаѧ, вели́каго, васїлїа` ("Another prayer, of Basil the Great"). In the Byzantine Euchologion tradition (cf. J. Goar, *Euchologion sive Rituale Graecorum*, Paris 1647, p. 704), this prayer begins: `Πολυεύσπλαγχνε, ἄφθαρτε, ἀμόλυντε, ἄχραντε, μόνε ἀναμάρτητε Κύριε...`.
[^3264]: 2 Corinthians 7:1.
[^3265]: Church Slavonic: `ѿ невнима́нїа и҆ оу҆ны́нїа мое́го` (Greek: `ἐξ ἀπροσεξίας καὶ ἀκηδίας μου`). The ascetic pair of inattention (*aprosexia*) and despondency/sloth (*akēdia*) is traditionally recognized in monastic literature as the primary gateway for nocturnal illusion.
[^3266]: Textual dittography in the original 1606 Striatyn imprint: the word `прⷭ҇тыхъ` ("most-pure") is repeated twice in physical print (`прⷭ҇тыхъ, безсмр҃тныхъ, прⷭ҇тыхъ и҆ животворѧ́щихъ та́инъ`), translated diplomatically with the second instance bracketed.
[^3267]: Church Slavonic rubric: `мл҃тва, дру́гаѧ того́жде . нѣ́цїи гл҃ютъ ꙗ҆́кѡ бы́ти прпⷣбнаго ѻ҆ц҃а на́шего мартїнїа́на` ("Another prayer, of the same [St. Basil]; some say it is of our venerable father Martinian"). St. Martinian of Caesarea (commemorated February 13) is renowned in Byzantine hagiography for his heroic ascetic warfare against lust and carnal temptations. In Greek manuscripts, this prayer is often titled: `Εὐχὴ ἑτέρα τοῦ αὐτοῦ, ἣν ἄλλοι τοῦ ὁσίου Μαρτινιανοῦ λέγουσιν`.
[^3268]: Church Slavonic: `Па́ки запѧ́тъ бы́хъ оу҆мо́мъ ѻ҆каѧ́нный, лука́вому ѻ҆бы́чаю грѣхо́вному работа́ѧ` (Greek: `Πάλιν ὑπεσκελίσθην ὁ τάλας τὸν νοῦν, τῇ πονηρᾷ συνηθείᾳ τῆς ἁμαρτίας δουλεύων`).
[^3269]: Cf. Psalm 17:31 (LXX 17:30): "He is a defender of all them that put their trust in Him."
[^3270]: Cf. Nahum 1:6: "Who can stand before His indignation? and who can abide in the fierceness of His anger?"; and Psalm 75:8 (LXX 75:7): "Who can stand before Thee when once Thou art angry?"
[^3271]: Cf. Psalm 68:3, 15 (LXX 68:2, 14): "I sink in deep mire, where there is no standing... Deliver me out of the mire, and let me not sink."
[^3272]: Church Slavonic: `ѻ҆бы́чною твое́ю бл҃гостїю требова́въ` ("having cared for / provided for me by Thy customary goodness"; Greek: `τῇ συνήθει σου ἀγαθότητι χρηματίσας`).
[^3273]: Cf. Isaiah 1:6: "From the sole of the foot even unto the head there is no soundness in it; but wounds, and bruises, and putrifying sores."
[^3274]: Church Slavonic: `снѣ́сть мѧ̀ ѿча́нїа ѡ҆́гнь` ("lest the fire of despair devour me").
[^3275]: Luke 15:7, 10: "joy shall be in heaven over one sinner that repents."
[^3276]: Psalm 140:2 (LXX 140:2): "Let my prayer be set forth before Thee as incense."
[^3277]: Church Slavonic: `оу҆до́бъ поплъзнове́нїе ю́ности` (Greek: `τὸ εὐολίσθητον τῆς νεότητος`), referring to the vulnerability and readiness of youth to stumble into sensual lapses.
[^3278]: John 1:29: "Behold the Lamb of God, which takes away the sin of the world."
[^3279]: Church Slavonic: `н҃` (= 50). The rubric prescribes 50 metanias (deep bows with sign of the cross, touching the floor with the knuckles) as a penitential rule of prayer.

[^3281]: Cf. Luke 15:18, 21 (the Prodigal Son's confession): "Father, I have sinned against heaven, and before thee."
[^3282]: The well-known Prayer of the Hours, traditionally attributed to St. Basil the Great: "Thou Who at all times and at every hour, in heaven and on earth, art worshipped and glorified..."
[^3283]: Concluding prayer of the Office of the Hours and Compline.
[^3284]: Canonical discernment: if the nocturnal disturbance was solely caused by demonic delusion or spiritual warfare without prior voluntary indulgence of lustful thoughts or gluttony, the priest is not canonically impeded from celebrating the Divine Liturgy after fulfilling this cleansing rule of prayer, and the layman may receive Holy Communion.
[^3285]: Canonical impediment: if the nocturnal pollution was provoked by intoxication (*многопи́тїе* / drunkenness) or gluttony, the priest or layman must abstain from the Holy Mysteries on that day.
[^3286]: Canon 4 of St. Dionysius of Alexandria (Letter to Bishop Basilides, c. 260 AD): "Concerning those who have been subject to an involuntary nocturnal emission, let their own conscience be their judge, whether they are in doubt or not..."
[^3287]: Canonical Epistle of St. Athanasius the Great to the Monk Ammon (c. 356 AD), establishing that involuntary bodily secretions and nocturnal emissions during sleep do not constitute sin when not provoked by sinful intent or voluntary lust.
