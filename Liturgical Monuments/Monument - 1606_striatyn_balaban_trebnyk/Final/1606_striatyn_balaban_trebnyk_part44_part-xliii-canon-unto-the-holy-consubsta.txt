# PART XLIII: CANON UNTO THE HOLY, CONSUBSTANTIAL, LIFE-CREATING, AND UNDIVIDED TRINITY, AND UNTO ALL SAINTS, FOR THE OVERCOMING OF THE PESTILENTIAL SICKNESS [^3610]

*Canon unto the Holy, Consubstantial, Life-Creating, and Undivided Trinity, and unto All Saints, for the Overcoming of the Pestilential Sickness:*[^3611]

*Ode 1, Tone 8. Heirmos:*[^3612]

*Tone 8. Heirmos:* **"He plunged the armed Pharaoh into the deep, [working] wonders..."**[^3613]

*Troparion:*

**"O All-creating, and enthroned, and co-enthroned, and of equal power, and thrice-radiant Glory: O incomprehensible Father, Son, and Holy Spirit![^3614] Deliver Thy servants from the grievous sickness, that with thanksgiving we may glorify Thee."**[^3615]

*Troparion:*

**"A storm of sins has cast me down into the depth of weakness and frequent sicknesses;[^3616] even as surging billows they tempest-toss me, wretched one: O dominion of equal power, Holy Trinity, have tender compassion; save me who am grievously perishing!"**[^3617]

*Troparion:*

**"Deliver us Thy servants from the prevailing sin, O undivided Trinity, quenching with the dew of Thy mercy the parching heat of grievous sicknesses, and granting health, that in orthodox manner we may hymn Thee[^3618] that in orthodox manner we may hymn Thee."**[^3619]

*Theotokion:*

**"Having borne in the womb the Deliverer, and Creator, and Lord, Who bare our infirmities, O All-pure One: do thou entreat Him, that thy servants may be delivered from the grievous sickness, O only helper of men!"**[^3620]

*Ode 3, Tone 8. Heirmos:* **"O Lord, Builder of the vault of heaven, [and of the Church...]"**[^3621]

*Troparion:*

**"Ye heavenly minds, angelic ranks, Thrones and Principalities, and Powers and Dominions, entreat Thee, the Benefactor and Savior:[^3622] free Thy servants from the destructive disease!"**[^3623]

*Troparion:*

**"That Thou mayest show forth in abundance the abyss of Thy love for mankind, O Master, O All-powerful One: free Thy servants from the death-bearing disease[^3624] from the death-bearing disease and from grievous sicknesses, O Thou Who alone art long-suffering!"**[^3625]

*Troparion:*

**"As friends of God and ministering spirits, ye Angels and Archangels, entreat Him that the disease may cease, that the plague may be destroyed, and that He may deliver us from death-bearing destructions."**[^3626]

*Theotokion:*

**"Christ the Lord, the Abyss of good things, coming forth from thee, showed thee forth as a fountain of healings, O all-spotless Maiden; therefore deliver thy servants who are tempest-tossed by the storm of sicknesses."**[^3627]

*Ode 4, Tone 8. Heirmos:* **"Thou art my Lord, O Christ, Thou art my strength, Thou [art my God...]"**[^3628]

*Troparion:*

**"Now truly the shadow of death has compassed us, and we have drawn near unto the gates of hades;[^3629] but do Thou Thyself, O Savior, as the Mighty One, raise us up; make marvelous Thy mercies, saving those who cry in unhesitating faith: Glory to Thy power, O Lover of mankind![^3630] Glory to Thy power, O Lover of mankind!"**[^3631]

*Troparion:*

**"O initiates of the mysteries of Christ, eyewitnesses and heralds, having received the gift of healings, and being spiritual physicians:[^3632] lead me out from distress, ye Apostles, and from the prevailing disease, entreating our Master and Deliverer and Lord!"**[^3633]

*Troparion:*

**"A storm of sins has now overtaken us; the billows of disease tempest-toss us; frequent sicknesses plunge us into the deep; tribulations and sicknesses have found us, wretched ones: O Apostles of the Lord, by your supplications reach forth a hand of help!"**[^3634]

*Theotokion:*[^3635]

**"Stricken by grievous and frequent sicknesses, O Virgin, we all fall down before thee: under thy sovereign protection, O Pure One, save us all; show compassion, O Bride of God, deliver from the plague and from the grievous disease, and heal our sicknesses, O Lady!"**[^3636]

*Ode 5, Tone 8. Heirmos:* **"Why hast Thou cast me off from [Thy presence, O Light unwaning...]"**[^3637]

*Troparion:*

**"Ye who have made fragrant the sea of delusion with your honey-flowing supplications, O holy Prophets, transform now all the bitterness of the destructive disease into divine sweetness by your efficacious succor!"**[^3638]

*Troparion:*

**"We have been pierced through by the arrows of sicknesses at Thy command, O Lord, and Thy hand has been heavily fixed upon us, O All-powerful One:[^3639] as the compassionate God, show compassion upon all by Thy tender mercy, through the prayers of Thy holy Martyrs[^3640] through the prayers of Thy holy Martyrs!"**[^3641]

*Troparion:*

**"Even as of old Thou didst raise up the widow's son by Thy command: having delivered Thy servants who have been mortified by the grievous sickness, O Word, quicken them, as Thou alone art good and compassionate, O Thou Who alone art the Lover of mankind!"**[^3642]

*Theotokion:*

**"In the night of this life, a storm of every affliction has overtaken me; the deep night of infirmity has covered me, O Virgin: but dawn upon me the light of refreshment, O All-pure One, and guide me unto the light of goodly help!"**[^3643]

*Ode 6, Tone 8. Heirmos:* **"Cleanse me, O Savior, [for many are mine iniquities...]"**[^3644]

*Troparion:*

**"We are encompassed in an exceedingly abyss-like deep of sicknesses, and the billows of destructive calamity stir us up: O Lord Helmsman, stretching forth a hand of help, save us now![^3645] save us now!"**

*Troparion:*

**"Even as of old, having braced the paralytic by Thy divine beckoning from the painful disease and the bed of affliction:[^3646] having shown compassion, grant health from heavy sickness, O Many-merciful One!"**

*Troparion:*

**"The choir of the Prophets, the assembly of the Apostles, and the host of the Sufferers entreat Thee now concerning Thy people, O Thou Who alone art Many-merciful:[^3647] O Good One, show compassion upon them!"**

*Theotokion:*

**"O Mary, pure treasury of virginity, cleanse us, and deliver us from diseases and tribulations and from the sickness that now prevails, that in faith we may glorify thee!"**[^3648]

*Kontakion, Tone 4, Podoben: "Thou hast appeared today" (Ꙗ҆ви́сѧ дне́сь):*[^3649]

**"An insurmountable protection unto the afflicted, and a ready intercessor unto those who place hope in thee: deliver from misfortunes, and despise us not, O defender of all!"**[^3650]

*Ode 7, Tone 8. Heirmos:* **"The fire was ashamed before the condescension of God..."**[^3651]

*Troparion:*

**"The furnace of immeasurable sicknesses scorches me, and the burning fever of all-destructive [plague] consumes me, a most shameless flame unceasingly;[^3652] but with the dew of Thy mercy, O Savior, refresh him who sings: Blessed is the God of our fathers!"**

*Troparion:*

**"Ye Prophets and Apostles, assemblies of Martyrs, and divine Teachers: by your supplications assuage the sickness of us who are ailing, and grant health as we sing: Blessed is the God of our fathers![^3653] grant health as we sing: Blessed is the God of our fathers!"**

*Troparion:*

**"O Word, Who didst raise up Lazarus:[^3654] now, as from a grave, having raised us up from the grievous sickness, make us alive, O Lord, that we may cry aloud a song of thanksgiving: Blessed is the God of our fathers!"**

*Theotokion:*

**"Being compassionate, and Mother of the All-compassionate One:[^3655] having had compassion, deliver the people who invoke thy mercies, O Virgin, and who sing: Blessed is the God of our fathers!"**

*Ode 8, Tone 8. Heirmos:* **"Sevenfold the furnace the Chaldean [tyrant fiercely kindled...]"**[^3656]

*Troparion:*

**"In pain we groan from the bed of our sickness, and in the destructive disease we cry unto Thee, the Lover of mankind; stretching out now the eyes of the heart, we ask for health: visit us, O Savior, and[^3657] raise [us] up to sing: Ye priests, sing; ye people, supremely exalt Him unto the ages!"**

*Troparion:*

**"O Thou Who hast mercifully clothed Thyself in our weakness, and wast well-pleased to be made like unto men:[^3658] by the prayers of Thy venerable ones,[^3659] save us who are in despair, and raise [us] up from the grave of despair to sing: Ye children, bless; ye priests, sing; ye people, supremely exalt Him unto the ages!"**[^3660]

*Troparion:*

**"O Creator of good things, Giver of healing, as Thou hast the bowels of compassion and the abyss of loving-kindness, O Long-suffering One:[^3661] with Thy visitation, visit Thy people [to deliver them] from the destructive sickness, and quicken [them] to cry aloud: Ye priests, bless; ye people, supremely exalt Him unto ages!"**[^3662]

*Theotokion:*

**"O mighty help, certain protection, most blameless hope of those in despair: visit Thy servants who suffer in sickness; lighten the burden of bitter disease; drive away the distress of the destructive sickness, and save Thy servants, O Virgin Theotokos!"**[^3663]

*Ode 9, Tone 8. Heirmos:* **"Heaven was amazed thereat, [and the ends of the earth were astonished...]"**[^3664]

*Troparion:*

**"O Immortal One, Who hast worked great wonders without number: show forth Thy mercies upon Thy servants, O God, as Thou art compassionate, and free us from the sickness that now holds us fast, through the supplications of Her that bare Thee, and of the choir of Thy trophy-bearers!"**[^3665]

*Troparion:*

**"By the prayers of Thy Angels and Archangels, of Prophets and Apostles, Martyrs, Venerable ones, and Hierarchs and of Thy Hieromartyrs: turn the bitter lamentation of Thy servants into joy, O All-powerful One; heal the sickness, lighten the diseases, and grant us health!"**[^3666]

*Troparion:*

**"Thee do I entreat, the Physician of souls and bodies, the Lord rich in mercy:[^3667] heal my many afflictions, take away [my] sicknesses, and deliver me from sorrows, as Thou art good and the only Benefactor, and save those who in faith purely magnify Thee!"**[^3668]

*Theotokion:*

**"O thou who hast borne the Compassionate and Merciful Master, Creator, and Lord: show forth now upon me thy customary compassions; deliver me from the grievous sickness that consumes my soul, O Virgin Theotokos, and grant me health, that unceasingly I may magnify thee!"**[^3669]

*Another prayer, against sickness in time of pestilence:*[^3674]

**"O Almighty Master, Long-suffering, Plenteous in mercy, easily reconciled, Lover of mankind, All-good, Exceeding Good, All-powerful Christ our God: Thou hast brought our nature from non-existence into[^3675] being for the sake of the sheer abundance of Thy goodness; Thou, having breathed into us an intelligent soul and the word,[^3676] hast honored us with Thine image; Thou hast vouchsafed unto us the delight of Paradise, God-like citizenship therein, and exceeding great divine converse.[^3677] When we cast away Thy commandment, and were despoiled through the craft and deceit of the demon, and fell away from Thy glory, and exchanged life and the kingdom for death and shameful slavery: Thou didst not despise [us] by reason of the ineffable abyss of Thy long-suffering and goodness, but didst visit in diverse manners through the Fathers and the Patriarchs.[^3678] Thou[^3679] didst give us a Law in the Scriptures for our conversion and instruction; Thou didst appoint Angels also, even guardians, teaching and guiding [us] how to attain unto that which is better.[^3680] And when we showed forth all these things to be vain and futile by our immeasurable inclination toward wickedness: Thou Thyself didst ineffably fashion the second creation and renewal of our nature at the end of the ages; and having fearfully united all our human nature with the fullness of Thy Divinity—sanctifying flesh with flesh, and soul with soul—and having delivered us from unnatural passions and death by Thy death and passions; and[^3681] and having vouchsafed immortal life through the keeping of Thy divine commandments: yet we have again transgressed these divine commandments, and walking after our lusts and desires, we diligently work every sin day by day: slander, blasphemy, malice, perjury, falsehood, foul speech, deceit, strife, envy,[^3682] and every shameful and indecent deed, both natural and unnatural; and what cannot be found even among the irrational beasts, this do we most impiously invent anew.[^3683] Our days have vanished in vanity;[^3684] we are stripped of Thy help;[^3685] we are become a reproach and a derision unto them that are round about us;[^3686] by us we have caused Thy holy and adorable Name to be blasphemed among the gentiles;[^3687] we have grown old in malice, and on the paths we have foolishly become lame; we all have turned aside, together we are become unprofitable; there is none that understands, no, not one;[^3688] priests and people together have fallen away.[^3689] For these things, by righteous judgment we have been cast out from Thee, and are rejected from Thy great protection; and wretched that we are, we have turned Thy manifold love for mankind into indignation. Plagues and captivity, and affliction and poverty, and[^3690] diverse and frequent deaths have sundered us exceedingly wretched ones, that by such scourges Thou mightest draw unto Thyself even those unwilling, O Lover of mankind; yet not even so did anything more come from us.[^3691] Thou didst deliver us into slavery to the nations, and into captivity, and slaughter, and despoilment by the lawless; and not even so did we understand, nor did we depart from our wickedness and from our iniquities.[^3692] When for a little while Thou didst marvelously draw us out from slavery, and beyond all hope didst free us from the ungodly: yet all the more did we add iniquity unto iniquity; and puffed up by Thy benefactions, having used freedom and relief for evil[^3693] for these things, O Exceeding Good Master—inasmuch as we are neither chastened by afflictions and bitter tribulations, nor become better on account of Thy benefactions and bounties—by righteous judgment Thou hast condemned us, and hast delivered us unto destruction, and utter slaughter, and final vanishing; so that plainly we are condemned to be utterly crushed and to vanish, by reason of which Thou hast thus unsparingly mown down the inheritance of our race before its time, so that according to the Scripture, neither root nor seed remains unto Israel;[^3694] but that we should be even as Sodom and even as Gomorrah, and those whom the violent deluge of old corrupted[^3695] with their very roots and with their very branches.[^3696] For neither does the sickness, having fallen upon us, carry off the dead gradually in measure; but as though a slaughterer were present, delivering unto each a deadly wound with the sword: so together did many receive both the blow and the sudden token of death,[^3697] and together darkness and death have suddenly covered all; so that no sooner have they said: 'We are struck,' than death straightway follows hard upon their heels; so that two and three are carried forth together from one and the same house, and are escorted unto burial, lying heaped together upon a single bier[^3698] and it is not even possible for those who remain to ask of neighbors who are perishing;[^3699] nor to have any kinsman to whom they might flee and go; for all simply together are piteously corrupted and sent down to Hades. But being hemmed in on every side by perplexity and helpless dismay, nor can one find what calamity it is meet to lament more: whether the untimely deaths of the present hour, or that men are thus snatched away suddenly; or those who have departed before, or those who after this are expiring, or those who in a little while are about to suffer this; or those who still think they exist and are free from the disease[^3700] yea, above all it is time to lament and weep for all, since not even a single hope is left; scarcely are we ourselves not numbered among the dead in a little while. Thus hopeless on every side do we lie, like flocks awaiting slaughter.[^3701] Today there surrounds fathers and mothers a choir of children, a choir of grandchildren, a choir of other kinsfolk and friends, so that houses are crowded with the multitude of those gathered together in great numbers by kinship and friendship; but on the morrow [they are left] childless, desolate, wretched, cast down;[^3702] and as those utterly cut off from all things, having completely laid aside all hope, and [being deprived] of life and of [solace in] old age, and of the posterity of the race that was to be, and together deprived of all natural and beloved and necessary friends. We are terrified therefore, O Master, Lover of mankind, by the magnitude of this intolerable calamity and scourge, and we are well-nigh beside ourselves; and perplexity and distress held us fast; and unutterable fear and trembling came upon us, and the shadow of death covered us;[^3703] and we were at our wits' end and utterly despaired. For neither do we reckon ourselves to be among the living, nor are we in a settled state of mind; but each one imagines that he has the wound and token upon himself[^3704] frequently touching his limbs, every hour being alarmed: if even a gnat alights upon him, or any insect bites him, supposing that ruin has come; and from terror, even before death, he thinks himself dead.[^3705] Therefore also we all walk about as half-dead, paralyzed, and on every side perplexed, finding not even a single refuge. And what is worse, neither dare we wretched ones lift up our eyes unto heaven, nor open our mouths and pray before Thee, being pricked by the goad of conscience;[^3706] but having our evil deeds, filled with every uncleanness, before our eyes, covered with shame of face, we judge ourselves unworthy of divine help and of Thy protection. This only, contrite in heart and humbled, overtaken [by distress], do we cry unto Thee, the good God: Righteous art Thou, O Lord, and righteous is Thy judgment in all that Thou hast brought upon us;[^3707] yea rather, Thou hast not dealt with us according to our iniquities, nor rewarded us according to our sins.[^3708] But even this scourge that is brought upon us, we confess to be more loving of mankind than it is righteous; for every scourge and wound and utter destruction brought upon us is little, yea, little in comparison with the great multitude of our iniquities. Therefore, by reason of the multitude of our sins, we have not boldness, O righteous Judge and Lord, to entreat Thy goodness that we might find release from this fearful threatening; yet the exceeding greatness of Thy tender-heartedness and the abyss of Thy compassions, O Plenteous-in-mercy Master, constrains us to take courage to seek that which is above our worthiness. Therefore, O All-good God and God of mercy, we pray Thy gentleness: stay this sharp and untimely sword of death that is brought upon us; and rebuke us not in Thy wrath,[^3709] neither deliver us over to perish utterly, for the sake of Thy holy Name. Neither withdraw Thy mercy from us, O Lover of mankind; nor break Thy covenant which Thou didst covenant with our fathers; nor make us smaller than all the nations.[^3710] Set the abyss of Thy compassions against the multitude of our evils. Against Thee only have we sinned, O Lord, yet Thee only do we serve; beside Thee we know no other God;[^3711] before Thee alone do we stand with contrite and humble heart, and weeping we cry aloud: 'Lord, have mercy!' Receive, O Lover of mankind, this our common contrition and heartache, even as Thou didst accept the tears of Hezekiah at the sorrow of his heart, and having delivered him from his death, didst grant a span of life unto the term of his demise.[^3712] For it is said: 'Hezekiah turned his face to the wall and wept;' and straightway the Master, Lover of mankind, sent the Prophet saying unto him: 'I have heard thy prayer, and I have seen thy tears; behold, I will add unto thy years fifteen years.' Do Thou Thyself therefore, O Lord gentle and free from malice, Who hast said: 'I desire not the death of the sinner, but that he turn and live':[^3713] be entreated also for us unworthy ones, and turn away Thy most righteous wrath from us, and be not angry with us unto ages.[^3714] Thou seest, O Master, that we are diminished, and have become impoverished: [even] Thine inheritance, and Thy holy people, and the fulness of all the earth, the shadow of which covered even the mountains.[^3715] Remember, O Lover of mankind, Thy voluntary Passion, the Cross and death, which Thou didst endure for us condemned ones; and let him not seize our souls like a lion, while there is none to deliver, nor any to save.[^3716] Thou knowest, O Long-suffering One, that not even a single plague touched the inhuman Egyptians so sorely as the corruption and destruction of their firstborn;[^3717] yet upon us the calamity is beyond telling, and the cruelty is incurable unto all together—unto the first, and the middle, and the last, before their hour so[^3718] snatched away suddenly.[^3719] Have compassion, O Lord, Reconciler of all,[^3720] upon the tears and weeping of infants and sucklings, and of every unadorned and unripe age;[^3721] and stay Thy righteous indignation against us; and let the slaughter cease, O Plenteous-in-mercy Lord.[^3722] For having received the wound and the mortal token,[^3723] the little children, being sensible of the evil and corruption, gaze at us with open mouth, and earnestly look upon us with both eyes, and reach out their hands, and with tears, and with stammerings and indistinct cries,[^3724] and by such gestures as they can make, they show forth their slaughter, and bewail their destruction; and by all manner of gestures they compel us, as it were, to help them[^3725] drawing us by their gestures, they as it were compel us [to help them]. While we, being unable from any quarter to give a hand unto them, flee unto Thee Who canst do all things; and of Thee alone do we ask help, and release from calamities; for unto Thee alone all things are possible which are impossible with men.[^3726] For blessed art Thou unto ages of ages. Amen."**

*Another Prayer, Supplicatory unto God, Said in Time of Pestilence and Famine, the Composition of the Most Holy Patriarch Kallistos:*[^3727]

*Let us pray to the Lord.*

**Priest:** **"O Lord our God, Who by Thy demiurgic command hast brought this visible creation from non-being into being;[^3728] and Who thereafter didst also fashion man with Thine own hands by Thine exceeding goodness;[^3729] and having before accomplished for him all inexhaustible and bountiful provision for food and drink; yet together, through the transgression of the commandment, didst deprive him of that incorruptible nourishment; and didst profitably grant unto him another, toilsome course of life, commanding the earth to bring forth fruits for our sustenance, and that of the irrational animals in manifold and diverse ways; and unto some indeed giving an abundance of earthly seeds for their need, but unto others otherwise according to Thy providential power, wherewith Thou lookest down upon the fish of the sea by Thy goodness, when Thy right hand opens, and fills every living thing with good pleasure;[^3730] Who didst perform great and marvelous signs both in the Old [Covenant] and in the New: give ear unto me, Thy sinful and unworthy servant. Even as once, through that Joseph who bore the prefiguration of Thine Only-begotten Son, Thou didst abundantly nourish all those people of Israel;[^3731] so also now, through Thine Only-begotten Son, our true God, grant those things that are for nourishment and for our relief; and bestow upon us the transformation of all grievous things, delivering us from the expected affliction and constraint of famine and pestilence, that Thy most-holy Name may be glorified: of the Father without beginning, and of Thine Only-begotten Son, and of Thy Most-holy and Good and Life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen."**[^3732]

*Another Prayer, Similar, of the Same:*[^3733]

*Let us pray to the Lord.*

**Priest:** **"O God of wonders, Who hast done great and marvelous things;[^3734] Who by Moses didst deliver ancient Israel from the bitter bondage of Pharaoh;[^3735] Who by Thy right hand didst guide him, and preservedst him unharmed unto the land of promise;[^3736] Who aforetime didst feed those myriad-numbered people unto fullness with those birds [of heaven] in[^3737] the exceedingly dry wilderness;[^3738] Who didst rain down manna like rain from an ever-flowing spring;[^3739] Who didst order the uncut rock to shower forth springs of water, and satisfiedst with the gushing forth of water all those who were tormented with thirst:[^3740] so also now nourish us by Thine accustomed love for mankind and goodness; grant unto us the things that are necessary, and make us self-sufficient for ministry and for our sustenance; show forth in us also Thy great and marvelous deeds; and satisfy us unto fullness with the blessing of Thy goodness; for all things are possible unto Thee, and nothing is impossible with Thee.[^3741] Yea, O Lord our God, Who didst feed thousands of people with a few loaves in[^3742] the wilderness:[^3743] nourish us also now by Thine ineffable tender-heartedness; and despise not the supplications of us, Thy sinful and unworthy servants; that Thy most-holy Name may be glorified: of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen."**[^3744]

### Notes

[^3610]: Part XLIII heading: A supplicatory canon against the pestilence (*морова́я болѣ́знь*), invoking the Holy Trinity and all the saints.
[^3611]: Rubric in red cinnabar: *Кано́нъ къ ст҃ѣ́й є҆диносꙋ́щнѣй и҆ животворѧ́щей и҆ нераздѣли́мѣй трⷪ҇ци, и҆ къ всѣ̑мъ ст҃ы̑мъ, въ препрѣ́нїе моровы́ѧ болѣ́зни* (Canon unto the Holy, Consubstantial, Life-Creating, and Undivided Trinity, and unto All Saints, for the Overcoming of the Pestilential Sickness).
[^3612]: Editorial continuation marker: Ode 1 heading spans physical leaf p1201 (fol. 593r) to p1202 (fol. 593v).
[^3613]: Tone 8 Sunday Heirmos incipit in red cinnabar: *Воѡрꙋже́нна фараѡ́на погрꙋзѝ, чюⷣ:* (Greek *Ἅρματηλάτην Φαραὼ ἐβύθισε* / Exod. 15:4).
[^3614]: Theological invocation of the Trinity as all-creating, co-enthroned, and of equal power (*въседѣ́телнаѧ и҆ сѣдѣ́лнаѧ, и҆ съпрⷭ҇то́лнаѧ, и҆ є҆диноси́лнаѧ, и҆ тресвѣ́тлаѧ сла́во*).
[^3615]: Patristic apophatic theology: addressing the incomprehensible Father (*ѡ҆́тче непостижи́ме* / Greek *Πάτερ ἀκατάληπτε*).
[^3616]: Scriptural metaphor of sin as a tempest casting the soul into the abyss of infirmity: Ps. 68:2–3, 15–16 LXX.
[^3617]: Liturgical petition invoking the divine mercy of the Holy Trinity to save the soul from sudden perdition.
[^3618]: Editorial continuation marker: text spans physical leaf p1202 (fol. 593v) to p1203 (fol. 594r).
[^3619]: Hymnographic image of the dew of divine mercy extinguishing the burning fever of pestilence (Dan. 3:50; Ps. 132:3 LXX).
[^3620]: Theotokion of Ode 1: Christ bearing human infirmities through the Incarnation (Isa. 53:4; Matt. 8:17).
[^3621]: Tone 8 Sunday Heirmos incipit in red cinnabar: *Нбⷭ҇номꙋ крꙋ́гꙋ врътхотво́рче Гдⷭ҇и, цр҃кви:* (O Lord, Builder of the vault of heaven / Greek *Οὐρανίας ἁψῖδος ὀροφοποιὲ Κύριε*).
[^3622]: Invocation of the celestial hierarchies according to Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite (*Небе́снїи оу҆мо́ве, а҆́гг҃лтїи чи́ни, престо́ли и҆ нача́ла, и҆ си́лы и҆ Гдⷭ҇тва*).
[^3623]: Liturgical plea to Christ the Benefactor and Savior to free His people from the destructive contagion (*гꙋби́телнаго свободѝ недꙋ́га*).
[^3624]: Editorial continuation marker: text spans physical leaf p1203 (fol. 594r) to p1204 (fol. 594v).
