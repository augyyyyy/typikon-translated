# PART XXVII: THE ORDER OF THE LITTLE SCHEMA (THAT IS, THE MANTIYA) [^1697]

*At the striking of the wood of the service,[^1698] and when the brethren begin the Hours, the ekklesiarch brings in him who desires to be tonsured; and he makes three prostrations before the holy doors, and to the choirs one each, then unto the hegumen. And then having gone out into the narthex, he puts off his customary clothing.[^1699] And again he is brought within the church by the ekklesiarch, and stands clad in a short garment, ungirded, barefoot, bareheaded, clad in a tunic only for decency's sake.[^1700]*

*And while the customary antiphons are being sung, after the Entrance the kontakion of the day is said at the "Glory...": and at the "Now and ever...", this troparion is sung, Tone 1:*[^1701]

*Tone 1:*  
Make haste to open unto me fatherly embraces; prodigally have I spent my life; but looking upon the inexhaustible wealth of Thy compassions, O Savior, despise not now an impoverished heart; for unto Thee, O Lord, in compunction I cry: I have sinned, Father, against heaven and before Thee![^1702]

*While this is being sung thrice, he who desires to be tonsured prostrates himself before the holy doors, where the hegumen is standing.[^1703]*

*And after the aforesaid troparion, the hegumen comes with the brethren near the pillars, and stands there. And candles are given [to them].[^1704] And he who desires to be tonsured makes three metanias before the holy doors, then unto the hegumen one.*

*And when he has arisen, the hegumen catechizes him thus:*[^1705]

**Hegumen:** **Open the ears of thine heart, O brother, and give ear to the voice of the Lord saying: "Come unto Me, all ye that labor and are heavy laden with sins, and I will give you rest. Take My yoke upon you, and learn Hegumen:** **...of Me, for I am meek and lowly in heart, and ye shall find rest unto your souls."[^1706] Now therefore, unto each question, with fear and joy give the fitting answer unto God. And know of a certainty that our Savior Himself, with His all-hymned Mother, and holy angels, and all His saints, is come hither, listening unto the words that proceed from thee; so that when He comes to judge the living and the dead, He will render unto thee not according to what thou art about to promise and profess, but according as thou shalt have kept and fulfilled [thy profession].[^1707] Therefore now, unto Hegumen:** **...the things wherein thou art about to be questioned by us, make answer.**

*After this he is questioned.*

*It is befitting to know that if the hegumen is a priest, he questions him himself; but if not, he is questioned by the priest.*[^1708]

*Question:*  
**Priest:** **Why hast thou come, brother, falling down before this holy altar and before this holy company?**

*Answer:*  
**Candidate:** **Desiring the ascetic life, honorable father.**[^1709]

*Question:*  
**Priest:** **Dost thou desire to be counted worthy of the angelic schema, and to be ranked in the choir of monastics?**

*Answer:*  
**Candidate:** **Yea, God helping [me], honorable father.**

*The Priest [says]:*

**Priest:** **Truly a good and blessed work hast thou chosen, if thou also wilt accomplish it; for good works are acquired through labor, and perfected through travail.**[^1710]

*Question:*  
**Priest:** **Dost thou approach the Lord of the free choice of thine own mind?**

*Answer:*  
**Candidate:** **Yea, God helping [me], honorable father.**

*Question:*  
**Priest:** **Is it of some distress or constraint?**[^1711]

*Answer:*  
**Candidate:** **Nay, honorable father.**

*Question:*  
**Priest:** **Dost thou renounce the world and the things that are in the world, according to the commandment of the Lord?**[^1712]

*Answer:*  
**Candidate:** **Yea, honorable father.**

*Question:*  
**Priest:** **Wilt thou abide in the monastery and in the ascetic life until thy last breath?**

*Answer:*

**Candidate:** **Yea, God helping [me], honorable father.**

*Question:*  
**Priest:** **Wilt thou preserve thyself in virginity, chastity, and piety?**[^1713]

*Answer:*  
**Candidate:** **Yea, honorable father.**

*Question:*  
**Priest:** **Wilt thou preserve obedience unto the hegumen and unto all the brotherhood in Christ?**

*Answer:*  
**Candidate:** **Yea, honorable father.**

*Question:*  
**Priest:** **Wilt thou endure every constraint and sorrow of the monastic life, for the sake of the Kingdom of heaven?**

*Answer:*  
**Candidate:** **Yea, God helping [me], honorable father.**

*Then the instruction:*[^1714]

**Priest:** **Take heed, brother, what manner of promises thou givest unto the Master Christ; for the angels are standing by invisibly, recording...**

**Priest:** **...this thy confession, concerning which thou shalt be examined at the Second Coming of our Lord Jesus Christ.[^1715] I declare unto thee therefore the most perfect life, wherein a conversation according to the likeness of the Lord is manifested, testifying what things it is befitting for thee to choose, and what things it is befitting to flee from.[^1716] For this purpose hast thou purposed, child, to approach and serve the Lord; if therefore thou desirest to be a monk, before all things cleanse thyself from all defilement of flesh and spirit, perfecting holiness in the fear of God.[^1717] Acquire humble-mindedness, whereby thou shalt become an inheritor of eternal good things; put away...**

**Priest:** **...the arrogance of worldly custom; have obedience toward all; be without murmuring in the services commanded thee; in prayer patient; in vigils not sluggish; in temptation sorrow not; in fasts slacken not; and know that by prayer and fasting it is befitting for thee to entreat God; in infirmities faint not; discern also evil thoughts; for the enemy will not cease offering thee the memory of thy former worldly life, and hatred toward a virtuous way of life; it is befitting for thee therefore, having begun**[^1718] the way that leads unto the Kingdom of heaven, not to turn back; for thou shalt not be fit for the Kingdom of heaven.[^1719] Prefer thou nothing above God. Love thou neither father, nor mother, nor brethren, nor any of thine own kin; neither love thyself above God; nor the kingdoms of the world, or any manner of repose or honor.[^1720] Turn not away from poverty, nor affliction, nor human humiliation, nor anything else which thou deemest to be grievous, lest thou be hindered from running after Christ; but ever looking unto the hopes of those who have lived godly good things, and meditating upon the martyrs and venerable ones from the beginning of the world, who acquired these things through many sweats and labors, and countless blood and deaths. Be sober in all things, suffer hardship as a good soldier of Christ.[^1721] For our Lord and God Himself, being rich, for our sake became poor, that we might be enriched by His Kingdom. It is befitting therefore that we also become imitators of Him, and endure all things for His sake, advancing in His commandments day and night. For the Lord Himself said: If any man will come after Me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow Me;[^1722] which is to be ever prepared, even unto death, for every fulfillment of His commandments. For Thou shalt hunger and thirst, and undergo vexation and reproach, reviling and persecution, and be weighed down by many other afflictions, whereby life according to God is delineated. But when Thou hast suffered all these things, Rejoice, He said, for great is your reward in heaven;[^1723] in Christ Jesus our Lord, to Whom be glory unto the ages. Amen.

*Question:*  
**Priest:** **Dost thou confess all these things thus, through hope in the power of God; and dost thou promise to abide in these vows...**

**Priest:** **...even unto the end of thy life, by the grace of Christ?**

*Answer:*  
**Candidate:** **Yea, God helping [me], honorable father.**

*And immediately the priest [says] this prayer:*

**Priest:** **The all-bountiful and all-merciful God, Who opens the unsearchable goodness of His most pure compassions unto everyone that comes unto Him with desire and warm love, Who has said: Even if a woman should forget the offspring of her womb, yet will I not forget thee;[^1724] Who also knows thy desire, and unto thy resolve adds the power that is from Himself, for the fulfillment of His commandments: may He receive, and embrace, and protect [thee],...**

**Priest:** **...and may He be unto thee a firm wall from the face of the enemy, a rock of endurance, a cause of consolation, a giver of strength, a provider of good courage, a fellow-contestant unto thy courage, lying down and rising up with thee, sweetening and gladdening thy heart with the comfort of His Holy Spirit, vouchsafing thee also the portion of the holy and venerable fathers of ours: Anthony, Euthymius, Sabbas, and those with them.[^1725]**

*And for women:*  
**Priest:** **Of the holy venerable-martyr Thecla, Euphraxia, Olympias, and those with them.**

*The Priest continues:*  
**Priest:** **...together with whom also mayest thou inherit the Kingdom of heaven; in Christ Jesus our Lord, to Whom be glory and dominion, and kingdom and power, with the Father...**

**Priest:** **...and with the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.**

*Then, while he bows his head, and the deacon says: Let us pray to the Lord, the priest lays the ritual book [Obrazosloviye][^1726] upon his head, and says this prayer in the hearing of all:*

**Priest:** **O Lord our God, Who hast legislated that those who have left behind all worldly things, and kin, and friends, and have followed Thee, are worthy of Thee: receive also this Thy servant, [Name], who has renounced all these things according to Thy holy commandments; and guide him unto Thy truth, as he falls down before Thee without wavering. Hedge him about with the power...**

**Priest:** **...of Thy Holy Spirit, so that every contrary device shall have no power to work upon him, granting him endurance unto pleasing Thee ever, through the prayers of our most pure Lady, the Theotokos, and of all the saints who from the ages have been well-pleasing unto Thee.**

*Exclamation:*  
**Priest:** **For blessed and glorified is Thy most honorable and majestic Name, of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.**

*And immediately the scissors are laid upon the Holy Gospel.*

**Priest:** **Peace be unto all.**

**Deacon:** **Bow your heads unto the Lord.**

*The Priest says:*  
**Priest:** **O Lord our God, the hope and refuge of all that hope in Thee,...**

**Priest:** **...Who hast shown us diverse ways of salvation through the incarnation of Thy Christ: receive Thy servant, [Name], who has forsaken worldly lusts, and offered himself unto Thee, the Master, as a living sacrifice, well-pleasing.[^1727] Take away from him every carnal lust, and irrational assumptions; that by the putting off of insensitive hair, he may likewise put off irrational thoughts and deeds, and be vouchsafed to take upon him Thy good yoke, and Thy light burden, and to take up the cross, and to follow Thee, the Master. Preserve him in Thy holiness, and grant him a good resolve, unto the keeping of Thy holy commandments,...**

**Priest:** **...joining him in an acceptable time unto the choir of Thine elect. Through the grace and love for mankind of Thine Only-begotten Son, with Whom Thou art blessed, together with Thy most holy and good and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.**

*Then the priest, having stretched forth his hand upon the Holy Gospel, says:*[^1728]

**Priest:** **Behold, Christ stands here invisibly. Look, that no one compels thee to come unto this habit. Look, whether thou of thine own free will desirest the betrothal of the great angelic schema?**

*Answer:*  
**Candidate:** **Yea, honorable father, of [mine own] purpose.**

*And after the promise, the priest says unto him thrice:*

**Priest:** **Take the scissors, and give them unto me.**

*And when he has given him the scissors, the priest says again unto him:*

**Priest:** **Behold, thou receivest them from the hand of Christ. Look to Whom thou makest vow, and unto Whom thou drawest near, and Whom thou dost renounce.**

*And the priest, having received the scissors from the Holy Gospel, says:*[^1729]

**Priest:** **Blessed is God, Who desires all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth; Who is blessed unto ages of ages. Amen.**

*Then, tonsuring him crosswise, he says:*

**Priest:** **Our brother, [Name], is tonsured...**

**Priest:** **...of the hair of his head, in the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Let us all say for him: Lord, have mercy.**

*And the brethren sing:*  
**Choir:** **Lord, have mercy** *(thrice)*.

*Then he invests him in the garment, saying:*  
**Priest:** **Our brother, [Name], is clothed in the garment of gladness,[^1730] in the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Let us all say for him: Lord, have mercy.**

*Afterwards he places upon him the paramand,[^1731] saying:*  
**Priest:** **Our brother, [Name], is girded about his loins with the power of truth, unto the mortification of the body, and unto the renewal of the spirit. In the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Let us all say for him: Lord, have mercy.**

**Priest:** **Our brother, [Name], is covered with the helmet of the hope of salvation,[^1732] in the Name...**

**Priest:** **...of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Let us all say for him: Lord, have mercy.**

**Priest:** **Our brother, [Name], receives the paramand,[^1733] the betrothal of the great angelic schema, unto a garment of incorruption and purity. In the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Let us all say for him: Lord, have mercy.**

**Priest:** **Our brother, [Name], is shod with sandals in the preparation of the gospel of peace,[^1734] in the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Let us all say for him: Lord, have mercy.**

**Priest:** **Our brother, [Name], has received the betrothal of the great angelic schema,[^1735] in the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Let us all say for him: Lord, have mercy.**

*And then:*  
**Deacon:** *Let us pray to the Lord.*

**Priest:** **O Lord our God, Who hast brought this Thy servant, [Name], into Thy spiritual court: number him with Thy reasonable flock. Purify his mind from carnal lusts and the vain deceit of this life; and grant unto him unceasingly to remember the good things prepared for them that love Thee, and that have crucified themselves unto this life for the sake of Thy Kingdom. For Thou art the Shepherd and Bishop[^1736] of our souls, and unto Thee we send up glory: to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.**

*And if, therefore, there be no Liturgy, there are said these deacon's petitions, and the Epistle, and the Gospel.[^1737] But if there be [a Liturgy], a candle is given unto the tonsured one, and after the Gospel, he kisses the Cross, and the kiss takes place.*

**Deacon:**  
*In peace let us pray to the Lord.*  
*For the peace from above, and for the salvation of our souls, [let us pray to the Lord].*  
*For the peace of the whole world, and for the good estate [of the holy churches of God, and for the union of all, let us pray to the Lord].*  
*For our brother, [Name], and for his protection and help from God, let us pray to the Lord.*  
*That blamelessly, uncondemned, and without stumbling he may accomplish his purpose of the monastic habit, let us pray to the Lord.*  
*That he may abide in all piety, reverence, and purity, let us pray to the Lord.*

**Deacon:**  
*That he may put off the old man, and put on the new man, who is created after God's likeness, let us pray to the Lord.*  
*For the remission and forgiveness of his sins, let us pray to the Lord.*  
*That we may be delivered from all affliction, wrath, and need, let us pray to the Lord.*  
*Help us, save us, have mercy on us, and keep us, O God, [by Thy grace].*  
*Commemorating our all-holy, immaculate, most blessed [and glorious Lady, Theotokos and ever-virgin Mary, with all the saints, let us commend ourselves and one another, and all our life unto Christ our God].*

*Exclamation:*  
**Priest:** **For holy art Thou, O our God, and unto Thee we send up glory: [to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.]**

**Choir:** **As many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ.[^1738]**

*Prokeimenon, Tone 3:*  
**Reader:** **The Lord is my illumination and my Savior.**[^1739]  
*Verse:* **The Lord is the defender of my life.**

*The Reading is from the Epistle of Paul to the Ephesians.*

**Reader:** **Brethren, be strong in the Lord, and in the power of His might.[^1740] Put on the whole armor of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil. For our wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the world-rulers of the darkness of this age, against the spiritual [hosts] of wickedness in the heavenly places. Therefore take up the whole armor of God, that ye may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand. Stand therefore, having girt your loins with truth, and having put on the breastplate of righteousness; and having shod your feet...**

**Reader:** **...with the preparation of the gospel of peace. Above all, taking up the shield of faith, wherewith ye shall be able to quench all the fiery darts of the evil one. And take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God.**

*Alleluia.*  
*Verse:* **This poor man cried, and the Lord heard him, and saved him out of all his afflictions.[^1741]**

*The Gospel according to Matthew.*  
**Priest:** **The Lord said: He that loves father or mother more than Me is not worthy of Me; and he that loves son or daughter more than Me is not worthy of Me. And he that does not take his cross, and follow after Me, is not worthy of Me.[^1742]**

**Priest:** **...Come unto Me, all ye that labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest. Take My yoke upon you, and learn from Me; for I am meek and lowly in heart, and ye shall find rest unto your souls. For My yoke is easy, and My burden is light.**

**Deacon:**  
*Have mercy on us, O God, according to Thy great mercy, we pray Thee, hear and have mercy.*  
*Again we pray for the remission and forgiveness of the sins of the servant of God, [Name].*

*Exclamation:*  
**Priest:** **For Thou art a merciful God and the Lover of mankind, and unto Thee we send up glory: to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.**  
**Choir:** **Amen.**

*And he gives him the Cross, saying:*  
**Priest:** **The Lord said: If any man will come after Me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow Me.[^1743]**

*And giving [him] the lighted candle, he says:*  
**Priest:** **The Lord said: Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father Who is in heaven.[^1744]**

*And the kiss takes place, while they sing the idiomelon. Tone 1:*  
**Choir:** **Let us acknowledge, brethren, the power of the mystery:[^1745] for when the Prodigal Son returned from sin unto the fatherly house, the all-good Father came forth to meet him and kissed him;...**

**Choir:** **...and again imparts the knowledge of His glory, and makes a mystical joy for the hosts on high, slaying the fatted calf; that we also may live worthily unto the Father Who is the Lover of mankind that sacrificed, and unto the most glorious Victim,[^1746] the Savior of our souls.**

*And the rest of the Liturgy. And the dismissal takes place.*

*It should be known that such a monk is obliged to remain in the church for five days, abstaining from all work except reading, if he know how [to read].[^1747]*

### Notes

[^1697]: Physical leaf p602 (fol. 292v). *ПОСЛѢДОВАНЇЕ МАЛАГО ѠБРАЗА* (Gk. *Ἀκολουθία τοῦ μικροῦ σχήματος*). The Order of the Little Schema (or Little Habit), historically also designated as the Order of the Mantiya (*мантия*), constituting the full formal profession of monastic vows in the Byzantine-Ruthenian tradition.
[^1698]: Physical leaf p602 (fol. 292v). The striking of the wood (*оударе́нїе дре́ва слу́жбы*) refers to the liturgical sounding of the wooden semantron (*клепало* / *било* / Gk. *σήμαντρον*) to summon the brotherhood to the Hours and the Divine Liturgy, during which the tonsure is celebrated.
[^1699]: Physical leaf p602 (fol. 292v). The candidate divests himself in the church narthex (*притворъ*) of his customary secular or novice garments, symbolizing the putting off of the old man (cf. Ephesians 4:22; Colossians 3:9) before entering the nave for tonsure.
[^1700]: Physical leaf p603 (fol. 293r). The rubric specifies that the candidate stands before the holy doors clothed only in a simple inner tunic (*срачица* / Gk. *χιτώνιον*), barefoot, unbelted, and bareheaded, expressing absolute voluntary poverty, penitence, and surrender before God.
[^1701]: Physical leaf p603 (fol. 293r). The tonsure is integrated into the Little Entrance of the Divine Liturgy. Following the entrance, after the kontakion of the day at "Glory...", this special penitential troparion is chanted at "Now and ever...".
[^1702]: Physical leaf p603 (fol. 293r). Monastic tonsure troparion, Tone 1: *Ѡбъꙗ́тїа ѿча ѿвръстѝ ми потщи́сѧ* (Gk. *Τὰς πατρικὰς ἀγκάλας διανοῖξαί μοι σπεῦσον*), directly adapted from the parable of the Prodigal Son (Luke 15:11–32).
[^1703]: Physical leaf p604 (fol. 293v). While the brethren chant the troparion *Ѡбъꙗ́тїа ѿча* thrice, the candidate prostrates himself cruciformly upon the floor before the Royal Doors where the hegumen stands.
[^1704]: Physical leaf p604 (fol. 293v). Lighted tapers/candles are distributed to the brotherhood standing near the central pillars (*стлъпѡ́къ*), symbolizing the light of good works and the illumination of the angelic life.
[^1705]: Physical leaf p604 (fol. 293v). The catechetical exhortation and preliminary examination of monastic vows begins with the words of Christ: Matthew 11:28–29.
[^1706]: Physical leaf p605 (fol. 294r). Matthew 11:29.
[^1707]: Physical leaf p605 (fol. 294r). The hegumen solemnly warns the candidate that Christ, the Mother of God, and the heavenly host are invisibly present, and that judgment will be rendered not merely on verbal promises, but on actual observance of ascetic struggle.
[^1708]: Physical leaf p606 (fol. 294v). The rubric provides that if the monastery hegumen is an ordained hieromonk (*іере́й*), he conducts the interrogation himself; if he is not ordained, an ordained priest conducts the examination.
[^1709]: Physical leaf p606 (fol. 294v). The candidate articulates his primary monastic motive: desire for the ascetic/fasting life (*житїа по́стническаго* / Gk. *ἀσκητικοῦ βίου*).
[^1710]: Physical leaf p607 (fol. 295r). Traditional monastic interrogation formula: *Въи́стинноу добро̀ дѣ́ло и бл҃же́нно избра́ єси* (Gk. *Ὄντως καλὸν ἔργον καὶ μακάριον ἐξελέξω, ἀλλ᾽ ἐὰν καὶ τελειώσῃς αὐτό...*).
[^1711]: Physical leaf p607 (fol. 295r). Interrogation confirming free will: the candidate must affirm that he is not entering monastic life under compulsion, debt, fear of civil punishment, or worldly distress.
[^1712]: Physical leaf p607 (fol. 295r). The vow of renunciation of the world (*Ѡрица́еши ли сѧ ми́ра*), grounded in 1 John 2:15–17.
[^1713]: Physical leaf p608 (fol. 295v). The three fundamental monastic vows: lifelong perseverance / stability of place (*stabilitas loci*), chastity/virginity (*дѣтство и цѣломудрїе*), and obedience (*послушанїе*).
[^1714]: Physical leaf p608 (fol. 295v). The solemn catechetical instruction (*Та́же ѻглаше́нїе* / Gk. *Κατήχησις*) delivered by the priest/hegumen immediately following the examination of vows.
[^1715]: Physical leaf p609 (fol. 296r). The vows are described as an irrevocable written record inscribed by the holy angels, to be examined before the judgment seat of Christ at His Second Coming.
[^1716]: Physical leaf p609 (fol. 296r). The monastic way is termed "the most perfect life" (*съвръше́нное житїѐ* / Gk. *τελειοτάτη πολιτεία*) because it strives for the direct imitation of Christ's earthly life.
[^1717]: Physical leaf p609 (fol. 296r). 2 Corinthians 7:1: "Having therefore these promises, dearly beloved, let us cleanse ourselves from all filthiness of the flesh and spirit, perfecting holiness in the fear of God."
[^1718]: Physical leaf p610 (fol. 296v). Practical ascetical instruction detailing vigilance against demonic thoughts (*помысли* / Gk. *λογισμοί*), steadfastness in fasting, patience in sickness, and perseverance in prayer and vigils, continuing on leaf p611.
[^1719]: Physical leaf p611 (fol. 297r). Allusion to Luke 9:62: "No man, having put his hand to the plough, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God." The instruction warns the monastic novice that regression into worldly preoccupations forfeits the heavenly calling.
[^1720]: Physical leaf p611 (fol. 297r). Allusion to Christ's evangelical demands in Matthew 10:37 ("He that loves father or mother more than Me is not worthy of Me") and Luke 14:26.
[^1721]: Physical leaf p612 (fol. 297v). 2 Timothy 2:3: "Thou therefore endure hardness, as a good soldier of Jesus Christ." The passage also draws directly upon 2 Corinthians 8:9: "For ye know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that, though He was rich, yet for your sakes He became poor, that ye through His poverty might be rich."
[^1722]: Physical leaf p612 (fol. 297v). Direct citation of the Dominical logion in Matthew 16:24 (cf. Mark 8:34; Luke 9:23): "If any man will come after Me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow Me."
[^1723]: Physical leaf p613 (fol. 298r). Allusion to the eighth and ninth Beatitudes (Matthew 5:11–12; Luke 6:22–23): "Blessed are ye, when men shall revile you... Rejoice, and be exceeding glad: for great is your reward in heaven."
[^1724]: Physical leaf p614 (fol. 298v). Direct scriptural citation of Isaiah 49:15: "Can a woman forget her sucking child, that she should not have compassion on the son of her womb? yea, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee."
[^1725]: Physical leaf p615 (fol. 299r). Commemoration of the historic archetypes of Byzantine coenobitic and eremitic monasticism: St. Anthony the Great (†356), St. Euthymius the Great (†473), and St. Sabbas the Sanctified (†532). The cinnabar rubric *А҆ жена́мъ* ("And for women") supplies the feminine monastic equivalents: St. Thecla the Protomartyr, St. Euphraxia of Tabennisi, and St. Olympias the Deaconess.
[^1726]: Physical leaf p616 (fol. 299v). The ritual laying of the Sluzhebnik (*ѻ҆бразосло́вїе*, Gk. *εὐχολόγιον*) upon the bowed head of the candidate during the priestly prayer, symbolizing the imposition of the liturgical yoke and ecclesiastical transmission of monastic discipline.
[^1727]: Physical leaf p618 (fol. 300v). Allusion to Romans 12:1 ("present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God") and Matthew 11:29–30 ("Take My yoke upon you... For My yoke is easy, and My burden is light").
[^1728]: Physical leaf p619 (fol. 301r). The solemn warning before the Holy Gospel affirming that monastic tonsure is strictly voluntary and that no coercion or external constraint has compelled the candidate to take the vows of the angelic habit (*вели́каго а҆гг҃лскаго чи́на*).
[^1729]: Physical leaf p620 (fol. 301v). Allusion to 1 Timothy 2:4: "Who will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth." The ceremonial taking and handing of the shears from the Holy Gospel is performed thrice to signify unconditional submission to the divine will and Christ Himself.
[^1730]: Physical leaf p621 (fol. 302r). Allusion to Isaiah 61:10: "He hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, He hath covered me with the robe of righteousness, as a bridegroom decks himself with ornaments." The monastic habit (rason/chiton) is designated the "garment of gladness" (*риза веселия*), symbolizing the joyous putting off of worldly decay.
[^1731]: Physical leaf p621 (fol. 302r). Allusion to Ephesians 6:14 ("having your loins girt about with truth") and Colossians 3:5 ("Mortify therefore your members which are upon the earth"). The rubric designates this vesting as the paramand (*парамандътъ*), accompanied by the formula of girding the loins unto the mortification of the flesh and spiritual renewal.
[^1732]: Physical leaf p621 (fol. 302r). Allusion to 1 Thessalonians 5:8 ("and for an helmet, the hope of salvation") and Ephesians 6:17 ("take the helmet of salvation"). The monastic head covering (klobuk / koukoulion) signifies the protection of the mind against demonic assault.
[^1733]: Physical leaf p622 (fol. 302v). The formula designates the reception of the paramand (*парамантъ*) as the solemn betrothal of the great angelic schema (*обручение великаго агглскаго образа, въ одежду нетлѣния и чистоты*). In Byzantine-Slavic monastic theology, the Lesser Schema (*малый образ*) functions as an earnest or betrothal anticipating the full ascetical perfection of the Great Schema.
[^1734]: Physical leaf p622 (fol. 302v). Direct citation of Ephesians 6:15: "And your feet shod with the preparation of the gospel of peace." The monastic sandals signify readiness to walk in the paths of evangelical obedience without hindrance.
[^1735]: Physical leaf p622 (fol. 302v). The formula for investing the candidate in the monastic mantle (*mandyas* / *pallium*), reiterating the reception of the betrothal of the great angelic habit.
[^1736]: Physical leaf p623 (fol. 303r). Citing 1 Peter 2:25: "For ye were as sheep going astray; but are now returned unto the Shepherd and Bishop [Gk. ἐπίσκοπος; ChSl. посѣтитель] of your souls." The prayer also draws upon 1 Corinthians 2:9 ("the things which God hath prepared for them that love Him") and Galatians 6:14 ("crucified unto the world").
[^1737]: Physical leaf p624 (fol. 303v). Rubrical directive specifying the bifurcation of the monastic tonsure rite: when celebrated independently outside the Divine Liturgy, the litany, Epistle, and Gospel are inserted directly; when celebrated within the Divine Liturgy, the readings are integrated into the Liturgy of the Word.
[^1738]: Physical leaf p625 (fol. 304r). Citing Galatians 3:27: "For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ." Sung here crosswise as the newly tonsured monk is led into the midst of the brethren, manifesting the tonsure as a reaffirmation and seal of baptismal illumination.
[^1739]: Physical leaf p625 (fol. 304r). Prokeimenon in Tone 3, citing Psalm 26:1 (LXX; MT 27:1): "The Lord is my illumination and my Savior; whom shall I fear? The Lord is the defender of my life; of whom shall I be afraid?"
[^1740]: Physical leaf p626 (fol. 304v). The Epistle pericope Ephesians 6:10–17, expounding the whole armor of God (*panoplia* / *всеоружие*), which forms the spiritual foundation for each monastic garment and weapon of spiritual warfare.
[^1741]: Physical leaf p627 (fol. 305r). Alleluia verse citing Psalm 33:7 (LXX; MT 34:6): "This poor man cried, and the Lord heard him, and saved him out of all his afflictions."
[^1742]: Physical leaf p627 (fol. 305r). The monastic Gospel pericope combining Matthew 10:37–38 and Matthew 11:28–30, articulating the cost and reward of discipleship: forsaking familial attachments, taking up the cross, and entering into Christ's restful yoke.
[^1743]: Physical leaf p629 (fol. 306r). Dominical logion from Matthew 16:24 (cf. Mark 8:34; Luke 9:23) spoken at the delivery of the hand cross unto the newly tonsured monk.
[^1744]: Physical leaf p629 (fol. 306r). Dominical logion from Matthew 5:16 spoken at the delivery of the lighted wax candle unto the newly tonsured monk, signifying the light of good works and evangelical witness.
[^1745]: Physical leaf p629 (fol. 306r). The classical tonsure idiomelon (*Да познаимъ братие тайны силу* / Gk. Ἐπιγνῶμεν, ἀδελфоί, τοῦ μυστηρίου τὴν δύναμιν), based upon Christ's parable of the Prodigal Son (Luke 15:11–32). The brethren bestow the fraternal kiss of peace upon the new monk while this hymn is chanted.
[^1746]: Physical leaf p630 (fol. 306v). Allusion to Luke 15:23–24 and 1 Corinthians 5:7: the slain fatted calf of the parable is mystically interpreted as Christ Himself, the sacrificed Victim whose death brings joy to the heavenly hosts and salvation unto penitents.
[^1747]: Physical leaf p630 (fol. 306v). The concluding rubric prescribing that the newly tonsured monk remain in the church for five full days in continuous ascetical contemplation, prayer, and reading of the Scriptures, sequestered from all worldly and manual labors.
