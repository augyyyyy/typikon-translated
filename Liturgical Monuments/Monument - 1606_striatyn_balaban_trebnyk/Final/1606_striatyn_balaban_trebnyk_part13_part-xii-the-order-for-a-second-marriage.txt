# PART XII: THE ORDER FOR A SECOND MARRIAGE [^249]

THE ORDER FOR DIGAMISTS

*The Priest says:*
**Priest:**
Blessed is our God...

*Then the Trisagion; after Our Father, the troparion of the day. After this, the litany:*[^250]

[Fol. 73v. Initial M.]
**Deacon:**
In peace let us pray to the Lord.
For the peace from above, and for the salvation of our souls...
For this holy temple, and those that with faith and...
For the servants of God [Names], and for their protection from God and their common life, let us pray to the Lord.[^251]

That they may live together well in unity, let us pray to the Lord.

**Deacon:**
*Help us, save us, have mercy on us, and keep us...*
*Remembering our all-holy, immaculate, most blessed Lady...*

**Priest:**
*Exclamation:*
For unto Thee is due all glory, honor, and worship:

*Then: Let us pray to the Lord.*

[Fol. 74r. Initial B.]
**Priest:**
*Prayer:*
O Eternal God, Who hast brought into unity things that were divided, and hast set for them an indissoluble bond of love; Who didst bless Isaac and Rebecca, and didst show them to be heirs of Thy promise: Do Thou Thyself bless also these Thy servants, guiding them into every good work. For a merciful and man-befriending God art Thou, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy[^252] Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

**Priest:**
*Peace be unto all.*

**Deacon:**
*Bow your heads unto the Lord.*

[Fol. 74v. Initial G.]
**Priest:**
*Prayer of Head-Bowing:*
O Lord our God, Who didst betroth beforehand the Church from the Gentiles as a pure virgin: bless this betrothal, and unite and preserve these Thy servants in lowliness of mind and oneness of mind. For unto Thee is due all glory, honor, and worship, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^253]

*Then the priest, taking the rings, acts as is written above in the Crowning. After this, the friend of the bridegroom exchanges the rings. And the priest says:*[^254]
**Priest:**
Let us pray to the Lord.

[Fol. 75r. Initial V.]
**Priest:**
*Prayer:*
O Master, Lord our God, Who sparest all and providest for all, Who knowest the secret things of men and hast knowledge of all things: Purify our sins, and forgive the iniquities of Thy servants, calling them unto repentance, granting them pardon of offenses, purification of sins, remission of transgressions voluntary and involuntary. O Creator and Fashioner, Who knowest the frailty of human nature; Who didst forgive Rahab the harlot, and didst accept the repentance of the Publican: Remember not the sins of our ignorance from our youth. For if Thou shouldst mark iniquities, O Lord, O Lord, who[^255] shall stand? What flesh shall be justified before Thee? For Thou alone art righteous, sinless, holy, plenteous in mercy, tender-hearted, and repenting of the evils of men. Do Thou, O Master, Who hast taken unto Thyself these Thy servants, unite them one to another in love; grant them the conversion of the Publican, the tears of the harlot, the confession of the thief: that by repentance from their whole heart, fulfilling Thy commandments in unity and peace, they may be counted worthy of Thy heavenly kingdom. For Thou art the Builder of all things, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^256]

**Priest:**
*Peace be unto all.*

**Deacon:**
*Bow your heads unto the Lord.*

[Fol. 76r. Initial G.]
**Priest:**
*Prayer of Head-Bowing:*
O Lord Jesus Christ, Word of God, Who wast lifted up upon the precious and life-creating Cross, and didst tear up the handwriting against us, and didst deliver us from the oppression of the devil: Purify the iniquities of Thy servants; for, not being able to bear the heat and burden of the day, and the burning of the flesh, they come together unto the second communion of marriage, as Thou didst ordain through Thine elect vessel, Paul the Apostle, saying for the sake of us humble ones: 'It is better to marry in the Lord than to burn.' Do Thou Thyself, as good and the Lover of mankind, have mercy and forgive,[^257] purify, remit, forgive our debts: for Thou art He that hast taken our infirmities upon Thy shoulders. For there is none sinless, even though his life be but one day, that he should be without blemish; save only Thou alone, Who didst clothe Thyself in flesh without sin, and hast granted unto us the incorruption of eternal blessings. For Thou art our God, the God of the penitent, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^258]

**Deacon:**
*Let us pray to the Lord.*

[Fol. 76v. Initial B.]
**Priest:**
O Holy God, Who didst fashion man from the dust, and from his rib didst create woman...

*This prayer is written in the order of crowning, before the Apostle: (see fol. 66).[^259]*

*Upon the conclusion of this prayer, taking the crowns, he crowns the bridegroom, saying:*

The servant of God, *[Name],* is crowned in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.[^260]

*Likewise he crowns also the bride, saying:*

The maidservant of God, *[Name],* is crowned in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.

*After this he blesses them thrice, saying:*

O Lord our God, with glory and honor hast Thou crowned them.

*And so everything in order, as is written above in the Order of Crowning, changing nothing.*

*And the dismissal.*

[Fol. 77r. Tailpiece: Woodcut floral vignette with acanthus leaves.]

[Fol. 77v. Blank leaf at the conclusion of Gathering 8 (и), concluding the Order for a Second Marriage, separating the nuptial rites from the Order of Holy Unction.]

[Fol. 78r. Headpiece: Woodcut engraving depicting two angels holding an oval medallion inscribed: "Anointing with Oil" (*ПОМАЗАНЇЕ Є҆ЛЕО́МЪ*), depicting priests ministering at the bedside of the sick person, flanked by floral vases and foliage.]

### Notes

[^249]: Fol. 73v. The Order for a Second Marriage (*Послѣ́дованїе на двобра́чныѧ* / *Ἀκολουθία εἰς δίγαμον*). Unlike the rite of primary Crowning (Part IX), which begins with the solemn Eucharistic doxology (*"Blessed is the Kingdom..."*), the Order for Digamists opens with the simple sacerdotal blessing (*"Blessed is our God..."*) followed by the Trisagion and penitential prayers, reflecting the theological understanding of digamy as an accommodation to weakness rather than an unrepeatable sacrament of the mystical marriage of Christ and the Church.
[^250]: Fol. 73v. Introductory Trisagion and Troparion of the Day. The rubric prescribes the complete Ordinary Beginning (*Начало обычное*): the Trisagion prayers through the Lord's Prayer (*Our Father*), concluded by the Troparion of the current liturgical day or patron saint of the church, before initiating the litany.
[^251]: Fol. 73v. The Nuptial Litany for Digamists. The petitions are abbreviated to the essential cosmic and pastoral supplications, concluding with a petition specifically tailored to the second union: praying for God's divine protection (*по Бз҃ѣ покро́вѣ*) and peaceful cohabitation (*съжи́тельства и҆́хъ*).
[^252]: Fol. 74r. The Betrothal Prayer for Digamists (*Бж҃е вѣ́чный, и҆́же растоѧ́щаѧ събра́въ...*). Greek: *Ὁ Θεὸς ὁ αἰώνιος, ὁ τὰ διῃρημένα συναγαγὼν εἰς ἑνότητα...* The first prayer of betrothal from the primary marriage rite (cf. Part VIII) is here incorporated into the digamy sequence, imploring God to grant unity and the blessing bestowed of old upon Isaac and Rebecca (Genesis 24).
[^253]: Fol. 74v. Prayer of Head-Bowing at Betrothal (*Гдⷭ҇и Бг҃ъ на́шъ, и҆́же ѿ ꙗ҆зы́къ предъѡброучи́въ цр҃ковь...*). Greek: *Κύριε ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν, ὁ τὴν ἐξ ἐθνῶν προμνηστευσάμενος Ἐκκλησίαν παρθένον ἁγνήν...* A typology of Christ betrothing the Church from among the Gentiles, praying for humility (*въ смире́нїи*) and spiritual concord (*въ съєдине́нїи*) between the spouses.
[^254]: Fol. 74v. Rite of the Nuptial Rings for Digamists (*Прїе́мъ сщ҃енникъ пръ́стени...*). The rubric refers back to the Betrothal rite (cf. Part VIII, fol. 61v–62r), where the priest takes the rings, signs the spouses' foreheads thrice with them, places them on their right hands, and the friend of the bridegroom (*женихо́въ дроу́гъ* / *paranymphos*) exchanges them thrice.
[^255]: Fol. 75r. The First Solemn Penitential Prayer for Digamists (*Влⷣко Гдⷭ҇и Бж҃е на́шъ, и҆́же въсѣ́хъ щадѧ̀...*). Greek: *Δέσποτα Κύριε ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν, ὁ πάντων φειδόμενος...* This prayer embodies the distinct theology of the second-marriage rite, framing the union within an earnest plea for divine mercy and remission of sins (*проще́нїе съгрѣше́нїѡмъ*). It invokes Old and New Testament archetypes of divine forgiveness: Rahab the harlot (Joshua 2; Hebrews 11:31) and the Publican (Luke 18:13–14). The prayer also incorporates the penitential Psalter versicles: *"Remember not the sins of my youth, nor of mine ignorance"* (Psalm 24:7 LXX [MT 25:7]) and *"If Thou shouldst mark iniquities, O Lord, O Lord, who shall stand?"* (Psalm 129:3 LXX [MT 130:3]).
[^256]: Fol. 75v. The Triple Archetype of Evangelical Repentance in the Nuptial Rite. Citing three classic New Testament exemplars of saving repentance: the Publican's humility and justification (Luke 18:13–14), the sinful woman washing Christ's feet with her tears (Luke 7:37–48), and the Good Thief's dying confession on the cross (Luke 23:40–43). The prayer concludes with a Trinitarian doxology confessing God as the fashioner and ruler of the cosmos (*строи́тель всѧ́ческихъ* / *ὁ οἰκονόμος τῶν ἁπάντων*).
[^257]: Fol. 76r. Sacerdotal Prayer of Head-Bowing and the Pauline Theology of Second Marriage (*Гдⷭ҇и І҆и҃се Хрⷭ҇тѐ Сло́ве Бж҃їи...*). Greek: *Κύριε Ἰησοῦ Χριστέ, ὁ Λόγος τοῦ Θεοῦ...* This prayer synthesizes Christ's redemptive work upon the Cross—tearing up the handwriting of our sins (Colossians 2:14)—with the pastoral reality of human frailty. Quoting the Gospel parable of the laborers who could not endure "the burden and heat of the day" (Matthew 20:12) and St. Paul's pastoral counsel to the Corinthians ("It is better to marry in the Lord than to burn," 1 Corinthians 7:9, 39), the prayer justifies the second marriage as a therapeutic concession (*oikonomia*) granted to prevent spiritual ruin.
[^258]: Fol. 76v. Christ Taking Infirmities Upon His Shoulders and Universal Sinfulness. Evoking Isaiah 53:4 ("He hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows") and Luke 15:5 (the Good Shepherd placing the lost sheep upon His shoulders), and citing the Patristic aphorism derived from Job 14:4–5 LXX ("For none is clean from stain, even if his life on earth be but of one day"). The prayer confesses Christ as the sole sinless One who clothed Himself in human flesh without sin, and exalts Him as "the God of the penitent" (*Бг҃ъ ка́ющихсѧ*).
[^259]: Fol. 76v. Internal Cross-Reference to the Crowning Prayer (see fol. 66, physical leaf p149). The rubric directs the priest to read the classic third prayer of Crowning (*Бж҃е ст҃ы́и, и҆́же созда́вый ѿ пе́рсти чл҃ка...* / *Ὁ Θεὸς ὁ ἅγιος, ὁ πλάσας ἐκ χοὸς τὸν ἄνθρωπον...*), which appears earlier in the codex on fol. 66r (physical leaf p149), immediately preceding the Prokimenon and the reading of the Epistle.
[^260]: Fol. 77r. The Crowning of Digamists and Alignment with the Solemn Nuptial Office. Although strict canonical tradition (St. Nicephorus Canon 2; Council of Neocaesarea Canon 7) discouraged or barred nuptial crowning for second marriages, the pastoral practice of the Kyivan-Ruthenian Church, following the custom of the Great Church of Constantinople as expounded by Nicetas of Heraclea (cf. fol. 72v–73r), incorporates the sacramental crowning formula: *"The servant of God... is crowned in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit"* and the triple blessing *"O Lord our God, with glory and honor hast Thou crowned them"* (Psalm 8:5 LXX [MT 8:6]). The rubric directs that the remainder of the office—including the Epistle (Ephesians 5:20–33), Gospel (John 2:1–11), litanies, the Lord's Prayer, the Common Cup, and the dance of Isaiah around the tetrapod—is celebrated identically to the first marriage ("changing nothing"), followed by the dismissal.
