# PART XLIX: PRAYERS AT THE BLESSING AND BREAKING OF THE ARTOS [^3869]

*Prayer at the Blessing of the Artos:*[^3870]

**Priest:** With joy and gladness let us glorify our risen God, and let us run unto the feast to meet the Resurrection![^3871] Even as the Cherubim and Seraphim glorify [Thee] today, O holy Apostles, today the heavenly hosts having come, worshiped the risen God;[^3872] and earthly men having beheld Thy glory, sang praises unto Thee and glorified Thee, the God of all things.[^3873] Do Thou Thyself, O Lord, come and bless this table, even as Thou didst bless the six apostolic pairs:[^3874] two blessed, two glorified, two calling out and saying: "Glory to God in the highest, and on[^3875]" earth peace, good will toward men!"[^3876] We worship Thee, Who wast tried without sin for our sake.[^3877] Do Thou, O Lord, bless this artos, even as Thou didst bless the five loaves and the two fishes;[^3878] and even as Thou didst bless the granaries of Joseph in drought and waterlessness;[^3879] for Thou art the life and resurrection, O Christ our God,[^3880] and unto Thee do we send up glory, with Thine unoriginate Father, and with Thine all-holy, good, and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. [Amen.][^3881]

*Prayer at the Breaking of the Artos:*[^3882]

**Priest:** O Lord our God, Who in Thy dispensation in the flesh didst bless the five loaves and the two fishes, and from them[^3883] didst satisfy five thousand in the wilderness:[^3884] do Thou Thyself now, O Master, bless these loaves, and those who have brought them; and unto those for whom they were brought, grant Thy rich and hundredfold blessing, O Christ God;[^3885] through the prayers of Thy most pure Mother, and of Thine holy Apostles, and of Saint Polycarp,[^3886] and of all the saints, and by Thy holy third-day Resurrection.[^3887] For Thou art the Bestower of good things, and unto Thee do we send up glory, with Thine unoriginate Father, and Thine all-holy, good, and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^3888]

### Notes

[^3869]: Decorative woodcut headpiece and title marking the beginning of Part XLIX: Prayers at the Blessing and Breaking of the Artos (Мл҃тва на бл҃гвнїи а҆́ртоуса; Greek Εὐχὴ εἰς τὴν εὐλόγησιν τοῦ ἄρτου / Ἀκολουθία τοῦ Ἀρτοκлаσίας τοῦ Ἄρτου). The Artos (Greek Ἄρτος, "leavened bread") is a large loaf of leavened bread bearing the stamped icon of Christ's Resurrection, blessed on the night of Holy Pascha at the conclusion of the Divine Liturgy. It symbolizes the physical presence of the risen Christ among His disciples during the forty days between His Resurrection and Ascension. Throughout Bright Week, the Artos remains on the tetrapod or before the iconostasis, is carried in daily Paschal processions, and is solemnly broken and distributed unto the faithful on Bright Saturday.
[^3870]: Rubric introducing the solemn prayer for the blessing of the Artos on the night of Holy Pascha.
[^3871]: Exultant Paschal opening petition: the faithful are called to run with spiritual joy unto the feast to encounter the risen Lord, echoing the haste of the Myrrh-bearing women and the Apostles Peter and John running to the empty tomb (John 20:4).
[^3872]: Celestial adoration of the Resurrection: the angelic hosts (Cherubim and Seraphim) join with the Holy Apostles and earthly creation in worshiping Christ arisen from the dead.
[^3873]: Doxological recognition of Christ's universal sovereignty as God over all creation, visible and invisible.
[^3874]: Mark 6:7: "And He called unto Him the twelve, and began to send them forth by two and two (δύο δύο)." The six apostolic pairs (ше́сть сꙋпру́гъ а҆пто́лскихъ) represent the twelve Apostles sent forth into the world to proclaim the Gospel and bless the nations.
[^3875]: Luke 2:14: the angelic hymn of the Nativity applied here to the cosmic peace and divine reconciliation accomplished by the Paschal victory of Christ. The prayer continues onto fol. 627r (leaf p1269).
[^3876]: Conclusion of the angelic hymn from Luke 2:14: "...and on earth peace, good will toward men!"
[^3877]: Heb. 4:15: "For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin (χωρὶς ἁμαρτίας)."
[^3878]: Matt. 14:17–21; Mark 6:38–44; Luke 9:13–17; John 6:9–14: the miraculous multiplication of the five loaves and two fishes in the wilderness, prefiguring the abundant spiritual sustenance provided by Christ in the Paschal banquet.
[^3879]: Gen. 41:48–57: Joseph gathering and blessing the granaries of Egypt during the seven years of plenty, sustaining the nations during the subsequent severe drought and famine. The Old Slavonic witness employs the archaic terms ве́дождїе (drought / rainlessness) and ве́зводїе (waterlessness / aridity).
[^3880]: John 11:25: "Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believes in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live."
[^3881]: Concluding Trinitarian doxology of the prayer at the blessing of the Artos.
[^3882]: Rubric introducing the second prayer over the Artos: the Prayer at the Breaking of the Artos (Мл҃тва на преломле́нїи а҆́ртоу́са), celebrated on Bright Saturday after the Prayer behind the Ambo at the Divine Liturgy.
[^3883]: Opening thanksgiving recounting the incarnate economy of Christ (плотско́е смотре́нїе; Greek ἔνσαρκος οἰκονομία) and His miraculous feeding of the multitudes in the desert. The prayer continues onto fol. 627v (leaf p1270).
[^3884]: Matt. 14:21: "And they that had eaten were about five thousand men, beside women and children."
[^3885]: Matt. 13:8, 23; Luke 8:8: petition for a hundredfold blessing (бога́тное истокра́тное бл҃венїе) upon those who have prepared and offered the Artos, and upon all the faithful who partake thereof for the healing of soul and body.
[^3886]: Commemoration of Hieromartyr Polycarp, Bishop of Smyrna (disciple of St. John the Theologian, martyred c. 155/156 AD). In Byzantine and Ruthenian euchological tradition, St. Polycarp is invoked in agricultural, seed, and bread blessings because of the Greek etymology of his name (πολύκαρπος, "abounding in fruit", "bearing much fruit"), symbolically connecting the martyr's fruitfulness in faith with the fruitful multiplication of bread and earthly sustenance.
[^3887]: Solemn commemoration of Christ's third-day Resurrection from the dead (ст҃ое тридне́вное въскрⷭ҇сенїе), the fountain of eternal life and incorruption.
[^3888]: Concluding Trinitarian doxology sealing the Order of the Artos and the conclusion of Cohort 127.
