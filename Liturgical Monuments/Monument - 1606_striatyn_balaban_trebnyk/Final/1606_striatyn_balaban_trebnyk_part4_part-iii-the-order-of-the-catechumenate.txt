# PART III: THE ORDER OF THE CATECHUMENATE FOR THOSE WHO WISH TO BE BAPTIZED [^82]

*The catechumen is brought, or is carried if he be an infant, unto the church. And they divest him, and stand him facing toward the east, unloosed of his belt, uncovered, and barefoot, having his hands hanging down.[^83] And the priest breathes upon him thrice, and signs his face and breast thrice, and lays his hands upon his head,[^84] and says this prayer:*

**Deacon [or Priest]:** Let us pray to the Lord.

**Priest:**
[Fol. 15v. Decorative woodcut initial O.]
In Thy Name, O Lord God of truth, and [in the Name] of Thine Only-begotten Son, and of Thy Most Holy Spirit: I lay my hand upon this Thy servant, *[Name]*, who has been accounted worthy to flee unto Thy holy Name, and to be preserved under the shelter of Thy wings.[^85] Remove from him that ancient deception, and fill him with faith, hope, and love in Thee; that he may know that Thou art the only true God, and Thine Only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, and Thy Holy Spirit. Grant him to walk in all Thy commandments, and to do and keep the things pleasing unto Thee: for if a man do them, he shall live in them. Inscribe him in the book of life, and unite him unto the flock of Thine inheritance,[^86] that Thy holy Name may be glorified in him, and [the Name] of Thy beloved Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, and of Thy life-creating Spirit. Let Thine eyes look upon him mercifully at all times, and Thine ears give ear unto the voice of his supplication. Gladden him in the works of his hands, and in all his generation, that he may give thanks unto Thee, worshipping and glorifying Thy great and most high Name, and may praise Thee alway in all the days of his life: for all the heavenly powers sing praise unto Thee, and Thine is the glory unto ages of ages. Amen.

*First Exorcism.*[^87]

**Deacon [or Priest]:** Let us pray to the Lord.

**Priest:**
[Fol. 16v. Decorative woodcut initial Z.]
The Lord rebukes thee, O devil: He Who came into the world and dwelt among men, that He might destroy thy tyranny and deliver mankind; Who upon the Tree exposed the adverse powers, when the sun was darkened, and the earth was shaken, and the graves were opened, and the bodies of the saints arose; Who destroyed death by death, and brought to naught him that had the power of death, that is, thee, O devil. I adjure thee by God, [I adjure thee by God,] Who showed forth the tree of life, and appointed the cherubim and the flaming sword turning every way to keep it: be rebuked and depart! For I adjure thee by Him Who walked upon the sea as upon dry land, and rebuked the tempest of winds; at Whose glance the deeps dry up, and at Whose rebuke the mountains melt away.[^88] For He now commands thee through us: Fear, come forth, and depart from this creature; and turn not back, neither hide thyself in him, neither encounter him, nor work in him, neither in night, nor in day, nor in an hour, nor at midday; but depart into thine own abyss [tartarus], until the prepared great day of judgment. Fear God Who sits upon the cherubim and looks down upon the deeps; before Whom tremble angels and archangels, thrones, dominions, principalities, authorities, powers, the many-eyed cherubim, and the six-winged seraphim; before Whom the heaven and the earth tremble, the sea, and all that in them is. Come forth and depart from the signed and newly chosen soldier of Christ our God. For I adjure thee by Him Who walks upon the wings of the wind, Who makes His angels spirits, and His ministers a flame of fire: come forth and depart from this creature with[^89] [with] all thy power and thine angels. For glorified is the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto [ages of ages. Amen.]

*Second Exorcism.*[^90]

**Deacon [or Priest]:** Let us pray to the Lord.

**Priest:**
[Fol. 18r. Decorative woodcut initial B.]
O God, holy, fearful, and glorious, Who art incomprehensible and unsearchable in all Thy works and Thy power: Thou Who didst ordain for thee, O devil, the torment of eternal punishment: through us, His unworthy servants, He commands thee and all thy collaborative power to depart from him that has newly been signed in the Name of our Lord Jesus Christ, our true God. I adjure thee therefore, all-evil, unclean, defiled, abominable, and alien spirit, by the power of Jesus Christ, Who has all power in heaven and on earth; Who said unto the deaf and dumb demon: "Come out of the man, and enter no more into him!" Depart, recognize thy vain power, which had no authority even over the swine! Remember Him Who commanded thee at thy request to enter into the herd of swine. Fear God, by Whose command the earth was established upon the waters; Who created heaven, and set the mountains by a plummet, and the valleys by a measure; and placed sand as a boundary for the sea, and a firm path in the mighty waters; Who touches the mountains, and they smoke;[^91]

Who decks Himself with light as with a garment; Who covers His upper chambers with waters; Who founded the earth upon its firmness, and it shall not be shaken unto ages of ages; Who calls forth the water of the sea, and pours it out upon the face of all the earth: come forth and depart from him that is preparing for holy illumination! I adjure thee by the saving Passion of our Lord Jesus Christ, and His precious Body and Blood, and His fearful coming: for He shall come and will not delay, judging all the earth and thee, and He will torment thy collaborative power in fiery Gehenna, where the worm[^92] dies not, and the fire is not quenched: for the dominion is of Christ our God, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages [of ages. Amen.]

*Third Exorcism Prayer.*[^93]

**Deacon [or Priest]:** Let us pray to the Lord.

**Priest:**
[Fol. 19v. Decorative woodcut initial G.]
O Lord of hosts [Sabaoth], God of Israel, Who healest every disease and every infirmity: look down upon Thy servant, *[Name]*, seek out, claim, and drive away from him all workings of the devil. Rebuke the unclean spirits, and drive them away, and cleanse the work of Thy hands; and, putting forth Thy swift operation, crush Satan under his feet shortly; and grant him victory over him and over his unclean spirits: that, having obtained mercy from Thee, he may be accounted worthy of Thine immortal and heavenly Mysteries, and send up glory unto Thee, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*Fourth Exorcism Prayer.*[^94]

**Deacon [or Priest]:** Let us pray to the Lord.

**Priest:**
[Fol. 20r. Decorative woodcut initial S.]
O Sovereign Lord, Who hast made man according to Thine own image and likeness, and given him the power of life everlasting; and then, when he had fallen through sin, didst not despise him, but didst provide salvation for the world through the incarnation of Thy Christ: do Thou now, having delivered this Thy creature from the bondage of the enemy, receive him into Thy heavenly kingdom. Open his spiritual eyes, that the illumination of Thy Gospel may shine in him. Yoke with his life an angel of light, delivering him from every snare of the adversary, from encounter with the evil one, from the noonday demon, and from wicked apparitions.[^95]

*And he breathes again thrice, and signs his brow, and his mouth, and his breast, and says this prayer:*

**Deacon [or Priest]:** Let us pray to the Lord.

**Priest:**
[Fol. 20v. Decorative initial I.]
Expel from him every wicked and unclean spirit, hiding and nesting in his heart.

*(And he says thrice:)*
A spirit of deception and wickedness; a spirit of idolatry and all covetousness; a spirit of lying and of all impurity, wrought according to the prompting of the devil. And make him a reason-endowed sheep of the holy flock of Thy Christ, an honorable member of Thy Church, a consecrated vessel, a child of light, and an heir of Thy kingdom: that, having lived according to Thy commandments, and preserved the seal unbroken, and kept the garment undefiled, he may attain the blessedness of Thy chosen ones in Thy kingdom.[^96]

**Exclamation:**
Through the grace and compassions and love for mankind of Thine Only-begotten Son, with Whom Thou art blessed, together with Thy Most Holy, good, and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. [Amen.]

*And when he that is to be baptized is divested and ungirded, the priest turns him toward the west, with his hands uplifted. And the priest says:*[^97]

**Priest:** Dost thou renounce Satan?

**Catechumen:** I renounce Satan.

**Priest:** And all his works, and all his service, and all his angels, and all his pride?

*(And it is said thrice. And the catechumen, or his sponsor, answers at each phrase.)*

*If he be an infant and cannot speak, then let the sponsor [kum] say on his behalf:* "He renounces Satan," *and the rest.*

*Likewise, if he be a foreigner [of another tongue] and knows not how to answer, let the sponsor [answer on his behalf]* let him speak on his behalf, or the deacon.[^98]

*Thereafter the priest says again:*
**Priest:** Dost thou renounce Satan?

*He that is being baptized says:*
**Catechumen:** I have renounced Satan.

*And when he has said it the third time, the priest says unto him:*
**Priest:** And breathe, and spit upon the demon.

*And when he has done this, the priest turns him again facing toward the east, having his hands down. And he says, thrice:*
**Priest:** Dost thou pledge thyself unto Christ?

*And he that is being baptized, or the sponsor, answers:*
**Catechumen [or Sponsor]:** I pledge myself unto Christ.

**Priest:** Dost thou believe in Him?

**Catechumen [or Sponsor]:** I believe in Him as King and God.

*And he says:*
**Catechumen [or Sponsor]:**
I believe in one God, the Father Almighty... unto the end, thrice.

*And when he has completed* the Holy Creed, *he says again unto him thrice:*
**Priest:** Hast thou pledged thyself unto Christ?

*And he answers:*
**Catechumen [or Sponsor]:** I have pledged myself.

**Priest:** Bow down before Him.

*And he bows down, saying:*
**Catechumen [or Sponsor]:**
I bow down before the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit: Trinity consubstantial and undivided.

*Then the priest says:*
**Priest:** Blessed is God, Who wills all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth; now and ever, and unto ages of ages.[^99]

*Then he says this prayer:*
**Deacon [or Priest]:** Let us pray to the Lord.

**Priest:**
[Fol. 22v. Decorative initial V.]
O Master, Lord our God, call Thy servant, *[Name]*, unto Thy holy illumination, and count him worthy of this great grace of Thy holy baptism. Wash away from him the oldness [of sin], and regenerate him unto eternal life; and fill him with the power of Thy Holy Spirit, into the union of Thy Christ: that he may no longer be a child of the flesh, but a child of Thy kingdom; through the good pleasure and bounty of Thine Only-begotten Son, with Whom Thou art blessed, together with Thine All-holy, and good, and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^100]

[Fol. 23r. Tailpiece: Renaissance woodcut vignette ornament with scrolled acanthus leaves and central palmette.]

[Fol. 23v. Headpiece: Elaborate Renaissance woodcut engraving depicting the Baptism of an Infant (*КР҃ЩЕНЇЕ Ѻ҆ТРОЧАТА*) in an oval medallion supported by two winged angels amidst scrolling acanthus vines.]

### Notes

[^82]: Fol. 15r. Part III marks the start of the liturgical celebration of Holy Baptism itself, beginning with the Rite of Making a Catechumen (*Чинъ быва́емый на оглаше́нїе*). The opening leaf is adorned with a monumental Renaissance strapwork headpiece featuring a central medallion of Christ the Great High Priest / Emmanuel blessing with both hands, flanked by adoring angels, cornucopias, and pomegranates.
[^83]: Fol. 15r. The introductory rubrics prescribe the ancient Byzantine posture of the candidate at the church doors: facing eastward toward the sanctuary, stripped of outer garments down to the tunic, ungirded, bareheaded, and barefoot, with hands lowered at the sides, symbolizing the renunciation of worldly adornments and submission to Christ.
[^84]: Fol. 15r. The triple ritual action opening the catechumenate: threefold insufflation (*доу́нетъ три́щи*) driving out the enemy, threefold signing of the brow and breast with the Cross, and imposition of the priest's right hand (*cheirothesia*) claiming the candidate for Christ.
[^85]: Fol. 15v. The First Prayer of the Catechumenate (*Ѡ҆ и҆́мени твое́мъ Гд҃и Бж҃е и҆́стины...*) is an ancient euchological formula attested in the 8th-century Barberini Euchologion (Barberini Gr. 336, fol. 195r). It quotes Ezekiel 20:11 and Leviticus 18:5 (*"which if a man do, he shall live in them"*; cf. Romans 10:5).
[^86]: Fol. 16r. Scriptural allusions to the "book of life" (*кни́гы живо́тныѧ*, Revelation 3:5; 20:12) and Christ's spiritual flock (Psalm 27:9 / LXX 28:9; 1 Peter 5:3).
[^87]: Fol. 16v. The First Exorcism (*Запрѣще́нїе пръ́вое*), traditionally attributed to St. Basil the Great, commands the devil to depart by virtue of Christ's incarnation, His Passion on the Cross, the harrowing of Hades, and His destruction of the power of death (Hebrews 2:14; Matthew 27:51–53; Jude 1:9).
[^88]: Fol. 17r. Continuation of the First Exorcism of St. Basil the Great from Fol. 16v. The euchological text asserts the absolute cosmic sovereignty of God over Satan, citing the primordial placement of the cherubim and the turning flaming sword to guard the Tree of Life (Genesis 3:24), Christ walking upon the sea as upon dry land (Matthew 14:25), rebuking the winds and sea (Mark 4:39), the drying up of the deeps at His glance (Nahum 1:4), and the melting of mountains at His rebuke (Psalm 96:5 / LXX 96:5). The exorcism explicitly bans demonic intrusion across all temporal divisions (night, day, hour, and noonday).
[^89]: Fol. 17v. The demonic host is commanded to depart into tartarus (*тартаръ*, the abyssal prison of fallen spirits; cf. 2 Peter 2:4; Jude 6) until the Great Day of Judgment. The prayer invokes the celestial hierarchy—angels, archangels, thrones, dominions, principalities, authorities, powers, many-eyed cherubim (Ezekiel 1:18; 10:12), and six-winged seraphim (Isaiah 6:2)—and quotes Psalm 103:3–4 (LXX 103:3–4: *"Who walks upon the wings of the wind; Who makes His angels spirits, and His ministers a flame of fire"*). The candidate is solemnly designated as the "newly chosen soldier of Christ our God" (*ново и҆ꙁбра́ннаго во́ина Хрⷭ҇та̀ Бг҃а на́шего*), evoking the patristic typology of baptism as the enlistment oath (*sacramentum militare*) of the Christian soldier.
[^90]: Fol. 18r. The Second Exorcism (*Запрѣще́нїе вто́рое*), likewise attributed in classical Byzantine and Ruthenian euchologia to St. Basil the Great. The priest acts not by his own power, but in persona Christi through whom God Himself commands Satan and his allied force (*споспѣ́шная си́ла*, Greek: συνεργὸς δύнаμις) to depart from the candidate newly signed with the sign of the Cross (*новознамена́нный*).
[^91]: Fol. 18v. Dominical and scriptural catena demonstrating the complete impotence of demonic power before Christ: the exorcism of the deaf and dumb spirit (*ре́кшаго глоухо́моу и҆ беꙁгла́номоу бѣ́соу: и҆ꙁы́ди ѿ чл҃ка...*, Mark 9:25) and Christ's permission to the Legion demons to enter the herd of swine (Matthew 8:31–32; Mark 5:12–13; Luke 8:32–33), proving that demons cannot harm even animals without divine permission. These dominical miracles are framed within Old Testament cosmological proofs of God's almighty creative power: establishing the earth upon the waters (Psalm 135:6 / LXX 135:6), weighing the mountains and valleys (Isaiah 40:12), setting the sand as the eternal bound of the sea (Jeremiah 5:22), and touching the mountains so that they smoke (Psalm 103:32 / LXX 103:32).
[^92]: Fol. 19r. Continuation of the cosmological praise from Psalm 103 (LXX 103:2, 3, 5): wrapping Himself in light as in a garment, laying the beams of His chambers in the waters, and establishing the earth upon its foundations never to be moved. The exorcism culminates in an eschatological adjuration by Christ's saving Passion, precious Body and Blood, and His fearful Second Coming, in which He shall not delay (Habakkuk 2:3; Hebrews 10:37) to judge the world and cast the demonic powers into the unquenchable fire and the undying worm of Gehenna (Isaiah 66:24; Mark 9:44, 48).
[^93]: Fol. 19v. The Third Exorcism Prayer (*Мл҃тва запрѣще́нїю тре́тїа*), attributed in Byzantine euchologia to St. John Chrysostom. The petition invokes the "Lord Sabaoth, God of Israel" (*Гдⷭ҇и саваѡ́ѳъ Бг҃е і҆и҃левъ*) to heal the candidate and crush Satan under his feet shortly, directly citing Romans 16:20 (*"And the God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly"*).
[^94]: Fol. 20r. The Fourth Exorcism Prayer (*Мл҃тва д҃, запрѣще́нїю*), also attributed to St. John Chrysostom. The prayer recalls the creation of humanity in the image and likeness of God (Genesis 1:26), the tragedy of the Fall, and the divine economy of salvation through the Incarnation of Christ (John 3:17; 1 Timothy 1:15). It petitions God to open the noetic/spiritual eyes (*мы́сленѣи ѻ҆́чи*, Greek: νοεροὺς ὀφθалμούς; Ephesians 1:18) of the candidate.
[^95]: Fol. 20v. The prayer petitions God to assign to the candidate an "angel of light" (*а҆́гг҃лъ свѣ́телъ*) to preserve him from all hostile assaults, the encounter of the adversary, the noonday demon (*бѣ́съ полоу́денный*, Greek: δаймо́нїон месемврїно́н; Psalm 90:6 / LXX 90:6), and evil apparitions. The rubrics instruct the priest to breathe thrice upon the candidate and sign his brow, mouth, and breast, followed by the threefold expulsion formula commanding the departure of the spirits of error, deceit, idolatry, covetousness, lying, and uncleanness.
[^96]: Fol. 21r. The positive transformation of the candidate into a reason-endowed sheep (*ѻ҆́вча слове́сно*, Greek: πρόβατον λογικόν) of Christ's flock (John 10:14–16), an honorable member of the Church (1 Corinthians 12:27), a consecrated vessel (2 Timothy 2:21), a child of light (Ephesians 5:8), and an heir of the Kingdom (Romans 8:17). The candidate is exhorted to keep the baptismal seal (*печа́ть*, Greek: σфραγίς) unbroken and the white baptismal robe (*ри́за*, Greek: χιτών) undefiled (Galatians 3:27; Revelation 3:4). The prayer closes with the classical Trinitarian ekphonesis.
[^97]: Fol. 21v. The Rite of Renunciation of Satan (*Apotaxis* / *Ѽрица́нїе*). The candidate is divested of outer garments and untied, symbolizing the shedding of fallen mortality and earthly entanglements, and turned toward the West (the symbol of darkness, sunset, and demonic power) with hands raised in repudiation. The 1606 Striatyn edition incorporates distinct Ruthenian pastoral rubrics, using both *по́пъ* and *сщ҃енникъ* for priest, and introducing the vernacular term *коу́мъ* (godparent / sponsor) alongside the classical term *въспрїе́мникъ* (*anadochos*). Specific pastoral provisions allow the godparent to answer on behalf of infants unable to speak (*а҆́ще ли бꙋ́детъ мла́до...*) and non-Slavonic foreigners (*и҆ноплеме́нни*). The term *стоу́да є҆го̀* translates Greek *πομπῆς αὐτοῦ* ("his pomps/parade").
[^98]: Fol. 22r. The transition from the Renunciation of Satan (*Apotaxis*) to the Adherence to Christ (*Syntaxis*). Note the distinctive Ruthenian euchological terminology: while modern Synodal Church Slavonic editions employ the verb *сочетава́тися* (*сочетава́еши ли сѧ Хрїстꙋ̀*, rendering Greek συντάσσομαι), the 1606 Striatyn imprint uses *ѡ҆бѣщава́тися* (*Ѡ҆бѣща́еши ли сѧ Хрⷭ҇тꙋ̀* / *Ѡ҆бѣща́юсѧ*), emphasizing the covenantal pledge/vow made to Christ. Also noteworthy is the pastoral provision continuing from Fol. 21v permitting the godparent (*коу́мъ*) or the deacon to respond on behalf of foreigners (*и҆ноплеме́нни*) unable to speak Church Slavonic. The candidate is directed to turn eastward with hands lowered (symbolizing submissive devotion, in contrast to hands raised westward during the renunciation of Satan), followed by the profession of the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed thrice.
[^99]: Fol. 22v. The post-credal confirmation: the priest interrogates the candidate in the perfect tense (*Ѡ҆бѣща́лъ ли сѧ є҆сѝ Хрⷭ҇тꙋ̀* / *Ѡ҆бѣща́хсѧ*), followed by the act of adoration (*proskynesis*) before the Holy Trinity. The priest recites the dominical doxology quoting 1 Timothy 2:4 (*"Who wills all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth"*). Then follows the final pre-baptismal prayer of the catechumenate (*Влⷣко Гдⷭ҇и Бг҃е на́шъ, призовѝ раба̀ твое́го...*), petitioning God to summon the candidate to the "holy illumination" (*ст҃ое просвѣще́нїе*, the ancient patristic term for baptism, Greek: φωτισμός).
[^100]: Fol. 23r. Conclusion of the prayer of the catechumenate. The euchological text draws upon Johannine and Pauline theology: the washing away of the "oldness" (*ѻ҆ветша́нїе*, Romans 6:6; Ephesians 4:22), rebirth unto eternal life (John 3:3–5; Titus 3:5), and transformation from a mere "child of the flesh" (*ча́до тѣле́сное*, Romans 9:8) into a "child of the kingdom" (*ча́до црⷭ҇твїа*). The prayer concludes with the full Trinitarian ekphonesis and a Renaissance floral woodcut tailpiece marking the structural close of the Office of the Catechumenate.
