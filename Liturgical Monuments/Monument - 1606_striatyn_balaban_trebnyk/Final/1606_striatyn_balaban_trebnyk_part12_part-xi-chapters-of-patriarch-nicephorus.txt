# PART XI: CHAPTERS OF PATRIARCH NICEPHORUS CONCERNING DIGAMISTS [^245]

CHAPTERS OF NICEPHORUS, PATRIARCH OF CONSTANTINOPLE, THE CONFESSOR, CONCERNING DIGAMISTS

[Fol. 72v. Initial D.]
A digamist is not crowned, but is forbidden to partake of the immaculate Mysteries for two years. And a trigamist, or one who enters into a third marriage, for three years.[^246]

*From the Answers of the Blessed Nicetas, Metropolitan of Heraclea, Concerning Which He Was Questioned by Bishop Constantine:*[^247]

[Fol. 72v. Initial O.]
Strict canonical rigor indeed is not accustomed to crown digamists; but the Great Church's custom observes not such things, but places bridal crowns upon digamists also; and no one was ever condemned on this account. Nevertheless, for one year, or two, they are forbidden divine Communion. Moreover, they have ordained that the presbyter who has blessed the marriage should not sup with them, according to the Seventh Canon of the Council of Neocaesarea.[^248]

### Notes

[^245]: Fol. 72v. Patriarch St. Nicephorus the Confessor and Byzantine Canonical Discipline Concerning Digamy (*Гла́вы Ники́фора Патрїа́рха... ѡ двобра́чныхъ*). St. Nicephorus I, Patriarch of Constantinople (806–815, d. 828), championed icon veneration and authored authoritative canonical rulings (*κανόνες* / *главы*). In Byzantine sacramental theology, a first marriage uniquely icons the eternal, indissoluble mystery of Christ and the Church (Ephesians 5:32). Subsequent marriages (digamy and trigamy), while tolerated by pastoral condescension (*oikonomia*) to avert greater moral lapse (1 Corinthians 7:9), were regarded as concessions to human frailty, traditionally accompanied by penitential excommunication (*epitimia*) rather than festive nuptial crowning.
[^246]: Fol. 72v. Canonical Periods of Abstention for Second and Third Marriages. Nicephorus's rule prescribes a two-year abstention from Holy Communion for digamists (*двоже́нецъ*) and three years for trigamists (*триже́нецъ*), drawing upon the earlier penitential canons of St. Basil the Great (Canons 4 and 50) and the Council of Neocaesarea (Canon 3). In subsequent Byzantine and Slavic practice, these penitential timeframes were subject to the discretion and mitigation of the spiritual father.
[^247]: Fol. 72v. The Canonical Rescripts of Metropolitan Nicetas of Heraclea (*Ѡ ѿвѣща́нїи бл҃жнаго Ники́ты митрополи́та И҆раклїи́скаго...*). Nicetas, Metropolitan of Heraclea (late 11th century), authored celebrated canonical responsa to queries posed by Bishop Constantine. Here he distinguishes between strict canonical rigor (*ἀκρίβεια* / *ѡпа́сство*)—which strictly forbade the crowning of second unions—and the living customary practice of the Great Church of Constantinople (*вели́кыѧ цр҃кве ѡ҆бы́чай*), which permitted the crowning of digamists while maintaining the canonical penance regarding Eucharistic communion.
[^248]: Fol. 73r. The Council of Neocaesarea (c. 314–319 AD), Canon 7. Canon 7 of Neocaesarea explicitly forbids a presbyter from dining at the wedding banquet of digamists (*вече́рѧти съ ни́ми не оу҆законоположи́ша*): "A presbyter ought not to be present at the marriage feast of those who marry a second time; for, seeing that the digamist is in need of penance, what presbyter can he be who, by his presence at the feast, gives his sanction to the marriage?" The inclusion of this canon in the Balaban Trebnyk underscores the dual theological tension preserved in the Kyivan-Ruthenian recension between canonical discipline and pastoral mercy.
