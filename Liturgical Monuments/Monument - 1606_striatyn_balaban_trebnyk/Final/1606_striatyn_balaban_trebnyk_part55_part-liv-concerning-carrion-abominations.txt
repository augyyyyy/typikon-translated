# PART LIV: CONCERNING CARRION [ABOMINATIONS] WHICH IT IS NOT BEFITTING TO EAT [^4032]

We command therefore, also concerning that which is strangled, and concerning carrion [abominations]: that no one among Christians should taste such things; nor having slaughtered [an animal], to drain the blood and bake it, which certain senseless persons do; for the Law of God commands that meat in the blood—the soul—shall not be eaten (cf. Gen. 9:4; Lev. 17:11–14); for the blood of every beast is its life [soul].[^4033]

Likewise also those who set nets for the sake of hunting, and beasts or creeping things are strangled therein, and they taking them eat [them], or [an animal] hunted and strangled by dogs: unto such persons the Law of God forbids to do these things; except only that which is slain, either with an arrow, or with a spear, or with a sword by human hands[^4034] *Of the Holy Fathers:*

...so that its blood be poured out: this let everyone eat. But if anyone, besides this, eats anything else of the forbidden things: let such a one be separated for one hundred and fifty days, [performing] twelve prostrations each day.[^4035] But as for that which is seized by a hawk, any kind of reptile [or creature]: if anyone takes it quickly and suddenly, while it is still warm in its blood: then let him slaughter it, and let everyone eat [it]; but if he delays, not to eat such a thing.[^4036] But apart from such things as were commanded for every Christian to eat: all other strangled and unslaughtered things let them be cast out unto dogs and creeping things [scavengers].[^4037]

*Those who eat a raven, or a crow, a jackdaw, a cuckoo, a hawk, or whatever kind of*[^4038] *Injunctions:*

...eagle, or gull: let such persons repent under penance for one year, [performing] one hundred prostrations.[^4039]

*Those who eat a wolf, a fox, a bear, a dog, a cat, a hedgehog, a dormouse, a marten, a squirrel, and all other things whatsoever are unclean: a grass-snake, a turtle, and every beast, both small and great; or a horse, or a donkey; whether of wild beasts or of pastured herds, whatsoever the Law of God designates [as unclean]:*[^4040]

If anyone be found eating this of his own will and desire: let him repent for four years; but if he ate through constraint and necessity: let him repent for one year, [performing] fifteen prostrations.[^4041]

*If into a well from which water does not flow out, there fall any reptile [or animal], and be drowned*[^4042] *Concerning Forbidden Foods:*

...any [creature], and be drowned; or an animal: if it be found quickly and taken out, let three buckets of that water be poured out; and so let everyone drink from that well—that is, if that which was drowned in it, whether an animal or a reptile, is found quickly on the first day.[^4043] But if it remains until the second day or until the third, and thereafter is taken out: let nine buckets of that water be poured out, and so let them pour in holy water (*hagiasma*), and let them drink again from that well.[^4044] Thus let them pour in holy water if, being [found] quickly, they suddenly cleanse it. But if, having remained for many days, it rots in it: let them pour out from it forty buckets of water, and so let[^4045] *Injunctions:*

...the priest come with a server [cleric], and kindle three candles around it, and cense, and say the prayer, and sanctify it; and then let them drink from it.[^4046] But if its water flows and goes forth outside: such a thing harms us in no way.[^4047] But if [an unclean creature] falls into wine, or into oil: if it be found quickly, let it be taken out, and let the priest come and sanctify it.[^4048] But if it delays until it begins to rot in it unto the second day: let it be decanted into another clean vessel, and let it be sanctified by the priest. But if it remains until it rots [completely]: it is not befitting to taste it, but let it all be poured out onto the ground.[^4049]

*If it happens that any reptile [or vermin] whatsoever breeds [in wheat]*[^4050] *Concerning Forbidden Foods:*

...or of unclean animals in wheat, and defiles it: then let him wash the wheat well with water, and let it be sanctified by the priest, and then let them grind it and eat [it]; but let them not make prosphora from that wheat.[^4051]

*If a Jew defiles anything whatsoever with his hand, having taken hold of it, whether in wine, or in oil, or anything else of such things:*[^4052]

It is not befitting for a Christian to taste it; but let the priest come and sanctify it, and thereafter let it be tasted.

*Whosoever eats with an Armenian, or with a Paulician, or with any other heretic whatsoever, and especially if anyone has affection [close fellowship] with such a one: unto him we say that he shall desist from this, and come pure unto the church*[^4053] *Injunctions:*

...and if he has done this through ignorance, let the priest cleanse him.[^4054] But if he knows and has heard the admonition, let him again sanctify him, having given him a small penance from the sanctions. But if he does not obey the admonition, but desires to eat and drink with them: let such a one not eat with a Christian, and let him not be received into church, but let every Christian turn away from him as from an idolater.[^4055] And if ever he comes again unto confession: let him be separated for repentance for three years; and during two years let there not be received from his house either prosphora or any offering whatsoever; and in the third year let his prosphora be received in church, and so[^4056] *Of the Holy Fathers:*

...let him complete the third year in repentance.

*If anyone communicates [has fellowship] with a Hebrew, and is like-minded with him in their faith:*[^4057]

And if ever he returns unto Christianity: let him be separated for repentance for nine years: three years in the place of the catechumens; and thereafter let his prosphora be received in church, and let him perform the remainder in repentance; and in the ninth year let him receive communion of the Holy Mysteries.[^4058]

*Those who marry off their sons or daughters unto heterodox persons:*[^4059]

Let such persons be separated for five years; likewise also the priests who crowned [married] them, let them also be separated for three years, outside the church.[^4060]

*A grain-caster [sorcerer], or anyone else of such persons, who knows this diabolical work, or*[^4061] *Injunctions:*

...concerning wine, or by any other bewitchment [*ochybom*]: if they desist from this, let them be separated for four years, [performing] one hundred prostrations each day.[^4062]

*If anyone betroths a maiden, and thereafter someone else seduces her and defiles her, wishing to take her [to wife]:*[^4063]

This is not befitting; but if her first betrothed desires [her] again, let him take her.[^4064]

*Concerning the Memorial of the Departed:*[^4065]

Unto the departed, therefore, it is befitting to make memorials and oblations: on the third day (*tretyny*), and the ninth day (*devyatyny*), and the twentieth day (*dvadesyatyny*), and the fortieth day (*chetyrydesyatyny*), and at six months, and at the completed year; likewise also on Meatfare Saturday, and Cheesefare Saturday, and on the seventh Saturday after Pascha [Ancestral Saturday before Pentecost]: of these[^4066] *Of the Holy Fathers:*

...for on account of these things we believe that they find absolution of their sins.[^4067]

*Those who estrange themselves from prayers and chanting of their own will, and walk like beasts:*[^4068]

Let them be separated for repentance for one year, [performing] one hundred metanias [prostrations].

*Whosoever of his own will casts himself down and kills himself: let them neither chant over him, nor ever make memorial for him. But if not of his own will he falls, but is killed and dies: let them chant over him, and let them make memorial.*[^4069]

*If anyone goes out unto adultery, or unto fornication, or unto theft, or unto brigandage; and if in any way from these things he is killed and dies:*[^4070]

Let no one chant over him, and let them make no memorial whatsoever for him.

*Injunctions:*

*Unto every parent it is befitting to marry his son when the years of his age are completed, fifteen years; and the daughter, twelve years; for this is the true law:*[^4071]

*Concerning Women:*

A woman who practices sorcery, or a man: let them cease and repent; but if not, let them[^4157] *Of the Holy Fathers:*

...be separated from Christianity.[^4158]

A woman, if she drinks herbs to destroy the fruit of her womb so as not to bear children: let her be separated, and let her repent for five years; three hundred prostrations.[^4159]

And if she moreover has a husband, and does this: it is far worse; neither let the church receive her oblation, nor her prosphora, unless she repents. And if her husband also took counsel with her concerning this, and both do this: let him likewise be separated.[^4160]

A woman, if she lusts after another man, and destroys her husband by sorceries: then if she was seduced by a certain man, and he took her not to wife: let her be separated for five years. But if he takes her to wife, and she takes that man[^4161] *Injunctions:*

...having accomplished an unlawful deed: let both be separated for fifteen years; and let them receive penance concerning murder, to perform the same as an intentional murderer.[^4162]

And if she betrays her husband unto murder, even as Delilah did Samson: then let her be under penance all the days of her life.[^4163]

A woman, if through some craft of the enemy she of her own will slays her infants at birth: let her be separated and repent all the days of her life.[^4164]

A fornicating woman, if she drinks herbs so as not to bear; or having given birth, suffocates her infant: let her repent for nine years; one hundred and seven prostrations.[^4165]

A woman who is a procuress, or a man: let them repent[^4166] *Of the Holy Fathers:*

...for three years.[^4167] But if it be a monk or a nun: five years.[^4168]

We command women to confess in the church narthex, and to have the outer doors open for the sake of avoiding scandal.[^4169]

We have commanded also fully professed monks and those preserving virginity that they shall not receive women unto themselves for confession; only those who have attained unto chaste and orderly old age. Let such a one be assigned unto these, lest he be scandalized in his thoughts; for through women the devil wages warfare against monks and the saints.[^4170]

And whenever any one of them desires to hear a woman's confession, let him first strengthen himself with prayers and supplications, singing the Psalms: [Psalm 50] "Have mercy on me, O God,"[^4171] *Injunctions:*

...and [Psalm 95 / 99 / 65] "Sing unto the Lord, all the earth." And after this let him receive any woman whatsoever unto confession.[^4172]

He that commits these acts of self-pollution and fornication: let him receive a penance of three years; twenty-four prostrations.[^4173]

Now self-pollution has two varieties: one with the hands, the other between one's thighs; and that with the hands is worse than that between the thighs; yet both are evil and corrupt.[^4174]

There is also female self-pollution, which women commit one upon another. Therefore it is fitting for the spiritual father to examine all these things, and to give them a penance of one year.[^4175]

Therefore on account of these things, [to avoid] every stumbling, we command devout women[^4176] *Of the Holy Fathers:*

If any woman enters into church, and while she is there perceives her customary flow: having discovered it, let her depart at that very hour, having no sin.[^4178]

But if, having noticed it, she remains for the sake of human shame, or receives the oblation: let her be separated from the church and from communion for six months; fifty prostrations.[^4179]

Before a woman is cleansed from that which happens according to her custom, let her not bake prosphora, neither let her touch it in any manner whatsoever.[^4180]

She that has her customary flow, if she arrives at the extremity of death: let her be vouchsafed communion without hindrance.[^4181]

If any woman drinks a potion that she may not conceive a child: seven years; two hundred prostrations.[^4227]

*Of the Holy Fathers:*[fol. 674r][^4237]

Likewise also a man, both a ruler and one who is in authority: in the same manner let him be forbidden.

Forasmuch, therefore, as from the beginning God created man and woman, that He might raise up the world and His own possession; He gives unto us also to behold a certain custom: so that from that which is seen each month by them, making manifest conception unto that which comes to be; lest, as some say, that a woman experiences childbirth from the man, but from the woman there is nothing. ### Notes

[^4032]: Major headpiece of Part LIV: *Ѡ҆ мръщи́нах и҆́хже не достои́т ꙗ҆́сти* ("Concerning Carrion [Abominations] Which It Is Not Befitting to Eat"). Title introducing the canonical treatise on dietary purity, regulating meats forbidden under biblical and Byzantine canon law (Apostolic Canon 63; Council in Trullo, Canon 67).
[^4033]: Scriptural and canonical prohibition of blood and strangled meat: The text cites the foundational biblical prohibitions of Genesis 9:4 ("flesh with the life thereof, which is the blood thereof, shall ye not eat") and Leviticus 17:11–14, reaffirmed for Christians at the Apostolic Council of Jerusalem (Acts 15:20, 29). It specifically condemns the superstitious culinary practice of catching, baking, or cooking coagulated blood.
[^4034]: Canonical hunting laws and meat purity: Prohibiting the consumption of game animals strangled in traps or hunting nets (*да́вѧсѧ въ ни́хъ ско́ти*), or mangled and strangled by hunting dogs (*ѿ пе́съ гони́мо и҆ оу҆да́влено*), permitting only game that was cleanly dispatched with blood drained by an arrow, spear, or blade struck by human hand.
[^4035]: Canonical penance for eating forbidden or strangled meat: The sanction of 150 days of separation (*сто падсѧтъ днїи*) with twelve daily prostrations reflects the penitential canons of the Nomocanon (cf. John the Faster, Canon 44; Syntagma of Matthew Blastares, letter B, chapter 8), enforcing the strict apostolic injunction of Acts 15:20, 29 against tasting blood or strangled flesh (*удавленина*).
[^4036]: Casuistry of predatory raptors (*кра́гоуи*, hawk/falcon): In Byzantine-Slavic nomocanonical practice (cf. Syntagma B.8; Nomocanon of the Great Euchologion), game caught by a trained hunting falcon or hawk was permissible to eat only if the hunter recovered the animal immediately while it remained alive and "still warm in its blood" (*ещѐ те́пло соу́щее въ кро́ви свое́и*), and ritually slaughtered it by cutting the throat to drain the blood. If the prey had already died or suffocated before ritual slaughter, it fell under the apostolic ban on strangled meat (*оу҆да́вленое*).
[^4037]: Disposal of strangled and carrion meat: Meat contaminated by asphyxiation or natural death is forbidden for human consumption and must be cast out to scavenging animals (*ѱѡ́мъ и҆ га́дѡмъ да̀ помѣта́етсѧ*), adhering to Exodus 22:31: "neither shall ye eat any flesh that is torn of beasts in the field; ye shall cast it to the dogs."
[^4038]: Canonical catalogue of forbidden birds: Echoing the Levitical dietary code (Lev. 11:13–19; Deut. 14:11–18), the Nomocanon enumerates birds of prey and carrion eaters: raven (*га́вранъ*), crow (*вра́на*), jackdaw (*га́лица*), cuckoo (*коукови́ца*), hawk (*кра́гоуи*), and continuing onto leaf p1322 with eagle and seagull.
[^4039]: Byzantine loanword *гла́росъ* (Greek γλάρος, gull/seagull): Listed alongside the eagle (*ѡ҆ре́лъ*) among prohibited birds of prey and shorebirds, based on Leviticus 11:13–18 (LXX: λάρος). Consumption of such birds incurs one year of penance and one hundred prostrations.
[^4040]: Comprehensive Slavic Nomocanon catalogue of forbidden quadrupeds and reptiles: Enumerating wolf (*влъкъ*), fox (*лиси́ца*), bear (*ме́чка*, Ukrainian dialectal *ведмідь/мечка*), dog (*песъ*), cat (*ко́тка*), hedgehog (*е҆жъ*), dormouse (*плъхъ*, Old Church Slavonic and Ukrainian *щур/вовчок*), marten (*коуна̀*), squirrel (*вѣ́верица*), grass-snake (*смокъ*), and turtle/tortoise (*же́лва*). The prohibition also extends to equine flesh—horse (*конь*) and donkey (*ѡ҆селъ*)—reinforcing both Mosaic hygiene (Lev. 11:2–8) and early medieval conciliar prohibitions (e.g. Pope Gregory III's letter to St. Boniface in 732, condemning the consumption of horsemeat as an abomination).
[^4041]: Distinction between willful transgression and duress in canonical penance: The Nomocanon draws a sharp juridical distinction between willful consumption (*свое́ю во́лею и҆ хотѣ́нїемъ*), which incurs four years of excommunication, and consumption driven by famine or extreme duress (*понево́ли и҆ поноу́жди*), which is mercifully reduced to one year with fifteen daily prostrations.
[^4042]: Ritual purity of wells and water reservoirs: Beginning the canonical rulings on water sources contaminated by the carcass of an unclean creature or drowned animal (cf. Lev. 11:29–38; Nomocanon of John the Faster; Syntagma of Matthew Blastares, letter K, chapter 14).
[^4043]: Canonical graduation of well cleansing by duration of contamination: The Nomocanon establishes a tripartite temporal rubric for well purification: (1) if the carcass is discovered and removed immediately on the first day before decomposition begins, drawing and discarding three buckets (*вѣ́дра трѝ*) suffices to restore the water for drinking.
[^4044]: Lustration with *hagiasma* (*а҆гїа́змо*): If the carcass remains submerged until the second or third day, nine buckets (*вѣ́дръ де́вѧть*) must be drawn out, followed by the infusion of consecrated holy water (*hagiasma*, Greek ἁγίασμα) from the Feast of Theophany or the Lesser Blessing of Waters, which ritually restores sanctification.
[^4045]: Advanced decomposition of carcasses in cisterns: When putrefaction has taken place over many days (*и҆згнїе́тъ въ не́мъ*), the rubric requires draining forty buckets (*четыридесѧ́тъ вѣ́дръ воды̀*), followed by the complete liturgical rite of lustration and blessing conducted by a priest with candles and incense (detailed on leaf p1324).
[^4046]: Liturgical rite for re-consecrating a defiled well: The ritual prescribed here corresponds directly to the office found in the Trebnyk: "Prayer over a Well Contaminated by Something Unclean" (*Молитва над кладенцем, в негоже впадет нечисто кое*). The priest lights three candles around the mouth of the well in honor of the Holy Trinity, censes the perimeter, recites the prayer of lustration, and sprinkles holy water.
[^4047]: Exemption for running water: Following ancient biblical and Byzantine sanitary jurisprudence (Lev. 11:36: "Nevertheless a fountain or pit, wherein there is plenty of water, shall be clean"), running or flowing springs (*вода̀ и҆сти́четъ и҆ внѣ̀ и҆де́тъ*) self-purify dynamically and are not rendered unclean by fallen vermin once the offending matter is carried away.
[^4048]: Contamination of liquid stores (wine and oil): Liquid commodities held in domestic vessels (pitchers, casks, amphorae) are subject to graduated remedies. Immediate removal of the foreign creature followed by a priestly blessing preserves the wine or oil for domestic use.
[^4049]: Putrefaction of liquid commodities: If vermin rots completely within the vessel, the defilement is deemed irremediable; the substance cannot be eaten or tasted (*недосто́итъ въкоуси́ти е҆̀*) and must be poured out onto the earth, though certain Nomocanons permit tainted oil to be burned in church lamps or used for leather-dressing rather than food.
[^4050]: Infestation of dry grain stores: Introducing canonical hygiene for granaries where mice, beetles, or other vermin (*га́дъ*) contaminate stored wheat.
[^4051]: Prohibition against using cleansed grain for liturgical prosphora: While thoroughly washed and sanctified wheat may be consumed by the faithful for daily nourishment, it is permanently disqualified from being offered at the Proskomide for Eucharistic loaves (*про́сфꙋры ѿ тоа̀ пшени́ца да не тво́рѧтъ*). The Liturgy demands unblemished physical and ritual purity for the Bread of Oblation (cf. Sluzhebnik rubrics: *Учительное известие*).
[^4052]: Medieval canonical boundaries regarding Jewish food handling: In conformity with Byzantine Nomocanons (cf. Apostolic Canon 70; Council in Trullo, Canon 11, barring Christians from eating unleavened bread with Jews or accepting medical care from them), popular Ruthenian canon law required priestly blessing (*ѡ҆ст҃и́тъ*) before Christians consumed liquids handled directly by non-Christians, thereby asserting ecclesiastical jurisdiction and safeguarding community cohesion.
[^4053]: Canonical prohibition of table fellowship with heretics: Reaffirming the ancient conciliar strictures against commensality with heterodox groups (Council of Laodicea, Canons 32–33, 37; Apostolic Canons 45, 64; Council in Trullo, Canon 11). The text explicitly singles out Armenians (*а҆рмени́нъ*, non-Chalcedonian Miaphysites) and Paulicians (*павли́кїа́нъ*, dualist Bogomil/Manichaean dissidents influential in the Balkans and Eastern Europe), demanding repentance and liturgical reconciliation.
[^4054]: Graduated pastoral remedies for communion with schismatics or heretics: Distinguishing between actions done unwittingly (*невѣде́нїемъ*), which require only priestly absolution and lustration, and willful disobedience after formal pastoral admonition (*наказа́нїе*).
[^4055]: Social and liturgical ostracism of recalcitrant dissenters: A Christian who persistently refuses to sever table fellowship with condemned heretics is treated as a pagan idolater (*ꙗ҆́ко ѿ і҆долослꙋжи́телѧ*), suffering complete exclusion from the Eucharistic assembly and civil-ecclesiastical boycott (cf. Matt. 18:17; 1 Cor. 5:11; 2 John 1:10–11).
[^4056]: Re-admission through three-year penance and rejection of liturgical offerings: The penitential path requires three years of excommunication. For the first two years, his family's prosphora and liturgical oblations are rejected at the altar (*да не прїа́то бꙋ́детъ ѿ до́мꙋ е҆го ни про́сфоуры*), reflecting the ancient canonical station of the *consistentes* / *bystanders* or *mourners*, before being gradually restored in the third year.
[^4057]: Apostasy into Judaism (*причасти́тсѧ, и҆ съ ни́мъ є҆диномꙋ́дрьствꙋетъ въ вѣ́рѣ и҆́хъ*): Dealing with baptized Christians who apostatize to Judaism or adopt Judaizing theological tenets (a prominent pastoral concern in Rus' and Ruthenian history, particularly following the late 15th-century Novgorod-Moscow Judaizing controversies).
[^4058]: Nine-year penitential progression for apostasy: Reflecting ancient canons on the reconciliation of lapsi and apostates (cf. Council of Nicaea, Canons 11–12; Council of Ancyra, Canons 4–9; St. Basil the Great, Canon 73). The penitent spends three years in the narthex among the catechumens (*въ мѣ́сто ѻ҆глаше́нныхъ*), followed by six years in the nave among the bystanders before being admitted to Holy Communion in the ninth year.
[^4059]: Intermarriage with heterodox spouses (*на и҆новѣ́рныхъ*): In Byzantine-Slavic canon law (Council in Trullo, Canon 72; Council of Laodicea, Canons 10, 31), marriages between Orthodox Christians and heretics or infidels are strictly invalid and forbidden unless the non-Orthodox partner converts to Orthodoxy.
[^4060]: Clerical sanctions for performing illicit mixed marriages: Priests who unlawfully celebrate matrimonial crownings (*вѣнча́вшїи ѧ҆̀*) between Orthodox and non-Orthodox parties incur severe disciplinary suspension and excommunication for three years outside the church (*внѣ̀ цр҃кве*).
[^4061]: *Житовле́къ* ("grain-caster" / sorcerer): A specialized Early Modern Slavic term designating practitioners of agrarian folk magic, divination, or sorcery who cast grain or bewitched crops to manipulate harvest yields, weather, or human affections (derived from *жито*, grain/rye, and *влещи*, to drag or draw). The Nomocanon categorizes such folk occult practices as diabolical sorcery (*дїа́воле дѣ́ло*).
[^4062]: Agrarian sorcery and bewitchment (*очи́бомъ*): Dialectal/archaic Ruthenian *очибъ* / *очибокъ* denotes the "evil eye", fascination, or bewitchment through glances or incantations. Practicing or consulting such sorcery carries four years of penance and one hundred prostrations daily upon renunciation of the sin (cf. Council of Ancyra, Canon 24; Council of Laodicea, Canon 36; St. Basil the Great, Canons 65, 72).
[^4063]: Violation of betrothal (*ѡ҆брꙋче́нїе*): In Byzantine and Ruthenian canon law (Council in Trullo, Canon 98; Council of Ancyra, Canon 11), a formal ecclesiastical betrothal possesses canonical force equivalent to marriage; seducing or abducting a betrothed maiden constitutes adultery and abduction (*въсхыще́нїе*).
[^4064]: Restoration of the betrothed maiden: If the seducer attempts to legitimize his crime by marrying the woman, canon law forbids it (*то̀ не подоба́етъ*), preserving the prior right of the original betrothed husband to take her to wife if he freely chooses to forgive the incident.
[^4065]: *Ѡ па́мѧти ѹ҆ме́ршихъ* ("Concerning the Memorial of the Departed"): Rubric introducing the canonical schedule for commemorative Divine Liturgies, panakhidas, and kollyva offerings on behalf of the deceased.
[^4066]: Traditional cycle of liturgical commemorations for the dead: The Ruthenian Trebnyk codifies the ancient Byzantine memorial intervals: (1) 3rd day (*трети́ны*), in honor of Christ's Resurrection on the third day; (2) 9th day (*девѧти́ны*), invoking the nine ranks of angels to aid the departed soul; (3) 20th day (*двадесѧти́ны*), a traditional Slavic half-way station; (4) 40th day (*четыридесѧти́ны*), corresponding to Christ's Ascension after forty days; (5) half-year (*ше́сть мц҃ь*); and (6) annual anniversary (*лѣ́то и҆сплъ́нено*). It additionally prescribes the universal Soul Saturdays: Meatfare Saturday (*соубо́та мѧсопоу́стнаѧ*), Cheesefare Saturday (*соубо́та сы́рнаѧ*), and the Saturday of the Fathers before Pentecost (the seventh Saturday after Pascha, *соубо́та се́дмаѧ по па́сцѣ*).
[^4067]: Theological efficacy of commemorative prayers and oblations: The Church teaches that the Eucharistic sacrifice (*жръ́твы*) and liturgical intercessions of the faithful on behalf of departed Christians bring refreshment, forgiveness, and absolution of sins through divine mercy (cf. St. John Chrysostom, *Homily 41 on 1 Corinthians*; St. John of Damascus, *On the Virtues and Vices / On Those Who Have Fallen Asleep in Faith*).
[^4068]: Wilful neglect of public worship: The canon targets chronic absenteeism where Christians willingly detach themselves from church prayer and psalmody (*оу҆странѧ́ютсѧ са́ми ѿ свое́ѧ во́лѧ*) to live in brute neglect like beasts (*хо́дѧтъ ꙗ҆́коже ско́ти*), imposing one year of excommunication and one hundred metanias (cf. Apostolic Canon 80; Council in Trullo, Canon 80, excommunicating those absent from church for three consecutive Sundays without necessity).
[^4069]: Canonical prohibition of funeral rites and memorial services for suicides: In accordance with Canon 14 of Timothy of Alexandria, one who intentionally takes his own life (*во́лею свое́ю паде́тсѧ и҆ оу҆бїе́тъ себѐ*) is denied ecclesiastical burial, funeral psalmody (*нѝ да̀ пою́тъ*), and memorial services (*нѝ да̀ тво́рѧтъ е҆мꙋ̀ па́мѧть нико́гдаже*). The rubric carefully distinguishes deliberate suicide from accidental death (*не во́лею свое́ю... но оу҆бїе́тсѧ, и҆ оу҆мре́тъ*), for which full liturgical burial and memorials are granted.
[^4070]: Denial of Christian burial to criminals slain in the act of sin: Persons slain while actively committing mortal offenses—adultery, fornication, theft, or armed brigandage (*разбо́йничество*)—are excluded from church burial and remembrance, as they died unrepentant in the commission of violent or illicit deeds (cf. Nomocanon of the Great Euchologion, chapter 178).
[^4071]: Reiteration of canonical age of matrimonial majority and parental obligation: In accordance with Byzantine civil-ecclesiastical law (Ecloga II.1.1; Procheiron I.1; Nomocanon in 14 Titles), fifteen years for males and twelve years for females mark the legal boundary of pubertal maturity and matrimonial capacity.
[^4072]: Parental culpability for adult children's sins: Byzantine-Slavic nomocanons place direct moral and spiritual liability upon negligent parents who delay arranging lawful marriages for eligible adult children, rendering them spiritually responsible if an unmarried child falls into fornication (*се́и грѣ́хъ на роди́телехъ е҆́стъ*). Conversely, if premature sexual misconduct occurs before canonical age, personal guilt falls strictly on the offender.
[^4073]: Canonical severity against arson (*за́жжетъ*): Arson of residences, homesteads (*дво́ръ*), or agricultural threshing floors (*гꙋмно̀*) was regarded in both Roman-Byzantine and Slavic law as an egregious crime of malicious destruction and endangerment of community survival, warranting twenty years of penance upon confession (cf. Syntagma of Matthew Blastares, letter P, chapter 2).
[^4074]: Mitigating penance in cases of retaliatory vengeance or severe civil retribution: If the arsonist suffers violent retribution or total confiscation of property from the victims before judicial ecclesiastical penance is assigned, the spiritual father is directed to remit half the canon (ten years rather than twenty, with two hundred prostrations daily instead of four hundred), recognizing that temporal suffering already partially satisfied retributive justice.
[^4075]: Standard penance for arson: Under the full unmitigated canon of twenty years, the penitent is assigned four hundred daily prostrations (*покло́новъ*) alongside fasting and exclusion from Holy Communion.
[^4076]: Involuntary or accidental arson (*не хотѧ̀*): Where fire spread by accident or without malicious intent, the canon halves the term to ten years of penitence.
[^4077]: Digamy in advanced age (*вто́рое посага́нїе твори́тъ лѣ́ты сы́ мнѡ́гыми*): Byzantine canon law discouraged digamy, especially among the elderly for whom marriage could no longer serve procreative purposes, imposing two years of penance (cf. St. Basil the Great, Canon 4; Council of Neocaesarea, Canon 3).
[^4078]: Pastoral concession to young widowers: In accordance with St. Paul's pastoral instruction in 1 Corinthians 7:8-9 ("It is good for them if they abide even as I; but if they cannot contain, let them marry"), the canon permits young widowers to remarry without severe stigma, while praising voluntary continence.
[^4079]: Canonical censure of elderly digamy: While young widowers are permitted second marriage for the avoidance of passion, an elderly man remarrying without necessity was viewed canonically as yielding to intemperance.
[^4080]: Penance for elderly digamist: Three years of excommunication and twenty-four daily prostrations.
[^4081]: Trigamy (*тре́тїе поса́гнетъ*): Third marriage is characterized canonically as "moderate fornication" (*блꙋ́дъ бо нариче́тсѧ*), incurring four years of exclusion and sixty-six daily prostrations (cf. St. Basil the Great, Canon 4; St. Gregory of Nazianzus, *Oration 37*).
[^4082]: Tetragamy (*четве́ртое*): Fourth marriage was strictly prohibited in Byzantine-Slavic canon law, branded as bestial promiscuity following the famous "Tome of Union" (AD 920) under Patriarch Nicholas Mystikos after Emperor Leo VI's fourth marriage, requiring eight years of excommunication and one hundred daily prostrations.
[^4083]: Canon on habitual fornication (*блꙋ́дникъ*): Assigns twelve years of excommunication (cf. St. Basil the Great, Canon 59).
[^4084]: Penance for self-pollution (*малакі́ю творѧ́и*): Church Slavonic *малакі́а* (Greek μαλακία) denotes onanism / self-pollution, penalized by three years of penance and thirty prostrations (cf. John the Faster, Canon 13).
[^4085]: Canon on unnatural vice between men (*мꙋжело́жникъ*): Slavic Nomocanon assigns five years of penance and thirty-six prostrations (mitigated according to the penitential of John the Faster, contrasting with the stricter Basilian canon of fifteen years).
[^4086]: Unnatural carnal acts within heterosexual unions (*не в̾ потре́бное е҆стество̀, но въ а҆федро́нъ*): Church Slavonic *а҆федро́нъ* (Greek ἀφεδρών, latrine / posterior) designates unnatural sodomitic intercourse within or outside marriage, condemned under Canon 18 of John the Faster with four years of penance and one hundred and fifty daily prostrations.
[^4087]: Aggravation of penance for habitual or multiple offenses: The spiritual father must adjust canonical sanctions proportionally based on frequency and number of partners.
[^4088]: Canonical principle of differential guilt and moral proportionality: Classical Byzantine casuistry (elaborated in the Nomocanons of John the Faster and Matthew Blastares) requires confessor priests to weigh circumstantial severity—distinguishing isolated lapses from multiple partnerships.
[^4089]: Bestial sin with inedible beasts: Differentiates carnal sin involving inedible working beasts (horses, donkeys, dogs) from cattle, prescribing three years for isolated offenses and ten to twelve years for chronic recidivism.
[^4090]: Stricter penalty for bestiality with edible livestock: Bestiality with livestock raised for human consumption incurred fifteen years of penance (cf. St. Basil the Great, Canons 60 and 63; Council of Ancyra, Canons 16-17).
[^4091]: Destruction of defiled edible livestock: In accordance with Mosaic law (Leviticus 20:15-16) and Byzantine canonical jurisprudence (Syntagma of Blastares, letter K, chapter 18), any livestock involved in bestial pollution was slain and cast to scavenging animals, strictly forbidden from the Christian table or market.
[^4092]: Specific penance for bestial sin with a dog: Nine years of penance and one hundred and sixty prostrations.
[^4093]: Adultery with a married woman (*съ моужа́тою жено́ю*): Six years of penance with five hundred daily prostrations (a pastoral mitigation of Basil Canon 58's fifteen-year excommunication).
[^4094]: Seduction without subsequent marriage (*съ дѣ́цею, и҆ не по́иметъ ю҆̀ въ женоу̀*): Church Slavonic *дѣ́ца* (from *дѣвица*, maiden/virgin) denotes an unmarried maiden. If the seducer refuses marriage, he incurs nine years of penance, three hundred prostrations, and civil-ecclesiastical forfeiture of assets for her dowry (cf. Exodus 22:16-17; Byzantine Ecloga XVII.23).
[^4095]: Violation of a betrothed woman (*ѡ҆броуче́на и҆номоу моу́жоу*): Canonically treated on par with adultery (cf. Deuteronomy 22:23-24). In addition to spiritual penance, a civil fine of twenty-four *zlatytsi* (Byzantine gold solidi / nomismata) is levied for the Great Church (*велицѣ́и цр҃кви*).
[^4096]: Secret violation of a maiden: Where the offense remains concealed from public civil tribunals, secret confession requires a heightened voluntary penance of twelve years.
[^4097]: Pastoral discretion of the confessor (*по разꙋже́нїи ѿца̀ дх҃овнаго*): The spiritual father evaluates sincerity and circumstances to temper the canonical tariff of three hundred prostrations.
[^4098]: Canonical jurisprudence on rape and absence of consent: Grounded directly in Deuteronomy 22:25-27, Byzantine canon law absolves a victim violated in isolation where no help was available to hear her cries. The confessor imposes only a nominal cleansing penance based on ecclesial rank (cf. St. Basil the Great, Canon 49; St. Gregory of Nyssa, Canon 3).
[^4099]: Evidentiary test of non-consent in populated areas: In accordance with Deuteronomy 22:23-24 and Byzantine civil-ecclesiastical codes (Ecloga XVII.24; Procheiron XXXIX.27), if a woman suffered violation within a populated town or dwelling without crying out for assistance, canon law presumes tacit complicity or failure of resistance.
[^4100]: Imposition of halved penance for contested duress: Where a woman claims duress in an inhabited area without corroborating outcries, canon law assesses mitigated liability by imposing half the regular canonical penance for voluntary fornication.
[^4101]: Confessor's reference to the written Nomocanon: Explicit reference to the canonical codex (*пи́сано въ кни́зѣ се́и тако́вое пра́вило*), enjoining confessors to consult standard penitential texts rather than inventing arbitrary penalties.
[^4102]: Sacrilegious fornication with a nun (*съ чрънори́зицею*): Violation of a consecrated monastic incurs nine years of penance and one hundred and fifty prostrations, viewed as sacrilege against Christ's bride (cf. Council of Chalcedon, Canon 16; St. Basil the Great, Canon 6).
[^4103]: Distribution of guilt in consensual relations with a nun: If the nun willingly consented, greater responsibility falls on her as a consecrated monastic (nine years of excommunication) than on the layman (five years).
[^4104]: Seduction and secularization of a nun: Serious crime of inducing a nun to abandon her monastic habit and contract a worldly marriage.
[^4105]: Lifelong excommunication for apostasy from the monastic state: Stripping a monastic of her angelic habit (*ѻ҆́бразъ и҆́ноческыи*) to enter into secular marriage incurs total exclusion from the Holy Mysteries until the hour of death (cf. Council in Trullo, Canon 44).
[^4106]: Mitigation upon restoration to monastic life: If the apostate marriage is dissolved and the nun is returned to her cloister, the layman's penance is reduced to nine years.
[^4107]: Aggravated penalty for violation of a nun of the Great Schema (*вели́каго ѻ҆́браза*): The Great and Angelic Schema (*megaloschemos*) entails irrevocable solemn vows, carrying twelve years and one thousand prostrations for the nun, and nine years and three hundred prostrations for the man.
[^4108]: Spiritual incest with a baptismal co-sponsor (*съ кꙋмо́ю свое́ю*): Canon 53 of the Council in Trullo established spiritual relationship (*cognatio spiritualis*) established at baptism as superior to bodily kinship, punishing carnal relations between a child's father and godmother (*kuma*) as spiritual incest with nine years of excommunication.
[^4109]: Degrees of spiritual affinity: Extension of matrimonial and carnal impediments to siblings of baptismal sponsors.
[^4110]: Penance for carnal sin with a godparent's sister: Four years of excommunication and seventy prostrations.
[^4111]: Direct spiritual incest with a goddaughter (*съ дще́рею свое́ю кр҃щеною*): Spiritual fatherhood contracted through baptism creates a primary impediment; violation of one's own goddaughter is penalized by twelve years of excommunication and three hundred prostrations.
[^4112]: Sins within the circle of spiritual brotherhood: Carnal relations with the mother of one's sworn or spiritual brother (*бра́та свое́го дх҃овнаго*) incur nine years of penance.
[^4113]: Carnal relations with a spiritual brother's daughter: Five years of penance and one hundred prostrations.
[^4114]: Carnal relations with a spiritual brother's wife: Six years of penance and one hundred prostrations.
[^4115]: Carnal relations with a spiritual brother's sister: Four years of penance and thirty-six prostrations.
[^4116]: Affinal incest with a mother-in-law or sister-in-law (*съ те́щею, и҆ли съ све́стїю*): Intercourse with relatives by marriage incurs five years of penance and sixty-six prostrations (cf. Leviticus 18:8-17; Council of Neo-Caesarea, Canon 2).
[^4117]: Double affinity (*съ двѣма̀ сестра́ма* / *два̀ бра́та съ є҆дино́ю жено́ю*): Carnal conjunction of one man with two sisters, or two brothers with one woman, violates the fundamental canonical prohibition of shared affinity in the second degree, requiring five years of excommunication and one hundred and fifty prostrations.
[^4118]: Impediment between children of godparents (*коумо́вныи дѣ́ти*): In Byzantine-Slavic canon law, the children of a godparent and the children of the baptized person were barred from intermarriage down to the third and fourth degrees of spiritual kinship. Any illicit carnal union between them is dissolved and punished by five years of penance (cf. Edict of Emperor Leo VI; Blastares, Syntagma, letter G, chapter 8).
[^4119]: Prohibition of intermarriage between children of sworn spiritual brothers: Extends the impediment of compaternity to the offspring of men joined in liturgical brotherhood (*adelphopoiesis*).
[^4120]: Sanctions for violence between spiritual brothers: Because liturgical brotherhood was sworn before God on the Gospel, hatred, quarreling, or physical violence between sworn brothers was treated as sacrilegious perjury, incurring sixty days of immediate excommunication and twelve daily prostrations.
[^4121]: Perpetual excommunication for unforgiving spiritual brothers: Unreconciled malice between sworn brothers brings permanent expulsion from the church (*вы́ноу* = continually/perpetually), as they have broken their solemn oath sworn to Christ and grieved the Spirit of love.
[^4122]: Canon of liturgical sworn brotherhood (*adelphopoiesis* / *братотворе́нїе*): Authentic definition of a spiritual brother (*дх҃о́вный бра́тъ*) in the Ruthenian Church: one who made a solemn covenant of brotherhood over the Holy Gospel during the liturgical rite of *bratotvorenie* (a recognized office in the Euchologion / Trebnyk, distinct from natural brotherhood).
[^4123]: Pastoral merit of taking the initiative in reconciliation (*предвари́въ є҆ди́нъ ѿ ни́хъ*): Drawing upon Christ's teaching in Matthew 5:23-24, the Nomocanon promises a twofold divine reward (*соугоу́боу*) to the party who humbles himself first by offering a metania (*покла́нїе*) to solicit peace.
[^4124]: Rejection of offerings from the unyielding and rancorous: An unyielding Christian who refuses reconciliation is barred from communion and from offering liturgical bread (*просфоура̀*), fulfilling the Patristic principle that God rejects gifts offered with a rancorous heart (cf. St. Basil the Great, *Moralia*; Council of Carthage, Canon 90).
[^4125]: Mutual excommunication for reciprocal malice: If both parties persist in bitterness, both are excluded from ecclesiastical fellowship until genuine forgiveness from the heart (*ѿ въсего ср҃ца*) is restored.
[^4126]: Censure of concealed rancor and silent malice: The canon explicitly targets silent enmity and smoldering grudge-bearing (*млꙋча́ще дрꙋ́гъ дрꙋ́га ненави́дѧтъ*), requiring public or pastoral mediation before two or three witnesses (continued on fol. 663 / leaf 1341).
[^4127]: Examination and ecclesiastical reconciliation before witnesses: Drawing upon Matthew 18:15-17 ("take with thee one or two more, that in the mouth of two or three witnesses every word may be established... and if he neglect to hear them, tell it unto the church"), the canon requires contentious brethren—even natural brothers by blood (*по пло́ти бра́тїа*)—to submit their dispute to formal ecclesiastical examination (*пред̾ цр҃ко́вїю и҆стѧза́тисѧ*). Obstinate refusal to reconcile incurs formal excommunication.
[^4128]: Canon against rancor and grudge-bearing (*памѧтозло́бникъ*): Church Slavonic *памѧтозло́бникъ* translates Greek μνησίкаκος (one who harbors resentment or remembers evil). The Nomocanon penalizes unresolved malice with one year of exclusion from communion and twelve daily prostrations (cf. St. John Climacus, *Ladder of Divine Ascent*, Step 9).
[^4129]: Penance for slander and calumny (*клеве́тникъ*): Two years of exclusion and eighteen daily prostrations (cf. Apostolic Canon 84; Council in Trullo, Canon 87).
[^4130]: Perjury and false testimony (*лъжесвѣди́тель*): False witness in civil or ecclesiastical matters incurs three years of excommunication and twenty-four prostrations, reflecting the severe Scriptural prohibition (Exodus 20:16; Proverbs 19:5).
[^4131]: Censure of revilers and scoffers (*роуга́тель*): Verbal reviling or blasphemous mockery incurs one year of penance and twelve daily prostrations (cf. 1 Corinthians 6:10).
[^4132]: Penalty for eavesdropping and tale-bearing (*припослоу́шникъ*): Church Slavonic *припослоу́шникъ* (Greek ἐπήκοος / ψιθυριστής) designates a secret listener, eavesdropper, or tale-bearer who breeds discord by spying on conversations, penalized by one year of repentance and fifteen prostrations.
[^4133]: Standard canonical penance for perjury (*клѧтвопрестꙋ́пникъ*): Three years of excommunication and one hundred and fifty prostrations (a pastoral mitigation from the stricter Basilian canon of ten to eleven years; cf. St. Basil the Great, Canon 64).
[^4134]: Syntactical enjambment across leaves p1341 and p1342: The opening aphorism condemning the nature of oath-breaking begins at the foot of fol. 663r (*клѧтво-*) and concludes on fol. 663v (*престꙋ́пникъ подо́бенъ е҆́стъ е҆ретикоу̀*).
[^4135]: Oath-breaking equated with heresy: The Nomocanon equates a perjurer (*клѧтвопрестꙋ́пникъ*) with a heretic (*подо́бенъ е҆́стъ е҆ретикоу̀*), because an individual who takes God's Name in vain to confirm a falsehood effectively denies God's truth and sovereignty.
[^4136]: Agrarian crime of boundary displacement (*преѡра́ѧй ни́воу ра́ломъ*): Moving or ploughing over an established boundary mark to encroach upon a neighbor's field was an ancient crime denounced in Mosaic law (Deuteronomy 19:14, 27:17: "Cursed be he that removes his neighbour's landmark") and Byzantine agrarian legislation (Nomos Georgikos / Farmer's Law). The canon imposes four years of penance and sixteen prostrations.
[^4137]: Burglary and house robbery (*крады́й до́мы*): Breaking into and stealing from inhabited dwellings incurs three years of penance and one hundred prostrations (cf. St. Basil the Great, Canon 61; St. Gregory of Nyssa, Canon 6).
[^4138]: Canonical penance for lying (*лъжа́й*): Habitual mendacity incurs six months of exclusion from the Holy Mysteries and twelve daily prostrations.
[^4139]: Cattle rustling (*крады́й скѡ́ты*): Theft of livestock carries a heavy penalty of five years of excommunication and three hundred prostrations, reflecting the severe economic disruption caused to rural agrarian communities.
[^4140]: Minor theft of domestic poultry and eggs (*крады́й кѡ́коши, и҆ли ꙗ҆и́ца*): Theft of small domestic animals (hens or eggs) is treated as petty larceny, requiring one year of penance and thirty-six prostrations alongside restitution.
[^4141]: Pilfering from orchards (*крады́й ѡ҆во́щїа*): Stealing fruit from trees or gardens is penalized with six months of penance and fifteen prostrations (cf. Byzantine Farmer's Law §59-62).
[^4142]: Violent extortion and forcible seizure of property (*въсхыща́ѧй и҆ ѿе́млѧй чоужда́ѧ*): Church Slavonic *въсхыща́ѧй* renders Greek ἁρπάζων (rapacity / predatory theft). Forcible deprivation of another's goods incurs five years of penance and one hundred prostrations (cf. St. Basil the Great, Canon 61; Council in Trullo, Canon 61).
[^4143]: Judicial parity between malicious informers and thieves (*о҆ба́дникъ и҆ та́ть*): Church Slavonic *о҆ба́дникъ* denotes a malicious accuser, informer, or extortionate delator (Greek συκοφάντης / διάβολος). Canon law places the calumniator on the exact same moral and judicial level (*є҆ди́нъ сꙋ́дъ прїи́мꙋтъ*) as a common thief.
[^4144]: Complicity in fencing stolen goods: Syntactical continuation onto leaf p1343 detailing canonical liability for harboring thieves and trafficking in stolen property.
[^4145]: Equal guilt of fences and receivers of stolen property: The person who knowingly receives or purchases stolen goods (*прїе́млѧй та́тѧ и҆ кра́домая ѿ ни́хъ*) is held to identical canonical liability as the primary thief (*та́кожде да пока́етсѧ ꙗ҆́коже и҆ та́ть*).
[^4146]: Sacrilegious crime of grave-robbing (*крады́й грѡ́бы*): Tomb desecration and grave robbery (Greek τυμβωρυχία) incurs five years of penance and five hundred prostrations (cf. St. Basil the Great, Canon 66; St. Gregory of Nyssa, Canon 7).
[^4147]: Usury and extortion (*лихои́мецъ и҆ сребролю́бецъ*): The taking of usurious interest or unrighteous financial gain is repeatedly condemned in Orthodox canon law (Apostolic Canon 44; First Ecumenical Council, Canon 17; Council in Trullo, Canon 10), prescribing three years of penance and four hundred prostrations.
[^4148]: Commercial fraud through adulterating wine with water (*влива́ѧй во́дꙋ въ вино̀*): Diluting wine for retail sale defrauds the consumer and violates merchant integrity (cf. Isaiah 1:22: "thy wine is mixed with water; thy merchants mix wine with water"). The Nomocanon penalizes this commercial deceit with five years of penance and sixty-six prostrations.
[^4149]: Suspension of habitual drunkards from the Holy Mysteries: Consistent with Apostolic Canons 42 and 43, habitual inebriation (*пїѧ́ница*) bars an individual from receiving Holy Communion until active sobriety and repentance are verified.
[^4150]: Canonical penalty for post-inebriation vomiting: A layman who becomes intoxicated unto vomiting incurs sixty days of immediate excommunication.
[^4151]: Desecration of the Holy Mysteries through post-communion vomiting: Syntactical continuation onto leaf p1344 outlining the severe canonical penalty for vomiting after receiving Holy Communion, barring the individual through multiple feast-day cycles.
[^4152]: Multi-month Eucharistic exclusion for communion profanation during Great Lent: If an individual vomits the Holy Mysteries during Great Lent through negligence or intemperance, he is barred from communion throughout the entire liturgical year for up to two hundred days (*до двѣ́стѣ дні́и*), performing twelve daily prostrations.
[^4153]: Total fast before Holy Communion: Breaking the Eucharistic fast by consuming any food or drink whatsoever prior to communion incurs one year of penance (cf. Council of Carthage, Canon 41; Council in Trullo, Canon 29).
[^4154]: Heightened penance for violating Eucharistic fast with dairy or eggs: Ingesting animal products (cheese and eggs) prior to communion escalates the penance to two years of exclusion.
[^4155]: Severe penalty for consuming meat before Holy Communion: Consuming flesh meat before communion carries three years of exclusion and sixty-six prostrations, reflecting the grave contempt shown for the Eucharistic Body and Blood.
[^4156]: Sacrilegious defiance of spiritual father's suspension: An individual who communicates while bound under an active canonical suspension without his confessor's blessing is condemned as a "scoffer of God" (*роуга́тель Бж҃їи*), incurring three additional years of excommunication and total rejection of his liturgical offerings (*ни про́сфꙋры да прїе́млетъ ѿ него̀ іере́й*).
[^4157]: Witchcraft and sorcery (*жена̀ влъхвоу́ющи*): Syntactical transition onto leaf p1345 introducing the special canonical section governing offenses committed by women (*Ѡ҆ жєна́хъ*), beginning with the condemnation of divination and sorcery.
[^4158]: Total expulsion from the Christian community for unrepentant sorcery: Failure to desist from witchcraft, divination, or demonic enchantments incurs complete excision from the Christian commonwealth (*ѿлꙋчи́тсѧ ѿ хр҃тїа́ньства*; cf. Apostolic Canon 61; Council of Ancyra, Canon 24; Council in Trullo, Canon 61).
[^4159]: Abortifacient potions and contraception (*бы́лїе е҆́же растли́ти пло́дъ чре́ва*): Drinking herb concoctions (*бы́лїе*) to destroy the fetus in the womb or induce sterility is classified canonically as voluntary homicide, penalized by five years of excommunication and three hundred prostrations (a pastoral adaptation of St. Basil the Great, Canon 2, and Council in Trullo, Canon 91).
[^4160]: Heightened guilt and rejection of offerings in marital abortion: Procuring an abortion within wedlock is judged an aggravated sin (*па́че злѣ́йше е҆́стъ*); the church rejects all oblations and prosphora from the woman until repentance, and imposes identical excommunication upon the husband if he consented to or counseled the destruction of their offspring.
[^4161]: Seduction and mariticide through sorcery: Syntactical transition onto leaf p1346 addressing the case of a married woman who destroys her husband through magical potions or charms to marry an adulterous paramour.
[^4162]: Equating magical mariticide with premeditated murder: If the adultery and poisoning of the husband are consummated, both the woman and her paramour are sentenced to fifteen years of penance, subject to the full rigors of the canons on intentional homicide (*ѹ҆бїи́ца во́лныи*; cf. St. Basil the Great, Canons 8 and 56).
[^4163]: Betrayal of a husband to murder (Delilah typology): Invoking the biblical archetype of Delilah who betrayed Samson to his enemies (Judges 16), a wife who betrays her husband unto violent death incurs lifelong canonical excommunication (*въ всѧ дни живота̀ свое́го да запрѣти́тсѧ*).
[^4164]: Neonaticide and postpartum infant murder (*своѧ̀ ѡ҆троча́та ѹ҆бива́етъ на рожде́нїи*): Murdering an infant at birth through demonic promptings (*навѣ́томъ вра́жїимъ*) results in lifelong penitential exclusion (mitigated only on the deathbed; cf. Council of Ancyra, Canon 21).
[^4165]: Abortion and infant suffocation in fornication: A woman who conceives out of wedlock and drinks abortifacient herbs or suffocates (*ѹ҆дави́ти*) her newborn to conceal her shame is sentenced to nine years of penance and one hundred and seven prostrations.
[^4166]: Pandering and procuration (*жена̀ мавли́стрїа, и҆ли мꙋ́жъ*): Syntactical transition onto leaf p1347. Church Slavonic *мавли́стрїа* (Greek μαυλίστρια) designates a bawd, procuress, or matchmaker of illicit liaisons.
[^4167]: Penance for lay pandering: Three years of excommunication for procuring or facilitating fornication for others.
[^4168]: Aggravated penalty for monastic procuration: If a monk or nun engages in procuration or facilitates illicit carnal liaisons, the penance increases to five years.
[^4169]: Confession of women restricted to the narthex (*въ притво́рѣ цр҃ко́внѣмъ*): To safeguard pastoral purity and prevent public scandal, female penitents must be heard exclusively in the church narthex (porch), with the outer doors kept open to public view (cf. Syntagma of Blastares, Nomocanonical tit. 9).
[^4170]: Monastic confessors restricted to chaste old age: Monks and virgins are prohibited from receiving women for confession; only monastics who have attained venerable and temperate old age (*до ста́рости цѣломꙋ́дрьныѧ*) may be deputed, acknowledging the spiritual warfare waged by the adversary through female temptation.
[^4171]: Pre-confessional psalmody (Psalm 50): Syntactical continuation onto leaf p1348 requiring the priest to fortify his mind through prayer and psalmody before hearing women's confessions.
[^4172]: Required recitation of Psalm 95 (LXX) before women's confessions: The priest recites Psalm 50 (*Miserere*) and Psalm 95:1 / 99:1 / 65:1 LXX ("Sing unto the Lord, all the earth") to establish prayerful vigilance prior to administering confession to women.
[^4173]: Penance for self-pollution (*малакі́а*): Reiteration of the standard three-year penance and twenty-four daily prostrations for masturbatory vice (cf. John the Faster, Canon 13).
[^4174]: Casuistic distinction between manual and femoral onanism: Distinguishes between manual masturbation (*роука́ма*) and femoral stimulation (*съ бе́дрома свои́ма*), evaluating the manual form as more spiritually corrupting while condemning both as wicked.
[^4175]: Homosexual vice among women (*же́ньскаа малакі́а*): Lesbian sexual behavior is explicitly identified and subjected to confessor interrogation, prescribing one year of penance (cf. Romans 1:26; St. Basil the Great, Canon 62; John the Faster, Canon 17).
[^4176]: Injunction to devout women; syntactical continuation across leaves p1348 and p1349: The admonition commanding modesty and penance for women concludes with the prostration tariff on the succeeding page.
[^4177]: Folio misnumbering in physical 1606 imprint: Leaf p1349 bears the printed folio number `х҃ѻ́є` (675) in the top right margin, which represents a typographical error by the 1606 Striatyn press for folio 667 (`х҃ѯз`), as confirmed by the continuous signature sequence (`з з з г҃` for `ѕ ѕ ѕ г҃`, sheet 3 of quire 66) and the adjacent folio 668 (`х҃ѯи`, leaf p1351).
[^4178]: Sudden onset of menstruation during liturgical worship: In accordance with the canonical epistle of St. Dionysius of Alexandria (Canon 2) and St. Timothy of Alexandria (Canon 7), a woman who discovers the sudden onset of her menses while present in church must immediately withdraw without guilt or condemnation.
[^4179]: Penalty for remaining in church or receiving communion during menstruation: If a woman remains out of human vanity or shame, or dares to approach the chalice or antidoron while physically unclean, she is barred from church and communion for six months, with fifty daily prostrations.
[^4180]: Prohibition on baking or handling prosphora during menses: A woman during her monthly flow is strictly forbidden from kneading or baking altar bread (*про́сфоуры*), or touching liturgical vessels.
[^4181]: Deathbed viaticum concession during menstrual impurity: In accordance with pastoral economia and Canon 13 of the First Ecumenical Council, bodily uncleanness is superseded by mortal peril; a woman facing imminent death must not be denied the Holy Viaticum.
[^4182]: Postpartum viaticum at the point of death: Syntactical transition onto leaf p1350 extending the deathbed viaticum concession to newly delivered mothers facing mortality before the completion of their forty-day purification.
[^4183]: Deathbed prayers and communion for parturient women: A woman who has given birth and faces imminent death receives the prayer of absolution and the Holy Mysteries immediately without awaiting the customary forty days.
[^4184]: Resumption of forty-day purification upon postpartum recovery: If the parturient woman recovers from mortal illness, she completes the forty-day postpartum period of purification in fasting and prayer, performing forty daily prostrations, and communicates on the fiftieth day.
[^4185]: Pastoral admonition to bishops: Introducing the section of canons governing the clergy (*Ѡ҆ і҆ере́ехъ за́повѣди*), instructing bishops to teach and correct all ranks of clergy and flock with meekness and humility rather than wrath or malice (cf. 2 Timothy 2:24-25; 1 Peter 5:2-3).
[^4186]: Deposition of brawling or violent clerics: Any priest who participates in brawls, strife, or physical battery (*на бїе́нїа кра́молы и҆де́тъ*), regardless of whether he claims righteous provocation, is subject to canonical deposition from holy orders (cf. Apostolic Canons 27 and 65; Council in Trullo, Canon 9).
[^4187]: Deposition of digamous priests with preservation of lay communion: A priest who contracts a second marriage after ordination is permanently deposed from celebrating the Divine Liturgy, but is permitted to receive Holy Communion as a layman without penance (cf. Apostolic Canon 17; St. Basil the Great, Canon 12).
[^4188]: Prohibition against celebrating Liturgy in coarse peasant slippers (*ѻ҆пи́нцехъ*): Church Slavonic *ѻ҆пи́нцехъ* (Ukrainian *опи́нці* / *постоли́*) refers to coarse raw-hide peasant moccasins or peasant footwear tied with thongs. The canon strictly prohibits priests from celebrating the Holy Mysteries in improper, soiled, or undignified peasant shoes, requiring clean and befitting liturgical footwear.
