# PART I: ORDER FOR THE NAMING OF A CHILD ON THE EIGHTH DAY

[Fol. 1r. Headpiece: woodcut engraving with a central oval vignette depicting the Crucifixion of Christ with the Holy Virgin Mary and Saint John the Theologian standing by the Cross, framed by Renaissance strapwork, acanthus foliage, winged cherubim, and pelicans.]

THE ORDER OF THE DEDICATION OF A CHILD[^31]

*Be it known that on the eighth day after birth, the newborn infant is brought by the midwife[^32] to the church, to receive a name.*
*And the priest makes prayer over him before the church doors. First he signs his forehead, mouth, and breast. And he says:* **Let us pray to the Lord.**
*Prayer to sign the child, and to name his name:*

*At the Dedication [of a Child]*

O Lord our God, unto Thee do we pray, and of Thee do we make supplication: let the light of Thy countenance be signed upon this Thy servant, [N.], and let the Cross of Thine Only-begotten Son be signed upon his heart and upon his thoughts, that he may flee from the vanity of the world, and follow after Thy commandments.[^33] And grant, O Lord, that Thy holy Name may remain unrenounced upon him, being united in due time unto Thy Holy Church, and perfected by the most pure and dread Mysteries of Thy Christ:[^34] that having lived according to Thy commandments, he may preserve the seal unharmed, and keep the robe undefiled, and obtain the blessedness of the elect in Thy Kingdom: by the grace and compassions and love for mankind of Thine Only-begotten Son, with Whom Thou art blessed, together with Thine All-holy, and good, and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.[^35]

*And so he dismisses them.*

*Prayer for the midwife after receiving the child:*[^36]

O Lord Jesus Christ our God, Who wast born before the ages of the Father without a mother, and in the latter years wast sent to be born on earth without a father, having come forth from an unwedded Mother; Who didst receive gifts from the magi, glorification from the shepherds, singing from the angels; how [Thou didst accept] wrapping in swaddling clothes, and being laid in a manger of brute beasts:

laid in a manger of brute beasts: do Thou now also come by Thy visitation, and sanctify this Thy servant who hath been the midwife that received the infant; and save her who hath come unto Thy holy church, granting purification of soul and body, through the prayers and supplications of our most pure Lady, the Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary, and of all the saints. For Thou art He that blesses and sanctifies all things, O Christ our God; and so bless the soul of Thy servant, [N.], [saying the name,] and unto Thee do we send up glory, with the Father, and the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*Order on the third day for a woman after giving birth to a child:*[^37]

*The priest says:*
**Priest:** Blessed is our God...
*And we [say] the Trisagion; O Most Holy Trinity; after [which:* Our Father...*]*

**Priest:** For Thine is the kingdom...

*Troparion, Tone 4:*[^38]
Thy Nativity, O Virgin Theotokos, has announced joy unto all the universe; for from Thee has shone forth the Sun of Righteousness, Christ our God; and having loosed the curse, He has given blessing, and having abolished death, He has granted us life eternal.

*Kontakion, Tone 4:*
Joachim and Anna were freed from the reproach of childlessness, and Adam and Eve from the corruption of death, O Most Pure, by Thy holy Nativity. This do Thy people celebrate, being delivered from the guilt of transgressions, crying out unto Thee: The barren woman gives birth to the Theotokos and the nourisher of our Life.

*Then the prayer:*

*Let us pray to the Lord.*

O Lord our God, Who from the dust didst fashion man, and from him didst form woman, and didst honor her with equal dignity; and when they transgressed Thy commandment, Thou didst cast [them] out of Paradise into this laborious life, and didst say unto the man: In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread; and unto the woman: In sorrow shalt thou bring forth children:[^39] Who Thyself didst deign sinlessly to be born of a woman without sin for the salvation of our race: do Thou now also, we pray Thy tender mercy, sanctify this Thy servant, [N.], [saying the name,] who has given birth to an infant; and cleanse her bodily defilement and spiritual passion, and make her worthy to keep her house purely and blamelessly; and cleanse every doubt from her soul.[^40] For Thou art He that blesses and sanctifies all things, O Christ our God, and unto Thee do we send up glory, with the Father, and the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*Second Prayer, to the Same [Woman]. Let us pray to the Lord.*

Unto Thee, O God and Creator of all creation, Who didst make the entrances and goings out of man, fleeing for refuge do we make supplication: and upon this Thy servant, [N.], [saying the name,] let Thy holy Name be signed, and that of Thine Only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus Christ. Deliver her from all uncleanness, and from the cohabitation of the devil, and from every manner of defilement;[^41] and make her a chosen vessel, that she may be unto Thee a pure handmaid; and let her be signed with the power of Thy precious and life-creating Cross from this present day and hour, and be sanctified with the seal of Thy Christ: with Whom Thou art blessed, together with Thine All-holy, and good, and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

*Third Prayer, to the Same [Woman], and for the Women Who Were Present at That Childbirth.*[^42]
*Let us pray to the Lord.*

O Master, Lord Jesus Christ, Word of God and of the Father, Only-begotten Son: Who for our salvation, by the good pleasure of Thine Unoriginate Father, and the cooperation of Thine consubstantial and co-eternal Spirit, having descended at the end of the ages into the womb of the Holy Virgin, and having become incarnate and been born ineffably and unutterably as Thou Thyself alone knowest, having become perfect man, that Thou mightest deliver us from the enemy's tyranny; and having abolished the first curse, that Thou mightest exalt us to Heaven by the God-given gift of divinity, and seat us upon the Father's throne; Who didst keep Thy Mother, Ever-Virgin both before childbirth and after childbirth, free of pain:[^43] do Thou so deign, O compassionate Master, all-good Lord Jesus Christ, now also that Thy servant who has given birth, [N.], [saying the name,] may be delivered from every snare of the enemy; and being sanctified by Thy grace, cleanse and sanctify her by the gift and visitation of the Holy Spirit: that both the women who ministered unto her when she gave birth, and likewise those who were present in this house, as they come may all receive Thy sanctification without doubt. Grant also that the one who has been born may be preserved above the snare of the enemy, and be counted worthy of the bath of regeneration,[^44] and become an honorable vessel of Thy grace, and by the communion of Thy most pure and divine Mysteries become an heir of Thy Heavenly Kingdom: that for this cause also Thine all-holy Name may be glorified, with the Father, and with the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

[In the margin: Note well.]

*And after this he sprinkles the whole house with holy water.*

*It is fitting to know:*[^45]

*If the newborn infant is sick and does not nurse, it is not fitting to wait for the fifth or sixth day, and then baptize, as some have evilly said; but in that very hour wherein he is born, wash him and immediately baptize him, lest he die unillumined. For if after five months from conception it happen that by some cause the infant is miscarried, they are guilty under the laws and the canons of the penalty for murder;[^46] how much more ought one to avoid the judgment concerning those already born, lest they pass away unillumined.*

*And on the fortieth day [the child] is again brought to be churched,[^47] that is, to begin to be brought into the church.*

*And he is brought by the mother, being already cleansed and washed; [the godparent] being present who will receive him after baptism;[^48] and she having bowed her head together with the infant before the doors of the church.*

*The priest begins:*
**Priest:** Blessed is our God...
*Then:*
**Reader:** Holy God... *(3)*. Most Holy Trinity...
*After* Our Father:
**Priest:** For Thine is the kingdom...

*Troparion, Tone 1:*[^49]
Rejoice, O Virgin Theotokos, full of grace! For from Thee has shone forth the Sun of Righteousness, Christ our God, illumining those who are in darkness. Rejoice thou also, O righteous elder, who dost receive in thine arms [who dost receive in thine arms] the Liberator of our souls, Who grants us the Resurrection.[^50]

*Kontakion, Tone 1:*[^51]
Thou Who didst sanctify the Virgin's womb by Thy birth, and didst bless the hands of Symeon as was fitting, having anticipated [us], hast now also saved [us], O Christ God; but grant peace in times of war to our commonwealth,[^52] and strengthen the rulers whom Thou hast loved, O only Lover of mankind.

*Then, touching her head, he signs her crosswise, saying:*
**Priest:** Let us pray to the Lord.

O Lord our God, unto Thee do we pray, and of Thee do we ask, and we make supplication: for Thou art He that is, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, Who hast created all rational and irrational nature by Thy word, and hast brought all things from non-existence into being: do Thou also by Thy will save this Thy servant, [N.], [saying the name,] and cleanse her from every defilement and every sin, as she approaches Thy holy church, that she may without condemnation be counted worthy of Thy Holy Mysteries.

*If, therefore, before the fortieth day the infant has died, read the prayer up to this point;[^53] but if he is alive, read this also unto the end:*

And this infant born of her do Thou bless, and sanctify, and cause to grow, and endow with understanding: for Thou hast brought him into the sensory light, that he may be counted worthy of the spiritual light,[^54] at the time which Thou hast pre-ordained; and that he] may be numbered with Thy holy flock, for the sake of Thine Only-begotten Son, with Whom Thou art blessed, together with Thine All-holy, and good, and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*The priest says:*
**Priest:** Peace be unto all.
**Reader:** And to thy spirit.
**Priest:** Bow your heads unto the Lord.
**Reader:** Unto Thee, O Lord.

*Then this prayer secretly:*[^55]

O Lord our God, Who didst come into the world for the salvation of the human race: come Thou also unto this Thy servant, [N.], [saying the name,] and count her worthy through Thy venerable presbyterate of the entrance into the temple of Thy glory; wash away her defilement of soul and body at the completion of the forty days, making her worthy of the communion of Thy precious Body and Blood.

**Priest (Exclamation):** For blessed and glorified is Thine all-honorable and majestic Name, with the Father, and with the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*Then the priest himself, having received the child in his hands, carries him into the sanctuary;[^56] and he touches his mouth to both sides of the doors in the likeness of kissing. Likewise, having brought him to the Holy Table, he positions the infant as though bowing, going around three times. And if he be of the male sex, he does thus; but if it be of the female sex, she is not brought into the altar, but only to the holy doors and the icons, as has been said.[^57] And when these things have been done, the priest says to himself:*

**Priest:** Now lettest Thou dismiss Thy servant, O Master... unto the end.[^58]

*After this, he places the infant before the doors of the sanctuary, and says the prayer:*
**Priest:** Let us pray to the Lord.

O Lord our God, Who on the fortieth day wast brought as an infant unto the temple of the Law by Mary, Thine unwedded Mother, and wast received into the arms of righteous Symeon: do Thou Thyself, O Almighty Master, bless also this infant who has been brought to appear before Thee, the Creator of all, and cause him to grow unto every good work that is well-pleasing unto Thee, driving away from him every contrary power by the signing of the form of Thy Cross: for Thou art He that preserves infants, O Lord; that being counted worthy [that being counted worthy] of Holy Baptism, he may obtain the portion of the elect of Thy Kingdom, being preserved with us by the grace of the Holy, and Consubstantial, and Undivided Trinity. For unto Thee is due all thanksgiving, honor, and worship, with Thine Unoriginate Father, and with Thine All-holy, and good, and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*After the Amen, he signs the child.*
*And the mother with the child, having stood before the holy icon of the Mother of God, makes forty bows.[^59] The priest makes the dismissal. And so the receiver receives him, and departs.*
*And the receiver is called the sponsor [koum].[^60]*

### Notes

[^31]: Fol. 1r. Slavonic *Чи́нъ ѡ҆бновле́нїе ѻ҆троча́те* translates the Greek Τάξις εἰς τὸ ἀνακαινίσαι παιδίον ("Order to dedicate/renew a child"), the ancient Byzantine rite for the naming and signing of an infant on the eighth day after birth in imitation of Christ's Circumcision and naming (Luke 2:21).
[^32]: Slavonic *ѡ҆ба́бы* (*от бабы*), that is, by the midwife (повивальная бабка), who according to ancient liturgical custom presented the newborn child at the entrance of the church prior to the mother's forty-day purification.
[^33]: Cf. Psalm 4:7 (LXX 4:6): "The light of Thy countenance, O Lord, is signed upon us." Masoretic numbering: Ps 4:6.
[^34]: The prayer looks forward from the eighth-day naming rite to the child's future Holy Baptism, Chrismation ("the seal"), and first reception of Holy Communion ("the most pure and dread Mysteries").
[^35]: The eighth-day naming and dedication rite (begun on fol. 1r, p19) concludes with this solemn Trinitarian doxology and dismissal at the entrance of the church.
[^36]: Slavonic *Мл҃тва ба́бѣ по прїѧ́тїи мл҃нца* translates the Byzantine euchological prayer for the midwife (*ба́ба* / *повитуха*) who received the newborn child, purifying her and blessing her return to the liturgical assembly.
[^37]: Fol. 2v. Slavonic *Чи́нъ въ тре́тїй де́нь женѣ̀ пороже́нїи ѻ҆троча́те* ("Order on the third day for a woman after giving birth to a child"). Whereas many classical Greek and South Slavic euchologia designate these prayers for the first day after childbirth, the Striatyn Trebnyk assigns them specifically to the third day in the child's home.
[^38]: Fol. 3r. The Troparion (*Рожество̀ твоѐ Бц҃е Дв҃о*) and Kontakion (*І҆ѡакі́мъ и҆ А҆́нна поноше́нїа безча́дства*) of the Nativity of the Most Holy Theotokos (September 8), Tone 4, are appointed as the opening hymnody, theologically framing all Christian childbearing within the salvation-historical mystery of the Virgin's birth.
[^39]: Fol. 3v. The prayer quotes the ancestral curses upon Adam and Eve following the Fall: Genesis 3:19 ("In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread") and Genesis 3:16 ("In sorrow shalt thou bring forth children"), contrasting mortal labor and sorrow with Christ's sinless Nativity from the Virgin.
[^40]: Fol. 4r. Slavonic *соумнѣ́нїе* reflects Greek δισταγμός or ῥύпас, denoting the natural spiritual distress, emotional turmoil, and vulnerability experienced by the mother in childbed.
[^41]: Fol. 4r. Slavonic *ѿ съжи́тельства дїа́воля, и҆ ро́да вся́каго пога́нствїа* ("from the cohabitation of the devil, and from every manner of defilement/paganism"). The petition reflects the pastoral exorcistic protection invoked by the Church against demonic harassments and pagan folk superstitions historically associated with post-partum recovery.
[^42]: Fol. 4v. The third prayer is explicitly addressed not only to the mother, but also to the kinswomen and neighbors who assisted at the birth (*жена́мъ прилꙋчи́вшимїся на рожде́нїи то́мъ*), extending the Church's blessing and sanctification to the entire household.
[^43]: Fol. 5r. The prayer emphasizes the patristic dogma of the Virgin Mary's painless parturition (*неболѣ́знноу съблю́лъ є҆сѝ*), by which the curse of Eve was healed in the uncorrupt birth of Christ.
[^44]: Fol. 5v. "Bath of regeneration" translates the Pauline phrase *ба́ня па́кибытїѧ́йская* (Titus 3:5, Greek: λουτρὸν παλιγγενεσίας), referring to the forthcoming Holy Mystery of Baptism.
[^45]: Fol. 6r. In the outer margin, the red callout *зрѝ* ("Note well") highlights this pastoral rubric, which mandates immediate emergency baptism for sickly infants and forbids delaying until the customary fifth, sixth, eighth, or fortieth day.
[^46]: Fol. 6r. The citation of legal and canonical penalties for miscarriages occurring after five months of gestation reflects Byzantine and Slavic canonical jurisprudence (e.g. Canons of St. Basil the Great, Canons 2 and 8; Council in Trullo, Canon 91; Syntagma / *Kormchaia Kniga*), which classified the destruction of a formed fetus as homicide.
[^47]: Fol. 6v. Slavonic *въ цр҃ковьсти́тися* (to be churched, воцерковлятися; Greek: Σαранτισμός / εἰσκκλησιασμός) opens the solemn Order of Churching on the Fortieth Day, patterned upon the Presentation of Christ in the Temple (Luke 2:22–39) and the Mosaic purification (Leviticus 12:1–8).
[^48]: Fol. 6v. The rubric prescribes that the mother present the child before the church doors, accompanied by the intended sponsor (godparent) who will receive the child after baptism.
[^49]: Fol. 6v. Troparion of the Meeting of the Lord (Presentation of Christ in the Temple, February 2), Tone 1 (*Ра́дꙋйся ѡ҆брадо́ванная Бц҃е Дв҃о*), sung as the child is brought to the sanctuary doors.
[^50]: Fol. 7r. Continuation and conclusion from fol. 6v (p30) of the Troparion of the Meeting of the Lord (Presentation of Christ in the Temple, February 2), Tone 1 (*Ра́дꙋйся ѡ҆брадо́ванная Бц҃е дв҃о...*), quoting Luke 2:29–32.
[^51]: Fol. 7r. Kontakion of the Meeting of the Lord (*І҆́же оу҆тро́бꙋ дв҃ичю ѡ҆ст҃ивъ рожество́мъ си...*). Although in later standard liturgical editions this kontakion is appointed under Tone 1, some Ruthenian sources designate it Tone 1 while preserving the archaic pre-Nikonian petitions.
[^52]: Fol. 7r. Slavonic *оу҆мирѝ въ бра́нехъ житїѐ и҆ оу҆крѣпѝ цр҃ѧ [царѝ] и҆́хъже възлюби́лъ є҆си* ("grant peace in wars to life, and strengthen the kings/rulers whom Thou hast loved"). The Striatyn Trebnyk preserves the traditional Byzantine/Ruthenian civil petition for Christian rulers facing wartime adversity.
[^53]: Fol. 7v. This conditional rubric accounts for pastoral situations of neonatal infant mortality occurring before the completion of the forty days. If the child has died, the prayer of churching serves purely as the post-partum purification of the mother, omitting the child-oriented petitions.
[^54]: Fol. 7v. The Greek euchological theology contrasts "sensory light" (*свѣ́тъ чю́вственый*, Greek: αἰσθηтὸν φῶς) of physical birth into the material world with "spiritual/intelligible light" (*вразꙋ́мный свѣ́тъ*, νοηтὸν φῶς) imparted in Holy Illumination (Baptism).
[^55]: Fol. 8r. The second prayer of churching is designated to be recited secretly (*втаѝ*, Greek: μυστικῶς) while the mother and infant bow their heads before the entrance of the nave. It prays specifically for the mother's readmission to the Holy Mysteries of the Eucharist after her forty days of seclusion.
[^56]: Fol. 8v. Slavonic *жръто́вникъ* (literally "altar of sacrifice" or "prothesis") is used interchangeably here with *ѡ҆лта́рь* (sanctuary/chancel). The rubric directs the priest to receive the male infant into his arms and carry him into the sanctuary through the Holy Doors.
[^57]: Fol. 8v. The traditional canonical distinction in the rite of churching between male and female infants: male infants are carried through the Holy Doors around the Holy Table (*ст҃а́ѧ трапе́за*), whereas female infants are brought up to the Holy Doors (*сты̑ѧ две́ри*) and venerate the principal icons of the iconostasis.
[^58]: Fol. 9r. The Canticle of Symeon (Luke 2:29–32, *Nunc Dimittis* / *Нн҃ѣ ѿпоуща́еши*) is recited by the priest at the conclusion of bringing the child into the sanctuary, directly identifying the infant's reception in the church with Christ's Presentation in the Temple.
[^59]: Fol. 9v. The prescription of forty prostrations or metanias (*покло́новъ четыредесѧ́ть*) performed by the mother before the principal icon of the Theotokos symbolizes the completion of the forty days of post-partum purification, echoing the scriptural number of purification and testing.
[^60]: Fol. 9v. Slavonic *прїе́мникъ же гле́тсѧ, коу́мъ* ("and the receiver is called the koum [godparent/sponsor]"). *Koum* (Ukrainian/East Slavic *кум*, from Latin *compater*) is the vernacular East Slavic term for the baptismal sponsor who officially receives the child at the church doors.
