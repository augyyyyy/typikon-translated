# PART XXI: THE ORDER OF BURIAL [^1409]

*When one of the Orthodox has reposed, his kinsmen immediately summon the priest; and having arrived at the house wherein the remains lie, and having put on the epitrachelion and put incense into the censer, he begins in the usual manner:*

> **Priest:** **"Blessed is our God..."**

*And those with him say:*

> **People:** **"Holy God..."** **"All-Holy Trinity..."** after **"Our Father..."**[^1410]

> **Priest:** **"For Thine is the kingdom..."**

*And these troparia, Tone 4:*

*Troparion, Tone 4:*
With the spirits of the righteous made perfect, give rest to the soul of Thy servant, O Savior, preserving it in the blessed life which is with Thee, O Lover of mankind.[^1411]

*Troparion:*
In Thy resting place, O Lord, where all Thy saints repose, give rest also to the soul of Thy servant, for Thou alone art the Lover of mankind.

*Glory...*

*Troparion:*
Thou art the God Who descended into hades and loosed the bonds of the bound: do Thou Thyself give rest also to the soul of Thy servant.[^1412]

*Both now...*

*Theotokion:*
O only pure and spotless Virgin, who without seed didst bear God: intercede that our souls may be saved.

*The priest [says] the litany:*

> **Priest:** ""
Have mercy on us, O God, according to Thy great mercy, we pray Thee, give ear and have mercy.[^1413]
Furthermore we pray for the repose of the soul of the servant of God [Name], departed this life.
That every transgression committed by him, voluntary and involuntary, may be forgiven him.
That the Lord God may establish his spirit where the righteous repose.
The mercies of God, the kingdom of heaven, and the remission of his sins, let us ask of Christ, the immortal King and our God.

*Let us pray to the Lord:*

O God of spirits and of all flesh, Who hast trampled down death, and abolished the devil, and given life unto Thy world:[^1414] do Thou Thyself, O Lord give rest to the soul of Thy departed servant [Name], in a place of light, in a place of green pasture, in a place of refreshment, whence sickness, sorrow, and sighing have fled away.[^1415] Every sin committed by him, in word, or in deed, or in thought, do Thou as the good One and Lover of mankind forgive. For there is no man that shall live and not sin; for Thou alone art without sin; Thy righteousness is an everlasting righteousness, and Thy word is truth.[^1416]

*Exclamation:*
For Thou art the resurrection and the life and the repose of Thy departed servant [Name], O Christ our God, and unto Thee do we send up glory together with Thine unoriginate Father, and Thy most holy and good and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

*Glory... Both now... and the dismissal:*

*And if, then, all things for the departure are ready, the priest immediately makes the beginning again. And we begin to chant: **"Holy God..."** with fear and all contrition. And having taken up the body of the deceased, we go forth to the church, the priests going before with candles and the deacons with censers. And when they come into the church, the remains are placed in the narthex opposite the great doors; then*[^1417] *we begin:* **"Come, let us worship..."** *(Thrice).*

*Psalm 90:*[^1418]
He that dwells in the help of the Most High shall abide in the shelter of the God of heaven.
He shall say unto the Lord: Thou art my protector and my refuge; my God, in Him will I hope.
For He shall deliver thee from the snare of the hunters, and from troubling speech.
With His pinions shall He overshadow thee, and under His wings shalt thou hope; His truth shall encircle thee with a shield.
Thou shalt not be afraid of the terror of the night, nor of the arrow that flies by day;
Nor of the thing that walks in darkness, nor of mishap and the noonday demon.[^1419]
A thousand shall fall at thy side and ten thousand at thy right hand, but it shall not come near thee.
Only with thine eyes shalt thou behold, and see the reward of sinners.
For Thou, O Lord, art my hope; Thou hast made the Most High thy refuge.
No evil shall come upon thee, and no scourge shall draw near unto thy body.[^1420]
For He shall give His angels charge concerning thee, to keep thee in all thy ways.
Upon their hands shall they bear thee up, lest at any time thou dash thy foot against a stone.
Upon the asp and basilisk shalt thou tread, and trample down the lion and dragon.
Because he has set his hope on Me, I will deliver him; I will protect him, because he has known My name.
He shall call upon Me, and I will answer him; I am with him in affliction; I will deliver him and glorify him.
With length of days will I satisfy him, and show him My salvation.

*After this, with a most elevated voice they chant the Blameless [Verses].[^1421] And at the end of each verse we sing the refrain: "Blessed art Thou, O Lord, teach us Thy statutes."*

*Psalm 118:*[^1422]
Blessed are the blameless in the way, who walk in the law of the Lord.
Blessed are they that search out His testimonies; with their whole heart shall they seek Him.
For they that work iniquity have not walked in His ways.
Thou hast commanded Thy precepts to be kept diligently.
O that my ways were directed to keep Thy statutes!
Then shall I not be put to shame, when I look upon all Thy commandments.
I will confess Thee in uprightness of heart, when I have learned the judgments of Thy righteousness.
I will keep Thy statutes; do not utterly forsake me.
By what means shall a young man direct his way? By keeping Thy words.[^1423]
With my whole heart have I sought Thee; cast me not away from Thy commandments.
In my heart have I hidden Thy words, that I might not sin against Thee.
Blessed art Thou, O Lord; teach me Thy statutes.
With my lips have I declared all the judgments of Thy mouth.

In the way of Thy testimonies have I found delight, as in all manner of riches.
In Thy commandments will I meditate, and I will understand Thy ways.
In Thy statutes will I meditate; I will not forget Thy words.
Reward Thy servant; give me life, and I will keep Thy words.
Open Thou mine eyes, and I will understand the wonders of Thy law.
A sojourner am I upon the earth; hide not Thy commandments from me.
My soul has longed to desire Thy judgments at all times.
Thou hast rebuked the proud; cursed are they that turn aside from Thy commandments.[^1424]
Take away from me reproach and contempt, for I have sought after Thy testimonies.
For princes sat and slandered me; but Thy servant meditated on Thy statutes.
For Thy testimonies are my meditation, and Thy statutes are my counselors.
My soul has cleaved unto the earth; quicken Thou me according to Thy word.
I have declared my ways, and Thou didst hear me; teach me Thy statutes.
Make me to understand the way of Thy statutes, and I will meditate on Thy marvels.
My soul slumbered from grief; strengthen Thou me in Thy words.[^1425]
Remove from me the way of iniquity, and by Thy law have mercy on me.

I have chosen the way of truth, and Thy judgments have I not forgotten.
I have cleaved unto Thy testimonies; O Lord, put me not to shame.
I ran the way of Thy commandments, when Thou didst enlarge my heart.
Set before me for a law, O Lord, the way of Thy statutes, and I will seek it out continually.
Give me understanding, and I will search out Thy law; and I will keep it with my whole heart.
Guide me in the path of Thy commandments, for I have desired it.
Incline my heart unto Thy testimonies, and not to covetousness.
Turn away mine eyes from beholding vanity; quicken Thou me in Thy way.
Establish unto Thy servant Thy word, for Thy fear.[^1426]

Take away my reproach which I have dreaded; for Thy judgments are good.
Behold, I have longed after Thy commandments; quicken Thou me in Thy righteousness.
And let Thy mercy come upon me, O Lord, even Thy salvation according to Thy word.
So shall I give answer to them that reproach me with a word; for I have hoped in Thy words.
And take not the word of truth utterly out of my mouth; for I have hoped in Thy judgments.
So shall I keep Thy law continually, forever and unto ages of ages.[^1427]
And I walked at large; for I sought out Thy commandments.
And I spoke of Thy testimonies before kings, and was not ashamed.
And I meditated on Thy commandments which I loved exceedingly.
And I lifted up my hands unto Thy commandments, which I loved; and I meditated continually on Thy statutes.
Remember Thy words unto Thy servant, wherein Thou hast caused me to hope.
This has comforted me in my lowliness, that Thy word has quickened me.
The proud have transgressed exceedingly, yet have I not declined from Thy law.
I remembered Thy judgments from of old, O Lord, and was comforted.
Grief has taken hold of me because of the sinners that forsake Thy law.
Thy statutes were my songs in the place of my sojourning.[^1428]
I remembered in the night Thy name, O Lord, and kept Thy law.
This has happened unto me, because I sought after Thy statutes.
Thou art my portion, O Lord; I said that I would keep Thy law.
I entreated Thy face with my whole heart; have mercy on me according to Thy word.
I thought on Thy ways, and turned my feet unto Thy testimonies.
I made ready and was not disquieted, that I might keep Thy commandments.
The cords of sinners have entangled me, and Thy law have I not forgotten.[^1429]
At midnight I arose to give thanks unto Thee for the judgments of Thy righteousness.
I am a companion of all them that fear Thee and of them that keep Thy commandments.
The earth, O Lord, is full of Thy mercy; teach me Thy statutes.
Thou hast dealt kindly with Thy servant, O Lord, according to Thy word.
Teach me goodness and instruction and understanding, for I have believed Thy commandments.
Before I was humbled I transgressed; for this cause have I kept Thy word.
Good art Thou, O Lord, and in Thy goodness teach me Thy statutes.
The iniquity of the proud has been multiplied against me; but with my whole heart will I search out Thy commandments.
Their heart is curdled like milk; but as for me, in Thy law[^1430] have I meditated.
It is good for me that Thou hast humbled me, that I might learn Thy statutes.
The law of Thy mouth is good to me, above thousands of gold and silver.
Thy hands have made me and fashioned me; give me understanding, and I shall learn Thy commandments.
*Have mercy on Thy servant.*[^1431]
They that fear Thee shall see me and be glad, because I have hoped in Thy words.
I know, O Lord, that Thy commandments are righteousness,[^1432] and that in truth Thou hast humbled me.
Let Thy mercy be for my comfort, according to Thy word unto Thy servant.
Let come unto me Thy compassions, that I may live; for Thy law is my meditation.
Let the proud be put to shame, for they have transgressed against me unjustly; but as for me, I will meditate on Thy commandments.
Let those that fear Thee turn unto me, and those that know Thy testimonies.
Let my heart be blameless in Thy statutes, that I may not be put to shame.
My soul faints for Thy salvation; I have hoped in Thy words.
Mine eyes fail for Thy word, saying: When wilt Thou comfort me?
For I am become like a wineskin in the frost;[^1433] yet have I not forgotten Thy statutes.
How many are the days of Thy servant? When wilt Thou execute judgment on them that persecute me?
Transgressors have told me idle tales,[^1434] but not as Thy law, O Lord.
All Thy commandments are truth; they persecuted me unjustly: help Thou me.
They had almost consumed me upon the earth; but I forsook not Thy commandments.
*Quicken Thou me according to Thy mercy,*[^1435] and I shall keep the testimonies of Thy mouth.
Forever, O Lord, Thy word abides in heaven.
Thy truth is from generation to generation; Thou hast founded the earth, and it abides.
By Thine ordinance the day abides, for all things are Thy servants.

Unless Thy law had been my meditation, I should then have perished in my humiliation.
Forever will I not forget Thy statutes, for in them Thou hast quickened me.
*Save me; unless Thy law [had been my meditation]...*[^1436]
*Say thrice. Then the deaconal petitions:*
> **Deacon:** "Again and again in peace let us pray to the Lord."
Again let us pray for the repose of the soul of the servant of God, [Name], and that every transgression, voluntary and involuntary, may be forgiven him.
That the Lord God may establish his soul where the righteous repose.
The mercies of God, the kingdom of heaven, and the remission of his sins, let us ask of Christ the immortal King and our God.
Let us pray to the Lord.

Thy zeal has consumed me,[^1446] because mine enemies have forgotten Thy words.

I am young and despised, yet have I not forgotten Thy statutes.
Thy righteousness is righteousness forever, and Thy law is truth.
Afflictions and distresses have found me; Thy commandments are my meditation.

*Tone 5:*
I called to mind the Prophet crying aloud: "I am earth and ashes"; and again I looked into the graves and beheld the bare bones, and I said: Who then is this? A king, or a beggar? Or a righteous man, or a sinner? But give rest, O Lord, unto Thy servant with the righteous.[^1522]

*Tone 6:*
My beginning and my substance was Thy creative command; for wishing to fashion me as a living creature from both the invisible and the visible natures, Thou didst form my body from the earth, and gavest me a soul by Thy divine and life-creating breathing. Therefore, O Savior, give rest unto Thy servant in the land of the living and in the tabernacles of the righteous.[^1523]

*Tone 7:*
Having created man in the beginning according to Thine image and likeness, Thou didst place him in paradise to have dominion over Thy creatures. But deceived by the envy of the devil, having partaken of the food, he became a transgressor of Thy commandments; therefore Thou didst sentence him, O Lord, to return again unto the earth whence he was taken, and to ask for rest.[^1524]

*Tone 8:*
I weep and I lament when I contemplate death, and behold our beauty, fashioned according to the image of God, lying in the graves, formless, inglorious, having no shape. O wonder! Truly by God's command, as it is written, Who grants rest unto the departed.[^1525]

*Troparion:*
By reason of unoriginate begetting and proceeding, I worship the Father Who begat, I glorify the Son Who was begotten, and I hymn the Holy Spirit Who shone forth with the Father and the Son.[^1533]

*Theotokion:*
How dost thou pour forth milk from thy breasts, O Virgin? How dost thou nourish the Nourisher of creation? Even as He Himself knows[^1534] He Who brought forth water from the rock, streams of water for the thirsty people, as it is written.[^1535]

*Prokeimenon, Tone 6:* Blessed is the way wherein thou walkest today, for a place of rest has been prepared for thee.[^1536]
*Verse:* Unto Thee, O Lord, will I call.[^1537]

*Epistle: The Reading is from the Epistle of Paul to the Thessalonians:*[^1538]
Brethren, I would not have you to be ignorant concerning them that are asleep, that ye sorrow not, even as others who have no hope. For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so them also that sleep in Jesus will God bring with Him. For this we say unto you by the word of the Lord, that we who are alive and remain unto the coming of the Lord shall not precede them that are asleep. For the Lord Himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trumpet of God; and the dead in Christ shall rise first; then we who are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air; and so shall we ever be with the Lord.[^1539]

*Alleluia. Blessed is he whom Thou hast chosen and taken, O Lord.*[^1540]

*Gospel according to John:*[^1541]
The Lord said unto the Jews who came unto Him: Truly, truly, I say unto you, he that hears My word, and believes on Him that sent Me, has everlasting life, and shall not come into judgment, but is passed from death unto life. Truly, truly, I say unto you, the hour is coming, and now is, when the dead shall hear the voice of the Son of God; and they that hear shall live. For as the Father has life in Himself, so has He given to the Son to have life in Himself; and has given Him authority to execute judgment also, because He is the Son of Man. Marvel not at this; for the hour is coming in which all that are in the graves shall hear the voice of the Son of God, and shall come forth: they that have done good, unto the resurrection of life; and they that have done evil, unto the resurrection of judgment.[^1542] I can of Mine own self do nothing; as I hear, I judge and My judgment is just; because I seek not Mine own will, but the will of the Father Who sent Me.[^1543]

> **Deacon:** ""
Have mercy on us, O God, according to Thy great mercy:
Again we pray for the repose of the soul of the departed servant of God, [Name], and that he may be forgiven every transgression, both voluntary and involuntary;
That the Lord God may establish his soul where the righteous rest;
The mercies of God, the heavenly kingdom, and the remission of his sins, let us ask of Christ, the Immortal King and our God.[^1544]

*And upon the conclusion thereof, the first among the fathers, or the presiding hierarch, says the prayer:* O God of spirits and of all flesh *with a loud voice, having approached near to the relics; likewise also all the priests who are with him.*

*And it is to be known that while each litany is said by the deacon, for as long as the supplication is said by him, each of the priests who is present says the written prayer secretly according to his rank near the relics, and makes the exclamation:*

> **Priest:** "For Thou art the resurrection and the life..."

*In this manner it is said with a loud voice by the first priest, or the hierarch, as was stated above.*

*And the spiritual father reads this prayer over the deceased:*[^1545]

In the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, and of our Most Holy Lady the Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary, and of the holy heavenly Hosts, of the honorable Prophet, Forerunner, and Baptist John, and of the holy all-laudable Apostles, and of the holy three hundred and eighteen Fathers who were in Nicaea.[^1546] For the Lord, having commanded His Apostles, said: "If men sin against you seventy times seven, forgive them, that they may be forgiven in heaven; whatsoever ye shall loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven."[^1547] Therefore, according to the commandment of the Lord and God and our Savior Jesus Christ, I also, the priest, [Name], forgive thee, my spiritual child, [Name], that thou mayest be forgiven in this world and in that which is to come, when the righteous Judge shall come to judge the living and the dead. For unto Him is due all glory, honor, and worship, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^1548]

*After this takes place the Last Kiss:*[^1549]

*And we sing the following Stichera. Tone 2, Podoben: "When from the Tree" (Е҆гда̀ ѿ дрѣ́ва тѧ̀):*[^1550]

*Sticheron 1:* Come, brethren, let us give the last kiss unto the deceased, praising God; for behold, he has departed from his kindred and hastens unto the grave, no longer caring for vain things and the much-toiling flesh. Where are now kinsfolk and friends? Behold, we are parted! For him who has departed, let us pray unto the Lord that He may give him rest.[^1551]

*Sticheron 2:* What parting is this, O brethren! What weeping, what lamentation in the present hour! Come therefore, kiss him who a little while ago was with us. For he is delivered unto the grave; he is covered with stones; he dwells in darkness; he is buried with the dead. But all we who are kinsfolk and friends are now parted! For him who has departed, let us pray unto the Lord that He may give him rest.[^1552]

*Sticheron 3:* Now is all the deceitful triumph of vain life dissolved; for the spirit has departed from its tabernacle; the clay has blackened; the vessel is shattered: speechless, senseless, dead, motionless! Escorting the departed unto the grave, let us pray unto the Lord to give him rest unto ages.[^1553]

*Sticheron 4:* What is our life? Truly a flower, and a vapor, and morning dew! Come therefore, let us look clearly into the graves: where is bodily beauty? Where is youth? Where are the eyes and the form of the flesh? All have withered like grass, all have vanished! Come, let us fall down before Christ with tears.[^1554]

*Sticheron 5:* Great is the weeping and lamentation, great is the groaning and distress, the parting of the soul! Hades and destruction is the life of temporal things; an unsubstantial shadow, a deceitful dream, unseasonably imagining, labor unto earthly life! Far off let us flee from the world and from every sin, that we may inherit the heavens.[^1555]

*Sticheron 6:* Beholding him lying dead before us, let us take the image of the final hour; for behold, he departs like smoke from the earth, he has withered like a flower, he has been mown down like grass, wrapped in grave-cloths, covered with earth. Leaving the departed unseen, let us pray unto Christ to give him rest unto ages.[^1556]

*Sticheron 7:* Come, ye that are of the race of Adam, let us behold him who is laid in the earth, who is according to our likeness: he has put off all comeliness, dissolved in the grave, wasted away by the stench of worms in darkness, covered with earth! Leaving the departed unseen, let us pray unto Christ to give him rest unto ages.[^1557]

*Sticheron 8:* When the soul is about to be violently snatched from the body by fearsome angels, it forgets all kinsfolk and acquaintances, and takes thought for appearing before the judgment seat that is to come. O the vanity and the much-toiling flesh! Then, entreating the Judge, let us all pray for the departed, that the Lord may forgive him the deeds he has done.[^1558]

*Sticheron 9:* Come unto the grave, O brethren, let us see the ashes and dust from which we were created! Whither go we now? What indeed have we become? Who is the poor man, or who the rich? Or who is the master, and who the free? Are not all dust? The beauty of the countenance has rotted, and death has withered all the flower of youth.[^1559]

*Sticheron 10:* Truly vanity and corruption are all the sweet and glorious things of life! For all of us vanish, all of us die: kings and princes, judges and the mighty, rich and poor, and all human nature. For now those who once were in life are laid in the tombs: whom let us pray unto the Lord to give rest.[^1560]

*Sticheron 11:* All the bodily instruments are now seen to be idle, which a little while before were active; all are inactive, dead, insensate. For the eyes have closed, the feet are bound, the hands have fallen still, and hearing with them; and the tongue is locked in silence, delivered unto the grave. Truly[^1561] all human things are vanity!

*Theotokion:* Save those that hope in thee, O Mother of the unwaning Sun, O Birthgiver of God! By thy prayers ask of the all-good God, we pray, to give rest unto him who has now been translated, where the souls of the righteous repose. Show him forth as an heir of divine good things in the courts of the righteous, in everlasting memory, O all-immaculate one.[^1562]

*Glory... Tone 6:*[^1563]

*Doxastikon:* Beholding me lying before you speechless and breathless, weep for me, all ye brethren and friends, and kinsfolk and acquaintances! For yesterday I conversed with you, and suddenly the fearsome hour of death came upon me.

But come, all ye that love me, and kiss me with the last kiss; for no longer shall I walk with you or converse henceforth. For I depart unto the Judge, where there is no respect of persons: for slave and master stand together, king and soldier, rich and poor, in equal dignity; for each from his own deeds shall either be glorified or put to shame. But I ask and beseech all, unceasingly to pray for me unto Christ God, that I may not be appointed according to my sins unto the place of torment, but that He may appoint me where is the light of life.[^1564]

*Both now... Theotokion:*[^1565]

*Theotokion:* By the prayers of Her that bare Thee, O Christ, and of Thy Forerunner, of the Apostles, Prophets, and Hierarchs, of the Venerable and Righteous, and of all the Saints, give rest unto Thy reposed servant.[^1566]

*Then the Trisagion. All-Holy Trinity. Our Father. For Thine is the kingdom. And the Troparia: "With the spirits of the righteous..." and the rest. The litany by the deacon. The general prayer. And after the exclamation: Glory... Both now... The Dismissal:*[^1567]

May He Who rose from the dead, Christ our true God, through the prayers of His all-pure Mother, of the holy, glorious, and all-praised Apostles, of our venerable and God-bearing Fathers, and of all the Saints: [appoint] the soul of His servant who has been translated from us[^1568] in the tabernacles of the righteous; give him rest in the bosom of Abraham, number him among the righteous, and have mercy upon us, as He is good and the Lover of mankind. Amen.[^1569]

Eternal memory unto thee, O our brother worthy of blessedness and everlasting memory! *(Thrice)*

*And having thus taken the remains, we depart unto the grave, singing: "Holy God..." and the rest. And the remains are laid in the grave. And the priest, having taken earth upon a shovel in the form of a cross, casts it upon the remains, saying:*

The earth is the Lord's, and the fullness thereof; the world, and all that dwell therein.

*And then he pours oil upon the remains from a lamp, or ashes from the censer. And thus according to*[^1570] custom they close the grave, while these Troparia are being said: "With the spirits of the righteous..." and all the rest, and the Dismissal.[^1571]

Glory to God Who has thus been well pleased.

*End of the Funeral Sequence for Laymen.*[^1572]

### Notes

[^1409]: Folio 229r (physical page 475). Major structural division: The Order of Burial (Послѣдованїе къ погребенїю). The rubric specifies the initial domestic rites: immediately upon death, the relatives summon the priest to the home where the body lies; the priest vests in the epitrachelion and censes the deceased before beginning the service.
[^1410]: Rubrical instruction for the Usual Beginning (Обычное начало) chanted at the house of the deceased: the Trisagion Prayers through the Lord's Prayer ("Holy God...", "All-Holy Trinity...", "Our Father..."), followed by the priestly doxology ("For Thine is the kingdom...").
[^1411]: Folio 229v (physical page 476). The renowned Funeral Troparia (Tone 4) chanted at the home: *With the spirits of the righteous made perfect...* (Со духи праведными скончавшихся...). Direct Scriptural allusion to Hebrews 12:23 ("to the spirits of just men made perfect").
[^1412]: Allusion to Christ's descent into hades to loose the bonds of those held captive, drawing on 1 Peter 3:19 ("He went and preached unto the spirits in prison") and Psalm 106:14 (LXX 106:14 / MT 107:14: "and He broke their bonds asunder").
[^1413]: Folio 230r (physical page 477). The Funeral Litany of Fervent Supplication (Сугубая ектения об упокоении) offered in the home of the deceased, petitioning for the forgiveness of all sins voluntary and involuntary and the repose of the soul with the righteous.
[^1414]: Opening of the great Priestly Prayer for the Departed: *O God of spirits and of all flesh...* (Боже духовъ и всякия плоти...). Scriptural allusion to Numbers 16:22 and 27:16 ("the God of the spirits of all flesh") and Hebrews 2:14 (Christ destroying through death him that had the power of death, that is, the devil).
[^1415]: Folio 230v (physical page 478). Continuation of the prayer of absolution: the threefold peaceful haven of repose—a place of light, green pasture, and refreshment (Psalm 22:2 LXX: "In a place of green pasture, there He hath made me to dwell; beside the water of rest hath He nurtured me" [Masoretic: Ps 23:2]). Allusion also to Isaiah 35:10 and 51:11 ("sorrow and sighing shall flee away").
[^1416]: Allusion to the universal human condition of sinfulness from 3 Kingdoms 8:46 (LXX 3 Kgs 8:46 / MT 1 Kgs 8:46: "for there is no man that sins not"), 2 Chronicles 6:36, and Ecclesiastes 7:20. Followed by the citation of Psalm 118:142 (LXX 118:142 / MT 119:142: "Thy righteousness is an everlasting righteousness, and Thy law is truth") and Psalm 118:160.
[^1417]: Folio 231r (physical page 479). Rubric for the funeral procession transferring the body from the home to the church: the priest gives the dismissal at the home, initiates the service again, and the clergy and people bear the body of the deceased to the church in solemn procession with processional candles/lamps and censers while chanting the Trisagion (*Holy God*). Upon arrival at the church, the coffin is set in the narthex (притворъ) facing the royal doors.
[^1418]: Folio 231v (physical page 480). Opening of the church station in the narthex: the triple *Come, let us worship*, followed by Psalm 90 (LXX 90 / MT 91:1-7a), traditionally chanted over the deceased as a psalm of divine protection and trust.
[^1419]: Septuagint versification of Psalm 90:1-6 (LXX 90:1-6 / Masoretic: Psalm 91:1-6).
