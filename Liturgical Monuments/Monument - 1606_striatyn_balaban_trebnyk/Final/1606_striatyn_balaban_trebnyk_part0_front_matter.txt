## PREFACE: TO THE READER

*Preface to this Book.*

*Hedeon Balaban, by the mercy of God humble Bishop of Halych, Lviv, and Kamianets, Exarch of the Ecumenical Throne of the Great Church of Constantinople: To the reverend priests and deacons, and to the other fellow ministers of the sacred clergy, and to the brethren beloved in the Lord, I pray for salvation.*

Seeing that many zealous lovers of the Divine law take care concerning the sacred books, by which the Church of God is adorned, and the faith is defended, the praise of the Most High is multiplied, the faithful are confirmed, and heretics are confounded; and the people, raised up as if by a certain spur from slothfulness, are moved unto learning and are encouraged; and above all, by which God is entreated. Therefore, being desirous to print this book, I deemed it right to inform you in this preface—even as you were previously informed of this in the Liturgies[^3]—whence I arrived at this work, God prospering me: namely, having been moved by the writings of the blessed Meletios, Pope of Alexandria;[^4] but also, more than ten years ago, a synod having taken place in Brest of Lithuania,[^5] under Mikhail Rahoza, Metropolitan of Kyiv,[^6] and the other bishops then assembled, before his apostasy with his partisans from the One Holy Eastern Catholic Apostolic Church: there was much inquiry and consideration by them concerning the great discrepancy of Church rites, and especially concerning the Euchologion,[^7] that is, the Molitvoslovets or Trebnyk: by which, being instructed as by a certain artistic guidance, those who perform sacred rites accomplish decently all things pertaining to the perfect calling of Christianity, and agree in their prayers unto God. And having considered these matters at the synod, they entrusted to humble me, the Bishop of Lviv, to take all care and diligence concerning the correction of that book of the said Trebnyk. And shortly thereafter, Metropolitan Mikhail, having apostatized with certain bishops from the Holy Eastern Church and cast off the pastorship of the Ecumenical Patriarch, departed along the path of insubordination according to the devise of their own hearts. Yet I, following my own shepherd,[^8] did not apostatize; and keeping in remembrance the task entrusted unto me, I gathered many Trebnyks, both from these local regions and from the lands of Wallachia, Moldavia, and Serbia:[^9] in which, when I saw the great discrepancy, concerning this I grieved exceedingly; but I found a remedy for my sorrow through an epistle sent to the most blessed Meletios, Pope of Alexandria—who had attained to the height of external wisdom and of our noble instruction, as an excellent steersman of the ship of Christ, who was then administering the place of the Throne of Constantinople[^10]—informing him concerning the discordance and lack of correction in our Slavonic books, whether on account of unskilled scribes or because of certain other causes; and at the same time beseeching him that, for the sake of certain and unconflicting knowledge of the Church services, he would send us a Greek Euchologion. And he did not disregard my petition, but sent me a Sluzhebnik, and this book, the Trebnyk, well corrected according to the ancient Trebnyks of the Holy Mountain;[^11] which also he signed with his own hand with his pastoral blessing, commanding that it be printed for the common benefit of the renowned Russian race.[^12] And since I had as my fellow laborer in this work the noble Teodor Yurievych Balaban, the son of my brother, with whom having established a printing house in our ancestral estate of Striatyn,[^13] fittingly, as was most urgent, with typographical instruments, having previously an urge toward this, as I said before, [encouraged] by the most blessed Meletios: we first printed the Sluzhebniks,[^14] and thereafter we resolved to print this Trebnyk; in which, lest anyone reproach us among those who delight in dissension concerning certain matters—especially concerning the Proskomedia,[^15] and concerning the particles of bread placed upon the diskos on account of discordance with ancient manuscript Sluzhebniks: let each know that not from my own opinion or speculation did I thus set this down; but having commanded [the translators] to interpret into Slavonic with all diligence the understanding and exposition concerning this formerly of blessed memory Jeremiah, Ecumenical Patriarch,[^16] who also traveled as a pilgrim here among us, and likewise of the most blessed Meletios, from the Greek books sent unto us by him, we have thus set it down. And having translated also this book, the Trebnyk,[^17] to issue it for the common need of the churches: yet lest there be any differing opinion concerning this also, we commanded that there be a synod of priests experienced in the sacred rites, to inspect and examine it. And I saw it to be necessary that what is found in our old Slavonic Trebnyks—namely, how to receive those coming from various heresies unto the Holy Catholic and Apostolic Church,[^18] the Consecration of the Great Myron,[^19] and the rest—lest the book be deficient in this also, we commanded to append this as well. And if there are any who take offense at this, that the authors of all the sequences are not known, let such know that neither do we know [the authors] for the other sequences of the Church's holy mysteries—namely, Baptism, Holy Unction,[^20] and others; yet we know from the commandments of the Holy Apostles that these were instituted by them. Concerning which also the fiery-tongued Dionysius the Areopagite in the book *On the Ecclesiastical Hierarchy* clearly testifies (that these were handed down by the Apostles),[^21] and the rest by the Holy Fathers; which, having received from both, we firmly maintain. For if, as the Great Basil says, we begin to neglect unwritten customs as having little force, we shall unawares bring harm even unto the written things themselves.[^22] And the Divine Chrysostom said: Hence it is manifest that the Apostles did not hand down all things by writing, but many things also unwritten; and both the one and the other are alike worthy of belief. Therefore we also deem the tradition of the Church worthy of belief: it is a tradition; seek nothing further.[^23] And when I was occupied with other necessary Church needs, and was taking care concerning other books—namely, Psalters and Didactic Gospels—to issue them from my printing house of Krylos,[^24] the aforementioned noble Teodor Yurievych Balaban zealously applied himself unto the work of this book: a man flourishing in many righteous deeds, and exceedingly [a fervent lover] of the pious dogma and the traditions of the fathers, a fervent lover, who not in word only, but also in deed kept blamelessly the piety of the fathers. And especially where many of the multitude who go abroad—I mean to Italy[^25]—being beguiled by the vain philosophy of secular learning, fall into various heresies, and being stripped of piety fall away from the Church. But he, having lived there for a long time in his youth, harmed not piety, and turned aside not even a little from the traditions of the Eastern Church; but even as one in the midst of fire remained unburned, or as one in the midst of the sea's surges suffered no shipwreck. Therefore I praise him; on account of the righteousness of his virtue I praise him; whose zeal and care in this was to issue into the light of day the books necessary for the Church: among which, after the Sluzhebnik, he took diligence to print this Trebnyk from his printing house of Striatyn, with the counsel and blessing of our humility, at his own cost and expense, having gathered for this work men skilled in that craft. Who, before this book had yet reached its completion, passed away, the faithful servant of his Lord, Teodor Yurievych, in the year after the Nativity of Christ one thousand six hundred and six, on the twenty-fourth day of the month of May.[^26]
And the composition of this book is proportioned to the span of man's age,[^27] from when he is born, a man receives the naming of a name, and Holy Baptism, with the anointing of the Great Myron, and the other sacred rites according to the Church tradition even unto his demise. Nor have we omitted the monastic sequences; likewise the prayerful supplication sequences for various necessary needs. And because the canons are not found among all priests, for this reason, having selected the most necessary from the Apostolic and Holy Fathers' commandments, we have appended them at the end of this book.[^28] Which do you, receiving with gratitude and love, honor, performing what has been labored over and corrected sacredly without any gainsaying, in concord; [remembering] those who exerted themselves and [brought] all diligence into this work, having brought it for the sake of your common benefit. And especially remember zealously the departed and ever-memorable servant of God, Teodor, at the accomplishment of the dread, bloodless sacrifice,[^29] and in the other lityas and supplications: that by grateful love and devotion to books you may also sharpen us for the work of other books.
Fare well, praying for us both here and after our departure from this flesh, being bound unto us by love.[^30]
