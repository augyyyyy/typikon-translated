# PART LV: CONCERNING THOSE WHO ABDUCT WOMEN FOR MARRIAGE [^4275]

*The eleventh canon of the Council of Ancyra:* Maidens who have been betrothed to someone, and then abducted by others: even if they have unlawfully forced them, it commands that they be restored again to those who betrothed them; for if they openly choose to take them, they are not to be rejected on account of that which was done without their will.[^4276]

*The twenty-seventh of the Fourth Council, and the ninety-second of the Sixth Council command thus:* Those who abduct women under pretext of cohabitation, or who assist them, or who consent and help: if they be clerics, let them be deposed; but if laymen[^4277] *Concerning Abductors:*[fol. 679v][^4278]

...let them be delivered over to anathema: for upon the same charges they condemn also those who consent to help and assist them; and all the more fittingly. For he who desires abduction, even if he offers an exceedingly rotten defense, driving toward an unseemly deed, having been constrained in his mind—that is to say, overcome [by passion]; but to an accomplice, who among those possessing understanding will grant pardon? [To him] nothing else [can be attributed] except that he has his own malice defiling him alongside that loathsome deed, which leads unto other improprieties and throws the orderly governance of civil society into disorder.[^4279] And an accomplice is he who actively carries out the abduction; while an abettor is he who devises plots and provides counsel.[^4280]

*Imperial Laws Concerning Those Taking for Marriage:*[fol. 680r][^4281]

If anyone abducts a woman, whether betrothed or unbetrothed, or a widow, whether noble, or a slave, or a freedwoman; and especially if she is consecrated [a nun]; and even if anyone abducts his own betrothed maiden:[^4282]

If, then, he commits the abduction with weapons, either with swords or with clubs: such a one shall be punished by the sword.[^4283] And those who assisted them, or were privy to it, or willingly escorted [her], or contributed any effort whatsoever: being severely beaten and shorn, let their noses be cut off; since those seeking to abduct are ready also to murder those who resist them.[^4284] But if he committed the abduction without any weapons whatsoever *Concerning Abductors:*[fol. 680v][^4285]

...committed [the abduction without any weapons]: let the hand of the abductor be cut off. And those who cooperated in any manner whatsoever, being beaten and shorn, let them be banished; whether the woman was willing or the abduction was done unwillingly; and especially if they attempted to abduct any women who were living with husbands. Moreover, if the parents of the woman attempt to accept [the match], they are worthy to be shut up in prison; and if a slave is caught having assisted in the deed, let him be burned [at the stake].[^4286]

*Statute:* He who abducts a virgin or a widow cannot have her as a wife, even if her father consents to it and forgives the offense.[^4287]

*Statute:* Let not the abducted woman be married to him who abducted her; but even if her parents consent to such[^4288] *Concerning Marriage of a Woman:*[fol. 681r][^4289]

...cohabitation, her parents shall be cast into prison.

*Statute:* In one way is he punished who has abducted a maiden, and in another way he who has deflowered a virgin by force. For abduction, being shamelessness on the part of the abductor, has no pardon; whereas corruption committed by force, if pardon be accepted from the violated maiden, receives forgiveness, and they are permitted to live together.[^4290]

*Statute:* An abducted woman obtains the property of him who abducted her, if she does not wish to be lawfully joined with him. For if she does not wish to do this, both her property and that of the abductor are confiscated, as well as [the property of] those who consented unto the wickedness, whether they be their parents or strangers.[^4291]

*Statute:* Abduction is greater than adultery; and he who abducts a married woman[^4292] *Concerning Those Abducting for Marriage:*[fol. 681v][^4293]

...or a virgin, is punished to the uttermost, and a third party may accuse him, even if he has been forgiven by the maiden. If a slave discloses the abduction of a virgin, he shall be granted freedom, or if he exposes an abduction that had already been forgiven.[^4294]

*Statute:* A slave who abducts or conceals another's [female slave who is a] harlot is guilty neither as a thief nor as guilty of corruption; for he did this not for the sake of theft, but for the sake of pleasure. Nevertheless, he is fined in his property by the prince, and is brought to chastity.[^4295]

Moreover, the seventeenth Novel of Justinian forbids granting boundaries of immunity [sanctuary] from arrest unto abductors of maidens; but even during the days of Holy Pascha, abductors of maidens are apprehended and bound.[^4296]

### Notes

[^4275]: Folio 679r header and title: *хо҃ѳ • Ѡ҆ въсхища́ющихъ на бра́къ жены̀* (*Concerning Those Who Abduct Women for Marriage*). Greek: *Περὶ τῶν ἁρπαζόντων πρὸς γάμον γυναῖκας*.
[^4276]: Canon 11 of the Local Council of Ancyra (314 AD): abducted betrothed maidens are to be restored to their original betrothed spouses if the latter are willing.
[^4277]: Canon 27 of the Fourth Ecumenical Council (Chalcedon, 451 AD) and Canon 92 of the Sixth Ecumenical Council (Trullo / In Trullo / Quinisext, 692 AD): deposition for clergy and anathema for laity who abduct women under pretext of marriage or who assist abductors. Text continues syntactically across the physical folio turn onto leaf p1374 (fol. 679v).
[^4278]: Folio 679v header: *ѡ҆ въсхища́ющихъ* (*Concerning Abductors:*). Canonical censure and anathema extended to accomplices and instigators who aid bride abduction.
[^4279]: Juridical and moral distinction between the abductor driven by passion (*по́хо въсхище́нїю... премо́гши*) and the calculating accomplice who acts from pure malice (*зло́боу*), disrupting public order (*гражда́нства не оу҆стро́єно полага́етъ бл҃гочи́нїе*).
[^4280]: Nomocanonical definitions: *сꙋдѣ́йствоуѧй* (active co-perpetrator who executes the kidnapping) and *съслага́ѧй* (accessory/conspirator who devises the plots and supplies counsel).
[^4281]: Folio 680r header: *х҃п • ѡ҆ и҆́ма[щихъ] на бра́къ* (*Concerning Those Taking for Marriage:*). Section title: *Закѡ́ни цр҃тїи* (*Imperial / Civil Laws:*), drawing from Byzantine civil law (Justinian's *Codex* IX.13, *Novels* 143 and 150; *Ecloga* XVII.23; *Prochiron* XXXIX.27).
[^4282]: Universal scope of the law against abduction: covering betrothed, unbetrothed, widows, nobles, slaves, freedwomen, and nuns (*ѡ҆сщ҃е́нна*).
[^4283]: Armed abduction (using swords or clubs) punished by decapitation (*мече́мъ да томи́мъ бꙋ́детъ*).
[^4284]: Mutilation (rhinotomy / cutting off noses) and flogging for accessories, informants, and accomplices. Text continues syntactically across the physical folio turn onto leaf p1376 (fol. 680v).
[^4285]: Folio 680v header: *ѡ҆ въсхища́ющихъ* (*Concerning Abductors:*). Amputation of the hand (*роука̀ да оусѣче́тсѧ*) for unarmed abduction, and flogging, tonsure, and exile for accomplices.
[^4286]: Imprisonment for parents who collude in or accept the forced marriage of their abducted daughter; burning at the stake (*да и҆жже́жетсѧ*) for slaves assisting in the abduction.
[^4287]: Absolute civil nullity of marriage between an abductor and his victim, even if the father grants retrospective consent.
[^4288]: Legal prohibition against the victim marrying her kidnapper; parents who subsequently acquiesce are committed to prison. Text continues syntactically across the physical folio turn onto leaf p1377 (fol. 681r).
[^4289]: Folio 681r header: *х҃па • на бра́къ ж[ены̀]* (*...for Marriage of a Woman*).
[^4290]: Juridical distinction between violent abduction (*похище́нїе*, which is unpardonable) and forceful defloration (*понꙋ́ди растли́въ дв҃цꙋ*, which permits subsequent marriage if the violated maiden chooses to grant forgiveness).
[^4291]: Civil forfeiture: an abducted maiden who refuses to marry her kidnapper receives his property; refusal to enforce this results in total confiscation of both parties' assets to the fiscus.
[^4292]: Statutory grading of the crime: abduction is classified as an offense more heinous than adultery (*вѧ́щше є҆́стъ прелюбодѣ́йства*). Text continues syntactically across the physical folio turn onto leaf p1378 (fol. 681v).
[^4293]: Folio 681v header: *ѡ҆ въсхища́ющи на бра́[къ жены̀]* (*Concerning Those Abducting for Marriage*). Capital punishment for abducting a married woman or virgin, regardless of private forgiveness; anyone may act as public prosecutor.
[^4294]: Manumission granted to any slave who reveals a bride abduction or exposes a concealed crime.
[^4295]: Special rule concerning a slave who abducts or harbours a female slave who is a harlot: exempted from capital penalties for kidnapping or corruption, but fined by the ruler and subject to disciplinary chastisement.
[^4296]: Reference to Novel 17 of Emperor Justinian (*Седмьна́десѧтлаа же і҆ꙋстинїа́нова но́вла*): denial of the right of church asylum / sanctuary to abductors of maidens, permitting their arrest and binding even during Holy Pascha.
