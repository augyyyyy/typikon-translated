# PART XXXVII: CONCERNING STAUROPEGIA [^2962]

*A Stauropegion takes place thus:*

*The Patriarch is notified concerning the erection of a temple. And there is an authoritative writing, either unto his Exarch, or unto one of the hierarchs, for its foundation, consecration, and completion of the temple, regarding patriarchal stauropegia.*

*And he commands also that there be a wooden Cross, upon which the patriarchal scribes write; on the one side thus:*[^2963]

**"Patriarchal Stauropegion, consecrated in the name of Saint [Name], in the God-saved city of [Name] in the eparchy of [Name], by the personal will of [Name], Most Holy Ecumenical Patriarch."**

*And on the other side:*[^2964]

**"In the reign of our most pious Tsars [Names], in the month [such and such], in the year [such and such]."**

*And the Cross is sent from the Patriarch, by virtue of which the temple is to be erected.*

*Then the hierarch, having received it, performs the above-written office at the foundation.*

*And when he performs the consecration, he also sets up the Cross behind the Holy Table, upon a certain high, adorned structure having a receptacle, either of stone, or of iron, or of bronze, as is visible in the Great"*[^2965] *(Rubric continued)* ...Church.[^2966]

*Prayer over the Stauropegion:*

**Priest:** **"O Lord God Almighty, Who didst prefigure of old by the rod of Moses the precious and life-creating Cross of Thy beloved Son, our Lord Jesus Christ: do Thou Thyself bless and sanctify this place by the power and operation of the precious, life-creating, and all-honorable wood of the Cross, unto the driving away of demons and of every adversarial power; preserving both this place and this house, and the souls of those who dwell here; through the prayers of our most glorious Lady, the Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary. For blessed and glorified is Thy kingdom, of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages."**

[Fol. 489v. Blank leaf without print (vacat), concluding Gathering (*ѡ̃ ѡ̃*) and the liturgical section on church foundation, the Precious Cross, and patriarchal stauropegia. It preserves later 17th-century manuscript ownership and donation inscriptions in East Slavic cursive (skoropis'): "This book, called the Sluzhebnik... monastery..."][^2967] [Fol. 490r. Woodcut headpiece at the head of the division: Two Renaissance floral vases with scrolling acanthus ornament, flanking the divisional heading][^2968]

### Notes

[^2962]: Division title: *On Stauropegia* (`Ѡ҆ СТАѪРОПИГІ́АХЪ` / Greek: *Περὶ σταυροπηγίων*). This canonical chapter codifies the juridical and liturgical procedure for establishing a patriarchal stauropegion—a monastery or church enjoying extraterritorial immunity from the local diocesan bishop and placed directly under the jurisdiction of the Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople. In the late 16th and early 17th centuries, the status of stauropegion was of vital ecclesiological importance in the Ruthenian Church (e.g. the Lviv Dormition Brotherhood).
[^2963]: Inscriptional protocol: the Patriarch sends an official decree authorizing his Exarch or a designated hierarch to lay the foundation and consecrate the church, accompanied by a wooden patriarchal cross inscribed on both faces by patriarchal scribes.
[^2964]: Reverse inscriptional formula: the inscription on the reverse face records the civil and imperial dating formula (`При црⷭтвѣ̀ бл҃гочти́вѣйшихъ цр҃ехъ на́шихъ и҆́мѧ ре́къ...`) alongside the day, month, and year.
[^2965]: Rubric: at the consecration of a stauropegial church, the patriarchal cross is erected directly behind the Holy Table (`въдрꙋжа́етъ и҆ крⷭтъ съза́ди ст҃ыѧ трапе́зы`) within an elevated, ornamental structure or holder made of stone, iron, or bronze, explicitly modeled after the liturgical arrangement in the Great Church of Constantinople (Hagia Sophia).
[^2966]: Conclusion of the rubric from leaf p1006 (`...въ велицѣй цр҃кви`).
[^2967]: Codicological note: physical folio 489v (leaf p1008) is an unprinted blank verso (*vacat*) concluding Gathering `ѡ̃ ѡ̃` (Gathering 52) of the 1606 imprint. In this exemplar, later 17th-century East Slavic cursive (*skoropis'*) hands inscribed ownership and donation records: *Сїа Книга глаголемая Служебникъ... монастыра...* ("This book, called the Sluzhebnik... of the monastery...").
[^2968]: Gathering mark and headpiece: leaf p1009 marks the start of Gathering 53 (`цц` / *Ts ts*, fol. 490r), embellished with an elaborate Renaissance headpiece featuring two vases brimming with flowers and scrolling acanthus foliage.
