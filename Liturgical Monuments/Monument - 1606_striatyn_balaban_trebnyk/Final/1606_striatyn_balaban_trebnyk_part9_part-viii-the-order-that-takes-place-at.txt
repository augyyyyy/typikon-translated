# PART VIII: THE ORDER THAT TAKES PLACE AT BETROTHAL [^183]

*After the Divine Liturgy, the priest standing in the sanctuary, those desiring to be joined together shall stand before the holy doors; the man indeed on the right, and the woman on the left. Their two rings are placed on the right side of the holy table: the golden one first, [and the] silver [one]; the silver leaning toward the right, and the golden toward the left, near one another. And the priest signs the heads of the newly betrothed thrice. And he gives unto them lighted tapers; and having led them within into the church, he censes cruciformly. And it is said by the deacon:*[^184]

**Deacon:**
Bless, Master.

**Priest:**
Blessed is our God always, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

**Choir:**
Amen.

**Deacon:**
In peace let us pray unto the Lord.

For the peace from above, and for the salvation of our souls, let us pray unto the Lord.

For the peace of the whole world, for the good estate of the holy churches of God, and for the union of all, let us pray unto the Lord.

For this holy house, and for those who enter with faith, reverence, and the fear of God, let us pray unto the Lord.

For our Archbishop, *[Name]*, for the venerable priesthood, the diaconate in Christ, for all the clergy and the people, let us pray unto the Lord.

For the servants of God, *[Name]* and *[Name]*, who now betroth themselves one unto another, and for their salvation, let us pray unto the Lord.[^185]

That there may be granted unto them children for the succession of the race, and all petitions unto salvation, let us pray unto the Lord.

That there may be sent down upon them perfect, peaceful love, and help, let us pray unto the Lord.

That they may be blessed in unity, and steadfast faith, let us pray unto the Lord.

That they may be preserved in blameless conduct and manner of life, let us pray unto the Lord.

That the Lord our God may grant unto them an honorable marriage, and a bed undefiled, let us pray unto the Lord.[^186]

For our deliverance from all tribulation, wrath, and necessity, let us pray unto the Lord.

Help us, save us, have mercy on us, and keep us, O God, by Thy grace.

Commemorating our all-holy, immaculate, most blessed and glorious Lady, the Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary, with all the saints, let us commit ourselves and one another, and our whole life unto Christ our God.

[Choir: Unto Thee, O Lord.]

*Ekphonesis:*

**Priest:**
For unto Thee is due all glory, honor, and worship: to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, [now and ever, and unto ages of ages.]

[Choir: Amen.]

*The priest says the prayer aloud.*

**Deacon:**
Let us pray unto the Lord.

**Priest:**
[Fol. 55r. Decorative woodcut initial B.]
O Eternal God, Who hast brought together into unity the things that were divided, and hast ordained for them an indissoluble bond of love; Who didst bless Isaac and Rebecca, and didst show them forth to be the heirs of Thy promise: do Thou Thyself bless these Thy servants also, guiding them into every good work.[^187]

*Ekphonesis:*

For a merciful God art Thou, and the Lover of mankind, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

**Choir:**
Amen.

**Priest:**
Peace be unto all.

**Deacon:**
Bow your heads unto the Lord.

**Priest:**
[Fol. 55v. Decorative woodcut initial G.]
O Lord our God, Who from among the Gentiles hast pre-espoused the Church as a pure virgin: bless this betrothal, and unite and preserve these Thy servants in peace and oneness of mind.

*Ekphonesis:*

For unto Thee is due all glory, honor, and worship: to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

**Choir:**
Amen.

*Then the priest, having taken the rings, gives first to the man the golden one; afterwards the silver one to the woman. And giving [it] to the man, he says:*[^188]

**Priest:**
The servant of God, *[Name]*, is betrothed unto the handmaid of God, *[Name]*, in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

*After this, he says also unto the woman:*

**Priest:**
The handmaid of God, *[Name]*, is betrothed unto the servant of God, *[Name]*, in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*And when he has said this unto each thrice, he makes the sign of the cross with the ring over their heads; and he places them upon their right fingers. Afterwards, the sponsor (kum), or the friend of the bridegroom, exchanges the rings of the newly betrothed. And the priest says the prayer:*[^189]

**Deacon:**
Let us pray unto the Lord.

**Priest:**
[Fol. 56r. Decorative woodcut initial G.]
O Lord our God, Who didst journey with the servant of the patriarch Abraham into Mesopotamia, when he was sent to betroth a wife for his master Isaac; and didst reveal unto him through the mediation of the water pitcher to betroth Rebecca: do Thou Thyself bless the betrothal of these Thy servants, *[Name]* and *[Name]*, and confirm the word spoken by them; establish them in the holy union that is from Thee.[^190] For Thou from the beginning didst create male and female, and by Thee is a wife betrothed unto a husband for help, and for the succession of the human race. Do Thou Thyself therefore, O Lord our God, Who hast sent forth truth unto Thine inheritance, and Thy promise unto Thy servants our fathers, Thine elect in every generation: look down upon Thy servant, *[Name]*, and upon Thy handmaid, *[Name]*, and confirm their betrothal in faith, and in unity, and in truth, and in love. For Thou, O Lord, didst show that betrothal is to be given, and to be confirmed in all things: by a ring was authority given unto Joseph in Egypt;[^191] by a ring was Daniel glorified in the land of Babylon; by a ring was the truth of Tamar made manifest; by a ring our Heavenly Father was compassionate toward His son, for He said: 'Put a ring upon his right hand, and having slain the fatted calf, let us eat and make merry.' This Thy right hand, O Lord, armed Moses in the Red Sea; for by Thy true word the heavens were established, and the earth founded. And the right hand of Thy servants shall be blessed by Thy mighty word, and by Thine exalted arm.[^192] Do Thou Thyself therefore, even now, O Master, bless this putting on of the rings with a heavenly blessing; and may the angel of the Lord go before them all the days of their life. For Thou art He Who blesses and sanctifies all things, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^193]

*Then the litany:*

**Deacon:**
Have mercy on us, O God, according to Thy great mercy, we pray Thee, give ear and have mercy.

**Choir:**
Lord, have mercy. *(Thrice)*

**Deacon:**
Again let us pray for our bishop, *[Name]*.

**Choir:**
Lord, have mercy. *(Thrice)*

**Deacon:**
Again let us pray for the servants of God, *[Name]* [and *Name*], who are betrothed unto one another, let us all say:

**Deacon:**
Again let us pray for all our brotherhood, and for all Christians.

**Choir:**
Lord, have mercy. *(Thrice)*

**Priest:**
*Exclamation:*
For Thou art a merciful God and the Lover of mankind, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

**Choir:**
Amen.

*Then the dismissal:*[^194]

[Fol. 58r. Tailpiece: Symmetrical Renaissance foliate woodcut ornament with four-petaled central rosette and acanthus flourishes.]

[Fol. 58v. Headpiece: Monumental woodcut engraving depicting the Mystery of Crowning in church: the celebrant under an architectural ciborium blesses the bridal couple before the tetrapod in the presence of sponsors, surmounted by two winged angels bearing wreaths within a medallion flanked by foliate urns.]

### Notes

[^183]: Fol. 53r. Part VIII: The Office of Betrothal, the Royal Doors, and the Gold and Silver Rings on the Altar (*ПОСЛѢ́ДОВА́НЇЕ БЫВА́ЄМОЕ Ѡ҆ БРꙊЧЕ́НЇЮ*). In the 1606 Striatyn Trebnyk, Betrothal (*Обруче́нїе* / ἀρραβών) is preserved as a distinct liturgical office conducted immediately following the Divine Liturgy (*По Бж҃тве́нои литꙋргі́и*). The bridal pair stands outside the iconostasis before the Royal Doors (*предъ ст҃ы́ми двьрьмѝ*), the bridegroom on the right and the bride on the left. The two rings—one gold and one silver—are placed upon the right side of the holy altar table (*ст҃а́ѧ трапе́ꙁа*). The Ruthenian text specifies: *зла́тыи перше́ный, сре́брный ѹ҆кланѧ́ѧсѧ къ де́снымъ, зла́тыи же къ шꙋ́и̑мъ* ("the golden one first, [and the] silver [one]; the silver leaning toward the right, and the golden toward the left, near one another"). In Byzantine and Ruthenian mystagogy (commented upon by St. Symeon of Thessalonica, *De Sacramentis* 273), the gold ring signifies the solar brilliance of Christ and the authority of the husband, while the silver ring signifies the lunar radiance of the Church and the spouse; their proximity on the altar sanctifies their impending exchange and marital bond.
[^184]: Fol. 53v. The Signing of the Bridal Pair, Lighted Tapers, Entrance, and Initial Blessing (*зна́менаетъ гла́вы новобра́чныхъ г҃. и҆ да́стъ и҆̀мъ свѣ́ща въжже́ны...*). The priest blesses the bridal pair by making the sign of the cross thrice over their heads and gives them lighted tapers, symbolizing the lamps of the wise virgins awaiting the Heavenly Bridegroom (Matthew 25:1–13) and the spiritual joy and purity of Christian union. The rubric directs him to lead them within the nave of the church (*вну́тръ въ цр҃ковь*) and cense cruciformly. The deacon petitions: "Bless, Master" (*Бл҃ви влⷣко*), and the priest initiates the rite with the euchological doxology: "Blessed is our God always, now and ever, and unto ages of ages" (*Бл҃ве́нъ Бг҃ъ на́шъ...*), followed immediately by the Great Litany (*Ми́ромъ Гдⷭ҇у помоли́мсѧ*).
[^185]: Fol. 54r. Special Petitions of the Betrothal Litany (*Ѡ҆ рабѣ́хъ Бж҃їихъ, и҆́мѧ ре́къ, и҆́же нн҃ѣ ѡ҆брꙋча́ющихсѧ дроу́гъ дроу́гꙋ...*). The Great Litany is enriched with specific euchological petitions for the betrothed couple, faithfully translating the Byzantine Greek Euchologion (*ὑπὲρ τῶν δούλων τοῦ Θεοῦ τῶν νῦν ἀρραβωνιζομένων ἀλλήλοις*): praying for their mutual salvation; for the granting of children for the succession of the human race (*ча́да къ воспрїѧ́тїе ро́да* / Greek: εἰς διαδοχὴν γένους); for the sending down upon them of perfect and peaceful love and divine assistance (*любвѝ съвръше́ннѣй ми́рнѣй, и҆ по́мощи*); and for their blessing in conjugal unity.
[^186]: Fol. 54v. The Petition for an Honorable Marriage and Undefiled Bed (*Ꙗ҆́ко да̀ Гдⷭ҇ь Бг҃ъ на́шъ да́рꙋетъ и҆̀мъ бра́къ че́стенъ, и҆ ло́же несквръ́но...*). The litany weaves the apostolic injunction of Hebrews 13:4 ("Marriage is honorable in all, and the bed undefiled") directly into the liturgical petition, praying that the Lord God grant them an honorable marriage and an undefiled bed, and that they may be preserved in blameless conduct (*непоро́чное пребыва́нїе*) and unwavering faith (*твръ́даѧ вѣ́ра*).
[^187]: Fol. 55r. The First Euchological Prayer of Betrothal: Isaac and Rebecca (*Бж҃е вѣ́чный, и҆́же раздѣле́ннаѧ събра́въ въ є҆ди́нство...*). This ancient euchological prayer (Greek: Ὁ Θεὸς ὁ αἰώνιος, ὁ τὰ διῃρημένα συναγαγὼν εἰς ἑνότητα) invokes the biblical archetype of Isaac and Rebecca (Genesis 24) as the historical pattern of divinely guided marital union and inheritance of the covenant promise (*наслѣ́дники тѣ́хъ свое́му ѡ҆бѣтова́нїю*). God is addressed as the One Who establishes an indissoluble bond of love (*сою́ꙁъ любвѐ положи́въ нераздрꙋши́мъ*), gathering together disparate persons into ontological conjugal unity.
[^188]: Fol. 55v. The Bridal Ecclesiology of the Church and the Bestowal of the Rings (*и҆́же ѿ ꙗ҆ꙁы́къ пре́ѡ҆броучи́въ цр҃ковь дв҃оу чи́стоую...*). The second prayer roots Christian betrothal in Pauline bridal ecclesiology (2 Corinthians 11:2: "For I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Christ"), confessing Christ as having pre-espoused the Church from among the Gentiles. The priest takes the rings from the altar table and bestows the gold ring first upon the bridegroom and then the silver ring upon the bride, pronouncing the sacramental declaration: *Ѡ҆броуча́етъ ра́бъ Бж҃їи...* ("The servant of God is betrothed unto the handmaid of God...").
[^189]: Fol. 56r. The Threefold Cross with the Rings, the Sponsor (*Kum*), and the Friend of the Bridegroom (*по́семъ и҆ꙁмѣнѧ́етъ пе́рстени новобра́чныхъ, кꙋ́мъ, и҆лѝ дрꙋ́гъ женихо́въ...*). The rubric specifies that the priest makes the sign of the cross thrice with the ring over the head of each candidate before placing it upon their right hands (*на де́сныхъ и҆́хъ пе́рстѣхъ*). The subsequent threefold exchange of rings is performed not by the priest, but by the sponsor (*коу́мъ* / *kum*) or the best man / friend of the bridegroom (*дрꙋ́гъ женихо́въ*, rendering the Greek παράνυμφος), reflecting the ancient customary participation of the wedding sponsor in sealing the covenant.
[^190]: Fol. 56v. Eliezer's Mission in Mesopotamia and the Water Pitcher Sign (*ходата́йствомъ водоно́са ѡ҆броучи́ти реве́коу ѿкры́въ...*). The lengthy final prayer of betrothal (Greek: Κύριε ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν, ὁ τῷ παιδὶ τοῦ πατριάρχου Ἀβραὰμ συμπορευθεὶς ἐν τῇ Μεσοποταμίᾳ) recalls the mission of Abraham's servant Eliezer to Haran in Mesopotamia (Genesis 24). Eliezer prayed for a divine sign at the well of water: that the maiden who offered to draw water from her pitcher (*водоно́съ* / ὑδρία) for him and his camels would be the bride appointed by God for Isaac. The prayer proclaims God as the Creator of male and female (Genesis 1:27, Matthew 19:4), establishing woman as a helper unto man (Genesis 2:18) and for the continuation of mankind (*прїѧ́тїе ро́да чл҃ча* / διαδοχὴν τοῦ γένους τῶν ἀνθρώπων).
[^191]: Fol. 57r. Biblical Typology of the Ring: Joseph, Daniel, Tamar, and the Prodigal Son (*пе́рстенѧ ра́ди да́стьсѧ вла́сть і҆ѡ́сифоу въ є҆гѵ́птѣ...*). This euchological prayer marshals a sequence of Old and New Testament precedents illustrating the signet ring (*пе́рстень* / δακτύλιος) as the divine token of authority, vindication, covenant fidelity, and paternal adoption: Pharaoh investing Joseph with his signet ring as viceroy of Egypt (Genesis 41:42); the sealing of the lions' den and Daniel's deliverance in Babylon (Daniel 6:17, Bel and the Dragon 14:11–14); Tamar's vindication before Judah by means of his signet ring, cord, and staff (Genesis 38:18, 25); and the father bestowing the signet ring upon the repentant prodigal son as the restoration of sonship (Luke 15:22). The prayer links these historical tokens to the cosmic covenant established by the right hand of God, which divided the Red Sea under Moses (Exodus 15:6) and founded the heavens by the Word of Truth (Psalm 32:6 LXX [MT 33:6]).
[^192]: Fol. 57v. The Cosmic Foundation and the Angelic Guide of the Espoused (*землѧ̀ ѡ҆сно́вана... и҆ а҆́гг҃лъ Гдⷭ҇нь да пре́йдетъ пре́д ними...*). Continuing the betrothal prayer (Greek: Ὁ Θεὸς ὁ αἰώνιος...), the euchology extends God's cosmic ordering—the establishment of the heavens and foundation of the earth (Psalm 103:5 LXX [MT 104:5])—unto the micro-cosmos of the Christian home. The priest petitions for the angel of the Lord to precede the couple as their guardian and companion throughout all the days of their earthly life, evoking the angel who guided Eliezer (Genesis 24:7) and Raphael who accompanied Tobias (Tobit 5:16–22).
[^193]: Fol. 57v. The Betrothal Exclamation and Insistent Litany (*Та́же є҆кте́нїа: Поми́лꙋй на́съ Бж҃е...*). Following the euchological doxology ("For Thou art He Who blesses and sanctifies all things... unto ages of ages"), the deacon intones the Insistent Litany (*Сугубая ектения* / ἐκτενὴς ἱκεσία). In the 1606 Striatyn imprint, the litany contains specific petitions for the ruling bishop and for the newly betrothed couple (*ѡ҆ рабѣ́хъ Бж҃їихъ... ѡ҆броуча́ющихсѧ дроу́гъ дроу́гоу*), culminating in the communal petition *реце́мъ всѝ* ("let us all say").
[^194]: Fol. 58r. Autonomous Betrothal Dismissal and Structural Independence of the Rite (*Та́же ѿпоу́стъ*). In early Byzantine and 16th–17th century Ruthenian liturgical practice, Betrothal (*Обруче́нїе*) frequently remained an autonomous liturgical office celebrated weeks or months prior to the solemn Crowning (*Вѣнча́нїе*), rather than immediately conflated with it as in standard modern practice. The rubric *Та́же ѿпоу́стъ* ("Then the dismissal") followed by an ornamental tailpiece confirms the completion of Betrothal as a self-contained liturgical unit.
