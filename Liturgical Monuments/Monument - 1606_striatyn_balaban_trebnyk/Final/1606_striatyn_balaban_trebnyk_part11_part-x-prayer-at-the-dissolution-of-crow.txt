# PART X: PRAYER AT THE DISSOLUTION OF CROWNS ON THE EIGHTH DAY [^243]

[Fol. 71v. Initial G.]
**Priest:**
O Lord our God, Who hast blessed the crown of the year, and didst deliver these crowns to be set upon those who by the law of marriage are joined one to another, as it were giving unto them the reward of chastity because they were joined in pureness unto the marriage ordained by Thee: Do Thou Thyself, in the dissolution also of these crowns, bless those who have been joined one to another; and preserve their union unsevered, that they may ever give thanks unto Thine all-holy name, of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^244]

**Deacon:**
*Bow your heads unto the Lord.*

[Fol. 72r. Initial S.]
**Priest:**
*Prayer of Head-Bowing:*
Having come together in concord, Thy servants, O Lord, and having completed the rite of the marriage that was in Cana of Galilee, and having folded away the tokens that were therein, send up glory unto Thee, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*And the dismissal takes place.*

[Fol. 72v. Headpiece: Woodcut engraving with foliate strapwork, acanthus, and mythological figures flanking a central cartouche.]

### Notes

[^243]: Fol. 71v. The Office of the Dissolution of Crowns on the Eighth Day (*Мл҃тва на раздрѣше́нїе вѣнце́въ, въ ѡ́смыи дн҃ь*). Greek: *Εὐχὴ ἐπὶ ἀπολύσει στεφάνων εἰς τὴν ὀγδόην ἡμέραν*. In ancient Byzantine usage, the bridegroom and bride wore their wedding crowns for seven days following the nuptial mystery, returning to the church on the eighth day for a solemn rite of uncrowning and prayer before returning to daily secular life. While in later practice the crowns were removed at the end of the Crowning service, the prayer on the eighth day was retained in the euchologies. The opening words of the prayer evoke Psalm 64:12 LXX [MT 65:11] ("Thou shalt bless the crown of the year of Thy goodness").
[^244]: Fol. 72r. The Conclusion of the Prayer of the Dissolution of Crowns and the Prayer of Head-Bowing (*Съгла́снѣ прише́дше рабѝ твоѝ Гдⷭ҇и...* / *Εἰς συμφωνίαν ἐλθόντες...*). Explaining the Greek phrase *τὰ ἐν αὐτῷ σύμβολα συστείλαντες* rendered into Church Slavonic as *съпрѧта́вше ꙗ҆́же въ не́мъ знаме́нїа* ("having folded away / stowed the tokens that were therein"). In Byzantine and Ruthenian euchological practice, this refers to the formal ceremonial deposition and stowing of the bridal crowns, the common cup, and the nuptial ribbons, marking the liturgical termination of the marriage celebrations and the return of the couple to ordinary domestic life under divine protection.
