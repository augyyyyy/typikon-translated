# PART VII: THE ORDER OF HOW IT IS BEFITTING TO RECEIVE THOSE COMING FROM THE LATINS UNTO OUR ORTHODOX FAITH [^174]

*He is placed before the holy sanctuary.*

*The priest having made the beginning:*

**Priest:**
Blessed is our God...

*Then, the Trisagion [prayers]. And the Great Litany. Ekphonesis:*

**Priest:**
For unto Thee is due all glory, [honor, and worship: to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Choir: Amen.]

*Afterwards the priest says unto him:*

**Priest:**
[Fol. 49v. Decorative cinnabar initial O.]
Dost thou renounce the Latin faith, and their Sabbath-fasting, which Peter the Stammerer established in Rome?[^175]

*And he says:*

**Penitent:**
[Fol. 50r. Decorative cinnabar initial O.]
I renounce the Latin faith, and their Sabbath-fasting, and all Latin teaching, and their unleavened bread. And I believe in the holy, consubstantial Trinity, according to the good pleasure of God.

*The priest says: "I believe in one God...", thrice; verse by verse, all unto the end. And he repeats after him.*

*After this, the priest says this prayer:*

**Priest:**
[Fol. 50r. Decorative cinnabar initial B.]
O God our Savior, Who willest all men to be saved...

*Look back: turn two leaves (see fol. 48r)[^176] and thou shalt find it; read unto the end.*

*And after the prayer he gives him holy water, and washes him upon the face and upon the hands. And he anoints him with holy myron as a newly baptized person.*[^177]

*And after the anointing with myron, he says over him this prayer:*

**Priest:**
[Fol. 50v. Decorative cinnabar initial G.]
O Lord our God, Who hast deigned to show forth this Thy servant, *[Name]*, perfect, through right faith which is toward Thee, and through the seal of Thy holy myron: do Thou, Master of all, preserve in him the true faith which is toward Thee; and nurture him in righteousness, and adorn him with all Thy gifts. Always, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*And having received [chrismation], he receives the commandment not to eat meat for seven days. And on the eighth day let him be washed in a clean place, so that that water be not trodden underfoot.*[^178] *Then, the Trisagion [prayers]. And Psalm 22:*[^179]

**"The Lord is my shepherd,"** *all unto the end.*

*THEN THIS PRAYER:*

**Priest:**
[Fol. 51r. Decorative woodcut initial I.]
[O Thou] Who hast granted unto Thy servant deliverance of sins by reason of holy baptism, and hast bestowed upon him renewal of life: do Thou Thyself, Master and Lord, ever deign to make the illumination of Thy countenance to shine in his heart. Preserve the strength of his faith unassailed by enemies. Guard pure and blameless in him the incorruption of the garments wherewith he has been clothed, preserving unbroken in him the spiritual seal by Thy grace; and be merciful unto him and unto us, according to the multitude of Thy compassions.[^180]

*Ekphonesis:*

**Priest:**
For blessed and glorified is Thine all-honorable and majestic name, of the Father, and of the Son...

**Priest:**
...and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*And the priest, having taken warm water and a sponge, washes him where he anointed him with holy myron, saying:*[^181]

**Priest:**
Thou art baptized. Thou art illumined.  
Thou art washed. Thou art sanctified:  
in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.  

**Priest:**
Peace be unto all.

**Deacon:**
Bow your heads unto the Lord.

*The priest says the prayer secretly:*

**Priest:**
[Fol. 51v. Decorative woodcut initial I.]
[He] that has put on Thee, Christ our God, has bowed his head with us unto Thee. Preserve him to abide an unconquered champion against those who vainly [wage] enmity wage warfare against him and against us; and by Thine incorruptible crown, show him forth even unto the end a conqueror of all.

*Ekphonesis:*

**Priest:**
For Thine it is to have mercy and to save us, [O Christ] our God, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

[Choir: Amen.]

*And he imparts unto him the Holy Mysteries.*[^182]

*Then the litany,*

*and the dismissal.*

[Fol. 52r. Tailpiece: Renaissance woodcut ornament with grotesque mask and scrollwork.]

[Fol. 52v. Blank leaf at the conclusion of Gathering 7 (ж), concluding the Rite of Reception of Latins.]

[Fol. 53r. Headpiece: Monumental woodcut engraving depicting Christ Pantocrator / Great High Priest in a medallion flanked by angels amidst foliate and pomegranate rinceaux.]

### Notes

[^174]: Fol. 49v. Part VII: The Rite for the Reception of Latins in the 1606 Striatyn Trebnyk (*ЧИ́НЪ КА́КО ПОДОБА́ЄТ ПРЇИМА́ТИ приходѧ́щихъ ѿ лати́нъ...*). Part VII witnesses the official liturgical rite of the Ruthenian Orthodox Church under the jurisdiction of the Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople for receiving Roman Catholics into Orthodoxy following the Council of Brest (1596). The Balaban print strictly adheres to the decisions of the Council of Constantinople (1484), which repealed the Union of Florence and decreed that Latins are received through renunciation of errors, anointing with holy chrism (*myron*), and holy communion, rather than through re-baptism. This practice stood in contrast both to the late-Byzantine rigorism of the 13th–14th centuries and to the later 1620 Moscow Council of Patriarch Filaret Romanov, which mandated re-baptism of Latins until the Pan-Orthodox Synod of Moscow in 1667 formally restored reception by Chrismation.
[^175]: Fol. 49v. The Renunciation of Latin Sabbath-Fasting and "Peter the Stammerer" (*Ѡ҆рица́ешилисѧ вѣ́ры лати́ньскыѧ, и҆ сꙋбо́тства и҆́хъ, є҆́же Пе́тръ гоугни́выи въ ри́мѣ оу҆ста́вилъ є҆́стъ*). The abjuration singled out by the opening interrogation focuses on two traditional Byzantine grievances: (1) Latin fasting on Saturdays (*сꙋбо́тство* / Sabbath-fasting), prohibited by Apostolic Canon 64 and Trullan Canon 55; and (2) the polemical figure of "Peter the Stammerer" (*Пе́тръ гоугни́выи* / *Petr Gugnivyi*). In medieval East Slavic and Ruthenian polemical literature—most notably the popular 14th–16th century *Tale of Peter the Stammerer* (*Сказание о Петре Гугнивом*), frequently interpolated into the Slavonic *Kormchaia Kniga* (Nomokanon)—the historical divergence of the Latin Church was mythologized as having been instituted by an alleged Roman bishop or cleric named Peter the Stammerer, who was said to have corrupted Roman liturgical traditions after the severance of communion. Modern historical scholarship identifies this epithet as a polemical transposition and conflation of Peter the Fuller (*Petros Gnapheus*) or Peter Mongus (*Petros Moggos*, literally "the stammerer" or "hoarse-voiced") into Roman church history.
[^176]: Fol. 50r. Internal Folio Cross-Reference to the Reception Prayer (*Смотрѝ наза́дъ ѡ҆братѝ два̀ листы ѡ҆брѧ́щеши...*). The rubric instructs the priest to turn back two physical leaves to Fol. 48r (leaf p113), where the euchological prayer of reconciliation (*"O God our Savior, Who willest all men to be saved..."*) is printed in full. This cross-referencing practice illustrates the typographer's economy of space in the 1606 Balaban print, avoiding the duplicate typesetting of lengthy prayers common to both the reconciliation of Non-Chalcedonians and that of Western Christians.
[^177]: Fol. 50r. Pre-Chrismation Ablution with Holy Water (*И҆ по мл҃твѣ да́сть є҆мꙋ̀ во́дꙋ ст҃ще́ннꙋ. И҆ ѻ҆мы́етъ и҆̀ по лицоу̀ и҆ по роука́ма...*). Prior to anointing with holy chrism, the candidate's face and hands are washed with sanctified water (*во́да свѧще́ннаѧ* / *hagiasma*). This symbolic purification signifies the washing away of Latin ritual customs and heterodox communion, serving as an outward lustrative preparation before receiving the unction of holy myron (*му́ромъ сты́мъ ꙗ҆́ко новокрще́ннаго*) as an illuminated member of the Orthodox Church.
[^178]: Fol. 50v. The Sanctity of the Ablution Water and Prohibition against Desecration (*да̀ ѻ҆мы́етсѧ на чи́стѣ мѣ́стѣ, ꙗ҆́ко ненастоупи́тисѧ водѣ̀ то́й*). The rubric mandates that on the eighth day the post-chrismation ablution must take place in an untrodden, clean place (*на чи́стѣ мѣ́стѣ*) to ensure that the water containing dissolved holy chrism is not stepped on (*ненастоупи́тисѧ водѣ̀ то́й*). In canonical Orthodox pastoral practice, this ablution water was poured directly into the *thalassidion* (piscina / sacrarium) of the church altar or into a dry well or under the roots of a fruit-bearing tree, guarding the sanctified chrism from profanation.
[^179]: Fol. 50v. Psalm 22 (LXX 22 / MT 23) as the Mystagogical Octave Psalm (*ѱало́мъ, к҃в. Гдⷭ҇ь пасе́тъ мѧ̀...*). The recitation of Psalm 22 according to Septuagint versification (*"The Lord shepherds me, and nothing shall fail me"*, LXX Psalm 22 [MT Psalm 23]) forms an integral part of the early Byzantine post-baptismal octave mystagogy. Commented upon by St. Cyril of Jerusalem in his *Mystagogical Catecheses* (Catechesis IV), the psalm prophetically details the sacramental journey of the neophyte: the green pasture and water of rest (baptism), the oil anointing the head (chrismation), and the prepared table and cup of unmixed wine (the Holy Eucharist).
[^180]: Fol. 51r. The Eighth-Day Ablution Prayer (*[И҆́]же и҆збавле́нїе съгрѣше́нїемъ ра́ди ст҃го кр҃ще́нїа рабꙋ̀ твое́моу дарова́въ...*). This prayer adapts the ancient eighth-day euchology of post-baptismal ablution found in the Barberini Euchologion (gr. 336) and early Slavonic Trebnyks for use over a reconciled Latin convert. It celebrates the convert's remission of sins (*и҆збавле́нїе съгрѣше́нїемъ*), petitions for the illumination of his heart with the light of the divine countenance (Psalm 4:7 / 66:2), and prays for the preservation of the incorrupt baptismal robe (*нетлѣ́нїе ѻ҆дѣѧ́нїа*) and the intact seal of the Holy Spirit (*нероуши́ма дх҃овнаѧ печа́ть*).
[^181]: Fol. 51v. The Ritual Ablution Formula and Head-Bowing Prayer (*Кр҃ти́лсѧ є҆сѝ. Просвѣти́лсѧ є҆сѝ. Ѻ҆мы́лсѧ є҆сѝ. Ѻ҆с͠ти́лсѧ є҆сѝ...*). The priest performs the ritual ablution of the holy chrism using warm water and a sponge (*гоу́ба* / Greek: σπόγγος), wiping the loci of anointing while reciting the ancient quadruplicate baptismal declaration: "Thou art baptized. Thou art illumined. Thou art washed. Thou art sanctified..." (cf. 1 Corinthians 6:11: "but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God"). This is immediately followed by the liturgical greeting of peace and the silent inclination prayer (*мл҃тва та́йнѡ* / *εὐχὴ κеклиμένη*), which echoes Galatians 3:27 (*"For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ"*) and petitions for the convert's victory as an "unconquered champion" against the spiritual adversary.
[^182]: Fol. 52r. The Communion of the Reconciled Latin Penitent and Concluding Rites of Part VII (*и҆ причаща́етъ є҆го̀ ст҃ы́хъ та́инъ...*). The reception of Roman Catholics culminates in their immediate admission to the Holy Mysteries (*прича́стїе ст҃ы́хъ та́инъ*), following the ancient canonical principle articulated by Canon 95 of the Council in Trullo (692) and confirmed by the Council of Constantinople (1484). Having abjured Latin liturgical divergence, confessed the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed without the *Filioque*, and received the unction of holy chrism (*myron*), the penitent is admitted to full Eucharistic fellowship, followed by the concluding litany and dismissal (*ѿпоу́стъ*).
