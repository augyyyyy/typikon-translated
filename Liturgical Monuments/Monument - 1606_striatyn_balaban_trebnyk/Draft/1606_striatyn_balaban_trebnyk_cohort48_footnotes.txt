[^1402]: Folio 227r (physical page 471) concluding the prayer at the departure of the soul. Allusion to Psalm 145:7-8 (LXX 145:7-8): "The Lord looseth them that are fettered; the Lord setteth upright them that are crushed" (Κύριος λύει πεπεδημένους, Κύριος ἀνορθοῖ καтерραγμένους / "Господь рѣшитъ окованныя... возводитъ низверженныя"). [Masoretic: Ps 146:7-8]. The phrase "hope of the hopeless" (наде́жа ненача́ннымъ) translates the Greek ἀνελπίστων ἐλπίς; the 1606 text reads `ненача́ннымъ`, an attested Ruthenian variant/phonetic spelling for `нечая́ннымъ` (those without hope or expectation).
[^1403]: Cinnabar rubrical abbreviation `и҆́мѧ ре́къ` (*Name*), directing the priest to name the dying person. The prayer concludes with an allusion to the "eternal mansions" (John 14:2: "In My Father's house are many mansions").
[^1404]: Folio 227v (physical page 472). A distinctive Ruthenian pastoral formula of absolution: *Prayer at the confession of a brother, which is given by the spiritual father to one who is about to die in the answer of faith* (Молитва на исповѣданїе брату, яже даетъся отъ отца духовнаго, въ вѣрѣ отвѣта хотящому умирати). This prayer reflects the pastoral care of the dying in the Kyivan and Galician traditions, confirming the penitent's final absolution before death.
[^1405]: Scriptural and liturgical echoes in the absolution prayer: John 1:29 (Christ bearing the sins of the world); Matthew 12:32 (remission in the present age and in the age to come); 2 Corinthians 5:10 and Romans 14:10 (standing uncondemned before the judgment seat of Christ); and 1 Timothy 2:4 / Ezekiel 33:11 (God desiring the life and salvation of all).
[^1406]: Folio 228r (physical page 473) opens with a large decorative engraved woodcut headpiece introducing the canonical ruling of Theodore Balsamon, Greek Patriarch of Antioch (c. 1140–1204), one of the foremost Byzantine canonists. His responsa were incorporated into the Slavic *Kormchaia Kniga* (Nomocanon) and included in 16th-17th century Ruthenian Trebnyks as authoritative guidance on burial practice.
[^1407]: The canonist distinguishes between consecrated churches (ѻсщенныя цѣркви) possessing a consecrated altar table, consecrated with holy chrism and holy martyr relics, and unconsecrated oratories or prayer houses (молитвеныя храми).
[^1408]: Folio 228v (physical page 474). Canonical and legal citations: the "second chapter of the first title of the fifth book of ecclesiastical laws" refers to the Byzantine Nomocanon in 14 Titles / Basilika (Title I, ch. 2), reaffirming the strict prohibition against intramural burials in consecrated temple space. The "civil ancient law" refers to Roman-Byzantine civil legislation (Codex Justinianus and Novel 133; reinforced by Leo VI Novel 53), which banned lay burials within cities and inside sacred shrines, reserving holy church burial exclusively for martyrs and emperors. Simple prayer houses, lacking consecrated altars, were exempted.
[^1409]: Folio 229r (physical page 475). Major structural division: The Order of Burial (Послѣдованїе къ погребенїю). The rubric specifies the initial domestic rites: immediately upon death, the relatives summon the priest to the home where the body lies; the priest vests in the epitrachelion and censes the deceased before beginning the service.
[^1410]: Rubrical instruction for the Usual Beginning (Обычное начало) chanted at the house of the deceased: the Trisagion Prayers through the Lord's Prayer ("Holy God...", "All-Holy Trinity...", "Our Father..."), followed by the priestly doxology ("For Thine is the kingdom...").
[^1411]: Folio 229v (physical page 476). The renowned Funeral Troparia (Tone 4) chanted at the home: *With the spirits of the righteous made perfect...* (Со духи праведными скончавшихся...). Direct Scriptural allusion to Hebrews 12:23 ("to the spirits of just men made perfect").
[^1412]: Allusion to Christ's descent into hades to loose the bonds of those held captive, drawing on 1 Peter 3:19 ("He went and preached unto the spirits in prison") and Psalm 106:14 (LXX 106:14 / MT 107:14: "and He broke their bonds asunder").
[^1413]: Folio 230r (physical page 477). The Funeral Litany of Fervent Supplication (Сугубая ектения об упокоении) offered in the home of the deceased, petitioning for the forgiveness of all sins voluntary and involuntary and the repose of the soul with the righteous.
[^1414]: Opening of the great Priestly Prayer for the Departed: *O God of spirits and of all flesh...* (Боже духовъ и всякия плоти...). Scriptural allusion to Numbers 16:22 and 27:16 ("the God of the spirits of all flesh") and Hebrews 2:14 (Christ destroying through death him that had the power of death, that is, the devil).
[^1415]: Folio 230v (physical page 478). Continuation of the prayer of absolution: the threefold peaceful haven of repose—a place of light, green pasture, and refreshment (Psalm 22:2 LXX: "In a place of green pasture, there He hath made me to dwell; beside the water of rest hath He nurtured me" [Masoretic: Ps 23:2]). Allusion also to Isaiah 35:10 and 51:11 ("sorrow and sighing shall flee away").
[^1416]: Allusion to the universal human condition of sinfulness from 3 Kingdoms 8:46 (LXX 3 Kgs 8:46 / MT 1 Kgs 8:46: "for there is no man that sinneth not"), 2 Chronicles 6:36, and Ecclesiastes 7:20. Followed by the citation of Psalm 118:142 (LXX 118:142 / MT 119:142: "Thy righteousness is an everlasting righteousness, and Thy law is truth") and Psalm 118:160.
[^1417]: Folio 231r (physical page 479). Rubric for the funeral procession transferring the body from the home to the church: the priest gives the dismissal at the home, initiates the service again, and the clergy and people bear the body of the deceased to the church in solemn procession with processional candles/lamps and censers while chanting the Trisagion (*Holy God*). Upon arrival at the church, the coffin is set in the narthex (притворъ) facing the royal doors.
[^1418]: Folio 231v (physical page 480). Opening of the church station in the narthex: the triple *Come, let us worship*, followed by Psalm 90 (LXX 90 / MT 91:1-7a), traditionally chanted over the deceased as a psalm of divine protection and trust.
[^1419]: Septuagint versification of Psalm 90:1-6 (LXX 90:1-6 / Masoretic: Psalm 91:1-6). The Ruthenian and Church Slavonic text strictly follows the Greek Septuagint rendering: "from troubling speech" (ἀπὸ λόγου ταραχώδους / ѿ словесѐ мѧте́жна, MT: "from the noisome pestilence"), "with His pinions shall He overshadow thee" (ἐν τοῖς μεταφрένοις αὐτοῦ ἐπισκιάσει σοι / пле́щима свои́ма ѡ҆сѣни́тъ тѧ), and "nor of mishap and the noonday demon" (καὶ δαιμονίου μεσημβриνοῦ / ѿ срѧ́ща и҆ бѣ́са полоу́деннаго, MT: "nor for the destruction that wastes at noonday").
