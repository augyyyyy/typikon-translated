[^3045]: Church Slavonic rubric in red cinnabar: *И҆́же въ ст҃ы́хъ ѻ҆ц҃а на́шего і҆ѡа́нна а҆рхїепⷭ҇па константинопо́лѧ златоу́стаго • Сло́во оу҆тѣши́телно въ ст҃ый и҆ вели́кїй четвръто́къ • Бл҃ви ѻ҆́ч҃е* ("Of our Father among the Saints, John Chrysostom, Archbishop of Constantinople: A Consoling Word on Holy and Great Thursday. Bless, father:"). In Byzantine and Old Cyrillic liturgical codices (Goar, *Euchologion*, pp. 586–587; Mansvetov, *Tserkovnyi Ustav*, p. 312), this homiletic exhortation to communicants is traditionally read by the spiritual father or deacon before the reception of Holy Communion on Great and Holy Thursday, the feast commemorating the Mystical Supper (*Cena Domini*), warning penitents against approaching the Holy Mysteries without sincere repentance, confession, and reconciliation.

[^3047]: The fear and trembling requisite for partaking of the Holy Mysteries, reflecting 1 Corinthians 11:27–29 and the Chrysostomic liturgy's communion call: *Со стра́хомъ Бо́жїимъ и҆ вѣ́рою пристоупи́те* ("With fear of God and faith draw near").
[^3048]: In the printed imprint on Fol. 523v (p1062), the text reads *не бо а҆́зъ є҆́смь гл҃ѧни оу҆ча́й ва́съ*, where *гл҃ѧни* is an epigraphic crasis or typesetting corruption for *глаго́лѧй и҆* ("speaking and"). The preacher disclaims personal authority, attributing the words of instruction to the grace of the Holy Spirit (cf. Matthew 10:20: "For it is not ye that speak, but the Spirit of your Father which speaketh in you").
[^3049]: Church Slavonic: *є҆ще́же и҆ ѿ своеѧ̀ ѻ҆глоуче́мъ съвѣ́сти*, where *ѻ҆глоуче́мъ* represents a printer error / contamination of *обличаемъ* (Greek: ἐλεγχόμενος ὑπὸ τοῦ ἰδίου συνειδότος, "convicted by his own conscience", cf. John 8:9) or *отлученъ* ("separated/excluded").
[^3050]: Scriptural citation of Hebrews 12:29 and Deuteronomy 4:24: *Бг҃ъ бо на́шъ ѻ҆́гнь поѧда́ѧй є҆́стъ* (καὶ γὰρ ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν πῦρ καταναλίσκον, "For our God is a consuming fire"). In Byzantine Eucharistic theology, the divine Fire of the Eucharist either purifies and illumines the contrite heart or burns and condemns the unrepentant (cf. St. Symeon Metaphrastes, Eucharistic Thanksgiving: "Consuming my sins as fire, yet enlightening my soul").
[^3051]: Scriptural quotation of 1 Corinthians 11:30: *сего ра́ди въ ва́съ мнѡ́ѕи не́мощни и҆ бѡ́лни, и҆ спѧ́тъ дово́лни* ("For this cause many among you are weak and sickly, and many sleep").
[^3052]: St. John Chrysostom's patristic exegesis on 1 Corinthians 11:30, explaining that the Apostle's word "sleep" (*спятъ*) euphemistically signifies physical death without sacramental absolution (*си́рѣчь оу҆мира́ютъ мнѡ́ѕи неи҆сповѣ́давшесѧ, и҆ безъ покаѧ́нїа*).
[^3053]: A comprehensive canonical catalogue of sins and spiritual impediments precluding reception of the Holy Mysteries, synthesizing the Pauline catalogues (1 Corinthians 6:9–10; Galatians 5:19–21; 1 Timothy 1:9–10) with Nomocanonical penances (e.g. Canons of St. Basil and St. John the Faster).
[^3054]: Church Slavonic: *манихе́й* (Manichaean), designating adherents of dualist heresy who denied the physical incarnation and the reality of Christ's Body and Blood, applied broadly in East Slavic canonical jurisprudence to heretics who reject Orthodox Eucharistic doctrine.
[^3055]: Scriptural quotation of Hebrews 10:31: *стра́шно бо впа́сти въ роу́цѣ Бг҃а жи́ва* (φοβερὸν τὸ ἐμπεσεῖν εἰς χεῖρας Θεοῦ ζῶντος, "It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God").
[^3056]: Scriptural quotation of Hebrews 4:12: *Ѻ҆стрѣ́йше бо є҆́стъ сло́во Бж҃їе па́че меча̀ ѻ҆бою́доу ѻ҆́стра • проходѧ́щее да́же до члѣ́нѡвъ и҆ мо́згѡвъ, и҆ кѡсті́й, и҆ мы́слїи, и҆ срⷣцъ* (τομώτερος ὑπὲρ πᾶσαν μάχαιραν δίστομον... ἄχρι μερισμοῦ ψυχῆς καὶ πνεύματος, ἁρμῶν τε καὶ μυελῶν).

[^3058]: Church Slavonic rubric: *Мл҃, на па́схꙋ дѣ́темъ дх҃о́внымъ* ("Prayer on Pascha for Spiritual Children"). Parallel to the Nativity prayer on leaf p1056 (Fol. 520v), this prayer was recited by the spiritual father or parish priest over his spiritual children following the Paschal Liturgy, recapitulating the liturgical cycle of the Great Fast and Holy Week.
[^3059]: The biblical archetype of the Holy Great Fast: the forty days of prayer and fasting undertaken by Christ in the Judean wilderness (Matthew 4:1–2; Luke 4:1–2), prefigured by Moses on Mount Sinai (Exodus 34:28) and Elijah on Mount Horeb (1 Kings 19:8).
[^3060]: Commemoration of the Third Sunday of Great Lent, the Sunday of the Veneration of the Precious and Life-Giving Cross (*Крестопоклонная неделя*), marking the midpoint of the ascetical journey.
[^3061]: The raising of the four-days-dead Lazarus of Bethany (John 11:1–44), celebrated on Lazarus Saturday immediately preceding Palm Sunday as the assurance of the universal resurrection.
[^3062]: Commemoration of Palm Sunday: Christ's triumphal entry into the holy city of Jerusalem, greeted by the children of the Hebrews bearing branches and chanting *Hosanna in the highest* (Matthew 21:8–9; John 12:12–13).
