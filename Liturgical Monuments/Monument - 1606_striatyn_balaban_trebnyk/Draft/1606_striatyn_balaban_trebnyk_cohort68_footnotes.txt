[^1862]: Slav. въ съкрыше́нїствѣ (vъ sъkryšenistvě), an archaic recensional variant formed on the root съкруша́ти / сокруше́нїе (contrition, brokenness of heart; cf. Ps. 50:19 LXX: се́рдце сокруше́нно и смире́нно).
[^1863]: Slav. въ хꙋдо́житвѣ (vъ xudožitvě), an Early Modern Ruthenian variant of художество, designating spiritual craftsmanship, ascetical mastery, or disciplined monastic praxis (Gk. τέхνη τῶν τεχνῶν, "art of arts").
[^1864]: The printed witness abbreviates съ пр҃ты (sъ pr[ěsvę]ty[mъ]) with a titlo, denoting the All-Holy (Пресвяты́й) Spirit, co-equal with the Father and the Son.
[^1865]: The rubric prescribes that the priest extend his right hand directly over the Holy Gospel resting on the analogion/tetrapod, signifying Christ's invisible presence and ratification of the monastic vow.
[^1866]: The outer margin of leaf 672 preserves a faint 17th-century contemporary cursive pen trial or liturgical cue in Ruthenian Skoropis: [Господи...] ([O Lord...]).
[^1867]: In this Ruthenian monastic recension, the candidate hands the scissors to the hegumen (пода́вшоу же є҆моу̀ нѡ́жицѣ и҆гꙋ́менові), from whose hand the presiding priest then takes them (И҆ взе́мъ їерѣй нѡ́жицѣ ѿ рꙋкѝ и҆гꙋ́меновы), reflecting the collegial participation of the local archimandrite or hegumen alongside the officiating hieromonk.
[^1868]: 1 Tim. 2:4.
[^1869]: The Ruthenian recension employs the traditional Byzantine third-person indicative active formula for the tonsure: Бра́тъ на́шъ и҆́мѧ ре́къ пострига́ет вла́сы главы̀ своеѧ̀ ("Our brother N. shears the hair of his head"), accompanied by the congregational response: рцѣ́мъ въсѝ ѻ҆ не́мъ: Гдⷭ҇и поми́лꙋй.
[^1870]: The investiture in the inner monastic robe or tunic (хитон / риза правды), invoking Isa. 61:10.
[^1871]: The paramandyas (парама́нтъ) of the Great Schema is a square cloth emblazoned with the instruments of the Passion, worn across the back and bound to the chest by cords, symbolizing the yoke of Christ and continuous mortification.
[^1872]: The cowl or pointed hood of the Great Schema (коу́коль беззло́бїа, "koukoulion of innocence"), embroidered with crosses and seraphim, interpreted liturgically as the helmet of saving hope (cf. 1 Thess. 5:8; Eph. 6:17).
[^1873]: The printed witness exhibits a mechanical truncation: а҆нала́, (analá,), an evident typographical defect for а҆нала́въ (analávъ; Gk. ἀνάλαβος). The analavos is the distinctive cruciform scapular of the Great Schema adorned with crosses, representing taking up the Cross upon one's shoulders (въспрїемлаѧ крⷭ҇тъ своѝ нара́мо).
[^1874]: The leather belt (пояс), symbolizing chastity, readiness for spiritual battle, and the mortification of carnal passions (cf. Luke 12:35; Eph. 6:14).
[^1875]: The monastic sandals or shoes (санда́лїа), signifying preparation to walk in the way of the Gospel of peace (cf. Eph. 6:15).
[^1876]: The concluding proclamation of the investiture, confirming that the monk has now fully received the Great Angelic Habit (вели́кїй а҆́гг҃лскїй ѻ́бразъ).
[^1877]: A vesting troparion in Tone 4 synthesizing the Pauline armor of God (Eph. 6:11–17) with the dominical promise of rest unto souls (Matt. 11:29).
[^1878]: Isa. 61:10 (LXX: 61:10).
[^1879]: The opening of the first major post-investiture euchological prayer over the newly tonsured schemamonk, invoking divine steadfastness and perseverance in the ascetic citizenship.
[^1880]: This prayer mystagogically reiterates each item of the Schema vestments (robe of righteousness, belt of mortification, koukoulion of humility, analavos of faith, sandals of peace) as spiritual armor against demonic powers.
[^1881]: Elijah the Tishbite and St. John the Forerunner are cited in Byzantine euchological tradition as the biblical archetypes and prototypic exemplars of the celibate, wilderness-dwelling monastic life.
[^1882]: 2 Tim. 4:7.
[^1883]: The printed witness features a dittography: оу҆слы́шитъ бл҃же́ннаго гла́са . гла́са . ("and may hear the blessed voice, voice"), which is regularized in translation while preserved in the epigraphic record.
[^1884]: Matt. 25:34.
[^1885]: The prayer of the inclination of heads (молитва главоприклонения), invoking Christ as the Good Shepherd and Overseer (пастырь и посѣтитель; cf. 1 Pet. 2:25) to preserve the candidate who has crucified himself to the world (cf. Gal. 6:14).
[^1886]: An important rubrical directive stipulating that if the Great Schema is conferred outside the Divine Liturgy, a special diaconal litany (настоѧ́щїа дїа́конства), followed by the Epistle and Gospel, must be celebrated.
[^1887]: The litany petitions are printed in the historical source as abbreviated incipits (e.g. Ѡ҆ съвы́шнеⷨ ми́рѣ, и҆ ѡ҆ сп҃се́нїи дꙋ́шъ), preserved here without unwritten additions in strict conformity with Rule 16.
[^1888]: Leaf 680 concludes with the incomplete word на- (na-), which is completed on leaf 681 as намѣре́нїе (naměrenie; "intent" or "purpose") of the monastic habit.
