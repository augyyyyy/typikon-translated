[^1138]: Eschatological courtroom imagery: The penitent envisions standing before Christ's dread and impartial tribunal (*на стра́шнѣмъ и҆ нелицемѣ́рнѣмъ твое́мъ прѣсто́лѣ*), anticipating the final judgment before the arrival of that day.
[^1139]: Confession of the conscience: Bowed down by inner conviction (*прекло́ненъ свое́ю совѣ́стію*), laying bare and exposing every lawless deed before God and His holy angels.
[^1140]: Scriptural allusion to Psalm 39:13 (LXX): *"For evils without number have encompassed me; my iniquities have taken hold upon me, and I am not able to look up; they are multiplied more than the hairs of my head"* (LXX Ps. 39:13: ἐπληθύνθησαν ὑπὲρ τὰς τρίχας τῆς κεφαλῆς μου).
[^1141]: Ascetic catalog of sins: Detailed self-accusation encompassing sins of the flesh, spirit, and tongue, transitioning from internal mental consent (*въѡбрази́хъ въ дꙋши́ мое́й*) to actualized external deeds.
[^1142]: Distinctive Ruthenian moral vocabulary: Note the presence of East Slavic / Ruthenian terms such as *коу́рсьство* (debauchery/fornication, from Ruthenian *курварство*) and *покле́пъ* (slander, defamation, false accusation).
[^1143]: Comprehensive enumeration of economic and social vices: *сребролю́бїе* (love of money), *любоимѣ́нїе* (avarice), *лихси́мство* (usury/extortion), *славолю́бїе* (vainglory), *многостѧжа́нїе* (covetousness of possessions), *самолю́бїе* (self-love), and *сквръноприбы́тчество* (shameful gain, Greek *αἰσχροκέρδεια*, 1 Peter 5:2, Titus 1:7).
[^1144]: Defilement of the faculties: Confessing the total corruption of the senses (*чю́вство*) and bodily members, rendered useless and subservient to the demonic will.
[^1145]: Scriptural quotation of Psalm 37:5 (LXX): *"For my iniquities are gone over my head; as a heavy burden they have pressed heavily upon me"* (LXX Ps. 37:5: ὅτι αἱ ἀνομίαι μου ὑπερῆραν τὴν κεφαλήν μου).
[^1146]: Theological premise of divine mercy: Asserting that no human sin, however monstrous, can overcome the boundless compassions and ineffable goodness of God's love for mankind (*чл҃колю́бїе* / Greek *φιλανθρωπία*).
[^1147]: Scriptural petition drawn from Psalm 16:7 (LXX): *"Make marvelous Thy mercies, O Thou that savest them that hope in Thee"* (LXX Ps. 16:7: θαυμάστωσον τὰ ἐλέη σου).
[^1148]: Typological archetypes of biblical repentance: Citing the Prodigal Son (Luke 15:11–32), the Publican (Luke 18:9–14), the Penitent Thief (Luke 23:39–43), King Manasseh (2 Chron. 33:12–13 / Prayer of Manasseh), and the Harlot who anointed Christ's feet (Luke 7:36–50).
[^1149]: Allusion to the Parable of the Laborers in the Vineyard: Citing Matthew 20:1–16, where those arriving at the eleventh hour received the full denarius of grace despite having performed no proportion of labor.
[^1150]: Unrestrained self-accusation: Acknowledging transgressions committed beyond measure (*пресогрѣши́вша вы́ше мѣ́ры*) in thought, word, and unnatural or irrational lusts (*безмѣ́стнымъ похотѣ́нїемъ и҆ безслове́снымъ жела́нїемъ*).
[^1151]: Grieving the Holy Spirit: An ascetic echo of Ephesians 4:30 ("And grieve not the Holy Spirit of God"), confessing sins committed night and day, willfully and involuntarily, openly and in secret.
[^1152]: The unmasking at the Judgment: Expectation of the dread exposure before Christ's tribunal, where all hidden sins are manifested exactly as they were committed (cf. 2 Cor. 5:10, 1 Cor. 4:5).
[^1153]: Scriptural citations from the Penitential Psalms: Directly echoing Psalm 6:2–3 (LXX) and Psalm 37:2 (LXX): *"O Lord, rebuke me not in Thine indignation, neither chasten me in Thy wrath. Have mercy upon me, O Lord, for I am weak"*; combined with the creaturely plea of Job 10:8 and Psalm 118:73 (*но̀ и҆̀ твоѐ созда́нїе а́зъ*).
[^1154]: Web of Penitential Psalm citations: Stringing together Psalm 37:3 (LXX: *"Thou hast pressed Thy hand heavily upon me"*), Psalm 50:6 (LXX: *"Against Thee only have I sinned, and done this evil before Thee"*), and Psalm 142:2 (LXX: *"And enter not into judgment with Thy servant, for in Thy sight shall no man living be justified"*).
[^1155]: Scriptural quote from Psalm 129:3 (LXX): *"If Thou, Lord, shouldest mark iniquities, O Lord, who shall stand?"* (LXX Ps. 129:3: ἐὰν ἀνομίας παρατηρήσῃς, Κύριε, Κύριε, τίς ὑποστήσεται;).
[^1156]: Allusion to the Prayer of Manasseh: *"And I am not worthy to behold and see the height of heaven for the multitude of mine iniquities"* (Prayer of Manasseh 9).
[^1157]: Early Christian and Patristic moral classification: Specifically identifying carnal sins including nocturnal/sensual emissions (*и҆стица́нїа*), effeminacy (*оу҆мягче́нїа*, Greek *μαλακίαι*, 1 Cor. 6:9), onanism (*рꙋкоблоу́дїа*), and the corruption of youth (*дѣторастлѣ́нїа*, Greek *παιдофθορίαι*, Didache 2:2).
[^1158]: Patristic catalog of sensual provocations: Noting rare ascetic terms such as *наскака́нїа* (wanton assaults or leaping, Greek *ἐπιπηδήσεις*) and *и҆скокта́нїа* (titillations, sensual dallying or wanton provocations, from CS *скокта́ти*).
[^1159]: Echo of Psalm 13:3 (LXX) and Romans 3:12: *"They are together become unprofitable"* (*непотрѣ́бенъ бы́хъ* / Greek *ἠχρειώθησαν*).
[^1160]: Scriptural opening of the plea for deliverance: Quoting Psalm 7:2 (LXX): *"O Lord my God, in Thee have I put my trust; save me from all them that persecute me, and deliver me"* (LXX Ps. 7:2: Κύριε ὁ Θεός μου, ἐπὶ σοὶ ἤλπισα).
[^1161]: Liturgical penitential formula: Pleading for remission and pardon (*ѡ҆сла́би, ѡ҆ста́ви, и҆ прости́*) according to the multitude of God's tender mercies (cf. Ps. 50:3 LXX).
[^1162]: Direct invocation of Psalm 50:3 (LXX): *"Have mercy upon me, O God, according to Thy great mercy, and according to the multitude of Thy compassions blot out my transgression"* (LXX Ps. 50:3: Ἐλέησόν με, ὁ Θεός, κατὰ τὸ μέγα ἔлеос σου).
[^1163]: Ascetic terminology of spiritual warfare: Deliverance from demonic assaults (*приложе́нїа*, Greek *προσβοлаί*) and habitual sins that grow along with the fallen soul (*съвъзраста́ющихъ є́й зѡ́лъ*).
[^1164]: Direct Pauline citation from Romans 5:20: *"Where sin abounded, grace did much more abound"* (*и҆де́же оу҆мно́жисѧ грѣ́хъ, преизоби́лоуетъ бл҃года́ть* / Greek *οὗ δὲ ἐπλεόνασεν ἡ ἁμαρτία, ὑπερεπερίσσευσεν ἡ χάρις*).
[^1165]: Doxological conclusion of the confessional pre-communion prayer: Proclaiming God as "the God of them that repent" (*Б҃ъ ка́ющихсѧ*), concluding with the Father Paul Doxology standard (*unto ages of ages*).
[^1166]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink introducing the second pre-communion prayer of St. John of Damascus (*ТОГО́ЖЕ СТ҃АГО І҆ѠА́ННА ДАМА́СКИНА • МЛ҃ТВА • КЪ СТ҃О́МОУ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ*).
[^1167]: Mystical allusions to the Song of Songs and the Psalter: Expressing spiritual love-wounding (*τετρωμένη ἀγάπης*, Song of Songs 2:5, 5:8) and consuming divine zeal quoting Psalm 118:139 (LXX: *"Thy zeal hath caused me to pine away"*, ἐξέτηξέ με ὁ ζῆλός σου).
[^1168]: Dogma of Theosis (*деификация*): Eucharistic theology of deification through the reception of the life-giving and deifying Blood (*живо́тныꙗ и҆ бг҃отво́рныꙗ ти кро́ве*) and partaking of the divine nature (2 Peter 1:4).
[^1169]: Eschatological anticipation drawn from 1 Thessalonians 4:17: Longing to meet Christ at His second coming, being caught up in the clouds in the air with all the elect (*на въздꙋ́шнѣмъ въсхище́нїи на ѡ҆блацѣ́хъ*, 1 Thess. 4:17: ἁρπαγησόμεθα ἐν νεφέλαις εἰς ἀπάντησιν τοῦ Κυρίου εἰς ἀέρα).
[^1170]: Trinitarian doxology concluding the second prayer: Glorifying the Son together with the unoriginate Father and the most holy, good, and life-creating Spirit.
[^1171]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink introducing the third pre-communion prayer of St. John of Damascus (*ТОГѠ́ЖЕ СТ҃ГО І҆ѠА́ННА ДАМА́СКИНА • МЛ҃ТВА • КЪ СТ҃О́Мꙋ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ*).
[^1172]: Drawing near unto Holy Communion: Trusting in the abyss of Christ's love for mankind (*поучи́нꙋ чл҃колю́бїа твое́го*), the penitent dares to approach the holy and life-creating Mysteries (continuing onto leaf p391).
