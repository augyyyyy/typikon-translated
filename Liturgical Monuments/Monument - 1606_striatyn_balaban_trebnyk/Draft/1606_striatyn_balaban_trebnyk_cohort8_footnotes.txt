[^112]: Fol. 27r. The cosmological anamnesis of the Great Sanctification of the Waters (*Ве́лїй є҆сѝ, Гдⷭ҇и...*). The text invokes the cosmic obedience of creation to the Creator: the crowning of the yearly cycle (Psalm 64:12 LXX), the trembling of the intelligible/spiritual powers (Greek: νοεραὶ δυνάμεις), the supplication of the stars, the stretching out of the heavens like a curtain (Psalm 103:2 LXX), the founding of the earth upon the waters (Psalm 135:6 LXX), and the bounding of the sea with sand (Jeremiah 5:22). Note the archaic Ruthenian dual/plural forms *мнѡгоѡчи́таѧ хероуві́мъ* and *шестокрила́таѧ серафи́мъ*.

[^113]: Fol. 27v. The Christological anamnesis and the purpose of the Incarnation. God, being uncircumscribed (*неописа́ненъ*, Greek: ἀπερίγραπτος), without beginning (*безнача́ленъ*, ἄναρχος), and ineffable (*неизглаго́ланъ*, ἀνεκλάλητος), took the form of a servant (Philippians 2:7), dwelling among men (Baruch 3:38 LXX), and sanctifying the virginal womb by His Nativity. The euchology proclaims the three theological duties: "We confess Thy grace, we proclaim Thy mercy, we conceal not Thy benefactions."

[^114]: Fol. 28r. The epiclesis upon the baptismal waters: *Са́мъ оу҆́бо чл҃колю́бче цр҃ю, прїидѝ и҆ннѣ̀ прише́ствїемъ ст҃а́го твое́го дх҃а. и҆ ѡ҆свѧтѝ во́доу сїю̀* ("Do Thou Thyself, therefore, O King, Lover of mankind, come even now through the descent of Thy Holy Spirit, and sanctify this water"). The text explicitly references the crushing of the dragons' heads in the Jordan (Psalm 73:13 LXX: "Thou didst crush the heads of the dragons upon the water"). The priest repeats the epiklesis thrice, accompanied by the threefold blessing with the hand.

[^115]: Fol. 28v. The breathing and signing of the waters. The priest breathes upon the surface of the water thrice (*доу́нетъ на́ водꙋ г҃.*) and signs it with the sign of the Cross (*прекрⷭ҇титъ рꙋкою*), reciting the apotropaic prayer: *Да съкроуша́тсѧ позна́менїемъ въѡбраже́нїа крⷭ҇та твоего̀ всѧ̑ проти́вныѧ си́лы* ("Let all adversary powers be crushed beneath the sign of the representation of Thy Cross") thrice. This is followed by the deprecation of airy and invisible phantoms (Greek: ἀέρια καὶ ἀφανῆ εἰδώλια) and demons of darkness, invoking Psalm 22:2 LXX (*во́дꙋ поко́ѧ*, "water of refreshment/repose").

[^116]: Fol. 29r. Baptism as ontological regeneration. The euchological catalogue enumerates the sevenfold gifts of baptismal grace: remission of sins, illumination of souls, the laver of regeneration (Titus 3:5), renewal of the Spirit, the gift of adoption (Romans 8:15), the garment of incorruptibility, and the fountain of life. Quoting Isaiah 1:16 LXX (*"Wash you, make you clean, put away evil from your souls"*), the prayer petitions that the candidate may put off the "old man" and put on the "new man" created after the image of God (Ephesians 4:22–24; Colossians 3:10).

[^117]: Fol. 29v. The famous typographical erratum and insertion (*вы́но* / *здѣ́*). In the printed text of Fol. 29v, the typesetter originally omitted the clause *и҆ въскр҃нїа бꙋ́детъ. и҆ сохрани́въ* (rendering Greek καὶ τῆς ἀναστάσεως γένηται, καὶ φυλάξας) between *прича́стникъ* on line 1 and *да́ръ* on line 2. Discovering the error during presswork, the Striatyn printers placed an asterisk above line 1 with the marginal notation *вы́но,* (Early Modern East Slavic: *вынос*, denoting an omission/insertion) and printed the missing line at the foot of the page (line 16) preceded by *здѣ́* ("here") and a corresponding red asterisk. The prayer concludes with the Pauline allusions to the prize of the high calling (Philippians 3:14) and enrollment among the firstborn in heaven (Hebrews 12:23).

[^118]: Fol. 29v–30r. The Rite of the Oil of Catechumens / Oil of Gladness (*є҆ле́й ра́дости*, Greek: ἔλαιον ἀγαλλιάσεως). The priest salutes the people with "Peace be unto all", instructs them to bow their heads, and bowing over the oil vessel, breathes upon it thrice and blesses it with his hand. The deacon holds the holy oil and intones "Let us pray to the Lord", introducing the ancient pre-baptismal prayer of blessing the oil (*Влⷣко Гдⷭ҇и Бг҃е ѻ҆ц҃ъ на́шихъ...*).

[^119]: Fol. 30r. Typological exegesis of Noah's dove and the olive branch (Genesis 8:11). The prayer explains the olive branch carried by the dove as an archetypal sign of reconciliation (*премѣне́нїа знаме́нїе*) and salvation from the flood, prefiguring the sacrament of baptismal grace and the confection of holy unction. The text affirms that the olive fruit served the mysteries of both the Old Covenant (consecration under the Law) and the New Covenant (perfection under Grace).

[^120]: Fol. 30v. The blessing and infusing of the oil into the font. The prayer requests that the oil become an "anointing of incorruption, an armor of righteousness [2 Corinthians 6:7], a renewal of soul and body, a fountain of life". Following the audible ekphonesis and choir's "Amen", the deacon calls "Let us attend" (*Вънме́мъ*), and the priest takes the oil and pours it dropwise upon the water in the form of three crosses, while singing *Alleluia* thrice with the congregation.

[^121]: Fol. 31r. The Pre-Baptismal Anointing (*Chrismation of the Catechumen* with the Oil of Gladness). Following the priestly blessing based on John 1:9 (*"Blessed is God, Who illumineth and sanctifieth every man that cometh into the world"*), the infant is brought to the priest. The priest dips his thumb/finger into the consecrated oil and signs the child in the form of a cross upon the forehead (*чело̀*), breast (*пръ́си*), face (*лицѐ*), and shoulders (*пле́щи*), reciting the formula of anointing: *Пома́зꙋетсѧ ра́бъ бж҃їй [и҆мⷬ҇къ], є҆ле́ѡмъ ра́дости...* (Psalm 44:8 LXX / Hebrews 1:9).

[^122]: Fol. 31r–31v. The bodily member formulas of anointing: breast ("Unto the healing of soul and body"), ears ("Unto the hearing of faith", Romans 10:17), feet ("That he may walk in Thy steps", 1 Peter 2:21; Psalm 118:105 LXX), and hands ("Thy hands have made me and fashioned me", Psalm 118:73 LXX / Job 10:8). In the 1606 Striatyn imprint, the formulas for the ears, feet, and hands are preserved in full liturgical order.

[^123]: Fol. 31v. The Canonical Baptismal Formula and Threefold Immersion. The priest holds the candidate upright, facing eastward, and pronounces the passive Byzantine baptismal formula (*Кр҃ща́етсѧ ра́бъ Бж҃їй [и҆́мѧ ре́къ]...*), immersing the candidate completely beneath the water at each of the three Persons of the Holy Trinity (*въ и҆́мѧ ѻ҆ц҃а... и҆ сн҃а... и҆ дх҃а ст҃а́го*). The Striatyn rubrics explicitly specify that the candidate is immersed and lifted up once at each Person (*низводѧ̀ є҆го̀ въ во́доу, и҆ възводѧ̀*), sealing the sacramental regeneration in accordance with Apostolic Canon 50.
