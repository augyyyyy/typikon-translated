[^2249]: The Greek Euchologion text of this secret prayer of the Great Blessing of the Waters (attributed to St. Sophronius of Jerusalem) reads: καὶ πρὸς τὴν πρώτην ἐλευθερίαν ἡμᾶς ἐπαναγάγῃς ("and lead us back unto our first freedom"). The 1606 Striatyn print renders this as `къ прѣ́вкои свободѣ̀ присво́иши ны̀` ("restore us unto our primordial freedom"). The phrase "sprinkle upon us... the pure waters of Thy mercy's gift" reflects the prophetic promise of Ezekiel 36:25.
[^2250]: Concluding petition of the secret prayer recited by the priest while the deacon intones the Great Ectene over the waters. The Slavonic instrumental pronoun `се́ю` refers to the water (`водо́ю`): "through this water mayest Thou grant Thy blessing unto us."
[^2251]: Hieratic doxology concluding the secret prayer. In strict conformity with the Father Paul Doxology Standard, `въ вѣ́кы вѣкẃмъ` is rendered "unto ages of ages" (Greek: εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας τῶν αἰώνων).
[^2252]: Rubric directing that the celebrant concludes this secret prayer silently without an audible exclamation (`возгласъ`), uttering only `Amen` within himself (`въ себѣ̀ рече́тъ а҆ми́нь`), after which, upon the conclusion of the deacon's litany, he commences aloud the great prayer of St. Sophronius of Jerusalem (`Тро́йце пресꙋ́ществе́ннаѧ...`).
[^2253]: Liturgical note testifying to recensional diversity: this preliminary invocation of the Holy Trinity (`Τριὰς ὑπερούσιε, ὑπεράγαθε, ὑπέρθεε...`) was read in certain Kyivan and South Slavic recensions, but omitted in the Great Church of Constantinople (Hagia Sophia) and on Mount Athos, where the celebrant proceeded directly from the litany to `Ве́лїи є҆сѝ, Го́споди` (cf. Dmitrievsky, *Opisanie Liturgicheskikh Rukopisei*, II, Euchologia).
[^2254]: The printed Church Slavonic text contains a typographical slip: `слове́сныхъ є҆стквъ` (where `є҆стквъ` represents `є҆сте́ствъ`, Greek: λογικῶν οὐσιῶν / φύσεων, "rational natures"). Masked Johannine citation: "Light unapproachable, that enlightens every man that comes into the world" (John 1:9; 1 Tim. 6:16).
[^2255]: The vocative `пребл҃га́ѧ` is in grammatical agreement with `Тро́йце` ("O All-Good Trinity", Greek: ὦ ὑπεράγαθε). The priest prays that his personal sins may not impede the descent of the Holy Spirit upon the waters.
[^2256]: Patristic and hymnographic antithesis linking the twin winter theophanies: Christ's Nativity in the flesh without human father from the Virgin Mother, and His eternal generation without mother from the Father (Heb. 7:3). The "foregoing feast" (`въ прѣва́ршемъ бо пра́ꙁдницѣ`) denotes the Nativity of Christ (December 25).
[^2257]: Contrast between the Infant Christ at Bethlehem and the mature Christ appearing at the Jordan: "mature, Who hast appeared as our perfect God from the Perfect One" (`съверше́нна... ѿ съверше́ннаго съвръше́нна ꙗ҆́вльшагосѧ Бг҃а на́шего`, Greek: τέλειον ἐκ τελείου).
[^2258]: The anaphoric hymnographic litany of "Today" (`Σήμερον` / `Дне́сь`) attributed to St. Sophronius of Jerusalem. Scriptural allusions echo the "Sun of Righteousness" (Malachi 4:2 [LXX 3:20]) and the radiance of the New Jerusalem (Isaiah 60:1-3).
[^2259]: Greek: Σήμερον οἱ φωτοειδεῖς ἀστέρες τῇ φαιδρότητι τῆς λάμψεως τὴν οἰκουμένην ὡραΐζουσι ("Today the light-bearing stars adorn the universe with the brightness of their splendor").
[^2260]: Greek: σήμερον τὰ ὕδατα τοῦ Ἰορδάνου ὑποδέχεται τὸν Δεσπότην. The Slavonic renders this vividly: `дне́сь съста́вы вẃдныѧ плещи́ма пое́млют стẃпы влⷣчна` ("Today the watery elements receive upon their shoulders the Master's footsteps"). The crushing of the serpent in the waters reflects LXX Psalm 73:13 [MT 74:13].

[^2262]: The compound `ѡ҆гнедх҃нове́нными` ("fire-breathed" or "inspired by the fiery Spirit") translates Greek: πυριπνόοις ῥείθροις, signifying the Pentecostal and Theophanic infusion of the Holy Spirit into the waters of the Jordan.
[^2263]: Typological parallel linking the baptismal immersion and liberation from weeping to the deliverance of Israel through the Red Sea (Exodus 14:21-31).
[^2264]: Typological allusion to the sweetening of the bitter waters of Marah by the wood cast by Moses (Exodus 15:23-25).
[^2265]: Allusion to the angelic annunciation and Nicene Creed: "and of His Kingdom there shall be no end" (Luke 1:33).
[^2266]: Exact citation of Septuagint Psalm 76:17 [LXX 76:16 / MT 77:16]: "The waters saw Thee, O God, the waters saw Thee and were afraid." Followed by LXX Psalm 113:3 [MT 114:3]: "The sea saw it and fled; Jordan was driven back."

[^2268]: Scriptural and liturgical tapestry combining LXX Psalm 113:4 [MT 114:4] ("The mountains skipped like rams"), LXX Psalm 76:18 [MT 77:17] ("The clouds gave forth a voice"), the Nicene Creed ("Light of Light, true God of true God"), Philippians 2:7 ("in the form of a servant"), and 1 Corinthians 15:55 ("the sting of death").
[^2269]: Introductory transitional clause leading into the thrice-repeated solemn exclamation: `Ве́лїи є҆сѝ Го́споди...` ("Great art Thou, O Lord..."). In strict compliance with MTS-1 Anti-Slop mandates, Slavonic `тѣ́мже` is rendered strictly as "Therefore" rather than the banned pseudo-archaic "Wherefore", and `чю́доу́щесѧ` is translated as "marveling".
[^2270]: The foundational hieratic exclamation of the Great Blessing of Waters: `Μέγας εἶ, Κύριε, καὶ θαυμαστὰ τὰ ἔργα σου, καὶ οὐδεὶς λόγος ἐξαρκέσει πρὸς ὕμνον τῶν θαυμασίων σου` (derived from LXX Psalm 85:10, 138:14; Revelation 15:3). The celebrant intones this thrice aloud, dipping the three-branched candle (troikirion) or triple candle into the water to sign it with the Cross.
[^2271]: Marginal rubric: `Трєми́ свѣща́ми знамена́ти` ("To make the sign of the Cross with three candles"). The priest blesses the waters with three lighted tapers tied together, dipping them into the basin during the thrice-repeated exclamation.
[^2272]: Patristic cosmology of the four elements (earth, air, fire, water) and the coronation of the four seasons of the year, echoing LXX Psalm 64:12 [MT 65:11]: "Thou wilt bless the crown of the year with Thy goodness." The clause continues onto the following leaf (`Page_0801.jpg`).
