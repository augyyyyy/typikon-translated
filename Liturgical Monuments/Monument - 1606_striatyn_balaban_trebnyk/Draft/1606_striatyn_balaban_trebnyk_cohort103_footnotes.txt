[^2985]: Ecclesiological and liturgical reconciliation: conclusion of the *Prayer over a Christian who was seduced into pagan error, and has returned again, and fled unto the Church of God* (`Мл҃тва хрⷭтїа́ниноу прельсти́вшоусѧ въ ꙗ҆зы́ческое прельще́нїе...` / Greek: *Εὐχὴ ἐπὶ χριστιανοῦ ἀπατηθέντος εἰς ἑλληνικὴν ἀπάτην...*), continued from physical leaf p1020. The prayer petitions for the complete uprooting from the penitent's heart of every custom of pagan godlessness (`всѧ́къ ѡ҆бы́чай ꙗ҆зы́чнаго безбо́жїа` / *πᾶν ἔθος τῆς ἑλληνικῆς ἀθεότητος*), restoration to blameless walking in God's commandments, and worthy, uncondemned communion of the Holy Mysteries. The prayer concludes with the truncated liturgical doxology incipit `нн҃ѣ и҆ прⷭно и҆ въ вѣ́кы :~` ("now and ever, and unto ages."). Fol. 496r continues the compositor's foliation anomaly initiated on fol. 493r (`ф҃` = 500), being numbered in the outer margin as `ф҃г` (503).
[^2986]: Canonical and euchological context of lustral purification: *Prayer over anything, if something unclean falls into a vessel: into wine, or into oil, or into a well, or into anything else; and it is fresh, not having decayed* (`Мл҃тва въ всѧ́комꙋ , а҆́ще впа́детъ что сквръ́но въ съсоу́дъ , въ ви́но , и҆ли въ є҆ле́й , и҆ли въ кла́денецъ , и҆ли въ и҆́но что̀ • и҆ бꙋ́детъ ново не разгнило́сѧ` / Greek: *Εὐχὴ ἐπὶ παντὸς μολυσθέντος σκεύους, οἴνου, ἢ ἐλαίου, ἢ μέλιτος, ἢ ἄλλου τινός*). In Byzantine and Slavic canonical jurisprudence (cf. the Nomocanon in the Kormchaia Kniga and the canonical responsa of Patriarch Luke Chrysoberges and Matthew Blastares), a rigorous distinction is drawn regarding unclean animals (mice, reptiles, insects) falling into liquid food vessels: if discovered immediately while the creature is whole and fresh (`ново не разгнило́сѧ`), the creature is cast out, the vessel is cleansed and censed, and the liquid is sanctified with holy water and prayer; if the carcass has decomposed, the liquid is deemed permanently defiled and must be poured out.
[^2987]: Rubrical movements and Scriptural theology of ritual purity: fol. 496v. The rubric directs that the contaminating object be cast out (`въ́врещи во́нъ`), another clean vessel washed both inside and outside (`и҆ внꙋ́трь , и҆ внѣоу́дꙋ`), and censed within and without (`покади́въ вноу́трьꙋдꙋ , и҆ внѣ́ꙋдꙋ`). The prayer opens with an illuminated initial `[С]` (`[С]ты́и вл҃ко Г҃и Іс҃ Х҃е Бг҃е на́шъ` / *Holy Master, Lord Jesus Christ our God*). The prayer grounds the purification of foods in Christ's teaching on true spiritual purity in Matthew 15:11 ("not that which goeth into the mouth defileth a man"), the apostolic doctrine of Titus 1:15 ("Unto the pure all things are pure"), and invokes Saint Paul as God's "chosen vessel" (Acts 9:15: `съсоу́домъ и҆збра́ннымъ` / *σκεῦος ἐκλογῆς*).
[^2988]: Scriptural citations: fol. 497r (misnumbered `ф҃д` = 504). The prayer quotes 1 Timothy 4:4–5 (`ничи́мже ѿмѣ́тнымъ • найпа́че же Бж҃їемъ сло́вомъ , и҆ мо-ли́твою ѡ҆сщ҃е́нномъ` / *οὐδὲν ἀπόβλητον μετὰ εὐχαριστίας λαμβανόμενον, ἁγιάζεται γὰρ διὰ λόγου Θεοῦ καὶ ἐντεύξεως*). The text petitions God, Who graciously looks down upon human infirmity (`ты̀ нн҃ѣ ѡ҆склабля́ѧй не́мощь на́шю` / Greek: *ὁ διαμειδιῶν τὴν ἀσθένειαν ἡμῶν*), to banish all psychological scrupulosity and demonic anxiety from the conscience of the faithful concerning the creature that fell into the vessel (`ѿженѝ ѿ съвѣ́сти ра́бъ твои́хъ`).
[^2989]: Epiphany water purification and priestly testing rubric: fol. 497v. Following the doxology, the rubric prescribes that the Priest pour holy water from the Feast of the Holy Theophany (`влива́етъ во́дꙋ ѿ ст҃ы́хъ Бг҃оѧвле́нїи`, the Great Agiasma) into the liquid cruciformly thrice (`крⷭтоѡбра́зно г҃`). Crucially, after the dismissal, the Priest is commanded to taste of the purified liquid first (`въкꙋсѝ прѣ́ждѣ іере́й`), thereby tangibly demonstrating to the faithful that the liquid is purified of all demonic pollution and fit for consumption. If the liquid has already decayed, it must be discarded (`а҆́ще ли бꙋ́детъ и҆згни́ло , да̀ пролїе́тсѧ`).
[^2990]: Codicological transition, Gathering 54 (`чч`), and Old Testament typologies of water purification: fol. 498r marks the beginning of Gathering 54, signed at the foot as `чч` and misnumbered in the outer margin as `ф҃є` (505). The office is entitled *Prayer over a well defiled by an unclean thing falling into it* (`Мл҃тва на ѡ҆сквръ́ньшимсѧ стоу́денце ѿ и҆здръже́нїи впа́дшаго нечи́стаго` / Greek: *Εὐχὴ ἐπὶ φρέατος μιανθέντος*). The preparatory rubric prescribes a substantial physical cleansing—drawing forty buckets of water from the well (`подоба́етъ пре́жде и҆злїа́ти и҆з̾ него м҃ , вѣ́дръ воды̀`)—prior to liturgical sanctification with three kindled candles and circular censing. The prayer opens with an illuminated initial `[Б]` (`[Б]е вели́кый и҆ ди́вный` / *O God, great and marvelous*), citing two major Old Testament prefigurations of water transformation: the sweetening of the bitter waters of Marah through the wood cast in by Moses (Exod. 15:23–25), and the healing of the barren waters of Jericho through salt cast in by the Prophet Elisha (4 Kings [2 Kings] 2:19–22).
[^2991]: Apostolic vision and apotropaic petition: fol. 498v. The prayer recalls Peter's vision at Joppa of the sheet let down from heaven containing all manner of beasts and reptiles, wherein divine voice proclaimed: "What God hath cleansed, call not thou common" (Acts 10:9–16), coupled again with Saint Paul's maxim in Titus 1:15. The petition asks for deliverance from every harmful thing, venomous reptile (`га́да ꙗ҆дови́таго`), uncleanness, and demonic sorcery or phantom deception (`потво́ра` / Greek: *γοητείας* / *φαρμακείας* / *φαντασίας*), followed by the rubric instructing the Priest to pour holy water into the well.
[^2992]: Healing office and Christological healing typologies: fol. 499r (Gathering 54, sheet 2: `чч в҃`, misnumbered `ф҃ѕ` = 506). Following the tasting of the well-water by the Priest and bystanders, a major therapeutic office opens: *Prayer over Every Manner of Infirmity* (`Мл҃тва на всѧкоѡ҆́бразнѣй не́мощи` / Greek: *Εὐχὴ ἐπὶ πάσης ἀσθενείας* or *Εὐχὴ ἐπὶ παντοίας νόσου*). The text begins with an illuminated initial `[С]` (`[С]ла́венъ Бг҃ъ бл҃гїи вра́чь дꙋша́мъ и҆ тѣ́лѡмъ на́шимъ` / "Glorious is God, the good Physician of our souls and bodies"). The prayer systematically gathers the central evangelical testimonies of Christ's healing power: Isaiah's prophecy of the Suffering Servant Who bore our infirmities and by Whose wound we were healed (Isa. 53:4–5; Matt. 8:17; 1 Pet. 2:24), the harrowing of Hades and granting of life to those in the graves (Paschal Troparion allusion), the resurrection of four-days-dead Lazarus (John 11), the healing of the fever of Peter's mother-in-law (Matt. 8:14–15; Mark 1:30–31; Luke 4:38–39), and the forgiveness of the two debtors (Luke 7:41–43).
[^2993]: Demonological and therapeutic petitions: fol. 499v. The prayer continues with Christ's forgiveness of the sinful woman (Luke 7:36–50) and appeals to God's patient repentance over human evils (Jonah 4:2; Joel 2:13: *μετανοῶν ἐπὶ ταῖς κακίαις τῶν ἀνθρώπων*). It petitions for specific relief from cephalalgia / headache (`гла́вноую боле́знь`), envious oppression (`зави́стную тѧготоу̀`), terrors, chills, fevers (`ѻ҆гнѧ̀`), the evil day and hour, the evil eye of malicious persons (`ѻ҆́чїю злы́хъ чл҃къ` / *ὀφθαλμῶν πονηρῶν* / *βασκανίας*), and the noon-day demon of Psalm 90:6 [LXX] (`бѣ́са полоу́деннаго` / *δαιμονίου μεσημβρινοῦ*). It culminates in David's prayer of repentance from Psalm 50:1 [LXX] (`помл҃уй мѧ Бг҃е по вели́цей мл҃ти твоей`).
[^2994]: Linguistic and codicological witness: fol. 500r (Gathering 54, sheet 3: `чч г҃`, misnumbered `ф҃з` = 507). Line 2 preserves the distinct Old Ruthenian / Church Slavonic verb form `ѡ҆цѣстѝ ѿ него̀ вьсѧ́ко безако́нїе` ("cleanse from him all lawlessness") from the root *cist-* (attesting to second palatalization morphology *цѣстити* / *ѡ҆цѣсти́ти* alongside standard *ѡ҆чи́стити*), translating Psalm 50:1 [LXX] (*ἐξάλειψον τὸ ἀνόμημά μου*). Following the priestly exclamation (`въꙁгла́съ`), the title of the second healing office appears in red cinnabar: *Second Prayer, for the Same* (`Мл҃тва в҃ , томоу́же`). The prayer opens with an illuminated initial `[В]` (`[В]лко Вседръжителю вра́чю дꙋша́м и҆ тѣ́лѡмъ` / *Master Almighty, Physician of souls and bodies*), invoking 1 Kings [1 Sam.] 2:6–7 and Job 5:18 (`смирѧ́ѧ и҆ възносѧ̀ • пока́зоуѧ и҆ па́ки и҆сцѣлѧ́ѧ` / *ὁ ταπεινῶν καὶ ἀνυψῶν, ὁ παιδεύων καὶ πάλιν ἰώμενος*).
[^2995]: Pneumatological and Christological restoration: fol. 500v. The prayer implores God to stretch forth His invisible healing arm (Ps. 135:12 [LXX]), raise the sick from the bed of infirmity, rebuke the spirit of disease (Luke 13:11–12), and cast out every affliction, burning fever (`ѻ҆гньницꙋ` / *πυреτόν*), and shaking ague / chills (`трѧса́вицоу` / *ῥῖγος*). It petitions for the remission of all deliberate and involuntary transgressions, concluding with an impassioned plea to spare God's own creation in Christ Jesus our Lord, sealed with the Trinitarian doxology unto ages of ages.
