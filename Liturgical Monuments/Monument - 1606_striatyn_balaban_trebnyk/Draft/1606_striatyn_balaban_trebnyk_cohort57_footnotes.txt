This specialized office, distinct from the burial of adults or priests, reflects the Byzantine theological tradition concerning infants who pass away before reaching moral culpability.

[^1600]: Fol. 272r. Psalm 19 (LXX 19:2–5): royal and supplicatory psalm chanted over the deceased child, petitioning God's help from the sanctuary and remembrance of sacrifice in the face of sorrow.

[^1601]: Fol. 272v. Conclusion of Psalm 19 (LXX 19:6–10): confession of divine deliverance through the holy right hand of the Lord, contrasting reliance on worldly chariots and horses with trust in the Name of the God of Jacob.

[^1602]: Fol. 272v. Rubric directing the chanting of Psalm 141 (LXX 141:1).

[^1603]: Fol. 273r. Psalm 141 (LXX 141:2–8): Davidic prayer of the distressed soul poured out before God as the sole portion in the land of the living when all human recourse has vanished.

[^1604]: Fol. 273v. Final verse of Psalm 141 (LXX 141:8): supplication for deliverance from prison and entry into the assembly of the righteous.

[^1605]: Fol. 273v. Psalm 27 (LXX 27:1–4): supplication imploring God not to remain silent lest the soul descend into the pit, and petitioning preservation from hypocritical workers of iniquity.

[^1606]: Fol. 274r. Psalm 27 (LXX 27:4–9): thanksgiving for answered prayer and the closing blessing over God's inheritance (*Спасѝ лю́ди Твоя̀, и благословѝ достоянїе Твоѐ*).

[^1609]: Fol. 274v. Theotokion to the Mother of the Uncreated Light (*Ма́ти свята́я пречи́стаго свѣ́та*), petitioning the Virgin's intercession with angelic hymnody.

[^1610]: Fol. 274v. Chanting of the funeral Kontakion (*Со святы́ми упоко́й*), followed by the exclamation, Trisagion, Prokeimenon in Tone 6 (*Блаже́нъ путь въ о́ньже по́йдеши ду́ше*), and announcement of the Epistle.

[^1611]: Fol. 275r. Epistle reading (1 Cor. 15:39–44): St. Paul's exposition of the bodily resurrection, contrasting earthly corruptible flesh with celestial incorruption and the natural earthly body with the spiritual resurrected body.

[^1612]: Fol. 275v. Conclusion of the Epistle (1 Cor. 15:44–45): typology of the first Adam as a living soul and the last Adam, Christ, as a life-giving spirit.
