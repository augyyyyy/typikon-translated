[^202]: Fol. 62r. Christ as the Priest and Lawgiver of Mystical Marriage (*та́йнаго и҆ прⷭ҇чтаго бра́ка сщ҃еннодѣ́йственникъ...*). Explaining the euchological title `ἱερουργός` / `сщ҃еннодѣ́йственникъ` applied to God/Christ in this second prayer of Crowning (Greek: Εὐλογητὸς εἶ, Κύριε ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν, ὁ τοῦ μυστικοῦ καὶ ἀχράντου γάμου ἱερουργὸς...), which defines God not merely as the creator of physical humanity, but as the active liturgical celebrant and consecrator of holy matrimony, establishing its sacramental character and guarding its incorruptibility against worldly decay.

[^203]: Fol. 62r. Primordial Creation and the Institution of Marriage (Genesis 2:18, 23). The euchology quotes the divine counsel prior to the creation of Eve: "It is not good for man to be alone; let us make him a helper fit for him" (Genesis 2:18), followed by Adam's ecstatic prophetic recognition of woman as bone of his bones and flesh of his flesh upon awakening from his deep sleep (Genesis 2:23).

[^204]: Fol. 62v. Scriptural Genesis and Synoptic Conflation (*сего̀ ра́ди ѡ҆ста́витъ чл҃къ ѿца̀ своего̀...*). The prayer combines the primordial covenantal formula of Genesis 2:24 ("Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife, and they shall be one flesh") with Christ's solemn dominical ratifying gloss in Matthew 19:6 and Mark 10:9: "What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder."

[^205]: Fol. 63r. The Typology of Divine Preservation (*съхранѝ и҆̀хъ... ꙗ҆́коже съхрани́лъ є҆сѝ...*). A liturgical litany of Old Testament salvific deliverances petitioned on behalf of the spouses: Noah preserved from the universal cataclysm within the ark (Genesis 7–8); Jonah preserved alive within the belly of the sea-monster (Jonah 2); and the Three Holy Youths (Hananiah, Azariah, and Mishael) preserved unburnt in the fiery furnace of Babylon by the angel who made the midst of the furnace as it were a moist whistling wind (Daniel 3:49–50 LXX).

[^206]: Fol. 63v. The Joy of St. Helen and the Crowning of the Forty Martyrs of Sebaste (*бл҃же́наѧ є҆ле́на... ст҃ы́ѧ ти четыридесѧ́тъ мч҃никы...*). The euchology links matrimonial joy to the exalted spiritual joy of St. Helen upon discovering the True and Life-Giving Cross in Jerusalem (c. 326 AD), while the petition remembering the Forty Martyrs of Sebaste (d. 320 AD)—who received forty celestial crowns descending from heaven as they froze in the icy lake—explicitly establishes the bridal crowns as crowns of martyrdom, bearing witness to selfless ascetic sacrifice and victory over worldly passions.

[^207]: Fol. 63v. The Parental Blessing Establishing the Home (*занѐ мл҃твы роди́телей оу҆твръжа́ютъ ѡ҆снова́нїа дѡмо́въ...*). Quoting the canonical wisdom of Sirach (Ecclesiasticus) 3:9: "The blessing of the father establisheth the houses of children; but the curse of the mother rooteth out foundations." The prayer explicitly extends the circle of euchological remembrance to the parents and sponsors assembled for the wedding feast.

[^208]: Fol. 64r. Vegetative Imagery of Blessing and Procreation (*ке́дры лива́нскыѧ, ꙗ҆́коже ло́зоу бл҃говѣ́твеноу...*). Integrating Old Testament poetic imagery of vital flourishing from Psalm 91:13 LXX [MT 92:12] ("the righteous shall flourish like a palm tree; he shall grow like a cedar of Lebanon") and Psalm 127:3 LXX [MT 128:3] ("thy wife shall be as a fruitful vine... thy children like olive plants round about thy table"), coupled with the apostolic benediction of 2 Corinthians 9:8 and the celestial luminescence of Philippians 2:15 ("among whom ye shine as lights in the world").

[^209]: Fol. 64v. The Third Crowning Prayer: Divine Imposition and Climax of Matrimonial Consecration (*Сщ҃е́нникъ па́ки мл҃твꙋ сїю̀ гл҃етъ велегла́сно: Бж҃е ст҃ы́и...*). This third euchological prayer (Greek: Ὁ Θεὸς ὁ ἅγιος, ὁ πλάσας ἐκ τοῦ χοὸς τὸν ἄνθρωπον...) constitutes the direct consecratory climax of the Crowning office. The rubric prescribes that the celebrant recite it *велегла́сно* ("with a loud voice" / ἐκφώνως), entreating God to reach down His invisible right hand from His holy habitation to yoke the bridegroom and bride together in mutual chastity and love prior to the imposition of crowns.

[^210]: Fol. 65r. The Rite of Imposition of Crowns (*прїе́мъ сщ҃е́нникъ вѣнца̀, вѣнча́етъ ѡ҆жени́вшагосѧ...*). The ritual imposition of wreaths or metallic crowns upon the heads of the couple constitutes the central sacramental act of Byzantine matrimony, signifying royal dignity as heads of a domestic church, mutual dominion over passions, and the crown of martyrdom.

[^211]: Fol. 65r. The Striatyn Crowning Formula: Recensional Witness (*Вѣнча́етсѧ ра́бъ Бж҃їи [и҆́мѧ], въ и҆́мѧ ѿц҃а...*). The 1606 Striatyn imprint preserves the concise archaic formula *Вѣнча́етсѧ ра́бъ Бж҃їи [и҆́мѧ], въ и҆́мѧ ѿц҃а и҆ сн҃а и҆ ст҃го дх҃а* without the explicit dative complement *рабѣ̀ Бж҃їей [и҆́мѧ]* found in standard later editions, reflecting an archaic early Byzantine euchological witness documented in Sinait. Gr. 959 and early South Slavic and Ruthenian imprints.

[^212]: Fol. 65r. The Triple Nuptial Blessing: Inked Recensional Witness (*Гдⷭ҇и Бж҃е на́шъ сла́вою и҆ чⷭ҇тїю вѣнча́лъ є҆сѝ и҆́хъ*). The 1606 text physically prints the past/perfect indicative form: *вѣнча́лъ є҆сѝ и҆́хъ* ("hast Thou crowned them"), directly citing the indicative wording of Psalm 8:6 LXX [MT 8:5] (*δόξῃ καὶ τιμῇ ἐστεφάνωσας αὐτόν* / *сла́вою и҆ че́стїю вѣнча́лъ є҆сѝ є҆го̀*), in contrast to the later harmonized precative imperative *вѣнча́й ѧ҆̀* ("crown them").

[^213]: Fol. 65r. The Nuptial Prokeimenon (Psalm 20:4 LXX [MT 21:3]). Tone 8: "Thou hast set upon their heads crowns of precious stones."

[^214]: Fol. 65v. The Prokeimenon Half-Verse (Psalm 20:5 LXX [MT 21:4]). "They asked life of Thee, and Thou gavest it them: length of days unto ages of ages." Note the grammatical dual verbal form in Church Slavonic: *проси́ста* ("they two asked"), precisely denoting the bridal pair.

[^215]: Fol. 65v. The Prokeimenon Verse (Psalm 20:2 LXX [MT 21:1]). "O Lord, in Thy strength the king shall be glad, and in Thy salvation how greatly shall he rejoice."

[^216]: Fol. 65v. The Nuptial Epistle Lection (Ephesians 5:20–33). Pericope 230: The foundational Pauline text setting forth the sacramental mystery of Christian marriage as the earthly mirror and icon of Christ's sacrificial love for His Bride, the Church.

[^217]: Fol. 66r. The Washing of Water by the Word (Ephesians 5:26). Alluding to baptismal rebirth (*τῷ λουτρῷ τοῦ ὕδατος ἐν ῥήмаτι* / *ба́нею водно́ю гл҃омъ*), through which the Church is purified and sanctified to become the unblemished Bride of Christ.

[^218]: Fol. 66v. The Conjugal Epilogue (Ephesians 5:33). Concluding the apostolic instruction with the call to mutual sacrificial love and reverence (*да бои́тсѧ* / *ἵνα φοβῆται*), understood not as servile terror, but as holy and affectionate awe within the hierarchy of love.

[^219]: Fol. 66v. The Nuptial Alleluia Tone 5. In Ruthenian chant tradition, the nuptial Alleluia is sung in Tone 5 with verses selected from Psalm 11 LXX.

[^220]: Fol. 66v. Nuptial Alleluia Verse (Psalm 11:8 LXX [MT 12:7]). "Thou, O Lord, shalt keep us, and shalt preserve us, from this generation, and forever."

[^221]: Fol. 66v. The Nuptial Gospel Lection (John 2:1–11). Pericope 6: The account of the Marriage at Cana of Galilee, where Jesus performed His first miracle at the request of His mother, changing water into wine and manifesting His glory.
