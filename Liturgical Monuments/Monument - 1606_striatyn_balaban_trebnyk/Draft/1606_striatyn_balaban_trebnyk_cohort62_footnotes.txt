[^1719]: Physical leaf p611 (fol. 297r). Allusion to Luke 9:62: "No man, having put his hand to the plough, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God." The instruction warns the monastic novice that regression into worldly preoccupations forfeits the heavenly calling.
[^1720]: Physical leaf p611 (fol. 297r). Allusion to Christ's evangelical demands in Matthew 10:37 ("He that loves father or mother more than Me is not worthy of Me") and Luke 14:26.
[^1721]: Physical leaf p612 (fol. 297v). 2 Timothy 2:3: "Thou therefore endure hardness, as a good soldier of Jesus Christ." The passage also draws directly upon 2 Corinthians 8:9: "For ye know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that, though He was rich, yet for your sakes He became poor, that ye through His poverty might be rich."
[^1722]: Physical leaf p612 (fol. 297v). Direct citation of the Dominical logion in Matthew 16:24 (cf. Mark 8:34; Luke 9:23): "If any man will come after Me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow Me."
[^1723]: Physical leaf p613 (fol. 298r). Allusion to the eighth and ninth Beatitudes (Matthew 5:11–12; Luke 6:22–23): "Blessed are ye, when men shall revile you... Rejoice, and be exceeding glad: for great is your reward in heaven."
[^1724]: Physical leaf p614 (fol. 298v). Direct scriptural citation of Isaiah 49:15: "Can a woman forget her sucking child, that she should not have compassion on the son of her womb? yea, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee."
[^1725]: Physical leaf p615 (fol. 299r). Commemoration of the historic archetypes of Byzantine coenobitic and eremitic monasticism: St. Anthony the Great (†356), St. Euthymius the Great (†473), and St. Sabbas the Sanctified (†532). The cinnabar rubric *А҆ жена́мъ* ("And for women") supplies the feminine monastic equivalents: St. Thecla the Protomartyr, St. Euphraxia of Tabennisi, and St. Olympias the Deaconess.
[^1726]: Physical leaf p616 (fol. 299v). The ritual laying of the Sluzhebnik (*ѻ҆бразосло́вїе*, Gk. *εὐχολόγιον*) upon the bowed head of the candidate during the priestly prayer, symbolizing the imposition of the liturgical yoke and ecclesiastical transmission of monastic discipline.
[^1727]: Physical leaf p618 (fol. 300v). Allusion to Romans 12:1 ("present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God") and Matthew 11:29–30 ("Take My yoke upon you... For My yoke is easy, and My burden is light").
[^1728]: Physical leaf p619 (fol. 301r). The solemn warning before the Holy Gospel affirming that monastic tonsure is strictly voluntary and that no coercion or external constraint has compelled the candidate to take the vows of the angelic habit (*вели́каго а҆гг҃лскаго чи́на*).
[^1729]: Physical leaf p620 (fol. 301v). Allusion to 1 Timothy 2:4: "Who will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth." The ceremonial taking and handing of the shears from the Holy Gospel is performed thrice to signify unconditional submission to the divine will and Christ Himself.
