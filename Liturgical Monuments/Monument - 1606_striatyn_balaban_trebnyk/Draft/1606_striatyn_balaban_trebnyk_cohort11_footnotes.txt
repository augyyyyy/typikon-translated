[^151]: Fol. 42r. The Post-Baptismal Hair Tonsure Formula (*Пострига́етсѧ ра́бъ бж҃їи...* / Greek: Κείρεται ὁ δοῦλος τοῦ Θεοῦ...). The priest clips the hair of the neophyte crosswise (*пострига́етъ є҆го̀ крⷭ҇тоѻбра́зно*), beginning on the crown of the head, then the back, and the right and left sides, dedicating the first-fruits of bodily growth unto God. The active indicative passive formula ("The servant of God is shorn...") seals the completion of the mystery of illumination, expressing the yoke of Christ and royal servitude under the divine King.

[^152]: Fol. 42r. The Capping with the *Koukoulion* or *Klobuchets* (*посе́мъ возлага́етъ нань коу́коль, ре́кше клобоу́чецъ гл҃ѧ: Ѻ҆дѣва́етсѧ ра́бъ Бж҃їи... въ коу́коль незло́бїа* / Greek: κουκούλλιον τῆς ἀκακίας). The 1606 Striatyn Euchologion witnesses the ancient post-baptismal investiture with the baptismal cowl or white cap (*klobuchets*), designated the "koukoulion of innocence" (or guilelessness / harmlessness; cf. 1 Corinthians 14:20: "in malice be ye children"). In early Byzantine and Old Slavonic ritual tradition (preserved in early manuscripts such as Euchologion Sinait. gr. 959 and Barberini gr. 336), this white headpiece remained upon the child's head throughout the seven days of the baptismal octave to protect the consecrated chrism upon the crown and brow until the eighth-day ablution.

[^153]: Fol. 42r. The Post-Baptismal Litany and Royal Commemoration (*є҆кте́нїа, вне́йже помина́етъ цр҃ѧ, и҆ въспрїе́мника съ новопросвѣще́нны[мъ]*). The litany following the tonsure and capping includes an explicit petition for the reigning sovereign: *Є҆ще мо́лимсѧ ѡ҆ бл҃гочтивомъ цр҃и нашемъ* ("Again we pray for our pious king..."). In the historical context of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth under King Sigismund III Vasa (reigned 1587–1632), the Ruthenian Orthodox Church preserved the traditional euchological title *цр҃ь* / *коро́ль* alongside supplications for the sponsor (*въспрїе́мникъ*) and the newly illumined child.

[^154]: Fol. 42v. Conclusion of the Eighth-Day Ablution Office and the Customary Dismissal (*И҆ быва́етъ по ѻ҆бы́чаю ѿпоу́стъ*). Following the ekphonesis of the fervent litany, the priest imparts the canonical liturgical dismissal (*apolysis*), formally concluding the eighth-day octave rites of ablution and tonsure.

[^155]: Fol. 43r. Part VI: The Reception of Converts and Ancient Conciliar Canons (*ЧИ́НЪ КА́КО ПОДОБА́ЄТ ПРЇИМА́ТИ и҆́же ѿ є҆ре́ти́къ...* / Greek: Τάξις πῶς δεῖ δέχεσθαι τοὺς ἐξ αἱρέσεων προσερχομένους...). The opening rubric of Part VI cites almost verbatim the canonical legislation established by the Second Ecumenical Council (Constantinople I, 381, Canon 7) and confirmed by the Quinisext Council in Trullo (692, Canon 95). These canons classify heterodox converts into three distinct canonical categories according to their baptismal status: (1) those requiring full threefold baptism (e.g. Eunomians, Montanists, Sabellians); (2) those received by chrismation and formal written abjuration (*libellus* / *и҆зрече́нїе*), comprising Arians, Macedonians (Pneumatomachians, *дх҃оу съпоста́ты*), Novatians/Catharoi (conflated here under *Савва́тники*), Quartodecimans (*четырина́десѧтники, рекѡ́мїи тетради́те*), and Apollinarians; and (3) those received solely through abjuration and the profession of the Orthodox faith (e.g. Nestorians, Monophysites).

[^156]: Fol. 43v. The Preparatory Fast, Catechetical Discipline, and the Term *па́надиѧ* (*пости́ти дни̑и д҃і , и҆ли па́надиѧ*). Before reconciliation, the candidate is placed under a strict fourteen-day ascetical regimen: fasting for fourteen days (*дни̑и д҃і*), attending morning and evening church services daily, and learning the Psalter (*оу҆чѧ́щоусѧ ѱалмы̀*) in the manner of a formal catechumen (*ꙗ҆́ко ѻ҆глаше́нъ*). The alternate phrase *и҆ли па́надиѧ* (or *панаги́а*) refers in Ruthenian and Greek monastic parlance to the two-week Dormition Fast preceding the Feast of the All-Holy Mother of God (*Panagia*, August 1–14), which constitutes an exact fourteen-day fast.

[^157]: Fol. 44r. The Recitation of the Creed and the Rubric *кр҃ща́ѧисѧ* (*приклона́етъ гла́вꙋ кр҃ща́ѧисѧ*). The candidate recites the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed verse by verse; if illiterate or unfamiliar with Church Slavonic, the sponsor dictates it for him line by line. The rubric denotes the candidate as *кр҃ща́ѧисѧ* ("the one being baptized"), reflecting the ancient euchological terminology where persons undergoing any stage of the catechumenate or sacramental reconciliation are designated broadly by the verbal noun of illumination.

[^158]: Fol. 44r. The Euchological Prayer of Reception: 1 Timothy 2:4 and Deliverance from Deception (*Бг҃е ст҃ы́й и҆ сп҃сителю на́шъ, хотѧ́й въсѣ́мъ чл҃кѡмъ сп҃тисѧ...* / Greek: Ὁ Θεὸς ὁ ἅγιος καὶ σωτὴρ ἡμῶν, ὁ πάντας ἀνθρώπους θέλων σωθῆναι...). The prayer opens with the apostolic proclamation of 1 Timothy 2:4 that God wills all men to be saved and to come unto the knowledge of the truth. It describes the convert as one who has "scarcely recovered himself from deception" (*є҆два̀ въскло́ньшагосѧ ѿ ле́сти*, Greek: μόλις ἀνανεύσαντα ἀπὸ τῆς πλάνης), petitioning the Divine Shepherd to receive him back into the rational sheepfold.

[^159]: Fol. 44v. The Johannine Theology of the One Flock (John 10:16). The euchology explicitly quotes Christ's discourse on the Good Shepherd in John 10:16: "Other sheep I have which are not of this fold; them also I must bring, and they shall hear My voice; and there shall be one fold, and one shepherd" (*ꙗ҆́ко и҆ и҆ны́ѧ ѻ҆́вца и҆́мамъ...*). This Scripture grounds the theological reconciliation of schismatics and heretics not as an innovation, but as the fulfillment of Christ's cosmic gathering of scattered souls into the unity of the Church through the seal of holy Chrismation and partaking of the Holy Mysteries.

[^160]: Fol. 45r. Post-Reconciliation Chrismation (*пома́зоуетъ є҆го̀ мѵ́ромъ ꙗ҆́ко новопросвѣще́ннаго, то́жде нарица́нїе творѧ̀ надъ ни́мъ*). Following the anathemas and profession of faith, the priest anoints the candidate with holy myron using the canonical sacramental formula identical to that of baptismal illumination: "The seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit. Amen." This reflects the second-class reception established by Canon 7 of Constantinople I and Canon 95 of Trullo, validating the outward Trinitarian form of their previous water immersion while imparting the sacramental grace of the Holy Spirit through Orthodox Chrismation.

[^161]: Fol. 45v. The Seven-Day Fasting and Octave Injunction (*повелѣва́етъ є҆мꙋ̀ з҃ дн҃ии ма́са не ꙗ҆́сти, и҆ лица̀ не оу҆мыва́ти*). Following Holy Communion, the newly reconciled Christian is instructed to observe seven days of meatless fasting and to refrain from washing his face, remaining in the spiritual state of a newly baptized person (*ꙗ҆́коже новокрща́емый та́ко пребыва́ти*), returning on the eighth day for the ritual ablution of the holy chrism.

[^162]: Fol. 45v. The Enrolment and Naming of a Convert from Paganism (*Мл҃тва знамена́ти чл҃ка прише́дша ѿ пога́ныхъ кр҃титисѧ, и҆ и҆́мѧ є҆моу̀ нарещѝ* / Greek: Εὐχὴ εἰς τὸ σημειῶσαι ἄνθρωπον ἐξ ἐθνῶν ἐρχόμενον βαπτισθῆναι, καὶ ὄνομα αὐτῷ ἐπιθεῖναι). This euchological prayer serves as the adult liturgical equivalent to the naming of an infant on the eighth day (*Chindanye* / *Пострижины*). It marks the formal signing with the cross on the brow and the bestowing of a Christian baptismal name upon an unbaptized catechumen converting from paganism (*ѿ пога́ныхъ*).

[^163]: Fol. 46r. Renunciation of Pagan Nomenclature and the Dignity of an "Equal Christian" (*да бꙋ́детъ и҆ се́и раве́нъ хрⷭ҇тїа́нинъ, зна́емъ ѿ дне́шнего днѐ*). The prayer explicitly beseeches God to purge the convert of every former pagan and heretical name (*всѧ́кого и҆́мене є҆рети́ческаго*), making him a "chosen vessel" (Acts 9:15) and an "equal Christian" (*раве́нъ хрⷭ҇тїа́нинъ* / Greek: ἰσοχριστιανός), invested with full spiritual citizenship in the Body of Christ without distinction of prior ethnicity or background (Galatians 3:28).

[^164]: Fol. 46v. Canonical Reception of Armenians and Jacobites (*А҆РМЕ́НИ Ѡ҆БРАЩА́ЮЩЕИСѦ... не кр҃щаютсѧ, но пома́зоуютсѧ му́ромъ*). In strict fidelity to the Byzantine canonical tradition (summarized in the Nomokanons and the *Syntagma* of Matthew Blastares), Non-Chalcedonians—specifically Armenians and Syrian Jacobites—are received into the Orthodox Church through Chrismation and the abjuration of their characteristic recensional and theological peculiarities, rather than by re-baptism, recognizing their valid historical Trinitarian baptism.

[^165]: Fol. 46v. The Renunciation of Armenian, Chazinzarian, and Jacobite Errors (*въсѧ́коую є҆́ресь а҆рме́нскоую, и҆ ханзиза́рскоую, и҆ і҆аковити́коую*). The abjuration formula explicitly targets three interrelated Oriental non-Chalcedonian groups: (1) the Armenians (*а҆рме́нскоую*); (2) the Chazinzarians (*ханзиза́рскоую* / Greek: Χαζινζάριοι, Chatzitzarioi, or Staurolatrae, from Armenian *khach* / *khachazart*, "worshippers of the Cross", a 7th-century Armenian sect or polemical epithet applied in Byzantine anti-Armenian treatises by Nicetas Choniates and Demetrius of Cyzicus); and (3) the Jacobites (*і҆аковити́коую*, Syrian Miaphysites following Jacob Baradaeus). The convert also explicitly renounces their liturgical customs: the use of unmixed wine in the eucharistic chalice, azymes (unleavened bread), and their distinctive fasting ordinances (such as the Fast of the Artzivourion / St. Sarkis).

[^166]: Fol. 46v. The Holy Symbol of Faith as *ст҃ыи ѻ҆́бразъ* (*И҆ гл҃етъ ве́сь ст҃ыи ѻ҆́бразъ, є҆́же є҆́стъ Вѣ́рꙋю въ є҆ди́наго Бг҃а*). In Church Slavonic euchological rubrics, the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed is frequently termed *ст҃ыи ѻ҆́бразъ* (translating the Greek τύπος τῆς πίστεως or σύμβολον τῆς πίστεως, "the sacred pattern / type / symbol of the faith"), which the convert recites in its entirety before being anointed with holy chrism.
