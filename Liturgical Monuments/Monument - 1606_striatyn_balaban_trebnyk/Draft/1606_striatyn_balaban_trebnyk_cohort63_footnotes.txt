[^1730]: Physical leaf p621 (fol. 302r). Allusion to Isaiah 61:10: "He hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, He hath covered me with the robe of righteousness, as a bridegroom decketh himself with ornaments." The monastic habit (rason/chiton) is designated the "garment of gladness" (*риза веселия*), symbolizing the joyous putting off of worldly decay.
[^1731]: Physical leaf p621 (fol. 302r). Allusion to Ephesians 6:14 ("having your loins girt about with truth") and Colossians 3:5 ("Mortify therefore your members which are upon the earth"). The rubric designates this vesting as the paramand (*парамандътъ*), accompanied by the formula of girding the loins unto the mortification of the flesh and spiritual renewal.
[^1732]: Physical leaf p621 (fol. 302r). Allusion to 1 Thessalonians 5:8 ("and for an helmet, the hope of salvation") and Ephesians 6:17 ("take the helmet of salvation"). The monastic head covering (klobuk / koukoulion) signifies the protection of the mind against demonic assault.
[^1733]: Physical leaf p622 (fol. 302v). The formula designates the reception of the paramand (*парамантъ*) as the solemn betrothal of the great angelic schema (*обручение великаго агглскаго образа, въ одежду нетлѣния и чистоты*). In Byzantine-Slavic monastic theology, the Lesser Schema (*малый образ*) functions as an earnest or betrothal anticipating the full ascetical perfection of the Great Schema.
[^1734]: Physical leaf p622 (fol. 302v). Direct citation of Ephesians 6:15: "And your feet shod with the preparation of the gospel of peace." The monastic sandals signify readiness to walk in the paths of evangelical obedience without hindrance.
[^1735]: Physical leaf p622 (fol. 302v). The formula for investing the candidate in the monastic mantle (*mandyas* / *pallium*), reiterating the reception of the betrothal of the great angelic habit.
[^1736]: Physical leaf p623 (fol. 303r). Citing 1 Peter 2:25: "For ye were as sheep going astray; but are now returned unto the Shepherd and Bishop [Gk. ἐπίσκοπος; ChSl. посѣтитель] of your souls." The prayer also draws upon 1 Corinthians 2:9 ("the things which God hath prepared for them that love Him") and Galatians 6:14 ("crucified unto the world").
[^1737]: Physical leaf p624 (fol. 303v). Rubrical directive specifying the bifurcation of the monastic tonsure rite: when celebrated independently outside the Divine Liturgy, the litany, Epistle, and Gospel are inserted directly; when celebrated within the Divine Liturgy, the readings are integrated into the Liturgy of the Word.
[^1738]: Physical leaf p625 (fol. 304r). Citing Galatians 3:27: "For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ." Sung here crosswise as the newly tonsured monk is led into the midst of the brethren, manifesting the tonsure as a reaffirmation and seal of baptismal illumination.
[^1739]: Physical leaf p625 (fol. 304r). Prokeimenon in Tone 3, citing Psalm 26:1 (LXX; MT 27:1): "The Lord is my illumination and my Savior; whom shall I fear? The Lord is the defender of my life; of whom shall I be afraid?"
[^1740]: Physical leaf p626 (fol. 304v). The Epistle pericope Ephesians 6:10–17, expounding the whole armor of God (*panoplia* / *всеоружие*), which forms the spiritual foundation for each monastic garment and weapon of spiritual warfare.
[^1741]: Physical leaf p627 (fol. 305r). Alleluia verse citing Psalm 33:7 (LXX; MT 34:6): "This poor man cried, and the Lord heard him, and saved him out of all his afflictions."
[^1742]: Physical leaf p627 (fol. 305r). The monastic Gospel pericope combining Matthew 10:37–38 and Matthew 11:28–30, articulating the cost and reward of discipleship: forsaking familial attachments, taking up the cross, and entering into Christ's restful yoke.
[^1743]: Physical leaf p629 (fol. 306r). Dominical logion from Matthew 16:24 (cf. Mark 8:34; Luke 9:23) spoken at the delivery of the hand cross unto the newly tonsured monk.
[^1744]: Physical leaf p629 (fol. 306r). Dominical logion from Matthew 5:16 spoken at the delivery of the lighted wax candle unto the newly tonsured monk, signifying the light of good works and evangelical witness.
[^1745]: Physical leaf p629 (fol. 306r). The classical tonsure idiomelon (*Да познаимъ братие тайны силу* / Gk. Ἐπιγνῶμεν, ἀδελфоί, τοῦ μυστηρίου τὴν δύναμιν), based upon Christ's parable of the Prodigal Son (Luke 15:11–32). The brethren bestow the fraternal kiss of peace upon the new monk while this hymn is chanted.
[^1746]: Physical leaf p630 (fol. 306v). Allusion to Luke 15:23–24 and 1 Corinthians 5:7: the slain fatted calf of the parable is mystically interpreted as Christ Himself, the sacrificed Victim whose death brings joy to the heavenly hosts and salvation unto penitents.
[^1747]: Physical leaf p630 (fol. 306v). The concluding rubric prescribing that the newly tonsured monk remain in the church for five full days in continuous ascetical contemplation, prayer, and reading of the Scriptures, sequestered from all worldly and manual labors.
