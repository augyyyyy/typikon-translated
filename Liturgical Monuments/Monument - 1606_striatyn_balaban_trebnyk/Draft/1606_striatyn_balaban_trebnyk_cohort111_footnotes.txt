[^3167]: Rubric continuation: *...in a garden, or in a vineyard, or in a field* (Слав. *...врътогра́дѣ, и҆ли виногра́дѣ, и҆ли на ни́вѣ*). Campsada (Слав. *Кампсада*, Greek *Καμψάδα* or *Κάμψαδα*) was the native village of the Holy Martyr Tryphon (commemorated February 1), located near Apamea in Phrygia. The narrative introduction recites the hagiographical account of the saint's youthful pastoral life pasturing geese (*го́уси па́сꙋщꙋ ми*), when a plague of agricultural pests descended upon the region.
[^3168]: Slav. *гнѣ́въ съни́де ѿ Бг҃а вседръжи́телѧ не то́кмо на мѣ́сто ѻ҆́но, но̀ и҆ въ ѻ҆́кр҃тныѧ предѣ́лы*—Greek *ὀργὴ κατῆλθεν ἀπὸ Θεοῦ παντοκράτορος οὐ μόνον ἐπὶ τὸν τόπον ἐκεῖνον, ἀλλὰ καὶ εἰς τὰ περικείμενα ὅρια*. The pestilence is depicted as a manifestation of divine chastisement (*гнѣ́въ*) affecting the entire ecosystem.
[^3169]: The text references the lake adjoining the village of Campsada (*па́че же и҆́же въ ве́си ѻ҆́ной живꙋ́щихъ прилежа́щаго є҆́зера*), traditionally identified with Lake Apamea / Aulocrene in Phrygia. The villagers approached Tryphon seeking his intercession before God.
[^3170]: The exhaustive catalogue of agricultural vermin, insects, and venomous pests reflects both classical Greek entomological vocabulary (*Εὐχολόγιον*) and East Slavic / Ruthenian zoological terms: caterpillar (*гꙋ́сѣница* / *κάμπη*), cankerworm (*чр҃вь* / *σκώληξ*), locust / wingless grasshopper (*прꙋ́гы* / *βροῦχος*), beetle / cockchafer (*хроу́стъ* / *κάνθαρος*), mole-cricket (*щꙋ́ры* / *γρυλλοτάλπη*), snout-beetle / grub (*връпела*), mole (*крыти́ца* / *ἀσπάλαξ*), mice (*мы́ши* / *μύες*), diverse midges (*мꙋ́шица* / *κωνώπιον*), grain-weevil (*гъгрі́нца* / *σιτόφιλος*), cloth/grain moths (*мо́лїе* / *σῆτες*), lice/gnats (*скни́пы* / *σκνῖπες*), destructive flies (*тлетво́рныѧ моу́хи* / *μυῖαι*), ants (*мра́вїе* / *μύρμηκες*), horseflies / gadflies (*ѡ҆́вады* / *οἶστροι*), wasps (*ѡ҆́сы* / *σφῆκες*), and venomous reptiles and arthropods (*ꙗ҆́звъ скорпі́а, ѕмі́а, мнѡгоно́жна*).
[^3171]: Slav. *зла́ца же, лькы̀, є҆́жеве, сла́ны, за́висти, лю́тости*—technical terminology for parasitic crop blights, fungal rusts (*зла́ца* / *ἐρυσίβη*), weeds/dodder (*лькы̀*), mildew/hail-rot (*є҆́жеве*), rime/frost damage (*сла́ны* / *πάχνη*), and atmospheric blastings (*за́висти*, *лю́тости*).
[^3172]: Title rubric: *And the exorcism is as follows* (Слав. *Заклина́нїе же си́цеко є҆́сть*; Greek *Ὁ δὲ ἐξορκισμὸς οὕτως ἔχει*). Here the celebrant shifts from narrative recollection to direct epigraphic adjuration (*Заклина́ю ва́съ* / *Ὁρκίζω ὑμᾶς*).
[^3173]: Angelic hierarchy and the Thrice-Holy Hymn: Allusions to the Cherubim and Seraphim crying "Holy, Holy, Holy is the Lord of Sabaoth" (Isaiah 6:2-3; Revelation 4:8) and the myriads of heavenly hosts ministering before the divine glory (Daniel 7:10; Revelation 5:11).
[^3174]: Scriptural and exorcistic motif: Banishment of destructive powers to wild mountains and unfruitful trees (*на ди́выѧ гѡ́ры на непло́днаа дре́ва*), mirroring Christ's authority over demons cast out into the wilderness or into swine (Matthew 8:28-32), while affirming God's providence providing nourishment even for wild creatures (Psalm 103:27-28 / LXX 103:27-28).
[^3175]: Patristic invocation of the covenantal God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob (Exodus 3:6; Matthew 22:32) and the eschatological reality of Christ returning to judge the living and the dead (2 Timothy 4:1; 1 Peter 4:5).
[^3176]: Slav. *но̀ и҆ воро́бїи посыла́емїи мое́ю мл҃твою поꙗдѧ́тъ вы*—a specific miracle attributed to Saint Tryphon in his hagiography, wherein flocks of sparrows and birds descended at his prayer to devour the swarms of destructive insects.
[^3177]: Allusion to the ineffable divine Tetragrammaton and Psalm 67:3 (LXX 67:3 / Masoretic 68:2): "as wax melteth before the face of fire, so let the wicked perish at the presence of God" (Слав. *ꙗ҆́ко во́скъ ѿ лица̀ ѻ҆гню̀*).
[^3178]: Liturgical rubric and prayer: *Prayer of Bowing of Heads* (Слав. *Главы̀ ва́шѧ Гд҃ви приклони́те*; Greek *Τὰς κεφαλὰς ὑμῶν τῷ Κυρίῳ κλίνατε*). Typological commemoration of God's blessing upon holy places: Bethlehem (birthplace of Christ, Micah 5:2; Matthew 2:1), Gethsemane (site of Christ's prayer, Matthew 26:36), and the blessing of the patriarch Jacob, renamed Israel (Genesis 32:28).
[^3179]: Divine blessing upon the house of David (2 Samuel 7:29) applied to the agrarian possession (*стѧжа́нїе*). Slav. *ѡ҆блѣ́нїи плодоноси́ти* is an archaic syncopated spelling for *ѡ҆би́льнїи* / *ѡ҆би́льнѣ* ("in abundance", Greek *ἐν εὐθηνίᾳ*). Intercessory invocation includes the three patron saints of agriculture and pests: Great-Martyr Tryphon, Great-Martyr Eustathius Placidas, and Great-Martyr Julian.
[^3180]: Title rubric: *Prayer over a Threshing Floor* (Слав. *Мл҃тва, надъ гоумно́мъ*; Greek *Εὐχὴ ἐπὶ ἅλωνα*). In traditional agrarian liturgy, the threshing floor (*гоумно́*, Greek *ἅλως*) was consecrated before winnowing to protect the grain harvest from decay, theft, and vermin, invoking Deuteronomy 28:8 and Psalm 103:15 (LXX 103:15 / Masoretic 104:15: "wine that maketh glad the heart of man, oil to make his face to shine, and bread that strengtheneth man's heart").
[^3181]: Title rubric: *Prayer over the Blessing of Nets* (Слав. *Мл҃тва . надъ бл҃венїемъ сѣ́тей*; Greek *Εὐχὴ εἰς τὸ εὐλογῆσαι δίκτυα*). Commemoration of the miraculous multiplication of the five loaves and two fishes feeding the five thousand (Matthew 14:15-21) and the invocation of Saint Peter the Apostle, the Galilean fisherman and leader of the Apostles (*пръвовръхо́внаго а҆плсо́м петра̀*, Greek *τοῦ πρωτοκορυφαίου τῶν ἀποστόλων Πέτρου*), for safety, abundant catch, and bodily health.
