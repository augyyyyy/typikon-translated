[^167]: Fol. 47r. The Christological Confession and Conciliar Anathemas of the Non-Chalcedonian Convert (*и҆ є҆ди́но оу҆́бѡ є҆стество̀ ст҃ѣ́и трⷪ҇ци и҆сповѣ́дꙋю...*). The penitent's profession of faith articulates the classic Chalcedonian Christological dogma against Monophysitism/Miaphysitism: confessing two distinct natures (*двѣ̀ є҆стествѣ̀*) in the incarnate Lord Jesus Christ—unconfused, unchangeable, indivisible, and inseparable (Council of Chalcedon, 451)—and affirming the impassibility of the divine nature (*Бж҃тво безстра́стно є҆́стъ*) against Theopaschism. The convert formally assents to the Seven Ecumenical Councils (*се́дмь събѡ́ровъ*) and pronounces anathema against ancient heresiarchs: Simon Magus (*Си́монъ пръ́выи є҆ретикъ*); Manes (founder of Manichaeism); Marcion; Arius and Eunomius (Arianism and Anomoeanism); Macedonius (Pneumatomachianism); Apollinarius; Paul of Samosata (Monarchianism); Sabellius (Modalism); Theodore of Mopsuestia (condemned at Constantinople II, 553); Nestorius (Nestorianism, Ephesus 431); and the Monophysite leaders Eutyches, Dioscorus of Alexandria (Chalcedon 451), Theodosius of Alexandria, and Timothy Aelurus.

[^168]: Fol. 47v. Anathemas against the Aphthartodocetic, Monophysite, and Monothelite Leaders (*І҆ꙋлїа́на. Петра̀ мо́гка...*). The catalogue continues with Julian of Halicarnassus (advocate of Aphthartodocetism); Peter Mongus (*Пе́тръ мо́гкъ*, Patriarch of Alexandria) and Peter the Fuller (*Пе́тръ крафе́й* / Gnapheus, Patriarch of Antioch), who interpolated the Theopaschite clause (*"Who was crucified for us"*) into the Trisagion; Severus of Antioch (*Севи́ръ безгла́вныи*), termed "headless" as leader of the Acephali; his organizer Jacob Baradaeus (*І҆а́ковъ цанца́лъ* / Zanzalus), from whom the Jacobite Syrian Church derives its historical appellation; Cyrus of Phasis/Alexandria and Marcian; Sergius I and Pyrrhus, Patriarchs of Constantinople and proponents of Monothelitism; and Polychronius (*Полихро́нїй младооу́мныи ста́рецъ*), the elderly Monothelite monk condemned at the Sixth Ecumenical Council (Constantinople III, 680–681) for attempting to prove his doctrine through an unsuccessful resurrection of the dead.

[^169]: Fol. 47v. The Imprecatory Oath and Civil Penalties of Perjury (*А҆́ще ли съ лже́ю и҆ ле́стїю сѐ творю̀...*). The candidate binds his conscience under an inviolable self-imprecation (*anathema*) invoking canonical and Scriptural archetypes of divine retribution: the perpetual vagabondage and trembling of fratricidal Cain (Genesis 4:12–14, LXX) and the incurable clinging leprosy of Gehazi, servant of Elisha, struck down for simony and deceit (4 Kingdoms 5:27 / 2 Kings 5:27). The formula combines spiritual damnation (*ѿринове́нъ, и҆ про́клѧтъ*) with explicit submission to the capital and corporeal penalties of imperial Byzantine civil jurisprudence (*гра́дскїи зако́нъ* / the *Basilika* and *Ecloga*), which prescribed severe civic infamy, banishment, and confiscation for relapsed heretics and perjured converts.

[^170]: Fol. 48r. The Threefold General Anathema and the Term *ѻ҆бновлѧ́емый* (*Въсѣ̑мъ є҆рети́кѡ́мъ а҆наѳе́ма, По три́щи. И҆ приклони́тъ гла́вꙋ ѻ҆бновлѧ́емый*). Following the recitation of specific heresiarchs and errors, the candidate seals his renunciation by proclaiming anathema against all heretical teachings three times (*по три́щи*). The rubrics designate the candidate as *ѻ҆бновлѧ́емый* ("the one being renewed" / regenerated; Greek: ὁ ἀνανεούμενος), denoting his sacramental rehabilitation from schism into the living communion of the Church.

[^171]: Fol. 48r. Scriptural Topoi of Reconciliation: 1 Timothy 2:4 and John 10:16 (*Бг҃е сп҃сителю на́шь, хотѧ́й всѣ̑мъ чл҃кѡмъ сп҃тисѧ...*). The euchological prayer grounds the reconciliation of Oriental non-Chalcedonians upon two central Scriptural passages: St. Paul's pastoral declaration of universal salvific intent in 1 Timothy 2:4 (*"Who will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth"*), and Christ's parable of the Good Shepherd in John 10:16 (*"And other sheep I have, which are not of this fold: them also I must bring, and they shall hear My voice; and there shall be one fold, and one shepherd"*). The phrase *є҆два̀ възъноу́вша ѿ ле́сти* (variant for *въскло́ньшагосѧ*, Greek: μόλις ἀνανεύσαντα ἀπὸ τῆς πλάνης) captures the image of one who has barely raised his head or awakened from the slumber of deception.

[^172]: Fol. 48v. Post-Abjuration Chrismation and Sacramental Integration (*пома́зоуетъ є҆го̀ му́ромъ ꙗ҆́ко новопросвѣще́ннаго. То́жде нарица́нїе творѧ̀ на́дъ нимъ*). The rubric specifies that the priest anoints the reconciled convert with holy myron (*мѵ́ро*) in the exact manner of a newly baptized person (*ꙗ҆́ко новопросвѣще́ннаго*), employing the canonical sacramental seal (*то́жде нарица́нїе* / "The seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit. Amen"). This conforms to Canon 95 of the Council in Trullo (692), which prescribes that Monophysites and Armenians, having retained valid historical Trinitarian baptism, are received into Orthodox communion through holy Chrismation and the immediate partaking of the Holy Mysteries (*прича́стїю чⷭ҇тнаго тѣ́ла, и҆ кро́ве*).

[^173]: Fol. 49r. The Post-Chrismation Clothing, Seven-Day Octave Fast, and Eighth-Day Ablution (*И҆ та́ко ѻ҆дѣва́етъ и҆̀. И҆ пороучи́тъ є҆моу̀ се́дмь дн҃ии ма́са не ꙗ҆́сти...*). The newly chrismated convert is invested in clean Christian garments (*ѻ҆дѣва́етъ и҆̀*), symbolizing the white baptismal robe of justification (*хитѡ́нъ пра́вды*). He is placed under an ascetical discipline for the duration of the seven-day octave: abstaining from meat (*ма́са не ꙗ҆́сти*) and refraining from washing his face, thereby preventing any profanation of the holy chrism upon his brow, eyes, nostrils, lips, ears, breast, and hands. On the eighth day (*въ ѻ҆смы́и же де́нь*), he returns to the church for the solemn liturgical washing (*ѻ҆мыва́нїе*), completing his sacramental integration into the Orthodox Church.

[^174]: Fol. 49v. Part VII: The Rite for the Reception of Latins in the 1606 Striatyn Trebnyk (*ЧИ́НЪ КА́КО ПОДОБА́ЄТ ПРЇИМА́ТИ приходѧ́щихъ ѿ лати́нъ...*). Part VII witnesses the official liturgical rite of the Ruthenian Orthodox Church under the jurisdiction of the Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople for receiving Roman Catholics into Orthodoxy following the Council of Brest (1596). The Balaban print strictly adheres to the decisions of the Council of Constantinople (1484), which repealed the Union of Florence and decreed that Latins are received through renunciation of errors, anointing with holy chrism (*myron*), and holy communion, rather than through re-baptism. This practice stood in contrast both to the late-Byzantine rigorism of the 13th–14th centuries and to the later 1620 Moscow Council of Patriarch Filaret Romanov, which mandated re-baptism of Latins until the Pan-Orthodox Synod of Moscow in 1667 formally restored reception by Chrismation.

[^175]: Fol. 49v. The Renunciation of Latin Sabbath-Fasting and "Peter the Stammerer" (*Ѡ҆рица́ешилисѧ вѣ́ры лати́ньскыѧ, и҆ сꙋбо́тства и҆́хъ, є҆́же Пе́тръ гоугни́выи въ ри́мѣ оу҆ста́вилъ є҆́стъ*). The abjuration singled out by the opening interrogation focuses on two traditional Byzantine grievances: (1) Latin fasting on Saturdays (*сꙋбо́тство* / Sabbath-fasting), prohibited by Apostolic Canon 64 and Trullan Canon 55; and (2) the polemical figure of "Peter the Stammerer" (*Пе́тръ гоугни́выи* / *Petr Gugnivyi*). In medieval East Slavic and Ruthenian polemical literature—most notably the popular 14th–16th century *Tale of Peter the Stammerer* (*Сказание о Петре Гугнивом*), frequently interpolated into the Slavonic *Kormchaia Kniga* (Nomokanon)—the historical divergence of the Latin Church was mythologized as having been instituted by an alleged Roman bishop or cleric named Peter the Stammerer, who was said to have corrupted Roman liturgical traditions after the severance of communion. Modern historical scholarship identifies this epithet as a polemical transposition and conflation of Peter the Fuller (*Petros Gnapheus*) or Peter Mongus (*Petros Moggos*, literally "the stammerer" or "hoarse-voiced") into Roman church history.

[^176]: Fol. 50r. Internal Folio Cross-Reference to the Reception Prayer (*Смотрѝ наза́дъ ѡ҆братѝ два̀ листы ѡ҆брѧ́щеши...*). The rubric instructs the priest to turn back two physical leaves to Fol. 48r (leaf p113), where the euchological prayer of reconciliation (*"O God our Savior, Who willest all men to be saved..."*) is printed in full. This cross-referencing practice illustrates the typographer's economy of space in the 1606 Balaban print, avoiding the duplicate typesetting of lengthy prayers common to both the reconciliation of Non-Chalcedonians and that of Western Christians.

[^177]: Fol. 50r. Pre-Chrismation Ablution with Holy Water (*И҆ по мл҃твѣ да́сть є҆мꙋ̀ во́дꙋ ст҃ще́ннꙋ. И҆ ѻ҆мы́етъ и҆̀ по лицоу̀ и҆ по роука́ма...*). Prior to anointing with holy chrism, the candidate's face and hands are washed with sanctified water (*во́да свѧще́ннаѧ* / *hagiasma*). This symbolic purification signifies the washing away of Latin ritual customs and heterodox communion, serving as an outward lustrative preparation before receiving the unction of holy myron (*му́ромъ сты́мъ ꙗ҆́ко новокрще́ннаго*) as an illuminated member of the Orthodox Church.

[^178]: Fol. 50v. The Sanctity of the Ablution Water and Prohibition against Desecration (*да̀ ѻ҆мы́етсѧ на чи́стѣ мѣ́стѣ, ꙗ҆́ко ненастоупи́тисѧ водѣ̀ то́й*). The rubric mandates that on the eighth day the post-chrismation ablution must take place in an untrodden, clean place (*на чи́стѣ мѣ́стѣ*) to ensure that the water containing dissolved holy chrism is not stepped on (*ненастоупи́тисѧ водѣ̀ то́й*). In canonical Orthodox pastoral practice, this ablution water was poured directly into the *thalassidion* (piscina / sacrarium) of the church altar or into a dry well or under the roots of a fruit-bearing tree, guarding the sanctified chrism from profanation.

[^179]: Fol. 50v. Psalm 22 (LXX 22 / MT 23) as the Mystagogical Octave Psalm (*ѱало́мъ, к҃в. Гдⷭ҇ь пасе́тъ мѧ̀...*). The recitation of Psalm 22 according to Septuagint versification (*"The Lord shepherdeth me, and nothing shall fail me"*, LXX Psalm 22 [MT Psalm 23]) forms an integral part of the early Byzantine post-baptismal octave mystagogy. Commented upon by St. Cyril of Jerusalem in his *Mystagogical Catecheses* (Catechesis IV), the psalm prophetically details the sacramental journey of the neophyte: the green pasture and water of rest (baptism), the oil anointing the head (chrismation), and the prepared table and cup of unmixed wine (the Holy Eucharist).

[^180]: Fol. 51r. The Eighth-Day Ablution Prayer (*[И҆́]же и҆збавле́нїе съгрѣше́нїемъ ра́ди ст҃го кр҃ще́нїа рабꙋ̀ твое́моу дарова́въ...*). This prayer adapts the ancient eighth-day euchology of post-baptismal ablution found in the Barberini Euchologion (gr. 336) and early Slavonic Trebnyks for use over a reconciled Latin convert. It celebrates the convert's remission of sins (*и҆збавле́нїе съгрѣше́нїемъ*), petitions for the illumination of his heart with the light of the divine countenance (Psalm 4:7 / 66:2), and prays for the preservation of the incorrupt baptismal robe (*нетлѣ́нїе ѻ҆дѣѧ́нїа*) and the intact seal of the Holy Spirit (*нероуши́ма дх҃овнаѧ печа́ть*).

[^181]: Fol. 51v. The Ritual Ablution Formula and Head-Bowing Prayer (*Кр҃ти́лсѧ є҆сѝ. Просвѣти́лсѧ є҆сѝ. Ѻ҆мы́лсѧ є҆сѝ. Ѻ҆с͠ти́лсѧ є҆сѝ...*). The priest performs the ritual ablution of the holy chrism using warm water and a sponge (*гоу́ба* / Greek: σπόγγος), wiping the loci of anointing while reciting the ancient quadruplicate baptismal declaration: "Thou art baptized. Thou art illumined. Thou art washed. Thou art sanctified..." (cf. 1 Corinthians 6:11: "but ye are washed, but ye are sanctified, but ye are justified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Spirit of our God"). This is immediately followed by the liturgical greeting of peace and the silent inclination prayer (*мл҃тва та́йнѡ* / *εὐχὴ κеклиμένη*), which echoes Galatians 3:27 (*"For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ"*) and petitions for the convert's victory as an "unconquered champion" against the spiritual adversary.
