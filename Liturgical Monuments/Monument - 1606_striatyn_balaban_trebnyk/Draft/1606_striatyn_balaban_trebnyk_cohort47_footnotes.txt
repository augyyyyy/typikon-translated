[^1385]: Scriptural allusion: Psalm 77:33 (LXX 77:33): "And their days vanished in vanity, and their years with haste" (καὶ ἐξέλιπον ἐν ματαιότητι αἱ ἡμέραι αὐτῶν καὶ τὰ ἔτη αὐτῶν μετὰ σπουδῆς / "исчезоша въ суетѣ дніе ихъ, и лѣта ихъ со тщаніемъ"). Combined with Psalm 17:6 (LXX 17:6): "The snares of death overtook me" (περιέσχον με ὠδῖνες θανάτου / "сѣти смертныя превариша мя"). [Masoretic: Ps 78:33; Ps 18:5].
[^1386]: The soul laments that in exchange for passing worldly indulgence (време́нное наслаже́ніе), it faces unending torment in the unquenchable fire (Matt 25:41), pleading that the mercy of the Theotokos may overtake it.
[^1387]: Allusion to Psalm 68:21 (LXX 68:21): "and I looked for one that would sorrow with me, but there was none; and for one that would comfort me, but I found none" (καὶ ὑπέμεινα συλλυπούμενον καὶ οὐχ ὑπῆрξεν καὶ παρακαλοῦντας καὶ οὐχ εὗρον / "и ждахъ сопечалующаго, и не бѣ, и утѣшающихъ, и не обрѣтохъ"), together with Psalm 37:12 (LXX 37:12): "My friends and my neighbors drew near and stood against me, and my near kinsmen stood afar off." [Masoretic: Ps 69:20; Ps 38:11].
[^1388]: Ode 8 Heirmos incipit: *Out of the flame for the godly didst Thou pour forth dew...* (Изъ пла́мене преподо́бнымъ ро́су источи́лъ еси́...). Allusion to the Song of the Three Holy Children in the Babylonian furnace (Daniel 3:49-50 / Song of the Three Children 26-27).
[^1389]: Allusion to the Gospel parable of the royal wedding feast in Matthew 22:13: "Then said the king to the servants, Bind him hand and foot, and take him away, and cast him into outer darkness; there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth."
[^1390]: Direct echoing of the laments of Job and Jeremiah: Job 3:11 ("Why died I not from the womb? Why did I not give up the ghost when I came out of the belly?") and Jeremiah 20:18 ("Why came I forth out of the womb to see labor and sorrow, that my days should be consumed with shame?"). [Masoretic: Job 3:11; Jer 20:18].
[^1391]: Allusion to the piercing of the side of Christ on the Cross in John 19:34: "one of the soldiers with a spear pierced His side, and forthwith came there out blood and water."
[^1392]: Patristic and liturgical representation of the aerial toll-houses (мытарєначалникы / telonia) presided over by the demonic spirits of the air (Eph 2:2; 6:12), combined with the eschatological sound of the last trumpet at the general resurrection (1 Cor 15:52; 1 Thess 4:16).
[^1393]: Ode 9 Heirmos incipit: *It is not possible for men to see God, upon Whom the ranks of angels dare not gaze...* (Бо́га человѣ́комъ невозмо́жно ви́дѣти, на Него́же не смѣ́ютъ чи́ни а́нгельстіи взира́ти...). The subsequent troparion directly cites Psalm 6:5 (LXX 6:6): "For in death there is no remembrance of Thee, and in hades who will confess unto Thee?" (ὅτι οὐκ ἔστιν ἐν τῷ θανάτῳ ὁ μνημονεύων σου, ἐν δὲ τῷ ᾅδῃ τίς ἐξομολογήσεταί σοι; / "яко нѣсть въ смерти поминай Тебе, во адѣ же кто исповѣстся Тебѣ;"). [Masoretic: Ps 6:5].
[^1394]: Epigraphic witness: the 1606 Striatyn print reads `Ниє́с` (likely a typographical error or variant contraction for *Никто́же* / *Ни єди́нъ*). The verse expresses the terrifying abandonment of the condemned soul, which can no longer be aided even by the angelic ranks, the prophets, apostles, or martyrs.
[^1395]: Dense concatenation of Gospel judgment parables: the barren fig tree commanded to be cut down as unprofitable (Luke 13:7), the withered vine-branch cast into the fire (John 15:6), and the unprofitable servant cast into outer darkness, bound hand and foot (Matt 22:13; 25:30).
[^1396]: Contrast between the material churches/temples of God, the human body consecrated as a temple of the Holy Spirit (1 Cor 3:16-17; 6:19), and the uncreated sanctuary of God, appealing to the Theotokos as the living temple to rescue the soul from the fiery burning of Gehenna (Mark 9:43-48).
[^1397]: Messianic quotation of Isaiah 9:6 ("For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given...") applied to the Incarnation of the Word through the virginal blood of Mary, pleading for His mercy at the violent separation of soul and body.
[^1398]: Folio 226v (physical page 470) opens with a decorative engraved floral headpiece and cinnabar title for the *Prayer said at the departure of the soul* (Молитва глаголемая на исходъ души). In the Ruthenian Trebnyk tradition, this priestly prayer is recited over the dying Christian immediately following the supplicatory canons when the soul struggles in agony.
[^1399]: Direct Scriptural quotation of 1 Timothy 2:4: "Who will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth" (ὃς πάντας ἀνθρώπους θέλει σωθῆναι καὶ εἰς ἐπίγνωσιν ἀληθείας ἐλθεῖν).
[^1400]: Quotation of the divine oath from Ezekiel 18:23 and 33:11: "As I live, saith the Lord God, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked; but that the wicked turn from his way and live" (LXX Ezek 33:11: εἰ βούλομαι τὸν θάνατον τοῦ ἀσεβοῦς, ὡς τὸ ἀποστрέψαι τὸν ἀσεβῆ ἀπὸ τῆς ὁδοῦ αὐτοῦ καὶ ζῆν αὐτόν).
[^1401]: Cinnabar rubrical designation `и́мрⷶк :` (*имярек* / *nomen* / *Name*), indicating the personal baptismal name of the dying person to be pronounced by the priest.
