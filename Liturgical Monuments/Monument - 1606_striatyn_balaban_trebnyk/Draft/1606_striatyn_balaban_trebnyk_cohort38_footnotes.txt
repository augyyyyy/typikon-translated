[^1098]: Penitential Eucharistic confession continuing from leaf p370 (*не въз̾гноуша́исѧ скврънѣ́йшихъ ѡ҆ноѧ оу҆́стъ и҆ нечистѣ́йшиⷯ*), in which the communicant entreats Christ not to abhor lips even more defiled and polluted than those of the penitent harlot in Luke 7:38, 45, who kissed the Savior's feet.
[^1099]: Patristic Eucharistic typology of the live burning coal (*оу҆́гль*) of Christ's Body and Blood, recalling Isaiah 6:6-7, wherein the seraphic coal purifies the prophet's lips, here applied to the reception of the Mysteries for sanctification, spiritual illumination, and bodily and spiritual health.
[^1100]: Multifold liturgical petitions beseeching deliverance from diabolical operations (*дїавольскыхъ дѣ́йствъ*), the overcoming and restraint of wicked personal habits, the mortification of passions (*оу҆мръщвле́нїе страсте́й*), and the preservation of the divine commandments.
[^1101]: Classical Byzantine Eucharistic metaphor of the spiritual or noetic wolf (*мы́сльнаго влъ́ка*, Greek *νοητοῦ λύκου*), designating the devil who preys upon and devours the sheep of Christ that remain too long estranged or distanced from the vivifying nourishment of Holy Communion (cf. John 10:12).
[^1102]: Comprehensive supplication for the total renewal and hallowing of the human person: soul, body, mind, heart, reins, and inward parts (*дш҃ю и҆ тѣ́ло, оу҆́мъ и҆ срⷣце, чревеса̀ и҆ оу҆тро́бꙋ*), entreating that the fear of God be deeply rooted in every bodily member and that divine sanctification remain irremovable.
[^1103]: Pastoral petition for divine guidance and governance (*ѻ҆крꙋмлѧ́ѧ*, "steering / directing", Greek *κυβερνῶν*) in peace, and for standing at the right hand of Christ among the sheep at the Last Judgment (Matthew 25:33).
[^1104]: Doxological conclusion of the prayer of St. John Chrysostom, invoking the intercessions of the all-pure Theotokos, the bodiless angelic ministers and celestial powers, and all the saints well-pleasing unto God from all ages.
[^1105]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink introducing another pre-communion prayer attributed to St. John Chrysostom (*ТОГО́ЖЕ СТ҃ГО І҆ѠА́ННА ЗЛАТООУ́СТАГѠ МЛ҃ТВА КЪ СТ҃ОМОУ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ*).
[^1106]: Direct evangelical allusion to the Parable of the Sheep and the Goats in Matthew 25:32-33, pleading that Christ not place the communicant on His left hand (*ѡ҆шоую́ю*) among the goats that have provoked Him to anger.
[^1107]: Direct evangelical quotation of the dreadful sentence of rejection in Matthew 25:12 and Luke 13:25, 27: pleading that Christ not say unto the communicant: "Amen, I say unto thee, I know thee not."
[^1108]: Ascetic supplication for the foundational preparatory virtues of Byzantine liturgical life: perpetual weeping (*пла́чь кѻегда̀*), heart compunction (*оу҆миле́нїе*), humility, and purification through the fear of God.
[^1109]: Patristic vision of the communicant's heart becoming a consecrated temple and dwelling of divine goodness through participation in the pure and life-creating Mysteries (cf. 1 Corinthians 3:16-17; 6:19).
[^1110]: Evangelical confession of persistent faith despite spiritual sloth and despondency, perpetually knocking at the doors of divine mercy (Matthew 7:7-8; Luke 11:9-10) and clinging to the path of Christ.
[^1111]: Scriptural praise echoing Psalm 144:9 LXX (Psalm 145:9 MT): "The Lord is good to all: and his tender mercies are over all his works," concluding with an ascription of eternal dominion unto God.
[^1112]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink heading the prayer of St. John Chrysostom for the remission of sins (*ТОГО́ СТ҃Ѣ́ЙШАГО І҆ѠА́ННА ЗЛАТООУ́СТАГО, МО: КЪ СТ҃О́Мꙋ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ*).
[^1113]: Christological confession acknowledging Christ as the singular Lord possessing divine authority to remit human transgressions (Matthew 9:6; Mark 2:10; Luke 5:24).
[^1114]: Classical liturgical supplication for the remission of sins committed in knowledge and in ignorance (*ꙗ҆́же въ вѣ́дѣнїи и҆ невѣ́дѣнїи съгрѣше́ннаѧ*), entreating reception of the Holy Mysteries without condemnation (1 Cor. 11:29).
[^1115]: Patristic Eucharistic fruits: communion received not unto heaviness or punishment (*не въ та́жесть ни въ моу́кꙋ*), but for the pledge and betrothal of the life and Kingdom to come (*въ ѡ҆броуче́нїе бꙋ́доущаго живота̀ и҆ цр҃твїа*, Greek *εἰς ἀρраβῶνα*).
[^1116]: Spiritual imagery of the Holy Eucharist as a protective wall and fortress (*въ стѣ́ноу и҆ по́мощъ*, Greek *τεῖχος*) against demonic adversaries and for the obliteration of transgressions.
[^1117]: Trinitarian doxology concluding the fifth prayer of St. John Chrysostom, sending up glory to the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.
[^1118]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink introducing the sixth pre-communion prayer of St. John Chrysostom (*ТОГО́ЖЕ СТ҃ГО І҆ѠА́ННА ЗЛАТООУ́СТАГО • МЛ҃ТВА КЪ СТ҃О́МОУ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ*).
[^1119]: Direct Pauline citation and penitential confession from 1 Corinthians 11:27, 29: acknowledging personal guilt and eating and drinking judgment unto oneself by failing to discern the Body and Blood of Christ.
[^1120]: Direct quotation of Christ's Eucharistic discourse in John 6:56: "He that eateth my flesh, and drinketh my blood, dwelleth in me, and I in him."
[^1121]: Liturgical supplication begging divine mercy rather than judicial rebuke and exposure (*не ѡ҆бличѝ менѐ грѣ́шнаго*, cf. Psalm 6:2 LXX / Psalm 37:2 LXX).
[^1122]: Multi-fold Eucharistic petition: Holy Communion received for enlightenment, salvation, healing, preservation, cleansing, and sanctification of both soul and body.
[^1123]: Ascetic prayer for deliverance from demonic delusions, fantasies, and deceitful operations (*всѧ́каго мечта́нїа, и҆ лоука́ваго дѣѧ́нїа, и҆ дѣ́йства дїа́вольска*) acting mentally within the bodily senses.
[^1124]: Spiritual fruits of Holy Communion: holy boldness and love toward God, amendment and strength of life, growth in virtue, fulfillment of the commandments, fellowship of the Holy Spirit, the viaticum of eternal life (*въ напоу́тїе живота̀ вѣ́чнаго*, Greek *εἰς ἐφόδιον ζωῆς αἰωνίου*), and a good defense before the dread judgment-seat of Christ.
[^1125]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink heading the seventh pre-communion prayer of St. John Chrysostom (*ТОГО́ЖЕ СТ҃И́ТЕЛѦ І҆ѠА́ННА ЗЛАТООУ́СТАГО, МЛ҃ТВА КЪ СТ҃О́МОУ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ*).
[^1126]: Classical penitential formula of remission (*Бж҃е ѻ҆сла́би ѡ҆ста́ви, оу҆щедрѝ*): entreating God to remit, pardon, have compassion, and forgive sins committed in word, deed, or thought, voluntarily or involuntarily, knowingly or in ignorance.
[^1127]: Intercessory supplication invoking the Theotokos, the noetic angelic ministers (*оу҆́мныхъ ти слꙋжи́телей*), and all the saints well-pleasing to God throughout the ages.
[^1128]: Petition for the uncondemned reception of the Holy Body and Precious Blood of Christ, unto the healing of soul and body and the cleansing of wicked thoughts.
[^1129]: Classical liturgical doxology attributing the Kingdom, power, and glory to the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit (cf. Matthew 6:13).
[^1130]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink introducing the pre-communion prayer and confession of faith of St. John Chrysostom (*ТОГО́ЖЕ СТ҃ГО І҆ѠА́ННА ЗЛАТООУ́СТАГО МЛ҃ТВА, КЪ СТ҃ОМОУ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ*).
[^1131]: The classical Byzantine pre-communion confession: combining the evangelical confession of St. Peter in Matthew 16:16 ("Thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God") with St. Paul's confession of personal unworthiness in 1 Timothy 1:15 ("Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief").
[^1132]: Dogmatic affirmation of the Real Presence: confessing with unwavering faith that the consecrated Gifts are in truth Christ's very own most pure Body and very own precious Blood.
[^1133]: Penitential supplication for mercy and remission of all voluntary and involuntary offenses, committed in word or deed, in knowledge or ignorance, in intellect or thought.
[^1134]: Continuation of the pre-communion confession onto leaf p380, beseeching partaking of the pure Mysteries unto the remission of sins and unto life everlasting.
[^1135]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink heading the concluding short pre-communion prayer of St. John Chrysostom (*ТОГО́ЖЕ СТ҃ГО І҆ѠА́ННА ЗЛАТООУ́СТАГО • МЛ҃ТВА • КЪ СТ҃МОУ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ*).
[^1136]: Concise Eucharistic petition pleading that communion not be unto condemnation, but rather grant cleansing, sanctification, and attainment of the life to come and of the Kingdom.
[^1137]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink introducing the pre-communion prayer of St. John of Damascus (*МЛ҃ТВА СТ҃ГО И҆ БГ҃ОНО́СНАГО ѾЦА НА́ШЕГО І҆ѠА́ННА ДАМАСКИ́НА*), designated as being composed after the manner of them that confess their sins (*ꙗ҆́ко въ ѻ҆́бразѣ и҆сповѣ́дающихсѧ*).
