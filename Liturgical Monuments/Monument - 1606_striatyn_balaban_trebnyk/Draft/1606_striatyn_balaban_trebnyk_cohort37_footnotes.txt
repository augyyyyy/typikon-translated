[^1060]: Eucharistic typology of the burning coal (*оу҆́гле*) and the seraph continuing from leaf p360 (*стра́шнаго ра́ди твое́го серафі́ма*), evoking the prophetic vision of Isaiah 6:6-7, in which a seraph purifies the prophet's lips with a live coal taken with tongs from the celestial altar, applied patristically to the vivifying Body of Christ.
[^1061]: Pauline petition for bodily mortification and spiritual renewal, alluding to Colossians 3:5 ("Mortify therefore your members which are upon the earth") and Galatians 5:16, effected through the divine descent (*наи́тїемъ*, Greek *ἐπιφοιτήσει*) of the Holy Spirit.
[^1062]: Evangelical meditation on the divine indwelling and mutual abiding of the Holy Trinity within the faithful believer, quoting Christ's promise in John 14:23: "If a man love me, he will keep my words: and my Father will love him, and we will come unto him, and make our abode with him."
[^1063]: Textual witness note on Church Slavonic *пи́ꙋменнѣ*: an apparent typographical ligature slip or vernacular Ruthenian compositorial form for *прїе́мнѣ* (*приємнѣ*, "acceptably / graciously / pleasingly") or *поиме́ннѣ* ("specifically / essentially"), paired with *неразлꙋ́чнѣ* ("inseparably", Greek *ἀχωρίστως*).
[^1064]: Patristic Eucharistic invocation addressing the consubstantial Word of God as the supreme protector and quencher of passionate desire (*наи́же въ мнѣ̀ всѧ́ко жела́нїе оу҆томи́тель*), reflecting Early Modern Ruthenian superlative syntax (*най-* + *-же*), and petitioning Christ to be all things unto His servant (cf. 1 Cor. 9:22, Col. 3:11).
[^1065]: High Priestly Prayer allusion: "unite Thyself unto me in one spirit" (1 Cor. 6:17) according to Christ's supplication unto the Father that the disciples may be one, even as the Father and the Son are one (John 17:21-23).
[^1066]: Pauline baptismal typology: "flesh of Thy flesh" (Ephesians 5:30; Genesis 2:23) and "bearing Thee from divine Baptism" (Galatians 3:27: "For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ").
[^1067]: Penitential confession acknowledging the defilement of the white baptismal garment of incorruption through post-baptismal sin (cf. Revelation 3:4; 16:15).
[^1068]: Direct quotation of Christ's Eucharistic discourse in John 6:56: "He that eateth my flesh, and drinketh my blood, dwelleth in me, and I in him."
[^1069]: Liturgical rubric in red cinnabar ink introducing the third pre-communion prayer attributed to St. Basil the Great (*ТОГО́ЖЕ ВАСИ́ЛЇА ВЕЛИ́КАГО МЛ҃ТВА, къ ст҃о́му причаще́нїю*).
[^1070]: Christological confession of the unconfused Incarnation: Christ receiving human nature—the creation of His own holy hands—subjected to passions through the Fall, yet without sin (Hebrews 4:15), and cleansing it with His precious Blood.
[^1071]: Meditation on the gratuitous divine economy of redemption, accomplished not for the righteous or those well-pleasing to God, but on behalf of sinners who had provoked Him to anger (Romans 5:8, 10).
[^1072]: Patristic Eucharistic title of Christ as the singular hope of salvation for those driven to despair by the magnitude of their transgressions (cf. 1 Timothy 1:15).
[^1073]: Direct quotation of Christ's medical-salvific aphorism in Matthew 9:12 / Mark 2:17 / Luke 5:31: "They that are whole need not a physician, but they that are sick."
[^1074]: Direct quotation of Christ's evangelical mission in Luke 19:10 and Matthew 18:11: "For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost."
[^1075]: Liturgical expression of Eucharistic fear and trembling: the communicant daring to approach the awesome Mysteries solely by relying on divine compassions (*щедрота́мъ*), entreating Christ to endure (*оу҆тръпѝ*) his unworthiness.
[^1076]: Liturgical supplication begging Christ not to abhor the sinner, nor to set him forth as guilty at the dread tribunal, despite drawing near in unworthiness (cf. 1 Corinthians 11:27).
[^1077]: Lexical note: Church Slavonic *любодѣ́йне* (*любодѣтельный* / Greek *φιλάγαθε* or *εὐεργέτα*), signifying the Lord as the bountiful Lover and Doer of good, granting His Body and Blood for the remission of sins.
[^1078]: Multi-fold Eucharistic petition: communion for the lightening of the crushing weight of iniquities, release from judicial guilt (*вины̀*), and the sanctification and cleansing of both soul and body.
[^1079]: Theological meditation on the shedding of Christ's divine and life-flowing Blood on the Cross on behalf of condemned sinners who are left without defense (*ѻ҆без̾ѿвѣ́тныхъ*).
[^1080]: Scriptural echo of 1 Corinthians 11:29: warning against partaking unto condemnation (*въ ѻ҆сꙋжде́нїе*) or a heavier defense/reckoning (*въ тѧжча́йшїи ѿвѣ́тъ*) instead of receiving the remission of sins.
[^1081]: Pauline liturgical disposition: approaching the dread Mysteries "with fear and trembling" (Philippians 2:12; Psalm 2:11 LXX), conscious of God's all-seeing omniscience (*всѧ̂ вѣ́дый*).
[^1082]: Epigraphic witness: Church Slavonic *съ здрога́нїемъ срⷣца, и҆ кості́и и҆са́мыхъ мѡ́зговъ* reflects Early Modern Ruthenian *здрогання* (shivering, trembling, shuddering; Polish *zadrżenie*), coupled with the Biblical motif of marrow and bones trembling before the divine Majesty (cf. Job 4:14; Hebrews 4:12).
[^1083]: Liturgical humility before the mystical table of the Eucharist, deemed too awesome even to behold with bodily eyes (cf. the prayer of the Cherubic Hymn in the Divine Liturgy).
[^1084]: Patristic Eucharistic metaphor of casting oneself into the boundless sea or abyss (*поучи́ноу*, Greek *πέλαγος*) of divine mercy (cf. Psalm 35:7 LXX [Psalm 36:6 MT]).
[^1085]: The dual emotional polarity of Byzantine Eucharistic reception: holy fear (*стра́хомъ*) at personal unworthiness, balanced by spiritual joy (*ра́достїю*) at the evangelical promise of forgiveness.
[^1086]: Scriptural citation of Psalm 24:18 LXX (Psalm 25:18 MT): "Look upon mine affliction [humility/lowliness] and my pain; and forgive all my sins."
[^1087]: Concluding petition of the third prayer of St. Basil, beseeching the eternal inheritance prepared for them that love God (1 Cor. 2:9) through the intercessions of the all-pure Theotokos and all the saints.
[^1088]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink heading the classical pre-communion prayer of St. John Chrysostom (*И҆́ЖЕ ВЪ СТ҃Ы́ХЪ ѼЦ҃А НА́ШЕГО І҆ѠА́ННА ЗЛАТООУ́СТАГО МЛ҃ТВА, КЪ СТ҃О́МУ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ*).
[^1089]: Direct quotation and liturgical adaptation of the Centurion's evangelical confession of unworthiness: "Lord, I am not worthy that thou shouldest come under my roof" (Matthew 8:8; Luke 7:6), comparing the sinner's soul to a derelict and collapsed dwelling.
[^1090]: Evangelical allusion to Christ's earthly homelessness in Matthew 8:20 and Luke 9:58: "the Son of man hath not where to lay his head."
[^1091]: Meditation on the Kenosis of the Son of God, Who descended from heavenly glory to humble Himself for the salvation of mankind (Philippians 2:6-8).
[^1092]: Nativity typology: Christ's condescension to be born in a cave and laid in a manger of irrational beasts (Luke 2:7), taken as the paradigm for His entrance into the communicant's soul.
[^1093]: The Chrysostomic Eucharistic parallel: entreating Christ to enter the manger of an unreasoning soul (*безслове́сныѧ дш҃а*) and a defiled body, just as He lay in the Bethlehem crib.
[^1094]: Evangelical narrative of Christ dining in Bethany in the house of Simon the leper (Matthew 26:6; Mark 14:3), showing His willingness to associate with the outcast and ritually unclean.
[^1095]: Spiritual transference of Simon's physical leprosy to the spiritual leprosy of sin that infects the soul of the communicant.
[^1096]: Evangelical account of the penitent woman with the alabaster box who washed Christ's feet with her tears and wiped them with the hairs of her head (Luke 7:37-38).
[^1097]: Evangelical motif of the harlot kissing Christ's feet (Luke 7:38, 45), continuing across the leaf transition onto leaf p371 (*не въз̾гноуша́исѧ скврънѣ́йшихъ ѡ҆ноѧ оу҆́стъ...*), beseeching the Lord not to abhor the unclean lips and tongue of the communicant.
