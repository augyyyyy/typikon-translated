In classical Byzantine and Ruthenian Euchologia (e.g. Goar, *Euchologion*, rite of confession; Trebnyk of Peter Mohyla), this prayer is recited by the spiritual father prior to hearing the penitent's confession, imploring divine mercy and discernment for himself as minister of the sacrament.

Scriptural allusion to Hebrews 4:13: "Neither is there any creature that is not manifest in His sight: but all things are naked and opened unto the eyes of Him with Whom we have to do" (*всѧ̑ на̑га и҆ ѿкрове́нна прѣⷣ ѡчи́ма твои́ма* / Greek: *πάντα δὲ γυμνὰ καὶ τετραχηλισμένα τοῖς ὀφθαλμοῖς αὐτοῦ*).
[^580]: Fol. 127v. Supplication of the presbyter for his own unworthiness, echoing Psalm 26:9 (LXX 26:9): "Turn not away Thy face from me" (*ни́же лица̀ твое́го ѿвратѝ ѿ ме́не*).
[^581]: Fol. 127v. Invocation of divine sanctification upon the minister: *неви́димою и҆ дх҃овною сѐи десни́цею* ("by this Thine invisible and spiritual right hand"), expressing that the priest's visible imposition of hands is but an icon of Christ's invisible right hand.
[^582]: Fol. 127v. Pastoral trembling of the priest before the awe-inspiring responsibility of the keys (cf. 1 Corinthians 9:27): *да не и҆́нѣмъ свобожде́нїе ѡ҆бѣщава́ѧ... са́мъ же ꙗ҆́ко ра́бъ неискоу́сенъ грѣхоу̀ пови́ненъ бꙋ́дꙋ* (lest preaching or promising remission to others, he himself become disqualified / Greek: *ἀδόκιμος*).
[^583]: Fol. 127v. Sacerdotal petition (*да невъзвращꙋ́сѧ* — "let me not turn back [humbled and put to shame]"), continuing directly onto Fol. 128r.
[^584]: Fol. 128r. Charismatic gifts implored for the confessor: power from on high (Luke 24:49), the word of wisdom (1 Corinthians 12:8), and the discernment of spirits (*раꙁсꙋжде́нїа дх҃ѡ́въ* / 1 Corinthians 12:10: *διάκρισις πνευμάτων*), vital for discerning the roots of sin and spiritual warfare.
[^585]: Fol. 128r. Pauline mystical petition: *въѡбрази́ ми Хрⷭта̀ въ оу҆́дѣхъ мои́хъ* (Galatians 4:19: *μορφωθῇ Χριστός* — that Christ be formed within the presbyter's faculties and members as an icon of the Good Shepherd).
[^586]: Fol. 128r. Rubric for naming the penitent: *и҆́мѧ ре́къ* ("having said the name"), indicating the insertion of the Christian name of the person coming to confession.
[^587]: Fol. 128r. Spiritual fatherhood and regeneration: the priest begets the penitent unto spiritual renewal (1 Corinthians 4:15: "I have begotten you"), edifying him upon the apostolic foundation (Ephesians 2:20: *на основа́ніи святы́хъ апо́столъ и҆ проро́къ*).
[^588]: Fol. 128r. Ecclesial integration: *насадѝ са́дъ и҆́стиненъ, въ ст҃ѣ́й свое́й въселе́нстѣй и҆ а҆пл҃тѣй цр҃кви* ("plant him as a true plant in Thy holy, catholic, and apostolic Church"; cf. Romans 6:5, Romans 11:17–24).
[^589]: Fol. 128r. Pastoral petition for the penitent's unfeigned faith (1 Timothy 1:5: *вѣ́ры нелицемѣ́рны*), continuing onto Fol. 128v.
[^590]: Fol. 128v. Pastoral prayer for contrition and honesty in confession: that the penitent may expose and denounce his own iniquity (*ѡ҆бличи́тъ безако́нїе своѐ*) without concealment, false shame, or self-justification.
[^591]: Fol. 128v. Life of righteousness and holiness following absolution, echoing Luke 1:75 ("in holiness and righteousness before Him, all the days of our life").
[^592]: Fol. 128v. Trinitarian ecphonesis concluding the first preparatory prayer of the spiritual father: *нн҃ѣ и҆ прⷭно и҆ въ вѣ́кы вѣкѡ́мъ, а҆ми́нь* ("now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen").
[^593]: Fol. 128v. Rubric of physical contrition: *Пото́мъ ка́ѧисѧ твори́тъ поклоне́нїа трѝ до землѧ̀, глаго́лѧ си́це* ("Then the penitent makes three prostrations to the ground, saying thus"), introducing the metanias and opening formula on Fol. 129r.

[^595]: Fol. 129r. Liturgical bodily posture of supplication: *та́же просте́ръ рꙋ́цѣ* ("then having stretched forth his hands"), an ancient orans/suppliant gesture of self-surrender before the Gospel and the Cross.
[^596]: Fol. 129r. Penitential confession formula: *И҆сповѣ́даю тебѣ̀ Го́споди Бо́же небесѝ и҆ землѝ всѧ̑ та́йнаѧ се́рдца мое́го* ("I confess unto Thee, O Lord God of heaven and earth, all the secrets of my heart"; cf. Matthew 11:25, Psalm 43:22 [LXX 43:21]).
[^597]: Fol. 129r. Second sacerdotal prayer over the penitent: *Сы́й превѣ́чный влады́ко Го́споди...* (Greek: *Ὁ ὢν προαιώνιος Δέσποτα Κύριε...*). This prayer resembles the ancient pre-baptismal and exorcistic prayers, here applied to the reconciliation of a penitent: recounting the creation of man in the image and likeness of God (Genesis 1:26), the Fall, and redemption through the Incarnation of Christ (*въчеловѣ́ченіемъ Христа̀ Твоего̀*).
[^598]: Fol. 129r. Illumination of the penitent's spiritual faculties: *ѿвръ́зи є҆мꙋ̀ ѡ҆́чи зрѣ́ти свѣ́тъ є҆ѵⷢлїа твое́го* ("open his eyes to behold the light of Thy Gospel"; cf. 2 Corinthians 4:4–6, Ephesians 1:18), continuing onto Fol. 129v.
[^599]: Fol. 129v. Guardian angel and protection from demonic attack, citing Psalm 90:6 (LXX 90:6: *бѣ́са полꙋ́деннаго* / Greek: *δαιμονίου μεσημβриνοῦ* — the midday demon, associated in the ascetic tradition of Evagrius Ponticus and John Cassian with acedia and spiritual despair).
[^600]: Fol. 129v. Classical exorcistic catalog of vices embedded within the penitential prayer: purging the passions that nest within the heart (cf. [^601]: Fol. 129v. Restoration of baptismal dignity: a reason-endowed sheep (*ѻ҆вча̀ слове́сно* / Greek: *πρόβατον λογικόν*) of Christ's flock (John 10:11–16), an honorable vessel (*сосꙋ́дъ че́стенъ* / 2 Timothy 2:21), a child of light (Ephesians 5:8), and an heir of the Kingdom (Romans 8:17).
[^602]: Fol. 129v. The moral prerequisite of obedience to the divine commandments (*по за́повѣдехъ твои́хъ пожи́въ*), continuing onto Fol. 130r.
[^603]: Fol. 130r. Baptismal imagery applied to the sacrament of penance: preserving the seal of repentance (*печа́ть покаѧ́нїа невреди́моу*) and the soul's undefiled garment (*дꙋше́внꙋю сѝ ри́зꙋ нескве́рнꙋ*), recalling the white baptismal robe (*chiton*) cleansed anew through tears of contrition.
[^604]: Fol. 130r. Trinitarian concluding ecphonesis, abbreviated in the printed ink witness: *нн҃ѣ и҆ прⷭно :~* ("now and ever [and unto ages of ages. Amen]").
[^605]: Fol. 130r. Section title: *И҆СПОВѢДА́НЇЄ КЪ Ѻ҃Ц҃ОУ дꙋхо́вномоу и҆́нокѡмъ* ("Confession unto the Spiritual Father for Monks"). This detailed monastic penitential catalog (*confessio generalis* / *поновле́ніе*) provides a systematic self-accusation formula tailored to monastics and cenobites, enumerating monastic transgressions against chastity, obedience, silence, community life, and ascetic vows.

[^608]: Fol. 130v. Monastic self-accusation against sins of the tongue and judgment: *Ѡклевета́нїа, ѻ҆сꙋжде́нїа* (calumnies and judging of brothers; cf. Matthew 7:1; James 4:11–12; Dorotheus of Gaza, *Discourses*, VI, "On not judging our neighbor").
