[^260]: Fol. 77r. The Crowning of Digamists and Alignment with the Solemn Nuptial Office. Although strict canonical tradition (St. Nicephorus Canon 2; Council of Neocaesarea Canon 7) discouraged or barred nuptial crowning for second marriages, the pastoral practice of the Kyivan-Ruthenian Church, following the custom of the Great Church of Constantinople as expounded by Nicetas of Heraclea (cf. fol. 72v–73r), incorporates the sacramental crowning formula: *"The servant of God... is crowned in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit"* and the triple blessing *"O Lord our God, with glory and honor hast Thou crowned them"* (Psalm 8:5 LXX [MT 8:6]). The rubric directs that the remainder of the office—including the Epistle (Ephesians 5:20–33), Gospel (John 2:1–11), litanies, the Lord's Prayer, the Common Cup, and the dance of Isaiah around the tetrapod—is celebrated identically to the first marriage ("changing nothing"), followed by the dismissal.

[^261]: Fol. 78r. The Order of the Holy Oil (Holy Unction / Euchelaion) Chanted by Seven Priests (*Послѣ́дованїе ст҃го ма́сла пѣва́ємаго ѿ седьмѝ попѡ́въ* / Greek: *Ἀκολουθία τοῦ Ἁγίου Εὐχελαίου*). The Sacrament of Holy Unction, instituted according to the Apostolic injunction in James 5:14–15 ("Is any sick among you? Let him call for the presbyters of the Church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord..."). In Byzantine and Kyivan euchology, the full solemn office is celebrated by a college of seven presbyters (reflecting the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit, Isaiah 11:2), though rubrics permit fewer if seven are unavailable. The rite opens with the preparation of the dish of wheat (*пшени́ца*), symbolizing new life and resurrection (John 12:24), holding a vessel filled with pure olive oil and red wine (commemorating the Good Samaritan, Luke 10:34), together with seven wands wrapped in cotton (*се́дмь па́личицъ*) for the sevenfold anointings.

[^262]: Fol. 78v. White Vestments, Paschal Symbolism, and the Censing at Holy Unction (*ѡ҆блъ́кшисѧ въ фело́ны бѣ́лы*). The rubrics prescribe that all ministering presbyters vest in white or bright phelonia (*λευκαῖς στολαῖς*), symbolizing the radiant joy, healing light, and spiritual resurrection bestowed through Christ's healing touch. The distribution of lit tapers to all seven presbyters and the initial circular censing around the analogion evoke the bridal and paschal character of the sacrament.

[^263]: Fol. 78v. Psalm 142 LXX [MT 143] as the Penitential Prologue to Holy Unction (*Ѱло́мъ рм҃в: Гдⷭ҇и оу҆слы́ши мл҃твоу мою̀...*). Psalm 142—the final Psalm of the Six Psalms of Matins—serves as the universal euchological opening for supplication in times of affliction and illness, pleading for divine mercy, guidance, and rescue from the spiritual and bodily snare of death.

[^264]: Fol. 79r. The Penitential Alleluia in Tone 6. In Byzantine and Slavonic liturgical tradition, chanting the Alleluia with penitential verses replaces the festive "God is the Lord" during Lenten services and rites of penitence and supplication, setting a somber, contrite tone for the unction office.

[^265]: Fol. 79r. Verses of Psalm 6:1–2 LXX [MT 6:1–2]. The Alleluia psalm-verses are drawn from Psalm 6, the first of the seven Penitential Psalms of the Psalter: *"O Lord, rebuke me not in Thine anger, neither chasten me in Thy wrath. Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I am weak; heal me, O Lord, for my bones are troubled."* In liturgical medicine, bodily illness is directly approached within the sphere of divine chastening and spiritual restoration.

[^266]: Fol. 79v. The Classical Byzantine Theotokion of Compassion (*Мл҃рдїа двє́ри ѿвръзѝ на́мъ...* / *Τῆς εὐσπλαγχνίας τὴν πύλην ἄνοιξον ἡμῖν...*). This ubiquitous troparion, common to the Ordinary Beginning of many services, invokes the Mother of God as the portal of divine mercy and refuge from bodily and spiritual perils (*ѿ бѣ́дъ* / *ἐκ τῶν περιστάσεων*).

[^267]: Fol. 79v. Psalm 50 LXX [MT 51]. King David's great psalm of repentance (*"Have mercy on me, O God, according to Thy great mercy..."*) stands as the indispensable threshold before every liturgical canon, purifying the soul for sacramental supplication.

[^268]: Fol. 79v. The Canon of Holy Unction by Arsenios and its Acrostic (*Кано́нъ, є҆моу́же краегране́сїе: мл҃тва є҆ле́а, ѱало́мъ Арсе́нїевъ*). Greek: *Εὐχὴ ἐλαίου, ψαλμὸς Ἀρσενίου*. The canon of Holy Unction is the work of Arsenios (9th century, likely Arsenios of Corfu or a Studite hymnographer). In Greek, the initial letters of the troparia form the alphabetical acrostic: *Εὐχὴ ἐλαίου, ψαλμὸς Ἀρσενίου*. As typical in Church Slavonic translations, the acrostic title is faithfully transcribed and translated in the rubric even though the Slavonic poetic verses cannot sustain the literal Greek acrostic wordplay.

[^269]: Fol. 79v. Ode 1 Heirmos: *Мо́рѧ чръмна́го поучи́нꙋ немо́кръ[ными стопа́ми...]*; Greek: *Θαλάσσης τὸ ἐρυθραῖον πέλαγος ἀβρόχοις ἴχνεσιν...* Tone 4. The printer records the opening incipit, breaking off at *немо́кръ-* ("unwetted..."), directing the chanters to complete the familiar heirmos of the Passover through the Red Sea (Exodus 14).

[^270]: Fol. 80r. The Oil of Divine Compassion and the Parable of the Wise Virgins. Greek: *Μύρῳ τῷ τῆς σῆς εὐσπλαγχνίας, Δέσποτα...* The opening troparion of the canon connects the physical oil of unction (*ἔлαιον*) with divine mercy (*ἔλεος*), playing upon the classical Patristic paronomasia (*elaion / eleos*). The preservation of the oil of the faithful (*съхранѧ́ѧ ма́сло вѣ́рныхъ*) evokes the oil of good works and grace preserved in the lamps of the wise virgins (Matthew 25:1–13).

[^271]: Fol. 80r. The Mystical Anointing and the Universal Earthly Mercy. Greek: *Ἵλεων τῷ σῷ ἐλαίῳ, Δέσποτα...* Invoking Psalm 32:5 LXX [MT 33:5] ("The earth is full of the mercy of the Lord"), the hymn affirms that sacramental anointing mystically communicates that transcendent divine mercy which surpasses all human comprehension (Philippians 4:7).

[^272]: Fol. 80v. Apostolic Commission of Anointing and Remission of Sins. Concluding the *Glory* troparion from leaf 80r, which invokes Mark 6:13 ("And they anointed with oil many that were sick, and healed them") and James 5:15 ("And the prayer of faith shall save the sick... and if he have committed sins, they shall be forgiven him"). The sacred unction acts both as bodily medicine and spiritual absolution.

[^273]: Fol. 80v. The Theotokion of Ode 1: The Abyss of Peace (*Поучи́на ми́рови* / Greek: *Ἡ μόνη τὸν ἄβυσσον εἰρήνης κυήσασα*). A play on words between *μύρον* (myrrh/chrism) and *εἰρήνη* (peace) / *мир* (world/peace), honoring the Mother of God as the bearer of the Prince of Peace (Isaiah 9:6), who possesses an ocean of therapeutic healing for afflicted mortals.

[^274]: Fol. 80v. Ode 3 Heirmos: *Весели́тсѧ ѡ҆ тебѣ̀ цр҃кви твоѧ̀ Хрⷭ҇ѐ...*; Greek: *Εὐφραίνεται ἐπὶ σοὶ ἡ Ἐκκλησία σου, Χριστέ...* Tone 4. The familiar heirmos of Ode 3 celebrates the joy of the Church crying aloud: "Thou art my strength, O Lord, my refuge and my firm foundation."

[^275]: Fol. 80v. Christ Marvelous Among Men. Greek: *Σὺ μόνος ὑπάρχων θαυμαστός...* Confessing Christ as the sole source of divine healing and compassion, imploring Him to bestow His sanctifying and restorative grace from on high (*съвы́шше*) upon the suffering Christian.

[^276]: Fol. 80v. The Olive Branch of Noah as Typology of Sacramental Peace (*Ма́сличный сꙋ́чецъ...*). Greek: *Ἐλαίας τὸν κλάδον ποτέ...* Recalling the dove returning to Noah's Ark with an olive twig in its beak, signaling that the waters of the flood had subsided from off the earth (Genesis 8:11). In Christian typology, the olive branch prefigures the reconciliation of God with mankind and the pacification of bodily and spiritual storms through the oil of unction.

[^277]: Fol. 81r. The Radiant Lamp of Divine Light in Holy Oil (*Свѣ́щꙋ бж҃тве́наго свѣ́та...*). Greek: *Λαμπάδα φωτὸς θείου...* Continuing the imagery of the lamp burning before the holy icons and the analogion, beseeching Christ, the true Light of the world (John 8:12), to illuminate the bodily darkness and spiritual weakness of those anointed in faith.

[^278]: Fol. 81r. Maternal Intercession Dispersing Bitter Malady (*При́зри мл҃тивно, мт҃и всѧ́ческимъ тво́рцу...*). Greek: *Ἔπιδε ἵλεως, Μῆτερ τοῦ πάντων Ποιητοῦ...* Pleading for the maternal intercession of the Virgin Mary, the Mother of the Creator, to loose and dissolve the bitter affliction (*го́ркоую зло́боу* / *πικρὰν ὀδύνην*) weighing down the suffering person.

[^279]: Fol. 81r. Sessional Hymn (Kathisma) of Holy Unction and the River of Mercy (*Сѣда́лна, гла́съ д҃. По: Пасты́рскїи сопль вѣща́нїа твоє̀*). Model melody: Greek: *Ὁ ποιμενικὸς αὐλὸς τῆς θεολογίας σου...* (*The shepherd's pipe of thy theology / preaching*), the famous podoben in Tone 4 originally composed for hierarch-theologians (St. Gregory of Nazianzus, Jan 25; St. John Chrysostom, Nov 13). Here adapted to the euchological supplication of Unction, praising Christ as the boundless river of mercy (Psalm 45:5 LXX [MT 46:4]) and inexhaustible fountain of miracles (*и҆сто́чникъ неза́вистно* / *πηγὴν ἀφθόνως*) who cleanses and heals all believers.

[^280]: Fol. 81v. Conclusion of the Sessional Hymn. Concluding the earnest plea of the faithful that they ever have recourse to Christ's divine goodness (*бл҃гти молѧ́щесѧ*).

[^281]: Fol. 81v. Model Melody: *Tone 4, Podoben: "Thou Who wast lifted up upon the Cross" (Възнесы́йсѧ)*. Greek: *Ὁ ὑψωθεὶς ἐν τῷ Σταυρῷ ἑκουσίως...* The solemn Tone 4 podoben of the Feast of the Universal Exaltation of the Cross (Sept 14) and general troparion of the Holy Cross.

[^282]: Fol. 81v. Christ the Supreme Physician and Deliverer from Death (*Вра́чъ и҆ помо́щникъ болѧ́щимъ...*). Greek: *Ἰατρὸς καὶ βοηθὸς τῶν καμνόντων...* This sessional hymn evokes the core pastoral heart of Holy Unction: confessing Christ as the sole true Healer of soul and body, pleading that the sick servant be snatched from the jaws of impending bodily death to glorify God in the assembly of the living (Psalm 117:17–18 LXX [MT 118:17–18]).

[^283]: Fol. 81v. Ode 4 Heirmos: *Въздви́жена тѧ̀ ви́дѣвше цр҃кви...*; Greek: *Ἐπαρθέντα σε ἰδοῦσα ἡ Ἐκκλησία ἐπὶ Σταυροῦ...* Tone 4. Drawing upon the prophecy of Habakkuk (Habakkuk 3:11), the Church contemplates Christ elevated upon the Cross as the Sun of Righteousness, standing steadfast in her liturgical station.

[^284]: Fol. 81v. Christ as Incorruptible Myrrh Poured Forth (*Ꙗ҆́ко нетлѣ́нно му́ро є҆си Хрⷭ҇ѐ, и҆злива́емое бл҃гтїю...*). Greek: *Ὡς ἄφθαρτον μύρον, Χριστέ, ἐκκενωθὲν τῇ χάριτι...* Alluding to the Song of Songs 1:3 ("Thy name is as ointment [myrrh] poured forth"), identifying Christ Himself as the fragrant, heavenly unguent that cleanses the cosmos and soothes the festering ulcers and sores of fallen mortality.
