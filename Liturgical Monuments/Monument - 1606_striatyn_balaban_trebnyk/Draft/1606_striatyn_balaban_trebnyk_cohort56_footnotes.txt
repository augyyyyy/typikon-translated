[^1578]: Fol. 267r. Theological anthropology of bodily dissolution at death: the severance of soul and body ordained by God so that evil might not become immortal (a classical Patristic theme rooted in St. Gregory of Nazianzus, *Oratio 38 in Theophania*, and St. John Damascene), reserving the reunion of soul and reconstituted flesh for the General Resurrection (*до ѡ҆́бщаго въскр҃ні́ѧ*).

[^1579]: Fol. 267r. Triadological supplication: the hierarch addresses the unoriginate Father (*безна́чалному ѿц҃ꙋ̀*), the Only-begotten Son (*є҆диноча́домꙋ твоемоу̀ сн҃оу*), and the consubstantial and life-creating Spirit (*є҆диносоу́щномꙋ и҆ животворя́щемꙋ ти дх҃ꙋ*), imploring that God's handiwork not be consumed by ultimate destruction.

[^1580]: Fol. 267v. The petition for the soul's ranking in the assembly of the righteous (*въ лицѣ̀ пра́ведныхъ*), appealing to God's immeasurable mercy (*безмѣ́рнаа твоѧ̀ ми́лость*) and ineffable love for mankind (*чл҃колю́бі́е неи҆зрече́нное*).

[^1581]: Fol. 267v. Detailed catalog of canonical impediments, curses, and excommunications: parental imprecations from father or mother (*поклѧ́твою ѿчею, и҆ли ма́терні́ю*), self-curses (*сво́емꙋ проклѧ́ті́ю попа́дъ*), clerical censure resulting from grievance caused to a priest (*кое́го ѿ ѡ҆сщ҃е́нныхъ ѡ҆горчи́въ*), and episcopal excommunication neglected through sloth (*ѿ а҆рхі́ереѧ въ тѧ́жкое ѿлоуче́ні́е въпа́дъ, ѡ҆ не́мже неради́въ, и҆ въ лѣ́ности пребыва́ѧ*).

[^1582]: Fol. 268r. Apostolic authority of the keys (Matt. 16:19; Matt. 18:18): Christ's grant of binding and loosing to the Apostle Peter (*врьхо́вномоу петроу̀ сі́ю вла́сть да́вый*) transmitted through sacramental ordination unto the episcopate and priesthood to remit sins of both soul and body.

[^1583]: Fol. 268r. Forgiveness in both ages: invoking absolution in the present age and in that to come (*въ ны́нѣшнемъ вѣ́цѣ, и҆ въ бꙋ́доущемъ*, echoing Matt. 12:32), through the intercession of the Theotokos and all saints.

[^1584]: Fol. 268r. Title and opening rubric for the Second Prayer of Absolution (*Мли́тва втора́ѧ, томоу́жде*), proclaimed over the deceased by the hierarch or priest.

[^1585]: Fol. 268v. Petrine confession and ecclesiological authority: the founding of the Church upon the chief of the disciples, Peter (Matt. 16:18), the conferral of the keys of the Kingdom of Heaven (*клю́ча є҆моу̀ да́въ црⷭ҇тві́а нбⷭ҇наго*), and the transmission of this authority unto the ministers of the Church.

[^1586]: Fol. 269r. Tripartite classification of sins: remitting all offenses committed in word, deed, or thought (*сло́вомъ, и҆лѝ дѣ́ломъ, и҆лѝ въ мы́сли*).

[^1587]: Fol. 269r. Liberation from self-inflicted bonds arising from arrogance (*ѡ҆ себѣ̀ ѿ възноше́ні́а*), external episcopal suspensions, and diabolical stumbling (*зави́сті́ю и҆ споспѣ́шествомъ лоука́ваго*).

[^1588]: Fol. 269v. Final resolution of the body into nature (*тѣ́лꙋ же ѿ тебе създа́нномоу є҆сткꙋ̀ да́тисѧ*) and reception of the soul among the saints who have pleased God throughout all ages (*съ ст҃ы́ми и҆́же ѿ вѣ́ка тебѣ̀ бл҃гоꙋго́ждъшими*).

[^1589]: Fol. 269v. Renaissance woodcut vignette (foliate tailpiece) marking the formal conclusion of the Prayers of Absolution over the departed.

[^1590]: Fol. 270r. Woodcut headpiece depicting Renaissance scrollwork with tritons wielding clubs and serpents, opening Part XXIII: The Order for Burial on Holy Pascha and throughout Bright Week (*Подобаетъ вѣдати, аще кто преставится на святое воскресенїе, или въ который любо день свѣтлыя недѣля*).

[^1591]: Fol. 270r. Suspension of ordinary funeral mourning during Bright Week: the Typikon explicitly forbids singing the customary funeral sequences (*ничто́же надъ ни́мъ пое́тъ сѧ ѻ҆бы́чно ꙋ҆со́пше*), substituting the radiant joy of the Paschal Canon of St. John of Damascus (*Воскресе́нїя день*).

[^1592]: Fol. 270v. Intercalation of Paschal and funeral hymnography: the Paschal Hypakoe (*Предвари́ша оу҆́тро*), followed after Ode 6 by the funeral Kontakion (*Со святы́ми упоко́й*) and Oikos (*Самъ еди́нъ есѝ безсме́ртный*), bridging the grief of physical bereavement with the certainty of Christ's resurrection.

[^1593]: Fol. 270v. Paschal liturgical substitutions: the baptismal canticle *As many as have been baptized into Christ* (Gal. 3:27) replaces the customary Trisagion (*Святы́й Бо́же*); the first Matins Resurrection Gospel (Matt. 28:16–20) is proclaimed; and the final kiss of peace is accompanied by the triumphant greeting *Christ is risen!* (*Хрїсто́съ воскре́се*).

[^1594]: Fol. 270v. Chanting of the Paschal Exapostilarion *Having fallen asleep in the flesh* (*Пло́тїю оу҆сну́въ*, twice), followed by the Funeral Evlogetaria.

[^1595]: Fol. 271r. Concluding Paschal hymnody: the Funeral Evlogetaria (*Благослове́нъ есѝ Го́споди, научи́ мя оправда́ниемъ Твои́мъ: А́нгельскїй собо́ръ*), followed by the Paschal Stichera with their verses (*Да воскре́снетъ Богъ* / *Па́сха свяще́нная*), and the Paschal troparion *Christ is risen* sung thrice.

[^1596]: Fol. 271r. Liturgical ethos of Christian burial in Paschaltide: interment is conducted not with lamentation, but *with thanksgiving and with joy* (*съ бл҃годаре́ні́емъ, и҆ ра́дості́ю*), reflecting the Orthodox conviction that for the believer who dies in the radiant light of Pascha, death is transformed into life and entrance into the eternal banquet.

[^1597]: Fol. 271v. Blank leaf concluding Part XXIII (the Paschal Funeral Order) before Part XXIV (The Order for the Burial of Infants) begins on Fol. 272r.
