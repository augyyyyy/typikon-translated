*Troparion:*
Hope strengthened the choirs of the Martyrs, and with winged ardor inflamed them toward Thy love, prefiguring unto them the future, truly unshakable rest: whereof vouchsafe Thy faithful one, O Good One, who has been translated.[^1511]

*Troparion:*
Be well pleased, O Christ, that he who has been translated in faith may obtain the radiant and divine illumination of Thy countenance, granting him rest in the bosom of Abraham, as Thou alone art merciful, and vouchsafing him eternal blessedness.[^1512]

*Troparion:*
O Thou Who art by nature good and merciful, and desirous of mercy, an abyss of compassion...

*Theotokion:*
We know thee, O pure one, to be the holy Tabernacle, and the Ark, and the Table of the law of grace; for through thee remission was granted unto those justified by the blood of Him Who was incarnate of thy womb, O all-immaculate one.[^1514]

*The deacon:*
**Deacon:** Again and again in peace let us pray to the Lord.
*And the rest. And after the exclamation, we begin the Idiomela of John Damascene the monk. Tone 1:*[^1515]

*Tone 1:*
What earthly pleasure abides unmixed with sorrow? Or what glory...

=== LEAF p523 ===
...stands upon the earth immutable? All things are fainter than a shadow, and all things are more deceptive than a dream; in a single hour, death takes all these things away. But in the light of Thy countenance, O Christ, and in the delight of Thy beauty, give rest unto him whom Thou hast chosen, as Thou art the Lover of mankind.[^1516]

*If thou wilt sing also the other stanzas, look in the Monastic Funeral (see fol. 334);[^1517] but we have set down here as it is in the Euchologion of the Most Holy Patriarch Meletios, without alteration.[^1518] Tone 2:*

*Tone 2:*
Woe is me! What a struggle the soul undergoes as it is parted from the body! Woe is me! How many tears it sheds then...

=== LEAF p524 ===
...and there is none to have mercy upon it! Lifting its eyes unto the Angels, it prays in vain; stretching out its hands unto men, it has none to help. Therefore, my beloved brethren, having reflected upon our brief life, let us ask of Christ rest for him who is translated, and for our souls great mercy.[^1519]

*Tone 3:*
All human things are vanity, whatsoever abides not after death; riches abide not, nor does glory go down with us; for when death comes upon us, it destroys all these things. Therefore let us cry aloud unto the immortal Christ: Give rest unto him who has been translated from us, where the abode of all who rejoice...

=== LEAF p525 ===
...is found.[^1520]

*Tone 4:*
Where is worldly attachment? Where is the fantasy of transient things? Where is gold and silver? Where is the multitude of servants and the tumult? All are dust, all are ashes, all are a shadow! But come, let us cry aloud unto the immortal King: O Lord, vouchsafe Thy eternal good things unto him who has been translated from us, and give him rest in the ageless blessedness.[^1521]

*Tone 5:*
I called to mind the Prophet crying aloud: "I am earth and ashes"; and again I looked into the graves and beheld the bare bones, and I said: Who then is this? A king, or a beggar? Or...

=== LEAF p526 ===
...a righteous man, or a sinner? But give rest, O Lord, unto Thy servant with the righteous.[^1522]

*Tone 6:*
My beginning and my substance was Thy creative command; for wishing to fashion me as a living creature from both the invisible and the visible natures, Thou didst form my body from the earth, and gavest me a soul by Thy divine and life-creating breathing. Therefore, O Savior, give rest unto Thy servant in the land of the living and in the tabernacles of the righteous.[^1523]

*Tone 7:*
Having created man in the beginning according to Thine image and likeness, Thou didst place him in paradise to have dominion over Thy...

=== LEAF p527 ===
...creatures. But deceived by the envy of the devil, having partaken of the food, he became a transgressor of Thy commandments; therefore Thou didst sentence him, O Lord, to return again unto the earth whence he was taken, and to ask for rest.[^1524]

*Tone 8:*
I weep and I lament when I contemplate death, and behold our beauty, fashioned according to the image of God, lying in the graves, formless, inglorious, having no shape. O wonder! What is this mystery that has befallen us? How have we been surrendered unto corruption, and how have we been yoked unto death? Truly by God's...

...command, as it is written, Who grants rest unto the departed.[^1525]

*Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God.*
*Troparion:*
