[^1173]: Eucharistic fear of burning: The dread of approaching the Eucharistic coal (cf. Isaiah 6:6–7), fearing to be consumed by the divine fire (*да невъмѣ́сто просвѣще́нїа ѡ҆па́ленъ бꙋ́дꙋ*), echoing the liturgical warnings in the Byzantine Pre-Communion prayers.
[^1174]: Fourfold fruits of Communion: Reception of the Holy Mysteries *unto enlightenment, unto delight, unto joy, and unto gladness of soul and body* (*въ просвѣще́нїе, въ наслажде́нїе, въ ра́дость, и҆ въ весе́лїе дꙋши и҆ тѣ́лоу мое́моу*).
[^1175]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink introducing the fourth pre-communion prayer of St. John of Damascus (*ТОГО́ЖЕ І҆ѠА́ННА ДАМА́СКИНА • МЛ҃ИТВА КЪ СТ҃О́МОУ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ*), continuing immediately onto leaf p392.
[^1176]: Intercession for enemies: The penitent prays for those who hate, persecute, reproach, and slander him (cf. Matt. 5:44, Luke 6:27–28), pleading that no one may suffer condemnation on his account either in this age or in the age to come. Marginal rubric `ді҃` denotes Prayer 14 of the sequential pre-communion series.
[^1177]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink introducing the first pre-communion prayer of St. Symeon the Metaphrast (*СТ҃ГО СѶМЕѠ́НА МЕТАФРА́СТА • МЛ҃ТВА КЪ СТ҃О́Мꙋ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ*).
[^1178]: Christological prologue of St. Symeon the Metaphrast: Addressing Christ as the *only pure and incorrupt Lord* (*Еди́нъ чⷭ҇тыи и҆ нетлѣ́нный Гьⷭ҇*), Who in His ineffable philanthropy assumed human nature from the pure virginal blood of the Theotokos (continuing onto leaf p393).
[^1179]: Incarnation by the Holy Spirit: Confessing the virginal conception through the overshadowing of the Holy Spirit and the good pleasure of the eternal Father (Luke 1:35).
[^1180]: Pauline Christological titles: Christ as *Wisdom of God, and Peace, and Power* (1 Cor. 1:24, Eph. 2:14).
[^1181]: Instruments of the Passion (*Arma Christi*): The Crucifixion, Cross, nails, spear, and death invoked as spiritual weapons to put to death the carnal passions of the flesh (Col. 3:5).
[^1182]: Despoiling of hades and mystical burial: Spiritual typology linking Christ's burial and the harrowing of hell (*а҆́дова плѣни́лъ є҆сѝ црⷭ҇твїа*) to the mortification of evil thoughts and the destruction of demonic spirits.
[^1183]: Resurrection of the Forefather: Commemorating Christ's raising of Adam (*па́дшаго пр҃аѡ҆ц҃а*) by His third-day resurrection (continuing onto leaf p394).
[^1184]: Deification of human nature through the Ascension: The dogmatic theology of Christ deifying the assumed flesh (*пло́тьское ѻ҆божи́въ въспрїѧ́тїе*) and seating human nature at the right hand of the Father (Heb. 1:3).
[^1185]: Right-hand portion of the elect: Scriptural allusion to Matthew 25:31–34, pleading through Holy Communion to be counted among the sheep on the right hand.
[^1186]: Pentecostal vessel: Echo of Acts 2:1–4, petitioning that the communicant become a receptive temple and dwelling-place of the Holy Spirit (*прїѧ́телище... прише́ствїа*).
[^1187]: The Parousia and meeting in the clouds: Eschatological petition drawn from 1 Thessalonians 4:17 and Acts 17:31, longing to meet the Judge and Creator upon the clouds at His glorious return (continuing onto leaf p395).
[^1188]: Trinitarian doxology concluding the first prayer of St. Symeon the Metaphrast.
[^1189]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink introducing the second pre-communion prayer of St. Symeon the Metaphrast (*ТОГО́ЖЕ СТ҃ГО СѶМЕѠ́НА МЕТАФРА́СТА МЛ҃ТВА КЪ СТ҃О́МОУ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ*). Marginal numeral `ѕ҃і` designates Prayer 16 in the codex's numbering scheme.
[^1190]: Mystical contemplative hymnography: The poetic apostrophe to the Trinity as an eternal stream of inexhaustible goodness (*бл҃гти неистощи́маѧ въ вѣ́къ текоу́щи*) and insatiable divine beauty (*доброто̀ мнѡгорачи́телнаѧ, неимы́ сы́тости*).
[^1191]: Justification by faith and hope: The ascetic cry *save me by faith alone, and through hope* (*вѣ́рою є҆ди́ною сп҃си́ мѧ, и҆ чрезъ наде́ждꙋ*), echoing the Pauline theme of salvation by grace through faith (Rom. 3:28, Eph. 2:8).
[^1192]: Classical Orthodox Trinitarian formula: *Trinity in Persons, and Unity in Essence* (*трⷭ҇це ли́ци, и҆ є҆дини́це сꙋ́ществомъ* / Greek Τριὰς τοῖς προσώποις, καὶ Μονὰς τῇ οὐσίᾳ).
[^1193]: Poetic strophic refrain: Repetition of the petition *By faith alone save me, and through hope* (*вѣ́рою є҆диною сп҃си мѧ̀, и҆ чрезъ наде́ждꙋ*).
[^1194]: God as Purifying Fire: Invoking the Divine Trinity as a cleansing fire (*Ѽ ѻ҆́гню чисти́телный*) to consume sins (cf. Heb. 12:29).
[^1195]: Ascetic psychology of the nous: The sovereignty of the intellect and mind (*оу҆́мъ*) reigning over the passions and sensory deliberations (*помышле́нїа*), reflecting Evagrian and Sinaitic neptic theology.
[^1196]: Intellectual illumination: Pleading for the *intellectual candle* (*оу҆́мноую свѣ́щю*) to dispel the darkness of sin and illuminate the inner chamber of the heart.
[^1197]: Whiteness of the penitent soul: Echoing Psalm 50:9 (LXX: *"Thou shalt wash me, and I shall be made whiter than snow"*, ἀκουτιεῖς με... ὑπὲρ χιόνα λευκανθήσομαι).
[^1198]: Petition for an Angel of Light: Asking the Holy Trinity to send a radiant angel as guardian, educator, and defender of the soul and body (continuing onto leaf p397).
[^1199]: Patristic angelology: Invoking the Guardian Angel as *tutor* (*пѣстоу́нъ*, Greek παιδαγωγός, Gal. 3:24), *protector* (*прѣста́тель*), *guardian of soul and body*, and *guide unto salvation*.
[^1200]: Ascetic tears of compunction: The ascetic doctrine of *penthos* (mourning) and tears as a second baptism, praying that streams of malice be dried up and showers of tears poured out.
[^1201]: God of the fallen and sinful: Theological affirmation that Christ came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance (Matt. 9:13, Mark 2:17, 1 Tim. 1:15), being accessible to all who have apostatized or transgressed.
[^1202]: Sacramental confession and patterns of repentance: Highlighting the divine institution of confession as the vehicle of salvation (*и҆сповѣ́данїа оу҆ста́ви є҆сѝ сп҃сенїе*), providing biblical exemplars of penitence (continuing onto leaf p398).
[^1203]: Repetition of the self-accusing catalog: Pleading for mercy as an unclean, deceitful, wretched, darkened, and defiled creature (*мнѣ̀ те́мномоу и҆ ѡ҆сквръне́номꙋ*).
[^1204]: Allusion to the Prayer of Manasseh: Quoting Manasseh 9: *"For my transgressions are multiplied more than the sand of the sea, and my iniquities are increased"* (*па́че числа̀ безчи́сленыхъ звѣ́здъ, па́че числа̀ пѣска̀ мо́рскаго съгрѣши́хъ ти*).
[^1205]: Comprehensive pollution of all faculties: Confessing that every imaginable form and shame of evil has been practiced in soul, thoughts, words, and deeds.
[^1206]: Fetters of sinful delusion: Comparing the delusion of the mind and presumption (*мнѣ́нїе*, Greek οἴησις) to heavy iron collars bowing down the neck of the soul (continuing onto leaf p399).
[^1207]: Inescapable bonds of sin: The constriction of the heart and impossibility of looking up, directly echoing Psalm 39:13 (LXX: *"my iniquities have taken hold upon me, and I am not able to look up"*).
[^1208]: Cosmic unworthiness: The fourfold anaphora of unworthiness (*недосто́йнѣ*): unworthily beholding the heavens, beholding human faces, counseling one's neighbor, and even treading upon the earthly dust.
[^1209]: Kindling of divine wrath: Expressing the gravity of voluntary transgression which inflames the righteous indignation of God (cf. Deut. 32:22).
[^1210]: Contempt of the divine will: Rejecting God's pure will and holy statutes, failing to desire any of His pure commandments (continuing onto leaf p400).
[^1211]: Scriptural quote from Psalm 18:10–11 (LXX): *"The judgments of the Lord are true... more desirable than gold and much precious stone"* (LXX Ps. 18:10–11: τὰ κρίματα Κυρίου ἀληθινά... ἐпиθυμητὰ ὑπὲρ χρυσίον).
[^1212]: Scriptural quote from the Prayer of Manasseh: Quoting Prayer of Manasseh 11: *"And now, behold, I bend the knee of my heart, entreating Thy goodness"* (*прекланѧ́ю ти срⷣца мое́го колѣ́нѣ* / Greek καὶ νῦν κλίνω γόνυ καρδίας).
[^1213]: Liturgical triad of bodily contrition: The physical prostration of soul and body expressed through the bowing of the knees of the heart, the darkened head, and the defiled neck.
[^1214]: Liturgical rhythmic confession: The solemn sixfold repetition of *I have sinned* (*съгрѣши́хъ*), cataloging offenses, injustices, transgressions of the commandments, judging of others, and shameless apostasy.
[^1215]: Boldness of penitent supplication: Trusting in divine goodness despite conscious guilt, daring to offer supplication (*но̀ моле́нїе творю̀ твое́й бл҃гти, но мольбоу приношꙋ̀*, continuing onto leaf p401).
