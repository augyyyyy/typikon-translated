[^4190]: Leaf p1351 (fol. 668r) carries the red running header *ст҃ы́хъ ѿц҃ъ* (*Of the Holy Fathers*). In the printed 1606 text, the bottom of leaf p1350 concludes with an incomplete canon concerning usurious commerce by priests (*І҆ере́й а҆́ще коу́плю дѣ́ѧй лихои́мїкомъ,*), while leaf p1351 resumes the regulations concerning women and childbirth from leaves p1349–p1350 without explicit transitional text, reflecting either a thematic juxtaposition in the Balaban source manuscript or a compositor's gathering arrangement in the Striatyn press.

[^4191]: *Ба́бы слꙋжа́щїи на рожде́нїихъ* (midwives serving at births). Midwives attending childbirth are permitted access to Holy Communion by the seventh day (*до се́дмаго дн҃е*) in view of their indispensable and benevolent ministry (*и҆́снꙋ потре́бноу слꙋ́жбꙋ слꙋ́жатъ*), whereas casual women attending without necessity are subjected to an exclusion from the church of twelve, fifteen, or twenty days according to their degree of involvement. Compare the Penitential Nomocanon (*Nomokanon po Bol'shomu Trebniku*, ch. 48) and the Nomocanon of John the Faster.

[^4192]: *А́ще ли жена̀ роди́тъ на ст҃о́ую па́схоу... и҆ да и҆спива́етъ а҆гїа́змо*: A woman who gives birth on Holy Pascha is permitted bodily washing and the drinking of holy water (*а҆гїа́змо*, Gk. ἁγίασμα) until the seventh day.

[^4193]: Leaf p1352 (fol. 668v) carries the red running header *за́повѣди* (*Injunctions*). This canon anathematizing a woman who abandons her husband out of abhorrence of marital union derives directly from Canon 14 of the Council of Gangra (c. 340): *Εἴ τις γυνὴ καταλιμπάνοι τὸν ἄνδρα, καὶ ἀναχωρεῖν ἐθέλοι, βδελυσσομένη τὸν γάμον, ἀνάθεμα ἔστω* ("If any woman abandon her husband, and resolve to depart from him, despising marriage: let her be anathema").

[^4194]: Scriptural citations: 1 Corinthians 11:3 ("The head of every man is Christ, and the head of the woman is the man") and Ephesians 5:23 ("For the husband is the head of the wife, even as Christ is the head of the church"). The rebellious wife is typologically paired with Delilah (*дали́да*, Judges 16) and Jezebel (*є҆заве́ль*, 1 Kings / 3 Kingdoms 21, 2 Kings / 4 Kingdoms 9).

[^4195]: The phrase *боу́и мꙋ́жъ* (a foolish, senseless, or unruly husband; cf. Gk. μωρός / ἄφρων) introduces a pastoral qualification to Pauline domestic subjection: when a husband is intellectually or morally deranged (*развраще́нъ ра́зꙋмомъ*) and his counsel defective (*съвѣ́тъ е҆го недоста́точна*), a wise wife is not bound to uncritical obedience in every matter.

[^4196]: Leaf p1353 (fol. 669r) carries the red running header *ст҃ы́хъ ѿц҃ъ* (*Of the Holy Fathers*). Concluding the sentence from p1352: *Ѻ҆вогда́ же и҆ моу́жъ да послоу́шаетъ женоу̀ свою̀* ("and sometimes let the husband also obey his wife"), emphasizing mutual marital discernment.

[^4197]: Direct canonical citation of Canon 17 of the Council of Gangra: *Εἴ τις γυνὴ διὰ νομιζομένην ἄσκησιν ἀποκείροιτο τὰς κόμας, ἃς ἔδωκεν ὁ Θεὸς εἰς ὑπόμνησιν τῆς ὑποταγῆς, ὡς ἀναλύουσα τὸ πρόσταγμα τῆς ὑποταγῆς, ἀνάθεμα ἔστω* ("If any woman, under pretence of asceticism, shaves or cuts off the hair which God gave her as a reminder of subjection: let her be anathema"). Cutting the hair is permissible solely upon entering the monastic state (*кро́мѣ то́чїю и҆ночьскаго житїа*).

[^4198]: The penance of one year's separation from the church and communion for cutting hair under demonic temptation (*наважде́нїемъ нѣ́кыимъ вра́жїимъ*) reflects the secondary penitential scale of John the Faster and the Slavonic Nomocanon.

[^4199]: Canon concerning a wife who deserts her husband and remarries abroad. Compare Canon 9 of St. Basil the Great and Canon 87 of the Council in Trullo (Quinisext).

[^4200]: Leaf p1354 (fol. 669v) carries the red running header *за́повѣди* (*Injunctions*). The prescribed penance includes xerophagy (*сꙋхо ꙗ҆доу́щи хлѣ́бъ свои*, eating only dry bread) on Mondays, Wednesdays, and Fridays, alongside thirty-six daily prostrations over nine years.

[^4201]: The second husband who knowingly cohabits with a runaway married woman shares her canonical guilt; however, if he contracted the marriage in ignorance of her prior union (*невѣ́дѣнїемъ поѧ́тъ ю҆̀*), a mitigated rule (*ма́ло пра́вила*) is assigned to him.

[^4202]: Spousal murder by witchcraft or poisoning (*чарова́нїемъ оу҆мори́тъ*) incurs lifelong ecclesiastical excommunication until the hour of death. Compare Apostolic Canon 65, Canon 7 of the Council of Ancyra, and Canons 8 and 56 of St. Basil the Great.

[^4203]: The concluding clause of leaf p1354 (*Тако́жде же и҆ жена̀ а҆́ще своего*, "Likewise also a woman, if [she betrays/destroys] her [husband]...") breaks off syntactically at the bottom margin without an immediate continuation on leaf p1355, where the text resumes a series of canons regarding priests.

[^4204]: Leaf p1355 (fol. 670r) carries the red heading *Ѡ҆ і҆ере́ехъ* (*Concerning Priests*). The opening clause (*да̀ слоу́житъ, ни же да̀ ѿпꙋща́етъ е҆ѧ̀*, "let him serve, neither let him put her away") continues the canonical rule on a priest whose wife is falsely or unprovenly suspected of infidelity, citing Matthew 19:9. Compare Canon 8 of the Council of Neo-Caesarea and Apostolic Canon 48.

[^4205]: Canonical impediment prohibiting a priest from continuing in sacred orders if he receives monastic tonsure while his wife marries another man, violating canonical order.

[^4206]: Prohibition of self-castration (*А́ще кто̀ са́мъ сѧ скази́тъ, да не бꙋ́детъ по́пъ, оу҆бїи́нца бо є҆́стъ себѣ̀*), based on Apostolic Canon 22 (*Ὁ ἀκρωτηριάσας ἑαυτόν, μὴ γινέσθω κληρικός· αὐτοφονευτὴς γάρ ἐστιν ἑαυτοῦ*) and Canon 1 of the First Ecumenical Council (Nicaea I, 325). Deacons guilty of this act are permanently barred from higher ordination (*ктомꙋ̀ да̀ не прихо́дитъ къ вѧ́щшаѧ*).

[^4207]: Deposition of a clergyman for fornication (*І҆ере́и а҆́ще блоуди́тъ, да и҆зврѣ́жетсѧ*), based on Apostolic Canon 25 and Canon 3 of St. Basil the Great. The presbytera is canonically permitted to separate from her fallen husband to maintain her purity.

[^4208]: Leaf p1356 (fol. 670v) carries the red running header *за́повѣди ст҃ы́хъ ѿц҃ъ* (*Injunctions of the Holy Fathers*). The separated wife of the deposed priest must remain unmarried; if the deposed cleric genuinely desists from sin, they may resume domestic cohabitation, which likewise applies to deacons and readers.

[^4209]: Scriptural exposition of conjugal equality citing 1 Corinthians 7:4 ("The wife does not have authority over her own body, but the husband does; and likewise the husband does not have authority over his own body, but the wife does"), Genesis 2:24, and Matthew 19:6. Marital separation is prohibited except under grave canonical necessity established by ecclesiastical synod and court (*събо́ромъ и҆ сꙋдо́мъ*).

[^4210]: Scriptural quotation from 1 Corinthians 6:18: *Πᾶν ἁμάρτημα ὃ ἐὰν ποιήσῃ ἄνθρωπος ἐκτὸς τοῦ σώματός ἐστιν· ὁ δὲ πορνεύων εἰς τὸ ἴδιον σῶμα ἁμαρτάνει* ("Every sin that a man does is outside the body; but he who commits fornication sins against his own body").

[^4211]: Leaf p1357 (fol. 671r) carries the red running header *ст҃ы́хъ ѿц҃ъ* (*Of the Holy Fathers*). Prescribing conjugal abstinence and separate beds (*дв҃а ѻ҆́дра и҆мѣ́ти*) on Sundays, Dominical Great Feasts, and Holy Week (*въ вели́коую седми́цоу*), in accord with Canon 3 of Dionysius of Alexandria, Canon 13 of Timothy of Alexandria, and the Slavonic Kormchaia Kniga.

[^4212]: Warning against incontinence: uncontrolled carnal indulgence extinguishes the remembrance of death (*па́мѧт смр҃тнаа*) and produces spiritual insensibility (*безъстра́шїе*, lack of the fear of God).

[^4213]: Canonical reception of a repentant prostitute (*блꙋ́дница*): cessation of the sin, sacramental confession, and solemn promise of amendment.

[^4214]: Leaf p1358 (fol. 671v) carries the red running header *за́повѣди* (*Injunctions*). The spiritual father is instructed to lean toward divine philanthropy (*на чл҃колꙋ́бїе възложи́въ*) after a brief penance, admitting the penitent woman to Holy Communion in imitation of Christ's reception of the sinful woman (Luke 7:36–50; cf. John 8:1–11).

[^4215]: Judicial safeguard: a woman accused of poisoning (*ѻ҆тра́вою погоуби́ла є҆́стъ*) is subjected to civil interrogation, torture, and confiscation of property only upon formal judicial verification, strictly prohibiting arbitrary violence before guilt is established.

[^4216]: The phrase *Не подоба́етъ же ни же досто́итъ ѻ҆соуди́ти чл҃ка...* articulates an ecclesiastical prohibition against vigilante accusations and private vengeance.

[^4217]: Leaf p1359 (fol. 672r) carries the red running header *ст҃ы́хъ ѿц҃ъ* (*Of the Holy Fathers*). Scriptural and canonical standard of proof requiring multiple credible witnesses (*мни́ꙁи свѣди́телїе*, cf. Deuteronomy 19:15, Matthew 18:16, 1 Timothy 5:19) before ecclesiastical trial (*по пра́вилоу цр҃ко́вномоу*). Private condemnation, lynching, robbery, and confiscation are condemned under anathema.

[^4218]: Capital civil-canonical penalty against anyone (slave or free) who forcibly or seductively leads a Christian into heresy (*въ є҆́ресь нѣ́коую*). Compare Title XVII of the Byzantine Ecloga and the Procheiron.

[^4219]: Civil-canonical penalty for manstealing / kidnapping (*оу҆кра́детъ чл҃ка... и҆ли дитѧ̀*), carrying over to leaf p1360.

[^4220]: Leaf p1360 (fol. 672v) carries the red running header *за́повѣди* (*Injunctions*). The penalty of hand amputation (*рꙋцѣ̀ е҆моу̀ да̀ ѿсѣкꙋ́тсѧ*) for stealing and selling a person reflects the criminal statutes of the Byzantine Ecloga (Ecloga XVII.32: *Ὁ ἐλεύθερον ἀνδραποδίζων χειροκοπείσθω*) and the Slavonic *Zakon Sudnyi Liudem*.

[^4221]: Mitigation of murder charge for a husband killing an adulterous wife *in flagrante delicto*: rather than capital punishment, he is sentenced to banishment into exile (*въ заточе́нїе*), and half his estate is forfeited to the Great Church (*вели́каа цр҃ковь*, i.e. the episcopal see or cathedral). Compare Basil Canon 8 and the Nomocanons.

[^4222]: Maternal neglect resulting in severe illness of a child: one year's penance with fifty daily prostrations.

[^4223]: Maternal negligence causing the accidental death of a child by falling from a height: one year's penance and one hundred and fifty daily prostrations (involuntary manslaughter, cf. Basil Canon 8).

[^4224]: Excessive or unjust corporal punishment resulting in a child's death (*бїе́тъ дитѧ̀ своѐ напра́сно, и҆ оу҆мре́тъ*): six years' penance and one hundred and fifty daily prostrations. Compare St. Basil the Great, Canons 8 and 52.
