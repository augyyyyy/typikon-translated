[^3008]: Church Slavonic rubric: *Бл҃годаре́нїе на ко́ливо на трапе́зѣ* ("Thanksgiving over the Kolyvo at the Trapeza"). In traditional Byzantine and Ruthenian practice, following the Divine Liturgy or memorial service, the *kolyvo* (boiled wheat with honey) is brought from the church into the monastic refectory (*trapeza*) or common dining hall, where it is elevated, blessed with thanksgiving, and distributed among the brethren while chanting festive or memorial acclamations.
[^3009]: The proclamation commemorates the civil and political authorities: *благочести́вымъ, и҆ дръжа́внымъ ст҃ы́мъ госпѡда́ремъ на́шимъ, и҆ цр҃емъ, и҆ кнѧѕе́мъ* ("our pious and sovereign holy rulers, and tsars, and princes"). This composite formula reflects the recensional context of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth and neighboring Ruthenian/Slavic lands, combining the title of *hospodar* (ruler/prince in Ruthenia and the Danubian principalities of Moldavia and Wallachia) with imperial (*tsar*) and princely (*kniaz*) titulature.
[^3010]: Church Slavonic heading: *Въ соубѡ́тоу* ("On Saturday"). Saturday is liturgically consecrated throughout the Byzantine rite to the memory of the departed, martyrs, and all reposed righteous ancestors, concluding the refectory kolyvo blessing with the solemn singing of *Eternal Memory* (*Вѣ́чнаѧ и҆̀мъ па́мѧть*).
[^3011]: Church Slavonic heading: *пра́здникѡмъ Госпо́дьскымъ* ("On Feasts of the Lord"). Variable commemorations insert the specific salvation-historical mystery of the feast: the Nativity (*ро́ждшемусѧ*), Theophany/Baptism (*кр҃ти́вшемусѧ*), Transfiguration (*преѡбрази́вшемусѧ*), or Ascension (*възне́сшемусѧ*), followed by corresponding commemorations for the Great Feasts of the Theotokos (Annunciation, Nativity of the Theotokos, Entry into the Temple, and Dormition).
[^3012]: The rubrics provide distinct health and salvation proclamations for ecclesiastical dignitaries: a bishop (*преѡсщ҃е́нномꙋ є҆пⷭ҇пꙋ*), an elder hegumen designated as *прпⷡ҇номꙋ въ є҆рмона́сѣхъ* ("venerable among hieromonks"), or any monastic brother, to which the choir/people respond thrice: *Сп҃си бж҃е... три́щи*.
[^3013]: Church Slavonic heading: *Мл҃тва на ѡ҆чїще́нїе цр҃кви, ѡ҆скверненно́й ѿ є҆рети́къ* ("Prayer for the Cleansing of a Church Defiled by Heretics"). Corresponding to the Byzantine Euchologion rite *Εὐχὴ ἐπὶ καθαρισμῷ ναοῦ μιαнθέντος ὑπὸ αἱρετικῶν*, this prayer was appointed for cases where a consecrated church or altar had been occupied, celebrated in, or profaned by heretics or schismatics, purging the sanctuary through invocations of the Holy Spirit and aspersion with holy water prior to the celebration of the Holy Mysteries.
[^3014]: Church Slavonic heading: *Мл҃тва ю҆́же гл҃етъ а҆рхїере́й, и҆ли дх҃о́вникъ проще́нноу • за грѣхы̀ всѧ̑ во́лныѧ и҆ нево́лныѧ...* ("Prayer of Absolution Said by a Hierarch or Spiritual Father for All Sins, Voluntary and Involuntary..."). Known across Slavic Euchologia by its running title *Праща́лнаѧ* (*Praščal'naja molitva*), this monumental prayer of absolution—traditionally ascribed to Patriarch John the Faster or ancient Byzantine patriarchal practice—is read over penitents, monastics at tonsure or death, or during times of public penance, functioning as an exhaustive liturgical absolution covering every canonical transgression, secret sin, and occult involvement.
[^3015]: The prayer anchors its petition in three gospel exemplars of divine mercy: the creditor who freely forgave two debtors of five hundred and fifty denarii (Luke 7:41–43); the penitent sinful woman whose many sins were forgiven because she loved much (Luke 7:36–50); and the paralytic at Capernaum whose physical healing was united to the remission of his sins (Matthew 9:1–8; Mark 2:1–12).
[^3016]: The catalogue of sins begins with spiritual and ecclesiastical censures: falling under curses, excommunication by a hierarch or priest, rash self-anathematization, violation of oaths made in the name of God, and cursing fellow Christians in anger (cf. Matthew 5:22; Apostolic Canons 25, 27; Basil the Great, Canons 28, 64).
[^3017]: Denunciation of magical, occult, and divinatory practices: consulting sorceresses (*чародѣ́ицы*), magi/soothsayers (*влъхвы*), and potion-makers (*ѻ҆тра́вницы*, makers of philtres and poisons) who perform illusions and enchantments (*ѡ҆бва́нїа*), strictly forbidden under Canons 61 and 65 of the Council in Trullo (Quinisext).
[^3018]: Systematic enumeration of carnal transgressions and sexual aberrations, distinguishing fornication, adultery, masturbation (*роу́чный блоу́дъ*), corruption of youth (*ѻ҆троча̀ растли́лъ*), sodomy (*содо́мскаа дѣ́ла*), and bestiality (*скотоло́жество*, copulation with quadrupeds or birds), closely adhering to the canonical penitential categories of John the Faster and the Nomokanon.
[^3019]: Ascetical and monastic failures: distraction of the mind during psalmody and divine worship (*стоѧ́щꙋ є҆мꙋ̀ на мл҃итвѣ, и҆ на пѣ́нїи, оу҆́мъ є҆го въ лоука́ваѧ и҆ жите́йскаа ѻ҆бхожда́аше*), secret eating (*скрѡ́вно ꙗ҆дѐ*, Greek λαθροφαγία, eating outside established hours or violating fasts surreptitiously), and nocturnal/bodily pollutions (*и҆стица́нїе*).
[^3020]: Sins of the heart and interpersonal faults: judging a brother's small speck while blind to one's own transgressions (Matthew 7:3–5), disobedience to one's spiritual father, acedia or spiritual despondency (*оуны̀*), and spiritual pride (*възвели́чисѧ ꙗ҆́ко цѣломоу́дренъ є҆́стъ мнѧ̀ себѐ*), continuing onto leaf p1051.
