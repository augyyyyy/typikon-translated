[^1258]: Transgression vs. Divine Philanthropy: The classical theological motif that no magnitude or multitude of human sin can ever surpass the inexhaustible ocean of God's love for mankind (*мнѡ́гое чл҃колꙋ́бїе*), citing St. Symeon the New Theologian's Hymn 17 (*Ἐκ τῶν θείων ἐρώτων*).
[^1259]: Divine Conversance with the Penitent: The mystical paradox that Christ deigns to converse with cleansed penitents as with His own intimate friends (*ꙗ҆́коже дрꙋгѡ́мъ свои́мъ бли́жнимъ*, cf. John 15:14–15).
[^1260]: The Communicant as Grass before Divine Fire: The bold ascetic paradox of the communicant being "grass" (*трава̀ сы́й*) daring to partake of divine Fire, yet marvelously preserved without being consumed, prefigured by the burning bush on Mount Sinai (Exodus 3:2, continuing onto leaf p412).
[^1261]: The Incombustible Bush (*Неопалимая Купина*): The Old Testament theophany of Exodus 3:2 applied typologically to the Eucharistic mystery, wherein divine fire dews rather than consumes the faithful receiver (*нн҃ѣ ѻ҆роша́емь неѻ҆па́лно*).
[^1262]: Whole-person Thanksgiving: The triple grateful confession uniting word (*сло́вомъ*), heart (*срⷣцемъ*), and the bodily and spiritual members (*оу҆́десы и҆ дш҃и и҆ тѣ́ла*).
[^1263]: Incipit of the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed: The rubric prescribes the recitation of the Confession of the Orthodox Faith (*и҆сповѣ́данїе правосла́вныѧ вѣ́ры*), printed only as its canonical opening incipit (*Вѣ́роую въ є҆ди́наго Б҃а*), strictly preserved without unwritten textual expansion per Mandate 16.
[^1264]: The Holy Thursday Eucharistic Troparion: The celebrated Byzantine hymn *Of Thy Mystical Supper* (*Ве́чери твоеѧ̀ та́йнѣй*), juxtaposing the betrayal of Judas with the saving confession of the Good Thief (Luke 23:42).
[^1265]: Epistolary Reading on the Institution of the Eucharist: The liturgical lection from 1 Corinthians 11:23–26 prescribed for the communicant's preparation, recounting the Pauline institution narrative.
[^1266]: The Pauline Anamnesis: The dominical command *This do in remembrance of Me* (*сѐ твори́те въ моѐ въспомина́нїе*, 1 Cor. 11:24–25), underscoring the Eucharistic commemoration of the Lord's death (continuing onto leaf p414).
[^1267]: The Grave Warning of Unworthy Partaking: 1 Corinthians 11:27–32, warning that eating and drinking unworthily incurs guilt against the Body and Blood of the Lord (*пови́ненъ бꙋ́детъ тѣ́лоу и҆ кро́ви Гдⷭ҇ни*) and eating judgment unto oneself.
[^1268]: Gospel Lection of the Bread of Life: The opening of John 6:48–54 addressed to the Jews, identifying Christ as the true Bread of Life (*А҆́зъ є҆́смь хлѣ́бъ живо́тный*) far surpassing the ancestral wilderness manna (continuing onto leaf p415).
[^1269]: The Living Bread and Eschatological Life: John 6:51, proclaiming that whoever eats of this living bread shall live unto ages, identifying the Eucharistic bread with Christ's flesh given for the life of the world.
[^1270]: The Johannine Eucharistic Imperative: John 6:53–54, demanding the eating of the flesh of the Son of Man and the drinking of His Blood as the indispensable prerequisite for eternal life and resurrection at the last day (continuing onto leaf p416).
[^1271]: Concluding Johannine Promise: John 6:54b (*and I will raise him up at the last day*), sealing the Scriptural pre-communion lectionary.
[^1272]: Interior Examination of Conscience: Rubric directing the communicant to search their conscience in mind and soul as they draw near the Holy Gifts (*ѡ҆блича́ѧ въ оу҆мѣ̀ совѣ́сть свою̀*).
[^1273]: Short Prayer of Approach: A brief penitential prayer acknowledging the terror of divine fire while appealing to God's purifying mercy, concluding with the Psalmic citation *It is good for me to cleave unto God* (Psalm 72:28 LXX).
[^1274]: Triple Pre-Communion Exclamation: Rubric prescribing the recitation, if desired even three times, of the awe-filled verses as one approaches the chalice.
[^1275]: The Eucharistic Coal of Isaiah: Echoing the seraphic live coal of Isaiah 6:6–7, warning that the deifying Blood consumes the unworthy while purifying sins (continuing onto leaf p417).
[^1276]: Spiritual and Intellectual Deification: The theological distinction that Christ's holy Flesh deifies the human spirit (*ѡ҆божа́етъ дх҃ъ*) while strangely and marvelously nourishing the mind (*оу҆́мъ же пита́етъ стра́ннѣ и҆ чю́днѣ*).
[^1277]: Rubric for Crosswise Hand Gesture: Prescribing the traditional Byzantine ceremonial posture of crossing one's hands upon the breast (*крⷭ҇тоо́бразно рꙋ́цѣ на пръ́сехъ положи́въ*) when approaching to receive the Holy Mysteries of the Crucified One.
[^1278]: Major Structural Division: The beginning of the Post-Communion Thanksgiving Prayers (*Послѣдованіе по святомъ причащеніи*), marked on leaf p418 by a prominent Renaissance woodcut headpiece, rubric in red display majuscules, and the first prayer numbered `а҃` in the margin.
[^1279]: Rubric of Immediate Thanksgiving: Admonishing the communicant not to delay, but immediately to sing praises and give fervent thanks unto God from the soul after receiving the life-creating Mysteries.
[^1280]: Prayer 1 of Saint Basil the Great: The classic Post-Communion prayer attributed to St. Basil the Great (*Мл҃тва ст҃го вели́каго васїлїа, по ст҃о́мъ прича́щенїи*), expressing profound gratitude for being accounted worthy of the heavenly gifts (continuing onto leaf p419).
[^1281]: The Catalogue of Eucharistic Graces: St. Basil's detailed enumeration of the spiritual fruits of Communion: healing of soul and body, driving away of demonic adversaries, enlightenment of spiritual sight, pacification of inner passions, unfeigned love, and keeping of the commandments.
[^1282]: Living No Longer Unto Oneself: Echoing 2 Corinthians 5:15 and Romans 14:7–8, praying that the communicant, preserved by divine holiness, may live no longer for self, but unto Christ (continuing onto leaf p420).
[^1283]: Eschatological Rest and the Ineffable Beauty: The sublime conclusion of St. Basil's prayer, longing to attain unto everlasting rest (*въ присносꙋ́щный доидоу̀ поко́й*) where the festival of the righteous never ends and the beauty of God's countenance is beheld forever.
[^1284]: Prayer 2 of Saint John Chrysostom: The post-communion prayer attributed to St. John Chrysostom (*И҆́же въ ст҃ы́хъ ѻ҆́ц҃а на́шего і҆ѡа́нна златооу́стаго мл҃, по ст҃о́ причаще́нїи*), marked with numeral `в҃` in the margin.
[^1285]: Ruthenian Lexical Witness *оу҆трѣ́пѣ*: The rare temporal adverbial form *оу҆трѣ́пѣ* (dial./early modern variant corresponding to *заутра* / *рано вранці* / *early this morning*), witnessing the morning celebration of the Divine Liturgy.
