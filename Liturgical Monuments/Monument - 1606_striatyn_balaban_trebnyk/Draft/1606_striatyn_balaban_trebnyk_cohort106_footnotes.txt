[^3023]: Scriptural citation of Matthew 18:18 (cf. Matthew 16:19): the apostolic authority to bind and loose committed by Christ to His Church, forming the canonical and theological foundation of sacramental absolution.
[^3024]: Rubric in red cinnabar: *Разрѣше́нїе прѡсщ҃еннаго патрїарха коньстантина града, кѵръ михаила, рекомаго аѱалона • мл҃тва и҆сповѣда́нїю* ("Absolution of the Most Sacred Patriarch of Constantinople, Kyr Michael, Surnamed Apsalon; a Prayer at Confession"). In Byzantine and South Slavic Euchologia (Goar, *Euchologion*, pp. 544–545; Dmitrievsky, *Opisanie*, II, pp. 278, 412), this personal absolution prayer is attributed to Patriarch Michael II Kourkouas (Oxeites, 1143–1146) or Michael III of Anchialos (1170–1178), transmitted under the epithet *Apsalas* / *Apsalon* (Greek: τοῦ Ἀψαλᾶ). In the Ruthenian Trebnik tradition, it constitutes an intimate pastoral absolution addressed directly to the penitent (*Бра́те, ꙗ҆́ко съкроуше́нно срⷣце мно́го показа́лъ є҆си...*).
[^3025]: Church Slavonic: *и҆ є҆ли́ка помѧноу́лъ є҆си, и҆ и҆списа́въ прише́лъ є҆си* ("and whatsoever things thou hast remembered, and having written them down hast come"). This explicit reference to the penitent bringing a written catalogue of sins (*исписавъ*) provides vital pastoral evidence for Early Modern Ruthenian penitential practice, wherein the faithful were encouraged to prepare for confession by conducting an examination of conscience and noting their sins in writing before approaching the spiritual father.
[^3026]: Church Slavonic rubric: *Мл҃тва на кле́ншихсѧ де́рзостїю* ("Prayer over Those Who Have Sworn Rashly/Audaciously"). Greek: *Εὐχὴ ἐπὶ τῶν ἐξ ἀπονοίας ἢ προπετείας ὀμοσάντων* (Goar, *Euchologion*, p. 556). In canonical jurisprudence (Matthew 5:34–37; James 5:12; St. Basil, Canons 10, 29, 64), taking rash, imprudent, or thoughtless oaths was considered a perilous spiritual transgression binding the conscience (*темне́нїе кла́твы*, "the darkening/bond of an oath"); this prayer looses the penitent from the spiritual burden and censure incurred by hasty swearing.
[^3027]: Church Slavonic rubric: *Мл҃тва въ по́стъ въходѧ́щимъ* ("Prayer for Those Entering upon a Fast"). Greek: *Εὐχὴ ἐπὶ τῶν εἰсеρχομένων εἰς τὴν ἁγίαν τεσσαρακοστήν* (Goar, *Euchologion*, p. 558), appointed to be read at the onset of Great Lent (or the seasonal fasts of the Nativity, Apostles, or Dormition) to invoke divine protection, angelic companionship, and bodily endurance for the ascetical journey.

[^3029]: Church Slavonic rubric: *Мл҃, на рожⷣтво хв҃о дѣ́темъ дх҃овнымъ* ("Prayer on the Nativity of Christ for Spiritual Children"). In 16th- and 17th-century Ruthenian parish and monastic usage, spiritual fathers assembled their penitents (*дѣти духовні*) on major feasts—particularly following the Nativity fast—to bestow an official pastoral blessing, reconcile penitents, and prepare them for Holy Communion.
