[^2951]: Elevation of the Precious Cross, First Elevation (facing East): concluding clause of the first petition (`твое́й , мо́лимтисѧ оу҆слы́ши и҆ поми́лꙋй`), followed by the first hundred of *Lord, have mercy* (*Господи помилуй*). The priest signs with the Cross thrice and slowly bows until his head is one span (*пядь*, approximately 18–20 cm) from the ground, then gradually straightens as the hundred petitions are chanted.
[^2952]: Rubric: at count 97 of the first centenary, the Ekklesiarches cues the choir and priest, who stands fully upright while the remaining three repetitions of *Lord, have mercy* are chanted with an elevated voice (*высоча́йшимъ гла́сомъ*) to complete the hundred.
[^2953]: Station to the South: the priest turns southward and signs with the Cross thrice before intoning the second petition for the sovereign and state (`Є҆щѐ мо́лимсѧ ѡ҆ бл҃гочти́вѣмъ , и҆ бг҃охрани́мѣмъ цр҃и на́шемъ...`).
[^2954]: Second Elevation (facing South) and Station to the West: following the second centenary, the priest turns westward to intone the third petition for the local archbishop, spiritual fathers, and monastic brotherhood.
[^2955]: Third Elevation (facing West) and Station to the North: following the third centenary, the priest turns northward to intone the fourth petition for all afflicted Christian souls (`Є҆щѐ мо́лимсѧ ѡ҆ всѧ́кой дꙋши хрⷭтїа́нстѣй...`).
[^2956]: Fourth Elevation (facing North): the choir chants the fourth centenary as the Cross is bowed and raised toward the north in the same manner as the previous stations.
[^2957]: Fifth Elevation (facing East): the priest returns to the eastward orientation for the final petition (for the monastery and serving clergy) and fifth centenary. At every station, the rubric requires the priest to bless the faithful thrice cruciformly with the basil-adorned Cross (`крⷭто́мъ къ наро́домъ , г҃ , крⷭта ѡ҆бра́зно`).
[^2958]: Kontakion of the Exaltation of the Cross: *Tone 4: "Thou Who wast lifted up upon the Cross of Thine own will..."* (`Възнесы́йсѧ на крⷭтъ во́лею` / Greek: *Ὁ ὑψωθεὶς ἐν τῷ Σταυρῷ ἑκουσίως*). The text in the Balaban Trebnyk reflects the received Byzantine/Ruthenian recension.
[^2959]: Ceremony of Veneration: the priest and superior (*престатель*, hegumen) make two bows, kiss the Precious Cross upon the analogion, make one metania, and bow to both monastic choirs. The brethren then venerate two by two while chanting the liturgical troparion *Кресту Твоему покланяемся Владыко* (Greek: *Τὸν Σταυρόν σου προσκυνοῦμεν, Δέσποτα*).
[^2960]: Folio cross-reference: the rubric notes *Ихже смотри на задѣ на листѣ то҃з* ("which look behind on folio 377"), referring back to physical leaf p783 in the codex for the full hymnographic text of the aposticha stichera *Tone 2: "Come, ye faithful, let us venerate the life-giving wood..."* (`Прїидѣ́те вѣ́рнїи , животворѧ́щему дре́вꙋ поклони́мсѧ`).
[^2961]: Woodcut emblem: fol. 488r opens with a Renaissance strapwork headpiece featuring a pelican in her piety in an oval cartouche, piercing her breast to feed her chicks with her own blood. In Christian iconology, this is a prominent Eucharistic and Christological symbol, with the motto *СПЛА ЖИВОТ* ("The Savior is Life").
[^2962]: Division title: *On Stauropegia* (`Ѡ҆ СТАѪРОПИГІ́АХЪ` / Greek: *Περὶ σταυροπηγίων*). This canonical chapter codifies the juridical and liturgical procedure for establishing a patriarchal stauropegion—a monastery or church enjoying extraterritorial immunity from the local diocesan bishop and placed directly under the jurisdiction of the Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople. In the late 16th and early 17th centuries, the status of stauropegion was of vital ecclesiological importance in the Ruthenian Church (e.g. the Lviv Dormition Brotherhood).
[^2963]: Inscriptional protocol: the Patriarch sends an official decree authorizing his Exarch or a designated hierarch to lay the foundation and consecrate the church, accompanied by a wooden patriarchal cross inscribed on both faces by patriarchal scribes.
[^2964]: Reverse inscriptional formula: the inscription on the reverse face records the civil and imperial dating formula (`При црⷭтвѣ̀ бл҃гочти́вѣйшихъ цр҃ехъ на́шихъ и҆́мѧ ре́къ...`) alongside the day, month, and year.
[^2965]: Rubric: at the consecration of a stauropegial church, the patriarchal cross is erected directly behind the Holy Table (`въдрꙋжа́етъ и҆ крⷭтъ съза́ди ст҃ыѧ трапе́зы`) within an elevated, ornamental structure or holder made of stone, iron, or bronze, explicitly modeled after the liturgical arrangement in the Great Church of Constantinople (Hagia Sophia).
[^2966]: Conclusion of the rubric from leaf p1006 (`...въ велицѣй цр҃кви`).
[^2967]: Codicological note: physical folio 489v (leaf p1008) is an unprinted blank verso (*vacat*) concluding Gathering `ѡ̃ ѡ̃` (Gathering 52) of the 1606 imprint. In this exemplar, later 17th-century East Slavic cursive (*skoropis'*) hands inscribed ownership and donation records: *Сїа Книга глаголемая Служебникъ... монастыра...* ("This book, called the Sluzhebnik... of the monastery...").
[^2968]: Gathering mark and headpiece: leaf p1009 marks the start of Gathering 53 (`цц` / *Ts ts*, fol. 490r), embellished with an elaborate Renaissance headpiece featuring two vases brimming with flowers and scrolling acanthus foliage.
[^2969]: Major division heading: *Prayers for Every Need* (`МЛ҃ТВЫ НА ВСІА́КОУ ПОТРЕ́БОУ` / Greek: *Εὐχαὶ εἰς διαφόρους περιστάσεις*). This heading introduces the comprehensive compendium of occasional prayers and minor benedictions within the Euchologion.
[^2970]: Penitential benediction: *Prayer over one who has eaten defiled meats of the pagans* (`Мл҃тва над̾ ꙗ҆доу́щимъ сквръ́наѧ мяса̀ ѿ поганъ` / Greek: *Εὐχὴ ἐπὶ τῶν φαγόντων μιαρόν τι*). In historical Ruthenian and Byzantine liturgical life, this prayer was read over Christians who had inadvertently, under duress, or during Ottoman/Tatar captivity eaten meat sacrificed to idols or forbidden by canonical dietary norms (Acts 15:29).
[^2971]: Prayer petition: the prayer asks that the penitent, having unknowingly or under compulsion tasted unclean food, be absolved and deemed worthy to receive the Holy Mysteries of Christ's Body and Blood without condemnation.
[^2972]: Doxology formula: the closing exclamation in the Slavonic text reads `ꙗ҆́ко Твое́ є҆́стъ црⷭтво и҆ си́ла и҆ сла́ва въ безъконе́чныѧ вѣ́кы , а҆ми́нь` ("unto endless ages, Amen"), an ancient liturgical variant of the eternal doxology.
