*Sticheron 8:* When the soul is about to be violently snatched from the body by fearsome angels, it forgets all kinsfolk and acquaintances, and takes thought for appearing before the judgment seat that is to come. O the vanity and the much-toiling flesh! Then, entreating the Judge, let us all pray for the departed, that the Lord may forgive him the deeds he has done.[^1558]

*Sticheron 9:* Come unto the grave, O brethren, let us see the ashes and dust from which we were created! Whither go we now? What indeed have we become? Who is the poor man, or who the rich? Or who is the master, and who the free? Are not all dust? The beauty of the countenance has rotted, and death has withered all the flower of youth.[^1559]

*Sticheron 10:* Truly vanity and corruption are all the sweet and glorious things of life! For all of us vanish, all of us die: kings and princes, judges and the mighty, rich and poor, and all human nature. For now those who once were in life are laid in the tombs: whom let us pray unto the Lord to give rest.[^1560]

*Sticheron 11:* All the bodily instruments are now seen to be idle, which a little while before were active; all are inactive, dead, insensate. For the eyes have closed, the feet are bound, the hands have fallen still, and hearing with them; and the tongue is locked in silence, delivered unto the grave. Truly...[^1561]

...all human things are vanity!

*Theotokion:* Save those that hope in thee, O Mother of the unwaning Sun, O Birthgiver of God! By thy prayers ask of the all-good God, we pray, to give rest unto him who has now been translated, where the souls of the righteous repose. Show him forth as an heir of divine good things in the courts of the righteous, in everlasting memory, O all-immaculate one.[^1562]

*Doxastikon:* Beholding me lying before you speechless and breathless, weep for me, all ye brethren and friends, and kinsfolk and acquaintances! For yesterday I conversed with you, and suddenly the fearsome hour of death came upon me.

But come, all ye that love me, and kiss me with the last kiss; for no longer shall I walk with you or converse henceforth. For I depart unto the Judge, where there is no respect of persons: for slave and master stand together, king and soldier, rich and poor, in equal dignity; for each from his own deeds shall either be glorified or put to shame. But I ask and beseech all, unceasingly to pray for me unto Christ God, that I may not be appointed according to my sins unto the place of torment, but that He may appoint me where is the light of life.[^1564]

*Then the Trisagion. All-Holy Trinity. Our Father. For Thine is the kingdom. And the Troparia: "With the spirits of the righteous..." and the rest. The litany by the deacon. The general prayer. And after the exclamation: Glory... Both now... The Dismissal:*[^1567]

May He Who rose from the dead, Christ our true God, through the prayers of His all-pure Mother, of the holy, glorious, and all-praised Apostles, of our venerable and God-bearing Fathers, and of all the Saints: [appoint] the soul of His servant who has been translated from us...[^1568]
