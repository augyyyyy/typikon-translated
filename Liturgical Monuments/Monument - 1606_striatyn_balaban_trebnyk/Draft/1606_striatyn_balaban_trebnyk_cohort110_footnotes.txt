[^3146]: Slav. *въ ти́хость претворѝ*—conclusion of the *Prayer for the Rebuking of Thunders and Lightnings* (begun on leaf p1089).
[^3147]: Title rubric: *Prayer against the Evil Turbulence of Winds, and a Tempest of the Sea* (Слав. *Мл҃, на злорастворе́нїе вѣ́трѡвъ, и҆ бꙋ́рю мо́рскыѧ*; Greek *Εὐχὴ ἐπὶ δυσαερίας ἀνέμων καὶ ζάλης θαλάσσης*).
[^3148]: Scriptural allusions to the hypostatic Word and Spirit creating all things ex nihilo (Genesis 1:1-2, John 1:1-3), setting the sand as the bound of the sea (Jeremiah 5:22; Job 38:8-11), and meting out heaven with the span and the mountains in a balance (Isaiah 40:12).
[^3149]: Slav. *проти́воу нестрада́телномꙋ же и҆ нераздрꙋ́шномꙋ оу҆правлѧ́ѧ*—Greek *πρὸς τὸ ἀπαθὲς καὶ ἀδιάλυτον ἰθύνων* ("directing [all things] toward the impassible and indissoluble").
[^3150]: Slav. *преесте́венымъ и҆ ѡ҆бы́чнымъ твои́мъ чловеколю́бїемъ*—Greek *τῇ ὑπερφυεῖ καὶ συνήθει σου φιλανθρωπίᾳ* ("by Thy supernatural and accustomed love for mankind").
[^3151]: Allusions to the ransom paid through Christ (1 Peter 1:18-19, 1 Timothy 2:6) and the ineffable communion of the Holy Spirit (2 Corinthians 13:13).
[^3152]: Liturgical and cosmic theology: nature's catastrophic disturbance is viewed as the reverberation of human disorder (*безъчи́нїе*), lawless deformity (*безъ ѻ҆́бразїе*), and sinful life (*зла́го житїа́ на́шего*); divine mercy restores creation to its natural harmony and cosmic order (*къ є҆стве́номоу съли́чествꙋ и҆ чи́нꙋ*).
[^3153]: Scriptural citation of Psalm 106:23-24 (LXX 106:23-24): "They that go down to the sea in ships, that do business in great waters; these see the works of the Lord, and His wonders in the deep."
[^3154]: Scriptural account of Christ sleeping in the boat and rebuking the winds and the sea (Matthew 8:23-27, Mark 4:35-41, Luke 8:22-25).
[^3155]: Marginal gloss: The main text reads *пла́вати* ("to sail"), while the outer margin provides the Early Modern Ruthenian (*prosta mova*) synonym *пло́вїти*.
[^3156]: Title rubric: *Prayer over the Offered Fruit of Every Kind of Produce* (Слав. *Мл҃тва, надъ приноси́мымъ плодомъ въсѧ́кого ѡ҆во́щїа*; Greek *Εὐχὴ ἐπὶ προσφερόντων ἀπαρχὰς ὀπωρῶν*). In Ruthenian and Church Slavonic usage, *ѡ҆во́щїе* encompasses tree fruits, garden vegetables, and cultivated edible produce.
[^3157]: Title rubric: *A Second Prayer for the Same* (Слав. *Мл҃тва втора́ѧ томоу́ждє*).
[^3158]: Liturgical offering formula: *твоѧ̑ ѿ твои́хъ приноси́ти тебѣ̀* ("offering unto Thee Thine own of Thine own"), echoing 1 Chronicles 29:14 and the Eucharistic Anaphora.
[^3159]: Scriptural allusion to the poor widow's offering of two mites (Mark 12:41-44, Luke 21:1-4).
[^3160]: Rubrical formula printed in cinnabar: *и҆́мѧ ре́къ* ("naming the name" / [Name]).
[^3161]: Major headpiece and Title Rubric: *Order Performed in Fields, or in a Vineyard, or in a Garden, if it Chance that they be Harmed by Creeping Things, or by Other Species [of Pests]* (Слав. *ЧИ́НЪ быва́емый на ни́вахъ, и҆ли въ виногра́дѣ, и҆ли въ врътогра́дѣ, а҆́ще слꙋ́читсѧ врежда́тисѧ и҆́мъ ѿ га́дъ, и҆ли ѿ и҆ныхъ ви́дъ*; Greek *Ἀκολουθία γενομένη εἰς χωράφιον, ἢ ἀμπελῶνα, ἢ κῆπον, εἰ τύχοι βλάπτεσθαι ὑπὸ ἑρπετῶν, ἢ ἄλλων εἰδῶν*).
[^3162]: Patrons invoked against agricultural pests: St. Tryphon of Campsada in Phrygia (commemorated Feb. 1), St. Eustathius Placidas (Sept. 20), and St. Julian of Tarsus / Cilicia (June 21).
[^3163]: Liturgical practice: The priest takes sanctified oil from the saint's vigil lamp (*є҆ле́й ѿ канди́ла*) and Great Sanctification Theophany water (*во́доу сты́хъ Бг҃оѧвле́нїи*), sprinkling the threatened plot crosswise while reciting the prayers.
[^3164]: Hexaemeron creation narrative: Genesis 1:1, 1:11-12 (vegetation, herb yielding seed, and fruit tree yielding fruit), and 1:14-18 (sun, moon, and stars).
[^3165]: Marginal gloss: The main text reads *ѻ҆пла́зньства лоука́ваго* ("the snare / assault of the deceitful one"), while the left margin supplies the vernacular Ruthenian gloss *проны́ри* ("guile, cunning deceit, machinations").
[^3166]: Title rubric: *Exorcistic and Commendatory Prayer of the Holy Martyr Tryphon, spoken in a garden, or in a vineyard, or in a field* (Слав. *Мл҃тва повѣ́тна и҆ заклина́телнаѧ ст҃го мч҃нка трѵ́фона, гл҃емаѧ въ врътогра́дѣ, и҆ли виногра́дѣ, и҆ли на ни́вѣ*; Greek *Εὐχὴ ἐξορκισμοῦ τοῦ ἁγίου ἐνδόξου μάρτυρος Τρύφωνος*).
