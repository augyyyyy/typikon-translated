[^1354]: Fol. 226r (`ск҃ѕ`). Ode 4 of the Second Canon to the Most Holy Theotokos (by Joseph the Hymnographer). The rubric *і҆рмо́съ · та́же ·* directs the continuation of the troparia following the heirmos. The soul implores nature (clouds, mountains, hills) to weep over its catastrophic estrangement from God.
[^1355]: Fol. 226r (`ск҃ѕ`). The dying soul addresses the Mother of God as the infallible and unashamed refuge in the terrifying hour of trial (*въ ча́съ и҆спыта́нїа*).
[^1356]: Fol. 226r (`ск҃ѕ`). The image of the outstretched hands of the Theotokos as the wings of a dove (*ꙗ҆́ко сщ҃еннѣ́ голꙋби́нѣ кри́лѣ*) draws upon Psalm 54:7 (LXX) and patristic maternal shelter motifs.
[^1357]: Fol. 226r (`ск҃ѕ`). Slavonic *Въздоу́шнаго кнѧ́ѕѧ наси́лника мꙋчителѧ, стра́шныхъ пꙋтестоѧ́телѧ* corresponds to Greek *τὸν ἀέριον τύραννον*, depicting the aerial prince and the terrifying inquisitors of words (*словои҆спыта́телѧ*) who examine the departing soul at the toll-houses.
[^1358]: Fol. 226v. Theotokion concluding Ode 4 of the Second Canon. The dying Christian confesses the realization of long-dreaded deathbed terror (*се́ мѧ влч҃це стра́хъ оу҆срѣ́те, є҆го́же и҆ боѧ́хсѧ*).
[^1359]: Fol. 226v. Tone 6, Ode 5 of the First Canon (by St. Andrew of Crete). Heirmos incipit: *Θείῳ φέγγει σου, Ἀγαθέ* (Slavonic: *Бж҃їимъ свѣ́томъ ти, Бл҃же*). The first troparion poignantly addresses the physical/sensible light of the sun and creation (*Свѣ́те чꙋ́вственый*), bidding it farewell as demonic darkness closes in.
[^1360]: Fol. 227r (`ск҃з`). Concluding sentence of Troparion 2 of Ode 5 (Canon 1). The soul reproaches the saints and friends of the Savior for their silence, pleading that a single united cry to the Mother of God would have obtained deliverance from darkness.
[^1361]: Fol. 227r (`ск҃з`). A dramatic personal deathbed farewell to the Archangel Michael (*Сп҃сисѧ вели́кїй кнѧ́же Бж҃їй Миха́иле, а҆́гг҃лѡмъ нача́лниче*), marking the physical cessation of bodily faculties: the lips, tongue, and eyes of the dying man can no longer move or call for help.
[^1362]: Fol. 227r (`ск҃з`). Theotokion of Ode 5 (Canon 1). The dying person bids a final earthly farewell to the sacred icon of the Mother of God (*нектомоу́ бо твое́го пренепоро́чнаа чⷭ҇та́го ѻ҆́браза оу҆зрю̀ ст҃ътный*), as sight fails and the night of death covers him.
[^1363]: Fol. 227r (`ск҃з`). Ode 5 of the Second Canon (Joseph the Hymnographer), preceded by the cinnabar transition rubric *і҆рмо́съ · та́же ·* The opening troparion paraphrases Psalm 26:9 and Psalm 68:18–19 (LXX).
[^1364]: Fol. 227v. The departing soul addresses its kinsmen according to the flesh (*по пло́ти срѡ́дници*) and spiritual brethren (*по дх҃оу бра́тїе*), urging them to mourn and lament over its final separation from earthly life.
[^1365]: Fol. 227v. The soul laments the total vanity and helplessness of mortal aid in the face of demonic captivity (*да не ꙗ҆́ко чл҃къ безпо́мощенъ, въ рꙋка́хъ вра́гъ мои́хъ затворе́нъ бꙋ́дꙋ*).
[^1366]: Fol. 227v. The soul articulates the threefold horror of eternal perdition: separation from the Mother of God, falling away from Christ the Savior, and eternal estrangement from all the saints.
[^1367]: Fol. 228r (`ск҃и`). Concluding sentence of the Theotokion of Ode 5 (Canon 2), begging that the boundless compassion of the Theotokos may triumph over the immense weight of personal sin.
[^1368]: Fol. 228r (`ск҃и`). Tone 6, Ode 6 of the First Canon (St. Andrew of Crete). Heirmos incipit: *Τοῦ βίου τὴν θάλασσαν* (Slavonic: *Жите́йскаго мо́рѧ*). The soul makes a dying bequest to the brethren to listen to its final contrite words.
[^1369]: Fol. 228r (`ск҃и`). In an extreme expression of ascetic deathbed compunction and self-abasement, the dying soul commands that its sinful body not even be buried in the earth, but cast out to the dogs (*ѱѡ́мъ повръзѣ́те*).
[^1370]: Fol. 228v. Concluding sentence of the troparion on the unburied corpse. The soul declares that funeral singing over the flesh is useless while the soul is being dragged into torment, begging rather that the dogs devour the heart that sinned.
[^1371]: Fol. 228v. Theotokion of Ode 6 (Canon 1). The soul implores random strangers and travelers who might pass by its discarded bones to utter a prayer to the Mother of God for the salvation of its soul.
[^1372]: Fol. 228v. Ode 6 of the Second Canon (Joseph the Hymnographer), introduced by *і҆рмо́съ , та́же ·* The silent dying person explains that though the tongue is immobilized, the heart cries out in the hidden fire of contrition (*вноу́трь възгара́етсѧ*).
[^1373]: Fol. 228v. The soul implores the Theotokos for a personal deathbed visitation (*на моѐ посѣще́нїе сънитѝ*), so that departing from the body in Her sight, it may go forth rejoicing.
[^1374]: Fol. 229r (`ск҃ѳ`). A vivid clinical and theological depiction of the agony of somatic dissolution: the severance of the natural bond (*растръза́еми соу́ꙋзъ*) and the disruption of the bodily constitution create unbearable anguish.
[^1375]: Fol. 229r (`ск҃ѳ`). The dying soul prays to be placed into the holy hands of angels, that their wings may shield it from seeing the dark, foul, and hideous sight of the demons (*ка́льный бѣсѡ́въ ѻ҆́бразъ*).
[^1376]: Fol. 229r (`ск҃ѳ`). Theotokion of Ode 6 (Canon 2). Evoking the Parable of the Wise and Foolish Virgins (Matt 25:1–13), the soul begs the Mother of God to rekindle its extinguished lamp with the holy oil of Her mercy and admit it to the heavenly bridal chamber.
[^1377]: Fol. 229r–229v. Chanting of the full Heirmos of Ode 6 (Canon 1): *Τοῦ βίου τὴν θάλασσαν...* (*Жите́йскаго мо́рѧ въздвиѕа́ема зрѧ̀...*), concluding the first six odes of the combined canons.
[^1378]: Fol. 229v. Kontakion in Tone 6, the renowned poetic masterpiece of St. Andrew of Crete from the Great Canon: *Ψυχή μου, ψυχή μου, ἀνάστα, τί καθεύδεις; τὸ τέλος ἐγγίζει...* (*Дꙋшѐ моѧ̀ дꙋшѐ моѧ̀ , въста́ни что̀ спи́ши...*), sung here at the separation of soul and body.
[^1379]: Fol. 230r (`сл҃`). Ikos of the Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete (*Ꙗ҆́ко вра́чество ви́дѧ ѿвръ́сто...*), portraying Satan's despair at the healing and salvation wrought by Christ, the "Son of Mary" and "Bethlehemite."
[^1380]: Fol. 230r (`сл҃`). Tone 6, Ode 7 of the First Canon (St. Andrew of Crete). Heirmos incipit: *Δροσοβόλον μὲν τὴν κάμινον* (Slavonic: *Хладода́вицоу оу҆́бо пе́щь*). The first troparion depicts the violent extraction of the soul from the flesh and its summons before the dread tribunal.
[^1381]: Fol. 230r (`сл҃`). The soul cries to the radiant angelic powers for mercy, confessing its lack of good deeds to balance its sins at the toll-houses (*мыта́рьства*).
[^1382]: Fol. 230v. Continuation of Troparion 3 of Ode 7 (Canon 1). The soul instructs the holy angels to fall down before Christ the Creator with tears, interceding for the work of His hands (Job 10:3, 14:15).
[^1383]: Fol. 230v. Theotokion of Ode 7 (Canon 1). The angels are urged to beseech the all-pure Sovereign Lady to add Her maternal intercession before God, confident that as His Mother and Nourisher She will be heard.
[^1384]: Fol. 230v. Ode 7 of the Second Canon (Joseph the Hymnographer), introduced by *і҆рмо́съ · та́же ·* The soul laments the dark and moonless night of death (*но́щъ смръ́тнаѧ... мра́чнаѧже и҆ безълоу́нна*) overtaking it unprepared for the long and fearsome journey.
