[^1216]: Divine tender-heartedness: The penitent appeals to Christ's utmost compassion (*кра́йнее бл҃гооу҆тро́бїе*) and malice-free goodness (*незло́бїе*), crying out for purification (*сп҃си и҆ ѡ҆цѣ́сти мѧ̀*).
[^1217]: Liturgical catalogue of remissions: The fourfold repetition of *remit* (*ѡ҆ста́ви*), pleading for release from the multitude of transgressions, the heavy debts of sin, defilements, and abominations.
[^1218]: Anaphora of protective petitions (*да не...*): Pleading not to be crushed, put to death, suddenly snatched away amidst wicked deliberations, or consumed by the fire of divine judgment together with one's abominations.
[^1219]: The Immortal Banquet: Allusion to the eschatological Messianic banquet and Eucharistic feast (*трапе́за безсъме́ртнаѧ*, cf. Luke 14:15–24, Matt. 22:1–14), praying not to be cast out from the eternal wedding feast or alienated from the fellowship of the saints (continuing onto leaf p402).
[^1220]: Monastic tonsure as second baptism: The traditional Patristic and Byzantine monastic theology regarding the Great Angelic Schema (*великий ангельский образ*) as a renewal of baptismal grace (*второе крещение*), pleading for its restoration after having polluted the first garment of baptism.
[^1221]: God-streaming blood: The theological compound *бг҃ото́чный* (Greek θεόρρυτος), describing the Eucharistic blood flowing from the side of Christ on the Cross (John 19:34).
[^1222]: Ascetic remembrance of death (*memento mori*): The vivid evocation of the terrifying hour of the soul's departure from the body (*въ ча́съ же коне́чный, и҆ го́ркїи съме́ртный*), a hallmark of Byzantine hesychastic prayer.
[^1223]: Demonic toll-houses and the soul's departure: Pleading for a gentle separation of soul and body, protection against demonic spirits encountered in the aerial realm, and the tearing up of the handwriting of sinful debts (cf. Col. 2:14, continuing onto leaf p403).
[^1224]: Tearing of the chirograph: Echoing Colossians 2:14 (*blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us*), praying that the demonic records of sinful debts be utterly obliterated and reduced to ashes in the abyss of hades.
[^1225]: Angelic escort of the soul: The Byzantine liturgical doctrine of radiant guardian angels escorting the departed soul safely through the demonic ranks (*ѿража́ющихъ плъ́кы бѣсѡ́вскыѧ*) into the heavenly realm.
[^1226]: The fiery-formed Throne of Glory: Biblical imagery of the Divine Merkabah and fiery throne of the Ancient of Days (Daniel 7:9, Ezekiel 1:26–28).
[^1227]: Divine Grace personified as Advocate: The theological audacity of presenting God's own grace (*бл҃года́ть*), mercy, and compassion as the primary advocate and intercessor (*хода́тай*, Greek μεσίτης) before His divine justice (continuing onto leaf p404).
[^1228]: Marian typological titles: The Mother of God invoked under canonical Old Testament types: the Adornment of the world, Jacob's Ladder (Gen. 28:12), Aaron's budding rod (Num. 17:8, Isa. 11:1), and the Living/Animate Throne of the Cherubim (*ѻ҆дꙋшевле́нныи прⷭ҇то́лъ*).
[^1229]: The nine angelic choirs: The complete ninefold celestial hierarchy of Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite invoked as lightning-rayed intercessors (*чи́ны млънїелоу́чны*): Thrones, Cherubim, Seraphim, Dominions, Powers, Authorities, Principalities, Archangels, and Angels.
[^1230]: St. John the Baptist and Forerunner: Commemorating his sanctification in his mother's womb prior to birth (Luke 1:15, 41) and his role as the dawn preceding the Sun of Righteousness unto those sitting in darkness (Luke 1:78–79).
[^1231]: Choirs of the righteous: The cosmic intercession of the heavenly Church, invoking the Old Testament patriarchs and prophets, followed by the New Testament apostles and crown-bearing martyrs (continuing onto leaf p405).
[^1232]: St. Mary of Egypt as model of repentance: Invoking St. Mary of Egypt (the quintessential exemplar of radical repentance in the Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete), celebrated as an earthly human who attained heavenly conversance (*нб҃оше́ствена же ѻ҆ба́че*).
[^1233]: St. Onuphrius the Great and desert asceticism: The 4th-century anchorite of the Egyptian Thebaid who lived sixty years naked in the wilderness, miraculously clothed only by hair and heavenly light, here portrayed as stripped of physical clothing but wondrously adorned with divine virtue.
[^1234]: Transgression from the womb: Echoing Psalm 50:7 (LXX: *"For behold, I was conceived in iniquities, and in sins did my mother bear me"*), confessing habitual sinfulness from the very womb (*ѿ ложе́снъ самѣ́хъ*).
[^1235]: Salvation beyond merit: Pleading for salvation not based on human works or merit (*чрѣ досто́инство* / Greek ὑπὲρ τὴν ἀξίαν), but solely through divine benevolence and hope (continuing onto leaf p406).
[^1236]: Doxological conclusion of Prayer 16: Pleading to praise God alongside the celestial hosts unto unnumbered ages (*въ вѣ́кы въсѧ̑ неи҆зчете́нныѧ*).
[^1237]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink introducing Prayer 17 of the Pre-Communion series: *Prayer before Holy Communion, of Saint Symeon the New Theologian, from the Hymns of Divine Love* (*МЛ҃ТВА КЪ СТ҃О́Мꙋ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ • СТ҃ГО СѶМЕѠ́НА НО́ВАГО БГ҃ОСЛО́ВА • Ѿ БЖ҃ТВНЫХЪ РА́ЧЇЙ*). The marginal numeral `з҃і` marks Prayer 17. The attribution *From the Hymns of Divine Love* cites the Greek collection *Ἐκ τῶν θείων ἐρώτων* (Hymn 17).
[^1238]: The classic opening of St. Symeon's prayer: The fourfold confession of unworthiness: *From defiled lips, from an abominable heart, from an unclean tongue, from a polluted soul* (*Ἀπὸ ῥυπαρῶν χειλέων, ἀπὸ βδελυρᾶς καρδίας, ἀπὸ ἀκαθάρτου γλώσσης, ἐκ ψυχῆς ἐρρυπωμένης*).
[^1239]: Boldness of the penitent: Asking Christ not to reject the sinner's words, gestures, or audacious daring (*ни дръ́зость*, Greek μηδὲ τὴν ἀναίδейαν, continuing onto leaf p407).
[^1240]: Prayer for bold speech (*parrhesia*): St. Symeon asks Christ to grant him freedom of speech (*дръзнове́ннѣ гл҃ати*) and divine instruction on how to pray properly (Rom. 8:26).
[^1241]: The repentant harlot of Luke 7:36–50: Direct allusion to the sinful woman who purchased alabaster ointment and anointed Christ's feet in Simon the Pharisee's house (*съгрѣши́хъ па́че блоу́дница*).
[^1242]: Christ addressed as the Divine Word (*Слово* / Logos): Invoking Christ under His Johannine title (John 1:1), pleading not to be cast away from His presence.
[^1243]: Tears as precious myrrh: The mystical assimilation of penitential tears to costly spikenard, daring to wash and kiss the feet of the Saviour.
[^1244]: Divine omniscience: Christ's intimate knowledge of the sinner's manifold offenses and inner brokenness (continuing onto leaf p408). Also note the typographical erratum in the printed heading, where folio numeral `рч҃е` (195) is erroneously repeated in place of `рч҃и` (198).
[^1245]: Spiritual diagnosis: Christ as the physician of souls Who sees both the ulcers of sin and the sincere disposition (*произволе́нїе*) and sighing of the heart.
[^1246]: Omnipresence and divine foreknowledge: Nothing escapes God's scrutiny, neither a single tear-drop, nor a fraction of a drop, nor the slightest movement of the heart (*движе́нїе срⷣчное*).
[^1247]: Scriptural quote from Psalm 138:16 (LXX): *"Thine eyes did see mine unformed substance, and in Thy book all shall be written"* (τὸ ἀκατέργαστόν μου εἶδον οἱ ὀφθαλμοί σου, καὶ ἐπὶ τὸ βιβλίον σου πάντες γραφήσονται / *несꙋдѣ́ланное моѐ ви́дѣша ѡ҆́чи твоѝ, въ кни́гах же твои́хъ написа́на тебѣ̀ соу́тъ*).
[^1248]: Scriptural quote from Psalm 24:18 (LXX): *"Look upon my lowliness and my toil, and forgive all my sins"* (ἴде τὴν ταπείνωσίν μου καὶ τὸν κόπον μου, καὶ ἄφες πάσας τὰς ἁμαρτίας μου / *ви́ждь смире́нїе моѐ, ви́ждь троу́д мои́ є҆ли́къ, и҆ грѣхы̀ ѡ҆ста́ви ми*).
[^1249]: Requisite interior disposition for Communion: Purity of heart, fearful contemplation (*стра́шнаѧ мы́сль*), and contrition of soul necessary to receive the undefiled Mysteries without incurring judgment (continuing onto leaf p409).
[^1250]: Eucharistic deification (*Theosis*): The core theological conviction of St. Symeon the New Theologian that the communicant is literally vivified and deified (*ѡ҆живля́етсѧ, и҆ ѡ҆божа́етсѧ* / Greek ζωοῦται καὶ θεοῦται) through the worthy reception of Christ's Body and Blood.
[^1251]: Dominical quotation from John 6:56: *"He that eateth My flesh, and drinketh My blood, dwelleth in Me, and I in him"* (*ὁ τρώγων μου τὴν σάρκα καὶ πίνων μου τὸ αἷμα ἐν ἐμοὶ μένει, κἀγὼ ἐν αὐτῷ*).
[^1252]: The Three-Sunned Light: Addressing Christ as the radiance of the Triune Godhead (*свѣ́тъ трисли́чный* / Greek φῶς τρισηλίου), expressing communion with the entire Holy Trinity through Christ.
[^1253]: Christological titles of intimacy: Christ addressed as *Giver of Life* (*живода́вецъ*), *my breath* (*дыха́нїе моѐ*), *my life* (*живо́тъ мои́*), and *my rejoicing* (*възрадова́нїе моѐ*).
[^1254]: Penitential approach: Drawing near to the chalice with tears and inner contrition, seeking remission of offenses (continuing onto leaf p410).
[^1255]: Protection from the Deceiver: The danger of spiritual vulnerability when alienated from divine grace, whereby the devil (*преле́стникъ*, the Deceiver) snatches the soul away and separates it from Christ's words.
[^1256]: Gospel types of repentance: Juxtaposing the Prodigal Son (Luke 15:11–32) and the penitent harlot (Luke 7:36–50), entreating Christ to extend the same unconditional acceptance to the communicant.
[^1257]: Consciousness of chief among sinners: Echoing 1 Timothy 1:15 (*"sinners, of whom I am chief"*), acknowledging personal guilt as surpassing all others (continuing onto leaf p411).
