[^1339]: Fol. 221r (`ск҃і`). A post-communion Thanksgiving prayer to the Mother of God (*Моли́тва къ прест҃ѣ́й Бц҃и, по причаще́нїи: Преблаже́нная Госпоже́...*), concluding the expedited order of holy communion for the sick.
[^1340]: Fol. 222r (`ск҃в`). Woodcut headpiece across the top of the folio. The title in ornamental display cinnabar (*ЧИ́НЪ БЫВА́ЕМЫЙ НА РАЗЛꙋЧЕ́НЇЕ, ДꙋШЍ ѿ ТѢ́ЛА*) initiates the classical Byzantine-Ruthenian office for the agony of death (*Ἀκολουθία εἰς ψυχορраγοῦντα*), chanted at the bedside of the dying.
[^1341]: Fol. 222r (`ск҃в`). A historical paper slip repair was pasted over lines 4–6 of the folio, reinforcing paper thinning caused by abraded ink on the verso (fol. 222v). The obscured characters are restored according to Ruthenian Trebnyk and classical Euchologion usage: `Е҆гда̀ кто̀ ѿ [хр҃тїа́нъ...] [...] и҆ прѣ́кѡ начне́тъ и҆знемаг[а́ти ... и҆ сщ҃енникъ] дх҃о́вникъ [въпра́]ша́етъ є҆го̀...` The rubric instructs the father-confessor to inquire gently into any lingering forgotten sin, unconfessed fault, or unforgiven grudge before beginning the office.
[^1342]: Fol. 222v. Psalm 50 (LXX 50; Masoretic Ps 51). The introductory liturgical framework consists of the standard Trisagion prayers, Lord have mercy (12 times), the triple invitation to worship, and the 50th Psalm.
[^1343]: Fol. 222v. Cinnabar rubric attributing the canon to "Lord Andrew of Crete" (*творе́нїе кѵ́ръ а҆ндре́а крїтскаго*, c. 660–740).
[^1344]: Fol. 222v. Tone 6, Heirmos of Ode 1: *Ὡς ἐν ἠπείρῳ πεζεύσας ὁ Ἰσραήλ* (Slavonic: *Ꙗ́ко по сꙋхоу ходи́въ їи́ль*), referring to the crossing of the Red Sea (Exodus 14).
[^1345]: Fol. 223r (`ск҃г`). The reading `въ[сѣ́]мъ грѣ́шникѡмъ` ("to all sinners") is abraded on the page, with only the initial `въ` and final `мъ` remaining legible; restored from parallel Ruthenian and Byzantine witnesses.
[^1346]: Fol. 223r (`ск҃г`). Rubric introducing the Second Canon to the Theotokos: *Дроугіи ка́нѡнъ молб҃енъ пр҃тѣ́й Бц҃и, ѿ лица̀ чл҃кꙋ дш҃ю раздѣлѧ́ющꙋ, и҆ немогꙋ́щꙋ гла́ти...* The acrostic in the Slavonic witness is given as *И҆зба́ви мѧ̀ чт҃аа бѣсѡ́въ моучи́телства* (Greek: *Ῥῦσαί με, σεμνὴ, δαιμόνων τυραννίδος*), followed by the author's name *І҆ѡ́сифъ* (Joseph the Hymnographer, c. 816–886). In the 1606 Striatyn imprint, the two canons are chanted in conjoined succession ode by ode.
[^1347]: Fol. 223v. Tone 6, Ode 1 Heirmos for the Second Canon (*Ꙗ҆́ко по сꙋхꙋ ходи̂ и҆и́ль*).
[^1348]: Fol. 224r (`ск҃д`). Tone 6, Heirmos of Ode 3 (Canon 1): *Οὐκ ἔστιν ἅγιος ὡς σύ, Κύριε ὁ Θεός μου* (Slavonic: *Нѣ́сть ст҃ъ ꙗ҆́коже ты Гд҃и Бг҃е*), based on the Canticle of Hannah (1 Kings / 1 Sam 2:2).
[^1349]: Fol. 224r (`ск҃д`). Slavonic *на стави́лѣ мѣри́лнѣмъ* translates Greek *ἐν ζυγῷ σταθμῶν* ("in the balance of the scales"), evoking the aerial examination and weighing of deeds at the separation of soul and body.
[^1350]: Fol. 224v. Cinnabar rubric *И҆́нъ і҆рмо́съ · та́же* indicates the transition to the troparia of Ode 3 of the Second Canon. The image of the "roaring noetic lion cubs" (*мы́сленїи рыка́юще скѵ́мни*) reflects Psalm 16:12 (LXX) and patristic demonology regarding the predatory spirits awaiting the dying soul.
[^1351]: Fol. 225r (`ск҃е`). Slavonic *Оу҆га́сшоу оу҆́бо ѿню́дъ ѻ҆рга́ноу слове́сномꙋ* (Greek: *Ἀποσβεσθέντος ἄρδην τοῦ ὀργάνου τοῦ λογικοῦ*), describing the physical deathbed cessation of speech while the dying person's inner heart continues to cry out to the Mother of God.
[^1352]: Fol. 225r (`ск҃е`). Tone 6, Heirmos of Ode 4 (Canon 1): *Χριστός μου δύнаμις, Θεὸς καὶ Κύριος* (Slavonic: *Хс҃ мнѣ̀ си́ла Бг҃ъ и҆ Гд҃ь*), based on the Canticle of Habakkuk (Habakkuk 3).
[^1353]: Fol. 225v. The troparion expresses the dramatic terror of the deathbed struggle (*по́двигъ всепа́гꙋбный*) where the soul implores both the celestial angelic orders and earthly friends for mercy and tears as it is unmercifully separated from the flesh.
