[^370]: Fol. 97r. Opening of the Second Epistle reading: Romans 15:1–3. The Apostle Paul establishes the duty of the strong to bear the infirmities of the weak, citing Psalm 68:10 (LXX 68:9): *поноше́нїа поносѧ́щихъ ти нападо́ша на мѧ̀* (Greek: *οἱ ὀνειδισμοὶ τῶν ὀνειδιζόντων σε ἐπέπεσον ἐπ' ἐμέ*), applied christologically to the suffering Savior.
[^371]: Fol. 97r. Continuation of the Second Epistle reading: Romans 15:4–7a. The Apostle emphasizes patience and comfort drawn from the Scriptures (*трѣпе́нїемъ и҆ оу҆тѣше́нїемъ писа́нїй* / *διὰ τῆς ὑπομονῆς καὶ διὰ τῆς παρακλήσεως τῶν γραφῶν*) that believers may with one mind and one mouth glorify the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ.
[^372]: Fol. 97v. Conclusion of the Second Epistle reading: Romans 15:7b (*прїемли́те дрꙋ́гъ дрꙋ́га, ꙗ҆́коже и҆ хс҃ъ прїѧ́тъ ва́съ въ сла́вꙋ бж҃їю* / Greek: *προσλαμβάνεσθε ἀλλήλους, καθὼς καὶ ὁ Χριστὸς προσελάβετο ὑμᾶς, εἰς δόξαν τοῦ Θεοῦ*).
[^373]: Fol. 97v. Alleluia in Tone 5 with verse from Psalm 88:2 (LXX 88:1): *Мл҃ти твоѧ̀ гн҃и въ вѣ́кы въспою̀* (Greek: *Τὰ ἐλέη σου, Κύριε, εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα ᾄσομαι* — "Thy mercies, O Lord, will I sing unto ages").
[^374]: Fol. 97v. Opening of the Second Gospel reading: Luke 19:1–5. The encounter of Jesus with Zacchaeus (*закхе́й* / *Ζακχαῖος*) in Jericho, who climbed a sycamore tree (*ꙗ҆годи́чина* / *συκομορέα*) to see the Lord.
[^375]: Fol. 98r. Conclusion of the Second Gospel reading: Luke 19:5b–10. Zacchaeus's repentance and fourfold restitution (*възвращꙋ̀ четвери́цею* / *ἀποδίδωμι τετραπλοῦν*), culminating in Christ's declaration of salvation to his household and the saving mission of the Son of Man.
[^376]: Fol. 98r. The Augmented Litany (*є҆ктенїа̀* / *ἐκτενής*) of Holy Unction repeated after the Second Gospel reading, petitioning divine mercy, life, health, salvation, and visitation for the sick person.
[^377]: Fol. 98v. Petitions of the Augmented Litany for the sick candidate (*и҆́мѧ ре́къ* / *nomen dicens*), entreating the remission of all voluntary and involuntary transgressions and deliverance from the bed of pain (*ѿ ѻ҆́дра болѣ́зньнаго* / Greek: *ἐκ κλίνης ὀδύνης*).
[^378]: Fol. 98v. Concluding ecphonesis of the Augmented Litany: *Ꙗ҆́ко мл҃тивый чл҃колю́бецъ бг҃ъ є҆сѝ...* (Greek: *Ὅτι ἐλεήμων καὶ φιλάνθρωπος Θεὸς ὑπάρχεις...*), sealed by the canonical Trinitarian doxology.
[^379]: Fol. 98v. The Second Consecratory Prayer of the Priest: *Бж҃е вели́кїй и҆ вы́шнїй...* (Greek: *Ὁ Θεὸς ὁ μέγας καὶ ὕψιστος, ὁ ὑπὸ πάσης κτίσεως προσκυνούμενος, σοφίας πηγή, ἀγαθότητος ὄντως ἀνεξιχνίαστον πέλαγος, καὶ οἰκτιρμῶν ἀπεριόριστον ἄβυσσος...*).
[^380]: Fol. 99r. Continuation of the Second Consecratory Prayer, invoking God's transcendence before the ages (*пре́жде вѣ́къ и҆ чюдесе́мъ бг҃ъ*), citing Genesis 8:21 on human weakness from youth and Ezekiel 33:11 on God desiring repentance rather than the death of a sinner.
[^381]: Fol. 99v. Anamnesis of the Incarnation and dominical parables of mercy: Matthew 9:13 (calling sinners to repentance), Luke 15:4–10 (the Lost Sheep and the Lost Drachma), John 6:37 (not casting out him that cometh), and Luke 7:36–50 (the penitent woman weeping at the Savior's feet).
[^382]: Fol. 99v. Quotation of the dominical agraphon on persistent repentance (*є҆ли́жды а҆́ще па́днеши, въста́ни и҆ сп҃се́шисѧ* — "As often as thou fallest, arise, and thou shalt be saved"; cf. Justin Martyr, *Dialogue with Trypho* 47) and Luke 15:7, 10 on heavenly joy over one repenting sinner.
[^383]: Fol. 100r. Epiklesis of the Second Consecratory Prayer: the priest entreats the descent of the grace of the All-holy Spirit (*блг҃отїю пр҃ста́го твое́го дх҃а*) upon the sick person (*и҆́мѧ ре́къ*), with complete absolution of sins in word, deed, or thought, and protection in the divine commandments.
[^384]: Fol. 100v. Eschatological conclusion of the Second Consecratory Prayer, frustrating the joy of the adversary (*врагоу̀ є҆го̀*), sealed by the solemn Trinitarian ecphonesis: *Твое́ бо є҆́стъ є҆́же ми́ловати и҆ сп҃ти ны́ Хе́ бг҃е...*.
[^385]: Fol. 100v. Third Prokeimenon in Tone 3: Psalm 26:1 (LXX 26:1): *Гдⷭь просвѣще́нїе моѐ и҆ сп҃ситель мо́й, кого́ сѧ оу҆бою̀...* (Greek: *Κύριος φωτισμός μου καὶ σωτήρ μου· τίνα φοβηθήσομαι;*).
[^386]: Fol. 100v. Announcement and opening of the Third Epistle reading: 1 Corinthians 12:27–28a (*вы̀ є҆сте тѣ́ло хв҃о и҆ оу҆́ди ѿ ча́сти...* / Greek: *Ὑμεῖς δέ ἐστε σῶμα Χριστοῦ καὶ μέλη ἐκ μέρους...*), outlining the diversity of charisms and offices in the Church.
[^387]: Fol. 101r. Continuation of the Third Epistle reading: 1 Corinthians 12:28b–13:2. The catalogue of spiritual gifts (*дарова́нїа и҆сцѣле́нїѡмъ, застꙋпле́нїа, и҆справле́нїе, ро́ди ꙗ҆зы́кѡмъ*) and the introduction to the Pauline Hymn to Love (*ἀγάπη* / *любы*).
[^388]: Fol. 101v. Conclusion of the Third Epistle reading: 1 Corinthians 13:2b–8a. The Apostle outlines the eternal qualities of Christian love. The 1606 Striatyn print renders *πάντα στέγει* as *всѧ̀ лю́битъ* ("loveth all things") alongside *всемꙋ̀ вѣ́рꙋ є҆́млетъ, всѐ оу҆пова́етъ, всѧ̀ терпи́тъ*.
[^389]: Fol. 101v. Alleluia in Tone 2 with verse from Psalm 30:2 (LXX 30:1): *На тѧ̀ гн҃и оу҆пова́хъ, да непостыждꙋ́сѧ въ вѣ́кы* (Greek: *Ἐπὶ σοί, Κύριε, ἤλπισα, μὴ καταισχυνθείην εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα* — "In Thee, O Lord, have I hoped, let me not be put to shame unto ages").
[^390]: Fol. 101v. Liturgical announcement of the Third Gospel reading: the Gospel according to Saint Matthew (Matthew 10:1, 5–8), describing Christ sending forth the Twelve Apostles with power to heal the sick and cleanse lepers.
