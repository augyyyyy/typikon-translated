[^98]: Fol. 22r. The transition from the Renunciation of Satan (*Apotaxis*) to the Adherence to Christ (*Syntaxis*). Note the distinctive Ruthenian euchological terminology: while modern Synodal Church Slavonic editions employ the verb *сочетава́тися* (*сочетава́еши ли сѧ Хрїстꙋ̀*, rendering Greek συντάσσομαι), the 1606 Striatyn imprint uses *ѡ҆бѣщава́тися* (*Ѡ҆бѣща́еши ли сѧ Хрⷭ҇тꙋ̀* / *Ѡ҆бѣща́юсѧ*), emphasizing the covenantal pledge/vow made to Christ. Also noteworthy is the pastoral provision continuing from Fol. 21v permitting the godparent (*коу́мъ*) or the deacon to respond on behalf of foreigners (*и҆ноплеме́нни*) unable to speak Church Slavonic. The candidate is directed to turn eastward with hands lowered (symbolizing submissive devotion, in contrast to hands raised westward during the renunciation of Satan), followed by the profession of the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed thrice.

[^99]: Fol. 22v. The post-credal confirmation: the priest interrogates the candidate in the perfect tense (*Ѡ҆бѣща́лъ ли сѧ є҆сѝ Хрⷭ҇тꙋ̀* / *Ѡ҆бѣща́хсѧ*), followed by the act of adoration (*proskynesis*) before the Holy Trinity. The priest recites the dominical doxology quoting 1 Timothy 2:4 (*"Who wills all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth"*). Then follows the final pre-baptismal prayer of the catechumenate (*Влⷣко Гдⷭ҇и Бг҃е на́шъ, призовѝ раба̀ твое́го...*), petitioning God to summon the candidate to the "holy illumination" (*ст҃ое просвѣще́нїе*, the ancient patristic term for baptism, Greek: φωτισμός).

[^100]: Fol. 23r. Conclusion of the prayer of the catechumenate. The euchological text draws upon Johannine and Pauline theology: the washing away of the "oldness" (*ѻ҆ветша́нїе*, Romans 6:6; Ephesians 4:22), rebirth unto eternal life (John 3:3–5; Titus 3:5), and transformation from a mere "child of the flesh" (*ча́до тѣле́сное*, Romans 9:8) into a "child of the kingdom" (*ча́до црⷭ҇твїа*). The prayer concludes with the full Trinitarian ekphonesis and a Renaissance floral woodcut tailpiece marking the structural close of the Office of the Catechumenate.

[^101]: Fol. 23v. Woodcut headpiece and title for the Order of Holy Baptism (*ПОСЛѢ́ДОВАНЇЕ СВѦТА́ГѠ КРЕЩЕ́НЇА*). The woodcut cartouche features an iconographic representation of infant immersion in a chalice-shaped font (*коу́пѣль*), captioned *КР҃ЩЕНЇЕ Ѻ҆ТРОЧАТА* ("The Baptism of an Infant"). In early printed Ruthenian euchologia, the Catechumenate and Baptism proper are demarcated as distinct liturgical orders, each preceded by its own decorative headpiece.

[^102]: Fol. 23v. The opening vestiary and rubrical prescriptions: the priest vests in white vestments (*бѣ́лаѧ ѡ҆де́жда*) and cuffs (*нарꙋ́квицѣ*, epimanikia), symbolizing the paschal joy and purity of baptismal illumination. All tapers are kindled, and the priest performs a solemn censing around the baptismal font, after which the deacon initiates the service with the classical dialogue formula: "Bless, Master" (*Бл҃гословѝ, влады́ко*).

[^103]: Fol. 24r. The Great Litany (*Вели́каѧ є҆кте́нїа*) of Holy Baptism. In accordance with early printed liturgical shorthand, the initial petitions of the general Great Litany are recorded in abbreviated incipits (concluding before the standard response *Гдⷭ҇и поми́лꙋй*), while the baptism-specific petitions are given in full. Note the petition for the local hierarch (*Ѡ҆ а҆рхїепи́скопѣ на́шемъ*), reflecting the diocesan episcopal structure of the Kyivan Metropolia under the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth.

[^104]: Fol. 24v. The special baptismal petitions of the Great Litany invoking the "supersubstantial Trinity" (*пресꙋще́ственнаѧ тро́ица*, Greek: ὑπερούσιος Τριάς). These petitions articulate the theological fruits of baptismal water: purification, illumination of the intellect (*просвѣще́нїе ра́зꙋма*), defense against invisible demonic assaults, incorruption, and adoption as a "child of light" (*сы́нъ свѣ́та*, Ephesians 5:8).

[^105]: Fol. 25r. Pauline baptismal theology in the litany: conforming unto the likeness of Christ's death and resurrection (*съѡ́бразенъ и҆ приѡбщи́тель сме́рти и҆ воскресе́нїю*, Romans 6:4–5). The preservation of the white baptismal robe (*ѡ҆де́жда кр҃ще́нїѧ*) is linked with the "betrothal of Christ" (*ѡ҆брꙋче́нїе Хрⷭ҇то́во*), expressing the mystical nuptial union between the baptized soul and Christ the Bridegroom.

[^106]: Fol. 25r. The litany designates the baptismal water as the "laver of regeneration" (*ба́нѧ па́кибытїѧ̀*, Greek: λουτρὸν палиγγενεσίας; Titus 3:5), granting remission of sins and the garment of incorruptibility. The leaf concludes with the standard concluding litany petitions: deliverance from affliction and the petition *Застꙋпѝ, сп҃сѝ...* (*Help us, save us...*).

[^107]: Fol. 25v. The conclusion of the Great Litany: the commemoration of the Theotokos (*Прⷭ҇тꙋ́ю, чⷭ҇тꙋю и҆ пребл҃гослове́ннꙋю Влⷣчцꙋ...*) and the final commendation (*Са́ми себѐ и҆ дрꙋ́гъ дрꙋ́га...*).

[^108]: Fol. 25v. The Priest's Secret Prayer during the Baptismal Litany (*Бл҃гоꙋтро́бный и҆ ми́лостивый Бг҃е...*). In the Byzantine and Ruthenian tradition, while the deacon intones the Great Litany at the font, the officiating presbyter recites this apologia pro seipso in a low voice (*чте́тъ въ себѣ̀ мл҃твꙋ сїю̀ та́и*). The prayer alludes directly to Psalm 7:9 (LXX 7:10: *"God tries the hearts and reins"*), Hebrews 4:13 (*"all things are naked and opened unto the eyes of Him with Whom we have to do"*), and Wisdom 11:23 (*"overlookest the sins of men unto repentance"*), pleading that the unworthiness of the minister may not impede the efficacy of the sacrament.

[^109]: Fol. 26r. Continuation of the priest's secret prayer. The text draws on 1 Corinthians 9:27 (*"lest that by any means, when I have preached to others, I myself should be a castaway"*, Slavonic: *неискоу́шенъ*) and Galatians 4:19 (*"until Christ be formed in you"*). The petition frames the newly baptized candidate within the ecclesiological architecture of Ephesians 2:20 (*"built upon the foundation of the Apostles and Prophets"*) and the prophetic metaphor of the planting of truth (*са́дъ и҆́стинный*, Isaiah 60:21; 61:3) in the Catholic Church.

[^110]: Fol. 26v. Rubric regarding the conclusion of the priest's secret prayer: the priest does not chant an audible exclamation (*ни въꙁглаша́етъ*), but recites even the concluding "Amen" secretly within himself (*но и҆ а҆ми́нь въ себѣ̀ гле́тъ*), preserving liturgical silence while the deacon finishes the litany petitions.

[^111]: Fol. 26v. The Great Prayer of Sanctification of the Baptismal Waters (*Ве́лїй є҆сѝ, Гдⷭ҇и, и҆ чю́дна дѣла̀ твоѧ̀...*), traditionally attributed in Byzantine and Ruthenian euchologia to St. Sophronius of Jerusalem (and identical in its opening stanzas to the Great Blessing of the Waters at Theophany). The threefold opening acclamation of God's unutterable cosmic wonders is followed by the cosmological anamnesis of creation ex nihilo (*ѿ небытїа̀ въ бытїѐ*) and divine providence (*про́мыслъ*). The text here introduces the ancient physical theory of the four elements (*ѿ четы́рехъ съста́въ*, the four elements: earth, water, air, fire).
