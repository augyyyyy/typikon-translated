[^2973]: Pastoral and canonical context: *Prayer over a youth who has apostatized, defiled himself, and is repenting* (`Мл҃тва , ѡ҆тро́чищꙋ ѿвръ́гшꙋсѧ , и҆ поганившꙋ . и҆ кающусѧ` / Greek: *Εὐχὴ ἐπὶ παιδὸς ἀρνησαμένου καὶ μιανθέντος καὶ μετανοοῦντος*). This pastoral office was appointed for young Christians who, having fallen into the hands of non-Christians (Tatars, Ottomans, or pagans) through capture, abduction, or slavery, or having succumbed to coercion or youthful frailty, had apostatized or participated in defiling pagan/Islamic rites, and subsequently escaped or returned desiring reconciliation with the Church. Fol. 491r is marked in the outer margin as `уч҃а` (491) and signed at the foot as Gathering 53, sheet 2 (`цц в҃`).
[^2974]: Scriptural citations: the prayer opens with the Pauline formulation of 1 Timothy 2:4 (`хотя́й всѣ̑мъ чл҃кѡмъ сп҃тисѧ , и҆ въ позна́нїе и҆́стинны прїитѝ` / Greek: *ὁ πάντας ἀνθρώπους θέλων σωθῆναι καὶ εἰς ἐπίγνωσιν ἀληθείας ἐλθεῖν*), joined to the parable of the lost sheep (Matt. 18:12, Luke 15:4) and the restoration of the divine image in humanity (Gen. 1:26–27).
[^2975]: Patristic and Scriptural allusions: fol. 491v. The prayer invokes the parable of the Prodigal Son (Luke 15:11–32: `ꙗ҆́ко блꙋ́днаго к тебѣ̀ Бг҃ꙋ и҆ ѿц҃ꙋ̀ възвраща́ющасѧ`), and asks that the restored youth be made a partaker with the faithful of God's "most pure priesthood" (`прчⷭты̑мъ ст҃е́нствомъ твои̑мъ` / Greek: *τῷ παναγίῳ ἱερατείῳ σου*), an allusion to the royal priesthood of all believers (1 Peter 2:9: *βασίλειον ἱεράτευμα*).
[^2976]: Doxology and prayer of head-bowing dialogue: fol. 492r (`уч҃в`, Gathering 53, sheet 3: `цц г҃`). The first prayer concludes with the standard Trinitarian doxology (`ны́нѣ и҆ при́сно и҆ въ вѣ́кы вѣкѡ́мъ , а҆ми́нь`), followed by the rubrical dialogue introducing the prayer of head-bowing (*κεφαλοκλισία*): the priestly blessing of peace (`Іере́й . Ми́ръ всѣ̑мъ`) and the bidding to bow before the Lord (`Гла́вы ва́ша Гв҃и приклони́те`).
[^2977]: Scriptural citations: fol. 492v. The prayer of head-bowing integrates Psalm 143:5 [LXX] (`Приклони́вый нб҃са̀ , и҆ съше́дъ` / *Κύριε, κλῖνον οὐρανοὺς καὶ κατάβηθι*), redemption from the ancestral curse (Gal. 3:13), angelic joy over one repenting sinner (Luke 15:7, 10: `ра́дости бы́ти на нб҃сѣхъ ѡ҆ є҆ди́номъ грѣ́шницѣ ка́ющемсѧ`), and Christ's promise of authority over adversarial powers (Luke 10:19: *ἰδοὺ δίδωμι ὑμῖν τὴν ἐξουσίαν τοῦ πατεῖν ἐπάνω ὄφεων καὶ σκορπίων, καὶ ἐπὶ πᾶσαν τὴν δύναμιν τοῦ ἐχθροῦ*).
[^2978]: Foliation anomaly: fol. 493r is misnumbered in the outer margin as `ф҃` (500) rather than `уч҃г` (493), jumping eight folios ahead due to a compositor's error in Gathering 53 (`цц д҃`). The erroneous 500-series foliation persists through folios 494r (`ф҃а` = 501) and 495r (`ф҃в` = 502).
[^2979]: Reconciliatory Chrismation rubric: the rubric directs the priest to take the Holy Myron and sign the returning penitent cruciformly upon the sensory gateways—the forehead, hands, and feet (`И҆ взе́мъ ст҃о́е му́ро зна́менаетъ и҆ на челѣ̀ , и҆ на рꙋка́хъ , и҆ на нога́хъ` / Greek: *Καὶ λαβὼν τὸ ἅγιον μύρον, σφραγίζει αὐτὸν εἰς τὸ μέτωπον, καὶ τὰς χεῖρας, καὶ τοὺς πόδας*). In Eastern canonical jurisprudence (Canon 7 of the Second Ecumenical Council and Canon 95 of the Council in Trullo), apostates who had received valid Trinitarian baptism are reconciled through the re-sealing of the gift of the Holy Spirit via Chrismation rather than re-baptism.
[^2980]: Initiation office: *Prayer when thou wishest to catechize a pagan* (`Мл҃тва є҆гда̀ хо́щеши ѡ҆гласи́ти ꙗ҆зы́чника` / Greek: *Εὐχὴ ἡνίκα βούλει κατηχῆσαι Ἕλληνα*). This ancient Byzantine euchological rite marks the formal admission of a pagan or non-Christian convert into the order of catechumens. The rubric prescribes that the candidate kneel outside the threshold before the church doors (`пре́дъ две́рьми` / Greek: *πρὸ τῶν πυλῶν*), signifying his status outside the ecclesial body prior to illumination. The priest signs him thrice cruciformly with the initial prayer quoting Titus 2:14 (`лю́ди прⷭносоу́щныѧ , ревни́телѧ блага́ымъ дѣ́лѡмъ` / Greek: *λαὸν περιούσιον, ζηλωτὴν καλῶν ἔργων*).
[^2981]: Illuminative prayer of the Catechumenate: fol. 494r (Gathering 53, sheet 5: `цц є҃`, numbered `ф҃а` = 501). The prayer petitions for the interior illumination of the candidate's senses (Ps. 118:18 [LXX]) and his preparation for baptismal rebirth ("the font of regeneration," Titus 3:5). Following the prayer, the priest repeats the triple cruciform blessing over the catechumen before intoning the second prayer of illumination (*Σύ, Δέσποτα, ἐπὶ σωτηρίᾳ τοῦ κόσμου ἐξαπέστειλας τὸν ἅγιόν σου Λόγον...*).
[^2982]: Canonical office of reconciliation: *Prayer over a Christian who was seduced into pagan error, and has returned again, and fled unto the Church of God* (`Мл҃тва хрⷭтїа́ниноу прельсти́вшоусѧ въ ꙗ҆зы́ческое прельще́нїе . и҆ па́ки възврати́вшоусѧ , и҆ цр҃кви Бж҃їи прибѣ́гшоу` / Greek: *Εὐχὴ ἐπὶ χριστιανοῦ ἀπατηθέντος εἰς ἑλληνικὴν ἀπάτην, καὶ πάλιν ἐπιστρέψαντος, καὶ τῇ ἐκκλησίᾳ τοῦ Θεοῦ προσδραμόντος*). This comprehensive penitential office addresses adult baptized Christians who lapsed into paganism, Islam, or heresy and subsequently returned seeking reconciliation. The opening invocation quotes 1 Timothy 4:10 (`сп҃се всѣ̑мъ чл҃кѡмъ , па́че же вѣ́рнымъ` / Greek: *ὅς ἐστι σωτὴρ πάντων ἀνθρώπων, μάλιστα πιστῶν*).
[^2983]: Historical context of captivity and epigraphic reading: fol. 495r (numbered `ф҃в` = 502). The text describes the concrete historical reality of Ruthenian and Byzantine Christians held in captivity by "godless enemies" (`ѿ плѣне́нїа безъбѡ́жныхъ вра̑гъ`), where lapse into error occurred either through lack of instruction or under torturous coercion (`и҆лѝ нꙋ́ждею томле́на`). At the start of line 16, the physical ink reads `чтѝ .`, which translates the Greek imperative *καθάρισον* ("cleanse [him]"), representing an abbreviated or reduced Slavonic imperative form of *чистити* (or compositor's omission of the prefix in *[ѡ҆]чистѝ*).
[^2984]: Theology of the baptismal character: fol. 495v (502v). The prayer contains one of the most vivid patristic metaphors for the indelible seal (*печатлѣ́нїе* / *σφραγίς*) of Holy Baptism. Though obscured during apostasy, the spark of baptismal grace (*и҆́скры сп҃снаго кр҃ще́нїа* / *ὁ... σπινθὴρ τοῦ σωτηρίου βαπτίσματος*) is never extinguished; fanned by the breeze of divine grace (*въздоу́хы бл҃гти твоеѧ̀* / *ταῖς τῆς σῆς χάριτος αὔραις*), it is noetically rekindled into a spiritual flame (*въ пла́мень дх҃о́венъ оу҆́мно распали́сѧ* / *εἰς φλόγα πνευματικὴν νοερῶς ἀναφθῇ*). The prayer continues across the gathering break onto physical leaf p1021 in Cohort 103.
