# PART XLVII: THE ORDER CONCERNING THE COMMUNION OF THE HOLY WATER OF THE THEOPHANY OF THE GREAT SANCTIFICATION [^3817]

*When it is not permitted to partake of the life-creating, most pure mysteries of Christ, of His Body and Blood: by the counsel of one's spiritual father, when the time directs, one partakes of the consecrated water, according to the customary preparation for sanctification; and the priest having made the beginning, he says these prayers:*[^3818]

**Priest:** Blessed is our God, always, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^3819]

*Heavenly King; Trisagion; after Our Father; Lord, have mercy (12 times); Come, let us worship (3 times); Psalm 50: Have mercy on me, O God; after these, these troparia:*[^3820]

*Troparion:* O Thou Who hast the cup of inexhaustible treasures, grant me to draw water unto the remission of sins, for I am constrained with thirst, O Compassionate Lord![^3821]

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*

*Theotokion:* O pure Virgin, palace of the Word, show my soul to be a temple of the Spirit; with water from the life-originating spring give me to drink, who am consumed by the burning of sin, that according to my duty I may glorify thee.[^3822]

*After these, the prayer of our father among the saints, John Chrysostom:*[^3823]

**Priest:** O Lord our God, Who art understood together with Thine immortal and unoriginate Father, and Who co-abidest with Thy co-eternal Spirit;[^3824] Who art the Giver of all sanctification; Who of old didst sanctify the waters by Thine own baptism,[^3825] and Who now ever sanctifiest them through Thine invocation: do Thou Thyself, through the descent and operation of Thy Holy Spirit, make also this holy water consecrated, that it may be unto me, Thy servant partaking thereof, for the enlightenment of the soul, for the sanctification of the body, for the mortification of passions, for the rejection of wicked thoughts, for a bulwark and putting away of the evil[^3826] assaults, and unto every profitable benefit; that being covered by its grace, I may remain unharmed by every snare, unto the glory of Thy most good and holy name; through the prayers of our most pure Lady, the Theotokos, and of all the saints. Amen.[^3827]

*Then the assembly makes three bows [metanias], chanting the troparion of the Baptism:*[^3828]

*Troparion of the Theophany, Tone 1:* When Thou, O Lord, wast baptized in the Jordan, the worship of the Trinity was made manifest; for the voice of the Parent bare witness unto Thee, calling Thee His beloved Son; and the Spirit, in the form of a dove, confirmed the steadfastness of the word. O Christ God, Who hast appeared and enlightened the world, glory be to Thee![^3829]

*Glory... Both now... Kontakion:*[^3830]

*Kontakion of the Theophany, Tone 4:* Today Thou hast appeared unto all the universe, and Thy light, O Lord, hath been signed upon us, who with understanding sing praise unto Thee: Thou hast come and hast appeared, the Light unapproachable![^3831]

*Then he partakes of the consecrated water with fear and reverence; and after this, say the prayer of our father among the saints, John Chrysostom:*[^3832]

**Officiant / Communicant:** Glory to Thine ineffable and unsearchable goodness! Glory to Thy most good and forbearing long-suffering! Glory to Thy compassions, that even now Thou hast made me, a sinner and Thine unworthy servant, worthy of sanctification through the partaking of this consecrated water[^3833] strengthen my life on account thereof, keeping me wholly unharmed; make me uncaught by the adversaries; show me unenslaved to unclean thoughts; renew me wholly, vivify me wholly unto good works, wing me wholly unto desire for Thee.[^3834]

*Ecphonesis:*

**Priest:** For Thou art the sweetness of our souls, and unto Thee do we send up glory, with Thine unoriginate Father, and Thine all-holy, good, and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. [Amen.][^3835]

*After this: It is truly meet; Glory... Both now...; Lord, have mercy (3 times); and the dismissal.*[^3836]

*[Engraving: Decorative woodcut headpiece across the top with a central medallion depicting the half-figure of Christ Blessing (IC XC) holding the Book of Gospels, flanked by Renaissance strapwork, acanthus foliage, and fruit clusters.]*

### Notes

[^3817]: Headpiece and title marking the beginning of Part XLVII: The Order Concerning the Communion of the Holy Water of the Theophany of the Great Sanctification (Въслѣдова́нїе ѡ҆ причаще́нїи ст҃ыѧ воды̀ и҆́же на Бг҃оꙗвле́нїе вели́каго ѻ҆свѧще́нїѧ; Greek Ἀκολουθία εἰς τὸ μεταλαβεῖν τοῦ ἁγιάσματος τῶν Θεοφανίων). This canon and penitential order provided pastoral consolation and spiritual medicine for Christians undergoing canonical penance (epitimia) who were barred from receiving the Holy Eucharist. In Byzantine and Kyivan canon law, Great Agiasma (sanctified water of Epiphany) functioned as an antidoron-like sanctification for penanced penitents under spiritual direction.
[^3818]: Introductory rubric detailing canonical penitential discipline: an individual impeded by grave sins or ecclesiastical penance from partaking of the Holy Mysteries (Holy Communion) may, upon the discernment and blessing of their spiritual father (confessor), partake of the Theophany water of the Great Blessing with proper fasting and prayerful preparation.
[^3819]: The opening liturgical blessing of the priest: "Blessed is our God..." (Εὐλογητὸς ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν).
[^3820]: Rubric prescribing the standard opening prayer sequence (Enarxis): Heavenly King, Trisagion, the Lord's Prayer, twelvefold Kyrie Eleison, the threefold invitation to worship, and the primary penitential Psalm 50 LXX (Ps. 51 KJV), followed by special supplicatory troparia.
[^3821]: Supplicatory troparion expressing spiritual thirst and repentance, invoking Christ as the possessor of the inexhaustible cup of divine mercy (cf. John 4:14; 7:37–38).
[^3822]: Penitential Theotokion addressing the Virgin Mary as the "palace of the Word" (сло́ва пола́то, Greek τοῦ Λόγου παλάτιον) and imploring the cooling and cleansing waters of divine grace to quench the burning fever of sin.
[^3823]: Rubric introducing the preparatory prayer attributed to St. John Chrysostom, recited prior to drinking the Great Agiasma.
[^3824]: High theological Trinitarian invocation: Christ co-understood with the unoriginate Father and co-abiding with the co-eternal Spirit (съразоумѣва́емъ... съпребыва́ѧ; Greek συμφραζόμενος... συνυπάρχων).
[^3825]: Allusion to Christ's baptism in the Jordan, whereby the waters were sanctified and cleansed of the demonic powers: Matt. 3:13–17; Mark 1:9–11; Luke 3:21–22.
[^3826]: Euchological petition enumerating the manifold spiritual and somatic effects of partaking of the Great Agiasma: illumination of mind, bodily healing, subduing of carnal passions, casting out of wicked thoughts, and defense against demonic assaults. The prayer continues onto fol. 622r (leaf p1259).
[^3827]: Conclusion of St. John Chrysostom's prayer before partaking of the Great Agiasma.
[^3828]: Rubric prescribing three metanias (deep reverences or prostrations) by the communicant or the assembly while chanting the festive troparion of the Epiphany.
[^3829]: The renowned Byzantine Troparion of the Theophany of the Lord (Greek Ἐν Ἰορδάνῃ βαπτιζομένου σου, Кύριε, ἡ τῆς Τριάδος ἐφανερώθη προσκύνησις), Tone 1.
[^3830]: Rubric directing the chanting of the Little Doxology followed by the festive Theophany Kontakion, continuing onto fol. 622v (leaf p1260).
[^3831]: The celebrated Theophany Kontakion of St. Romanos the Melodist, Tone 4: Greek Ἐπεφάνης σήμερον τῇ οἰκουμένῃ (Ruthenian recension variant reading: въсе́й въселе́ннѣй... неприкоснове́нный / Greek ἀπρόσιτον).
[^3832]: Rubric directing the communicant to drink the holy water with sacred fear and reverence, followed immediately by the post-communion thanksgiving prayer.
[^3833]: Opening of the post-communion thanksgiving prayer of St. John Chrysostom, giving thanks for the spiritual illumination and sanctification conferred by the Epiphany water. The prayer continues onto fol. 623r (leaf p1261).
[^3834]: Continuation of the thanksgiving prayer attributed to St. John Chrysostom recited after partaking of the Great Agiasma (Theophany water), from fol. 622v (leaf p1260). The printed Slavonic witness reads въсего̀ къ твое́моу къпери жела́нїю, where къпери represents a scribal or typographer's corruption for въперѝ (imperative of въперити / вперяти, Greek πτέρωσον / ἀνάпτερον, "give wings unto", "wing me wholly unto desire for Thee").
[^3835]: Concluding Trinitarian ecphonesis of St. John Chrysostom's thanksgiving prayer: "For Thou art the sweetness of our souls..." (Greek Ὅτι σὺ εἶ ὁ γλυκασμὸς τῶν ψυχῶν ἡμῶν). The printed text abbreviates the concluding liturgical formula to нн҃ѣ и҆ прⷭ҇но и҆ въ вѣ́ки вѣкѡ́мъ : ~, with the final "Amen" supplied diplomatically.
[^3836]: Concluding rubrics prescribing the Axion Estin (It is truly meet), the Little Doxology (Glory... Both now...), threefold Kyrie Eleison, and the liturgical dismissal (apolysis), marking the formal conclusion of Part XLVII.
