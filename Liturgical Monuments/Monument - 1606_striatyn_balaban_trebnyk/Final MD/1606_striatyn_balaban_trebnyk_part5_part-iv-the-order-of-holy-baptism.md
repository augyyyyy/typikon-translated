# PART IV: THE ORDER OF HOLY BAPTISM [^101]

*The priest enters into the church, and having vested in white priestly vesture and cuffs, all the candles being lighted, he takes the censer and goes unto the font, and censes round about. And having given back the censer, he bows.*[^102]

*Then the deacon says:*
**Deacon:** Bless, Master.

*And the priest [says] with a loud voice:*

**Priest:** Blessed is the kingdom of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. [Choir: Amen.]

*The Great Litany:*[^103]
**Deacon:**
In peace let us pray to the Lord.
For the peace from above, and for the salvation of our souls, [let us pray to the Lord].
For the peace of the whole world, and the good standing [of the holy churches of God, let us pray to the Lord].
For this holy temple, and those that [enter into it] with faith, [reverence, and the fear of God, let us pray to the Lord].
For our Archbishop, *[Name]*, for his venerable presbyterate, and the diaconate in Christ, [let us pray to the Lord].
That this water may be sanctified by the power, operation, and descent of the Holy Spirit, let us pray to the Lord.
That there may be sent down upon it the grace of redemption, the blessing of Jordan, let us pray to the Lord.
That there may come upon this water the cleans- ing operation of the supersubstantial Trinity, let us pray to the Lord.[^104]
That we may be illuminated with the light of understanding and piety through the coming of the Holy Spirit, let us pray to the Lord.
That it may receive the turning away of every assault of visible and invisible enemies, let us pray to the Lord.
That he who is baptized therein may be made worthy of the incorruptible kingdom, let us pray to the Lord.
For him who now approaches unto holy illumination, and for his salvation, let us pray to the Lord.
That he may be shown forth a child of light, and an heir of eternal blessings, let us pray to the Lord.

That he may be conformed unto the likeness and partake of the death and resurrection of Christ our God, let us pray to the Lord.[^105]
That he may preserve the garment of baptism and the betrothal of Christ undefiled and blameless in the dread day of Christ our God, let us pray to the Lord.
That this water may be unto him a laver of regeneration, unto the remission of sins, and a garment of incorruption, let us pray to the Lord.[^106]
That the Lord God may give ear unto the voice of our supplication, let us pray to the Lord.
That he and we may be delivered from all affliction, wrath, and necessity, let us pray to the Lord.
*Help us, save us, have mercy on us, and keep us Commemorating our Most Holy, pure, most blessed and glorious Lady, the Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary, with all the saints...*[^107]

*And while the deacon is saying this litany, the priest reads this prayer within himself secretly:*[^108]

**Priest:**
[Fol. 25v. Decorative initial B.]
O compassionate and merciful God, Who triest the hearts and reins, and Who alone knowest the secrets of men; for there is no thing that is unmanifest before Thee, but all things are naked and exposed before Thine eyes: knowing what concerns me, loathe me not, neither turn away Thy face from me; but take away my transgressions at this hour, Thou that overlookest the sins of men unto repentance. Wash away my bodily uncleanness and my spiritual defilement, and sanctify me wholly by Thine invisible power, and by Thy spiritual right hand: that while promising freedom unto others, through perfect faith in Thine ineffable love for mankind, I myself may not become reprobate as a slave of sin. Yea, O Master, Who alone art good and the Lover of mankind, let me not return humbled and put to shame; but send me power from on high, and strengthen me for the ministry of this Thy present Mystery, great and heavenly; and form the image of Thy Christ in him who is about to be reborn through my unworthiness.[^109] And build him upon the foundation of Thine Apostles and Prophets, and destroy him not; but plant him as a true planting in Thy holy, catholic, and apostolic Church; and pluck him not up: that as he advances in piety, through him also may be glorified Thine all-holy Name, of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

*It is proper to know that he does not make exclamation aloud, but says even the "Amen" within himself.*[^110]

*Then he reads this prayer with a loud voice:*[^111]

**Priest:**
Great art Thou, O Lord, and marvelous are Thy works, and no word is sufficient to sing the praise of Thy wonders.
*(And say this thrice.)*

[Fol. 26v. Decorative initial T.]
For by Thy will Thou hast brought all things out of non-existence into being; by Thy power Thou holdest creation together, and by Thy providence Thou directest the world. Thou hast composed creation; with four seasons Thou hast crowned the circle of the year.[^112] All the spiritual powers tremble before Thee; the sun sings Thy praise; the moon glorifies Thee; the stars make supplication unto Thee; the light obeys Thee; the abysses tremble before Thee; the springs serve Thee. Thou hast stretched out the heaven like a curtain; Thou hast established the earth upon the waters; Thou hast hedged the sea with sand; Thou hast poured out the air for breathing. The Archangelic powers minister unto Thee; the Angelic choirs worship Thee; the many-eyed Cherubim and the six-winged Seraphim, standing round about and flying, with fear cover themselves from Thine unapproachable glory.[^113] For Thou, being God uncircumscribed, without beginning, and ineffable, camest down upon the earth, taking the form of a servant, being made in the likeness of men. For Thou couldest not endure, O Master, through the tender compassion of Thy mercy, to behold mankind tormented by the devil; but Thou camest and didst save us. We confess Thy grace, we proclaim Thy mercy, we conceal not Thy benefactions. Thou hast set free the nature of our race; Thou didst sanctify the virginal womb by Thy nativity. All creation sings the praise of Thee Who didst manifest Thyself. For Thou, our God, didst appear upon the earth, and didst dwell among men. Thou the waters of Jordan didst sanctify, sending down from heaven Thy Holy Spirit, and didst break the heads of the dragons that nested there.[^114]

Do Thou Thyself, therefore, O King, Lover of mankind, come even now through the descent of Thy Holy Spirit, and sanctify this water. *(Thrice.)*

And grant unto it the grace of redemption, the blessing of Jordan. And make it a fountain of incorruption, a gift of sanctification, a loosing of sins, a healing of diseases, a destruction of demons, unapproachable to adversary powers, filled with Angelic strength; that they who plot against Thy creature may flee from it. For I have called upon Thy Name, O Lord, marvelous and glorious, and terrible unto adversaries.[^115]

*And he breathes upon the water thrice, and signs it with the sign of the Cross with his hand, and says:*

**Priest:**
Let all adversary powers be crushed beneath the sign of the representation of Thy Cross. *(Thrice.)*

And let all airy and invisible phantoms depart from us; and let no dark demon hide himself in this water; neither let there descend with him that is baptized (we beseech Thee, O Lord) an evil spirit, bringing darkness of thoughts and tumult of mind. But do Thou, O Master of all, manifest this water as water of repose, water of redemption, water of sanctification, cleansing of the defilement of flesh and spirit, loosing of bonds, remission of sins, illumination of souls, the laver of regeneration, renewal of the Spirit, the gift of adoption, the garment of incorruptibility, the renewal of the soul, a fountain of life.[^116] For Thou hast said, O Lord: "Wash you, make you clean, put away evil from your souls." Thou hast bestowed upon us regeneration from on high through water and the Spirit. Manifest Thyself, O Lord, in this water, and grant that he who is baptized therein may be transformed, so that he may put off the old man, corrupt according to the deceits of lust, and put on the new man, renewed according to the image of Him that created him; that having been united unto the likeness of Thy death through baptism, a partaker of the resurrection also shall he be, and having preserved the gift of Thy Holy Spirit,[^117] and increased the deposit of grace, he may receive the prize of the high calling, and be numbered among the firstborn enrolled in heaven.

*Ekphonesis:*
For unto Thee is due all glory, thanksgiving, honor, and worship, to God and our Lord Jesus Christ, together with Thine unoriginate Father, and with Thine all-holy, good, and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

**Priest:**
Peace be unto all.[^118]

*Bow your heads unto the Lord.*

*And having bowed his head over the vessel of oil, he breathes upon it thrice, and blesses with his hand, thrice. And the deacon, holding the oil in his hand, says:*

**Deacon:**
Let us pray to the Lord.

*And the priest says this prayer:*

**Priest:**
O Master, Lord God of our fathers, Who to them that were in the ark of Noah didst send a dove bearing a twig of olive in its mouth,[^119] the sign of reconciliation and of salvation from the flood, and didst prefigure through these the mystery of grace; and didst provide the fruit of the olive for the fulfillment of Thy holy mysteries; and thereby didst fill them that were under the Law with the Holy Spirit, and perfectest them that are under grace: Do Thou Thyself bless this oil also by the power, and operation, and descent of Thy Holy Spirit, that it may be an anointing of incorruption, an armor of righteousness, a renewal of soul and body, a fountain of life, an averting of every operation of the devil, for the removal of all evils, unto them that are anointed with faith, or that partake of it:[^120]

*And having stood erect, he exclaims aloud:*

**Priest:**
Unto Thy glory, and of Thine Only-begotten Son, and of Thine all-holy, and good, and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

**Choir:**
Amen.

**Deacon:**
Let us attend.

*And the priest, having taken the oil, makes crosses with drops upon the water thrice, singing Alleluia with the people, thrice. Then he exclaims aloud:*

**Priest:**
Blessed is God, Who enlightens and sanctifies every man that comes into the world, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.[^121]

**Choir:**
Amen.

*And he that is to be baptized is brought unto the priest. And he, having taken of the holy oil with his finger, makes the sign of the Cross upon the infant, on the forehead, and on the breast, and on the face, and on the shoulders, saying:*

**Priest:**
The servant of God, [Name], is anointed with the oil of gladness, in the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

**Choir:**
Amen.

*And he signs his breast and shoulders. On the breast indeed, saying:*

**Priest:**
Unto the healing of soul and body.

*And on the ears:*[^122]

**Priest:**
Unto the hearing of faith.

*On the feet:*

**Priest:**
That he may walk in Thy steps.

*On the hands:*

**Priest:**
Thy hands have made me and fashioned me.

*When his whole body has been anointed,*

*The priest baptizes him, holding him upright, and facing eastward, saying:*[^123]

**Priest:**
The servant of God, [Name], is baptized in the Name of the Father,

*and he immerses him once, saying:*
Amen.

and of the Son, Amen,

*and he immerses him and brings him up;*

and of the Holy Spirit, Amen, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*At each naming immersing him into the water, and bringing him up.*

*And after the baptism, the priest washes himself, singing with the people Psalm 31:*[^124]

Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered. Blessed is the man unto whom the Lord does not impute sin, neither is there guile in his mouth. Because I kept silence, my bones waxed old through my crying all the day long. For day and night Thy hand was heavy upon me; I turned into misery when the thorn was pierced into me. I acknowledged my sin, and mine iniquity have I not hidden. I said: I will confess against myself mine iniquity unto the Lord; and Thou forgavest the ungodliness of my heart. For this shall every one that is holy pray unto Thee in a time acceptable;

surely in a flood of many waters they shall not come nigh unto Him. Thou art my refuge from the affliction that surrounds me; my joy, deliver me from them that compass me about. I will give thee understanding, and instruct thee in this way wherein thou shalt go; I will fix Mine eyes upon thee. Be ye not as horse and mule, which have no understanding; whose jaws thou must bind with bit and bridle, lest they come nigh unto thee. Many are the scourges of the sinner; but mercy shall compass him that hopes in the Lord. Be glad in the Lord, and rejoice, ye righteous; and boast, all ye that are upright in heart.[^125]

*And this psalm is said thrice.*

*And if he that is baptized is an infant, he seats him in the font, the water reaching unto his neck, holding him with his left hand; and taking warm water with his right hand, he pours it upon his head, because care must be taken lest the infant drown; and he says the aforesaid words, that is: In the Name of the Father, and the rest. But if he that is baptized has reached understanding, he immerses him in three immersions as we have said above.*[^126]

*Thereafter he clothes him in a garment, saying:*

**Priest:**
The servant of God, *[Name]*, is clothed with the robe of righteousness, in the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*And he sings this troparion, in Tone 8:*[^127]

*Troparion, Tone 8:*
Grant unto me a robe of light, O Thou that deckest Thyself with light as with a garment, O greatly merciful Christ our God.[^128]

*And after he has been clothed, the priest prays again, saying this prayer:*

**Deacon:** Let us pray to the Lord.

**Priest:**
Blessed art Thou, O Lord God Almighty, Fountain of good things, Sun of Righteousness, Who hast caused the light of salvation to shine forth unto them that were in darkness, through the manifestation of Thine Only-begotten Son and our God; and Who hast granted unto us unworthy ones blessed cleansing in holy water, and divine sanctification in the life-creating anointing; Who even now hast been well pleased to regenerate Thy servant newly-illumined, *[Name]*, by water and the Spirit, and granted unto him remission of sins voluntary and involuntary.[^129] Do Thou Thyself, O Master of all, compassionate King, grant unto him also the seal of the gift of Thy Holy, all-powerful, and adorable Spirit, and communion of the holy Body and precious Blood of Thy Christ. Preserve him in Thy sanctification; confirm him in the Orthodox faith; deliver him from the evil one and all his snares; and by Thy saving fear keep his soul in purity and righteousness: that pleasing Thee in every deed and word, he may become a son and heir of Thy Heavenly Kingdom.

*Ekphonesis:*

**Priest:**
For Thou art our God, a God to show mercy and to save us, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.[^130]

**Choir:**
Amen.

*Then he anoints the baptized with the holy Chrism, making the sign of the Cross: on the forehead, on the eyes, on the breast, on the hands, and on the feet, saying:*[^131]

**Priest:**
The seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

*Then the priest with the sponsor and the newly-baptized walk round about the analogion, singing thrice:*[^132]

**Choir:**
As many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ. Alleluia. *(Thrice.)*

*Then the Prokeimenon, Tone 3:*

**Reader:**
The Lord is my illumination and my Savior; whom shall I fear?[^133]

*Verse:*
The Lord is the defender of my life; of whom shall I be afraid?

*Epistle to the Romans:*[^134]

**Reader:**
Brethren, as many of us as were baptized into Christ were baptized into His death. Therefore we were buried with Him through baptism into death; that like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life. For if we have become united with Him in the likeness of His death, so also shall we be partakers of His resurrection: knowing this, that our old man was crucified with Him, that the body of sin might be done away, that henceforth we should not serve sin. For he that is dead is justified from sin. Now if we died with Christ, we believe that we shall also live with Him: knowing that Christ, having been raised from the dead, dies no more; death has dominion over Him no more. For in that He died, He died unto sin once; but in that He lives, He lives unto God. Likewise reckon ye also yourselves to be dead indeed unto sin, but alive unto God in Christ Jesus our Lord.[^135]

**Choir:**
Alleluia.

**Deacon:**
Wisdom. Stand upright. Let us hear the Holy Gospel.

**Priest:**
The reading from the Holy Gospel according to Matthew.

**Deacon:**
Let us attend.

**Priest:**
At that time, the eleven disciples went into Galilee, into a mountain where Jesus had appointed them. And when they saw Him, they worshipped Him; but some doubted. And Jesus came and spake unto them, saying: All power is given unto Me in heaven and in earth. Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit: teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you. And lo, I am with you always, even unto the end of the age. Amen.[^136]

*Then the deacon says the litany:*[^137]

**Deacon:**
Have mercy on us, O God, according to Thy great mercy...

Again we pray for our pious sovereign.

Again we pray for our Bishop, *[Name]*;

Again we pray for mercy, life, and the remission of sins for the servant of God, *[Name]*, the sponsor;

Again we pray for the newly-illumined servant of God, *[Name]*, that he may be preserved in the faith of a pure confession, all the days of his life.

*Ekphonesis:*

**Priest:**
For Thou art a merciful God and the Lover of mankind, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

*Then: Glory... both now... Lord, have mercy (thrice). Bless.*

*Priest: The Dismissal:*

**Priest:**
May He Who deigned to be baptized in the Jordan by John for the sake of our salvation, Christ our true God, through the prayers of His most pure Mother, by the power of the precious and life-creating Cross, and of all the saints, have mercy on us and save us[^138] for He is good and the Lover of mankind.[^139]

*It is fitting to know that if there be a Liturgy, they carry the newly-illumined infant with candles at the Great Transfer [Entrance].[^140] But if the one who has been baptized be an adult, let him go himself before the divine Gifts at the Great Entrance, holding a candle in each hand. And he partakes of the Holy Mysteries of Christ our God, whether he be an infant or an adult.*

*And after the dismissal, he goes forth unto his own house holding burning candles, his kin escorting him. And if, therefore, the one baptized be an adult he is brought each day unto the church, likewise holding burning candles: to Vespers, and to Matins, and to the Liturgy, even unto the eighth day.*[^141]

### Notes

[^101]: Fol. 23v. Woodcut headpiece and title for the Order of Holy Baptism (*ПОСЛѢ́ДОВАНЇЕ СВѦТА́ГѠ КРЕЩЕ́НЇА*). The woodcut cartouche features an iconographic representation of infant immersion in a chalice-shaped font (*коу́пѣль*), captioned *КР҃ЩЕНЇЕ Ѻ҆ТРОЧАТА* ("The Baptism of an Infant"). In early printed Ruthenian euchologia, the Catechumenate and Baptism proper are demarcated as distinct liturgical orders, each preceded by its own decorative headpiece.
[^102]: Fol. 23v. The opening vestiary and rubrical prescriptions: the priest vests in white vestments (*бѣ́лаѧ ѡ҆де́жда*) and cuffs (*нарꙋ́квицѣ*, epimanikia), symbolizing the paschal joy and purity of baptismal illumination. All tapers are kindled, and the priest performs a solemn censing around the baptismal font, after which the deacon initiates the service with the classical dialogue formula: "Bless, Master" (*Бл҃гословѝ, влады́ко*).
[^103]: Fol. 24r. The Great Litany (*Вели́каѧ є҆кте́нїа*) of Holy Baptism. In accordance with early printed liturgical shorthand, the initial petitions of the general Great Litany are recorded in abbreviated incipits (concluding before the standard response *Гдⷭ҇и поми́лꙋй*), while the baptism-specific petitions are given in full. Note the petition for the local hierarch (*Ѡ҆ а҆рхїепи́скопѣ на́шемъ*), reflecting the diocesan episcopal structure of the Kyivan Metropolia under the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth.
[^104]: Fol. 24v. The special baptismal petitions of the Great Litany invoking the "supersubstantial Trinity" (*пресꙋще́ственнаѧ тро́ица*, Greek: ὑπερούσιος Τριάς). These petitions articulate the theological fruits of baptismal water: purification, illumination of the intellect (*просвѣще́нїе ра́зꙋма*), defense against invisible demonic assaults, incorruption, and adoption as a "child of light" (*сы́нъ свѣ́та*, Ephesians 5:8).
[^105]: Fol. 25r. Pauline baptismal theology in the litany: conforming unto the likeness of Christ's death and resurrection (*съѡ́бразенъ и҆ приѡбщи́тель сме́рти и҆ воскресе́нїю*, Romans 6:4–5). The preservation of the white baptismal robe (*ѡ҆де́жда кр҃ще́нїѧ*) is linked with the "betrothal of Christ" (*ѡ҆брꙋче́нїе Хрⷭ҇то́во*), expressing the mystical nuptial union between the baptized soul and Christ the Bridegroom.
[^106]: Fol. 25r. The litany designates the baptismal water as the "laver of regeneration" (*ба́нѧ па́кибытїѧ̀*, Greek: λουτρὸν палиγγενεσίας; Titus 3:5), granting remission of sins and the garment of incorruptibility. The leaf concludes with the standard concluding litany petitions: deliverance from affliction and the petition *Застꙋпѝ, сп҃сѝ...* (*Help us, save us...*).
[^107]: Fol. 25v. The conclusion of the Great Litany: the commemoration of the Theotokos (*Прⷭ҇тꙋ́ю, чⷭ҇тꙋю и҆ пребл҃гослове́ннꙋю Влⷣчцꙋ...*) and the final commendation (*Са́ми себѐ и҆ дрꙋ́гъ дрꙋ́га...*).
[^108]: Fol. 25v. The Priest's Secret Prayer during the Baptismal Litany (*Бл҃гоꙋтро́бный и҆ ми́лостивый Бг҃е...*). In the Byzantine and Ruthenian tradition, while the deacon intones the Great Litany at the font, the officiating presbyter recites this apologia pro seipso in a low voice (*чте́тъ въ себѣ̀ мл҃твꙋ сїю̀ та́и*). The prayer alludes directly to Psalm 7:9 (LXX 7:10: *"God tries the hearts and reins"*), Hebrews 4:13 (*"all things are naked and opened unto the eyes of Him with Whom we have to do"*), and Wisdom 11:23 (*"overlookest the sins of men unto repentance"*), pleading that the unworthiness of the minister may not impede the efficacy of the sacrament.
[^109]: Fol. 26r. Continuation of the priest's secret prayer. The text draws on 1 Corinthians 9:27 (*"lest that by any means, when I have preached to others, I myself should be a castaway"*, Slavonic: *неискоу́шенъ*) and Galatians 4:19 (*"until Christ be formed in you"*). The petition frames the newly baptized candidate within the ecclesiological architecture of Ephesians 2:20 (*"built upon the foundation of the Apostles and Prophets"*) and the prophetic metaphor of the planting of truth (*са́дъ и҆́стинный*, Isaiah 60:21; 61:3) in the Catholic Church.
[^110]: Fol. 26v. Rubric regarding the conclusion of the priest's secret prayer: the priest does not chant an audible exclamation (*ни въꙁглаша́етъ*), but recites even the concluding "Amen" secretly within himself (*но и҆ а҆ми́нь въ себѣ̀ гле́тъ*), preserving liturgical silence while the deacon finishes the litany petitions.
[^111]: Fol. 26v. The Great Prayer of Sanctification of the Baptismal Waters (*Ве́лїй є҆сѝ, Гдⷭ҇и, и҆ чю́дна дѣла̀ твоѧ̀...*), traditionally attributed in Byzantine and Ruthenian euchologia to St. Sophronius of Jerusalem (and identical in its opening stanzas to the Great Blessing of the Waters at Theophany). The threefold opening acclamation of God's unutterable cosmic wonders is followed by the cosmological anamnesis of creation ex nihilo (*ѿ небытїа̀ въ бытїѐ*) and divine providence (*про́мыслъ*). The text here introduces the ancient physical theory of the four elements (*ѿ четы́рехъ съста́въ*, the four elements: earth, water, air, fire).
[^112]: Fol. 27r. The cosmological anamnesis of the Great Sanctification of the Waters (*Ве́лїй є҆сѝ, Гдⷭ҇и...*). The text invokes the cosmic obedience of creation to the Creator: the crowning of the yearly cycle (Psalm 64:12 LXX), the trembling of the intelligible/spiritual powers (Greek: νοεραὶ δυνάμεις), the supplication of the stars, the stretching out of the heavens like a curtain (Psalm 103:2 LXX), the founding of the earth upon the waters (Psalm 135:6 LXX), and the bounding of the sea with sand (Jeremiah 5:22). Note the archaic Ruthenian dual/plural forms *мнѡгоѡчи́таѧ хероуві́мъ* and *шестокрила́таѧ серафи́мъ*.
[^113]: Fol. 27v. The Christological anamnesis and the purpose of the Incarnation. God, being uncircumscribed (*неописа́ненъ*, Greek: ἀπερίγραπτος), without beginning (*безнача́ленъ*, ἄναρχος), and ineffable (*неизглаго́ланъ*, ἀνεκλάλητος), took the form of a servant (Philippians 2:7), dwelling among men (Baruch 3:38 LXX), and sanctifying the virginal womb by His Nativity. The euchology proclaims the three theological duties: "We confess Thy grace, we proclaim Thy mercy, we conceal not Thy benefactions."
[^114]: Fol. 28r. The epiclesis upon the baptismal waters: *Са́мъ оу҆́бо чл҃колю́бче цр҃ю, прїидѝ и҆ннѣ̀ прише́ствїемъ ст҃а́го твое́го дх҃а. и҆ ѡ҆свѧтѝ во́доу сїю̀* ("Do Thou Thyself, therefore, O King, Lover of mankind, come even now through the descent of Thy Holy Spirit, and sanctify this water"). The text explicitly references the crushing of the dragons' heads in the Jordan (Psalm 73:13 LXX: "Thou didst crush the heads of the dragons upon the water"). The priest repeats the epiklesis thrice, accompanied by the threefold blessing with the hand.
[^115]: Fol. 28v. The breathing and signing of the waters. The priest breathes upon the surface of the water thrice (*доу́нетъ на́ водꙋ г҃.*) and signs it with the sign of the Cross (*прекрⷭ҇титъ рꙋкою*), reciting the apotropaic prayer: *Да съкроуша́тсѧ позна́менїемъ въѡбраже́нїа крⷭ҇та твоего̀ всѧ̑ проти́вныѧ си́лы* ("Let all adversary powers be crushed beneath the sign of the representation of Thy Cross") thrice. This is followed by the deprecation of airy and invisible phantoms (Greek: ἀέρια καὶ ἀφανῆ εἰδώλια) and demons of darkness, invoking Psalm 22:2 LXX (*во́дꙋ поко́ѧ*, "water of refreshment/repose").
[^116]: Fol. 29r. Baptism as ontological regeneration. The euchological catalogue enumerates the sevenfold gifts of baptismal grace: remission of sins, illumination of souls, the laver of regeneration (Titus 3:5), renewal of the Spirit, the gift of adoption (Romans 8:15), the garment of incorruptibility, and the fountain of life. Quoting Isaiah 1:16 LXX (*"Wash you, make you clean, put away evil from your souls"*), the prayer petitions that the candidate may put off the "old man" and put on the "new man" created after the image of God (Ephesians 4:22–24; Colossians 3:10).
[^117]: Fol. 29v. The famous typographical erratum and insertion (*вы́но* / *здѣ́*). In the printed text of Fol. 29v, the typesetter originally omitted the clause *и҆ въскр҃нїа бꙋ́детъ. и҆ сохрани́въ* (rendering Greek καὶ τῆς ἀναστάσεως γένηται, καὶ φυλάξας) between *прича́стникъ* on line 1 and *да́ръ* on line 2. Discovering the error during presswork, the Striatyn printers placed an asterisk above line 1 with the marginal notation *вы́но,* (Early Modern East Slavic: *вынос*, denoting an omission/insertion) and printed the missing line at the foot of the page (line 16) preceded by *здѣ́* ("here") and a corresponding red asterisk. The prayer concludes with the Pauline allusions to the prize of the high calling (Philippians 3:14) and enrollment among the firstborn in heaven (Hebrews 12:23).
[^118]: Fol. 29v–30r. The Rite of the Oil of Catechumens / Oil of Gladness (*є҆ле́й ра́дости*, Greek: ἔλαιον ἀγαλλιάσεως). The priest salutes the people with "Peace be unto all", instructs them to bow their heads, and bowing over the oil vessel, breathes upon it thrice and blesses it with his hand. The deacon holds the holy oil and intones "Let us pray to the Lord", introducing the ancient pre-baptismal prayer of blessing the oil (*Влⷣко Гдⷭ҇и Бг҃е ѻ҆ц҃ъ на́шихъ...*).
[^119]: Fol. 30r. Typological exegesis of Noah's dove and the olive branch (Genesis 8:11). The prayer explains the olive branch carried by the dove as an archetypal sign of reconciliation (*премѣне́нїа знаме́нїе*) and salvation from the flood, prefiguring the sacrament of baptismal grace and the confection of holy unction. The text affirms that the olive fruit served the mysteries of both the Old Covenant (consecration under the Law) and the New Covenant (perfection under Grace).
[^120]: Fol. 30v. The blessing and infusing of the oil into the font. The prayer requests that the oil become an "anointing of incorruption, an armor of righteousness [2 Corinthians 6:7], a renewal of soul and body, a fountain of life". Following the audible ekphonesis and choir's "Amen", the deacon calls "Let us attend" (*Вънме́мъ*), and the priest takes the oil and pours it dropwise upon the water in the form of three crosses, while singing *Alleluia* thrice with the congregation.
[^121]: Fol. 31r. The Pre-Baptismal Anointing (*Chrismation of the Catechumen* with the Oil of Gladness). Following the priestly blessing based on John 1:9 (*"Blessed is God, Who illuminates and sanctifies every man that comes into the world"*), the infant is brought to the priest. The priest dips his thumb/finger into the consecrated oil and signs the child in the form of a cross upon the forehead (*чело̀*), breast (*пръ́си*), face (*лицѐ*), and shoulders (*пле́щи*), reciting the formula of anointing: *Пома́зꙋетсѧ ра́бъ бж҃їй [и҆мⷬ҇къ], є҆ле́ѡмъ ра́дости...* (Psalm 44:8 LXX / Hebrews 1:9).
[^122]: Fol. 31r–31v. The bodily member formulas of anointing: breast ("Unto the healing of soul and body"), ears ("Unto the hearing of faith", Romans 10:17), feet ("That he may walk in Thy steps", 1 Peter 2:21; Psalm 118:105 LXX), and hands ("Thy hands have made me and fashioned me", Psalm 118:73 LXX / Job 10:8). In the 1606 Striatyn imprint, the formulas for the ears, feet, and hands are preserved in full liturgical order.
[^123]: Fol. 31v. The Canonical Baptismal Formula and Threefold Immersion. The priest holds the candidate upright, facing eastward, and pronounces the passive Byzantine baptismal formula (*Кр҃ща́етсѧ ра́бъ Бж҃їй [и҆́мѧ ре́къ]...*), immersing the candidate completely beneath the water at each of the three Persons of the Holy Trinity (*въ и҆́мѧ ѻ҆ц҃а... и҆ сн҃а... и҆ дх҃а ст҃а́го*). The Striatyn rubrics explicitly specify that the candidate is immersed and lifted up once at each Person (*низводѧ̀ є҆го̀ въ во́доу, и҆ възводѧ̀*), sealing the sacramental regeneration in accordance with Apostolic Canon 50.
[^124]: Fol. 32r. Psalm 31:1–6a LXX (*Блаже́ни и҆́мже ѿпоусти́шасѧ безако́нїа...* / *Beati quorum remissae sunt iniquitates*). In early Byzantine and Ruthenian euchologia, Psalm 31 is the joyful post-baptismal thanksgiving psalm chanted immediately following the candidate's emergence from the font. It proclaims the core mystery of baptismal regeneration: the absolute remission and covering of sins (Romans 4:7–8), the lifting of guilt, and deliverance from ungodliness. Note the archaic Ruthenian graphic variants in Fol. 32r: *възврати́хсѧ на стра́сть, є҆гда̀ оу҆нзѐ ми те́рнъ* (rendering LXX ἐν τῷ ἐνпаγῆναί μοι ἄκανθαν) and the abbreviation *позна̑* for *позна́хъ*.
[^125]: Fol. 32v. Psalm 31:6b–11 LXX and the threefold repetition (*Сї́й же ѱло́мъ гл҃етсѧ г҃.*). The euchology contrasts the spiritual understanding granted to the newly illuminated with the uncomprehending brute nature of the horse and mule (v. 9), whose jaws must be held with bit and bridle. The rubric requiring the psalm to be sung thrice reflects the ancient baptismal station before the chrismation and vesting, mirroring the Trinitarian nature of the mystery.
[^126]: Fol. 33r. The Ruthenian Pastoral Rubric on Infant Baptism vs. Candidates of Understanding (*смы́сленъ*). In this celebrated historical rubric, the 1606 Striatyn Euchologion records the early modern Ruthenian pastoral practice: if the candidate is an infant, the priest seats him in the font with the water reaching unto his neck, holding him securely with the left hand, and pours warm water over his head with the right hand (*водѣ̀ соу́щи по́шїю, придръжа̀ лѣ́вою роуко́ю. де́сноюже прїе́мъ те́плою во́доу възлива́етъ на главоу̀ є҆го̀*), explicitly citing pastoral prudence and safety lest the infant drown (*зане́же блюстѝ зали́тисѧ младе́нца*). If, however, the candidate has reached understanding (*а́ще ли є҆́стъ смы́сленъ кр҃ща́емый*), the priest immerses him completely in three immersions (*погружа́етъ є҆го̀ въ трѝ погроуже́нїа*).
[^127]: Fol. 33r. The Vesting of the Newly Baptized in the Robe of Righteousness (*ѡ҆де́жда пра́вды*, Greek: χιτὼν δικαιοσύνης). The priest clothes the newly illuminated in a clean white tunic or garment, pronouncing the Trinitarian vesting formula. The white garment represents the recovery of the robe of light and glory originally given to Adam in Paradise before the Fall, now restored through baptismal incorruption, prefiguring the eschatological wedding garment (Matthew 22:11–12; Galatians 3:27; Revelation 7:9).
[^128]: Fol. 33v. The Baptismal Troparion in Tone 8: *Ри́зоу ми пода́ждь свѣ́тлꙋ, ѡ҆дѣѧ́йсѧ свѣ́томъ ꙗ҆́ко ри́зою...* ("Grant unto me a robe of light, O Thou that deckest Thyself with light as with a garment..."). Based upon Psalm 103:2 LXX, this troparion is sung while the sponsor and priest arrange the white baptismal robe upon the child. It expresses the mystical illumination (*photismos*) of the soul through Christ, Who wraps Himself in uncreated light.
[^129]: Fol. 33v–34r. The Pre-Chrismation Euchological Prayer (*Блгⷭ҇ве́нъ є҆си Гдⷭ҇и Бг҃е въседръжи́телю...* / Greek: Εὐχὴ τοῦ ἁγίου Μύρου). Addressed to God the Father as the "Sun of Righteousness" (Malachi 4:2), this prayer explicitly articulates the indivisible organic unity of the three traditional mysteries of Christian initiation: cleansing in holy water (Baptism), the seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit through the life-creating anointing (Chrismation), and the immediate worthy reception of the Body and Blood of Christ (Eucharist: *и причаще́нїе ст҃а́го тѣ́ла, и҆ чт҃ныѧ кро́ве Хрⷭ҇та твое́го*).
[^130]: Fol. 34v. The Priestly Ekphonesis of Chrismation (*Ꙗ҆́ко ты̀ є҆си Бг҃ъ на́шъ Бг҃ъ ми́ловати и҆ сп҃сати насъ...*). The doxology seals the invocation of the Holy Trinity with the Father Paul standard ("now and ever, and unto ages of ages"), concluding the solemn prayer of consecration before the sacramental unction.
[^131]: Fol. 34v. The Holy Mystery of Chrismation (*Myropomazanie* / Greek: Χρῖσμα). The priest takes the consecrated Holy Chrism (*ст҃ымъ мѵ́ромъ*) and anoints the five bodily sensory centers in the form of the Cross: forehead (*чело̀*), eyes (*ѡ҆чеса̀*), breast (*пръ́си*), hands (*роу́цѣ*), and feet (*но́зѣ*), sealing each with the statutory biblical formula: "The seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit. Amen" (*Печа́ть да́ра ст҃го дх҃а, а҆ми́нь*; cf. 2 Corinthians 1:21–22; Ephesians 1:13; 4:30; Council of Laodicea, Canon 48).
[^132]: Fol. 34v. The Baptismal Circular Procession (*ѡ҆бхо́дѧтъ ѡ҆́крꙋгъ а҆нало́ѧ*). The priest, sponsor (*въспрїе́мникъ* / *кумъ*), and newly illuminated process three times around the analogion (or font) while chanting the ancient baptismal entrance hymn: *Є҆ли́ци въ Хрⷭ҇та̀ крⷭ҇ти́стесѧ, въ Хрⷭ҇та̀ ѡ҆блеко́стесѧ, а҆ллилоу́їа* (Galatians 3:27, sung thrice). The circular motion symbolizes the eternal, unending nature of the new covenant with Christ and participation in the joy of the heavenly choir.
[^133]: Fol. 34v. The Baptismal Prokeimenon in Tone 3 from Psalm 26:1 LXX (*Гдⷭ҇ь просвѣще́нїе моє̀ и҆ сп҃си́тель мо́й, кого́ сѧ оу҆бою̀...* / "The Lord is my illumination and my Savior; whom shall I fear?"). Illumination (*просвѣще́нїе*, Greek: φωτισμός) serves as the primary patristic term for Holy Baptism (cf. Justin Martyr, *First Apology* 61; Gregory of Nazianzus, *Oration 40*).
[^134]: Fol. 35r–35v. The Baptismal Epistle: Romans 6:3–11 (Pericope 91). The locus classicus of Pauline baptismal theology. St. Paul teaches that baptism is not a mere symbolic washing, but a mystical co-burial (*съпогребо́хомсѧ*) into the death of Christ and a resurrection unto newness of life, crucifying the "old man" and destroying the "body of sin" so that the believer is liberated from servitude to sin.
[^135]: Fol. 35v. Conclusion of the Epistle and the Liturgical Gospel Dialogue. The Pauline pericope culminates in the exhortation to reckon oneself dead unto sin, but alive unto God in Christ Jesus (Romans 6:11). The deacon then commands liturgical vigilance: *Премоу́дрость прѡ́сти оу҆слы́шимъ ст҃го є҆ѵⷢгліа* (Greek: Σοφία. Ὀρθοί. Ἀκούσωмен τοῦ ἁγίου εὐαγγελίου), answered by the announcement of the Holy Gospel according to Matthew.
[^136]: Fol. 35v–36r. The Great Commission Gospel: Matthew 28:16–20 (Pericope 116). The final verses of the Gospel of Matthew, proclaimed at every Byzantine baptism. The resurrected Lord, having received all power in heaven and earth, commissions the eleven apostles to go forth, make disciples of all nations, baptize them into the threefold Name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and keep His commandments, concluding with the perpetual promise: "And lo, I am with you always, even unto the end of the age."
[^137]: Fol. 36r. The Baptismal Augmented Litany (*є҆ктенїа̀*). In addition to the civil petitions for the sovereign and the hierarchy (the local bishop), the euchology introduces a unique personal petition for the sponsor/godparent (*ра́боу Бж҃їю [и҆́мѧ ре́къ въспрїе́мникоу]*), praying for mercy, life, peace, health, and remission of sins in recognition of the sponsor's grave spiritual sponsorship and covenantal pledge made at the font.
[^138]: Fol. 36v. The Petition for the Newly Illumined and the Baptismal Dismissal (*ѿпоу́стъ*). The litany concludes with a fervent supplication that the newly baptized child may be preserved in the "faith of a pure confession" (*въ вѣ́рѣ чи́стаго и҆сповѣда́нїа*) all the days of his life. The euchology then prescribes the proper baptismal dismissal formula commemorating Christ's baptism in the Jordan by John (*И҆́же въ І҆ѿрда́ни ѿ І҆ѡа́нна на́шего ра́ди сп҃се́нїа кр҃ти́тисѧ прїе́мый...*), which concludes on the succeeding folio (Fol. 37r / p91).
[^139]: Fol. 37r. Conclusion of the Baptismal Dismissal (*ѿпоу́стъ*). The text completes the liturgical formula begun on Fol. 36v: *ꙗ҆́ко бл҃гъ и҆ чл҃колꙕ́бецъ* ("...for He is good and the Lover of mankind"). In early modern Ruthenian euchologia, the dismissal seals the triple mystery of initiation (Baptism, Chrismation, and the subsequent Communion), acknowledging Christ's condescension to be baptized in the Jordan and invoking the intercession of the Theotokos, the precious Cross, and all the saints.
[^140]: Fol. 37r. The Great Transfer / Entrance (*вели́кїй перено́съ* / Greek: μεγάλη εἴσοδος) and Post-Baptismal Communion. The 1606 Striatyn Euchologion provides crucial historical evidence for the liturgical integration of Baptism within the Divine Liturgy. If the Liturgy is celebrated, the newly baptized infant is solemnly carried with tapers at the Great Transfer (*на вели́комъ перено́сѣ*), whereas an adult candidate walks in personal procession immediately preceding the Holy Gifts, holding a lighted taper in both hands (*дръжа̀ по свѣщѝ въ ѻ҆бо́ю рꙋкоу*). Both infant and adult immediately partake of the Holy Mysteries of Christ, preserving the undivided primitive order of Christian initiation. Furthermore, upon dismissal, the neophyte is escorted in joyful triumph back to his home bearing burning tapers.
[^141]: Fol. 37v. The Baptismal Octave of Illumination (*да́же до ѿсма́го днѐ*). In strict accordance with ancient Byzantine and Jerusalem practice (witnessed in the mystagogical catecheses of St. Cyril of Jerusalem and St. John Chrysostom), the newly illumined Christian wore the white baptismal robe (*chiton* / *kryzhmo*) and carried the lighted baptismal candle to church daily for seven consecutive days, attending Vespers, Matins, and the Divine Liturgy. Only on the eighth day (*ѿсмы́й дн҃ь*) was this octave brought to a liturgical close by the solemn ritual of Ablution and Hair-Tonsure.
