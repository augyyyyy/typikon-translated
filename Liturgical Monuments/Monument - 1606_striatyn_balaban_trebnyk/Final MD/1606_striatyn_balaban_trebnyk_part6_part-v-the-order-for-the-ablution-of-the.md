# PART V: THE ORDER FOR THE ABLUTION OF THE BAPTIZED ON THE EIGHTH DAY [^142]

*Again they bring him into the church, and the sponsor with him. And the priest looses the garments which are the papish from baptism, and the belt.[^143] And he says:*

> **Priest:** ""
Blessed is our God alway, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

> **Reader:** ""
Amen.
Heavenly King... Trisagion... Most Holy Trinity... After: Our Father...

> **Priest:** ""
For Thine is the kingdom...

*Then the troparion of the day, and of the saint that has occurred. Glory... both now... Theotokion.*

**Choir [or Reader]:**
Through the prayers, O Lord, of all the saints and of the Mother of God, give Thy peace unto us, and have mercy on us, as the only compassionate One.

*Then the priest says the prayers.*

> **Deacon:** ""
Let us pray to the Lord.

> **Priest:** ""
[Fol. 38v. Decorative woodcut initial I.]
O Thou Who hast granted remission of sins unto Thy servant through holy baptism, and hast bestowed upon him renewal of life:[^144] do Thou Thyself, O Master and Lord, be well pleased that the illumination of Thy countenance shine alway in his heart. Preserve the strength of his faith free from the snares of enemies; preserve spotless and unstained upon him the garment of incorruptibility wherewith he was clothed; keeping inviolate within him the spiritual seal by Thy grace; showing mercy both unto him and unto us, according to the multitude of Thy tender compassions.

*Ekphonesis:*

> **Priest:** ""
For blessed and glorified is Thy most honorable and majestic Name, of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

[Choir: Amen.]

*Second Prayer.*

> **Deacon:** ""
Let us pray to the Lord.

> **Priest:** ""
[Fol. 39r. Decorative woodcut initial V.]
O Master, Lord our God, Who through the font bestowest heavenly illumination upon them that are baptized; Who hast regenerated Thy newly-illumined servant by water and the Spirit,[^145] and hast granted unto him the remission of sins voluntary and involuntary: lay upon him Thy mighty hand, and preserve him by the power of Thy grace; preserve unstolen the pledge of the Spirit; and account him worthy of eternal life, and of pleasing Thee.

*Ekphonesis:*

> **Priest:** ""
For Thou art our sanctification, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

[Choir: Amen.]

> **Priest:** ""
Peace be unto all.

> **Deacon:** ""
Let us bow our heads unto the Lord.

*The priest says this prayer secretly:*

> **Priest:** ""
[Fol. 39v. Decorative woodcut initial O.]
He that has put on Thee, Christ our God, has bowed his head unto Thee with us; whom do Thou preserve, that he may remain an unconquered champion against them that vainly stir up enmity against him and against us;[^146] and by Thine incorruptible crown, show forth all victors even unto the end.

*Ekphonesis:*

> **Priest:** ""
For Thine it is to show mercy and to save us, O Christ our God, and unto Thee do we send up glory, with Thine unoriginate Father, and Thine All-holy, and good, and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

[Choir: Amen.]

*And he looses the belt from the child, and the garment which is the papish; and having removed them, he dips their ends into this pure water, and sprinkles the child, saying:*

> **Priest:** ""
Thou hast been justified; thou hast been enlightened; *and the rest.*

*And taking a new sponge saturated with water, he wipes his face and head, and breast, and the rest, saying:*

> **Priest:** ""
Thou hast been baptized; thou hast been enlightened; thou hast been anointed with myron; thou hast been sanctified; thou hast been washed: in the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^147]

*PRAYER at the Tonsure of the Hair.*

> **Deacon:** ""
Let us pray to the Lord.

> **Priest:** ""
[Fol. 40v. Decorative woodcut initial V.]
O Master, Lord our God, Who didst honor man with Thine image, composing him of a rational soul and a comely body, so that the body might serve the rational soul;[^148] for Thou hast placed the head in the highest position, and in it hast implanted the majority of the senses, not obstructing one another; and with hairs hast Thou covered the head, so that it may not be injured by changes of the winds; and all his members hast Thou planted fittingly, that for all things he might give thanks unto Thee, the excellent Craftsman: do Thou Thyself, O Master, a vessel of Thine election through Paul the Apostle having commanded us to do all things unto Thy glory: bless this Thy servant, *[Name]*, who has come to make a beginning of shearing the hairs of his head, together with his sponsor;[^149] and grant unto them alway to meditate upon Thy commandments, and to do the things well-pleasing unto Thee.

*Ekphonesis:*

> **Priest:** ""
For Thou art a merciful God and the Lover of mankind, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

[Choir: Amen.]

> **Priest:** ""
Peace be unto all.

> **Deacon:** ""
Let us bow our heads unto the Lord.

*The priest says this prayer secretly:*

> **Priest:** ""
[Fol. 41v. Decorative woodcut initial G.]
O Lord our God, Who through the fullness of the font hast sanctified by Thy grace them that believe in Thee: bless this present infant, and let Thy blessing descend upon his head. And even as Thou didst bless David the king through Samuel Thy prophet, so bless also the head of this Thy servant, *[Name]*, by the hand of me, a sinner, overshadowing him with Thy Holy Spirit; that advancing in stature and unto grey hairs of old age, he may send up glory unto Thee, and see the good things of Jerusalem all the days of his life.[^150]

*Ekphonesis:*

> **Priest:** ""
For unto Thee is due all glory, honor, and worship, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*And he shears him crosswise, saying:*

> **Priest:** ""
The servant of God, *[Name]*, is shorn: in the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^151]

*After this, he places upon him the koukoulion, that is to say, a small cap [klobuchets], saying:*[^152]

> **Priest:** ""
The servant of God, *[Name]*, is clothed in the koukoulion of innocence: in the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*Then the litany, in which he commemorates the king, and the sponsor with the newly-illumined one:*[^153]

**Deacon [or Priest]:**
Have mercy on us, O God, according to Thy great mercy, we pray Thee, give ear and have mercy.
Again we pray for our pious king, *[Name]*.
Again we pray for mercy and life of the servant of God, *[Name]*, the sponsor, and of the newly-baptized one.

*Ekphonesis:*

> **Priest:** ""
For Thou art a merciful God and the Lover of mankind, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

[Choir: Amen.]

*And the dismissal takes place according to custom.*[^154]

[Fol. 43r. Headpiece: Monumental Renaissance woodcut engraving depicting two symmetrical vases of carnations and rolling acanthus strapwork.]

### Notes

[^142]: Fol. 38r. Part V: The Order for the Ablution of the Baptized on the Eighth Day (*ПОСЛѢДОВÁНЇЄ ВЪ Є҆́ЖЕ ѼМЫ́ТИ КР҃ТИ́ВШАГО СѦ ВЪ Ѽ́СМЫЙ ДН҃Ь* / Greek: Εὐχαὶ τῆς Ἀπολούσεως μεθ' ἡμέρας ὀγδόην). Folio 38r opens with a monumental full-width woodcut headpiece characteristic of the Striatyn printing house (depicting two classical floral urns flanked by rolling acanthus scrolls), introducing the distinct liturgical office for releasing the candidate from the baptismal garments and washing away the physical chrism so as to guard the holy mystery from profane contamination.
[^143]: Fol. 38r. The Loosing of the Baptismal Garments and the Ruthenian Term *па́пишъ* (*раздрѣша́етъ ꙗ҆́же па́пишъ ѿ кр҃ще́нїа ѻ҆дѣва́ла , и҆ по́ѧсъ*). The rubric specifies that the priest unlooses the belt and the baptismal garments designated as *па́пишъ* (repeated on Fol. 40r: *ѻ҆дѣва́ло и҆́же па́пишъ*). In western Ukrainian and Galician-Ruthenian historical dialect, *папуш* / *папиш* designates a swaddled bundle, wrap, or linen infant binder (cognate with Romanian *păpușă* / Latin *pupa*, and related to Galician *папуша* for a wrapped sheaf or swaddling bundle). Here it serves as a vernacular technical term for the white swaddling cloth or baptismal wrapper (*savanon* / *kryzhmo*) wrapped securely around the infant over the white tunic.
[^144]: Fol. 38v. The First Euchological Prayer of Ablution (*И҆́же и҆збавле́нїе съгрѣше́нїемъ ра́ди ст҃го кр҃ще́нїа...* / Greek: Ὁ τὴν ἄφεσιν τῶν ἁμαρτιῶν διὰ τοῦ ἁγίου βαπτίσματος...). The prayer addresses God as the Author of spiritual regeneration, petitioning that the divine illumination of His countenance may shine perpetually in the candidate's heart, that the "shield of faith" may be guarded from demonic ambush, and that the "garment of incorruptibility" (*нетлѣ́нїе ѻ҆дѣѧ́нїа*) and "spiritual seal" (*дх҃овнаѧ печа́ть*) received in Chrismation may remain spotless, inviolate, and unassailable.
[^145]: Fol. 39r. The Second Prayer of Ablution: Heavenly Illumination and the Unstolen Pledge (*Влⷭ҇ко Гн҃и Бг҃е на́шь , и҆́же ра́ди коу́пели нб҃наго ѻ҆сїѧ́нїа...* / Greek: Δέσποτα Κύριε ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν, ὁ διὰ τῆς κολυμβήθρας...). The euchology commemorates the font as the conduit of heavenly illumination (*нб҃ное ѻ҆сїѧ́нїе* / οὐράνιος ἔλλαμψις) and invokes the divine hand of protection over the child to keep the "pledge of the Spirit" (*ѻ҆броуче́нїе дх҃а*, Greek: ἀρραβὼν τοῦ Πνεύματος; cf. 2 Corinthians 1:22, 5:5; Ephesians 1:14) "unstolen" (*некра́домо* / ἄσυλον) by adversary powers.
[^146]: Fol. 39v. The Prayer of Bowing of Heads: The Neophyte as an Unconquered Champion (*Ѻ҆дѣ́выисѧ въ тѧ̀ Хрⷭ҇та̀ и҆ Бг҃а на́шего...* / Greek: Ὁ ἐνδυσάμενος σὲ τὸν Χριστὸν...). Reflecting the Pauline imagery of Galatians 3:27 ("as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ"), the prayer portrays the baptized person as an athlete or champion (*подви́жникъ* / Greek: ἀθλητής) entering the spiritual arena against invisible foes, beseeching the Lord to grant him final victory and the crown of incorruption.
[^147]: Fol. 40r. The Ritual Ablution and Sacramental Formula (*Кр҃ти́лсѧ є҆сѝ . просвѣти́лсѧ є҆си . мѵ́ромъ пома́залсѧ є҆сѝ...*). The rubric directs the priest to unbind the belt and the baptismal swaddling cloth (*па́пишъ*), dip their extremities into the water, sprinkle the child with the declaration "Thou hast been justified, thou hast been enlightened" (1 Corinthians 6:11), and then wipe the anointed sensory centers (head, face, breast) with a fresh sponge saturated with water. The formula explicitly enumerates the stages of initiation: baptism (*кр҃ти́лсѧ є҆си*), illumination (*просвѣти́лсѧ є҆си*), chrismation (*мѵ́ромъ пома́залсѧ є҆си*), sanctification (*ѡ҆ст҃и́лсѧ є҆си*), and washing (*ѡ҆мы́лсѧ є҆си*).
[^148]: Fol. 40v. The Mystery of the Tonsure of Hair: The Theological Anthropology of the Craftsman (*МЛ҃ТВА на постриже́нїе вла́сѡвъ* / Greek: Εὐχὴ ἐπὶ κουρᾷ τριχῶν). This profound euchological prayer develops a classical Patristic theology of the human organism (drawing upon Gregory of Nyssa and Nemesius of Emesa): God is hailed as the "excellent Craftsman" (*до́брый хꙋдо́жникъ* / ἄριστος τεχνίτης) Who endowed man with a rational soul (*дꙋша́ слове́снаѧ*) and an harmonious physical body, setting the head as a citadel upon the heights, adorning it with unconfused sensory organs, and covering it with hair as a shield against meteorological tempests.
[^149]: Fol. 41r. The Apostolic Mandate and the Dedication of the First-Fruits (*сътвори́ти нача́токъ стри́щи вла́сы*). The prayer cites the apostolic rule of St. Paul—the "chosen vessel" (Acts 9:15)—to do all things unto the glory of God (1 Corinthians 10:31). The clipping of the hair is offered not as an aesthetic trimming, but as a sacrificial first-fruits (*aparchē* / *нача́токъ*) of the candidate's body offered unto Christ in thanksgiving for his illumination.
[^150]: Fol. 41v. The Royal Anointing of David and the Blessing of Old Age (Psalm 127:5 LXX). The concluding prayer of tonsure typologically links the blessing of the candidate's head with the biblical anointing of King David by the Prophet Samuel at Bethlehem (1 Samuel / 1 Kings 16:13). The priest prays that the newly enlightened Christian may advance in age from infancy unto venerable grey hairs (*сѣди́ны ста́рости* / πολιὰ γήρως), beholding the prosperity of the mystical Jerusalem all the days of his life (Psalm 127:5 LXX; Masoretic Psalm 128:5).
[^151]: Fol. 42r. The Post-Baptismal Hair Tonsure Formula (*Пострига́етсѧ ра́бъ бж҃їи...* / Greek: Κείρεται ὁ δοῦλος τοῦ Θεοῦ...). The priest clips the hair of the neophyte crosswise (*пострига́етъ є҆го̀ крⷭ҇тоѻбра́зно*), beginning on the crown of the head, then the back, and the right and left sides, dedicating the first-fruits of bodily growth unto God. The active indicative passive formula ("The servant of God is shorn...") seals the completion of the mystery of illumination, expressing the yoke of Christ and royal servitude under the divine King.
[^152]: Fol. 42r. The Capping with the *Koukoulion* or *Klobuchets* (*посе́мъ возлага́етъ нань коу́коль, ре́кше клобоу́чецъ гл҃ѧ: Ѻ҆дѣва́етсѧ ра́бъ Бж҃їи... въ коу́коль незло́бїа* / Greek: κουκούλλιον τῆς ἀκακίας). The 1606 Striatyn Euchologion witnesses the ancient post-baptismal investiture with the baptismal cowl or white cap (*klobuchets*), designated the "koukoulion of innocence" (or guilelessness / harmlessness; cf. 1 Corinthians 14:20: "in malice be ye children"). In early Byzantine and Old Slavonic ritual tradition (preserved in early manuscripts such as Euchologion Sinait. gr. 959 and Barberini gr. 336), this white headpiece remained upon the child's head throughout the seven days of the baptismal octave to protect the consecrated chrism upon the crown and brow until the eighth-day ablution.
[^153]: Fol. 42r. The Post-Baptismal Litany and Royal Commemoration (*є҆кте́нїа, вне́йже помина́етъ цр҃ѧ, и҆ въспрїе́мника съ новопросвѣще́нны[мъ]*). The litany following the tonsure and capping includes an explicit petition for the reigning sovereign: *Є҆ще мо́лимсѧ ѡ҆ бл҃гочтивомъ цр҃и нашемъ* ("Again we pray for our pious king..."). In the historical context of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth under King Sigismund III Vasa (reigned 1587–1632), the Ruthenian Orthodox Church preserved the traditional euchological title *цр҃ь* / *коро́ль* alongside supplications for the sponsor (*въспрїе́мникъ*) and the newly illumined child.
[^154]: Fol. 42v. Conclusion of the Eighth-Day Ablution Office and the Customary Dismissal (*И҆ быва́етъ по ѻ҆бы́чаю ѿпоу́стъ*). Following the ekphonesis of the fervent litany, the priest imparts the canonical liturgical dismissal (*apolysis*), formally concluding the eighth-day octave rites of ablution and tonsure.
