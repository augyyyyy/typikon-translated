# PART XX: CANONICAL CHAPTERS OF PATRIARCH THEODORE CONCERNING BURIAL IN CHURCHES [^1406]

*The Funeral Order. From the Answers of the Most Holy Patriarch, the Lord Theodore. Question:*

According to the ancient custom of that place, the remains of Orthodox [Christians] are buried in city churches. I seek therefore to be taught whether this is sinless.

*Answer:*

There is a great difference between churches: those that are consecrated, receiving dedication and the erection of an altar table, by anointing with holy chrism and the deposition of holy relics of martyrs; and those that are not thus consecrated, but hold the rank of a house of prayer.[^1407] Therefore in the former in which the holy relics of martyrs are visibly treasured up, and which have received in their midst anointing with holy chrism: let human remains of whatever kind by no means be buried, according to the second chapter of the first title of the fifth book of ecclesiastical [laws], which says: "Let no one bury a dead body in church." And according to the civil ancient law, which says: "It is unlawful to bury anyone in church, unless visibly a martyr's body is to lie there." But in those that are not so consecrated, being designated holy houses of prayer, remains may be buried without harm.[^1408]

### Notes

[^1406]: Folio 228r (physical page 473) opens with a large decorative engraved woodcut headpiece introducing the canonical ruling of Theodore Balsamon, Greek Patriarch of Antioch (c. 1140–1204), one of the foremost Byzantine canonists. His responsa were incorporated into the Slavic *Kormchaia Kniga* (Nomocanon) and included in 16th-17th century Ruthenian Trebnyks as authoritative guidance on burial practice.
[^1407]: The canonist distinguishes between consecrated churches (ѻсщенныя цѣркви) possessing a consecrated altar table, consecrated with holy chrism and holy martyr relics, and unconsecrated oratories or prayer houses (молитвеныя храми).
[^1408]: Folio 228v (physical page 474). Canonical and legal citations: the "second chapter of the first title of the fifth book of ecclesiastical laws" refers to the Byzantine Nomocanon in 14 Titles / Basilika (Title I, ch. 2), reaffirming the strict prohibition against intramural burials in consecrated temple space. The "civil ancient law" refers to Roman-Byzantine civil legislation (Codex Justinianus and Novel 133; reinforced by Leo VI Novel 53), which banned lay burials within cities and inside sacred shrines, reserving holy church burial exclusively for martyrs and emperors. Simple prayer houses, lacking consecrated altars, were exempted.
