# PART XIX: THE ORDER PERFORMED AT THE SEPARATION OF THE SOUL FROM THE BODY [^1340]

*When anyone of [Christians begins grievously] to fail [in health], the priest-confessor questions him in a quiet voice: if any deed, or word, by reason of forgetfulness, or any malice toward anyone is unconfessed or unforgiven, the confessor is obliged to investigate and inquire into all things one by one from the dying person, as one who will give an account to the Master Christ.[^1341] And after that, he makes the beginning.*

*The Priest says:* **"Blessed is our God..."** *Then: the Trisagion; All-Holy Trinity; after "Our Father": "For Thine is the kingdom..." Lord, have mercy (12 times). Come, let us worship (thrice). And Psalm 50: "Have mercy on me, O God, according to Thy great mercy..."[^1342] Then the Canon on the Separation of the Soul from the Body, the composition of Lord Andrew of Crete:[^1343]*

*Tone 6. Ode 1. Heirmos:*[^1344]
*As Israel walked on foot in the deep as on dry land, and saw the pursuer Pharaoh drowning, he cried: Let us sing unto God a song of victory!*

*Troparion:*
Come, gather together, all ye who have lived piously in life; weep for a soul estranged from the glory of God, and having served the shameful demons with all diligence.

*Troparion:*
Now indeed the time of all my life has passed away like smoke; and there have come upon me the rest of the angels sent from God, seeking my wretched soul without mercy.

*Troparion:*
Behold, there stands before me a multitude of evil spirits, holding the records of my sins; and they cry out vehemently, insolently seeking my humble soul.

*Theotokion:*
Unto whom else shall I cry out? Who will receive my weeping from affliction and the sighing of my heart, save only thou, O all-pure one, the hope of Christians, and [of all] sinners?[^1345]

*A second supplicatory canon to the all-holy Theotokos, from the person of a man struggling in the agony of death and unable to speak, the acrostic of which is: Deliver me, O Pure One, from the tyranny of demons. By Joseph. Tone 6:*[^1346]

*Ode 1. Heirmos:*[^1347]
*As Israel walked on foot in the deep as on dry land, and saw the pursuer Pharaoh drowning, he cried: Let us sing unto God a song of victory!*

*Troparion:*
Like drops of rain, my evil and few days, diminishing little by little through the yearly cycle, are already disappearing; O Lady, save me!

*Troparion:*
By thy tender-heartedness and by thy manifold compassions, O Lady, being naturally inclined to mercy: in this dreadful hour, stand before me, an invincible Helper!

*Troparion:*
Great fear now holds fast my soul; an ineffable trembling and affliction is upon her, as she is about to go forth from the body, O all-pure one; do thou comfort her!

*Glory...*

*Troparion:*
O certain Refuge for the sinful and the humble, make known unto me thy mercy, O pure one, and deliver me from the hands of the demons; for like many dogs they have surrounded me.

*Both now...*

*Theotokion:*
Behold, the time for help; behold, the time of thy protection; behold, O Lady, the time concerning which day and night I fervently fell down and prayed unto thee!

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*[^1348]
*There is none holy as Thou, O Lord my God, Who hast exalted the horn of Thy faithful, O Good One, and hast established us upon the rock of Thy confession.*

*Troparion:*
O my good friends and acquaintances, why do ye not weep? Why do ye not lament for him who was once beloved, a friend and a brother, but is now a stranger to God and to you all?

*Troparion:*
O my beloved brethren, beholding my deeds weighed in the balance of the scales of judgment,[^1349] pray Christ, the God of all, that He be merciful unto me!

*Troparion:*
Calling to mind, therefore, O beloved ones, my living together with you, pray Christ that He look down upon me, unhappily deprived of life and tormented!

*Glory...*

*Theotokion:*
Incline thine ear unto me, O Mother of my God, from the height of thy manifold glory, O good one; give ear unto my last sigh, and stretch forth thy hand unto me!

*Another heirmos. Then:*[^1350]

*Troparion:*
Seeing this day from afar, O Lady, and continually pondering it as having arrived, with warm tears I prayed unto thee not to forget me.

*Troparion:*
The roaring noetic lion cubs have encircled me, and seek to snatch away and bitterly tear me to pieces; do thou break their teeth and jaws, O pure one, and save me!

*Troparion:*
Since the vocal organ is now utterly extinguished, and the tongue is bound, and the voice is closed up,[^1351] in contrition of heart I pray thee, O my Savior-Protectress, save me!

*Glory...*

*Troparion:*
In iniquities was I conceived; why was I born? And I have provoked the good God to anger; I go away to be tormented in the eternal fire.

*Both now...*

*Theotokion:*
Turn not away from me thy manifold compassions; close not thy heart of love for mankind, O pure one; but stand before me now, and in the hour of judgment have mercy on me!

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*[^1352]
*Christ is my power, God and Lord, the venerable Church sings with godly dignity, crying out with a pure mind, keeping festival in the Lord.*

*Troparion:*
Woe is me, the prodigal! Alas for me, the wretched! For I stretch out my hands unto my friends, and I shed tears from my eyes, and no one helps me.

*Troparion:*
An all-destructive struggle has come before my soul; I raise my eyes unto God's radiant angels, crying: Leave me to live a little while! But no one listens.

*Troparion:*
Lament for me, weep for me, ye angelic hosts, and all ye earthly lovers of Christ; for without mercy is my soul parted from the body.[^1353]

*Glory...*

*Theotokion:*
O Lady, Lady, help now my bewildered soul, looking unto thy protection alone; despise me not, therefore, O good one, that I should be delivered up unto the enemies!

*Heirmos; then:*[^1354]

*Troparion:*
O clouds, drop down dew as tears; O mountains and hills, weep piteously over me, who have departed far from God and drawn near unto the demons.

*Troparion:*
O unashamed, unerring refuge unto all that are in distress: O all-spotless Sovereign Lady, be thou a refuge unto me in the hour of trial.[^1355]

*Troparion:*
Having stretched forth thy most pure and all-venerable hands like the consecrated wings of a dove: beneath their shelter and shadow protect me, O Sovereign Lady.[^1356]

*Glory...*

*Troparion:*
The aerial prince, the violent tormentor, the dread toll-gatherer of the pathways, and the ruthless inquisitor of words: vouchsafe that I may pass by him unhindered,[^1357] as I depart from the earth.

*Both now...*

*Theotokion:*
Behold, O Sovereign Lady, fear hath met me, which also I did dread; behold, a great struggle hath encompassed me, wherein be thou my helper, O hope of my salvation.[^1358]

*Ode 5. Heirmos: By Thy divine light, O Good One...*

*Troparion:*
O sensible light, shine upon me, that I may behold Thee if but for a little while, O Christ; for never again shall I behold Thee: for a cloud of demons hath suddenly fallen upon me, and the darkness of my shameful deeds covers me.[^1359]

*Troparion:*
O all ye beloved of the Savior, why have ye shown yourselves unmerciful toward me in my wretchedness and all-afflicted state? For if ye had but uttered a single voice unto the common Sovereign Lady, ye would have delivered me from darkness.[^1360]

*Troparion:*
Farewell, O great prince of God Michael, chief of the angels! For no longer shall I invoke thy holy name to aid me; for my lips, my tongue, and mine eyes are struck silent.[^1361]

*Theotokion:*
Farewell, thou also, O undefiled and pure Mother of my Christ! For no longer shall I behold thine all-spotless and pure holy countenance; for the light in me hath gone out, and night hath covered me.[^1362]

*Heirmos; then:*

*Troparion:*
Forget me not, O good lady, nor turn away thy face from me, thy servant; but hear me, for I am afflicted, and give heed unto my soul, and deliver it.[^1363]

*Troparion:*
O my kinsmen according to the flesh, and brethren according to the spirit, familiar friends and acquaintances: weep, sigh, lament! For behold, I am now parted from you.[^1364]

*Troparion:*
There is none that delivers in any wise, and in truth no one that helps; do thou aid me, O Sovereign Lady, lest like a helpless man I be shut up in the hands of mine enemies.[^1365]

*Glory...*

*Troparion:*
O how shall I endure that separation from thee, O Mother of God, which is truly mournful and grievous; and the bitter falling away from my Savior, and the estrangement from all the saints?[^1366]

*Both now...*

*Theotokion:*
May the multitude of my sins not prevail over thine abundant tender mercy, O Sovereign Lady; but may thy mercy encompass me, and may it cover all mine iniquities.[^1367]

*Ode 6. Heirmos: The sea of life...*

*Troparion:*
Incline your ear unto me, O brethren, and attentively hear the brief utterance of a contrite voice; do not disobey, and ye shall surely receive a reward from God.[^1368]

*Troparion:*
Behold, now the soul is violently parted from the wretched body; bury not the body in the earth, for it is not worthy: but cast it out and throw it to the dogs![^1369]

*Troparion:*
For what profit is it unto me, while my soul is dragged into dreadful torments, that my body should be sung over by you? Leave it unburied, so that the dogs may devour my heart.[^1370]

*Theotokion:*
Ye strangers passing by, as ye behold the bones dragged by the dogs, take pity and cry from the soul: "Help, O Sovereign Lady, the soul of this passible body!"[^1371]

*Heirmos; then:*

*Troparion:*
My lips are silent, and my tongue speaks not, but my heart utters its voice: for the fire of contrition, devouring it, burns within, and with unuttered cries calls upon thee, O Virgin.[^1372]

*Troparion:*
Look upon me from on high, O Mother of God, and mercifully give heed now to descend for my visitation, that having seen thee, I may depart from the body rejoicing.[^1373]

*Troparion:*
The bond is severed, the laws of natural cohesion and of all bodily composition are torn asunder; they bring upon me unbearable distress and anguish.[^1374]

*Glory...*

*Troparion:*
Commit me, O Sovereign Lady, into the holy and venerable hands of the holy angels, so that sheltered by their wings, I may not behold the dishonorable, gloomy, and foul form of the demons.[^1375]

*Both now...*

*Theotokion:*
O all-venerable Bridal Chamber of God, vouchsafe me the heavenly noetic bridal chamber, having kindled my extinguished and unilluminated lamp with the holy oil of thy mercy.[^1376]

*Heirmos:*
Beholding the sea of life surging with the tempest of temptations, and fleeing to Thy tranquil haven, I cry unto Thee: Lead up my life from corruption, O Most Merciful One![^1377]

*Then the Little Litany, and the exclamation.*

*Kontakion, Tone 6:*
O my soul, my soul, arise! Why sleepest thou? The end is at hand, and thou shalt be confounded; arise therefore, that Christ God may spare thee, Who is everywhere present and fills all things.[^1378]

*Ikos:*
Seeing the medicine of Christ opened, and healing pouring forth therefrom unto Adam, the devil suffered and was wounded; lamenting as one receiving disaster, he cried aloud unto his companions: "What shall I do unto the Son of Mary? He slays me, the Bethlehemite, Who is everywhere present and fills all things!"[^1379]

*Ode 7. Heirmos: The furnace moist with dew...*

*Troparion:*
Behold, having dragged it forth, they have fiercely torn my wretched soul from the body, and lead it away unto the dread Judge; remembering therefore my life together with you, O beloved, make memory of me.[^1380]

*Troparion:*
Have mercy on me, O most radiant angels of God the Almighty, and deliver me from all the wicked toll-houses; for I have no good deeds to set against the balance of evil actions.[^1381]

*Troparion:*
When ye ascend, O my holy angels, to stand before the judgment seat of Christ, bending your knees, cry out unto Him with weeping: "Have mercy, O Creator of all, on the work of Thy most pure hands, and cast it not away from Thyself!"[^1382]

*Theotokion:*
Bowing down before the most pure Sovereign Lady, beseech the Mother of our God, that She may bend Her knees together with you and incline God unto mercy; for being His Mother and Nourisher, She shall be heard.[^1383]

*Heirmos; then:*

*Troparion:*
The night of death hath overtaken me unprepared, dark and moonless, sending me forth unready upon that long and dreadful journey; may thy mercy accompany me, O Sovereign Lady![^1384]

*Troparion:*
Behold, all my days have vanished, truly in vanity, as it is written, and my years with haste; and the bitter snares of death have overtaken my soul, which hold me fast.[^1385]

*Troparion:*
For fleeting temporal enjoyment, I am about to be tormented endlessly, O Sovereign Lady, and for a little carnal pleasure, to fall into the fire; but may thy mercy overtake me![^1386]

*Glory...*

*Troparion:*
Surrounding me from every side they advance, holding me fast everywhere; and my soul shrinks back and is terrified, filled with great turmoil; comfort it, O Pure One, by thine appearing!

*Both now...*

*Theotokion:*
I have found no one who sorrows with me in my affliction,[^1387] nor any who comforts me, O Sovereign Lady; for my friends and acquaintances have together forsaken me now; but, O my hope, in no wise do thou forsake me!

*Ode 8. Heirmos: Out of the flame for the godly...*[^1388]

*Troparion:*
Sigh over me in my suffering, all ye ends of the earth, and weep together; for from on high the sentence hath come: having bound my hands and feet, to cast me out![^1389]

*Troparion:*
Having erred beyond all men, unto thee, O guardian of my wretched soul, do I cry out, sighing bitterly: behold the eternal affliction of my bonds, and cease not to pray for me!

*Troparion:*
Why did I come forth from the womb of my mother, being born unto passions?[^1390] How did I not perish immediately, being destined to be tormented in the eternal fire? Alas! Who will weep for me, or who will remember me?

*Troparion:*
Since I was delivered unto demons, and was brought down by force unto the bottom of hades, I know that all have forgotten me; but do thou remember me, O angel of the Lord!

*Theotokion:*
O Mother of my Christ, how hast thou forgotten me? How hast thou not inclined unto mercy Him Who poured forth blood from His ribs, that He might save those whom He created?[^1391] Alas! How hath the mercy of my Christ been shut up?

*Heirmos; then:*

*Troparion:*
As the philanthropic Mother of God the Lover of mankind, look with a gentle and merciful eye when my soul departs from the body, that I may glorify thee, O holy Theotokos!

*Troparion:*
Vouchsafe me to flee the regiments of the bodiless barbarians, and to soar above the aerial chasms, and to ascend unto heaven, that I may glorify thee, O holy Theotokos!

*Troparion:*
O thou who didst bear the Lord Almighty, drive far away from me the bitter chief toll-gatherers of the ruler of this world, when I am about to pass away, that I may glorify thee, O holy Theotokos![^1392]

*Troparion:*
When the great, last trumpet sounds terribly and dreadfully, and the sinners arise unto the resurrection of judgment, remember me then, O holy Theotokos!

*Glory Troparion:*
Before all creation, both visible and invisible, when I stand before Christ trembling, naked, and shaken with fear: remember me then, O holy Theotokos!

*Both now...*

*Theotokion:*
O exalted chamber of Christ the Master, having sent down thy grace from on high, overtake me now in the day of distress, that I may glorify thee, O holy Theotokos!

*Ode 9. Heirmos: It is not possible for men to see God...*[^1393]

*Troparion:*
No longer shall I remember, for in hades there is no remembering the Lord; but sitting covered in grievous darkness, awaiting the resurrection of all earthly beings, that being condemned I may be cast into the fire.

*Troparion:*
No one any longer will remember me, neither the multitude of angels, nor the assembly of archangels, nor the choir of prophets and apostles, nor the multitude of martyrs, and of all the righteous; for alone shall I draw out the punishment for the evil deeds which I did while living.[^1394]

*Troparion:*
Weep over me, lament for me now, O bottom of hades, and abyss, darkness, and Tartarus! And groan over me, all ye torments! For I alone, a prodigal from among Christians, have been delivered unto you to be tormented bitterly!

*Theotokion:*
O Mother of Christ, look into the abyss; behold the soul delivered unto torments to be tortured bitterly; and bending thy knees, shed tears, that He may be inclined by thy prayers, Who gave His blood for me, and may lead me up!

*Heirmos; then:*

*Troparion:*
Behold, the bitter sentence hath gone forth; behold, the barren fig tree, as unprofitable, is cut down; behold, the withered branch is cast into the fire; now the unprofitable servant is sent away into outer darkness, bound hand and foot piteously![^1395]

*Troparion:*
O how shall I gaze upon the Invisible One? How shall I endure that dreadful vision? How shall I dare to open mine eyes? How shall I venture to look upon my Master, Whom from my childhood I have not ceased constantly to provoke to wrath?

*Troparion:*
O holy Maiden, Mother of God, look mercifully upon my lowliness; receiving this my piteous and last supplication offered unto thee with closed lips, and having received it, mercifully fulfill it!

*Troparion:*
Having defiled the holy temples, leaving behind the defiled temple of the body, I pray thee, the all-holy temple of God, O Maiden Virgin Mother: that my soul may escape the outer darkness and the fierce burning of Gehenna![^1396]

*Troparion:*
Grant the streams of my tears to be for the cleansing of my sins, O Good One, receiving the contrition of my heart as I trust in thee, the Hope of the good: that thou mayest deliver me without cost from dreadful torment, as the source of goodness itself, O Mother of God!

*Glory Troparion:*
Beholding the end of my life draw near, and contemplating how my soul has been the worker of unseemly thoughts and deeds, O all-pure one, I am fiercely wounded by the arrows of conscience; but being mercifully inclined, be thou my protectress!

*Both now...*

*Theotokion:*
A Son was given unto us for the sake of mercy, the Son of God and eternal King of angels, having come forth as man from thy pure blood; make Him merciful, O Maiden, unto my wretched soul fiercely torn away from my wretched body![^1397]

*Prayer said at the departure of the soul:*[^1398] **"Let us pray to the Lord."**

O Master, Lord Almighty, Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, Who desirest all men to be saved and to come unto the knowledge of the truth,[^1399] Who desirest not the death of a sinner, but rather that he should turn and live:[^1400] we pray and make supplication unto Thee, loose the soul of Thy servant [Name][^1401] from every bond, and deliver him from every curse. Forgive him the transgressions which are from his youth, known and unknown, in deed and in word, both those purely confessed, and those through forgetfulness, or through shame concealed; for Thou alone art He that looses those that are bound, and sets upright those that are crushed; the Hope of the hopeless,[^1402] Who art able to remit the sins of every man that hath hope in Thee. Yea, O Lord, Lover of mankind, command that he be released from the bonds of the flesh and of sins; and receive in peace the soul of this Thy servant [Name],[^1403] and give him rest in the eternal mansions with Thy saints: through the grace of Thine Only-begotten Son, our Lord and God and Savior Jesus Christ, with Whom Thou art blessed, together with Thy most holy and good and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*Prayer at the confession of a brother, which is given by the spiritual father to one who is about to die in the answer of faith:*[^1404]

The Lord Who became man for our sake and bears the sins of the whole world: may He, by His goodness and all-merciful grace, receive the confession of all that he hath uttered before Him unto my unworthiness; may He forgive thee all things, both in the present age and in that which is to come; and may He vouchsafe to present thee uncondemned before His judgment seat: Who desires life and grants salvation unto all; Who is blessed unto ages of ages. Amen.[^1405]

### Notes

[^1340]: Fol. 222r (`ск҃в`). Woodcut headpiece across the top of the folio. The title in ornamental display cinnabar (*ЧИ́НЪ БЫВА́ЕМЫЙ НА РАЗЛꙋЧЕ́НЇЕ, ДꙋШЍ ѿ ТѢ́ЛА*) initiates the classical Byzantine-Ruthenian office for the agony of death (*Ἀκολουθία εἰς ψυχορраγοῦντα*), chanted at the bedside of the dying.
[^1341]: Fol. 222r (`ск҃в`). A historical paper slip repair was pasted over lines 4–6 of the folio, reinforcing paper thinning caused by abraded ink on the verso (fol. 222v). The obscured characters are restored according to Ruthenian Trebnyk and classical Euchologion usage: `Е҆гда̀ кто̀ ѿ [хр҃тїа́нъ...] [...] и҆ прѣ́кѡ начне́тъ и҆знемаг[а́ти ... и҆ сщ҃енникъ] дх҃о́вникъ [въпра́]ша́етъ є҆го̀...` The rubric instructs the father-confessor to inquire gently into any lingering forgotten sin, unconfessed fault, or unforgiven grudge before beginning the office.
[^1342]: Fol. 222v. Psalm 50 (LXX 50; Masoretic Ps 51). The introductory liturgical framework consists of the standard Trisagion prayers, Lord have mercy (12 times), the triple invitation to worship, and the 50th Psalm.
[^1343]: Fol. 222v. Cinnabar rubric attributing the canon to "Lord Andrew of Crete" (*творе́нїе кѵ́ръ а҆ндре́а крїтскаго*, c. 660–740).
[^1344]: Fol. 222v. Tone 6, Heirmos of Ode 1: *Ὡς ἐν ἠπείρῳ πεζεύσας ὁ Ἰσραήλ* (Slavonic: *Ꙗ́ко по сꙋхоу ходи́въ їи́ль*), referring to the crossing of the Red Sea (Exodus 14).
[^1345]: Fol. 223r (`ск҃г`). The reading `въ[сѣ́]мъ грѣ́шникѡмъ` ("to all sinners") is abraded on the page, with only the initial `въ` and final `мъ` remaining legible; restored from parallel Ruthenian and Byzantine witnesses.
[^1346]: Fol. 223r (`ск҃г`). Rubric introducing the Second Canon to the Theotokos: *Дроугіи ка́нѡнъ молб҃енъ пр҃тѣ́й Бц҃и, ѿ лица̀ чл҃кꙋ дш҃ю раздѣлѧ́ющꙋ, и҆ немогꙋ́щꙋ гла́ти...* The acrostic in the Slavonic witness is given as *И҆зба́ви мѧ̀ чт҃аа бѣсѡ́въ моучи́телства* (Greek: *Ῥῦσαί με, σεμνὴ, δαιμόνων τυραννίδος*), followed by the author's name *І҆ѡ́сифъ* (Joseph the Hymnographer, c. 816–886). In the 1606 Striatyn imprint, the two canons are chanted in conjoined succession ode by ode.
[^1347]: Fol. 223v. Tone 6, Ode 1 Heirmos for the Second Canon (*Ꙗ҆́ко по сꙋхꙋ ходи̂ и҆и́ль*).
[^1348]: Fol. 224r (`ск҃д`). Tone 6, Heirmos of Ode 3 (Canon 1): *Οὐκ ἔστιν ἅγιος ὡς σύ, Κύριε ὁ Θεός μου* (Slavonic: *Нѣ́сть ст҃ъ ꙗ҆́коже ты Гд҃и Бг҃е*), based on the Canticle of Hannah (1 Kings / 1 Sam 2:2).
[^1349]: Fol. 224r (`ск҃д`). Slavonic *на стави́лѣ мѣри́лнѣмъ* translates Greek *ἐν ζυγῷ σταθμῶν* ("in the balance of the scales"), evoking the aerial examination and weighing of deeds at the separation of soul and body.
[^1350]: Fol. 224v. Cinnabar rubric *И҆́нъ і҆рмо́съ · та́же* indicates the transition to the troparia of Ode 3 of the Second Canon. The image of the "roaring noetic lion cubs" (*мы́сленїи рыка́юще скѵ́мни*) reflects Psalm 16:12 (LXX) and patristic demonology regarding the predatory spirits awaiting the dying soul.
[^1351]: Fol. 225r (`ск҃е`). Slavonic *Оу҆га́сшоу оу҆́бо ѿню́дъ ѻ҆рга́ноу слове́сномꙋ* (Greek: *Ἀποσβεσθέντος ἄρδην τοῦ ὀργάνου τοῦ λογικοῦ*), describing the physical deathbed cessation of speech while the dying person's inner heart continues to cry out to the Mother of God.
[^1352]: Fol. 225r (`ск҃е`). Tone 6, Heirmos of Ode 4 (Canon 1): *Χριστός μου δύнаμις, Θεὸς καὶ Κύριος* (Slavonic: *Хс҃ мнѣ̀ си́ла Бг҃ъ и҆ Гд҃ь*), based on the Canticle of Habakkuk (Habakkuk 3).
[^1353]: Fol. 225v. The troparion expresses the dramatic terror of the deathbed struggle (*по́двигъ всепа́гꙋбный*) where the soul implores both the celestial angelic orders and earthly friends for mercy and tears as it is unmercifully separated from the flesh.
[^1354]: Fol. 226r (`ск҃ѕ`). Ode 4 of the Second Canon to the Most Holy Theotokos (by Joseph the Hymnographer). The rubric *і҆рмо́съ · та́же ·* directs the continuation of the troparia following the heirmos. The soul implores nature (clouds, mountains, hills) to weep over its catastrophic estrangement from God.
[^1355]: Fol. 226r (`ск҃ѕ`). The dying soul addresses the Mother of God as the infallible and unashamed refuge in the terrifying hour of trial (*въ ча́съ и҆спыта́нїа*).
[^1356]: Fol. 226r (`ск҃ѕ`). The image of the outstretched hands of the Theotokos as the wings of a dove (*ꙗ҆́ко сщ҃еннѣ́ голꙋби́нѣ кри́лѣ*) draws upon Psalm 54:7 (LXX) and patristic maternal shelter motifs.
[^1357]: Fol. 226r (`ск҃ѕ`). Slavonic *Въздоу́шнаго кнѧ́ѕѧ наси́лника мꙋчителѧ, стра́шныхъ пꙋтестоѧ́телѧ* corresponds to Greek *τὸν ἀέριον τύραννον*, depicting the aerial prince and the terrifying inquisitors of words (*словои҆спыта́телѧ*) who examine the departing soul at the toll-houses.
[^1358]: Fol. 226v. Theotokion concluding Ode 4 of the Second Canon. The dying Christian confesses the realization of long-dreaded deathbed terror (*се́ мѧ влч҃це стра́хъ оу҆срѣ́те, є҆го́же и҆ боѧ́хсѧ*).
[^1359]: Fol. 226v. Tone 6, Ode 5 of the First Canon (by St. Andrew of Crete). Heirmos incipit: *Θείῳ φέγγει σου, Ἀγαθέ* (Slavonic: *Бж҃їимъ свѣ́томъ ти, Бл҃же*). The first troparion poignantly addresses the physical/sensible light of the sun and creation (*Свѣ́те чꙋ́вственый*), bidding it farewell as demonic darkness closes in.
[^1360]: Fol. 227r (`ск҃з`). Concluding sentence of Troparion 2 of Ode 5 (Canon 1). The soul reproaches the saints and friends of the Savior for their silence, pleading that a single united cry to the Mother of God would have obtained deliverance from darkness.
