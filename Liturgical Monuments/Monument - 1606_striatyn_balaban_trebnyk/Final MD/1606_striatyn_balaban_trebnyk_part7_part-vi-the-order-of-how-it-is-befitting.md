# PART VI: THE ORDER OF HOW IT IS BEFITTING TO RECEIVE THOSE WHO COME TO THE HOLY AND CATHOLIC APOSTOLIC CHURCH FROM HERETICS UNTO THE ORTHODOX FAITH [^155]

*For Arians, and Macedonians—that is, adversaries of the Spirit—Sabbatians who call themselves pure, and Quartodecimans, who are called Tetradites, and Apollinarians, we receive when they give a written confession [libellus] and anathematize their own heresy, and every senseless heresy, that they may hold the mind even as the holy Church of God and apostolic [Church].*

*He is brought whensoever he desires [to come unto] the Orthodox faith. And it is befitting for him to fast fourteen days, or [the fast of the] Panagia, and to exercise himself in prayers morning and evening, learning the psalms, as a catechumen. And thus is it befitting that he be accounted worthy of the Orthodox faith.*[^156]

*And the priest places him before the holy font, and says unto him thus: "Say:"*

**Penitent:**
Anathema to *[Name of heresiarch]*, and his teaching, and his accomplices, and those that hold the same mind with him. For I renounce him and every heretical teaching.

And I believe in the Holy and Consubstantial Trinity, according to the exposition of the Holy Fathers, who have rightly set forth the word of truth.

*Then he recites "I believe in one God" unto the end, verse by verse.*

*But if he knows it not, let his sponsor recite it, and let him recite after him.*

*And the priest interrogates him thrice:*

**Priest:**
Believest thou in the Holy and Consubstantial Trinity?

*And he answers:*

**Penitent:**
I believe.

*And the one being baptized [received] bows his head. And the priest prays:*[^157]

**Priest:**
[Fol. 44r. Decorative woodcut initial B.]
O Holy God and our Savior, Who desirest that all men should be saved and come unto the knowledge of the truth: receive this Thy servant, *[Name]*, who has scarcely recovered himself from deception,[^158] and who has loved to come unto the knowledge of the truth. For Thou, O Lord, didst say: "Other sheep I have which are not of this fold; them also I must bring, and they shall hear My voice; and there shall be one fold, and one shepherd."[^159] Shepherd him unto Thy glory, in the true knowledge according to the exposition of Thy holy and glorious Apostles; and account him worthy of the seal of the holy myron, and of communion of the holy Body and Blood. Show forth this Thy servant, *[Name]*, complete, that he also may be numbered among Thy flock, unto the praise and glory of Thy Name.

*Ekphonesis:*

**Priest:**
For Thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*And he anoints him with myron even as a newly-illumined one, making the same invocation over him.*[^160]

*And again he says a prayer:*

**Priest:**
[Fol. 45r. Decorative woodcut initial G.]
O Lord our God, Who hast accounted worthy to show forth complete this Thy servant, *[Name]*, for the sake of the right-believing faith which is toward Thee, and of the seal of Thy holy myron: do Thou, O Master of all, preserve in him the true faith which is toward Thee; nurture him in righteousness; and adorn him with all Thy gifts. For Thou art our God, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. [Amen.]

*And after these things, he communicates him. And he commands him for seven days not to eat meat, and not to wash his face, but even as a newly-baptized one so to abide, and on the eighth day to be washed.*[^161]

[Fol. 45v. Tailpiece: Two mirrored woodcut ornamental floral vignettes.]

*PRAYER TO SIGN A PERSON WHO HAS COME FROM THE HEATHEN TO BE BAPTIZED, AND TO NAME HIM.*[^162]

**Priest:**
[Fol. 45v. Decorative woodcut initial K.]
Unto Thee, O God of all creation visible and invisible, Who didst make all things by the Word: this man is brought, that upon him may be signed the light of Thine Only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, and of Thy Holy Spirit. Deliver him, O Lord, from all uncleanness, and from every deception of the adversary, and from every heretical name. Make him a chosen vessel; that this man also may be an equal Christian, known from this day forth, bearing the seal of Christ, Thy true God: with Whom Thou art blessed, together with Thine All-holy, and good, and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^163]

*ARMENIANS TURNING UNTO OUR ORTHODOX FAITH ARE NOT BAPTIZED, BUT ARE ANOINTED WITH MYRON, GIVING A CONFESSION OF THIS MANNER:*[^164]

**Penitent:**
[Fol. 46v. Decorative initial Ya.]
I, *[Name]*, come unto the Orthodox Catholic and Apostolic Church; and I renounce and anathematize every heresy: Armenian, and Chazinzarian, and Jacobite; and their feasts, and their fasts, and food and drink, and all their heresy.[^165]

And I believe in one God...

*And he recites the entire holy symbol, which is: "I believe in one God"*[^166] **Penitent:**
...and one essence indeed of the Holy Trinity do I confess. And in the bodily dispensation of our Lord Jesus Christ, two natures do I confess, of Godhead and of manhood; and the Savior's Passion I declare Him to have suffered in the flesh, and not in the Godhead; for the Godhead is impassible.

I receive also the seven holy Ecumenical Councils; and I anathematize all whom they anathematize:[^167] Simon the first heretic; and Manes; and Marcion, and those with him; and Arius, and Eunomius; Macedonius, and Apollinarius; Paul of Samosata; Sabellius; Theodore; Nestorius; Eutyches; Dioscorus; Theodosius; Timothy;

**Penitent:**
...Julian; Peter the Mongus, and Peter the Fuller, who unite suffering unto the Godhead; Severus the headless, and his disciple Jacob Zanzalus; Cyrus and Marcian; Sergius, and Pyrrhus; and Polychronius the childish elder.[^168] From all these I separate myself purely.

And if I do this with falsehood and deceit, and say in my heart that I will return unto my former heresy: now therefore let all the curses come upon me, and the trembling of Cain, and the leprosy of Gehazi; and in addition may I be liable to the inexorable punishment of the civil law; and in the age to come may I be cast out and accursed. And[^169] **Penitent:**
...may my soul be numbered with Satan and the demons.

Anathema to all heretics! *Thrice.*[^170]

*And the one being renewed bows his head. And the priest says the prayer:*

**Priest:**
[Fol. 48r. Decorative cinnabar initial B.]
O God our Savior, Who willest all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth: receive Thy servant, *[Name]*, who has scarcely recovered himself from deception, and has desired to come unto the knowledge of the truth.[^171] For Thou didst say, O Lord: "Other sheep I have which are not of this fold; them also I must bring, and they shall hear My voice; and there shall be one flock, and one shepherd." Shepherd him in...

**Priest:**
...Thy glory and true understanding, according to the commandment of Thy holy and glorious Apostles. And make him worthy of the seal of the holy myron, and of the communion of the precious Body and Blood; and show forth Thy servant perfect, that he too may be a partaker of Thy flock, unto the praise and glory of Thy majesty.

*Ekphonesis:*

**Priest:**
For Thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

[Choir: Amen.]

*And after this he anoints him with myron as a newly illumined one, making the same invocation over him.*[^172]

*THEN THE PRAYER.*

**Priest:**
[Fol. 49r. Decorative cinnabar initial G.]
O Lord our God, Who hast deigned to show forth this Thy servant, *[Name]*, perfect, through faith of true belief which is toward Thee, and through the seal of Thy holy myron: do Thou, Master of all, preserve in him the true faith, increase him in righteousness, and adorn him with all Thy gifts. For Thou art our God, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

[Choir: Amen.]

### Notes

[^155]: Fol. 43r. Part VI: The Reception of Converts and Ancient Conciliar Canons (*ЧИ́НЪ КА́КО ПОДОБА́ЄТ ПРЇИМА́ТИ и҆́же ѿ є҆ре́ти́къ...* / Greek: Τάξις πῶς δεῖ δέχεσθαι τοὺς ἐξ αἱρέσεων προσερχομένους...). The opening rubric of Part VI cites almost verbatim the canonical legislation established by the Second Ecumenical Council (Constantinople I, 381, Canon 7) and confirmed by the Quinisext Council in Trullo (692, Canon 95). These canons classify heterodox converts into three distinct canonical categories according to their baptismal status: (1) those requiring full threefold baptism (e.g. Eunomians, Montanists, Sabellians); (2) those received by chrismation and formal written abjuration (*libellus* / *и҆зрече́нїе*), comprising Arians, Macedonians (Pneumatomachians, *дх҃оу съпоста́ты*), Novatians/Catharoi (conflated here under *Савва́тники*), Quartodecimans (*четырина́десѧтники, рекѡ́мїи тетради́те*), and Apollinarians; and (3) those received solely through abjuration and the profession of the Orthodox faith (e.g. Nestorians, Monophysites).
[^156]: Fol. 43v. The Preparatory Fast, Catechetical Discipline, and the Term *па́надиѧ* (*пости́ти дни̑и д҃і , и҆ли па́надиѧ*). Before reconciliation, the candidate is placed under a strict fourteen-day ascetical regimen: fasting for fourteen days (*дни̑и д҃і*), attending morning and evening church services daily, and learning the Psalter (*оу҆чѧ́щоусѧ ѱалмы̀*) in the manner of a formal catechumen (*ꙗ҆́ко ѻ҆глаше́нъ*). The alternate phrase *и҆ли па́надиѧ* (or *панаги́а*) refers in Ruthenian and Greek monastic parlance to the two-week Dormition Fast preceding the Feast of the All-Holy Mother of God (*Panagia*, August 1–14), which constitutes an exact fourteen-day fast.
[^157]: Fol. 44r. The Recitation of the Creed and the Rubric *кр҃ща́ѧисѧ* (*приклона́етъ гла́вꙋ кр҃ща́ѧисѧ*). The candidate recites the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed verse by verse; if illiterate or unfamiliar with Church Slavonic, the sponsor dictates it for him line by line. The rubric denotes the candidate as *кр҃ща́ѧисѧ* ("the one being baptized"), reflecting the ancient euchological terminology where persons undergoing any stage of the catechumenate or sacramental reconciliation are designated broadly by the verbal noun of illumination.
[^158]: Fol. 44r. The Euchological Prayer of Reception: 1 Timothy 2:4 and Deliverance from Deception (*Бг҃е ст҃ы́й и҆ сп҃сителю на́шъ, хотѧ́й въсѣ́мъ чл҃кѡмъ сп҃тисѧ...* / Greek: Ὁ Θεὸς ὁ ἅγιος καὶ σωτὴρ ἡμῶν, ὁ πάντας ἀνθρώπους θέλων σωθῆναι...). The prayer opens with the apostolic proclamation of 1 Timothy 2:4 that God wills all men to be saved and to come unto the knowledge of the truth. It describes the convert as one who has "scarcely recovered himself from deception" (*є҆два̀ въскло́ньшагосѧ ѿ ле́сти*, Greek: μόλις ἀνανεύσαντα ἀπὸ τῆς πλάνης), petitioning the Divine Shepherd to receive him back into the rational sheepfold.
[^159]: Fol. 44v. The Johannine Theology of the One Flock (John 10:16). The euchology explicitly quotes Christ's discourse on the Good Shepherd in John 10:16: "Other sheep I have which are not of this fold; them also I must bring, and they shall hear My voice; and there shall be one fold, and one shepherd" (*ꙗ҆́ко и҆ и҆ны́ѧ ѻ҆́вца и҆́мамъ...*). This Scripture grounds the theological reconciliation of schismatics and heretics not as an innovation, but as the fulfillment of Christ's cosmic gathering of scattered souls into the unity of the Church through the seal of holy Chrismation and partaking of the Holy Mysteries.
[^160]: Fol. 45r. Post-Reconciliation Chrismation (*пома́зоуетъ є҆го̀ мѵ́ромъ ꙗ҆́ко новопросвѣще́ннаго, то́жде нарица́нїе творѧ̀ надъ ни́мъ*). Following the anathemas and profession of faith, the priest anoints the candidate with holy myron using the canonical sacramental formula identical to that of baptismal illumination: "The seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit. Amen." This reflects the second-class reception established by Canon 7 of Constantinople I and Canon 95 of Trullo, validating the outward Trinitarian form of their previous water immersion while imparting the sacramental grace of the Holy Spirit through Orthodox Chrismation.
[^161]: Fol. 45v. The Seven-Day Fasting and Octave Injunction (*повелѣва́етъ є҆мꙋ̀ з҃ дн҃ии ма́са не ꙗ҆́сти, и҆ лица̀ не оу҆мыва́ти*). Following Holy Communion, the newly reconciled Christian is instructed to observe seven days of meatless fasting and to refrain from washing his face, remaining in the spiritual state of a newly baptized person (*ꙗ҆́коже новокрща́емый та́ко пребыва́ти*), returning on the eighth day for the ritual ablution of the holy chrism.
[^162]: Fol. 45v. The Enrolment and Naming of a Convert from Paganism (*Мл҃тва знамена́ти чл҃ка прише́дша ѿ пога́ныхъ кр҃титисѧ, и҆ и҆́мѧ є҆моу̀ нарещѝ* / Greek: Εὐχὴ εἰς τὸ σημειῶσαι ἄνθρωπον ἐξ ἐθνῶν ἐρχόμενον βαπτισθῆναι, καὶ ὄνομα αὐτῷ ἐπιθεῖναι). This euchological prayer serves as the adult liturgical equivalent to the naming of an infant on the eighth day (*Chindanye* / *Пострижины*). It marks the formal signing with the cross on the brow and the bestowing of a Christian baptismal name upon an unbaptized catechumen converting from paganism (*ѿ пога́ныхъ*).
[^163]: Fol. 46r. Renunciation of Pagan Nomenclature and the Dignity of an "Equal Christian" (*да бꙋ́детъ и҆ се́и раве́нъ хрⷭ҇тїа́нинъ, зна́емъ ѿ дне́шнего днѐ*). The prayer explicitly beseeches God to purge the convert of every former pagan and heretical name (*всѧ́кого и҆́мене є҆рети́ческаго*), making him a "chosen vessel" (Acts 9:15) and an "equal Christian" (*раве́нъ хрⷭ҇тїа́нинъ* / Greek: ἰσοχριστιανός), invested with full spiritual citizenship in the Body of Christ without distinction of prior ethnicity or background (Galatians 3:28).
[^164]: Fol. 46v. Canonical Reception of Armenians and Jacobites (*А҆РМЕ́НИ Ѡ҆БРАЩА́ЮЩЕИСѦ... не кр҃щаютсѧ, но пома́зоуютсѧ му́ромъ*). In strict fidelity to the Byzantine canonical tradition (summarized in the Nomokanons and the *Syntagma* of Matthew Blastares), Non-Chalcedonians—specifically Armenians and Syrian Jacobites—are received into the Orthodox Church through Chrismation and the abjuration of their characteristic recensional and theological peculiarities, rather than by re-baptism, recognizing their valid historical Trinitarian baptism.
[^165]: Fol. 46v. The Renunciation of Armenian, Chazinzarian, and Jacobite Errors (*въсѧ́коую є҆́ресь а҆рме́нскоую, и҆ ханзиза́рскоую, и҆ і҆аковити́коую*). The abjuration formula explicitly targets three interrelated Oriental non-Chalcedonian groups: (1) the Armenians (*а҆рме́нскоую*); (2) the Chazinzarians (*ханзиза́рскоую* / Greek: Χαζινζάριοι, Chatzitzarioi, or Staurolatrae, from Armenian *khach* / *khachazart*, "worshippers of the Cross", a 7th-century Armenian sect or polemical epithet applied in Byzantine anti-Armenian treatises by Nicetas Choniates and Demetrius of Cyzicus); and (3) the Jacobites (*і҆аковити́коую*, Syrian Miaphysites following Jacob Baradaeus). The convert also explicitly renounces their liturgical customs: the use of unmixed wine in the eucharistic chalice, azymes (unleavened bread), and their distinctive fasting ordinances (such as the Fast of the Artzivourion / St. Sarkis).
[^166]: Fol. 46v. The Holy Symbol of Faith as *ст҃ыи ѻ҆́бразъ* (*И҆ гл҃етъ ве́сь ст҃ыи ѻ҆́бразъ, є҆́же є҆́стъ Вѣ́рꙋю въ є҆ди́наго Бг҃а*). In Church Slavonic euchological rubrics, the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed is frequently termed *ст҃ыи ѻ҆́бразъ* (translating the Greek τύπος τῆς πίστεως or σύμβολον τῆς πίστεως, "the sacred pattern / type / symbol of the faith"), which the convert recites in its entirety before being anointed with holy chrism.
[^167]: Fol. 47r. The Christological Confession and Conciliar Anathemas of the Non-Chalcedonian Convert (*и҆ є҆ди́но оу҆́бѡ є҆стество̀ ст҃ѣ́и трⷪ҇ци и҆сповѣ́дꙋю...*). The penitent's profession of faith articulates the classic Chalcedonian Christological dogma against Monophysitism/Miaphysitism: confessing two distinct natures (*двѣ̀ є҆стествѣ̀*) in the incarnate Lord Jesus Christ—unconfused, unchangeable, indivisible, and inseparable (Council of Chalcedon, 451)—and affirming the impassibility of the divine nature (*Бж҃тво безстра́стно є҆́стъ*) against Theopaschism. The convert formally assents to the Seven Ecumenical Councils (*се́дмь събѡ́ровъ*) and pronounces anathema against ancient heresiarchs: Simon Magus (*Си́монъ пръ́выи є҆ретикъ*); Manes (founder of Manichaeism); Marcion; Arius and Eunomius (Arianism and Anomoeanism); Macedonius (Pneumatomachianism); Apollinarius; Paul of Samosata (Monarchianism); Sabellius (Modalism); Theodore of Mopsuestia (condemned at Constantinople II, 553); Nestorius (Nestorianism, Ephesus 431); and the Monophysite leaders Eutyches, Dioscorus of Alexandria (Chalcedon 451), Theodosius of Alexandria, and Timothy Aelurus.
[^168]: Fol. 47v. Anathemas against the Aphthartodocetic, Monophysite, and Monothelite Leaders (*І҆ꙋлїа́на. Петра̀ мо́гка...*). The catalogue continues with Julian of Halicarnassus (advocate of Aphthartodocetism); Peter Mongus (*Пе́тръ мо́гкъ*, Patriarch of Alexandria) and Peter the Fuller (*Пе́тръ крафе́й* / Gnapheus, Patriarch of Antioch), who interpolated the Theopaschite clause (*"Who was crucified for us"*) into the Trisagion; Severus of Antioch (*Севи́ръ безгла́вныи*), termed "headless" as leader of the Acephali; his organizer Jacob Baradaeus (*І҆а́ковъ цанца́лъ* / Zanzalus), from whom the Jacobite Syrian Church derives its historical appellation; Cyrus of Phasis/Alexandria and Marcian; Sergius I and Pyrrhus, Patriarchs of Constantinople and proponents of Monothelitism; and Polychronius (*Полихро́нїй младооу́мныи ста́рецъ*), the elderly Monothelite monk condemned at the Sixth Ecumenical Council (Constantinople III, 680–681) for attempting to prove his doctrine through an unsuccessful resurrection of the dead.
[^169]: Fol. 47v. The Imprecatory Oath and Civil Penalties of Perjury (*А҆́ще ли съ лже́ю и҆ ле́стїю сѐ творю̀...*). The candidate binds his conscience under an inviolable self-imprecation (*anathema*) invoking canonical and Scriptural archetypes of divine retribution: the perpetual vagabondage and trembling of fratricidal Cain (Genesis 4:12–14, LXX) and the incurable clinging leprosy of Gehazi, servant of Elisha, struck down for simony and deceit (4 Kingdoms 5:27 / 2 Kings 5:27). The formula combines spiritual damnation (*ѿринове́нъ, и҆ про́клѧтъ*) with explicit submission to the capital and corporeal penalties of imperial Byzantine civil jurisprudence (*гра́дскїи зако́нъ* / the *Basilika* and *Ecloga*), which prescribed severe civic infamy, banishment, and confiscation for relapsed heretics and perjured converts.
[^170]: Fol. 48r. The Threefold General Anathema and the Term *ѻ҆бновлѧ́емый* (*Въсѣ̑мъ є҆рети́кѡ́мъ а҆наѳе́ма, По три́щи. И҆ приклони́тъ гла́вꙋ ѻ҆бновлѧ́емый*). Following the recitation of specific heresiarchs and errors, the candidate seals his renunciation by proclaiming anathema against all heretical teachings three times (*по три́щи*). The rubrics designate the candidate as *ѻ҆бновлѧ́емый* ("the one being renewed" / regenerated; Greek: ὁ ἀνανεούμενος), denoting his sacramental rehabilitation from schism into the living communion of the Church.
[^171]: Fol. 48r. Scriptural Topoi of Reconciliation: 1 Timothy 2:4 and John 10:16 (*Бг҃е сп҃сителю на́шь, хотѧ́й всѣ̑мъ чл҃кѡмъ сп҃тисѧ...*). The euchological prayer grounds the reconciliation of Oriental non-Chalcedonians upon two central Scriptural passages: St. Paul's pastoral declaration of universal salvific intent in 1 Timothy 2:4 (*"Who will have all men to be saved, and to come unto the knowledge of the truth"*), and Christ's parable of the Good Shepherd in John 10:16 (*"And other sheep I have, which are not of this fold: them also I must bring, and they shall hear My voice; and there shall be one fold, and one shepherd"*). The phrase *є҆два̀ възъноу́вша ѿ ле́сти* (variant for *въскло́ньшагосѧ*, Greek: μόλις ἀνανεύσαντα ἀπὸ τῆς πλάνης) captures the image of one who has barely raised his head or awakened from the slumber of deception.
[^172]: Fol. 48v. Post-Abjuration Chrismation and Sacramental Integration (*пома́зоуетъ є҆го̀ му́ромъ ꙗ҆́ко новопросвѣще́ннаго. То́жде нарица́нїе творѧ̀ на́дъ нимъ*). The rubric specifies that the priest anoints the reconciled convert with holy myron (*мѵ́ро*) in the exact manner of a newly baptized person (*ꙗ҆́ко новопросвѣще́ннаго*), employing the canonical sacramental seal (*то́жде нарица́нїе* / "The seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit. Amen"). This conforms to Canon 95 of the Council in Trullo (692), which prescribes that Monophysites and Armenians, having retained valid historical Trinitarian baptism, are received into Orthodox communion through holy Chrismation and the immediate partaking of the Holy Mysteries (*прича́стїю чⷭ҇тнаго тѣ́ла, и҆ кро́ве*).
[^173]: Fol. 49r. The Post-Chrismation Clothing, Seven-Day Octave Fast, and Eighth-Day Ablution (*И҆ та́ко ѻ҆дѣва́етъ и҆̀. И҆ пороучи́тъ є҆моу̀ се́дмь дн҃ии ма́са не ꙗ҆́сти...*). The newly chrismated convert is invested in clean Christian garments (*ѻ҆дѣва́етъ и҆̀*), symbolizing the white baptismal robe of justification (*хитѡ́нъ пра́вды*). He is placed under an ascetical discipline for the duration of the seven-day octave: abstaining from meat (*ма́са не ꙗ҆́сти*) and refraining from washing his face, thereby preventing any profanation of the holy chrism upon his brow, eyes, nostrils, lips, ears, breast, and hands. On the eighth day (*въ ѻ҆смы́и же де́нь*), he returns to the church for the solemn liturgical washing (*ѻ҆мыва́нїе*), completing his sacramental integration into the Orthodox Church.
