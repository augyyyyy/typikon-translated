# PART XVII: PRAYERS OF THANKSGIVING AFTER HOLY COMMUNION [^1278]

*When thou hast obtained the most precious communion of the gifts of the life-creating Mysteries, sing immediately, give great thanks, and say these things fervently from the soul unto God:*[^1279]

*Prayer of Saint Basil the Great, after Holy Communion:*

I thank Thee, O Lord my God, that Thou hast not rejected me, a sinner, but hast vouchsafed me to be a partaker of Thy holy things. I thank Thee that Thou hast vouchsafed me, unworthy though I be, to partake of Thine all-pure and heavenly gifts. But, O Master[^1280] Lover of mankind, Who for our sake didst die and rise again, and didst bestow upon us these Thy dread and life-creating Mysteries: grant that these may be unto me for the healing of both soul and body, for the driving away of every adversary, for the enlightenment of the eyes of my heart, for the peacemaking of my soul's powers, for faith unashamed, for love unfeigned, for the fullness of wisdom, for the keeping of Thy commandments, for the increase of Thy divine grace, and for the attainment of Thy kingdom;[^1281] that preserved by Thy holiness, I may ever remember Thy grace, and no longer live unto myself,[^1282] but unto Thee, our Master and Benefactor; and thus departing this life in hope of eternal life, I may attain unto everlasting rest, where the voice of them that keep festival is unceasing, and infinite is the delight of them that behold the ineffable beauty of Thy countenance. For Thou art the true desire and ineffable gladness of them that love Thee, O Christ our God, and all creation doth glorify Thee unto ages. Amen.[^1283]

*Prayer of our father among the saints, John Chrysostom, after Holy Communion:*[^1284]

I thank Thee, O Lord, Lover of mankind, that according to Thy great goodness Thou hast vouchsafed me this morning to be a partaker of Thine all-pure Body and precious[^1285] Blood; and Thou didst not abhor me as one defiled, nor as unworthy of the reception of Thy holy Mysteries didst Thou cast me away by Thine invisible and divine power; but Thou wast well pleased that I, a sinner, should be nourished by Thine immortal table.[^1286] Whereof keep Thou the life-creating grace unalienated in my humble soul, and make Thy enlightenment unquenchable from me, enlightening in me all understanding and every sense, keeping me untouched and unstumbling from every sin, and ungrasped by the darkness of sin; that I may ever glorify and thank Thee, and according to[^1287] Thy will walk all the days of my life; through the prayers of our all-pure Sovereign Lady Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary, of Thy bodiless ministers, and of all the saints who from the ages have been well-pleasing unto Thee. Amen.[^1288]

*Of the same Saint John Chrysostom, prayer after Holy Communion:*[^1289]

O Master, Christ God, King of ages and Creator of all: I thank Thee for all the good things which Thou hast given me, and for the communion of Thine all-pure and life-creating Mysteries. I pray Thee, O Good One and Lover of mankind: keep me under the shelter of Thy wings,[^1290] and grant me with a pure conscience, unto my last breath worthily to partake of Thy holy things, unto remission of sins, and life eternal.[^1291] For Thou art the Bread of life, and the Fountain of holiness, and the Giver of good things; and unto Thee do we send up glory, with Thine unoriginate Father, and Thine all-holy and good and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^1292]

*Of the same Saint John Chrysostom, prayer after Holy Communion:*[^1293]

Having been mystically accounted worthy to be a partaker of Thine all-pure Body and Thy precious Blood, O Christ God, I sing praise and bless, I worship and glorify, and magnify Thy salvations, O Lord, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*Of Saint John of Damascus, prayer after Holy Communion:*[^1294]

O Jesus, my God, incomprehensible and invisible;[^1295] O Fire, Who makest Thine angels a burning fire;[^1296] Who by Thine ineffable love hast delivered unto me for food Thy divine Flesh, receiving me as a partaker of Thy Godhead for the sake of the communion of Thine all-pure Body and Thy precious Blood: pass through my whole body and spirit, into all my bones and marrow,[^1297] burning up my sins, enlightening my soul, making clear my mind, illuminating my body, and making an abode in me with Thine all-blessed Father, that I also may abide in Thee evermore;[^1298] through the prayers of Thine all-pure Mother, and of all the saints. Amen.

*Of Saint Cyril of Alexandria, prayer after Holy Communion:*[^1299]

May Thy holy Body, O Lord Jesus Christ my God, be unto me for life and sanctification, and Thy precious Blood for remission of sins and unto life eternal. And at Thy holy judgment set me on Thy right hand;[^1300] and may this thanksgiving be unto me for joy and health, unto the healing of both soul and body; for Thou art blessed unto ages. Amen.

*Of Saint Symeon the Metaphrast, prayer after Holy Communion:*[^1301]

Thou Who willingly hast given me Thy Flesh for food, Who art fire consuming the unworthy: burn me not, O my Creator, but rather enter into my members effectually, and into all my joints, into my inmost parts and heart, burning up the thorns of all my transgressions;[^1302] cleanse my soul, sanctify all my thoughts, establish my mind together with my bones; illuminate the simple fivefold count of the senses;[^1303] nail me wholly to Thy fear evermore;[^1304] covering, keeping, and guarding me from every soul-destroying deed and word. Cleanse, wash, and enlighten me; adorn and give me understanding; show me forth as an abode of Thy Spirit alone, and no longer as an abode of sin; that being Thy temple through the entrance of communion, like fire burn up every evildoing and every passion.[^1305] As intercessors I bring unto Thee all the saints: the angelic hierarchies of the bodiless hosts, and Thy Forerunner, and the all-wise Apostles, and with them Thine all-pure Mother;[^1306] whose entreaties receive, O compassionate Christ my God, and reveal me as a child of Thy light, and show me forth as a minister unto Thyself. For Thou alone art the sanctification of our souls, O Good One, and[^1307] their radiance; and unto Thee, as is fitting unto God and Master, do we send up glory, all the days of our life, and now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*Prayer to the All-Holy Theotokos, after Holy Communion:*[^1308]

All-holy Sovereign Lady Theotokos,[^1309] light of my darkened soul, my hope, shelter, refuge, consolation, and rejoicing: I thank thee that thou hast accounted me, unworthy though I be, to be a communicant of the all-pure Body and precious Blood of thy Son. But, O thou that didst bear the True Light, enlighten the spiritual eyes of my heart; O thou that didst give birth to the Fountain of immortality,[^1310] quicken me who am slain by sin![^1311] O Mother of the merciful and compassionate God, have mercy on me, and give me weeping and contrition in my heart, and humility in my thoughts, and recovery from the captivity of my reasonings;[^1312] and vouchsafe me, unto my last breath, uncondemned to receive the sanctification of the all-pure Mysteries, unto the healing of both soul and body. And grant me, O Sovereign Lady, tears of repentance and confession, that I may sing and glorify thee all the days of my life; for thou art blessed and most glorified unto ages. Amen.

*Prayer after Holy Communion:*[^1313]

O living Lord, I thank Thee with trembling; for being Fire by nature, Thou dost not burn, but nourishest.[^1314] Pass through therefore effectually into my members, and into all my joints, into my inmost parts and heart; cleanse, wash away every defilement, purifying the fivefold count of the senses.[^1315]

*Then:* **"It is truly meet..."** *and the Trisagion Prayers. After the Our Father, the troparion of whichever saint thou desirest, either of the current day, or of the saint of the temple.*[^1316]

*Glory..., both now... Theotokion.*

*Lord, have mercy (12 times).*

*Then: Glory..., both now...*

*Lord, have mercy (thrice). Lord, bless. And the dismissal:*

**"Through the prayers of our holy fathers, O Lord Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us. Amen."**[^1317]

### Notes

[^1278]: Major Structural Division: The beginning of the Post-Communion Thanksgiving Prayers (*Послѣдованіе по святомъ причащеніи*), marked on leaf p418 by a prominent Renaissance woodcut headpiece, rubric in red display majuscules, and the first prayer numbered `а҃` in the margin.
[^1279]: Rubric of Immediate Thanksgiving: Admonishing the communicant not to delay, but immediately to sing praises and give fervent thanks unto God from the soul after receiving the life-creating Mysteries.
[^1280]: Prayer 1 of Saint Basil the Great: The classic Post-Communion prayer attributed to St. Basil the Great (*Мл҃тва ст҃го вели́каго васїлїа, по ст҃о́мъ прича́щенїи*), expressing profound gratitude for being accounted worthy of the heavenly gifts (continuing onto leaf p419).
[^1281]: The Catalogue of Eucharistic Graces: St. Basil's detailed enumeration of the spiritual fruits of Communion: healing of soul and body, driving away of demonic adversaries, enlightenment of spiritual sight, pacification of inner passions, unfeigned love, and keeping of the commandments.
[^1282]: Living No Longer Unto Oneself: Echoing 2 Corinthians 5:15 and Romans 14:7–8, praying that the communicant, preserved by divine holiness, may live no longer for self, but unto Christ (continuing onto leaf p420).
[^1283]: Eschatological Rest and the Ineffable Beauty: The sublime conclusion of St. Basil's prayer, longing to attain unto everlasting rest (*въ присносꙋ́щный доидоу̀ поко́й*) where the festival of the righteous never ends and the beauty of God's countenance is beheld forever.
[^1284]: Prayer 2 of Saint John Chrysostom: The post-communion prayer attributed to St. John Chrysostom (*И҆́же въ ст҃ы́хъ ѻ҆́ц҃а на́шего і҆ѡа́нна златооу́стаго мл҃, по ст҃о́ причаще́нїи*), marked with numeral `в҃` in the margin.
[^1285]: Ruthenian Lexical Witness *оу҆трѣ́пѣ*: The rare temporal adverbial form *оу҆трѣ́пѣ* (dial./early modern variant corresponding to *заутра* / *рано вранці* / *early this morning*), witnessing the morning celebration of the Divine Liturgy.
[^1286]: Eucharistic Table and Preservation of Grace: The continuation of St. John Chrysostom's second post-communion prayer, entreating that the communicant's humble soul may keep the grace of the immortal table unalienated (*неѿе́млемꙋ*).
[^1287]: The Unquenchable Light and Spiritual Senses: The petition that the communicant's understanding and bodily senses be illumined by divine unquenchable light (*просвѣще́нїе твоѐ неоу҆гаси́мо*), preserved unstumbling from sin (continuing onto leaf p422).
[^1288]: Bodiless Ministers and Heavenly Intercessors: The prayer's concluding invocation of the all-pure Sovereign Lady Theotokos, the bodiless ministers (*безплѡ́тныхъ ти слꙋжи́тель*, referring to the angelic orders), and all saints who have pleased God from the ages.
[^1289]: Prayer 3 of Saint John Chrysostom: The post-communion prayer attributed to St. John Chrysostom (*Того́ ст҃го і҆ѡа́нна златооу́стаго мл҃тва, по ст҃о́мъ причаще́нїи*), marked with numeral `г҃` in the margin, addressing Christ as King of ages and Creator of all.
[^1290]: Shelter of Wings: The biblical imagery of divine protection under the wings of God (Psalm 16:8; 35:8; 90:4 LXX: *покро́вомъ крилоу̀ твоє́ю*, continuing onto leaf p423).
[^1291]: Communion Unto the Last Breath: The earnest petition to partake worthily of the Holy Mysteries unto one's last breath (*до послѣ́дняго и҆здыха́нїа*), sealing salvation and eternal life.
[^1292]: Christ as the Bread of Life and Fountain of Sanctity: The theological tituli applied to Christ (*хлѣ́б живо́тный*, *и҆сто́чникъ ст҃ыни*, *пода́тель бл҃гы́хъ*), echoing John 6:35, 48.
[^1293]: Prayer 4 of Saint John Chrysostom: A concise hymn-like post-communion prayer (*Того́ ст҃го і҆ѡа́нна златооу́стаго мл҃тва*), marked with numeral `д҃` in the margin, offering praise and worship for being mystically made a partaker of the Lord's Body and Blood (continuing onto leaf p424).
[^1294]: Prayer 5 of Saint John of Damascus: The post-communion prayer attributed to St. John of Damascus (*Ст҃го і҆ѡа́нна дамаски́на мл҃тва*), marked with numeral `е҃` in the margin.
[^1295]: Theological Lexical Witness *непостоѧ́нный*: The rare Church Slavonic term *непостоѧ́нный* (literally "unstanding", uncontainable, boundless) translating the Greek liturgical epithet *ὁ ἀκατάληπτος* (incomprehensible / uncontainable).
[^1296]: The Consuming Fire and Ministering Spirits: Allusion to Psalm 103:4 LXX (*Who makes His angels spirits, and His ministers a flame of fire*) and Hebrews 12:29 (*For our God is a consuming fire*), marveling that this same Divine Fire is given in love as nourishing food.
[^1297]: Piercing Joints and Marrow: The physical and spiritual penetration of the Eucharistic presence through every limb, bone, and marrow, directly reflecting the language of Hebrews 4:12 (continuing onto leaf p425).
[^1298]: Indwelling of the Trinity: Echoing Christ's promise in John 14:23 (*If a man love Me, he will keep My words: and My Father will love him, and We will come unto him, and make Our abode with him*), translated here as *ѡ҆би́тѣ сътворя́ѧ въ мнѣ̀ съ бл҃вє́ннымъ ѿце́мъ свои́мъ*.
[^1299]: Prayer 6 of Saint Cyril of Alexandria: The post-communion prayer attributed to St. Cyril of Alexandria (*Ст҃го кѷрі́лла а҆лєѯандрі́искаго мл҃тва*), marked with numeral `ѕ҃` in the margin.
[^1300]: Standing at the Right Hand: The eschatological petition to be placed at Christ's right hand at the Dread Judgment (Matthew 25:33: *и҆ на ст҃ѣ́мъ твоє́мъ сꙋдѣ̀ поста́ви мѧ̀ ѻ҆де́сноую тебѣ̀*).
[^1301]: Prayer 7 of Saint Symeon the Metaphrast: The famed Byzantine post-communion thanksgiving prayer attributed to St. Symeon the Metaphrast (*Ст҃го сѷмеѡ́на метафра́ста мл҃*), marked with numeral `з҃` in the margin.
[^1302]: Consuming the Thorns of Sins: The imagery of sin as soul-choking thorns burned away by divine fire (*попалѧ́ѧ тръ́нїе всѣ́хъ мои̑ съгрѣше́нїи*), recalling the Edenic curse of thorns (Genesis 3:18).
[^1303]: The Pentad of the Senses (*патери́ца*): The Greek liturgical loanword *патєри́цꙋ* translating *τὴν ἁπλῆν πεντάδα* ("the simple fivefold count" or pentad of the bodily senses), entreating that all five senses be illuminated by the divine presence.
[^1304]: Nailed to the Fear of God: Direct quotation of Psalm 118:120 LXX (*καθήλωσον ἐκ τοῦ φόβου σου τὰς σάρκας μου* / *всего̀ мѧ̀ пригвоздѝ стра́хꙋ твое́мꙋ*, continuing onto leaf p427).
[^1305]: The Communicant as Living Temple: The theology of the human person as a temple of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians 6:19), through whose entrance all demonic influence and wicked passion is driven away like fire.
[^1306]: The Heavenly Intercessory Choir: St. Symeon's marshalling of the angelic orders (*чинонача́лїа безпло́тныхъ*), the holy Forerunner John, the wise Apostles, and the Mother of God as advocates before the compassionate Christ.
[^1307]: Sanctification and Radiance: Acknowledging Christ alone as the true sanctification and radiance (*ѡ҆сщ҃е́нїе є҆ди́ныи... и҆ свѣ́тлость*) of Christian souls (continuing onto leaf p428).
[^1308]: Prayer 8 to the Most Holy Theotokos: The post-communion prayer addressed to the Mother of God (*мл҃тва, къ прⷭ҇тѣ́й Бц҃и*), marked with numeral `и҃` in the margin.
[^1309]: Marian Liturgical Tituli: The cascade of Marian praises (*свѣ́те помраче́нныѧ ми дш҃а, наде́жде и҆ покро́ве, прибѣ́жище и҆ оу҆твръжде́нїе, и҆ възра́дованїе моѐ*), acknowledging her role as mediatrix of the heavenly gifts.
[^1310]: Bearer of the True Light: Addressing the Virgin Mary as the Mother of the True Light (John 1:9) and the Fountain of Immortality (*и҆сто́чникъ безсмр҃тїа*), entreating the enlightenment of the spiritual eyes of the heart (Ephesians 1:18, continuing onto leaf p429).
[^1311]: Spiritual Quickening and Restoration: Petition to be restored to spiritual life from the deadness of sin (*ѡ҆животворѝ мѧ̀ оу҆мръщве́ннаго грѣхо́мъ*), begging for the gift of tears and compunction (*пла́чь и҆ съкроуше́нїе*).
[^1312]: Deliverance from Captivity of Reasonings: Asking for restoration from the wandering captivity of discursive worldly thoughts (*възведе́нїе въ плѣне́ныхъ по́мысломъ*, cf. 2 Corinthians 10:5).
[^1313]: Prayer 9 after Holy Communion: The concluding prayer of the post-communion series (*мл҃тва по ст҃о́мъ причаще́нїи*), marked with numeral `ѳ҃` in the margin on leaf p430.
[^1314]: The Nourishing Divine Fire: The mystery of God as Fire (Deuteronomy 4:24; Hebrews 12:29) which does not destroy the faithful receiver, but provides celestial nourishment (*є҆стество́ бо ꙗ҆́ко ѡ҆́гнь неѡпалѧ́еши, но пита́еши*).
[^1315]: Purifying the Fivefold Senses: The second occurrence in this sequence of the rare Ruthenian/Slavonic loanword *патєри́цеу* (Greek *πεντάς* / *πεντάδα*), denoting the purification of all five senses.
[^1316]: The Post-Communion Climax: The rubrical prescription ordering the recitation of *It is truly meet* (*досто́йно є҆́сть*), the Trisagion Prayers through the *Our Father*, and the troparion of the day or patron saint of the church.
[^1317]: Liturgical Dismissal (*Apolysis*): The rubrical conclusion prescribing the twelvefold *Lord, have mercy*, the triple *Lord, have mercy* with *Lord, bless*, and the final canonical dismissal formula (*За мл҃твъ ст҃ы ѿц҃ъ на́шихъ...*), solemnly sealing the rite of Thanksgiving after Holy Communion.
