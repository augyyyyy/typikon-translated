# PART XVIII: THE ORDER OF HOW IT IS BEFITTING TO GIVE COMMUNION TO A SICK PERSON IN HASTE [^1318]

*The Priest says:* **"Blessed is our God..."**[^1319]

*Then: the Trisagion; All-Holy Trinity; after "Our Father": "For Thine is the kingdom..." Lord, have mercy (12 times). Come, let us worship (thrice).*

*Then:* **"I believe in one God..."** *all the way to the end.*[^1320]

*After this, say:*

**"Of Thy Mystical Supper, O Son of God, receive me today as a communicant; for I will not speak of the mystery to Thine enemies,[^1321] nor will I give Thee a kiss as did Judas, but as the thief do I confess Thee: Remember me, O Lord, when Thou comest into Thy kingdom."**[^1322]

*Glory...*

*The King of heaven out of love for mankind appeared on earth and lived among men; for taking flesh from a pure Virgin and coming forth from Her with that which He assumed, one is the Son, double in nature, but not in person. Therefore, proclaiming Him in truth as perfect God and perfect Man, we confess Christ our God; Whom do thou beseech, O unwedded Mother, that He have mercy on our souls.*[^1323]

*Both now...*

*We have known God incarnate of thee,*[^1324] *O Virgin Theotokos; do thou beseech Him for the salvation of our souls.*

*Then this prayer:*[^1325]

**"May the Lord God most merciful have compassion on thee. May the Lord Jesus Christ grant thee every good petition. May the Lord Almighty deliver thee from every assault. May the Lord teach thee. May the Lord give thee understanding. May the Lord help thee. May the Lord save thee. May the Lord defend thee. May the Lord cleanse thee. May the Lord fill thee with spiritual joy. May the Lord be a protector to thy soul and body. May the Lord most good and merciful, the Lover of mankind, grant thee forgiveness of sins. May the Lord Jesus Christ in the Day of Judgment have mercy on thee, and all the days of thy life. Unto Him be glory, now and ever, and unto ages."**

*A second prayer, from the person of the sick one:*[^1326]

**"Cleanse me, O Lord, from my sins, Who for the sake of sinners didst shed Thy blood. O Master, Thou knowest that I am not worthy to look upon heaven, nor upon earth; but Thou Thyself hast said: 'He that eats My flesh, and drinks My blood, dwells in Me, and I in Him.'[^1327] Therefore, making bold, I come unto Thee in prayer; for Thou art a merciful God and the Lover of mankind; and unto Thee do we send up glory, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages."**

*A third prayer, similar to the second:*[^1328]

**"And I know that I am not worthy that Thou shouldest enter under the roof of the temple of my soul, for it is all desolate and burnt up, and Thou hast not with me a worthy place where to bow Thy head.[^1329] But since Thou desirest to be with me, trusting in Thy compassions I come unto Thee. Command the doors of my unworthy lips to be opened, that I may be satisfied with Thee alone. Enter into me, and cleanse me from every defilement of flesh and spirit; be Thou unto me a helper and protector, and account me worthy to stand on Thy right hand; by the prayers and supplication of our all-pure Lady, the Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary,[^1330] and of all the saints who from the ages have been well-pleasing unto Thee; for Thou art blessed unto ages. Amen."**

*Then the prayer against any defilement, which is said before holy communion:*[^1331]

**"O Lord our God, the only Good One and Lover of mankind, Who alone art holy and restest in the saints; Who by a vision revealed to Thy supreme Apostle Peter that nothing of the things created by Thee for the food and enjoyment of men is to be accounted defiled or unclean;[^1332] and Who through Thy chosen vessel, the Apostle Paul, didst command that all things are pure unto the pure:[^1333] do Thou Thyself, O all-holy Master, through the invocation of Thy dreadful and all-pure name, and by the sign of the precious and life-creating Cross, bless and cleanse Thy servant [Name], who has been defiled by every satanic work, from every hostile thing, from every venomous serpent, from every iniquity, from every stumbling block,[^1334] from every deceit, from every vanity, from every infirmity, from every plague, from every evildoing of the adversary devil, by day and by night. And now vouchsafe Thy servant [Name], unworthy though he be, according to Thy mercy to partake of Thine all-pure Mysteries; but first cleanse his soul and body from every defilement. And forgive him, O Lord, every transgression voluntary and involuntary, known and unknown, which he has sinned in all the days of his life, by word, or by deed, or by thought, until the present hour. And grant him, O Lord, this awesome ministry of the heavenly orders, and the communion of Thy life-creating Mysteries,[^1335] not unto judgment, nor unto condemnation, but unto the remission of sins, unto the communion of the Holy Spirit, unto the life of everlasting joy, which Thou hast prepared for Thy true servants. And now, O all-powerful Master, preserve Thy servant [Name] from every malice and sin; keep him undefiled and blameless from every adverse thing. And grant him, O Lord, to serve Thee in holiness and righteousness, until his last day and hour and breath. For Thou art He that blesses and sanctifies all things, and unto Thee do we send up glory, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen."**

*And thus he gives communion to the sick person.[^1336] And after holy communion he says this thanksgiving prayer of Saint Cyril of Alexandria, from the person of him who has received:*[^1337]

**"May Thy holy Body, O Lord Jesus Christ, be unto me for life and sanctification; and Thy precious Blood for the cleansing of sins; and at Thy holy judgment set me on Thy right hand; and let this thanksgiving be unto me for joy and health, and for the healing of soul and body; for Thou art blessed unto ages. Amen."**

*A prayer of Saint John Chrysostom, to the same effect:*[^1338]

**"O Master God, King of the ages and Creator of all, Who hast bestowed upon us good things, through the communion of Thine all-pure and life-creating Mysteries: I pray Thee, O Good One and Lover of mankind, preserve me under the shelter of Thy wings, and grant me with a pure conscience, unto my last breath, worthily to partake of Thy holy things, unto the remission of sins, and unto life"** everlasting. For Thou art the Bread of life, and the Fountain of holiness, and the Giver of good things; and unto Thee do we send up glory, with Thine unoriginate Father, and with Thine all-holy and good and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

*Prayer to the all-holy Theotokos, after communion:*[^1339]

O all-blessed Lady, beseech thy Son and our God, that we may be delivered from every evil; for through thee we have known the Son of God, and have been accounted worthy to be partakers of His precious and all-pure Body and Blood, hoping by thy prayers to attain unto life everlasting, glorifying the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

*Then:* **"Glory... Both now..."** *and the dismissal.*

### Notes

[^1318]: The Order of Administering Holy Communion to the Sick in Haste (Church Slavonic: *Чинъ како подобаетъ болному дати прича́стїе въско́рѣ*; Greek: *Ἀκολουθία εἰς τὸ μεταδοῦναι τῷ ἀσθενεῖ ταχέως*). This ancient office provides an abbreviated rite for administering the pre-sanctified reserved Eucharist (Viaticum) when a Christian is in imminent danger of death. Striatyn 1606, fol. 107r.
[^1319]: Slavonic *тр҃сто́е* (abbreviated *трисвятое*). The customary Initial Prayers (Trisagion through the Lord's Prayer) concluded by the priestly doxological exclamation (*"For Thine is the kingdom..."*).
[^1320]: The three-fold invitation to worship (*"Come, let us worship and fall down before Christ..."*) followed by the recitation of the entire Nicene-Constantinopolitan Symbol of Faith (*Credo*), confirming the communicant's steadfast profession of the Orthodox faith before receiving the Holy Mysteries.
[^1321]: The liturgical troparion chanted on Great and Holy Thursday and before the reception of Holy Communion: *"Of Thy Mystical Supper, O Son of God, receive me today as a communicant..."* (Greek: *Τοῦ δείπνου σου τοῦ μυστικοῦ σήμερον, Υἱὲ Θεοῦ, κοινωνόν με παράλαβε*). Striatyn 1606, fol. 107r.
[^1322]: The prayer of the Good Thief on the cross (Luke 23:42).
[^1323]: The Dogmatic Theotokion of Tone 8 (*"The King of heaven out of love for mankind appeared on earth..."* / Greek: *Ὁ Βασιλεὺς τῶν οὐρανῶν, διὰ φιλανθρωπίαν, ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς ὤφθη*), affirming the hypostatic union and two unconfused natures in Christ (*"double in nature, but not in person"* / Slavonic: *сꙋгꙋ́бъ є҆ство́мъ, а҆ не съста́вомъ*), chanted here to fortify the sick communicant in Orthodox Christology. Striatyn 1606, fol. 107v.
[^1324]: Theotokion: *"We have known God incarnate of thee, O Virgin Theotokos; do thou beseech Him for the salvation of our souls."*
[^1325]: A solemn priestly prayer of benediction and spiritual fortification over the sick communicant, invoking the Lord's mercy, teaching, illumination, defense, forgiveness, and salvation at the Day of Judgment. Striatyn 1606, fol. 108r.
[^1326]: Rubric: *"A second prayer from the person of the sick one."* In the Ruthenian and Byzantine Euchologia, when the sick person is capable of speech, he prays this confession directly; if incapacitated, the priest reads it in the first person on his behalf. Striatyn 1606, fol. 108v.
[^1327]: John 6:56: *"He that eats My flesh, and drinks My blood, dwells in Me, and I in Him."*
[^1328]: Rubric: *"A third prayer, similar to the second."* Striatyn 1606, fol. 108v.
[^1329]: Prayer attributed to St. John Chrysostom before Holy Communion: *"I know that I am not worthy that Thou shouldest enter under the roof of the temple of my soul, for it is all desolate and fallen..."* (cf. Matthew 8:8 / Luke 7:6). Striatyn 1606, fol. 109r.
[^1330]: Allusion to Matthew 8:20 and Luke 9:58: *"the Son of man hath not where to lay His head."*
[^1331]: The Prayer against Any Defilement (Greek: *Εὐχὴ ἐπὶ πάσης κηλῖδος*), prescribed to be read before communion if the communicant has suffered any nocturnal pollution, contact with impure substances, or demonic vexation. Striatyn 1606, fol. 109v.
[^1332]: Allusion to Acts 10:9–16, the vision of the sheet granted to the Apostle Peter on the rooftop in Joppa, commanding him to call nothing common or unclean which God has cleansed. Striatyn 1606, fol. 109v.
[^1333]: Allusion to Acts 9:15 (*"a chosen vessel unto Me"*) and Titus 1:15 (*"Unto the pure all things are pure"*).
[^1334]: Church Slavonic *потко́ръ* (from *потка́ти / потка́тися*, to stumble, encounter, or suffer an offense / affront / temptation); referring to demonic stumbling blocks or temptations. Striatyn 1606, fol. 110r.
[^1335]: An elevated liturgical petition describing the Holy Eucharist as the awful mystery celebrated in concert with the heavenly angelic ranks (*"this awesome ministry of the heavenly orders"*). Striatyn 1606, fol. 110v.
[^1336]: Rubric specifying the immediate administration of the Holy Gifts to the sick person with the liturgical spoon. Striatyn 1606, fol. 111r.
[^1337]: Thanksgiving prayer attributed to St. Cyril of Alexandria (*"Thy holy Body, O Lord Jesus Christ, be unto me for life and sanctification..."*), an ancient post-communion troparion preserved in Ruthenian and Byzantine euchologia. Striatyn 1606, fol. 111r.
[^1338]: Post-communion prayer of thanksgiving attributed to St. John Chrysostom (*"Master God, King of the ages and Creator of all..."* / Greek: *Δέσποτα Θεέ, Βασιλεῦ τῶν αἰώνων*). Striatyn 1606, fol. 111v.
[^1339]: Fol. 221r (`ск҃і`). A post-communion Thanksgiving prayer to the Mother of God (*Моли́тва къ прест҃ѣ́й Бц҃и, по причаще́нїи: Преблаже́нная Госпоже́...*), concluding the expedited order of holy communion for the sick.
