# PART XLVI: THE ORDER OVER CHILDREN DEPARTING TO LEARN THE SACRED SCRIPTURES [^3792]

*The priest having made the beginning; Trisagion; All-holy Trinity; Our Father; For Thine is the kingdom; then these troparia:*[^3793]

*Troparion:* As Thou didst come into the midst of Thy disciples, O Savior, and didst give them peace:[^3794] come also unto us and save us.

*Troparion:* Thy Holy Spirit, O Christ God, showed the unlettered disciples to be teachers, and diminished the delusion of the many-tongued through the diversity of tongues, as Thou art all-powerful.[^3795] With foreign tongues didst Thou renew Thy disciples, O Christ, that through them they might proclaim Thee, the Word and God, Who grantest great mercy unto our souls.[^3796]

*Troparion to the Holy Spirit:* O Heavenly King, Comforter, Spirit of truth, Who art everywhere present and fillest all things, Treasury of blessings and Giver of life: come and dwell in us, and cleanse us from every stain, and save our souls, O Good One.[^3797]

*Theotokion:* Open unto us the door of compassion, O blessed Virgin Theotokos! Hoping in thee, may we not fail, but may we be delivered by thee from adversities, for thou art the salvation of the Christian race.[^3798]

*After these the priest says the prayer over the head of the child:*

**Priest:** Let us pray to the Lord.[^3799]

**Priest:** O Lord Jesus Christ our God, Who didst dwell unfeignedly in the hearts of the twelve Apostles through the grace of the all-holy Spirit, Who descended in the appearance of fiery tongues,[^3800] and Thou didst open their lips, and they began to speak with other tongues:[^3801] do Thou Thyself, O Lord Jesus Christ our God, send this Thy Holy Spirit upon this child *[Name]*, and plant in the ears of his heart the Holy Scriptures, which Thy most pure hand did engrave upon the tablets for Moses the Lawgiver;[^3802] now and ever, and unto ages of ages. [Amen.][^3803]

*Another prayer:*

**Priest:** O Lord our God, Who didst dwell in and enlighten the hearts of the four and twenty elders,[^3804] and Thy holy disciples and Apostles, and Thy holy Apostles and Evangelists, John, Matthew, Mark, and Luke;[^3805] Who didst instruct and enlighten the holy First-Martyr Stephen:[^3806] do Thou Thyself also now, O Lord, enlighten the heart of Thy servant *[Name]*; through the prayers of our most blessed Lady, the Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary; by the power of the precious and life-creating Cross; of the precious, glorious Prophet, Forerunner, and Baptist John; of the holy, glorious, and all-praised Apostles; of the holy Apostle and Evangelist Matthew; of the holy Martyrs Agapitus, Procopius, and Philetus;[^3807] and of all the saints: for Thou art the illumination of our souls and bodies and unto Thee do we send up glory, with Thine unoriginate Father, and Thine all-holy, good, and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. [Amen.][^3808]

**Priest:** O Lord our God, Who didst honor us men with Thine image, and didst clothe us with self-determining will;[^3809] Who didst enter into the Temple in the midst of the feast and didst teach the people, while the people marveled and said, "From whence does this man know the Scriptures, having never learned?";[^3810] Who didst enlighten David to say: "Come, ye children, give ear unto me: I will teach you the fear of the Lord";[^3811] Who didst teach Solomon wisdom:[^3812] O God of all things, Word and King of all open the soul and heart, the mouth and mind of Thy servant *[Name]*, that he may understand and receive instruction, and do Thy will; and deliver him from every assault of the devil,[^3813] preserving him all the days of his life; and may he be prospering in all Thy commandments continually; through the prayers of Thy most pure Mother, and of all Thy saints: for Thou art the Overseer of our souls and bodies, O Christ God, and unto Thee do we send up glory, with the Father and the Most-holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^3814]

**Deacon:** Let us stand aright! Let us stand with the fear of God! Amen.[^3815]

*Then the litany and dismissal.*[^3816]

### Notes

[^3792]: Headpiece and title marking the beginning of Part XLVI: The Order over Children Departing to Learn the Sacred Scriptures (Въслѣдова́нїе на́дъ ѿроки ѿходѧ́щими ᲂу҆чи́тисѧ сщ҃еннымъ писа́нїамъ; Greek Ἀκολουθία εἰς παῖδας ἀπερχομένους μανθάνειν τὰ ἱερὰ γράμματα). This educational blessing rite reflects the humanist and ecclesiastical pedagogical revival in early 17th-century Ukraine and Belarus, where brotherhood schools (bratstva) and episcopal academies provided intensive instruction in Church Slavonic, Greek, Latin, and Scripture.
[^3793]: Introductory rubrics prescribing the standard opening blessing and Trisagion prayers, followed by festive and pedagogical troparia.
[^3794]: John 20:19, 26: Christ's post-Resurrection greeting of peace to the disciples in the Upper Room ("Peace be unto you"), here invoked to bring peace and clarity of mind to young students.
[^3795]: Pentecost troparion celebrating the miraculous transformation of the unlettered fishermen into teachers of the universe through the grace of the Holy Spirit: Безъ кни́жныѧ ᲂу҆ч҃нки дх҃ъ твои ст҃ый наказа́телѧ ꙗ҆вѝ Хе Бж҃е (cf. Acts 4:13: ἀγράμματοί εἰσι καὶ ἰδιῶται), overcoming the confusion of the nations through the inspired diversity of languages (Acts 2:1–11). The office continues onto fol. 618r (leaf p1251).
[^3796]: Continuation of the Pentecost troparion from fol. 617v (leaf p1250) on the descent of the Holy Spirit, alluding to Acts 2:4–11, where the Apostles were endowed with foreign tongues to proclaim Christ as the incarnate Word of God.
[^3797]: The venerable Byzantine prayer to the Holy Spirit (Greek Βασιλεῦ οὐράνιε, Παράκλητε), invoking the Spirit of Wisdom to enlighten the mind and soul of the young scholar.
[^3798]: Ancient Byzantine supplicatory Theotokion (Greek Τῆς εὐσπλαγχνίας τὴν πύλην ἄνοιξον ἡμῖν), imploring the Mother of God for spiritual protection against intellectual and moral stumbling.
[^3799]: Rubric prescribing the imposition of hands or position of the priest over the head of the child during the recitation of the educational blessing prayers.
[^3800]: Acts 2:3: "And there appeared unto them cloven tongues like as of fire, and it sat upon each of them."
[^3801]: Acts 2:4: "And they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and began to speak with other tongues, as the Spirit gave them utterance."
[^3802]: Exod. 31:18; 32:16; 34:1: "written with the finger of God" upon the tables of stone given to Moses the Lawgiver upon Mount Sinai.
[^3803]: The printed Slavonic witness truncates the concluding liturgical formula to нн҃ѣ и҆ прⷭ҇но и҆ въ вѣ́ки вѣкѡ́:~, which is supplied diplomatically.
[^3804]: Apoc. / Rev. 4:4, 10; 5:8, 14: the twenty-four elders surrounding the throne of God in the celestial liturgy, invoked here as exemplars of heavenly wisdom and spiritual illumination. The prayer continues onto fol. 619r (leaf p1253).
[^3805]: The four holy Evangelists are explicitly commemorated as the primary human instruments of divine revelation and the study of the New Testament.
[^3806]: Acts 6:8–10: "And Stephen, full of faith and power, did great wonders and miracles among the people... and they were not able to resist the wisdom and the spirit by which he spoke."
[^3807]: The invocation of the holy Martyrs Agapitus, Procopius, and Philetus represents a distinctive Byzantine-Slavic pedagogical patronage grouping, frequently invoked in historical euchologia for steadfastness of memory, quickness of intellect, and protection of students.
[^3808]: Concluding Trinitarian doxology of the second pedagogical blessing prayer.
[^3809]: Gen. 1:26–27; Sirach (Ecclus.) 15:14: man created in the image of God with rational free will and self-determination (самовла́стное хотѣ́нїе; Greek αὐτεξούσιος προαίρεσις).
[^3810]: John 7:14–15: "Now about the midst of the feast Jesus went up into the temple, and taught. And the Jews marveled, saying, How knows this man letters, having never learned?"
[^3811]: Ps. 33:12 LXX (Ps. 34:11 KJV): "Come, ye children, give ear unto me: I will teach you the fear of the Lord."
[^3812]: 3 Kings / 1 Kings 3:9–12; 4:29–34; Wis. 7:7: "Therefore I prayed, and understanding was given me: I called upon God, and the spirit of wisdom came to me."
[^3813]: Supplication for intellectual lucidity, moral uprightness, and spiritual protection against demonic obstruction or temptation (нало́гъ дїа́волъ, Greek ἐπήρεια τοῦ διαβόλου) during schooling.
[^3814]: Concluding ecphonesis of the prayer for a student beginning instruction.
[^3815]: Deaconal or priestly admonition (Greek Στῶμεν καλῶς, στῶμεν μετὰ φόβου Θεοῦ) exhorting attentiveness and reverent posture before the concluding litany.
[^3816]: Concluding rubrics prescribing the standard litany of fervent supplication (ektenia) and the liturgical dismissal (apolysis), marking the end of Part XLVI.
