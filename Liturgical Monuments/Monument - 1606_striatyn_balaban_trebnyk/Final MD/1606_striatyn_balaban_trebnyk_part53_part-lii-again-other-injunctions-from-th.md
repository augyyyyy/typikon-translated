# PART LII: AGAIN: OTHER INJUNCTIONS FROM THE BOOKS OF THE LAW: CONCERNING DEGREES OF KINSHIP [^3954]

*Concerning Forbidden Marriages and concerning the Diverse Degrees of Lineage Which It Is Befitting to Guard, from Holy Baptism, and concerning That Which Is by[^3955]* flesh and blood.[^3956] Together with this, there is also kinship with marital in-laws, and it is spoken of among men. And it is divided into three orders: into the ascending, and into the descending, and into the collateral.[^3957]

The ascending are those who have begotten and do beget: that is, father, grandfathers, and grandmothers. And the descending are those begotten of us: that is, sons, and daughters, and grandsons, and granddaughters. And the collateral are, as is manifestly said, those holding lineage together with us: that is, brothers, and sisters, and those [descended] from them.[^3958]

And three are the orders of boundaries forbidding related persons in marriage: One order and first boundary: that which is from holy and salvific baptism. The second order and boundary:[^3959]

...that of those manifestly recognized in person according to the kinship of the blood of the flesh.[^3960]

The third order and boundary: that of those recognized from marital affinity; that is, of two-fold in-laws: first, and second, and third.[^3961]

And their degrees, and boundaries, and rules have many distinctions, and rigorous examinations, which it is befitting for us true Christians very diligently to examine concerning marriages; and by no means unto the end should there be found even a lower degree of lineage [than permitted]; and only then may a Christian marriage be accomplished, as is fitting for the servants of God to be, and for such a marriage to be blessed by God: whether with a written contract or without a written contract, only according to the testimony[^3962] of many witnesses, there being testimony of men and women.[^3963]

And when a man is fifteen years of age, so let him enter into marriage; and when a woman is twelve years of age, so let her also enter into marriage; by the consent and desire of their parents, so that the union may be according to much testimony and with blessing.[^3964]

*Concerning Forbidden Marriages:*

That which is forbidden from holy baptism is this: A godfather from his baptized daughter [goddaughter]; and from the sister of that baptized daughter. But the other relatives of the father and mother of such a daughter are not forbidden to marry the relatives of the godfather.[^3965]

If anyone baptizes a maiden, and thereafter takes her as a bride for his son, let such be separated; and the parents who consented are to be excommunicated to do penance.[^3966]

Let it be known concerning relatives according to the flesh: parents, children unto parents; brothers unto brothers; and their children, called first cousins; likewise also their children, called second cousins; likewise also the children unto second cousins, which are third cousins; and unto all these it is not fitting to be joined in marriage.[^3967] But the children of third cousins may assemble and be joined in lawful marriage.[^3968]

*Concerning Degrees:*

Give ear and understand the first degree: as father unto son, and son unto father, are of the first degree, even as it was the first degree of begetting.[^3969]

And grandfather unto grandson are of the second degree, even as it was a degree of begetting. In like manner also a brother unto his own brother is of the second degree, even as there are two degrees; for it calls the begettings degrees.[^3970]

If anyone asks thee, saying: "Of what degree is thy father?" Say thou: "Of the first; for my father begot me, and this was the first begetting, and this is the first degree." And if he says unto thee: "Of what degree is thy grandfather?" Say thou unto him: "Of the second; for my grandfather begot my father, and that is one degree;[^3971]

...and my father begot me, and this is the first degree, but the second begetting; therefore of the second degree is my grandfather unto me."[^3972]

That we may speak manifestly: begin therefore to ascend upon a ladder, and thou desirest to step upon the first step; and this is that which we call the first, of the father. And thereafter from the first step thou shalt step ascending up higher upon the second step; and concerning this we say: my grandfather is the second step.[^3973]

And if he says unto thee: "And of what degree unto thee are thy brethren?" Say thou unto him: "That these also are of the second degree unto me; for my father begot me, and this was the first degree, one begetting; and thus he begot my brethren; and behold, two degrees."[^3974]

In like manner also first cousins [first brother's children], according to the law of kinship, are of the fourth degree; neither is it befitting, nor is it right to join them in marriage.[^3975]

Likewise also the children of first cousins, being second cousins [second brother's children], are of the sixth degree; neither is it befitting for these to marry one another. And if it shall happen that this marriage has taken place, the law commands that they be separated, and rather that they do penance.[^3976]

Likewise also the children of second cousins are third cousins [third brother's children], and are of the eighth degree; neither is it right for these to enter into marriage. But if it has happened from ignorance that such a marriage has taken place, or[^3977] *Concerning Forbidden Marriages:*

...or for the sake of some unknown matter, then let them not be separated now, but let them remain; nevertheless, it is fitting for them concerning such a sin to be diligent with almsgiving and fasting.[^3978] In knowledge, however, let heed be taken unto this, that it by no means take place. Leaving aside therefore the generation and kinship of third cousins from that point unto the eighth degree, and what follows: without any prohibition let them be taken into marriage; within the eighth degree, however, let there be no marriage, that is, for example: the eighth degree is called [the boundary] of the entire generation of third cousins; and if it should happen to take place through some ignorance, let them be separated.[^3979] These things, therefore, are concerning kinship, and[^3980] of natural blood; and concerning these we have written and established for certain knowledge.

*18. Concerning Relatives by Marriage:*[^3981]

That which pertains unto known relatives from marital kinship is this: If anyone takes a widow as wife, and that woman has a daughter by another husband: she is a daughter unto him also, even as unto that woman; and it is fitting for him to keep himself exceedingly from her, as from his very own daughter. In like manner also a father-in-law from the wife of his son; and a son from his stepmother; and a brother-in-law from the wife of his brother, and of a brother-in-law.[^3982]

Let a son look upon the face of his mother with great fear, and let him render honor unto her always. Let a father take heed unto himself concerning the gaze upon his daughter, and let him keep himself.[^3983] In like manner also *Concerning Forbidden Marriages:*

...let brethren take heed unto themselves from their sisters.

Concerning relatives by marriage of whom we spoke above: when they shall assemble at weddings so that marriage may be sought, let it be carefully investigated, lest marital kinship should happen to exist, that is to say, in-laws. It is fitting to search with certainty and with much examination, even as the Divine law commands.[^3984] For there are in-laws of the first, second, and third degree; it is fitting to take heed concerning these. It is fitting also to know concerning this: that unto each spouse, husband and wife, the parents indeed of the husband—the father, and the mother, and the brethren—and the parents of his wife and her brethren, are called the first in-laws.[^3985]

And the first cousins of the husband and of his wife: these are called second in-laws. And the second cousins of the wife and of the husband are again third in-laws.[^3986] And so the first in-laws are not taken in marriage unto the first in-laws, nor unto the second. In like manner also a son or a brother unto a husband does not mix with the first cousins of his wife; but the second cousins of the husband are taken and mixed without hindrance with the second and third cousins of his wife.[^3987]

Since therefore we have spoken concerning the degrees of lineage, and of natural blood, and of kinship, we say again: that until eight degrees of kinsmen and kinship are concluded, let heed be taken that there be no taking in marriage among them;[^3988] *Concerning the Degrees of Kinship:*

...and concerning kinship by marriage, that is, concerning in-laws: and these until the seventh degree is concluded, that is, of husband and wife.[^3989] If that woman has a first cousin, she is with the husband of that woman of the fourth degree. And the son of my first cousin is unto me a nephew, from my first cousin; and it is not fitting to take the first cousin of my wife.[^3990] Even as also my brother [cannot take] the sister of my wife, [nor] can he take my first cousin, nor my nephew, nor the nephew of my son, nor the first cousin of my son, nor the second cousins who are according to the lineage of my wife.[^3991] For a wife with her brethren are of the second degree; and her husband with[^3992] his own first cousin, are of the fourth degree; and the four degrees do not assemble together, nor can marital union exist within them, unless it reaches unto the measure and number of the seventh degree. Neither can the nephew of my wife take the daughter of my sister. For he is called the great maternal uncle who is the sister's brother, that is, brother and sister. And indeed the brother of my mother is unto me a great maternal uncle, and this is of the third degree by measure.[^3993] In like manner also the sister of my mother is unto me a great maternal aunt. And the first cousin of my mother or of my father is unto me a lesser maternal uncle and a lesser aunt.[^3994] And if there be my third cousins, or a son, *Of the Holy Fathers:*

...or a daughter, without hindrance let them be taken [in marriage] with the children, the third cousins of my wife.[^3995]

If anyone baptizes a child, and being godfather shall take him again as son-in-law unto his daughter: let them be separated; and the priest who blessed them, let him be deposed; for he is a godson according to the Holy Spirit.[^3996]

If anyone takes a wife, and lies with her, and thereafter defiles himself with his mother-in-law: then let that mother-in-law go and be tonsured in a monastery, and let her serve God concerning her soul; and let that husband live with his wife; let them labor concerning that sin for fifteen years, having received penance from their spiritual father.[^3997]

If anyone shall be the adoptive son of anyone, that is, an adopted son: such a one cannot defile himself with the wife of his named father, nor with his concubine, whom I call a paramour; therefore he is not to marry any woman whatsoever from the lineage of his named father.[^3998]

It is not fitting during the Holy Great Forty Days to perform dedications of martyrs, or baptisms, or crownings; except on Saturday and on Sunday to perform without hindrance the memory of saints, and for the departed, and baptisms. But if it should occur on account of mortal sickness, then on whatever day it may be, let the infant or anyone whatsoever be baptized.[^3999]

If anyone has a daughter by another wife, and thereafter comes and takes my sister as wife: then I no longer have [the right][^4000] *Of the Holy Fathers:*

...how to take and marry that daughter of his. And if she leaves her husband, and goes and takes another husband, and bears a daughter with him: then I have no way to marry that daughter, nor my son whom I have by another wife; for they are brethren. And if any woman whatsoever be betrothed unto my father, and thereafter, even if he has not lain with her: I have no way to marry her, for she is a mother unto me. In like manner also if she be the betrothed of my brother, even if he has not lain with her: yet she is a sister-in-law unto me, and I dare not touch her.[^4001]

The holy councils, of the holy Apostles, and of the venerable Fathers, commanded that a third marriage shall not exist. But if any priest, knowing this, blesses one who marries thrice: let him be deposed from the priesthood.[^4002]

If anyone does not bear children with his first wife, nor with his second: then let him now desist from marriage; if his second wife dies unto him, let him thus remain in purity in the Lord. But if being yet thirty-five or forty years of age, he marries again a third time: let such a one be forbidden unto repentance for five years, and thereafter let him receive communion. But if being young, he is thirty years of age: then let him do penance for three years. But if he is forty-five or fifty years old, and does this: the Holy Church of God does not receive such a one, as defiled by deceit.[^4003]

*Of the Holy Fathers:*

...and it forbids him for seven years to repent with great repentance; and then let him receive communion on holy Pascha. But if any priest dares to give him communion before he finishes seven years in repentance: then let him again begin to repent, that he may complete seven years. And that priest, if he did this with knowledge: let him be forbidden to serve for a certain time; for it is not befitting for a priest to be a mocker of God. As for those who say that it was commanded and that the Holy Church of God receives one who has married thrice, and one who has married four times: Anathema.[^4004]

*Concerning the Spiritual Father:*[^4005]

Take heed, therefore, O spiritual father: examine well concerning sins, how each one *Injunctions:*

...has zeal to repent. And unto the strong who have zeal, entrust two parts of the rule commanded unto them by the Scripture, and leave the third part upon thyself; but unto the weak and those who have not zeal, entrust half of what is commanded unto them, and half upon thyself.[^4006] And it is befitting for thee day and night to cast [thy care] upon God, and to pray unto Him for those who confess unto thee.[^4007] It is befitting also for thee to make for them twelve prostrations by day and by night:[^4008] and at the Midnight Office, three prostrations; and at Compline, three; and at Matins, three; and at the Hours [three]; so that from the snares of the enemy thou mayest be free, and mayest deliver others from demonic bonds[^4009] *Of the Holy Fathers:*

...deliver. But if thou art not worthy thus to do and to pray for them, and much more not even for thyself: then neither attempt the path of the spiritual fatherhood, nor be called [spiritual father], lest there be woe unto thee: ❧[^4010]

[^4000]: Syntactic continuity banner: the hypothetical case of reciprocal family marriage at the foot of leaf p1308 continues onto leaf p1309 ("...то̀ оу҆жѐ не и҆ма́мъ а҆́зъ ка́ко вза́ти и҆ посѧгноу́ти ѻ҆́нꙋ дще́ръ е҆го̀").
[^4001]: Betrothal (*obruchnitsa*) as a canonical impediment: under Byzantine-Slavic law (Trullan Canon 98; St. Basil Canon 78), formal ecclesiastical betrothal creates an affinal bond equivalent to consummated marriage, permanently barring the father or brother from marrying the betrothed maiden.
[^4002]: Canon law on third and fourth marriages: Council of Neocaesarea Canon 3, St. Basil Canons 4 and 50, and the Tomus Unionis (920 AD) of Patriarch Nicholas Mystikos. A fourth marriage is absolutely banned as polygamy; a third marriage is tolerated only under narrow restrictions; a priest who blesses an unlawful third marriage is deposed.
[^4003]: Penance and age thresholds for third marriages from the Tomus Unionis (920 AD): childless persons under 40 may receive a five-year penance; young persons under 30 receive three years penance; those aged 45 to 50 attempting a third marriage are excluded from communion as having acted out of lustful deceit.
[^4004]: Penance for trigamists and tetragamists (*троєбра́чнаго, и҆ че́тверобра́чнаго*): Byzantine canonical jurisprudence strictly regulated repeated marriages. While digamy was permitted with penance, trigamy was regarded as barely tolerable under severe canonical sanctions (cf. St. Basil the Great, *Letter 217*, Canon 79, imposing three to four years of excommunication; St. Nicephorus the Confessor, Canon 2; and the *Tomus Unionis* of 920 issued under Patriarch Nicholas I Mystikos, which permanently banned fourth marriages and restricted third marriages to persons under forty with substantial periods of exclusion from communion). The synodal anathema cited here reaffirms the strict Byzantine-Slavic nomocanonical consensus against treating digamy and trigamy as normal or unencumbered unions.
[^4005]: *Ѡ҆ дх҃о́вномъ ѻ҆ц҃и* ("Concerning the Spiritual Father"): Heading introducing pastoral directives for confessors (*дх҃о́вникъ*). The Nomocanon requires spiritual fathers to exercise discernment (*дѣ́ло разсꙋжде́нїѧ*) rather than applying penitential canons mechanically.
[^4006]: Pastoral apportionment of penitential discipline: The text reflects the ancient Slavic and Byzantine practice of spiritual co-bearing of burdens (cf. Gal. 6:2), wherein the confessor personally assumes a fraction of the penance—one third for spiritually robust penitents (*си́лнымъ оу҆сръ́дїе и҆моу́щимъ*), and half for the spiritually infirm (*немо́щнымъ и҆ не и҆моу́щимъ оу҆сръ́дїа*)—interceding before God through personal ascetical prayer and prostrations.
[^4007]: Injunction of unceasing pastoral intercession: The spiritual father is commanded day and night to cast his care upon God (*дн҃ь и҆ но́щъ на́ Бг҃а възлага́ти*) and pray continuously on behalf of his penitents (*и҆ моли́тисѧ е҆моу̀ ѡ҆ и҆сповѣ́дающих́тисѧ*).
[^4008]: Daily rule of prostrations for the confessor: A prescribed daily total of twelve prostrations (*двана́десѧтъ покло́нъ*), distributed symmetrically across the canonical offices: three at the Midnight Office (*по́лоу́щница*), three at Compline (*па́вечерница*), three at Matins (*оу҆́треня*), and three at the Hours (*часы̀*).
[^4009]: Deliverance from spiritual warfare: The ascetical labor of the confessor provides double protection: keeping the priest free from demonic delusion and traps (*ѿ събла́знъ вра́жїихъ ты́ свобо́д быва́еши*), while unlocking penitents from diabolical bonds (*и҆ и҆нѣ́хъ ѿ бѣсо́вськыхъ съ́юзъ свобожда́еши*).
[^4010]: Warning against unauthorized or unqualified spiritual fatherhood: Echoing the canonical strictures of the Nomocanon in 14 Titles and the Syntagma of Matthew Blastares, priests who lack the ascetical maturity, personal purity, and continuous prayer life to sustain penitents are forbidden to assume the ministry of confessor, lest they bring spiritual disaster upon themselves (*да не го́ре ти бꙋ́детъ*).
