# PART XVI: THE ORDER OF HOLY COMMUNION [^893]

*If a priest, he says:* Blessed is our God...[^894] *If not a priest, the verse:* Through the prayers of our holy fathers, Lord Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us, amen.[^895] *And after the Amen, we begin:* Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee.[^896] *O Heavenly King... Trisagion... After "Our Father...":* For Thine is the kingdom... Lord, have mercy, 12.[^897] *Come, let us worship... 3.*[^898] *Then:* *Psalm 15.*[^899]

Preserve me, O Lord, for in Thee have I hoped.[^900] I said unto the Lord: Thou art my Lord, for of my goods Thou hast no need.[^901] Unto the saints that are in His earth, the Lord hath made marvelous all His desires in them.[^902] Their infirmities were multiplied; after these things they made haste. I will not assemble their assemblies of blood, neither will I make mention of their names with my lips.[^903] The Lord is the portion of mine inheritance and of my cup; Thou art He that restorest mine inheritance unto me.[^904] The lines are fallen unto me among my strongholds; for mine inheritance is strong unto me.[^905] I will bless the Lord Who hath given me understanding; moreover even unto the night my reins have instructed me.[^906]

I beheld the Lord ever before me, for He is at my right hand, that I may not be moved.[^907] Therefore my heart was glad, and my tongue rejoiced; moreover my flesh also shall dwell in hope.[^908] For Thou wilt not leave my soul in Hades, neither wilt Thou suffer Thine Holy One to see corruption.[^909] Thou hast made known to me the ways of life; Thou shalt fill me with joy with Thy countenance; pleasures are in Thy right hand for evermore.[^910]

*Psalm 21:*[^911]

O God, my God, attend unto me; why hast Thou forsaken me?[^912] Far from my salvation are the words of my transgressions.[^913] O my God, I will cry by day, and Thou wilt not hear; and by night, and [it shall] not [be counted] for[^914] folly unto me.[^915] But Thou dwellest in the holy place, O Praise of Israel.[^916] In Thee our fathers hoped; they hoped, and Thou didst deliver them.[^917] Unto Thee they cried, and were saved; in Thee they hoped, and were not put to shame.[^918] But I am a worm, and no man; a reproach of men, and the outcast of the people.[^919] All they that saw me laughed me to scorn; they spoke with their lips, they shook the head:[^920] He hoped in the Lord, let Him deliver him; let Him save him, for He desires him.[^921] For Thou art He that drewest me forth from the womb, my hope from my mother's breasts.[^922] Unto Thee was I cast from the womb; from my mother's belly Thou art my God.[^923]

Depart not from Me, for affliction is near; for there is none to help Me.[^924] Many bullocks have compassed Me; fat bulls have beset Me round. They have opened their mouth against Me, as a ravening and a roaring lion. I am poured out like water, and all My bones are scattered.[^925] My heart is become like wax, melting in the midst of My belly. My strength is dried up like a potsherd, and My tongue cleaves to My throat; and Thou hast brought Me down into the dust of death. For many dogs have compassed Me; the assembly of the wicked hath beset Me round.[^926] They have pierced My hands and My feet; they have numbered all My bones.

My bones; they looked and stared upon Me. They parted My garments among them, and upon My vesture did they cast lots.[^927] But Thou, O Lord, remove not Thy help far from Me; attend unto My defense. Deliver My soul from the sword, and Mine only-begotten from the hand of the dog. Save Me from the mouth of the lion, and My lowliness from the horns of the unicorns.[^928] I will declare Thy name unto My brethren; in the midst of the church will I sing praise unto Thee. Ye that fear the Lord, praise Him; all ye seed of Jacob, glorify Him; let all the seed of Israel fear Him.[^929] For He hath not despised nor abhorred the prayer of the poor; neither hath He turned away His face from Me; but when I cried unto Him, He gave ear unto Me.[^930] From Thee is My praise; in the great church will I confess unto Thee; My vows will I pay before them that fear Him. The poor shall eat and be satisfied, and they that seek the Lord shall praise Him; their heart shall live unto ages of ages.[^931] All the ends of the earth shall remember and turn unto the Lord; and all the kindreds of the nations shall worship before Him. For the kingdom is the Lord's, and He is the ruler over the nations. All the fat ones of the earth have eaten and worshipped; all they that go down into the earth shall fall down before Him.[^932] And My soul unto Him shall live, and My seed shall serve Him.[^933] The generation to come shall be declared unto the Lord; and they shall declare His righteousness unto a people that shall be born, whom the Lord hath made.

*Psalm 22.*[^934]

The Lord is my shepherd, and nothing shall be lacking unto me.[^935] In a place of green pasture, there hath He made me to dwell; by the water of rest hath He nourished me. He hath converted my soul; He hath guided me into the paths of righteousness for His name's sake. For though I should walk through the midst of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for Thou art with me; Thy rod and Thy staff, they have comforted me.[^936] Thou hast prepared a table before me against them that afflict me.[^937] Thou hast anointed my head with oil, and Thy cup which inebriates me, how excellent it is! And Thy mercy shall pursue me all the days of my life, and that I may dwell in the house of the Lord unto length of days.

*Psalm 23.*[^938]

The earth is the Lord's, and the fullness thereof; the world, and all that dwell therein.[^939] He hath founded it upon the seas, and prepared it upon the rivers. Who shall ascend into the mountain of the Lord, or who shall stand in His holy place? He that is innocent in hands and pure in heart; who hath not received his soul in vain, nor sworn deceitfully unto his neighbor. He shall receive a blessing from the Lord, and mercy from God his Savior.[^940] This is the generation of them that seek the Lord, that seek the face of the God of Jacob. Lift up your gates, O ye princes, and be ye lifted up, ye everlasting gates; and the King of glory shall enter in.[^941] Who is this King of glory? The Lord strong and mighty, the Lord mighty in battle. Lift up your gates, O ye princes, and be ye lifted up, ye everlasting gates; and the King of glory shall enter in. Who is this King of glory? The Lord of hosts, He is the King of glory.

*Psalm 115.*[^942]

I believed, therefore have I spoken; but I was humbled exceedingly.[^943] I said in mine ecstasy: Every man is a liar. What shall I render unto the Lord for all that He hath rendered unto me?[^944] I will take the cup of salvation, and will call upon the name of the Lord. My vows will I pay unto the Lord in the presence of all His people. Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of His saints.[^945] O Lord, I am Thy servant; I am Thy servant and the son of Thine handmaid; Thou hast broken my bonds asunder. To Thee will I offer the sacrifice of praise, and will call upon the name of the Lord. My vows will I pay unto the Lord in the presence of all His people, in the courts of the Lord's house, in the midst of thee, O Jerusalem.

*Glory... Both now...*

*Alleluia, thrice. And these Troparia, Tone 8:*[^946]

Overlook mine iniquities, O Lord Who wast born of the Virgin, and cleanse my heart, making it a temple for Thine all-pure[^947] Body and Blood; neither cast Thou me away from Thy presence, O Thou Who without measure hast great mercy.[^948]

*Glory...*[^949]

Into the communion of Thy holy things how shall I, the wretched one, dare to enter?[^950] For if I dare to approach Thee with the worthy, my garment convicts me, for it is not a wedding garment, and condemnation do I procure for my most sinful soul. Cleanse, O Lord, the defilement of my soul, and save me, as Thou art the Lover of mankind.

*Both now...*[^951]

Great is the multitude of my transgressions, O Theotokos;[^952] unto thee have I fled, O all-pure one, requiring salvation. Visit my ailing soul, and pray unto thy Son and our God, that He grant unto me remission of the evils which I have done, O only blessed one.[^953]

*Then Psalm 50.*[^954]

*Have mercy on me, O God. Canon for Holy Communion. Tone 2. Ode 1. Heirmos:*[^955]

*Come, ye peoples.*

*Verse:* Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.[^956]

May Thy holy Body and precious Blood, O compassionate Lord, be unto me the Bread of eternal life, and healing of manifold infirmities.[^957]

*Verse:* Cast me not away from Thy presence, and take not Thy Holy Spirit from me.[^958]

Vouchsafe me, an unworthy one, O Master, to receive and eat with longing Thine all-pure Blood and Thy most holy Body,[^959] and with faith to drink.[^960]

*Glory...*[^961]

Defiled by improper deeds, wretched that I am, and unworthy of the communion of Thine all-pure Body and divine Blood, do Thou, O Christ, make me worthy thereof.[^962]

*Both now...*[^963]

O good and blessed Bride of God, who hast brought forth the unplowed Ear of grain that is salvation to the world: vouchsafe that I, eating thereof, may be saved.[^964]

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*

*Establish me upon the rock of faith.*[^965]

*Verse:* Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me.

Grant me drops of tears, O Christ, purifying the defilement of my heart,[^966] that being cleansed with a good conscience, I may come in faith and fear, O Master, unto the communion of Thy Gifts.[^967]

*Verse:* Cast me not away from Thy presence, and [take not Thy Holy Spirit from me].[^968]

May Thy most pure Body and divine Blood be unto me for the remission of sins, O Lover of mankind, and for communion of the Holy Spirit, and unto life eternal, and for estrangement from passions and afflictions.[^969]

*Glory...*[^970]

Vouchsafe us, O Lord, to partake without condemnation of Thy Body and of Thy precious Blood, and to hymn Thy goodness.[^971]

*Both now...*[^972]

O most pure table of the Bread of life that came down from on high for mercy's sake, and gives new life unto the world: vouchsafe even me now, unworthy though I am,[^973] to taste thereof with fear, and to live.[^974]

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 2:*

May the reception of Thine all-pure and life-creating Mysteries be fire and light unto me, O Savior, consuming the tares of sins, and enlightening all of me unto true theology; not betraying the Holy Things to enemies in fierce surrender, nor will I give Thee a deceitful kiss; but like the harlot I fall down before Thee, and like the thief confessing I cry unto Thee: Remember me, O Lord, when Thou comest into Thy kingdom.[^975]

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*

*Having come from the Virgin.*[^976]

*Verse:* Create in me a clean heart, O God; [and renew a right spirit within me].[^977]

Thou wast pleased for our sake, having become incarnate,[^978] O most merciful One, to be slain as a sheep for the sins of men; therefore I pray Thee to wash away also my transgressions.[^979]

*Verse:* Cast me not away from Thy presence, [and take not Thy Holy Spirit from me].[^980]

Heal the wounds of my soul, O Lord, and sanctify all of me; and vouchsafe, O Master, that I, wretched though I am, may be a partaker of Thy mystical and divine Supper.[^981]

*Glory...*[^982]

Let us all stand with fear and trembling, having the eyes of our heart directed on high, and crying unto the Savior: Establish and give understanding unto us in Thy fear, O merciful God.[^983]

*Both now...*[^984]

Make Thy compassionate Son merciful also unto me, O Sovereign Lady, and preserve me, thy servant, without sorrow and without[^985] blemish, that having received within me the spiritual Pearl, I may be sanctified.[^986]

*Ode 5. Heirmos:*

*O Giver of light.*[^987]

*Verse:* Create in me a clean heart, O God; and [renew a right spirit within me].[^988]

As Thou hast foretold, O Christ, so let it be unto Thy humble servant, and abide in me as Thou didst promise; for behold, I eat Thy divine Body and drink Thy Blood.[^989]

*Verse:* Cast me not away from Thy presence, [and take not Thy Holy Spirit from me].[^990]

O Word of God and God, may Thy divine Body be a live coal unto me, for the enlightenment of me who am darkened, and Thy Blood for the cleansing of my defiled soul.[^991]

*Glory...*[^992]

Having an all-defiled soul and unclean lips, I dare not now draw near unto Thee,[^993] O Christ, and receive Thy Body; but do Thou show me worthy.[^994]

*Both now...*[^995]

O Mary, Mother of God, venerable dwelling of sweet fragrance: by thy prayers make me a chosen vessel, that I may partake of the holy Mysteries of thy Son.[^996]

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*

*In the abyss of sins.*[^997]

*Verse:* Create in me a clean heart, O God; and [renew a right spirit within me].[^998]

Sanctify my mind, soul, and heart, O Savior, and my body; and vouchsafe, O Master, that I may approach Thine awesome Mysteries without condemnation.[^999]

*Verse:* Cast me not away from Thy presence, [and take not Thy Holy Spirit from me].[^1000]

May I be estranged from passions, and be vouchsafed an increase of Thy grace, and the confirmation of life, by reason of the communion[^1001] of Thy holy Mysteries, O Christ.[^1002]

*Glory...*[^1003]

With fear and trembling let us all approach, and receive the holy Body of Christ, and His holy and precious Blood.[^1004]

*Both now...*[^1005]

O God, holy Word of God, sanctify all of me now as I come unto Thy divine Mysteries, by the supplications of thy holy Mother.[^1006]

*Kontakion, Tone 2, Podoben: "Seeking the highest" (Вы́шнихъ и҆ща̀):*

Despise me not, O Christ, as I take the Bread, which is Thy Body, and Thy divine Blood, and partake, wretched as I am, of Thine all-pure and dread Mysteries, O Master; let it not be unto me, wretched though I am, for judgment, but let it be unto me for life eternal and immortal.[^1007]

*Ode 7.*[^1008]

*Heirmos: Unto the golden image.*[^1009]

*Verse:* Create in me a clean heart, O God; and [renew a right spirit within me].[^1010]

May the communion of Thine immortal Mysteries now be unto me a fountain of good things, O Christ: light, and life, and impassibility, and leading unto advancement and increase in divine virtue, O Thou only Good One, that I may glorify Thee.[^1011]

*Verse:* Cast me not away from Thy presence, [and take not Thy Holy Spirit from me].[^1012]

Delivered from passions, and enemies, and necessity, and every affliction, as I now draw near with trembling and love, with reverence, O Lover of mankind, unto Thine immortal and divine Mysteries, may I sing unto Thee: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers.[^1013]

*Glory*[^1014] O my wretched soul, O my passionate soul, be dismayed beholding the most glorious Mysteries; sighing, weep, beating thy breast and saying: O God, cleanse me even as the prodigal.[^1015]

*Both now...*[^1016]

O Lady graced of God, who beyond understanding didst bear Christ the Savior: I, thine unclean servant, now entreat thee, who art pure: as I now desire to approach the most pure Mysteries, cleanse all of me from defilement of flesh and spirit.[^1017]

*Ode 8. Heirmos:*

*He who into the fiery furnace.*[^1018]

*Verse:* Create in me a clean heart, O God; and [renew a right spirit within me].[^1019]

Of Thy heavenly, dread, and holy Mysteries, O Christ, and of Thy divine and mystical Supper, a partaker[^1020] vouchsafe me to be, despondent though I am, O God my Savior.[^1021]

*Verse:* Cast me not away from Thy presence, [and take not Thy Holy Spirit from me].[^1022]

Fleeing unto Thy compassion, O Good One, with fear I cry unto Thee: Abide in me, O Savior, and I, even as Thou hast said, in Thee; for behold, trusting in Thy mercy, I eat Thy Body and drink Thy Blood.[^1023]

*Glory...*[^1024]

I tremble as I receive the Fire, lest I be burned up like wax and like grass! O dread Mysteries! O compassion of God! How do I, being clay, partake of the divine Body and Blood, and am made incorruptible![^1025]

*Both now...*[^1026]

The divine Bread of life was truly baked in thy womb, O Mother of God,[^1027] preserving unharmed thine undefiled belly; therefore, as our nourisher, we hymn thee unto all ages.[^1028]

*Ode 9. Heirmos:*

*The Son of the unoriginate Father.*[^1029]

*Verse:* Create in me a clean heart, O God; and [renew a right spirit within me].[^1030]

Christ is here; taste and see the Lord: for having of old become like us for our sake, and having once offered Himself as a sacrifice unto His Father, He is ever slain, sanctifying them that partake.[^1031]

*Verse:* Cast me not away from Thy presence, and take not Thy Holy Spirit from me.[^1032]

May I be sanctified in soul with body, O Master; may I be enlightened, may I be saved; may I become Thy dwelling by the communion of the sacred Mysteries, having Thee dwelling within me with the Father and the Spirit,[^1033] O most merciful Benefactor.[^1034]

*Glory...*

May Thy Body and Thy most precious Blood, O my Savior, be unto me as fire and as light, consuming the substance of sin, and burning up the thorns of passions, and enlightening all of me who worship Thy Divinity.[^1035]

*Both now...*

God was incarnate of thy pure blood; whence every generation hymns thee, O Sovereign Lady, and the bodiless multitudes glorify thee, as having through thee clearly beheld the Sovereign of all, substantialized in humanity.[^1036]

*Then: It is truly meet... Trisagion. And after Our Father... Troparion, Tone 8:*[^1037]

When the glorious disciples at the washing of the supper were illuminated, then was Judas the ungodly, diseased with the love of silver, darkened; and unto lawless judges he betrayed Thee, the righteous Judge.[^1038] Behold, O lover of possessions, him who on account of them acquired insatiability, and gave himself unto hanging![^1039] Flee, O insatiable soul, that dared such things against the Teacher! O Lord Who art good towards all, glory unto Thee!

*Lord, have mercy (40), and twelve prostrations, or unto each according to his strength. After this, all the prayers in order:*[^1040]

*Prayer of Basil the Great unto Holy Communion:*

O Master, Lord Jesus Christ our God, King of them that reign, and Lord of them that rule;[^1041] Fountain of life and immortality; Maker of all creation, visible and invisible; co-eternal and co-unoriginate Son of the unoriginate Father; Who for Thine exceeding goodness in the last days didst clothe Thyself in flesh, and wast crucified, and buried for us ungrateful and perverse ones,[^1042] and by Thine own Blood didst renew our nature corrupted by sin: Thyself, O immortal King, Master, Lover of mankind, accept my sinful repentance; incline Thine ear unto me, and hear my words.[^1043]

For I have sinned, O Lord, I have sinned against heaven and before Thee,[^1044] and I am not worthy to gaze upon the height of Thy glory; for I have provoked Thy goodness, having transgressed Thy commandments, and not having given ear unto Thy precepts. But Thou, O Lord, being forgiving, longsuffering, and plenteous in mercy, hast not given me over to perish with mine iniquities, awaiting my conversion by every means. For Thou, O Lover of mankind, hast said by Thy prophet: *As I live, saith the Lord, I desire not the death of the sinner, but rather that he turn and live.*[^1045] For Thou willest not, O Master, to destroy the creation of Thy hands, nor dost Thou take pleasure in the perdition of mankind, but Thou willest that all should be saved, and come unto the knowledge of the truth.[^1046] Therefore I also, though I am unworthy of heaven and of earth, and of this fleeting life, having wholly subjected myself unto sin, and enslaved myself unto pleasures, and defiled Thine image; yet being Thy creature and Thy creation, I, wretched one, despair not of my salvation. But trusting in Thine immeasurable compassion, I draw near. Receive therefore even me, O Lord, Lover of mankind, as the harlot, as the thief, as the publican, and as the prodigal;[^1047] and take away my heavy burden of iniquities, Thou Who takest away the sins of the world,[^1048] and healest the infirmities of men; Who callest unto Thyself the weary and heavy laden, and givest them rest; Who camest not to call the righteous, but sinners unto repentance:[^1049] cleanse Thou me from all defilement of flesh and spirit; and teach me to accomplish holiness in Thy fear;[^1050] that in the pure testimony of my conscience, receiving a portion of Thy Holy Things, I may be united unto Thy holy Body and Blood, and may have Thee living and abiding in me with the Father and with the Holy Spirit. Yea, O Lord Jesus Christ my God, let not the communion of Thine all-pure and life-creating Mysteries be unto me for judgment, nor may I become weak in soul and body from partaking thereof unworthily; but grant me unto my last breath to receive without condemnation the portion of Thy Holy Things, for communion of the Holy Spirit, for provision on the way unto eternal life,[^1051] and for an acceptable defense at Thy dread judgment-seat; that I also, with all Thine elect, may be a partaker of Thine incorruptible and eternal good things, which Thou hast prepared for them that love Thee, O Lord, in which Thou art glorified unto ages of ages. Amen.[^1052]

*Of the same Basil the Great:*[^1053]

*Prayer unto Holy Communion:*

O Master, Lord our God, only-begotten Son and Word of the living and immortal Father, Who didst give Thyself as a sacrifice for us, for whose sake Thou didst become man, that Thou mightest show us conformed unto Thine image, and unite us unto Thyself by communion of Thy life-creating Mysteries:[^1054] I will extol Thee, for Thou hast lifted me up, and hast not made my foes to rejoice over me; but Thou hast brought up my soul from hades, and hast saved me from them that go down into the pit.[^1055] And now incline Thine ear unto me, and hear my words, and make me, unworthy though I am, a partaker, even as of Thine image, so also of Thy most pure Flesh and of Thy very precious Blood: for the remission of sins, for the forgiveness of transgressions, for communion of the Holy Spirit;[^1056] overlooking for me, for the riches of Thy goodness, all sins voluntary and involuntary, both those committed with my knowledge and those in ignorance. For though I am unworthy of heaven and of earth, yet I am Thy creature, and the work of Thine all-pure hands, even if by transgression of Thy commandments I have corrupted Thine image. Overlook my sins, O Thou Who alone takest away the sins of the world, and art the sinless Lord; for against Thee only have I sinned, and before Thee do I fall down, my good God.[^1057]

Even though by my evil deeds I continually provoke Thee to anger, abhor me not who am unclean in heart, defiled in lips, passionate in soul; but trusting in Thy mercy and drawing near, receive me as the harlot, and as the thief, and as the publican; and forgive me the multitude of my many evil deeds, as having authority to remit sins unto them that sin, for the sake of the communion of Thine all-pure Mysteries.[^1058] And as the coal of Thy holy Body burns up the passionate motions of the soul, cleanse my heart, as once the lips of Thy prophet, for the sake of Thine awesome[^1059] seraph:[^1060] enlighten also my thoughts with the light of Thy gladness, and fill me wholly with holiness, for Thou art holy by nature. And by communion of Thy Blood, fill my heart with gladness, and my mind with Thy rejoicing. Mortify all my bodily members through communion of Thy holy Mysteries; and free [me] from every carnal desire by the descent of Thy Holy Spirit.[^1061] Yea, O God, Who comest and makest Thine abode with the Father and with Thy Holy Spirit in all Thy saints;[^1062] Who hast deigned to be received by me, a sinner and unworthy one, in the bloodless sacrifice offered unto Thee, for the sake of Thine immeasurable goodness: do Thou abide in me acceptably[^1063] and inseparably unto the end of my life; and be Thou unto me an addition of life, against the destroyer; a light unto the revelation of Thy hidden mysteries; a guide unto the path of Thy commandments; unto the Heavenly Kingdom, unto eternal gladness; unto the fulfillment of wisdom; unto the completion of the word; unto the contemplation of Thy sayings; a revealer of mysteries of divine and noble things; strength against warring spirits; and the supreme quencher of every desire in me.[^1064] Yea, O God consubstantial with the Father, and Word of God unto us: be Thou all things unto me, Thy servant; and for the sake of the communion of Thy divine Gifts, unite Thyself unto me in one spirit, according to the prayer unto Thy Father and God on behalf of us unworthy ones:[^1065] for I am flesh of Thy flesh, and bearing Thee from divine Baptism,[^1066] even if I have defiled Thy garment.[^1067] For Thou art He that said: *He that eats My Flesh, and drinks My Blood, dwells in Me, and I in him.*[^1068] And Thine is the glory unto ages of ages. Amen.

*Prayer of the same Basil the Great, unto Holy Communion:*[^1069]

O holy, all-pure, undefiled Master, Who through Thy great mercy didst not disdain to receive without sin and without defilement our nature, which had become subject to passions through sin; and having cleansed it with Thy holy Blood, poured out for the sake of our salvation, the creation of Thy holy hands;[^1070] Who didst accomplish Thine ineffable dispensation, not for the sake of them that have not sinned against Thee, nor of them that have been well-pleasing unto Thee, but for the sake of them that provoked Thee to anger and turned away:[^1071] Who art the hope of salvation unto them that for their sins have despaired of all hope;[^1072] for Thou Thyself, O good One, hast said: *They that are whole need not a physician, but they that are sick;*[^1073] and: *Thou camest not to save them that are saved, but the lost.*[^1074] Do Thou therefore, O compassionate One, receive in Thy tender mercy also me, unworthy though I am, who trust in Thy compassions, and have dared to draw near unto Thy most pure Body and Blood; and endure to accept my unworthiness as a partaker of Thy dread Mysteries;[^1075] and abhor me not, neither be wroth, nor set me forth as guilty; seeing that I am sinful and unclean, and draw near unworthily.[^1076] But, O gracious Lord,[^1077] Who hast given unto us sinners Thy Body and Blood for the remission of sins: grant unto me, through the communion thereof, of Thine indwelling in me, Thine unprofitable servant, and of my abiding in Thee, my good Master and Savior. And make the communion of these things to be unto me for the lightening of the unspeakable burdens of mine iniquities, for deliverance from guilt, for the cleansing of soul and body;[^1078] for on behalf of sinners, condemned and without defense, hast Thou poured out Thy divine and life-flowing Blood by Thy great mercy:[^1079] which vouchsafe me to drink without condemnation, and to eat Thy life-creating Body. And let not communion be unto me for judgment, who eat and drink unworthily, O Lover of mankind, nor unto a heavier reckoning, instead of obtaining remission of my evil deeds.[^1080] For Thou knowest, Who knowest all things, that not presumptuously, nor as one uncondemned, or as being worthy, do I draw near with boldness; but with fear and trembling,[^1081] with quaking of heart, and of the bones and very marrow;[^1082] for I know myself self-condemned, and unworthy even to look with mine eyes upon Thy dread table.[^1083] Yet because of that whereby Thou hast shown unto sinners Thine ineffable mercy, through the fraction of Thy holy Body and the pouring out of Thy precious Blood: I desire to be nourished with Thy soul-nourishing food, O Christ our God; and I cast myself daringly into the abyss of Thy mercy.[^1084] Gazing upon Thy worshipful and incorruptible Mysteries, which Thou didst receive in Thy love for mankind for the remission of our sins, I am held by fear and joy: by the one indeed because of mine unworthiness, and by the other because of the hope of forgiveness for my sins.[^1085] Behold therefore, O Lord, my lowliness;[^1086] and grant me without guilt and without condemnation to receive Thy divine and deifying Mysteries: for cleansing, for salvation, for remission of my sins; for fleeing the punishment to come, and for obtaining glory; and for the reception of the things prepared for them that love Thee, and that live and abide in Thee:[^1087] by the intercession of Thine all-glorified and all-pure Mother, and of all the saints. Amen.

*Prayer of our father among the saints, John Chrysostom, unto Holy Communion:*[^1088]

Lord, I know that I am not worthy, nor sufficient, that Thou shouldest enter under the roof of the house of my soul, because it is all deserted and fallen;[^1089] and Thou hast not in me a worthy place where to lay Thy head.[^1090] But even as from the height Thou didst humble Thyself for our sake, so humble Thyself now also unto my lowliness.[^1091] And as Thou didst deign to lie in a cave and in a manger of brute beasts,[^1092] so receive [me], and enter into the manger of my unreasoning soul, and into my defiled body.[^1093] And even as Thou didst not disdain to enter and sup with sinners in the house of Simon the leper,[^1094] so deign to enter also into the temple of my humble soul, leprous and sinful.[^1095] And as Thou didst not thrust away the sinful harlot like unto me, who came and touched Thee,[^1096] so have compassion also upon me, a sinner, who come and touch Thee; and even as Thou didst not abhor her defiled and unclean lips that kissed Thee,[^1097] so also of mine lips, abhor not [lips] even more defiled and more unclean than hers;[^1098] nor my darkened and unclean and polluted lips, and my unclean tongue. *The Apostle to the Corinthians:*[^1265]

Brethren, I received of the Lord that which also I delivered unto you, that the Lord Jesus, in the night in which He was betrayed, took bread, and having given thanks, He brake it and said: Take, eat; this is My Body which is broken for you; this do in remembrance of Me. In like manner also the cup, after supper, saying: This cup is the New Testament in My Blood; this do, as often as ye drink it, in remembrance of Me. For as often as ye eat this bread and drink this cup, ye proclaim the Lord’s death, till He come. Therefore, whosoever shall eat this bread,[^1266] or drink the cup of the Lord unworthily, shall be guilty of the Body and Blood of the Lord. But let a man examine himself, and so let him eat of the bread, and drink of the cup. For he that eats and drinks unworthily, eats and drinks judgment unto himself, not discerning the Lord’s Body. For this cause many are weak and sickly among you, and not a few sleep. For if we would judge ourselves, we should not be judged. But when we are judged, we are chastened by the Lord, that we should not be condemned with the world.[^1267]

O man, seeing the deifying Blood, shudder!

[^1162]: Direct invocation of Psalm 50:3 (LXX): *"Have mercy upon me, O God, according to Thy great mercy, and according to the multitude of Thy compassions blot out my transgression"* (LXX Ps. 50:3: Ἐλέησόν με, ὁ Θεός, κατὰ τὸ μέγα ἔлеос σου).
[^1163]: Ascetic terminology of spiritual warfare: Deliverance from demonic assaults (*приложе́нїа*, Greek *προσβοлаί*) and habitual sins that grow along with the fallen soul (*съвъзраста́ющихъ є́й зѡ́лъ*).
[^1164]: Direct Pauline citation from Romans 5:20: *"Where sin abounded, grace did much more abound"* (*и҆де́же оу҆мно́жисѧ грѣ́хъ, преизоби́лоуетъ бл҃года́ть* / Greek *οὗ δὲ ἐπλεόνασεν ἡ ἁμαρτία, ὑπερεπερίσσευσεν ἡ χάρις*).
[^1165]: Doxological conclusion of the confessional pre-communion prayer: Proclaiming God as "the God of them that repent" (*Б҃ъ ка́ющихсѧ*), concluding with the Father Paul Doxology standard (*unto ages of ages*).
[^1166]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink introducing the second pre-communion prayer of St. John of Damascus (*ТОГО́ЖЕ СТ҃АГО І҆ѠА́ННА ДАМА́СКИНА • МЛ҃ТВА • КЪ СТ҃О́МОУ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ*).
[^1167]: Mystical allusions to the Song of Songs and the Psalter: Expressing spiritual love-wounding (*τετρωμένη ἀγάπης*, Song of Songs 2:5, 5:8) and consuming divine zeal quoting Psalm 118:139 (LXX: *"Thy zeal hath caused me to pine away"*, ἐξέτηξέ με ὁ ζῆλός σου).
[^1168]: Dogma of Theosis (*деификация*): Eucharistic theology of deification through the reception of the life-giving and deifying Blood (*живо́тныꙗ и҆ бг҃отво́рныꙗ ти кро́ве*) and partaking of the divine nature (2 Peter 1:4).
[^1169]: Eschatological anticipation drawn from 1 Thessalonians 4:17: Longing to meet Christ at His second coming, being caught up in the clouds in the air with all the elect (*на въздꙋ́шнѣмъ въсхище́нїи на ѡ҆блацѣ́хъ*, 1 Thess. 4:17: ἁρπαγησόμεθα ἐν νεφέλαις εἰς ἀπάντησιν τοῦ Κυρίου εἰς ἀέρα).
[^1170]: Trinitarian doxology concluding the second prayer: Glorifying the Son together with the unoriginate Father and the most holy, good, and life-creating Spirit.
[^1171]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink introducing the third pre-communion prayer of St. John of Damascus (*ТОГѠ́ЖЕ СТ҃ГО І҆ѠА́ННА ДАМА́СКИНА • МЛ҃ТВА • КЪ СТ҃О́Мꙋ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ*).
[^1172]: Drawing near unto Holy Communion: Trusting in the abyss of Christ's love for mankind (*поучи́нꙋ чл҃колю́бїа твое́го*), the penitent dares to approach the holy and life-creating Mysteries (continuing onto leaf p391).
[^1173]: Eucharistic fear of burning: The dread of approaching the Eucharistic coal (cf. Isaiah 6:6–7), fearing to be consumed by the divine fire (*да невъмѣ́сто просвѣще́нїа ѡ҆па́ленъ бꙋ́дꙋ*), echoing the liturgical warnings in the Byzantine Pre-Communion prayers.
[^1174]: Fourfold fruits of Communion: Reception of the Holy Mysteries *unto enlightenment, unto delight, unto joy, and unto gladness of soul and body* (*въ просвѣще́нїе, въ наслажде́нїе, въ ра́дость, и҆ въ весе́лїе дꙋши и҆ тѣ́лоу мое́моу*).
[^1175]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink introducing the fourth pre-communion prayer of St. John of Damascus (*ТОГО́ЖЕ І҆ѠА́ННА ДАМА́СКИНА • МЛ҃ИТВА КЪ СТ҃О́МОУ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ*), continuing immediately onto leaf p392.
[^1176]: Intercession for enemies: The penitent prays for those who hate, persecute, reproach, and slander him (cf. Matt. 5:44, Luke 6:27–28), pleading that no one may suffer condemnation on his account either in this age or in the age to come. Marginal rubric `ді҃` denotes Prayer 14 of the sequential pre-communion series.
[^1177]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink introducing the first pre-communion prayer of St. Symeon the Metaphrast (*СТ҃ГО СѶМЕѠ́НА МЕТАФРА́СТА • МЛ҃ТВА КЪ СТ҃О́Мꙋ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ*).
[^1178]: Christological prologue of St. Symeon the Metaphrast: Addressing Christ as the *only pure and incorrupt Lord* (*Еди́нъ чⷭ҇тыи и҆ нетлѣ́нный Гьⷭ҇*), Who in His ineffable philanthropy assumed human nature from the pure virginal blood of the Theotokos (continuing onto leaf p393).
[^1179]: Incarnation by the Holy Spirit: Confessing the virginal conception through the overshadowing of the Holy Spirit and the good pleasure of the eternal Father (Luke 1:35).
[^1180]: Pauline Christological titles: Christ as *Wisdom of God, and Peace, and Power* (1 Cor. 1:24, Eph. 2:14).
[^1181]: Instruments of the Passion (*Arma Christi*): The Crucifixion, Cross, nails, spear, and death invoked as spiritual weapons to put to death the carnal passions of the flesh (Col. 3:5).
[^1182]: Despoiling of hades and mystical burial: Spiritual typology linking Christ's burial and the harrowing of hell (*а҆́дова плѣни́лъ є҆сѝ црⷭ҇твїа*) to the mortification of evil thoughts and the destruction of demonic spirits.
[^1183]: Resurrection of the Forefather: Commemorating Christ's raising of Adam (*па́дшаго пр҃аѡ҆ц҃а*) by His third-day resurrection (continuing onto leaf p394).
[^1184]: Deification of human nature through the Ascension: The dogmatic theology of Christ deifying the assumed flesh (*пло́тьское ѻ҆божи́въ въспрїѧ́тїе*) and seating human nature at the right hand of the Father (Heb. 1:3).
[^1185]: Right-hand portion of the elect: Scriptural allusion to Matthew 25:31–34, pleading through Holy Communion to be counted among the sheep on the right hand.
[^1186]: Pentecostal vessel: Echo of Acts 2:1–4, petitioning that the communicant become a receptive temple and dwelling-place of the Holy Spirit (*прїѧ́телище... прише́ствїа*).
[^1187]: The Parousia and meeting in the clouds: Eschatological petition drawn from 1 Thessalonians 4:17 and Acts 17:31, longing to meet the Judge and Creator upon the clouds at His glorious return (continuing onto leaf p395).
[^1188]: Trinitarian doxology concluding the first prayer of St. Symeon the Metaphrast.
[^1189]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink introducing the second pre-communion prayer of St. Symeon the Metaphrast (*ТОГО́ЖЕ СТ҃ГО СѶМЕѠ́НА МЕТАФРА́СТА МЛ҃ТВА КЪ СТ҃О́МОУ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ*). Marginal numeral `ѕ҃і` designates Prayer 16 in the codex's numbering scheme.
[^1190]: Mystical contemplative hymnography: The poetic apostrophe to the Trinity as an eternal stream of inexhaustible goodness (*бл҃гти неистощи́маѧ въ вѣ́къ текоу́щи*) and insatiable divine beauty (*доброто̀ мнѡгорачи́телнаѧ, неимы́ сы́тости*).
[^1191]: Justification by faith and hope: The ascetic cry *save me by faith alone, and through hope* (*вѣ́рою є҆ди́ною сп҃си́ мѧ, и҆ чрезъ наде́ждꙋ*), echoing the Pauline theme of salvation by grace through faith (Rom. 3:28, Eph. 2:8).
[^1192]: Classical Orthodox Trinitarian formula: *Trinity in Persons, and Unity in Essence* (*трⷭ҇це ли́ци, и҆ є҆дини́це сꙋ́ществомъ* / Greek Τριὰς τοῖς προσώποις, καὶ Μονὰς τῇ οὐσίᾳ).
[^1193]: Poetic strophic refrain: Repetition of the petition *By faith alone save me, and through hope* (*вѣ́рою є҆диною сп҃си мѧ̀, и҆ чрезъ наде́ждꙋ*).
[^1194]: God as Purifying Fire: Invoking the Divine Trinity as a cleansing fire (*Ѽ ѻ҆́гню чисти́телный*) to consume sins (cf. Heb. 12:29).
[^1195]: Ascetic psychology of the nous: The sovereignty of the intellect and mind (*оу҆́мъ*) reigning over the passions and sensory deliberations (*помышле́нїа*), reflecting Evagrian and Sinaitic neptic theology.
[^1196]: Intellectual illumination: Pleading for the *intellectual candle* (*оу҆́мноую свѣ́щю*) to dispel the darkness of sin and illuminate the inner chamber of the heart.
[^1197]: Whiteness of the penitent soul: Echoing Psalm 50:9 (LXX: *"Thou shalt wash me, and I shall be made whiter than snow"*, ἀκουτιεῖς με... ὑπὲρ χιόνα λευκανθήσομαι).
[^1198]: Petition for an Angel of Light: Asking the Holy Trinity to send a radiant angel as guardian, educator, and defender of the soul and body (continuing onto leaf p397).
[^1199]: Patristic angelology: Invoking the Guardian Angel as *tutor* (*пѣстоу́нъ*, Greek παιδαγωγός, Gal. 3:24), *protector* (*прѣста́тель*), *guardian of soul and body*, and *guide unto salvation*.
[^1200]: Ascetic tears of compunction: The ascetic doctrine of *penthos* (mourning) and tears as a second baptism, praying that streams of malice be dried up and showers of tears poured out.
[^1201]: God of the fallen and sinful: Theological affirmation that Christ came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance (Matt. 9:13, Mark 2:17, 1 Tim. 1:15), being accessible to all who have apostatized or transgressed.
[^1202]: Sacramental confession and patterns of repentance: Highlighting the divine institution of confession as the vehicle of salvation (*и҆сповѣ́данїа оу҆ста́ви є҆сѝ сп҃сенїе*), providing biblical exemplars of penitence (continuing onto leaf p398).
[^1203]: Repetition of the self-accusing catalog: Pleading for mercy as an unclean, deceitful, wretched, darkened, and defiled creature (*мнѣ̀ те́мномоу и҆ ѡ҆сквръне́номꙋ*).
[^1204]: Allusion to the Prayer of Manasseh: Quoting Manasseh 9: *"For my transgressions are multiplied more than the sand of the sea, and my iniquities are increased"* (*па́че числа̀ безчи́сленыхъ звѣ́здъ, па́че числа̀ пѣска̀ мо́рскаго съгрѣши́хъ ти*).
[^1205]: Comprehensive pollution of all faculties: Confessing that every imaginable form and shame of evil has been practiced in soul, thoughts, words, and deeds.
[^1206]: Fetters of sinful delusion: Comparing the delusion of the mind and presumption (*мнѣ́нїе*, Greek οἴησις) to heavy iron collars bowing down the neck of the soul (continuing onto leaf p399).
[^1207]: Inescapable bonds of sin: The constriction of the heart and impossibility of looking up, directly echoing Psalm 39:13 (LXX: *"my iniquities have taken hold upon me, and I am not able to look up"*).
[^1208]: Cosmic unworthiness: The fourfold anaphora of unworthiness (*недосто́йнѣ*): unworthily beholding the heavens, beholding human faces, counseling one's neighbor, and even treading upon the earthly dust.
[^1209]: Kindling of divine wrath: Expressing the gravity of voluntary transgression which inflames the righteous indignation of God (cf. Deut. 32:22).
[^1210]: Contempt of the divine will: Rejecting God's pure will and holy statutes, failing to desire any of His pure commandments (continuing onto leaf p400).
[^1211]: Scriptural quote from Psalm 18:10–11 (LXX): *"The judgments of the Lord are true... more desirable than gold and much precious stone"* (LXX Ps. 18:10–11: τὰ κρίματα Κυρίου ἀληθινά... ἐпиθυμητὰ ὑπὲρ χρυσίον).
[^1212]: Scriptural quote from the Prayer of Manasseh: Quoting Prayer of Manasseh 11: *"And now, behold, I bend the knee of my heart, entreating Thy goodness"* (*прекланѧ́ю ти срⷣца мое́го колѣ́нѣ* / Greek καὶ νῦν κλίνω γόνυ καρδίας).
[^1213]: Liturgical triad of bodily contrition: The physical prostration of soul and body expressed through the bowing of the knees of the heart, the darkened head, and the defiled neck.
[^1214]: Liturgical rhythmic confession: The solemn sixfold repetition of *I have sinned* (*съгрѣши́хъ*), cataloging offenses, injustices, transgressions of the commandments, judging of others, and shameless apostasy.
[^1215]: Boldness of penitent supplication: Trusting in divine goodness despite conscious guilt, daring to offer supplication (*но̀ моле́нїе творю̀ твое́й бл҃гти, но мольбоу приношꙋ̀*, continuing onto leaf p401).
[^1216]: Divine tender-heartedness: The penitent appeals to Christ's utmost compassion (*кра́йнее бл҃гооу҆тро́бїе*) and malice-free goodness (*незло́бїе*), crying out for purification (*сп҃си и҆ ѡ҆цѣ́сти мѧ̀*).
[^1217]: Liturgical catalogue of remissions: The fourfold repetition of *remit* (*ѡ҆ста́ви*), pleading for release from the multitude of transgressions, the heavy debts of sin, defilements, and abominations.
[^1218]: Anaphora of protective petitions (*да не...*): Pleading not to be crushed, put to death, suddenly snatched away amidst wicked deliberations, or consumed by the fire of divine judgment together with one's abominations.
[^1219]: The Immortal Banquet: Allusion to the eschatological Messianic banquet and Eucharistic feast (*трапе́за безсъме́ртнаѧ*, cf. Luke 14:15–24, Matt. 22:1–14), praying not to be cast out from the eternal wedding feast or alienated from the fellowship of the saints (continuing onto leaf p402).
[^1220]: Monastic tonsure as second baptism: The traditional Patristic and Byzantine monastic theology regarding the Great Angelic Schema (*великий ангельский образ*) as a renewal of baptismal grace (*второе крещение*), pleading for its restoration after having polluted the first garment of baptism.
[^1221]: God-streaming blood: The theological compound *бг҃ото́чный* (Greek θεόρρυτος), describing the Eucharistic blood flowing from the side of Christ on the Cross (John 19:34).
[^1222]: Ascetic remembrance of death (*memento mori*): The vivid evocation of the terrifying hour of the soul's departure from the body (*въ ча́съ же коне́чный, и҆ го́ркїи съме́ртный*), a hallmark of Byzantine hesychastic prayer.
[^1223]: Demonic toll-houses and the soul's departure: Pleading for a gentle separation of soul and body, protection against demonic spirits encountered in the aerial realm, and the tearing up of the handwriting of sinful debts (cf. Col. 2:14, continuing onto leaf p403).
[^1224]: Tearing of the chirograph: Echoing Colossians 2:14 (*blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us*), praying that the demonic records of sinful debts be utterly obliterated and reduced to ashes in the abyss of hades.
[^1225]: Angelic escort of the soul: The Byzantine liturgical doctrine of radiant guardian angels escorting the departed soul safely through the demonic ranks (*ѿража́ющихъ плъ́кы бѣсѡ́вскыѧ*) into the heavenly realm.
[^1226]: The fiery-formed Throne of Glory: Biblical imagery of the Divine Merkabah and fiery throne of the Ancient of Days (Daniel 7:9, Ezekiel 1:26–28).
[^1227]: Divine Grace personified as Advocate: The theological audacity of presenting God's own grace (*бл҃года́ть*), mercy, and compassion as the primary advocate and intercessor (*хода́тай*, Greek μεσίτης) before His divine justice (continuing onto leaf p404).
[^1228]: Marian typological titles: The Mother of God invoked under canonical Old Testament types: the Adornment of the world, Jacob's Ladder (Gen. 28:12), Aaron's budding rod (Num. 17:8, Isa. 11:1), and the Living/Animate Throne of the Cherubim (*ѻ҆дꙋшевле́нныи прⷭ҇то́лъ*).
[^1229]: The nine angelic choirs: The complete ninefold celestial hierarchy of Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite invoked as lightning-rayed intercessors (*чи́ны млънїелоу́чны*): Thrones, Cherubim, Seraphim, Dominions, Powers, Authorities, Principalities, Archangels, and Angels.
[^1230]: St. John the Baptist and Forerunner: Commemorating his sanctification in his mother's womb prior to birth (Luke 1:15, 41) and his role as the dawn preceding the Sun of Righteousness unto those sitting in darkness (Luke 1:78–79).
[^1231]: Choirs of the righteous: The cosmic intercession of the heavenly Church, invoking the Old Testament patriarchs and prophets, followed by the New Testament apostles and crown-bearing martyrs (continuing onto leaf p405).
[^1232]: St. Mary of Egypt as model of repentance: Invoking St. Mary of Egypt (the quintessential exemplar of radical repentance in the Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete), celebrated as an earthly human who attained heavenly conversance (*нб҃оше́ствена же ѻ҆ба́че*).
[^1233]: St. Onuphrius the Great and desert asceticism: The 4th-century anchorite of the Egyptian Thebaid who lived sixty years naked in the wilderness, miraculously clothed only by hair and heavenly light, here portrayed as stripped of physical clothing but wondrously adorned with divine virtue.
[^1234]: Transgression from the womb: Echoing Psalm 50:7 (LXX: *"For behold, I was conceived in iniquities, and in sins did my mother bear me"*), confessing habitual sinfulness from the very womb (*ѿ ложе́снъ самѣ́хъ*).
[^1235]: Salvation beyond merit: Pleading for salvation not based on human works or merit (*чрѣ досто́инство* / Greek ὑπὲρ τὴν ἀξίαν), but solely through divine benevolence and hope (continuing onto leaf p406).
[^1236]: Doxological conclusion of Prayer 16: Pleading to praise God alongside the celestial hosts unto unnumbered ages (*въ вѣ́кы въсѧ̑ неи҆зчете́нныѧ*).
[^1237]: Rubric in red cinnabar ink introducing Prayer 17 of the Pre-Communion series: *Prayer before Holy Communion, of Saint Symeon the New Theologian, from the Hymns of Divine Love* (*МЛ҃ТВА КЪ СТ҃О́Мꙋ ПРИЧАЩЕ́НЇЮ • СТ҃ГО СѶМЕѠ́НА НО́ВАГО БГ҃ОСЛО́ВА • Ѿ БЖ҃ТВНЫХЪ РА́ЧЇЙ*). The marginal numeral `з҃і` marks Prayer 17. The attribution *From the Hymns of Divine Love* cites the Greek collection *Ἐκ τῶν θείων ἐρώτων* (Hymn 17).
[^1238]: The classic opening of St. Symeon's prayer: The fourfold confession of unworthiness: *From defiled lips, from an abominable heart, from an unclean tongue, from a polluted soul* (*Ἀπὸ ῥυπαρῶν χειλέων, ἀπὸ βδελυρᾶς καρδίας, ἀπὸ ἀκαθάρτου γλώσσης, ἐκ ψυχῆς ἐρρυπωμένης*).
[^1239]: Boldness of the penitent: Asking Christ not to reject the sinner's words, gestures, or audacious daring (*ни дръ́зость*, Greek μηδὲ τὴν ἀναίδейαν, continuing onto leaf p407).
[^1240]: Prayer for bold speech (*parrhesia*): St. Symeon asks Christ to grant him freedom of speech (*дръзнове́ннѣ гл҃ати*) and divine instruction on how to pray properly (Rom. 8:26).
[^1241]: The repentant harlot of Luke 7:36–50: Direct allusion to the sinful woman who purchased alabaster ointment and anointed Christ's feet in Simon the Pharisee's house (*съгрѣши́хъ па́че блоу́дница*).
[^1242]: Christ addressed as the Divine Word (*Слово* / Logos): Invoking Christ under His Johannine title (John 1:1), pleading not to be cast away from His presence.
[^1243]: Tears as precious myrrh: The mystical assimilation of penitential tears to costly spikenard, daring to wash and kiss the feet of the Saviour.
[^1244]: Divine omniscience: Christ's intimate knowledge of the sinner's manifold offenses and inner brokenness (continuing onto leaf p408). Also note the typographical erratum in the printed heading, where folio numeral `рч҃е` (195) is erroneously repeated in place of `рч҃и` (198).
[^1245]: Spiritual diagnosis: Christ as the physician of souls Who sees both the ulcers of sin and the sincere disposition (*произволе́нїе*) and sighing of the heart.
[^1246]: Omnipresence and divine foreknowledge: Nothing escapes God's scrutiny, neither a single tear-drop, nor a fraction of a drop, nor the slightest movement of the heart (*движе́нїе срⷣчное*).
[^1247]: Scriptural quote from Psalm 138:16 (LXX): *"Thine eyes did see mine unformed substance, and in Thy book all shall be written"* (τὸ ἀκατέργαστόν μου εἶδον οἱ ὀφθαλμοί σου, καὶ ἐπὶ τὸ βιβλίον σου πάντες γραφήσονται / *несꙋдѣ́ланное моѐ ви́дѣша ѡ҆́чи твоѝ, въ кни́гах же твои́хъ написа́на тебѣ̀ соу́тъ*).
[^1248]: Scriptural quote from Psalm 24:18 (LXX): *"Look upon my lowliness and my toil, and forgive all my sins"* (ἴде τὴν ταπείνωσίν μου καὶ τὸν κόπον μου, καὶ ἄφες πάσας τὰς ἁμαρτίας μου / *ви́ждь смире́нїе моѐ, ви́ждь троу́д мои́ є҆ли́къ, и҆ грѣхы̀ ѡ҆ста́ви ми*).
[^1249]: Requisite interior disposition for Communion: Purity of heart, fearful contemplation (*стра́шнаѧ мы́сль*), and contrition of soul necessary to receive the undefiled Mysteries without incurring judgment (continuing onto leaf p409).
[^1250]: Eucharistic deification (*Theosis*): The core theological conviction of St. Symeon the New Theologian that the communicant is literally vivified and deified (*ѡ҆живля́етсѧ, и҆ ѡ҆божа́етсѧ* / Greek ζωοῦται καὶ θεοῦται) through the worthy reception of Christ's Body and Blood.
[^1251]: Dominical quotation from John 6:56: *"He that eats My flesh, and drinks My blood, dwells in Me, and I in him"* (*ὁ τρώγων μου τὴν σάρκα καὶ πίνων μου τὸ αἷμα ἐν ἐμοὶ μένει, κἀγὼ ἐν αὐτῷ*).
[^1252]: The Three-Sunned Light: Addressing Christ as the radiance of the Triune Godhead (*свѣ́тъ трисли́чный* / Greek φῶς τρισηλίου), expressing communion with the entire Holy Trinity through Christ.
[^1253]: Christological titles of intimacy: Christ addressed as *Giver of Life* (*живода́вецъ*), *my breath* (*дыха́нїе моѐ*), *my life* (*живо́тъ мои́*), and *my rejoicing* (*възрадова́нїе моѐ*).
[^1254]: Penitential approach: Drawing near to the chalice with tears and inner contrition, seeking remission of offenses (continuing onto leaf p410).
[^1255]: Protection from the Deceiver: The danger of spiritual vulnerability when alienated from divine grace, whereby the devil (*преле́стникъ*, the Deceiver) snatches the soul away and separates it from Christ's words.
[^1256]: Gospel types of repentance: Juxtaposing the Prodigal Son (Luke 15:11–32) and the penitent harlot (Luke 7:36–50), entreating Christ to extend the same unconditional acceptance to the communicant.
[^1257]: Consciousness of chief among sinners: Echoing 1 Timothy 1:15 (*"sinners, of whom I am chief"*), acknowledging personal guilt as surpassing all others (continuing onto leaf p411).
[^1258]: Transgression vs. Divine Philanthropy: The classical theological motif that no magnitude or multitude of human sin can ever surpass the inexhaustible ocean of God's love for mankind (*мнѡ́гое чл҃колꙋ́бїе*), citing St. Symeon the New Theologian's Hymn 17 (*Ἐκ τῶν θείων ἐρώτων*).
[^1259]: Divine Conversance with the Penitent: The mystical paradox that Christ deigns to converse with cleansed penitents as with His own intimate friends (*ꙗ҆́коже дрꙋгѡ́мъ свои́мъ бли́жнимъ*, cf. John 15:14–15).
[^1260]: The Communicant as Grass before Divine Fire: The bold ascetic paradox of the communicant being "grass" (*трава̀ сы́й*) daring to partake of divine Fire, yet marvelously preserved without being consumed, prefigured by the burning bush on Mount Sinai (Exodus 3:2, continuing onto leaf p412).
[^1261]: The Incombustible Bush (*Неопалимая Купина*): The Old Testament theophany of Exodus 3:2 applied typologically to the Eucharistic mystery, wherein divine fire dews rather than consumes the faithful receiver (*нн҃ѣ ѻ҆роша́емь неѻ҆па́лно*).
[^1262]: Whole-person Thanksgiving: The triple grateful confession uniting word (*сло́вомъ*), heart (*срⷣцемъ*), and the bodily and spiritual members (*оу҆́десы и҆ дш҃и и҆ тѣ́ла*).
[^1263]: Incipit of the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed: The rubric prescribes the recitation of the Confession of the Orthodox Faith (*и҆сповѣ́данїе правосла́вныѧ вѣ́ры*), printed only as its canonical opening incipit (*Вѣ́роую въ є҆ди́наго Б҃а*), strictly preserved without unwritten textual expansion per Mandate 16.
[^1264]: The Holy Thursday Eucharistic Troparion: The celebrated Byzantine hymn *Of Thy Mystical Supper* (*Ве́чери твоеѧ̀ та́йнѣй*), juxtaposing the betrayal of Judas with the saving confession of the Good Thief (Luke 23:42).
[^1265]: Epistolary Reading on the Institution of the Eucharist: The liturgical lection from 1 Corinthians 11:23–26 prescribed for the communicant's preparation, recounting the Pauline institution narrative.
[^1266]: The Pauline Anamnesis: The dominical command *This do in remembrance of Me* (*сѐ твори́те въ моѐ въспомина́нїе*, 1 Cor. 11:24–25), underscoring the Eucharistic commemoration of the Lord's death (continuing onto leaf p414).
[^1267]: The Grave Warning of Unworthy Partaking: 1 Corinthians 11:27–32, warning that eating and drinking unworthily incurs guilt against the Body and Blood of the Lord (*пови́ненъ бꙋ́детъ тѣ́лоу и҆ кро́ви Гдⷭ҇ни*) and eating judgment unto oneself.
[^1268]: Gospel Lection of the Bread of Life: The opening of John 6:48–54 addressed to the Jews, identifying Christ as the true Bread of Life (*А҆́зъ є҆́смь хлѣ́бъ живо́тный*) far surpassing the ancestral wilderness manna (continuing onto leaf p415).
[^1269]: The Living Bread and Eschatological Life: John 6:51, proclaiming that whoever eats of this living bread shall live unto ages, identifying the Eucharistic bread with Christ's flesh given for the life of the world.
[^1270]: The Johannine Eucharistic Imperative: John 6:53–54, demanding the eating of the flesh of the Son of Man and the drinking of His Blood as the indispensable prerequisite for eternal life and resurrection at the last day (continuing onto leaf p416).
[^1271]: Concluding Johannine Promise: John 6:54b (*and I will raise him up at the last day*), sealing the Scriptural pre-communion lectionary.
[^1272]: Interior Examination of Conscience: Rubric directing the communicant to search their conscience in mind and soul as they draw near the Holy Gifts (*ѡ҆блича́ѧ въ оу҆мѣ̀ совѣ́сть свою̀*).
[^1273]: Short Prayer of Approach: A brief penitential prayer acknowledging the terror of divine fire while appealing to God's purifying mercy, concluding with the Psalmic citation *It is good for me to cleave unto God* (Psalm 72:28 LXX).
[^1274]: Triple Pre-Communion Exclamation: Rubric prescribing the recitation, if desired even three times, of the awe-filled verses as one approaches the chalice.
[^1275]: The Eucharistic Coal of Isaiah: Echoing the seraphic live coal of Isaiah 6:6–7, warning that the deifying Blood consumes the unworthy while purifying sins (continuing onto leaf p417).
[^1276]: Spiritual and Intellectual Deification: The theological distinction that Christ's holy Flesh deifies the human spirit (*ѡ҆божа́етъ дх҃ъ*) while strangely and marvelously nourishing the mind (*оу҆́мъ же пита́етъ стра́ннѣ и҆ чю́днѣ*).
[^1277]: Rubric for Crosswise Hand Gesture: Prescribing the traditional Byzantine ceremonial posture of crossing one's hands upon the breast (*крⷭ҇тоо́бразно рꙋ́цѣ на пръ́сехъ положи́въ*) when approaching to receive the Holy Mysteries of the Crucified One.
