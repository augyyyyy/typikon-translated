# PART XIII: THE ORDER OF THE HOLY OIL CHANTED BY SEVEN PRIESTS [^261]

*Let them do thus: Let them pour wheat into a dish, and set in the dish a lamp filled with wine and oil, and seven wands for the anointing; and let them set it in the midst of the church upon an analogion, whereon is also the Holy Gospel; but if it happen to be in a house, upon a covered table; and there is given to the priests to each a candle; and they stand around the oil, vested in white phelonia. And the first priest, taking the censer with the deacon, censes around the analogion and the whole church, and begins:*[^262]

**Priest:** Blessed is our God, always, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

*Then the Trisagion: O Most Holy Trinity: after Our Father: For Thine is the Kingdom: Lord, have mercy (12 times). Come, let us worship (thrice). Then:*

*Psalm 142: O Lord, hear my prayer... Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen. Alleluia (thrice).*[^263]

*Then the Little Litany:*

**Deacon:** Again and again in peace, let us pray to the Lord.

**Choir:** Lord, have mercy.

**Deacon:** Help us, save us, have mercy on us, and keep us, O God, by Thy grace.

**Choir:** Lord, have mercy.

**Deacon:** Commemorating our all-holy, pure, most blessed and glorious Lady, the Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary, with all the Saints, let us commend ourselves and one another, and our whole life unto Christ our God.

**Choir:** To Thee, O Lord.

**Priest:** For unto Thee is due all glory, honor, and worship, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages.

**Choir:** Amen.

*Then: Alleluia, in Tone 6.*[^264]

*Verse: O Lord, rebuke me not in Thine anger, neither chasten me in Thy wrath.*[^265]

*Verse: Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I am weak.*

*Then the troparia:*

Have mercy on us, O Lord, have mercy on us; for, destitute of all defense, we sinners offer this supplication unto Thee as Master: Have mercy on us, O Lord, have mercy on us.

*Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit.*

O Lord, have mercy on us, for in Thee have we put our trust; be not exceedingly wroth with us, neither remember our iniquities; but look down even now as Thou art compassionate, and deliver us from our enemies; for Thou art our God, and we are Thy people; all are the works of Thy hands, and we call upon Thy name.

*Both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

Open unto us the doors of compassion, O blessed Theotokos, that hoping in thee we may not perish, but may be delivered by thee from adversities; for thou art the salvation of the Christian race.[^266]

*Then Psalm 50: Have mercy on me, O God...*[^267]

*Then the Canon, whose acrostic is: "A prayer of the oil, a psalm of Arsenios."*[^268]

*Tone 4. Ode 1. Heirmos:*

*Having traversed the watery deep of the Red Sea with unwetted feet*[^269] *Troparion:*

With the oil of Thy tender compassion, O Master, ever comforting souls together with bodily passions, and preserving the oil of the faithful, do Thou Thyself even now show mercy unto those who flee unto Thee through oil.[^270]

*Verse: O Master, plenteous in mercy, O Lord, have mercy and heal Thy servant.*

*Troparion:*

Having Thy mercy, O Master, filling all the earth: when we are mystically anointed with Thy divine oil, O Savior, we faithfully ask that Thy mercy which passes understanding be granted unto us.[^271]

*Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit.*

*Troparion:*

Through the anointing of Thy priests, O Lover of mankind, having commanded Thine Apostles to bestow forgiveness upon Thy sick servants, whom Thou markest all with mercies through the oil of Thy compassion.[^272]

*Both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

*Theotokion:*

O pure Virgin, who alone didst give birth unto the Abyss of Peace: by thy supplications unto God, deliver thy servants from grievous diseases and sorrows, that we may unceasingly praise thee.[^273]

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*

*Thy Church rejoices in Thee, O Christ...*[^274]

*Troparion:*

Thou Who alone art marvelous among men, and merciful unto the faithful: unto him who is grievously afflicted, grant Thy grace from on high, O Christ.[^275]

*Verse: O Master, plenteous in mercy, O Lord, have mercy and heal Thy servant.*

*Troparion:*

Having once revealed by Thy divine behest the olive branch unto the abating of the deluge, O Lord: save and have mercy upon him that suffers.[^276]

*Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit.*

*Troparion:*

Make radiant the lamp of divine light in Thy mercy, O Christ; and have mercy, O Master, through Thine oil, upon these who are now being anointed with faith.[^277]

*Both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

*Theotokion:*

Look down mercifully from on high, O Mother of the Creator of all; and by thy supplications dispel the bitter affliction of him that is sick.[^278]

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 4, Podoben: "The shepherd's pipe of thy preaching" (Пасты́рскїи сопль вѣща́нїа твоє̀):*[^279]

As a divine river possessing an abundance of mercy, as a depth of multitude of forgiveness: show forth, O Compassionate One, Thy mercy of divine streams, and heal all; pour forth unstintingly a fountain of wonders, and wash us all; for unto Thy goodness we ever pray.[^280]

*Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

*Tone 4, Podoben: "Thou Who wast lifted up upon the Cross" (Възнесы́йсѧ):*[^281]

Being the Physician and Helper of the suffering, the Savior and Deliverer of the infirm, as Master and Lord of all: grant health unto Thy sick servant; show compassion and have mercy on him that is grievously ill, and deliver him from death, O Christ, that he may glorify Thy divine dominion.[^282]

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*

*The Church, beholding Thee lifted up upon the Cross...*[^283]

*Troparion:*

As Thou art incorruptible myrrh, O Christ, poured forth by grace and cleansing the world: show compassion and sanctify with Thy divine mercies those who anoint bodily wounds.[^284]

*Troparion:*

Having signed all the senses of Thy servant, O Master, with the sign of the serenity of Thine oil: make his entrance impassable and untouchable unto all adversary powers.[^285]

*Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit.*

*Troparion:*

Commanding the sick to call upon the sacred ministers of Thy divine mysteries, and through their prayer and the anointing of Thy mercy to be saved, O Lover of mankind: save by Thy mercy him who is suffering.[^286]

*Both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

*Theotokion:*

O Theotokos, Ever-Virgin most pure, firm protection and guardian, wall and haven, ladder and rampart: have mercy and save him who flees unto thee alone.[^287]

*Ode 5. Heirmos:*

*Thou, O my Lord, didst come as a light into the world*[^288] *Troparion:*

Thou Who art an abyss of mercy, O God, have mercy, O Merciful One, by Thy divine oil, upon him who suffers, as Thou art a compassionate Lord.[^289]

*Troparion:*

Having mercifully sanctified from on high our souls and bodies with the Sign of the Cross and with Thy divine sign, O Christ, in Thy goodness heal all.[^290]

*Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit.*

*Troparion:*

In Thine ineffable and marvelous wonder, the harlot of old who anointed Thy body with myrrh, O Compassionate One, was cleansed; show compassion also unto Thy servant.[^291]

*Both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

*Theotokion:*

O all-lauded pure one, Ever-Virgin Sovereign Lady, have mercy upon those that are anointed with Thy divine oil, and save Thy servant.[^292]

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*

*I will sacrifice unto Thee with the voice of praise, O Lord*[^293] *Troparion:*

Thou Who rulest by the oil of swift forgiveness, O Lover of mankind, crowning prophets and high priests with grace by Thy sign: grant mercy also unto him who suffers.[^294]

*Troparion:*

Let neither affliction nor subtle demons touch the divine anointing of the senses that are signed, O Savior, but shield him beneath Thy protection by Thy power.[^295]

*Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit.*

*Troparion:*

Stretch forth Thy hand from on high, O Lover of mankind, upon this divine oil, and sanctify it; and show forth Thy servant to be superior to tribulations and diseases, O Master.[^296]

*Both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

*Theotokion:*

Thy temple, O Mother of the Creator, was in God a fruitful olive tree, whose fullness of the world of mercy hath appeared; which do thou save by thy prayers, and grant relief unto Thy servants.[^297]

*Kontakion, Tone 4, Podoben: "Seeking the highest" (Вы́шниихъ):*[^298]

Being an all-good fountain of mercy: O Compassionate One, deliver from every affliction those who with fervent faith fall down before Thine ineffable oil; and taking away their diseases, grant divine grace from on high.[^299]

*Ode 7. Heirmos:*

*In the furnace the Abrahamic children...*[^300]

*Troparion:*

Thou art rich in mercy and compassions, healing for all the passions of the soul and bodily afflictions; and do Thou Thyself heal and physician this man who is suffering in diseases.[^301]

*Troparion:*

Anointing the heads of all with oil and granting gladness unto Thy servant, joyfully unto him who asks mercy of Thee, grant the rich mercies of Thy deliverance, O Lord.[^302]

*Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit.*

*Troparion:*

Thy sign, O Word, hath become a weapon against demons, a fire burning up carnal passions, through the prayers of the priests; by which, O Lover of mankind, those who suffer are delivered as from bonds.[^303]

*Both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

*Theotokion:*

Having contained within thy womb in God-befitting manner Him Who holds all things in His grasp, and having ineffably given Him flesh, O Mother of God: we beseech thee to propitiate Him on behalf of this sufferer.[^304]

*Ode 8. Heirmos:*

*Daniel stretched forth his hands...*[^305]

*Troparion:*

Thou showest unstinting mercy unto all according to Thy great mercy, O Compassionate One. Therefore we all have gathered together, mystically prefiguring the multitude of Thy compassions, offering the anointing with holy oil unto the sufferer; do Thou also visit him.[^306]

*Troparion:*

With the streams of Thy mercy, O Christ, and by the anointing of Thy priests, wash away, as a compassionate Lord, the sicknesses and malice and insidious assault from him who is tormented in the straits of passions; that, being cleansed by Thee, he may sing praise unto Thee unto the ages.[^307]

*Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit.*

*Troparion:*

Now we invoke from on high Thine abundant mercy upon the consecration of this oil, O Master, Thrice-radiant Divine Unity: diminish not Thy mercy from Thy servant, O Savior, who with faith cries aloud: Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord; sing praise and exalt Him above all.[^308]

*Both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

*Theotokion:*

As a most glorious crown, O pure one, human nature received thy divine childbearing, which hath crushed the jaws of the enemies and mightily vanquished them; therefore with thy joyful radiances we together sing praise unto thee, O all-lauded Sovereign Lady.[^309]

*Ode 9. Heirmos:*

*A stone cut without hands...*[^310]

*Troparion:*

Look down from heaven, O Compassionate One; show forth Thy mercy unto all; grant now Thy protection and Thy power unto those who flee unto Thee, through the divine anointing of Thy priests, O Lover of mankind.[^311]

*Troparion:*

We have seen the multitude of Thy divine goodness, O all-good Savior, the gift of the Holy Spirit which, having ascended on high, Thou didst send unto Thy partakers, O Master; give now likewise the figure of divine joy, O Master, unto those who pray unto Thee.[^312]

*Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit.*

*Troparion:*

Show compassion and have mercy, O Savior, and deliver from fierce guile; rescue from the arrows of the evil one the souls and bodies of Thy servants, as a merciful God, making them worthy of the divine anointing.[^313]

*Both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

*Theotokion:*

Receiving hymns and supplications from thy servants, O Virgin: deliver from grievous diseases and from all assaults of the enemies, by thy prayers, him who with us flees unto thy divine shelter, O most pure one.[^314]

*It is truly meet...*[^315]

*Exapostilarion, Podoben: "Thou hast visited us from on high" (Посѣти́лъ ны є́стъ свы́ше):*[^316]

With Thy merciful eye, O Good One, look down upon our supplication who are gathered in Thy holy temple today, to anoint with divine oil Thy sick servants.[^317]

*Then the Stichera, Tone 4, Podoben: "Thou hast given a sign" (Да́сть зна́менїе):*[^318]

*Sticheron 1:*

Thou hast given Thy grace through Thine Apostles, O benign Lover of mankind, to heal the wounds and diseases of all through Thy holy oil; therefore, as a compassionate One, sanctify and have mercy now through Thine oil upon him who comes in faith; cleanse him from every disease, and richly make him worthy of Thine incorruptible delight.[^319]

*Sticheron 2:*

Looking down uncircumscribed with Thine all-gentle eyes from heaven, as Thou art compassionate, O Lover of mankind, and having signed our senses with Thy right hand through Thy divine oil: [look upon him] who flees unto Thee with faith and asks remission of sins, and grant healing to soul and body, that he may glorify Thee with love, magnifying Thy dominion.[^320]

*Sticheron 3:*

Through the anointing of Thine oil and the touch of the holy priests from on high, O Lover of mankind: deliver Thy servants from sickness; wash away the defilements of the soul's fall, and drive away bodily illnesses; assuage sorrows and heal all wounds; and in every adversity show Thyself a helper unto us.[^321]

*Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit.*

*Theotokion:*

Thee, the most honorable royal palace, do I implore, O all-hymned one: cleanse my mind defiled by every sin, and make it a fair dwelling-place of the all-divine Trinity; that, being saved, I, thine unprofitable servant, may magnify thy power and boundless mercy.[^322]

*Both now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.*

*Theotokion:*

Preserve Thy servants from all afflictions, O blessed Theotokos, that we all may cry aloud unto thee: Rejoice, sole protection of our souls![^323]

*Then the Trisagion: "O Most Holy Trinity..."; after the "Our Father...", "For Thine is the kingdom..."; then the Troparion, Tone 4:*[^324]

Quick to protect, Thou Who alone art Christ: show forth swift visitation from on high, O our Savior; deliver Thy sick servant from illnesses, and raise him up from bitter wounds, to sing praise unto Thee and glorify Thee unceasingly, by the intercessions of the Theotokos, O Thou Who alone art the Lover of mankind.[^325]

*Then the first priest begins the deacon's litany; or the deacon, if there be one:*[^326]

**Deacon:** In peace let us pray to the Lord.

**Choir:** Lord, have mercy.

**Deacon:** For the peace from above, and for the salvation of our souls, let us pray to the Lord.

**Choir:** Lord, have mercy.

**Deacon:** For the peace of the whole world, and the good estate [of the holy churches of God, and the union of all, let us pray to the Lord.]

**Choir:** Lord, have mercy.

**Deacon:** For this holy temple, and for those that with faith [reverence, and fear of God enter therein, let us pray to the Lord.]

**Choir:** Lord, have mercy.

**Deacon:** That this oil may be blessed by the power, operation, and descent of the Holy Spirit, let us pray to the Lord.

**Choir:** Lord, have mercy.

**Deacon:** For the servant of God, [Name], and for divine[^327] visitation, and for the remission of his transgressions, and for the coming upon him of the grace of the Holy Spirit, let us pray to the Lord.

**Choir:** Lord, have mercy.

**Deacon:** Help us, save us, have mercy on us, and keep us, O God, by Thy grace.

**Choir:** Lord, have mercy.

**Deacon:** Commemorating our most holy, pure, and most blessed Lady, the Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary, with all the saints, let us commend ourselves and one another, and our whole life unto Christ our God.[^328]

**Choir:** To Thee, O Lord.

*Then the first of the priests says the prayer over the oil before the lamp:*

*Be it known that in the Great Church, instead of water, they pour wine into the lamp of the oil-receiver.*[^329]

**Deacon:** Let us pray to the Lord.

**Choir:** Lord, have mercy.

**Priest:** O Lord, Who by Thy mercy and Thy compassions healest the brokenness of our souls and bodies: do Thou Thyself, O Master, sanctify this oil, that it may be unto those that are anointed therewith for healing, and for the removal of every passion, defilement of flesh and spirit, and of all malice; that in this also Thy most holy Name may be glorified: of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^330]

*While this prayer is being said by the priests, these verses are sung. Tone 4:*[^331]

*Troparion 1, Tone 4:*

Quick to protect, Thou Who alone art Christ: show forth swift visitation from on high unto Thy suffering servants;

deliver them from sicknesses and bitter diseases; raise them up to sing praise unto Thee and glorify Thee, by the intercessions of the Theotokos, O Thou Who alone art the Lover of mankind.[^332]

*Troparion 2, Tone 4:*

Blinded in the eyes of my soul, I come unto Thee, O Christ, as the man blind from birth, crying unto Thee in repentance: Be merciful unto us, O Thou Who alone art beneficent![^333]

*Troparion 3, Tone 4:*

My soul, O Lord, grievously paralyzed by all manner of sins and unfitting deeds, raise up by Thy divine interposition, as of old Thou didst raise up the paralytic; that, being saved, I may cry aloud unto Thee, O Compassionate One: Grant me healing, O Christ![^334]

*Troparion 4, Tone 4:*

As a disciple of the Lord, righteous one, thou didst receive the Gospel; as a martyr, thou hast that which is incontrovertible; boldness, as the Brother of God; to intercede, as a hierarch: pray unto Christ God that our souls may be saved.[^335]

*Troparion 5, Tone 4:*

The Only-begotten Word of God the Father, coming unto us in the last days, O blessed James, showed thee forth as the first shepherd and teacher unto the people of Jerusalem, and a faithful steward of the spiritual mysteries; therefore we all honor thee, O Apostle.[^336]

*Kontakion of Saint Nicholas, Tone 3:*

In Myra, O holy one, thou wast shown forth as a hierarch; for having fulfilled the Gospel of Christ, O venerable one, thou didst lay down thy life for thy people;[^337]

saving the innocent from death; therefore thou wast sanctified as a great initiate of the mysteries of God's grace.[^338]

*Troparion of Saint Demetrius, Tone 3:*

The inhabited world found thee to be a great champion in adversities, O passion-bearer, overcoming the heathen; for even as thou didst cast down the pride of Lyaeus, strengthening Nestor for the combat, so, O holy one, pray unto Christ God to grant us great mercy.[^339]

*Troparion of Saint Panteleimon, Tone 3:*

Holy passion-bearer and healer Panteleimon, entreat the merciful God, that He grant remission of sins unto our souls.[^340]

*Troparion of the Holy Unmercenaries, Tone 8:*

Holy Unmercenaries and wonderworkers, visit our infirmities: freely ye have received, freely give.[^341]

*Kontakion of Saint John the Theologian, Tone 2:*

Thy greatnesses, O virgin, who shall recount? For thou gushest forth wonders and pourest out healings, and prayest for our souls, as the Theologian and friend of Christ.[^342]

*Theotokion:*

Fervent prayer, and insurmountable wall, fountain of mercy, haven of the world: earnestly do we cry aloud unto thee: O Theotokos and Sovereign Lady, come quickly, and deliver us from afflictions, thou who alone art swift to protect![^343]

*Then the Prokeimenon, Tone 4:*

**Reader:** I said: O Lord, have mercy on me, and heal my soul; for I have sinned against Thee.

*Verse:* The Lord will help Him on His bed of sickness.

*The reading is from the General Epistle of James:*[^344]

**Reader:** Brethren, take as an example of suffering affliction and of longsuffering the prophets, who have spoken in the Name of the Lord.

Behold, we count them blessed that endure. Ye have heard of the endurance of Job, and have seen the end intended by the Lord, that the Lord is full of compassion and merciful.

But above all things, my brethren, swear not, neither by heaven, nor by the earth, neither by any other oath; but let your "yea" be yea, and your "nay" be nay, lest ye fall into hypocrisy.

Is any among you suffering affliction? Let him pray. Is any cheerful? Let him sing psalms. Is any sick among you? Let him call for the priests of the church, and let them pray[^345] over Him, anointing Him with oil in the Name of the Lord. And the prayer of faith shall save the sick, and the Lord shall raise Him up; and if he have committed sins, they shall be forgiven Him. Confess therefore your trespasses one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed; for the prayer of a righteous man is of great power in its working.[^346]

*Alleluia, Tone 2:*

**Reader:** I will sing of mercy and judgment unto Thee, O Lord.[^347]

*The reading is from the Holy Gospel according to Luke:*

**Deacon:** Let us be attentive.

**Priest:** At that time, a certain lawyer came unto Jesus, testing Him and saying: Teacher, what shall I do to inherit eternal life? He said unto him: What is written in the law?[^348]

**Priest:** What is written in the law? How readest thou?

And he answering said: Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy strength, and with all thy mind; and thy neighbor as thyself.

And He said unto him: Thou hast answered right; this do, and thou shalt live.

But he, willing to justify himself, said unto Jesus: And who is my neighbor?

And Jesus answering said: A certain man went down from Jerusalem to Jericho, and fell among thieves, who stripped him of his raiment, and wounded him, and departed, leaving him half dead.[^349] And by chance

**Priest:** there came down a certain priest that way; and when he saw him, he passed by on the other side.

And likewise a Levite, when he was at the place, came and looked on him, and passed by on the other side.

But a certain Samaritan, as he journeyed, came where he was; and when he saw him, he had compassion on him,

and went to him, and bound up his wounds, pouring in oil and wine;

and set him on his own beast, and brought him to an inn, and took care of him.

And on the morrow when he departed, he took out two denarii, and gave them to the innkeeper,

and said unto him: Take care of him; and whatsoever thou spendest more, when I come again, I will repay thee.

Which now of these three, thinkest thou, was neighbor[^350]

**Priest:** unto him that fell among the thieves?

And he said: He that showed mercy on him.

Then said Jesus unto Him: Go, and do Thou likewise.[^351]

*Then the Litany:*

**Deacon:** Have mercy on us, O God, according to Thy great mercy, we pray Thee, give ear and have mercy.[^352]

**Choir:** Lord, have mercy. *(Thrice)*

**Deacon:** Again we pray for mercy, life, health, and salvation, for visitation, and for the remission of the sins of the servant of God, *[Name];*[^353]

**Choir:** Lord, have mercy. *(Thrice)*

**Deacon:** Again we pray that every transgression of his, voluntary and involuntary, may be forgiven him;[^354]

**Choir:** Lord, have mercy. *(Thrice)*

**Deacon:** Again we pray that the Lord God may send down upon him the grace of deliverance, and raise him up from his bed of sickness from weakness; let us all say: O Lord,[^355] give ear and have mercy.

**Choir:** Lord, have mercy. *(Thrice)*

*Exclamation:*

**Priest:** For Thou art a merciful God and the Lover of mankind, and unto Thee do we send up glory: to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^356]

**Deacon:** Let us pray to the Lord.

*The priest says this prayer:*

[Fol. 93v. Initial B.]
**Priest:** O Unoriginate, Incomprehensible, Holy of holies, Who didst send Thine Only-begotten Son, Who heals every disease and every infirmity of our souls and bodies: send down Thy Holy Spirit, and sanctify this oil; and make it unto this Thy servant, *[Name],* who is being anointed, for the complete deliverance of his sins, and for the inheritance of the Kingdom of Heaven.[^357]

*Be it known that some read this prayer only unto this point, with the exclamation: For Thine it is to have mercy and to save; but some read it also in its entirety unto the end.*[^358]

[Fol. 94r. Initial T.]
**Priest:** For Thou art God, great and marvelous, Who keepest Thy covenant and Thy mercy unto them that love Thee; Who grantest deliverance from sins for the sake of Thy holy Child Jesus Christ; Who didst regenerate us from sin; Who enlightenest the blind, and settest upright them that are cast down; Who lovest the righteous, and showest mercy unto sinners; Who didst renew us from darkness and the shadow of death, saying unto them that are in bonds: Come forth; and unto them that are in darkness: Be enlightened; for there hath shone in our hearts the light[^359]

**Priest:** of the knowledge of Thy face, through Whom for our sake Thou appearedst upon earth, and didst dwell among men.[^360] Unto them that received Thee Thou gavest power to become children of God, granting us the adoption of sons through the laver of regeneration, and making us free from the oppression of the devil. Because Thou didst not deign that we should be cleansed in blood, but by holy oil hast given us the figure of Thy Cross, so that we may be Christ's flock, a royal priesthood, a holy nation; and having cleansed us with water, and sanctified us with Thy Holy Spirit: do Thou Thyself, O Master and Lord, give grace unto this Thy ministry, even as Thou gavest unto Moses Thy servant, and unto Samuel[^361]

**Priest:** Thy beloved; and unto all that throughout every generation have pleased Thee: so make us also to be ministers of Thy New Covenant over this oil, which Thou hast purchased with the precious blood of Thy Christ; that, having put off worldly lusts, we may die unto sin and live unto righteousness, having put on our Lord Jesus Christ through the anointing of sanctification of this oil about to be applied. Be Thou, O Lord, this oil: an oil of gladness, an oil of sanctification, a royal garment, a helmet of power, the turning away of every operation of the devil, an unassailable seal, gladness of heart, eternal joy; so that they who are anointed with this [oil] of regeneration[^362]

**Priest:** oil may be fearsome unto adversaries, and may shine forth in the splendors of Thy saints, having no spot or blemish, that they may be received into Thine everlasting resting places, and receive the prize of the high calling.

*Exclamation:*

For Thine it is to have mercy on us and to save us, our God, and unto Thee do we send up glory: to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^363]

*And after the prayer, the priest takes of the holy oil, and anoints the one performing the Holy Unction, saying this prayer:*[^364]

[Fol. 95v. Initial O.]
**Priest:** O Holy Father, Physician of souls and bodies, Who didst send Thine Only-begotten Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, Who heals every disease[^365]

**Priest:** and delivers from death: heal also this Thy servant *[Name]* from the infirmity of soul and body that holds him, and quicken him by the grace of Thy Christ; by the intercessions of our all-pure Sovereign Lady the Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary; by the protection of the honorable bodiless Heavenly Powers; by the power of the Precious and Life-creating Cross; of the honorable, glorious Prophet, Forerunner, and Baptist John; of the holy, glorious, and all-praised Apostles; of the holy, glorious, and victorious Martyrs; of our venerable and God-bearing Fathers; of the holy and healing Unmercenaries Cosmas and Damian, Cyrus and John,[^366]

**Priest:** Panteleimon and Hermolaus; Sampson and Diomedes; Photius and Anicetus; of the holy and righteous Ancestors of God, Joachim and Anna; and of all the saints. Amen.[^367]

*Exclamation:*

For Thou art the fountain of healings, our God, and unto Thee do we send up glory: to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.

**Priest:** Master, Lord our God, Physician of souls and bodies,[^486] Who curest long-standing passions, and healest every infirmity and every disease among the people;[^487] Who desirest that all men should be saved and come unto the knowledge of the truth;[^488] Who willest not the death of a sinner, but rather that he turn and live.[^489] For Thou, O Lord, in the Old Covenant didst appoint repentance unto them that sin: unto David, and unto the Ninevites, and unto them that were before them.[^490] And furthermore, in Thy bodily coming, Thou didst not call the righteous, but sinners to repentance;[^491] as the publican, as the harlot,[^492]

**Priest:** the thief;[^493] and didst receive the blasphemous persecutor, the great Paul, by reason of repentance;[^494] and Peter, the supreme Apostle, who denied Thee thrice, Thou didst receive by reason of repentance,[^495] and didst command him, saying: Thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build My Church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it; and I will give thee the keys of the Kingdom of Heaven.[^496] Therefore we also, sinners, O good Lover of mankind, according to Thine unfailing promises, taking courage, pray unto Thee and beseech Thee in this hour: Give ear unto our supplication, and receive it even as incense offered unto Thee;[^497] and visit Thy servant *[Name],*[^498]

**Priest:** and if he have sinned in word, or in deed, or in thought, either by night or by day; or have fallen under a priestly curse, or under his own malediction, or have embittered himself by an oath and be accursed:[^499] Thee do we beseech, and unto Thee do we pray: Loosen, remit, forgive him, O God, overlooking his iniquities and his sins,[^500] both those committed by him in knowledge and in ignorance;[^501] and if in anything he have transgressed Thy commandments, or sinned as bearing flesh and living in the world, or through the operation of the devil:[^502] do Thou Thyself, as a good God and the Lover of mankind, forgive; for there is no man that shall live and not[^503]

**Priest:** sin; for Thou alone art without sin.[^504] Thy righteousness is an everlasting righteousness, and Thy word is truth;[^505] for Thou didst not create man for destruction, but for the keeping of Thy commandments, and for the inheritance of life incorruptible.[^506] For Thou art a God of mercies and compassions; and unto Thee do we send up glory, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^507]

*Prayer of Imposition of the Gospel:*[^509]

**Priest:** Holy King, compassionate and plenteous in mercy, Lord Jesus Christ, Son and Word of the living God,[^510] Who desirest not the death of a sinner, but that he turn and live:[^511] I lay not my sinful hand upon the head of him that is come unto Thee in sins, and asks through us of Thee the remission of sins; but Thy mighty and strong hand, which is in this Holy Gospel that my fellow ministers hold upon the head of Thy servant *[Name];*[^512] and I pray unto Thee with them, and beseech Thy merciful and malice-free love for mankind, O God our Savior:[^513] Who through Thy Prophet Nathan, when David repented[^514]

**Priest:** of his own sins, didst grant forgiveness; and didst accept the prayer of Manasseh concerning repentance:[^515] do Thou Thyself, O Master, accept also Thy servant, repenting of the sins committed by him, with Thine accustomed love for mankind, overlooking all his transgressions.[^516] For Thou art our God, Who didst command to forgive seventy times seven unto them that fall into sins;[^517] for as is Thy greatness, so also is Thy mercy.[^518] And unto Thee is due all glory, honor, and worship, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^519]

*And then the sick person kisses the Holy Gospel. And the priests, having finished the prayer, say this:*[^520]

**Priests:** The blessing of the Lord God and our Savior Jesus Christ, unto the healing of soul and body to the servant of God *[Name],* now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^521]

*And the priests begin to anoint one another, and all that seek this blessing, singing this Sticheron, Tone 4, Automelon:*[^522]

*Tone 4, Automelon: "Having a fountain of healings" (Исто́чникъ и҆сцѣле́нїемъ):*[^523]

**Choir:** Having a fountain of healings, O holy Unmercenaries, ye grant healings unto all that ask, as having been accounted worthy of great gifts from the ever-flowing fountain of our Savior.[^524] For the Lord said unto you, as unto peers of the Apostles: Behold, I have given you authority over unclean spirits, that ye should cast them out, and[^525]

**Choir:** to heal every infirmity and every disease.[^526] Therefore, having lived well according to His commandments, freely ye received, freely give;[^527] healing the passions of our souls and bodies.[^528]

*Glory... both now... Theotokion:*[^529]

*Theotokion:*

**Choir:** Held fast by grievous disease, pierced through on either side:[^530] my spirit faints in many sins, and my body again aches with sores and suppuration.[^531] From both heal me, O Theotokos; for thou canst do whatsoever thou willest, asking Christ Whom thou didst bear; Whom do thou beseech to grant compassion unto the world, and great mercy.[^532]

*And after this, having anointed one another mutually,*[^533] *they anoint also the sick person. And they say the litany, each in his turn:*[^534]

**Priest [or Deacon]:** Have mercy on us, O God, according to Thy great mercy, we pray Thee: give ear and have mercy.[^535]

**Priest [or Deacon]:** Again we pray for mercy, life, health, and salvation, and for the visitation of the servant of God *[Name].*[^536]

**Priest [or Deacon]:** That every transgression, voluntary and involuntary, may be forgiven him.[^537]

**Priest [or Deacon]:** That the Lord God may send down upon him the grace of deliverance, and raise him up from his bed of sickness; let us all say:[^538]

*Ecphonesis:*

**Priest:** For Thou art a merciful God and the Lover of mankind, and unto Thee do we send up glory: to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever,[^539]

**Priest:** and unto ages of ages.[^540]

*Then the dismissal.*[^541] *And he who called [the priests] for the Holy Unction makes a bow, saying:*

**He who called:** Bless me, holy fathers, and forgive me, a sinner. *(Thrice)*[^542]

### Notes

[^261]: Fol. 78r. The Order of the Holy Oil (Holy Unction / Euchelaion) Chanted by Seven Priests (*Послѣ́дованїе ст҃го ма́сла пѣва́ємаго ѿ седьмѝ попѡ́въ* / Greek: *Ἀκολουθία τοῦ Ἁγίου Εὐχελαίου*). The Sacrament of Holy Unction, instituted according to the Apostolic injunction in James 5:14–15 ("Is any sick among you? Let him call for the presbyters of the Church, and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord..."). In Byzantine and Kyivan euchology, the full solemn office is celebrated by a college of seven presbyters (reflecting the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit, Isaiah 11:2), though rubrics permit fewer if seven are unavailable. The rite opens with the preparation of the dish of wheat (*пшени́ца*), symbolizing new life and resurrection (John 12:24), holding a vessel filled with pure olive oil and red wine (commemorating the Good Samaritan, Luke 10:34), together with seven wands wrapped in cotton (*се́дмь па́личицъ*) for the sevenfold anointings.
[^262]: Fol. 78v. White Vestments, Paschal Symbolism, and the Censing at Holy Unction (*ѡ҆блъ́кшисѧ въ фело́ны бѣ́лы*). The rubrics prescribe that all ministering presbyters vest in white or bright phelonia (*λευκαῖς στολαῖς*), symbolizing the radiant joy, healing light, and spiritual resurrection bestowed through Christ's healing touch. The distribution of lit tapers to all seven presbyters and the initial circular censing around the analogion evoke the bridal and paschal character of the sacrament.
[^263]: Fol. 78v. Psalm 142 LXX [MT 143] as the Penitential Prologue to Holy Unction (*Ѱло́мъ рм҃в: Гдⷭ҇и оу҆слы́ши мл҃твоу мою̀...*). Psalm 142—the final Psalm of the Six Psalms of Matins—serves as the universal euchological opening for supplication in times of affliction and illness, pleading for divine mercy, guidance, and rescue from the spiritual and bodily snare of death.
[^264]: Fol. 79r. The Penitential Alleluia in Tone 6. In Byzantine and Slavonic liturgical tradition, chanting the Alleluia with penitential verses replaces the festive "God is the Lord" during Lenten services and rites of penitence and supplication, setting a somber, contrite tone for the unction office.
[^265]: Fol. 79r. Verses of Psalm 6:1–2 LXX [MT 6:1–2]. The Alleluia psalm-verses are drawn from Psalm 6, the first of the seven Penitential Psalms of the Psalter: *"O Lord, rebuke me not in Thine anger, neither chasten me in Thy wrath. Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I am weak; heal me, O Lord, for my bones are troubled."* In liturgical medicine, bodily illness is directly approached within the sphere of divine chastening and spiritual restoration.
[^266]: Fol. 79v. The Classical Byzantine Theotokion of Compassion (*Мл҃рдїа двє́ри ѿвръзѝ на́мъ...* / *Τῆς εὐσπλαγχνίας τὴν πύλην ἄνοιξον ἡμῖν...*). This ubiquitous troparion, common to the Ordinary Beginning of many services, invokes the Mother of God as the portal of divine mercy and refuge from bodily and spiritual perils (*ѿ бѣ́дъ* / *ἐκ τῶν περιστάσεων*).
[^267]: Fol. 79v. Psalm 50 LXX [MT 51]. King David's great psalm of repentance (*"Have mercy on me, O God, according to Thy great mercy..."*) stands as the indispensable threshold before every liturgical canon, purifying the soul for sacramental supplication.
[^268]: Fol. 79v. The Canon of Holy Unction by Arsenios and its Acrostic (*Кано́нъ, є҆моу́же краегране́сїе: мл҃тва є҆ле́а, ѱало́мъ Арсе́нїевъ*). Greek: *Εὐχὴ ἐλαίου, ψαλμὸς Ἀρσενίου*. The canon of Holy Unction is the work of Arsenios (9th century, likely Arsenios of Corfu or a Studite hymnographer). In Greek, the initial letters of the troparia form the alphabetical acrostic: *Εὐχὴ ἐλαίου, ψαλμὸς Ἀρσενίου*. As typical in Church Slavonic translations, the acrostic title is faithfully transcribed and translated in the rubric even though the Slavonic poetic verses cannot sustain the literal Greek acrostic wordplay.
[^269]: Fol. 79v. Ode 1 Heirmos: *Мо́рѧ чръмна́го поучи́нꙋ немо́кръ[ными стопа́ми...]*; Greek: *Θαλάσσης τὸ ἐρυθραῖον πέλαγος ἀβρόχοις ἴχνεσιν...* Tone 4. The printer records the opening incipit, breaking off at *немо́кръ-* ("unwetted..."), directing the chanters to complete the familiar heirmos of the Passover through the Red Sea (Exodus 14).
[^270]: Fol. 80r. The Oil of Divine Compassion and the Parable of the Wise Virgins. Greek: *Μύρῳ τῷ τῆς σῆς εὐσπλαγχνίας, Δέσποτα...* The opening troparion of the canon connects the physical oil of unction (*ἔлαιον*) with divine mercy (*ἔλεος*), playing upon the classical Patristic paronomasia (*elaion / eleos*). The preservation of the oil of the faithful (*съхранѧ́ѧ ма́сло вѣ́рныхъ*) evokes the oil of good works and grace preserved in the lamps of the wise virgins (Matthew 25:1–13).
[^271]: Fol. 80r. The Mystical Anointing and the Universal Earthly Mercy. Greek: *Ἵλεων τῷ σῷ ἐλαίῳ, Δέσποτα...* Invoking Psalm 32:5 LXX [MT 33:5] ("The earth is full of the mercy of the Lord"), the hymn affirms that sacramental anointing mystically communicates that transcendent divine mercy which surpasses all human comprehension (Philippians 4:7).
[^272]: Fol. 80v. Apostolic Commission of Anointing and Remission of Sins. Concluding the *Glory* troparion from leaf 80r, which invokes Mark 6:13 ("And they anointed with oil many that were sick, and healed them") and James 5:15 ("And the prayer of faith shall save the sick... and if he have committed sins, they shall be forgiven him"). The sacred unction acts both as bodily medicine and spiritual absolution.
[^273]: Fol. 80v. The Theotokion of Ode 1: The Abyss of Peace (*Поучи́на ми́рови* / Greek: *Ἡ μόνη τὸν ἄβυσσον εἰρήνης κυήσασα*). A play on words between *μύρον* (myrrh/chrism) and *εἰρήνη* (peace) / *мир* (world/peace), honoring the Mother of God as the bearer of the Prince of Peace (Isaiah 9:6), who possesses an ocean of therapeutic healing for afflicted mortals.
[^274]: Fol. 80v. Ode 3 Heirmos: *Весели́тсѧ ѡ҆ тебѣ̀ цр҃кви твоѧ̀ Хрⷭ҇ѐ...*; Greek: *Εὐφραίνεται ἐπὶ σοὶ ἡ Ἐκκλησία σου, Χριστέ...* Tone 4. The familiar heirmos of Ode 3 celebrates the joy of the Church crying aloud: "Thou art my strength, O Lord, my refuge and my firm foundation."
[^275]: Fol. 80v. Christ Marvelous Among Men. Greek: *Σὺ μόνος ὑπάρχων θαυμαστός...* Confessing Christ as the sole source of divine healing and compassion, imploring Him to bestow His sanctifying and restorative grace from on high (*съвы́шше*) upon the suffering Christian.
[^276]: Fol. 80v. The Olive Branch of Noah as Typology of Sacramental Peace (*Ма́сличный сꙋ́чецъ...*). Greek: *Ἐλαίας τὸν κλάδον ποτέ...* Recalling the dove returning to Noah's Ark with an olive twig in its beak, signaling that the waters of the flood had subsided from off the earth (Genesis 8:11). In Christian typology, the olive branch prefigures the reconciliation of God with mankind and the pacification of bodily and spiritual storms through the oil of unction.
[^277]: Fol. 81r. The Radiant Lamp of Divine Light in Holy Oil (*Свѣ́щꙋ бж҃тве́наго свѣ́та...*). Greek: *Λαμπάδα φωτὸς θείου...* Continuing the imagery of the lamp burning before the holy icons and the analogion, beseeching Christ, the true Light of the world (John 8:12), to illuminate the bodily darkness and spiritual weakness of those anointed in faith.
[^278]: Fol. 81r. Maternal Intercession Dispersing Bitter Malady (*При́зри мл҃тивно, мт҃и всѧ́ческимъ тво́рцу...*). Greek: *Ἔπιδε ἵλεως, Μῆτερ τοῦ πάντων Ποιητοῦ...* Pleading for the maternal intercession of the Virgin Mary, the Mother of the Creator, to loose and dissolve the bitter affliction (*го́ркоую зло́боу* / *πικρὰν ὀδύνην*) weighing down the suffering person.
[^279]: Fol. 81r. Sessional Hymn (Kathisma) of Holy Unction and the River of Mercy (*Сѣда́лна, гла́съ д҃. По: Пасты́рскїи сопль вѣща́нїа твоє̀*). Model melody: Greek: *Ὁ ποιμενικὸς αὐλὸς τῆς θεολογίας σου...* (*The shepherd's pipe of thy theology / preaching*), the famous podoben in Tone 4 originally composed for hierarch-theologians (St. Gregory of Nazianzus, Jan 25; St. John Chrysostom, Nov 13). Here adapted to the euchological supplication of Unction, praising Christ as the boundless river of mercy (Psalm 45:5 LXX [MT 46:4]) and inexhaustible fountain of miracles (*и҆сто́чникъ неза́вистно* / *πηγὴν ἀφθόνως*) who cleanses and heals all believers.
[^280]: Fol. 81v. Conclusion of the Sessional Hymn. Concluding the earnest plea of the faithful that they ever have recourse to Christ's divine goodness (*бл҃гти молѧ́щесѧ*).
[^281]: Fol. 81v. Model Melody: *Tone 4, Podoben: "Thou Who wast lifted up upon the Cross" (Възнесы́йсѧ)*. Greek: *Ὁ ὑψωθεὶς ἐν τῷ Σταυρῷ ἑκουσίως...* The solemn Tone 4 podoben of the Feast of the Universal Exaltation of the Cross (Sept 14) and general troparion of the Holy Cross.
[^282]: Fol. 81v. Christ the Supreme Physician and Deliverer from Death (*Вра́чъ и҆ помо́щникъ болѧ́щимъ...*). Greek: *Ἰατρὸς καὶ βοηθὸς τῶν καμνόντων...* This sessional hymn evokes the core pastoral heart of Holy Unction: confessing Christ as the sole true Healer of soul and body, pleading that the sick servant be snatched from the jaws of impending bodily death to glorify God in the assembly of the living (Psalm 117:17–18 LXX [MT 118:17–18]).
[^283]: Fol. 81v. Ode 4 Heirmos: *Въздви́жена тѧ̀ ви́дѣвше цр҃кви...*; Greek: *Ἐπαρθέντα σε ἰδοῦσα ἡ Ἐκκλησία ἐπὶ Σταυροῦ...* Tone 4. Rubric prescribing the final anointing of the sick person and the concluding Insistent Litany: *пома́зꙋютъ и҆ болѧ́щаго · и҆ гл҃ют є҆кте́нїю є҆ди́нъ по є҆диномꙋ ра́дом* (they anoint also the sick person; and they say the litany, one by one in turn).
