# PART XLIV: ON THE ELEVATION OF THE PANAGIA, WHEN ANYONE WISHES TO GO FORTH UNTO MERCHANDISE [^3745]

*On the Elevation of the Panagia, when anyone desires to go on merchandise:*

*When anyone desires to go forth, there is sung beforehand the Paraklesis, when he wishes.*

*Then therefore the Priest makes the beginning.*

*After this, the Trisagion. All-holy Trinity. Our Father. For Thine is the kingdom.*

*Then the penitential troparia: Have mercy on us, O Lord, have mercy on us.[^3746] Glory: O Lord, have mercy on us.[^3747] Both now: The doors of compassions...[^3748] Lord, have mercy (12 times).*

*O come, let us worship (3 times). And Psalm 50: Have mercy on me, O God...[^3749] Then: I believe in one God... Remit, forgive... Our Father. For Thine is the kingdom.*

*Then the following Theotokion troparia:*[^3750]

*Theotokion:* Most Holy Theotokos, in the time of my life forsake me not, and unto human protection entrust me not, but do thou thyself defend and save me.[^3751]

*Theotokion:* I, O holy Virgin Theotokos, flee unto thy protection; I know that I shall obtain salvation through thee, for thou canst help me, O Pure One.[^3752]

*Theotokion:* No one that hastens unto thee departs from thee put to shame, O pure Virgin Theotokos, but asks for grace and receives the gift according to the expediency of the petition.[^3753]

*Theotokion:* Whithersoever anyone is saved, thither in truth he flees; and what other such refuge is there as thou, O Theotokos, sheltering our souls?[^3754]

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:* Come unto the supplication of thy servants, O All-immaculate One, quelling the fierce assaults against us, exchanging every sorrow for us; for thee alone as a firm and sure foundation do we have, and thy protection have we acquired; let us not be put to shame, O Lady, who call upon thee; make haste unto the supplication of those who faithfully cry unto thee: Rejoice, O Lady, help of all, joy and protection and salvation of our souls![^3755]

*Theotokion:* Thou art the protectress of all, O Good One, who with faith flee unto thy mighty protection; for another mediatress before God in dangers and tribulations we sinners have not, who art ever an intercessor[^3756] burdened with many sins, O Mother of God the Most High; therefore unto thee do we fall down: deliver thy servants from every circumstance![^3757]

*And he elevates the Panagia, saying:*

**Officiant:** Great is the name of the Holy Trinity![^3758]

All-holy Lady Theotokos, help thy servant.[^3759]

*Another says:*

**Reader:** By her prayers, O God, have mercy and save us!

*Then:*

*Troparion:* Thy womb became a holy table, holding the Heavenly Bread, of Whom whosoever eats dies not, as the Nourisher of all hath said, O Mother of God.[^3760]

*Troparion:* Make us worthy of thy gifts, O Virgin Theotokos, overlooking our transgressions, and granting healings unto those who in faith receive thy blessing, O Most-pure One.[^3761]

*And they sing: It is meet and right... Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*[^3762]

*Hymn to the Theotokos:* In thee rejoices, O full of grace, all creation: the assembly of angels and the race of mankind; O sanctified temple and rational paradise, boast of virginity, from whom God was incarnate and became an Infant, He Who is our God before the ages! For He made thy womb a throne, and made thy body more spacious than the heavens! In thee rejoices, O full of grace, all creation; glory be to thee![^3763]

*Kontakion, Tone 8:*

*Kontakion:* To the Champion Leader the trophies of victory, and as ones delivered from evils, thanksgiving do we ascribe unto thee, thy city, O Theotokos! But as thou hast might invincible, from all[^3764] dangers free me, that I may cry unto thee: Rejoice, O Bride unwedded![^3765]

*Troparion:* All my hope do I place in thee, O Mother of God; keep me under thy protection.[^3766]

*Troparion:* Having thee, O Theotokos, as our hope and protectress, we fear not the assault of the enemy, for thou savest our souls.[^3767]

*Troparion:* O Lady, receive the supplications of thy servants, and deliver us from every necessity and sorrow.[^3768]

*And dismissal. But if he who desires to go on a journey is present, there is no dismissal, but the priest says:*

**Priest:** Let us pray to the Lord.

*And the prayer:*

**Priest:** O Lord our God, the true and living Way, Who didst journey with Thy servant Joseph[^3769] journey also, O Master, with Thy servant *[Name]*, and deliver [him] from every tempest and snare; and establish again peace and goodly strength, causing him to take care for all righteousness according to Thy commandments; and having become filled with earthly and heavenly good things, do Thou deign [that he] return again; for Thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages.[^3770]

*And another prayer, for those about to sail:*[^3771]

**Priest:** O Master Lord, Who didst deign to sail with Thy holy disciples and apostles, and didst rebuke the stormy wind, and didst command the waves of the sea to be stilled:[^3772] sail also with those departing in this ship, stilling every contrary wind; and raise up favorable winds, becoming their Helmsman everywhere; and turn away every assault of temptations from them; deign that they return rejoicing unto us who rejoice here; doing well and according to diligence the things begun by them, granting Thy grace in all their works, finding a saving and fair-weather haven for the voyagers and the ship; for unto Thee is due all glory, honor, and worship, to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.[^3773]

### Notes

[^3745]: Headpiece and title marking the beginning of Part XLIV: the Order for the Elevation of the Panagia for those setting out on merchandise or commercial journeys (Ѡ въꙁноше́нїи панагїи, е҆гда̀ хо́щетъ кто̀ на кꙋ́пли ѿитѝ). The Elevation of the Panagia (Greek Ὑψωσις τῆς Παναγίας), originally a monastic trapeza rite commending the brethren to the Mother of God, was adapted in Ruthenian and Byzantine Euchologia as a protective travel rite for merchants embarking on perilous voyages across land and sea.
[^3746]: The first penitential troparion of the standard liturgical sequence: Помилуй насъ, Господи, помилуй насъ (Miserere nostri, Domine).
[^3747]: The second troparion, chanted at "Glory": Слава... Господи, помилуй насъ, на Тя бо уповахомъ (cf. Isa. 64:8–9 LXX: "We are all the work of Thy hand... be not wroth very sore, O Lord").
[^3748]: The traditional Theotokion chanted at "Both now": И нынѣ... Милосердія двери отверзи намъ, благословенная Богородице.
[^3749]: Ps. 50 LXX (Miserere mei, Deus), recited accompanied by three metanias (прїидѣ́те поклони́мсѧ • г҃).
[^3750]: Rubric prescribing the chanting of five classic supplicatory Theotokia, which invoke the protection and intercession of the Virgin Mary over the traveler. The sequence continues onto fol. 612r (leaf p1239).
[^3751]: The renowned supplicatory Theotokion: Пресвятая Богородице, во время живота моего не остави мене, человѣческому заступленію не ввѣри мене, но Сама заступи и спаси мя.
[^3752]: The second Theotokion: Азъ, Дѣво святая Богородице, покровъ твой прибѣгаю, вѣмъ яко получу у тебе спасеніе...
[^3753]: The third Theotokion: Никтоже притекаѧй къ тебѣ посрамленъ отъ тебе отходитъ, Чистая Дѣво Богородице, но проситъ благодати, и пріемлетъ даръ на пользу прошенія.
[^3754]: The fourth Theotokion: Кождо идѣже спасается, тамо въ правду и притѣкаетъ; и кое иное таково прибѣжище якоже ты, Богородице...
[^3755]: The fifth Theotokion: Прииди къ моленію рабъ своихъ, Пренепорочная, утоляющи лютая на ны востанія... concluding with the joyful greeting: Радуйся, Владычице, всѣмъ помощнице, радости и покрове и спасеніе душъ нашихъ.
[^3756]: The celebrated dogmatic and supplicatory Theotokion: Всѣмъ предстательствуеши, Благая, прибѣгающимъ съ вѣрою въ державный твой покровъ (Greek Πάντων προστατεύεις, ἀγαθή). The text continues onto fol. 613r (leaf p1241).
[^3757]: Continuation and conclusion of the supplicatory Theotokion from fol. 612v (leaf p1240): Slavonic Ѡ҆бремененїи грѣхмѝ мно́гими, ма́ти Бг҃а вы́шнагѡ • тѣ́мже ти припада́емъ, и҆зба́ви ѿ всѧ́кагѡ ѻ҆бстоѧ́нїа рабы своя̀ (cf. Greek Πάντων προστατεύεις, ἀγαθή).
[^3758]: Ritual elevation of the Panagia bread (Greek Ὕψωσις τῆς Παναγίας). The officiant elevates the panagia vessel containing the designated prosphoric particle in honor of the Mother of God, exclaiming the ancient Trinitarian and Marian liturgical acclamation: Ве́лїе и҆́мѧ ст҃ыѧ тро́ица (Greek Μέγα τὸ ὄνομα τῆς Ἁγίας Τριάδος).
[^3759]: Personal supplication for the traveler: Пр҃таѧ влч҃це Бц҃е помозѝ рабꙋ твоемꙋ (Greek Παναγία Δέσποινα Θεοτόκε, βοήθει τῷ δούλῳ σου), followed by the choral response invoking her intercessions: Тоѧ̀ мл҃твами Бж҃е помилꙋй и҆ сп҃си на́ (Ταῖς αὐτῆς πρεσβείαις, ὁ Θεός, ἐλέησον καὶ σῶσον ἡμᾶς).
[^3760]: Allusion to John 6:48–51: "I am the bread of life... if any man eat of this bread, he shall live for ever." In Byzantine and Ruthenian Marian theology, the womb of the Virgin is typologically celebrated as the sacred mystical table bearing the Bread of Heaven (чре́во твоѐ ст҃аѧ трапе́за, и҆мꙋ́щїа хлѣ́бъ нб҃ный).
[^3761]: Troparion chanted during the fraction or consumption of the Panagia: Твои́хъ дарѡ́въ досто́йны ны́ сътворѝ Бц҃е дв҃о (Greek Τῶν σῶν δωρεῶν ἀξίους ἡμᾶς ποίησον, Θεοτόκε Παρθένε).
[^3762]: Rubric prescribing the chanting of the Marian hymn "It is meet and right" (Досто́йно е҆́сть), followed by the Little Doxology ("Glory... Both now...") and the celebrated Megalynarion hymn of St. John of Damascus.
[^3763]: The renowned hymn to the Mother of God attributed to St. John of Damascus: Ἐπὶ σοὶ χαίρει, Κεχαριτωμένη, πᾶσα ἡ κτίσις (Slavonic Ѡ҆ тебѐ ра́дꙋетсѧ Ѡ҆брадо́ваннаѧ всѧ́ка тва́рь), chanted in the Liturgy of St. Basil the Great and during festive and monastic trapeza rites.
[^3764]: The prooimion of the celebrated Kontakion of the Annunciation and Akathist Hymn: Greek Τῇ ὑπερμάχῳ στρατηγῷ τὰ νικητήρια. The 1606 Striatyn imprint witnesses the distinctive Ruthenian recensional translation Побо́рници воево́дѣ побѣди́тельнаѧ (from побо́рница, "champion / defender") rather than the standard Nikonian Взбра́нной воево́дѣ. The text continues onto fol. 614r (leaf p1243).
[^3765]: Conclusion of the Akathist Kontakion: Slavonic ꙗ҆́ко и҆мꙋ́щи дръжа́воу непобѣди́мꙋю, ѿ всѧ́кихъ мѧ̀ бѣ́дъ свободѝ • да̀ зово́у ти, ра́доусѧ невѣ́сто неневѣ́стнаа. The singular pronoun мѧ̀ ("free me", Greek με) adapts the civic hymn of Constantinople for the personal salvation and physical preservation of the traveler.
[^3766]: Ancient Byzantine compline troparion: Въсѐ ᲂу҆пова́нїе моѐ на́ тѧ възлага́ю, ма́ти Бо́жїа, съхранѝ мѧ̀ въ свое́мъ си кро́вѣ (Greek Τὴν πᾶσαν ἐλπίδα μου εἰς σὲ ἀνατίθημι, Μῆτερ τοῦ Θεοῦ, φύλαξόν με ὑπὸ τὴν σκέπην σου).
[^3767]: Marian supplicatory troparion: И҆мꙋ́ще тѧ̀ Бц҃е ᲂу҆пова́нїе и҆ пре́ста́телницꙋ, вра́жїй навѣ́тъ небои́мсѧ (Having thee, O Theotokos, as hope and mediatress, we fear not the enemy's assault).
[^3768]: Byzantine euchological troparion: Влч҃це прїимѝ мо́лбы ра́бъ свои́хъ, и҆ избави на́съ ѿ всѧ́коѧ ноу́жда и҆ скръби (Greek Δέσποινα, πρόσδεξαι τὰς δεήσεις τῶν δούλων σου).
[^3769]: Rubric and opening of the traveler's prayer (Εὐχὴ ἐπὶ ὁδοιπορούντων). The prayer addresses Christ as "the true and living Way" (John 14:6: "I am the way, the truth, and the life") and invokes the biblical typology of Joseph, whom the Lord guided, preserved, and prospered during his sojourn in Egypt (Gen. 39:2, 21; 45:5–8). The prayer continues onto fol. 614v (leaf p1244).
[^3770]: Doxological conclusion of the prayer for the wayfarer, invoking peace, divine strength, righteous conduct, and a safe return laden with temporal and spiritual blessings.
[^3771]: Rubric introducing the prayer for those embarking upon a sea voyage or river journey: И҆ дрꙋга́ѧ мл҃тва • на хотѧ́щи пла́вати (Greek Εὐχὴ ἑτέρα ἐπὶ τῶν μελλόντων πλεῖν). Maritime and fluvial commerce along the Dniester, Vistula, and Black Sea waterways constituted a perilous economic lifeline in 16th–17th century Ruthenian lands.
[^3772]: Scriptural allusion to the Gospel accounts of Christ calming the storm upon the Sea of Galilee: Matt. 8:23–27; Mark 4:36–41; Luke 8:22–25. The prayer continues onto fol. 615r (leaf p1245).
[^3773]: Byzantine maritime euchological prayer invoking Christ as Helmsman (кръмчи́й, Greek κυβερνήτης) to grant "saving and fair-weather haven" (спа́сное и҆ ведреное приста́нище, Greek λιμένα σωτήριον καὶ εὔδιον) unto the voyagers and their vessel.
