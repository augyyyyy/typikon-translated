# PART LIII: DEGREES OF KINSHIP: AND CONCERNING LAWFUL MARRIAGE, AND THOSE WHO MARRY UNLAWFULLY, AND CONCERNING THE DISTINCTION OF KINSHIP WHICH IS OF FLESH AND BLOOD [^4011]

*Where it is befitting to contract marriage, and where it is not befitting; and where it is fitting to bring any person unto marriage, and whom not to bring; and whom the law forbids, and whom it does not forbid, and whom it discerns:*[^4012]

Three boundaries there are concerning forbidden marriages:[^4013] One which is from salvific *Injunctions:*

...baptism; and the second, which is kinship according to the flesh; and the third, which is the union from marriage, that is, marital affinity [in-laws].[^4014] Moreover, marital in-laws also have many diverse rules according to conjunction, which it is fitting for true Christians very diligently to examine with investigation. And thus, when the man is fifteen years of age, and the woman twelve years of age, with the consent, desire, and blessing of parents, and of the priest.[^4015]

Be it known that what the law of holy baptism forbids:[^4016] it forbids only the baptizer from his baptized daughter [goddaughter], and forbids his son from her; and from the mother of the baptized daughter it forbids them; likewise also their other children born[^4017] *Of the Holy Fathers:*

...it forbids, that is, those of the baptizer, and of that woman whose children he baptizes; but their other relatives, lesser and greater, near and distant, it does not forbid from marriage.[^4018]

Relatives according to the flesh let them be examined well and diligently, and let them be corrected; for kinship, having a generative and lawful origin, is divided into the orders of superior and inferior degrees, and that which is from the right and left sides [collateral], and that which is from distant lineages.[^4019] And the superior of the lineage are: father, and mother, grandfather, and grandmother. And the inferior degrees are: son, and daughter, grandson, and granddaughter. And that which is from the right and left sides are: brother and sister, maternal uncle (*uika*) and paternal uncle/aunt (*lelya*), male first cousin (*anepsios*) and female first cousin (*anepsia*)[^4020] *Injunctions:*

...and indeed the paternal uncle (*stryika*) with the nephew (*anepsios*), a brother's son, are of the first degree;[^4021] and full brothers (*prisnii, yezhe samobratni*) are of the second degree; and first cousins (*pervii bratuchada*) are of the fourth degree; and second cousins (*vtorii bratuchada*) are of the sixth degree; and third cousins (*tretii bratuchada*) are of the eighth degree.[^4022] In all these degrees there is by no means any marital union. But the children and offspring of third cousins are unforbidden unto marriage; for lineage ascends unto the seventh degree, and the eighth is loosed [permitted].[^4023]

Likewise also let marital in-laws be examined well, lest in any way without discretion the union be unlawful, and the wrath of God overtake such persons: and especially bishops and[^4024] *Of the Holy Fathers:*

...spiritual fathers who do not direct rightly concerning these things.[^4025]

Be it known that my own brother by birth, and the brother of my wife, and her mother, are first in-laws; and their first cousins are second in-laws; likewise also their second cousins are third in-laws. And first in-laws are not to unite with first in-laws; nor first with second; but with third in-laws let first in-laws unite without hindrance.[^4026] But my sister with the brother of my wife by no means; nor is my brother's son or daughter to unite with the nephews/nieces (*anepsioi*) of my wife; nor unto my stepmother may I ever even contemplate [marriage], nor unto her daughter whom she has[^4027] *Injunctions:*

...by another husband and not by my father; nor my son unto my sister; nor my daughter with my brother; let them not dare even in mind to contemplate [marriage] unto the daughter of my wife whom she has by another husband, for she is my daughter; nor does my son whom I have by another wife dare anything toward her; nor unto the wife of my son do I dare to contemplate [marriage].[^4028]

Unto all this kinship, therefore, it was forbidden to unite in marriage. But my first cousins do not unite with the first female cousin of my wife; nor with her second female cousin; but my first cousin unites in marriage with the third female cousin of my wife without hindrance; likewise also[^4029] *Of the Holy Fathers:*

...my second cousins unto the third cousins of my wife. The nephews and nieces (*anepsioi*) of my wife do not unite with the children of my brother or of my sister. But the children of the second cousins of my wife unite without hindrance with the children of my second cousins; while the children of the second cousins of my wife do not touch the children of my second cousins.[^4030]

Likewise it is befitting to investigate and search out with certainty also concerning the other lineages that are analogous unto these lineages; and thus to establish marriage, and unite, and accomplish [it] without any doubt, even as is befitting for true Christians: ❧[^4031] *Injunctions:*

### Notes

[^4011]: Major headpiece of Part LIII: *Сте́пени съро́дитвомъ • и҆ ѡ бра́цѣ зако́нномъ, и҆ и҆́же незако́нно посѧгающихъ • и҆ ѡ разли́чїи съро́дичства* ("Degrees of Kinship: and concerning Lawful Marriage, and Those Who Marry Unlawfully, and concerning the Distinction of Kinship Which Is of Flesh and Blood"). This marks the opening of the comprehensive Nomocanon tractate detailing the computation of consanguinity, spiritual affinity, and affinal relationship under Ruthenian Orthodox canon law.
[^4012]: Comprehensive nomocanonical summary defining the scope of marital examination: identifying which persons may be licitly joined, which unions are explicitly barred by law, and which complex cases require pastoral and judicial discernment (*разсꙋжда́етъ*).
[^4013]: The three canonical grounds of matrimonial impediment (*три предѣ́лы ѡ възбране́ныхъ бра́цѣхъ*): (1) spiritual relationship arising from Holy Baptism (*ѿ спси́телнаго кр҃ще́нїа*); (2) consanguinity or relationship according to the flesh (*по пло́ти съро́дство*); and (3) affinity arising from marital union (*ѿ бра́ка съю́зъ, си́рѣчь сва́тове*).
[^4014]: Marital affinity (*сва́тове*): Relationship created by the union of husband and wife connecting their respective families. In Byzantine and Ruthenian canon law, affinity (*affinitas* / ἀγχιστεία) produces impediments analogous to consanguinity, barring marriages between in-laws within established degrees.
[^4015]: Diligent examination and canonical age of majority for matrimony: The Striatyn Trebnyk codifies the canonical minimum age of marriage in Byzantine-Slavic Nomocanons (Ecloga II.1.1; Procheiron I.1) as fifteen years for the groom (*мꙋ́жеви сꙋ́щꙋ, патына́десѧти лѣ́тѡмъ*) and twelve years for the bride (*женѣ̀, двана́десѧтимъ лѣ́тѡмъ*), accompanied by explicit parental consent and priestly examination and blessing.
[^4016]: Canonical impediments arising from spiritual affinity (*духовное родство* / πνευματικὴ συγγένεια): The Trullan Council (Council in Trullo / Quinisext, Canon 53) established that spiritual relationship from Holy Baptism surpasses physical union, prohibiting the marriage of a godfather to the widowed mother of his goddaughter.
[^4017]: Specific scope of baptismal impediments: The text specifies that the bar directly affects the sponsor (*крсти́тель*), his goddaughter (*кр́щеная е҆мꙋ̀ дще́рь*), the sponsor's son toward the goddaughter, and the sponsor toward the natural mother of the goddaughter (*ѿ ма́тере кр́щеныѧ дще́ре*).
[^4018]: Collateral exemption in baptismal affinity: Distant and collateral relatives of the godfather and godmother outside the vertical direct line of sponsorship are not barred from contracting marriage (*про́чаа и҆́хъ рожакы̀ ма́лыѧ и҆ вели́кыѧ... не възбранѧ́етъ ѿ бра́ка и҆́хъ*), consistent with the standard Byzantine Nomocanon rulings limiting spiritual affinity to the primary parties and their children.
[^4019]: The four canonical axes of consanguinity (*съро́дство по пло́ти*): (1) ascending line (*вы́шнѧѧ*); (2) descending line (*ни́жнѧѧ*); (3) collateral line (*ѿ стра́нъ де́сныхъ и҆ лѣ́выхъ*); and (4) remote lineages (*ѿ да́лнихъ рѡ́довъ*).
[^4020]: Terminology of collateral kinship in Early Modern Ruthenian: *оу҆́ика* (maternal uncle, Ukrainian *вуйко*), *ле́лѧ* (paternal relative / aunt, Ukrainian dialectal *лельо*, *вуйна*), and the Hellenisms *а҆неѱїе́и* / *а҆неѱїе́а* (nephew/niece or first cousins, directly borrowed from Byzantine Greek ἀνεψιός / ἀνεψιά).
[^4021]: Reckoning of degrees: The text notes the first collateral step between uncle (*стры́ика*) and nephew (*а҆неѱїе́мъ бра́товѣм сн҃омъ*), and full brothers (*приснїи, е҆́же самобра́тни*) in the second degree, following the Roman-Byzantine method of counting degrees by generation up to the common ancestor and down to the relative.
[^4022]: Degree computation among cousins: First cousins (*пръ́вїи бра́тоу ча́да*) are in the 4th degree; second cousins (*вто́рїи бра́тꙋ ча́да*) in the 6th degree; and third cousins (*тре́тїи бра́тоу ча́да*) in the 8th degree.
[^4023]: The canonical limit of consanguinity: In accordance with the decrees of the Constantinopolitan Synods under Patriarchs Sisinnios II (997) and Michael Cerularius (1054), marriages within consanguinity were prohibited through the 7th degree; the 8th degree is canonically permitted (*ѻ҆смы́й раздрѣши́лсѧ е҆́стъ*).
[^4024]: Canonical culpability and ecclesiastical sanctions: Bishops and spiritual confessors who neglect thorough investigation of marital relationships incur divine wrath and severe canonical censure for countenancing incestuous or unlawful unions.
[^4025]: Admonition to spiritual confessors on ecclesiastical governance: Reiterating that the confessor's duty encompasses not merely devotional direction but strict adherence to canonical jurisprudence.
[^4026]: Degrees of affinity (*сва́тове*): Full brother and brother-in-law or mother-in-law constitute the first degree of affinity; first cousins of spouses constitute second in-laws; second cousins constitute third in-laws. Marriages between first and second in-laws are barred; marriage is permitted only with third in-laws (*тре́тїимъ сва́тѡмъ пръ́вїи сва́тове да̀ смѣша́ютсѧ невъзбра́нно*).
[^4027]: Absolute affinal prohibitions: Total prohibition against a man marrying his sister-in-law, or a brother's child marrying a spouse's nephew or niece (*а҆неѱїе́ѡмъ*), as well as the absolute bar against marrying a stepmother (*ма́чеха*) or stepdaughter.
[^4028]: Detailed prohibitions on composite households: Barring unions between half-siblings, stepdaughters, and daughters-in-law, preventing the conflation of familial and marital roles within extended patriarchal households.
[^4029]: Cross-family cousin marriage rules in affinity: Prohibiting unions between first cousins of husband and first or second cousins of wife, while allowing unions between first cousin of husband and third cousin of wife.
[^4030]: Complex affinity combinations: Detailing permissions and restrictions between second and third cousins of the respective spouses, illustrating the delicate boundary between barred affinity and permissible remote kinship.
[^4031]: Canonical analogical deduction: Confessors and ecclesiastical judges are commanded to examine all other analogous degrees of relationship (*прили́чныхъ си́мъ рѡ́домъ*) according to the same mathematical and nomocanonical principles, ensuring that Christian marriages remain unassailable and free of doubt.
