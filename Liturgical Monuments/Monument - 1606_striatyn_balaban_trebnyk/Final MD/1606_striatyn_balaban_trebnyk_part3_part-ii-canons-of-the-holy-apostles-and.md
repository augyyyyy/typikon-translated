# PART II: CANONS OF THE HOLY APOSTLES AND OF THE GOD-BEARING HOLY FATHERS CONCERNING HOLY BAPTISM

[Fol. 10r. Headpiece: Renaissance woodcut engraving with strapwork, foliate scrollwork, and mythological figures flanking a central cartouche.]

CANONS OF THE HOLY APOSTLES AND OF THE GOD-BEARING HOLY FATHERS CONCERNING HOLY BAPTISM[^61]

*Canon 47 of the Holy Apostles says:*[^62]
If any bishop or presbyter shall baptize anew one who has true baptism, or shall not baptize one who has been defiled by the ungodly, let him be deposed: as mocking the Cross and the death of the Lord, and not distinguishing priests from false priests. Likewise also Canon 50 of the Council of Carthage ordains.[^63]

*Canon 49 of the Holy Apostles:*[^64]
To baptize into the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit, according to the Lord's com- mand, but not into three unoriginate, nor into three sons, nor into three parakletes: for one is the Unoriginate Father, by reason of having no cause; and one is the Son, by reason of His ineffable generation; and one is the Paraklete, the Holy Spirit, by reason of His procession from the Unbegotten.[^65] And those bishops or presbyters who do not so baptize incur the sentence of final deposition.

*And Canon 50:*[^66]
To accomplish three baptisms of one mystery, that is, three immersions in one baptism; and at each immersion to commemorate one Name of the Holy Trinity; but not a single baptism into the death of the Lord, [a single baptism into the death of the Lord] administered: that is, the one being baptized immersed once, and in that single immersion glorifying the death of the Lord, as the Eunomian heresy, full of folly, ordains (which also Canon 7 of the Second Council mentions). For the Lord said not: "Baptize into My death"; but: *Go, teach all nations, baptizing them in the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit* [Matthew 28:19]; and any bishop or presbyter who does not do thus [the canon] destroys by deprivation of the priesthood.[^67]

*Canon 7 of the Second Ecumenical Council:*[^68]
Moreover, Canon 7 of the Second Ecumenical Council also very accurately ordains how it is fitting to receive those turning from heresies:

[accurately ordains: for having divided] all into two classes, it legislates that some should be anointed with myron, while others should be baptized from the beginning: namely, the Arians, Macedonians, Sabbatians, and Novatians (who call themselves the pure and the left), and the Quartodecimans (that is, the Tetradites), and the Apollinarians.[^69] Since, therefore, they perform the divine baptism equally with us, it is not deemed proper to baptize them again; but upon their written renunciation and anathematization of their heresy, [the canon] commands to anoint them with the holy myron: the forehead, the eyes, and the nostrils, the mouth, and the ears; and signing them, to say: "The seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit."[^70]

[The seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit.] But the Eunomians, who baptize into a single immersion, and the Montanists, who are called Phrygians, and the Sabellians, who teach the Son-Fatherhood, and those like unto them: as judging and abiding in truth to be nothing better than unbaptized pagans, [the canon] commands immediately to be baptized when coming unto the Catholic Church.[^71] For if they have either not been baptized at all, or having been baptized were not signed according to the canons of the Orthodox Church, for this cause they are accounted by the divine Fathers as unilluminated.

*And Canon 48 of [the Council] in Laodicea legislates that those being enlightened be anointed with myron:*[^72]

[legislates that those being enlightened be anointed with myron:] for this [myron], being sanctified by prayers and the invocation of the Holy Spirit, sanctifies those who are anointed, and makes them partakers of the heavenly Kingdom of Christ, unless carelessness of life and the malice of deeds alienate us from this.

*And Canon 6 of the Council of Neocaesarea does not forbid pregnant women who desire it to be baptized:*[^73] for the unborn child within her does not partake with her of illumination, not having yet received from nature a disposition for good or for worse, nor is it possible to test what kind of disposition [the child] might have. But infants, through the actions of the anadochoi— [through the actions of the anadochoi—] that is, the sponsors [receivers]—laid upon them, being illuminated themselves, are made partakers of the Divine radiance.[^74]

*And Canon 6 of Timothy of Alexandria says:*[^75]
If a catechumen woman, on that day on which she was to be baptized, should experience her customary female condition, let her illumination be deferred unto a subsequent day.

*Of the same [Timothy], before all [Canon 1] says:*[^76]
If a catechumen child, or an adult man, when the sacred Sacrifice is being celebrated, having approached guilelessly, should partake of the Divine Gifts, the priest not knowing him to be a catechumen: let him immediately be [let him immediately be] baptized, for he has been called by the Lord.

*And Canon 4 of Saint Cyril says:*[^77]
Even if any of the catechumens undergo excommunication, having fallen under severe penance, and they are about to die, let them be baptized, and let them not depart unpartaking of human loving-kindness [grace].

*And Canon 2 of Timothy of Alexandria:*[^78]
Forbids a demon-possessed catechumen to be baptized before he is cleansed from the unclean demon: for it is not right that one who has given himself over to be a dwelling for an evil demon through turning aside unto wicked deeds should receive into himself the light of the Holy Spirit, unless he has been unreservedly freed from the dark and [freed from the dark and] evil spirit. But if it should come to him to die, [the canon] commands him to be baptized, that he may not depart from life empty and unpartaking of Divine grace.

*And the fourth [canon of Timothy] says:*[^79]
If a catechumen is troubled not by a demon, but is stricken by a disease of derangement of mind, so that he is not even able to reply to questions or make his profession; yet inasmuch as he showed of his own free choice that he embraced the faith, and was voluntarily enrolled among the catechumens: let him be baptized without doubt.

*And Canon 121 of the Council in Carthage says:*[^80]
The grace given unto us in baptism not only [grants] remission of sins, but [not only grants remission of sins, but] also brings power that we may no longer sin, unless through slothfulness, being self-willed, we embrace sinful deeds. And those who do not hold thus, [the canon] commits unto anathema.

### Notes

[^61]: Fol. 10r. The opening canonical florilegium of the Striatyn Trebnyk introduces the Holy Mystery of Baptism through ancient conciliar and patristic canons, establishing valid Trinitarian baptismal form and canonical criteria for the reconciliation of converts.
[^62]: Fol. 10r. Apostolic Canon 47 imposes deposition upon any bishop or presbyter who either repeats a valid baptism or recognizes invalid heretical baptism, affirming the unrepeatable character of Christian illumination (cf. Ephesians 4:5).
[^63]: Fol. 10r. Canon 50 of the Council of Carthage (Canon 57 in the Greek Syntagma / African Code) addresses the reception of Donatist infants and schismatics, forbidding the rebaptism of those baptized in the Catholic Church who fell into heresy.
[^64]: Fol. 10r. The printed text reads Canon 44 (*м҃д*), an erratum for Apostolic Canon 49 (*м҃ѳ*), which prescribes baptism in the threefold Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit (cf. Matthew 28:19) and deposes any cleric who baptizes into "three unoriginate" or "three sons."
[^65]: Fol. 10v. The theological gloss on Apostolic Canon 49 expounds classic Greek patristic Trinitarian monarchia: the Father alone is Unoriginate (*безначя́льный*) and without cause (*за є҆́же не и҆мѣ́ти вины̀*, Greek: ἀναίτιος); the Son is generated ineffably (*неизрече́ннаго ра́ди рожде́нїа*); and the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Unbegotten (*и҆схоже́нїа ра́ди ѿ нероже́нна*).
[^66]: Fol. 10v. Apostolic Canon 50 mandates three distinct immersions (*трѝ погрꙋже́нїа*) in the single Mystery of Baptism, invoking each Person of the Holy Trinity separately, and deposes anyone performing a single immersion into Christ's death.
[^67]: Fol. 11r. Eunomius of Cyzicus (d. c. 393), leader of the radical Anomoean Arians, rejected traditional threefold immersion and introduced single immersion into Christ's death, which was condemned at the Second Ecumenical Council (Constantinople I, 381).
[^68]: Fol. 11r. Canon 7 of the Second Ecumenical Council (Constantinople I, 381) establishes the classical Byzantine threefold classification for receiving converts: by Chrismation (Arians, Macedonians, Novatians, Quartodecimans, Apollinarians); by libellus/profession of faith (Nestorians, Monophysites); and by Baptism (Eunomians, Montanists, Sabellians, and pagans).
[^69]: Fol. 11v. Novatians (*наватїа́ны* / Cathari) referred to themselves as "the pure" (*каѳаро́й* / *чи́сты*). The enigmatic Slavonic phrase *и҆ лѣ́вы* translates Greek ἀριστερούς ("those on the left" or "the strict/left-handed"), an ancient colloquial or sect epithet referenced in Byzantine canonical commentaries (e.g. Balsamon, Zonaras).
[^70]: Fol. 11v. Second Ecumenical Council Canon 7 prescribes the sealing with Holy Myron of the forehead, eyes, nostrils, mouth, and ears with the sacramental formula: "The seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit" (*Σφραγὶς δωρεᾶς Πνεύματος Ἁγίου* / *Печа́ть да́ра Свѧта́го Дꙋ́ха*), quoting 2 Corinthians 1:22.
[^71]: Fol. 12r. Continuation of the canonical florilegium on baptism from Canon 7 of the Second Ecumenical Council (Constantinople I, 381; cf. Trullo Canon 95). Radical Anomoean Arians (Eunomians) who rejected threefold immersion, Montanists (Phrygians), and Sabellians who taught "Son-Fatherhood" (*сн҃оѡтче́ство*, Greek: υἱοπατορία, modalistic monarchianism conflating the Persons of the Trinity) are classified with unbaptized pagans and must be received exclusively by baptism *de novo*.
[^72]: Fol. 12r. Canon 48 of the Synod of Laodicea (c. 363–364) mandates post-baptismal chrismation with heavenly myron (*Ὅτι δεῖ τοὺς φωτιζομένους μετὰ τὸ βάπτισμα χρίεσθαι...*). The printed Slavonic witness reads *и҆нѣ́нымъ ... пома́зоватисѧ мѵ́ромъ просвѣща́емымъ оу҆законя́етъ*, an archaic scribal or compositor variant for the newly enlightened (*новопросвѣщаемымъ*) or "others" (*инѣмъ*), syntactically paired with *просвѣща́емымъ*.
[^73]: Fol. 12v. Canon 6 of the Council of Neocaesarea (c. 314–325) permits pregnant women to receive baptism at any time during gestation. The canonical argument establishes that the unborn child does not automatically share in the mother's illumination, since moral disposition (*προαίρεσις* / *произволе́нїе*) is an individual act of free volition.
[^74]: Fol. 13r. Fol. 12v–13r. The Ruthenian text directly incorporates the Greek technical canonical term *anadochoi* (*младе́нци же а҆надо́хоумъ*, Greek: ἀναδόχοις) followed by its Slavonic gloss (*сирѣ́чь въспрїе́мникомъ*, "that is, the sponsors/receivers"), establishing that infants are illumined through the proxy confession and liturgical guarantees of their baptismal godparents.
[^75]: Fol. 13r. Canon 6 of the Canonical Answers of St. Timothy, Patriarch of Alexandria (c. 381), directing that a female catechumen experiencing her menstrual period on the day appointed for baptism should have her illumination deferred until she is purified.
[^76]: Fol. 13r. Canon 1 of St. Timothy of Alexandria, cited in the Slavonic text as *прѣ́все* (Greek: πρὸ πάντων / Question 1), deciding that a catechumen child or adult who unwittingly partakes of the Holy Eucharist during the Divine Liturgy must be baptized immediately, having been called by the Lord.
[^77]: Fol. 13v. Canon 4 of the Canonical Epistle of St. Cyril of Alexandria (to Domnus of Antioch). Catechumens who incurred disciplinary excommunication or heavy penance and face mortal danger must not be allowed to die unbaptized, but are to receive illumination so as not to depart destitute of divine loving-kindness and human mercy (*бл҃гти чл҃чкыѧ*, translating Greek φιλανθρωπία).
[^78]: Fol. 13v. Canon 2 of St. Timothy of Alexandria. Catechumens afflicted with demonic possession are barred from receiving the illumination of the Holy Spirit while dominated by an unclean spirit, but in case of mortal peril (*in extremis*) they must be baptized before death.
[^79]: Fol. 14r. Canon 4 of St. Timothy of Alexandria. A catechumen incapacitated by delirium, fever, or mental illness who cannot make a personal profession of faith may be baptized without hesitation if witnesses confirm that he had previously expressed a conscious desire for baptism.
[^80]: Fol. 14r. Canon 121 of the Council of Carthage (418/419; Canon 111 in the Greek Syntagma / African Code Canon 125). Promulgated against the Pelagian heresy, this canon defines that baptismal grace confers not merely the negative pardon of past sins (*remissio peccatorum*), but positive supernatural power and divine assistance (*adiutorium*) enabling the Christian to resist future sin.
[^81]: Fol. 14v. The grotesque mascaron tailpiece (Renaissance woodcut cul-de-lampe featuring a bearded mask with strapwork volutes, floral crest, and looped ribbon handles) marks the typographical conclusion of the Canonical Florilegium on Baptism (Part II).
