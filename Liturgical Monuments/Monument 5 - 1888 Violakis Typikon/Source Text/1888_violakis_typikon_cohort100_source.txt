INTRODUCTION TO THIS DICTIONARY

This dictionary was compiled from various sources and defines each term either as it is used in this Typikon by George Violakis, the former First Chanter (Protopsaltis) of the Patriarchate of Constantinople, (published in Constantinople in 1888 and subsequently reprinted several times by the publishing firm of Vasilios Saliveros in Athens, Greece), or in other works generally concerning the order of the sacred services of the Greek Orthodox Church.
Within some services of the Orthodox Church, some of the terms presented here can take on an additional and even different meaning. Furthermore, because of the variety of opinion as to the origin of each term, etymological comments offered here should be considered as suggestions only.
Greek terms are given in the original Greek and are also rendered phonetically according to the transliteration table first used in the book The Byzantine Liturgy: Hymnology and Order by Theodore Bogdanos, published in 1993 by the then Greek Orthodox Diocese of Denver Choir Federation. This organization was later renamed, in 2007, as The Greek Orthodox Metropolis of Denver Church Music Federation.
The phonetic transliterations used here are in preference to the use of Latinized or Anglicized renditions used by some authors and scholars, so that the reader might become gradually more accustomed to the actual sound of the current Greek pronunciation of the words.
In each listing, the initial word in BOLD CAPITALS type is either given in English or is a Greek word spelled phonetically according to the transliteration table mentioned above. This word is then followed by the word in the Greek language enclosed in parenthesis and with its appropriate definite article. When any word is presented in SMALL CAPITALS in the body of a definition, this indicates that the word itself is defined in its proper alphabetical order. In each definition the word is only presented in small capitals the first time it is used. Each subsequent time the word is used it will be in normal type.

Other frequently used abbreviations are Lit.=literally and pl.=plural.
In considering the location of certain physical items and ritual events within a Greek Orthodox CHURCH building, one's understanding should be based on the usual and traditional floor plan of these churches where the SANCTUARY and ALTAR TABLE face East and the NARTHEX faces West.
As mentioned, this present list of definitions was compiled from numerous sources including internet searches. Some of the terms were also presented in the Glossary of The Byzantine Liturgy: Hymnology and Order by Theodore Bogdanos. In the present list, the definitions taken from or modified from the Bogdanos book are marked by an asterisk. The Bogdanos list was later expanded to include many additional citations for terms used in the Violakis Typikon itself or related to them. The terms were selected for inclusion because we felt they might be generally unfamiliar to the average reader (or in our case, the average church musician) or in need of further elucidation on other grounds. Also, because of the complexity of some subjects, the definitions have occasionally been expanded into small essays by the perceived necessity for greater clarity.
For a more detailed description of any sacred person or event, one may consult the various biographies (SYNAXARIA) in the Minea or the Megas Synaxaristis. For sources used, see the bibliography at the end of this dictionary.
