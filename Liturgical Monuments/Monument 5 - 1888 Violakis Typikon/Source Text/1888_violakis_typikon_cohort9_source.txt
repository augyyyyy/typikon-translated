CONCERNING THE PSALTER

  20. According to the ancient order of service, the Psalter is read before each of the Kathismata of each Stichologia; the Polyeleos (if there is one) is read before the Kathisma of the third Stichologia of the occurring feast; but now, to save time, on Sundays and feast days, the prescribed Kathismata of the Psalter as well as the Polyeleos (when it is a feast that has a Liti) or the Amomos (when it is a high Sunday with a non-celebrating Saint) are read before Matins. The Polyeleos of the Lord’s Feasts consists of Psalm 135 “Praise the Lord! praise His Name” and Psalm 136 “Give thanks to the Lord, because He is good.” For the Feasts of the Theotokos, on the other hand, the Polyeleos consists only of Psalm 45 “Beautiful words fill my mind.” For the celebrating Saints, the Readers who have an Eklogarion {see Glossary} may find in it the appropriate Polyeleos for each feast; if not, let them recite Psalms 135 and 136. Psalm 137 “By the rivers of Babylon” is read on the Sundays of Meatfare and Cheesefare. On every day, two Kathismata of the Psalter are read and during Great Lent three are read; but during the First week of Lent the entire Psalter is read entirely twice, from the Vespers of Palm Sunday until Holy Wednesday it is read entirely only once. It is not read from Holy Thursday until Saturday of Bright Week.

CONCERNING THE KATAVASIE

  27. On all the Sundays included in the intervals between the following periods — from the Leave-taking of the Feast of the Holy Cross until November 21, from the Leave-taking of the Feast of the Presentation until the inauguration of the Triodion, as well as the 2nd, 4th and 5th Sundays of Great Lent, from the Sunday of All Saints until July 27 and at all commemorations of Saints occurring on weekdays during the above periods—the Katavasie “I shall open my mouth” are sung. On Sundays occurring in the period between the Pre-feast and Post-feast of Feasts of the Lord and the Theotokos, and the feasts of Saints occurring during the week, the Katavasie of the occurring Feast of the Lord or Theotokos are sung. On the first four Sundays of the Triodion as well as on the First Sunday of Lent (if the Leave-taking of the feast of the Presentation has not passed), the Katavasie of the Triodion are sung. Regardless on what other day of the week, except the Sundays of the weeks of the Triodion, if the feast of a Saint occurs, the Katavasie “I shall open my mouth” are sung and, in the Liturgy, the Kontakion “Protectress of all Christians.”
