EXEGETICAL COMMENT BY ARISTINOS

“The disordered voice of a chanter is not acceptable.” Invoking even the tradition of Israel (and by implication the manner of worship in David’s Tent), Aristinos castigates “loud, jagged voices” that distract the worshiper from meditation and from prayerful reverence toward God, for God knows the silent, secret thoughts of His creatures; He does not need this tawdry loudness.

[ADVICE BY THEODORE THE STUDITE]
[A.D. 759-826]

The fourth of Our Holy Fathers among the Saints, Theodore the Studite addresses chanters and canonarchs [****] exhorting them to refine and further spiritualize their tasks. He first reminds them of the lofty, sacramental character of their ministry. He urges them to obey the established order of service and to be always conscious of the significance and the form of all types of hymns: e.g. kathismata, stichira.
In chanting and reciting, they should use a clear, well-rounded voice, following St. Paul’s exhortation that everything in the act of worship should be done with beauty and order (efschimonos ke kata taxin). The chanter should exercise restraint and avoid extravagance, such as singing a very high note and then, for effect, descend suddenly to a very low note. The good chanter should help other aspiring chanters to develop their God-given talent. Thus, God will compensate his humanly limited and finite service with infinite blessings.

ON CHANTERS

Violakis cites the 15th Canon of the Ecumenical Council in Laodicea [A.D. 364] which forbids anyone to chant in church services except ordained chanters who have musical training and talent. To avoid disorder and confusion in sound (chasmodia), only highly tutored and experienced chanters should be allowed to chant in order to create ordered beauty (eftaxia) and elicit in the churchgoer the proper emotions (sygkinisis).

GUIDING CRITERIA FOR CHANTERS

Violakis cites the following list of criteria [*****] for good chanting, proposed by the Music Committee and approved by the Church:

1. Chanters should be selected only if they match each other in quality of voice and in musical ability.

2. Ison (drone) holders and canonarchs (reciters) should be appointed at least three for each chant group and they should train twice or three times a week.
