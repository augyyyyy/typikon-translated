841

THE
ORDER OF SERVICE OF THE
AKATHIST HYMN
IN THE PATRIARCHAL CHURCH
----------

On Friday, about the Ninth Hour {3 p.m}, the Patriarch, having come
down to the Church with the customary entourage; is vested with
the Mandya (Mantle) in the Narthex. Carrying the Pastoral Staff
[Pateritsa], he enters the Nave, and, having blessed the people, ascends his
throne. While "For many years, [Master]" is sung by the Choirs, the
"Great Archimandrite , the Great Archdeacon, and the Second Deacon
receive Keron* and, after them, so do the Third Deacon and another
Deacon. The Great Archdeacon and the Second [Deacon] stand by the
[Patriarchal] Throne, while the other clergymen enter then into the Holy
Sanctuary. The Great Archimandrige exclaims "Blessed is our God. " The
Patriarch begins "Glory to You, our God, glory to You . . .Heavenly King,
the Comforter," etc. The designated Reader-Priest, standing across from
the Patriarch and by the Primikirios [Leader of Processions; and also a
participant in the chanting of the chanters],** reads the Trisagion and the rest
of the Small Compline. After the "It is meet," the First Choir begins
chanting the "Taking knowledge of the secret command" slowly and with
melody. While this hymn is being sung, the Hierarchs who are about to
officiate exit the Holy Sanctuary by its South Gate, preceded by the Great
Ecclesiarch, and stand each in his proper place [designated by seniority].

---
* [Receive Keron: A clergyman receives the permission and blessing of the highest ranking officiating clergyman (in this case the Patriarch) to participate in the service at hand.]
** [Offices (Offikia) such as the Primikirios and the Megas Logothetis involved certain ecclesiastical, political, and administrative duties during the Byzantine Empire. In post-Ottoman times, these offices and titles became largely ceremonial. See Iakovos Pilidis, Titli, Offikia ke Axiomata en ti Vyzantini Aftokratoria ke ti Christianiki Orthodoxo Ekklisia (Athens, 1985). Also Mega Efchologion (Venice, 1862; reprinted in Athens, 1986), pp. 686-691.]
