## Concerning the Co-Celebrant (Part 1) [Violakis, p. 907]

### CONCERNING THE CO-CELEBRATION OF CLERGY

When more than one priest concelebrate, beginning in the evening at Vespers, if an Entrance is performed, at least three priests are required. Standing in the middle of the temple forming a semicircle,¹ they chant outside the Holy Sanctuary the Lucernarium hymn **“O Gladsome Light”** (*Φῶς ἱλαρὸν*) up to the phrase: **“and Holy Spirit, God”** (*καὶ ἅγιον Πνεῦμα θεόν*); and from within the Holy Sanctuary the remainder of the hymn is completed; and after it the Prokimenon of the day is chanted once from within the Holy Sanctuary and twice from outside. At the Litiya, the priest first in rank [by ecclesiastical dignity or order of ordination] intones the petition: **“Hear us, O God our Savior”** (*Ἐπάκουσον ἡμῶν ὁ Θεός*), and the blessing: **“Peace be to all”** (*Εἰρήνη πᾶσι*); the second priest intones: **“O Merciful Master”** (*Δέσποτα πολυέλεε*); and the third priest intones: **“Lord Jesus Christ our God”** (*Κύριε Ἰησοῦ Χριστὲ ὁ Θεός*); then the first priest begins the Artoklasia Troparion: **“O Theotokos Virgin, rejoice”** (*Θεοτόκε Παρθένε*), assisted by the others; but he alone censes and circles around the Loaves together with the Deacon.

In the morning at the Divine Liturgy, the concelebrating priests chant nothing from within the Holy Sanctuary beyond what is customary for a priest celebrating alone, except the Entrance Hymn: **“O come, let us worship and fall down before Christ”** (*Δεῦτε προσκυνήσωμεν καὶ προσπέσωμεν Χριστῷ*) at the Small Entrance. Then is chanted the Dismissal Troparion (during which the priest first in seniority of ordination censes the Holy Table and the sacred Icons); then follows the Kontakion, and the Trisagion **“Holy God”** (*Ἅγιος ὁ Θεός*) for the third time.² The priestly Exclamations are recited by them in order, alternately one after the other. The priest first in rank blesses the Precious Gifts as well as the people, whenever required; and they receive the Pure Mysteries each according to his rank, with the last priest in seniority imparting communion also to the Deacon.

## The Funeral Service for Those Who Have Fallen Asleep (Part 1) [Violakis, p. 911]

### THE FUNERAL SERVICE FOR THOSE WHO HAVE FALLEN ASLEEP

Upon the death of one of the faithful, the clergy are summoned by the relatives of the deceased; and coming to the house wherein the body lies, if a hierarch is present, the priests and the deacons receive the liturgical blessing (*kairos*) from him. The priests put on their epitrachilia, and the deacons vest in sticharia and oraria, carrying censers and the dikerotrikera. Then one priest together with a deacon enter where the deceased lies, and the deacon exclaims: **“Master, give the blessing”** (*Εὐλόγησον Δέσποτα*). The priest: **“Blessed is our God, always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages”** (*Εὐλογητὸς ὁ Θεός...*); the deacon chants the Trisagion Prayers; the priest intones the exclamation: **“For Thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.”** (*Ὅτι σοῦ ἐστιν...*).

Then they chant in Tone 4 the Funeral Troparia:
- **“With the spirits of the righteous made perfect, give rest to the soul of Thy servant, O Savior, keeping him in the blessed life which is with Thee, O Lover of mankind.”** (*Μετὰ πνευμάτων δικαίων τετελειωμένων...*)
- **“In Thy place of rest, O Lord, where all Thy saints repose, give rest also to the soul of Thy servant, for Thou alone art immortal.”** (*Εἰς τὴν κατάπαυσίν σου Κύριε, ὅπου πάντες οἱ Ἅγιοί σου ἀναπαύονται...*)

* *Scholarly Apparatus on the Order of the House Trisagion and Funeral Rubrics:* In historical codices and canonical euchologia, the initial panikhida or funeral litany conducted at the home of the deceased initiates the rites of Christian burial. The designation *Trisagion* in this liturgical rubric encompasses the entire opening hexapsalmic or initial prayer cycle: the Thrice-Holy Hymn (*Ἅγιος ὁ Θεός*), the All-Holy Trinity prayer (*Παναγία Τριάς*), the Lord's Prayer (*Πάτερ ἡμῶν*), and the priestly doxological exclamation (*Ὅτι σοῦ ἐστιν*), preceding the four classical mortuary troparia in Tone 4 (*Nekrosima Troparia*). * *Scholarly Apparatus on the Funerary Procession and the Seventeenth Kathisma:* In later eras, urban distances between family residences, parish temples, and cemeteries expanded considerably beyond the compact parochial layout of nineteenth-century Constantinople. Consequently, the funeral processional chant of the Asmatic Trisagion (*Ἅγιος ὁ Θεός*), traditionally chanted continuously through the streets, is commonly begun when the casket enters the narthex from outside and maintained until placed before the solea and tetrapod in the nave. The funeral office itself centers upon the sublime funeral psalmody of the *Amomos* (Psalm 118 LXX), structured into three distinct stases divided by funeral litanies and tone modulations: the First Stasis is chanted in Tone 6 with the refrain *Alleluia*; the Second Stasis shifts to Tone 5 with the penitential refrain *“Have mercy on me, O Lord”*; and the Third Stasis modulates into Tone 8 with the resurrectional refrain *Alleluia*.

* *Scholarly Apparatus on the Textual Variants in the Second Evlogitarion:* The opening phrase of the second Troparion of the Funeral Evlogitaria exhibits a celebrated textual variant between the 1888 Constantinopolitan recension of George Violakis and older euchological witnesses. Violakis prints the poetic anthropomorphic reading *“O palame”* (*Ὁ παλάμῃ* — *“With the palm of Thy hand Thou didst fashion me”*), a textual tradition documented in the Athenian musical collections including the *Musikos Pandektis* (Athens, 1936, vol. 3, p. 376) and Nikos Karitsiotis, *Musikon Agiasmatarion* (Athens, 1951, p. 33). Conversely, the venerable Venetian and Roman liturgical monument editions, represented by the *Mega Euchologion* (Venice, 1862/1867, p. 407) and the *Mikron Euchologion* of the Apostolike Diakonia (Athens, 1956, p. 201), preserve the temporal adverbial incipit *“O palai men”* (*Ὁ πάλαι μέν* — *“Thou Who of old didst fashion me out of nothing”*). Both liturgical traditions preserve the identical theological confession: humanity fashioned *ex nihilo* by the divine hand according to the divine image, fallen through transgression, and restored to paradise through Christ's redemption.
