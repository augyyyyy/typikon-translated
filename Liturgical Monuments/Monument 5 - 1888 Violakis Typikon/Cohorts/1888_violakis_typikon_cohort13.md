## Concerning the Communion Hymn

**46.** The Sunday Communion Hymn **"Praise the Lord from the heavens, praise Him in the highest"** (*Αἰνεῖτε τὸν Κύριον ἐκ τῶν οὐρανῶν*, Ps. 148:1 LXX) is sung in the Divine Liturgies of Sundays, unless a Feast of the Theotokos or the Apodosis of a Dominical or Marian Feast coincides therewith. It is sung likewise on the Feast of the Circumcision of the Savior [January 1] on whatever day of the week the feast may fall, as well as on the Paramonies [Eves] of the Nativity of Christ and Theophany, when the Royal Hours are conjoined immediately with Vespers and the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great. On Dominical Feasts and on their Apodoses, on whatever day they occur, the proper Communion Hymns appointed for each feast are sung. On Marian Feasts and on September 1 [the Indiction / New Ecclesiastical Year] (with the exception of the Feast of the Annunciation, when we sing: **"The Lord hath chosen Zion; He hath desired it for His habitation"**, *Ἐξελέξατο Κύριος τὴν Σιών*, Ps. 131:13 LXX), even if they coincide with a Sunday, we sing: **"I will take the cup of salvation, and call upon the Name of the Lord"** (*Ποτήριον σωτηρίου λήψομαι*, Ps. 115:4 LXX). When the commemorations of the following Saints coincide with a Sunday, instead of **"Praise the Lord,"** other Communion Hymns characteristic of each are chanted: namely, on the three Feasts of the Holy Forerunner [his Conception, Nativity, and Beheading]: **"The righteous shall be in everlasting remembrance; he shall not be afraid of evil tidings"** (*Εἰς μνημόσυνον αἰώνιον ἔσται δίκαιος*, Ps. 111:6b LXX); on the Feasts of the Preeminent Apostles [Peter and Paul], as well as of the Twelve Apostles, the Four Evangelists, the Apostle Andrew, the Three Hierarchs, and Saints Constantine and Helen: **"Their sound is gone out into all the earth, and their words unto the ends of the world"** (*Εἰς πᾶσαν τὴν γῆν ἐξῆλθεν ὁ φθόγγος αὐτῶν*, Ps. 18:5 LXX); on the Feast of the Archangels [Synaxis of the Bodiless Powers, November 8]: **"Who maketh His angels spirits, and His ministers a flame of fire"** (*Ὁ ποιῶν τοὺς Ἀγγέλους αὐτοῦ πνεύματα*, Ps. 103:4 LXX); on the Third Sunday of the Great Fast [Veneration of the Cross] as well as on August 1 [Procession of the Precious Cross] (if the latter does not fall on a Sunday): **"The light of Thy countenance hath been signed upon us, O Lord"** (*Ἐσημειώθη ἐφ’ ἡμᾶς τὸ φῶς τοῦ προσώπου σου, Κύριε*, Ps. 4:7 LXX); on the four Sundays following Thomas Sunday [Myrrh-bearers, Paralytic, Samaritan Woman, Blind Man]: **"Receive the Body of Christ; taste of the Fountain of immortality"** (*Σῶμα Χριστοῦ μεταλάβετε*); on the Sunday of All Saints and on the commemorations of the holy Ancestors of God Joachim and Anna: **"Rejoice in the Lord, O ye righteous: praise is comely for the upright"** (*Ἀγαλλιᾶσθε, δίκαιοι, ἐν Κυρίῳ*, Ps. 32:1 LXX). In the Divine Liturgies celebrated on weekdays and on days falling between feasts [Afterfeasts] (excepting the days of the Apodoses themselves), the weekday Communion Hymns of the week are sung: on Monday [the Angels]: **"Who maketh His angels spirits, and His ministers a flame of fire"** (*Ὁ ποιῶν τοὺς Ἀγγέλους αὐτοῦ πνεύματα*); on Tuesday [the Forerunner]: **"The righteous shall be in everlasting remembrance"** (*Εἰς μνημόσυνον αἰώνιον ἔσται δίκαιος*); on Wednesday [the Theotokos]: **"I will take the cup of salvation, and call upon the Name of the Lord"** (*Ποτήριον σωτηρίου λήψομαι*); on Thursday [the Holy Apostles]: **"Their sound is gone out into all the earth, and their words unto the ends of the world"** (*Εἰς πᾶσαν τὴν γῆν ἐξῆλθεν ὁ φθόγγος αὐτῶν*); on Friday...

*(Printed Page 107)*

...*ῥήματα αὐτῶν*· τῇ Παρασκευῇ *Σωτηρίαν εἰργάσω ἐν μέσῳ τῆς γῆς, Χριστὲ ὁ Θεός*, καὶ τῷ Σαββάτῳ *Μακάριοι οὓς ἐξελέξω καὶ προσελάβου Κύριε, καὶ τὸ μνημόσυνον αὐτῶν εἰς γενεὰν καὶ γενεάν*. ## ΠΕΡΙ ΤΟΥ ΕΙΔΟΜΕΝ ΤΟ ΦΩΣ

Ἐν δὲ ταῖς Δεσποτικαῖς ἑορταῖς καὶ ταῖς Ἀποδόσεσιν αὐτῶν, τῇ Γ΄ Κυριακῇ τῶν Νηστειῶν καὶ τῷ Σαββάτῳ τοῦ Λαζάρου, ἀντὶ τούτου ψάλλονται τὰ τῶν ἑορτῶν Ἀπολυτίκια. Τῇ Μ. Πέμπτῃ τὸ Τροπάριον *Τοῦ δείπνου σου τοῦ μυστικοῦ* χῦμα· τῷ Μ. Σαββάτῳ τὸ *Μνήσθητι εὔσπλαγχνε καὶ ἡμῶν καθὼς ἐμνημόνευσας τοῦ λῃστοῦ ἐν τῇ βασιλείᾳ τῶν οὐρανῶν* εἰς ἦχον β΄ εἱρμολογικῶς. Ἀπὸ δὲ τῆς Κυριακῆς τοῦ Πάσχα μέχρι τῆς Ἀποδόσεως, καθ' ἑκάστην ψάλλεται τὸ *Χριστὸς ἀνέστη* καὶ ἀπὸ τῆς ἑορτῆς τῆς Ἀναλήψεως μέχρι τῆς Ἀποδόσεως τὸ *Ἀνελήφθης ἐν δόξῃ*. Τῷ πρὸ τῆς Πεντηκοστῆς Σαββάτῳ ἀντὶ τοῦ *Εἴδομεν τὸ φῶς* τὸ *Ὁ βάθει σοφίας*. Τῇ Κυριακῇ τῆς Πεντηκοστῆς τὸ Ἀπολυτίκιον *Εὐλογητὸς εἶ Χριστέ*, ἀπὸ δὲ τῆς Δευτέρας τοῦ ἁγίου Πνεύματος καὶ ἐφεξῆς τὸ *Εἴδομεν τὸ φῶς*. Ἐν δὲ ταῖς Προηγιασμέναις Λειτουργίαις ἀντὶ τοῦ *Εἴδομεν τὸ φῶς* λέγεται τὸ *Εὐλογήσω τὸν Κύριον*.

## Concerning "We Have Seen the True Light"

**47.** In every Divine Liturgy, even if it be on a Feast of the Theotokos, after the Deacon has said: **"With fear of God, with faith and with love draw near"** (*Μετὰ φόβου Θεοῦ, πίστεως καὶ ἀγάπης προσέλθετε*), and the Priest exclaims: **"O God, save Thy people, and bless Thine inheritance"** (*Σῶσον, ὁ Θεός, τὸν λαόν σου, καὶ εὐλόγησον τὴν κληρονομίαν σου*), the hymn **"We have seen the true Light, we have received the Heavenly Spirit, we have found the true Faith, worshipping the Undivided Trinity: for He hath saved us"** (*Εἴδομεν τὸ φῶς τὸ ἀληθινόν*) is sung in Tone 2. On Dominical Feasts and their Apodoses, on the Third Sunday of the Great Fast [Sunday of the Cross], and on Lazarus Saturday, instead of this hymn we sing the Troparion of the respective feast. On Great and Holy Thursday, we chant recitatively [*chyma*] the Troparion: **"Of Thy Mystical Supper, O Son of God, accept me today as a communicant..."** (*Τοῦ δείπνου σου τοῦ μυστικοῦ*); on Great and Holy Saturday, the troparion: **"Remember us also, O Compassionate One, as Thou didst remember the thief in the Kingdom of Heaven"** (*Μνήσθητι εὔσπλαγχνε καὶ ἡμῶν καθὼς ἐμνημόνευσας τοῦ λῃστοῦ ἐν τῇ βασιλείᾳ τῶν οὐρανῶν*) in Tone 2 irmologically. From Easter Sunday [Pascha] until its Apodosis, **"Christ is risen from the dead, trampling down death by death, and upon those in the tombs bestowing life"** (*Χριστὸς ἀνέστη*) is sung every day; and from the Feast of the Ascension until its Apodosis, we chant the Troparion: **"Thou hast ascended in glory, O Christ our God..."** (*Ἀνελήφθης ἐν δόξῃ*). On the Saturday before Pentecost [Soul Saturday], instead of **"We have seen the true Light,"** we chant the Troparion of the Departed: **"O Thou Who by the depth of wisdom..."** (*Ὁ βάθει σοφίας*). On the Sunday of Pentecost, the Troparion: **"Blessed art Thou, O Christ our God, Who hast revealed the fishermen most wise..."** (*Εὐλογητὸς εἶ Χριστέ*); from Monday of the Holy Spirit onward, we resume **"We have seen the true Light."** In the Liturgies of the Presanctified Gifts, instead of **"We have seen the true Light,"** the hymn recited is: **"I will bless the Lord at all times; His praise shall continually be in my mouth"** (*Εὐλογήσω τὸν Κύριον ἐν παντὶ καιρῷ*, Ps. 33:2 LXX).[^111]

## Concerning "The Blessing of the Lord"

**49.** Before the Dismissal of the Divine Liturgy, the Priest addresses unto the people the blessing: **"The blessing of the Lord and His mercy come upon you, through His grace and love for mankind, always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages"** (*Εὐλογία Κυρίου καὶ ἔλεος ἔλθοι ἐφ’ ἡμᾶς, τῇ αὐτοῦ χάριτι καὶ φιλανθρωπίᾳ πάντοτε, νῦν καὶ ἀεὶ καὶ εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας τῶν αἰώνων*). This blessing is imparted likewise at the end of Great Vespers in which the Artoklasia [Blessing of the Loaves] is celebrated, as was noted above in the section concerning Vespers. This is that coveted prayer for every Christian: the benediction bestowed in the Name of the Lord our God by the officiating Priest.[^113]

## Concerning "Through the Prayers"

**50.** The exclamation intoned by the Priest at the conclusion of every prayer and sacred office, and above all of the Divine Liturgy—**"Through the prayers of our holy Fathers, Lord Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us"** (*Δι’ εὐχῶν τῶν ἁγίων Πατέρων ἡμῶν, Κύριε Ἰησοῦ Χριστὲ ὁ Θεός, ἐλέησον ἡμᾶς*)—is the final seal [*episphragisma*]. During Bright Week, however, this exclamation is omitted; in its place, the Priest, after the Dismissal of both Vespers and the Liturgy, proclaims thrice: **"Christ is risen!"** (*Χριστὸς ἀνέστη!*), unto which the people respond each time: **"Truly He is risen!"** (*Ἀληθῶς ἀνέστη!*). Then the Priest proclaims: **"Glory to His third-day Resurrection!"** (*Δόξα τῇ αὐτοῦ τριημέρῳ ἐγέρσει!*), unto which the people respond: **"We worship His third-day Resurrection!"** (*Προσκυνοῦμεν αὐτοῦ τὴν τριήμερον ἔγερσιν!*); and immediately the entire hymn **"Christ is risen from the dead..."** is chanted through in full.

    Note that, whenever a Hierarch or Patriarch presides at the Divine Services, instead of **"Through the prayers of our Holy Fathers,"** the following is recited: **"Through the prayers of our holy Master [Bishop], Lord Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us. Amen"** (*Δι’ εὐχῶν τοῦ ἁγίου Δεσπότου ἡμῶν, Κύριε Ἰησοῦ Χριστὲ ὁ Θεός, ἐλέησον ἡμᾶς. Ἀμήν*).[^114]

# Order of the Daily Service

## Of Vespers,¹ Matins, and the Divine Liturgy

**1.** At Vespers, following the Introductory Psalm [Psalm 103 LXX], at **"Lord, I have cried unto Thee, hear me"** (*Κύριε ἐκέκραξα*) we sing Stichira Prosomoia from the Parakletike of the occurring Tone: two from the first three Stichira, one from the second three Stichira, and three of the Saint of the day from the Menaion. **"Glory... Both now and ever..."** followed by the Prosomoion Theotokion (or Stavrotheotokion on Tuesday and Thursday evening). Then **"O Joyous Light"** (*Φῶς ἱλαρόν*), the daily Prokimenon of the day, and after the prayer **"Blessed and glorified be the majesty of Thy Kingdom"** (*Εἴη τὸ κράτος*), the Aposticha from the Parakletike. **"Glory... Both now and ever..."** and the Prosomoion Theotokion (or Stavrotheotokion). Then **"Now lettest Thou Thy servant depart in peace"** (*Νῦν ἀπολύεις*), the Trisagion Prayers, and the Dismissal Troparion of the Saint. **"Glory... Both now and ever..."** with the appropriate Dismissal Theotokion taken from the daily Theotokia of the Eight Tones appended at the end of the Horologion or the Parakletike, followed by the complete Great Dismissal.

    1. If the occurring Saint of the day has six Stichira appointed in the Menaion, but lacks a proper Doxastikon, three of the Saint's Stichira are omitted, and in their place three Stichira from the Parakletike are sung, which precede those of the Saint.
    2. If the Saint has two Doxastika [one at Lord I Have Cried and one at the Aposticha], the Stichira of the Saint alone are chanted without those of the Parakletike, each being repeated twice to make six.
    3. If there are two Saints commemorated on the same day, each having their own proper Stichira in the Menaion, the Stichira of the Saints alone are sung, three for each Saint, omitting those of the Parakletike.
    4. If the Saint celebrated on Saturday has no proper Doxastikon, on Friday evening at **"Glory... Both now and ever..."** we sing the First [Dogmatic] Theotokion of the occurring Tone of the week. If the Saint has a Doxastikon, we chant **"Glory"** of the Saint, and **"Both now and ever"** with the First Dogmatic Theotokion of the Tone; and the Dismissal Troparion is likewise chanted for the Saint, followed by the proper Theotokion...

...in concordance with the Tone of the Dismissal Troparion. But if the Saint has no proper Doxastika, the Dismissal Troparion chanted is: **"Apostles, Martyrs, and Prophets"** (*Ἀπόστολοι Μάρτυρες*); **"Glory..."**, **"Remember, O Lord, as Thou art good, Thy servants"** (*Μνήσθητι, Κύριε, ὡς ἀγαθός, τῶν δούλων σου*); **"Both now and ever..."**, **"O Holy Mother of the ineffable Light"** (*Μήτηρ ἁγία ἡ τοῦ ἀφράστου φωτός*). (See the Dismissal Troparia for Friday evening in Tone 2 in the Horologion). At ordinary weekday Vespers, no Entrance is made (with the exception of Vespers occurring on the Apodoses of Dominical and Marian Feasts); therefore, immediately following the Prayer of the Ninth Hour, the Introductory Psalm [Psalm 103 LXX] is read, and after **"O Joyous Light"** (*Φῶς ἱλαρόν*) and the daily Prokimenon, the prayer **"Vouchsafe, O Lord"** (*Καταξίωσον, Κύριε*) is read without an intervening Litany. Immediately thereafter follows the Litany of Supplication: **"Let us complete our evening prayer unto the Lord"** (*Πληρώσωμεν τὴν ἑσπερινὴν δέησιν ἡμῶν*), etc. Under these rubrical conditions, the Royal Doors remain closed; and the Priest, entering through the South Deacon's Door, exits through the North Deacon's Door censing during **"Let my prayer be set forth as incense before Thee"** (*Κατευθυνθήτω*), vested only in his epitrachilion.

**2.** At Matins [Orthros], the Priest begins: **"Blessed is our God, always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages"**; **"Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee"**; **"O Heavenly King, Comforter, Spirit of Truth..."**; the Trisagion Prayers; the ecphonesis: **"For Thine is the Kingdom..."**; **"Lord, have mercy"** (twelve times); **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, both now and ever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen"**; **"O come, let us worship and fall down before God our King"** (thrice); Psalm 50 [Psalm 51 MT]; and the Midnight Office [Mesonyktikon] exactly as appointed in the Horologion. On Forefeast and Afterfeast days of Dominical and Marian Feasts, instead of **"Behold, the Bridegroom cometh at midnight"** (*Ἰδοὺ ὁ Νυμφίος*), the Troparion of the Forefeast is intoned, or the Dismissal Troparion of the Feast during Afterfeasts; and instead of the penitential troparia **"Remember, O Lord, as Thou art good, Thy servants,"** the proper festal Kontakia are recited. Immediately following the conclusion of the Midnight Office, the Penitential Troparia are chanted: **"Have mercy on us, O Lord, have mercy on us; for laying aside all excuse, we sinners offer this supplication unto Thee as Master: Have mercy on us"** (*Ἐλέησον ἡμᾶς, Κύριε, ἐλέησον ἡμᾶς*); **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit"**, **"Lord, have mercy on us, for in Thee have we hoped; be not exceedingly wroth with us, neither remember our iniquities, but look down even now as Thou art compassionate, and deliver us from our enemies..."** (*Κύριε, ἐλέησον ἡμᾶς*); **"Both now and ever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen"**, **"Open unto us the door of compassion, O blessed Theotokos, that hoping in thee we may not perish, but that through thee we may be delivered from adversities; for thou art the salvation of the Christian race"** (*Τῆς εὐσπλαγχνίας τὴν πύλην ἄνοιξον ἡμῖν*). (While this Marian troparion is being chanted, the curtain of the Royal Doors is drawn aside). Then the Priest, having vested in his epitrachilion, stands before the Holy Doors of the Sanctuary [Holy Bema] and facing eastward proclaims: **"Have mercy on us, O God, according to Thy great mercy, we pray Thee: hear us and have mercy."** The First Choir responds: **"Lord, have mercy"** (thrice). The Priest: **"Again we pray for the pious and Orthodox Christians."** The Second Choir: **"Lord, have mercy"** (thrice). The Priest: **"Again we pray for our Archbishop [or Bishop]..."** The First Choir: **"Lord, have mercy"** (thrice). The Priest: **"Again we pray that this holy temple and this city [or village], and every city and countryside may be preserved from wrath, famine, pestilence, earthquake, flood, fire, the sword, the invasion of aliens, and civil strife; and that our good God, Who loveth mankind, may be gracious, favorable, and conciliated, and turn away and dispel all wrath and sickness stirred up against us, and deliver us from His righteous threatening that hangeth over us, and have mercy on us."** Both Choirs chant the **"Lord, have mercy"** alternately, in four rounds of ten times each [forty times total]. The Priest continues: **"Again we pray that the Lord our God may hear the voice of the supplication of us sinners, and have mercy on us."** The Choirs: **"Lord, have mercy"** (thrice).
