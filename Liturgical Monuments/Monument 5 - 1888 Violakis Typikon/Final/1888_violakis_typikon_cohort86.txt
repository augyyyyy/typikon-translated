# Appendix: Service of the Akathist Hymn in the Patriarchal Church (Opening)

## The Order of Service of the Akathist Hymn in the Patriarchal Church [Violakis, p. 841]

# THE ORDER OF SERVICE OF THE AKATHIST HYMN IN THE PATRIARCHAL CHURCH

---

On Friday, about the Ninth Hour [3:00 p.m.], the Patriarch, having come down to the Church with the customary entourage, is vested with the Mandyas [Mantle] in the Narthex. Carrying the Pastoral Staff (*Pateritsa*), he enters the Nave, and, having blessed the people, ascends his Throne. While **"For many years, Master!"** (*Εἰς πολλὰ ἔτη, Δέσποτα*) is sung by the Choirs, the Great Archimandrite, the Great Archdeacon, and the Second Deacon receive Keron [the liturgical blessing and permission to officiate]* and, after them, so do the Third Deacon and another Deacon. The Great Archdeacon and the Second Deacon stand by the Patriarchal Throne, while the other clergymen enter into the Holy Sanctuary [Bema]. The Great Archimandrite intones the Exclamation: **"Blessed is our God always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen."** (*Εὐλογητὸς ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν*); the Patriarch begins: **"Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee!"**, **"O Heavenly King, Comforter, Spirit of truth..."** (*Δόξα σοι ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν, δόξα σοι... Βασιλεῦ οὐράνιε, Παράκλητε...*), and the designated Reader-Priest, standing opposite the Patriarch near the Primikirios [Leader of Processions and participant in the chanting of the chanters],** reads the Trisagion and Small Compline. After **"It is truly right"** (*Ἄξιόν ἐστιν*), the First Choir begins chanting the Troparion in Tone 8: **"Taking knowledge of the secret command"** (*Τὸ προσταχθὲν μυστικῶς*), slowly and melodiously. While this hymn is being sung, the Hierarchs who are about to officiate exit the Holy Sanctuary by its South Gate, preceded by the Great Ecclesiarch, and stand each in his proper place [designated by seniority].

---
* *Note on Keron:* A clergyman receives the permission and blessing of the highest-ranking officiating clergyman (in this case the Patriarch) to participate in the service at hand.  
** *Note on Court Offices:* Offices (*offikia*) such as the Primikirios and the Megas Logothetis involved certain ecclesiastical, political, and administrative duties during the Byzantine Empire. In post-Ottoman times, these offices and titles became largely ceremonial. (See Iakovos Pilidis, *Titli, Offikia ke Axiomata en ti Vyzantini Aftokratoria ke ti Christianiki Orthodoxo Ekklisia*, Athens, 1985; also *Mega Efchologion*, Venice, 1862; repr. Athens, 1986, pp. 686–691)...[^487]
