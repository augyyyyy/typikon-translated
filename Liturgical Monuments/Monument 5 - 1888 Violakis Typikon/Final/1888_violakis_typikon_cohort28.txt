4. After the Prayer of the Sixth Hour: **"O God and Lord of powers"** (*Θεὲ καὶ Κύριε τῶν δυνάμεων*), the Ninth Hour begins.

## Ninth Hour [Violakis, p. 265]

5.    The Reader recites **"O come, let us worship"** (*Δεῦτε προσκυνήσωμεν*) thrice and reads the psalms of the Ninth Hour [Psalms 109, 110, and 85 LXX]; and after **"He Who for our sake was born of a Virgin"** (*Ὁ δι’ ἡμᾶς γεννηθείς*), the Second Choir begins chanting the Idiomelon: **"Herod was struck with amazement"** (*Ἐξεπλήττετο ὁ Ἡρώδης*). Then the First Choir chants the Verse: **"God shall come from Teman, and the Holy One from the mountain overshadowed and densely forested"** (*Ὁ Θεὸς ἀπὸ Θαιμὰν ἥξει, καὶ ὁ Ἅγιος ἐξ ὄρους κατασκίου δασέος*, Habakkuk 3:3 LXX), and the Idiomelon: **"When Joseph, O Virgin, was wounded with sorrow"** (*Ὅτε Ἰωσήφ, Παρθένε*); the Second Choir takes up the conclusion of the Idiomelon: **"Together with the Father and the Holy Spirit"** (*Σὺν Πατρὶ καὶ Ἁγίῳ Πνεύματι*); the First Choir begins **"Glory to the Father..."**; the Second Choir says **"Both now and ever..."**. Then the Canonarch, standing in the midst of the nave and facing eastward, recites reverently in the cantillation style of the Gospel the Idiomelon: **"Today He Who holds all creation in the hollow of His hand is born of a Virgin"** (*Σήμερον γεννᾶται ἐκ Παρθένου ὁ δρακὶ τὴν πᾶσαν ἔχων κτίσιν*), repeating it thrice; he then continues: **"He Who in essence cannot be touched is wrapped in swaddling clothes as a mortal..."** (*Ῥάκει καθάπερ βροτὸς*), etc.; he likewise repeats thrice: **"We worship Thy Nativity, O Christ"** (*Προσκυνοῦμέν σου τὴν γέννησιν, Χριστέ*), and once: **"Show us also Thy divine Theophany"** (*Δεῖξον ἡμῖν καὶ τὰ θεῖά σου Θεοφάνεια*). And then the Choirs begin chanting **"Today He is born of a Virgin"** (*Σήμερον γεννᾶται ἐκ Παρθένου*) slowly and solemnly with melody. Following this, the Canonarch reads the Old Testament Prophecy [Isaiah 9:6–7] and the Epistle [Hebrews 2:1–10], and the Priest reads the Holy Gospel [Matthew 2:13–23]. After the Prayer of the Ninth Hour: **"O Master, Lord Jesus Christ our God"** (*Δέσποτα Κύριε Ἰησοῦ Χριστέ*), the Dismissal takes place.³ And Great Vespers joined with the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great begins.

*Note.* After the Prayer of the Ninth Hour, the liturgical order prescribes the Typika recited plain—namely, **"Bless the Lord, O my soul"** [Psalm 102 LXX], **"Praise the Lord, O my soul"** [Psalm 145 LXX], the Beatitudes, and finally the Dismissal. But because, for the sake of brevity, it has already become customary to omit them, for this reason the Dismissal is noted above immediately following the Prayer.[^199]
