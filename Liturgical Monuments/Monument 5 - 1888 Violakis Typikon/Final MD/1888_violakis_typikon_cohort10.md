## Concerning the Morning Gospel at Sunday Matins

**28.** On Sundays, after the Katavasia of the Eighth Ode, the Deacon (or the Priest in the absence of a Deacon) exclaims: **"Let us pray to the Lord."** The Choir: **"Lord, have mercy,"** thrice. The Priest: **"For Thou art holy, our God, and Thou dwellest among the saints..."** (*Ὅτι ἅγιος εἶ, ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν*). The Choirs: **"Let every breath praise the Lord"** (*Πᾶσα πνοὴ αἰνεσάτω τὸν Κύριον*). The Priest recites the appointed Resurrectional Eothinon [Morning] Gospel; whereafter follows **"Having beheld the Resurrection of Christ"** (*Ἀνάστασιν Χριστοῦ θεασάμενοι*), and Psalm 50 LXX (**"Have mercy on me, O God"**) is chanted; **"Glory..."**, **"Through the intercessions of the Apostles, O Merciful One, blot out the multitude of our transgressions"**; **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen."**, **"Through the intercessions of the Theotokos..."**; the Verse **"Have mercy on me, O God, according to Thy great mercy..."** (Psalm 50:1 LXX), and **"Jesus, having risen from the grave as He foretold, hath given us eternal life and great mercy."** Then the Priest:10 **"O God, save Thy people, and bless Thine inheritance..."** (*Σῶσον, ὁ Θεός, τὸν λαόν σου...*); exclamation: **"Through the mercies and compassions and love for mankind of Thine Only-Begotten Son..."** (*Ἐλέει καὶ οἰκτιρμοῖς...*). On the Sundays of the Triodion, after Psalm 50, instead of **"Glory..."**, **"Through the intercessions of the Apostles..."**, the Idiomela of the Triodion are chanted: **"Glory..."**, **"The doors of repentance do Thou open unto me, O Giver of Life"** (*Τῆς μετανοίας ἄνοιξόν μοι πύλας, Ζωοδότα*); **"Now and forever..."**, **"Guide me in the paths of salvation, O Theotokos"** (*Τῆς σωτηρίας εὔθυνόν μοι τρίβους, Θεοτόκε*); the verse **"Have mercy on me, O God..."**, and the Idiomelon **"When I ponder the multitude of my evil deeds..."** (*Τὰ πλήθη τῶν πεπραγμένων μοι δεινῶν*); then **"O God, save Thy people..."**; and subsequently **"More honorable than the Cherubim..."** (*Τὴν Τιμιωτέραν*) is chanted in verses.[^90]

## Concerning the Doxastikon of the Praises

**32.** On all Sundays on which the Resurrectional Eothinon [Morning] Gospel is read at Matins, the Eothinon Doxastikon is chanted at the Praises, inasmuch as the eleven Eothina Doxastika, like the Exapostilaria, are so to speak a synopsis of the eleven Eothina Gospels. When a Feast of the Theotokos or its Apodosis, or the Apodosis of a Feast of the Lord occurs on a Sunday, the Doxastika of the feasts are chanted instead of the Eothina Doxastika. The Eothina are likewise omitted on the following Sundays: namely, on all Sundays of the Triodion and Pentecostarion; on the Sunday of the Forefathers; on the Sunday before the Nativity of Christ; on the Sunday after the Nativity of Christ; on the Sunday before Theophany (when it coincides with January 5); on the three Sundays of the Holy Fathers of the First, Fourth, and Seventh Ecumenical Councils; as well as on the commemorations of the following Saints when their feasts occur on a Sunday: namely, the three feasts of the Forerunner (the Nativity [June 24], the Synaxis [January 7], and the Beheading [August 29]); the feast of the Holy Apostles Peter and Paul [June 29] and that of the Twelve Apostles [June 30]; the feast of each of the four Evangelists; that of the Taxiarchs [Archangels Michael and Gabriel, November 8]; that of the Three Hierarchs [January 30] (when their commemoration falls outside the Triodion); and on the First of the Indiction [September 1]. It is also customary at present to chant, instead of the Eothinon, the Doxastika of the following Saints: Saint George [April 23], Saint Demetrius [October 26], Saint Nicholas [December 6], and Saints Constantine and Helen [May 21], as well as of other Saints in whose honor the temple is dedicated or whose memory is celebrated with solemnity. ## Concerning the Theotokion Following the Doxastikon of the Praises

**33.** On Sundays, after the Resurrectional Eothinon Doxastikon of the Praises, at **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen."** the Theotokion **"Most blessed art thou, O Virgin Theotokos; for through Him Who was incarnate of thee, Hades is taken captive..."** (*Ὑπερευλογημένη ὑπάρχεις, Θεοτόκε Παρθένε*) is chanted invariably in Tone 2, even if a Feast of the Mother of God, or its Apodosis, or the Apodosis of a Feast of the Lord occurs. Excepted are the four Sundays following Pascha: namely, of the Myrrhbearers, of the Paralytic, of the Samaritan Woman, and of the Blind Man, on which, after the Doxastikon, instead of **"Most blessed art thou,"** the Paschal hymn **"The Day of Resurrection! Let us beam with festive joy, and let us embrace one another..."** (*Ἀναστάσεως ἡμέρα, καὶ λαμπρυνθῶμεν τῇ πανηγύρει*) is chanted. When on these Sundays the commemorations of Saints George, John the Theologian, and Constantine and Helen coincide, after **"Glory..."** of the Saints, at **"Now and forever..."** that of the Sunday is chanted, with the exception of Myrrhbearers Sunday, on which at **"Now and forever..."** **"The Day of Resurrection"** is intoned, because the Doxastikon of this Sunday is an Eothinon hymn.
  It must be noted that from Thomas Sunday until the Apodosis of Pascha, on commemorations of the aforementioned Saints coinciding with any day other than Sunday, after **"Glory..."** of the Saints at the Praises, at **"Now and forever..."** we chant the Doxastikon of the preceding Sunday, and not **"The Day of Resurrection."** Likewise, when the commemorations of the aforementioned Saints occur on Thomas Sunday or on the Feast of Mid-Pentecost until their Apodoses, the Theotokion of the feast is chanted at **"Now and forever..."** If the Feast of the Meeting of the Lord coincides with one of the first four Sundays of the Triodion...[^95]
