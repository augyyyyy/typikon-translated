**KOLLYVA** (*Κόλλυβα, τά*; Ukrainian / Slav. *Kolyvo*): Boiled wheat sweetened with honey, sugar, raisins, and pomegranate seeds, blessed in memorial services (Panakhyda / Parastasis) in honor of the departed, symbolizing the bodily resurrection of the dead according to the words of Christ: "Unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains alone; but if it dies, it bears much fruit" (John 12:24).

**KONTAKION, pl. KONTAKIA** (*Κοντάκιον, τό· pl. Κοντάκια, τά*; Slav. *Kondak*): The Kontakion was the preeminent poetical and hymnographic genre of early Byzantine hymnology, achieving its classic zenith in the inspired verse of Saint Romanos the Melodist (6th century), many of whose masterpieces remain central to the liturgical life of the Church. Thematically, the classical Kontakion presented an expansive narrative and dramatic homily upon the great feast of the day, notably the Nativity of Christ. Structurally, an ancient Kontakion consisted of 18 to 30 or more stanzas, each designated an **Ikos** (*Οἶκος*), composed in identical meter and rhythm and sung to the same melody. The initial letters of these stanzas formed an acrostic, spelling out the title of the feast, the poet’s name, or the Greek alphabet. These stanzas were preceded by a short independent introductory stanza called the **proimion** (*προοίμιον*) or **kukulion** (*κουκούλιον*), which established the musical Mode and the concluding refrain (**efymnion** / *ἐφύμνιον*) repeated at the end of every subsequent Ikos.
In contemporary Byzantine and Slavic practice, the ancient extended Kontakion has been largely replaced in the daily office by the Canon; today, only the introductory prelude (now bearing the name Kontakion) and a single Ikos are chanted at Matins between the Sixth and Seventh Odes of the Canon. Furthermore, this short Kontakion is chanted at the Divine Liturgy immediately preceding the Trisagion Hymn. The ordinary daily Kontakion chanted at the Divine Liturgy is the beloved Theotokion: **“The Protection of Christians”** (*Προστασία τῶν Χριστιανῶν*), honoring the Mother of God as an unashamed intercessor before the Creator.[^630]
