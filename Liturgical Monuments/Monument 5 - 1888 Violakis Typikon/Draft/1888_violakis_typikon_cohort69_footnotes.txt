At the Aposticha: the Idiomelon: "Thy grace hath shone forth, O Lord" (*Ἔλαμψεν ἡ χάρις σου, Κύριε*) twice, and the Martyrikon: "O Christ God, Who art glorified in the assembly of Thy Saints" (*Ὁ ἐνδοξαζόμενος ἐν ταῖς μνείαις τῶν Ἁγίων σου*); "Glory... While this is chanted, the clergy and people venerate the hand of the Patriarch (or Hierarch, or Priest) and ask for mutual forgiveness and reconciliation; only after this reconciliation is completed is "Through the prayers of our holy Fathers..." proclaimed. Footnote 5 of the Greek edition observes that according to ancient Typika, this last hymn (*Ὑπὸ τὴν σὴν εὐσπλαγχνίαν*) is said without chant in recitativo in a quiet voice, adding as the reason that no prostration is made at it, while the preceding three are chanted; but Patriarch Gregory VI desired that this hymn also be chanted like the preceding ones. These hymns are sung at all Sunday Vespers throughout Great Lent, including Palm Sunday evening, but on the other days of Holy Week all four are recited simply without melody. This liturgical order is observed on every Sunday evening up to the Fifth Sunday of Great Lent.
