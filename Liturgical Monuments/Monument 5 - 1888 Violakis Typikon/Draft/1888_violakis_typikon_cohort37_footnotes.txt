Textual and Hymnographical Note: The Heirmos of the Eighth Ode of the First Canon to Saint Euthymius, composed by Saint John of Damascus, appears in later printed editions as *Παῖδας εὐαγεῖς ἐν τῇ καμίνῳ* ("The holy Children in the furnace"); however, the alphabetical acrostic *Ὄλβος πέφηνας γηγενῶν Εὐθύμιε* ("A wealth to mortals hast thou appeared, O Euthymius") demands that the Eighth Ode begin with the letter *Y* (Upsilon). In the Venice 1533 Menaion of Stephanos Savios, the Heirmos begins *Υἱοὺς τῆς Σιὼν ἐν τῇ καμίνῳ* ("Sons of Zion in the furnace"), preserving the ancient and genuine poetic reading per the judgment of Patriarch Constantius I. Scholarly Rubrical Decree of Patriarch Constantine I the Byzantine concerning the Praises on Sundays: Explains the standard rubrical canon prescribing the Resurrectional Eothinon Doxastikon at "Glory..." at the Praises on ordinary Sundays of the year, but establishes that for the great Ecumenical Doctors Gregory the Theologian and John Chrysostom, out of just debt to their pastoral guidance and immortal writings illuminating the Catholic Church, when their commemorations coincide with a Sunday outside the Triodion, the Doxastikon of the Saint is chanted instead of the Eothinon, especially in the Great Church of Constantinople.
