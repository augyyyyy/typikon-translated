[^169]: Commemoration of the Holy Wonderworkers and Unmercenaries Cosmas and Damian of Asia, Sunday Vespers Propers, and Inter-Cohort Resumption (Violakis, pp. 206–207, November 1, § 2 concl.). Physical leaf p221 (printed page 206) and facing English leaf p222 (printed page 207) begin Cohort 23 with the concluding propers of Saturday Great Vespers for the feast of the Holy Unmercenaries Cosmas and Damian when celebrated on a Sunday, continuing directly across the cohort boundary from printed pages 204–205 (leaves p219–p220, Cohort 22). Following the 6 Resurrection Stichira and 4 Stichira of the Unmercenaries at "Lord, I have cried", the Doxastikon of the Saints is followed at "Both now and ever" by the First Dogmatic Theotokion of the Tone. After the Entrance with the censer and the Saturday evening Prokeimenon (*The Lord is King*, Ps. 92:1 LXX), the Resurrection Aposticha are sung, followed by the Doxastikon of the Saints, the Theotokion *Ὁ ποιητὴς καὶ λυτρωτής μου* ("My Maker and Redeemer"), the Resurrection Dismissal Troparion, the Troparion of the Saints (*Ἅγιοι Ἀνάργυροι καὶ θαυματουργοί*), and the Theotokion *Σὲ τὴν μεσιτεύσασαν* ("Thou who hast mediated for the salvation of our race").

[^170]: Order of Sunday Matins and Divine Liturgy for the Holy Unmercenaries Cosmas and Damian of Asia (Violakis, pp. 206–207, November 1, § 3). When November 1 coincides with a Sunday, the preparatory Matins sequence follows the standard Constantinopolitan parish typikon: after Psalm 50 [LXX], the Triadic Canon is sung followed by the Triadic hymn *Ἄξιόν ἐστιν* ("It is truly meet"), the Trisagion, the Hypakoe of the Tone, and the Hexapsalmos. At the Canons, the Resurrection Canon is combined with that of the Saints; after Ode 3, the Kathisma of the Saints is appointed with its Theotokion; after Ode 6, the Resurrection Kontakion and Ikos are chanted, followed by the Synaxarion from the Menaion. The Katavasiai *Ἀνοίξω τὸ στόμα μου* ("I shall open my mouth") are appointed according to the standard post-Byzantine usage prior to the Forefeast of the Entry. At the Divine Liturgy, the Beatitudes assign 4 troparia to the Tone and 4 to Ode 6 of the Canon of the Saints; the Epistle lection is 1 Corinthians 12:27–13:8 (pericope 153, the apostolic charisms of healing and the supremacy of divine love); the Gospel is that of the Sunday; and the Communion Verse is Psalm 148:1 LXX (*Αἰνεῖτε τὸν Κύριον ἐκ τῶν οὐρανῶν* / "Praise the Lord from the heavens").

[^171]: Synaxis of the Archistrategoi Michael and Gabriel and All the Bodiless Powers, Katavasiai of the Entry, and Festal Propers (Violakis, pp. 206–209, November 8, §§ 1–2). The feast of the Synaxis of the Archistrategoi Michael and Gabriel and all the Heavenly Bodiless Powers is ranked among the major non-dominical feasts of the liturgical year. At Great Vespers (§ 1), 6 festal Prosomoia are sung at "Lord, I have cried" followed by three Old Testament lections: Joshua 5:13–15 (the appearance of the Archistrategos of the Lord's host to Joshua before Jericho); Judges 6:11–24 (the Angel of the Lord calling Gideon); and Isaiah 14:7–20 (the fall of the morning star Lucifer). The Aposticha verses are Psalm 103:4 LXX (*Ὁ ποιῶν τοὺς Ἀγγέλους αὐτοῦ πνεύματα* / "Who makes His angels spirits, and His ministers a flame of fire") and Psalm 103:1 LXX. At Matins (§ 2), two canons are sung: the Canon to the Theotokos (*Ὑγρὰν διοδεύσας*, Tone 8) and the second Canon of the Bodiless Powers (*Τοὺς νοεροὺς τῶν Ἀσωμάτων τάξεων*, Tone 4, composed by Saint John the Monk). A distinctive rubrical feature of the Constantinopolitan Typikon is the appointment, beginning on November 8, of the festal Katavasiai of the Entry of the Theotokos (*Ἀνοίξω τὸ στόμα μου*) featuring specific Entry-themed incipits: at Ode 1 (*καὶ ᾄσω γηθόμενος ταύτης τὴν Εἴσοδον*), Ode 3 (*Ἐν τῇ σεπτῇ Εἰσόδῳ σου*), Ode 5 (*Ἔνδον εἰσῆλθες ἐν τῷ ναῷ τοῦ Θεοῦ*), and Ode 9 (*τὰ ἱερὰ Εἰσόδια τῆς Θεομήτορος*).

[^172]: Divine Liturgy of the Bodiless Powers and Sunday Concurrence (Violakis, pp. 208–211, November 8, §§ 3–6). For the Divine Liturgy (§ 3), the Beatitudes assign troparia from Odes 3 and 6 of the second Canon of the Archistrategoi. Following the Little Entrance with the Gospel, the festal Introit verse *Ὁ ἐν Ἁγίοις θαυμαστός* ("Who art wonderful in the Saints") is chanted, followed by the Dismissal Troparion *Τῶν Οὐρανίων Στρατιῶν* ("Of the Heavenly Hosts Archistrategoi"), the patronal troparion, and the Kontakion *Ὁ καθαρώτατος Ναὸς τοῦ Σωτῆρος* ("The most pure Temple of the Saviour"). The appointed lections are: Epistle: Hebrews 2:2–10 (pericope 305, Christ made lower than the angels to taste death for every man); Gospel: Luke 10:16–21 (pericope 51, "I beheld Satan as lightning fall from heaven"); and the Communion Verse is Psalm 103:4 LXX. When the feast occurs on a Sunday (§§ 4–6), the Resurrection propers take precedence in the first position at Vespers, Matins, and Divine Liturgy: at Vespers, 4 Resurrection and 6 Archangels; at Matins, the Kathismata interleave the Resurrection and festal texts across three stichologiae; at the Divine Liturgy, the Beatitudes take 4 of the Tone and 4 from Ode 6 of Canon 2, while the Epistle is of the feast and the Gospel is of the Sunday.

[^173]: Commemoration of the Holy Martyrs Menas, Victor, Vincent, and Stephanie; Theodore the Studite; John the Merciful; and Nilus the Ascetic (Violakis, pp. 210–213, November 11, §§ 1–3; November 12, § 1). November 11 commemorates the third-century military martyr Saint Menas of Egypt (d. 296), martyred with Victor and Vincent, and Saint Stephanie; combined with the great monastic reformer and confessor Saint Theodore the Studite (d. 826). Under the Constantinopolitan parish rule, when November 11 falls on a weekday (§ 1), both offices are combined according to the Menaion; however, when falling on a Sunday (§§ 2–3), the office of Saint Theodore is deferred to Compline (*Ἡ δὲ τοῦ Ὁσίου Ἀκολουθία ψάλλεται ἐν τοῖς Ἀποδείπνοις*) to allow the full union of the Resurrection and martyr propers at Vespers and Matins. At the Divine Liturgy (§ 3), the Epistle is 2 Corinthians 4:6–15 (pericope 176, "treasure in earthen vessels") and the Gospel is that of the Sunday. November 12 similarly commemorates Saint John the Merciful, Patriarch of Alexandria (d. 619), and Saint Nilus the Ascetic of Sinai (d. ca. 430); on a Sunday, the office of Saint Nilus is likewise transferred to Compline, and the Divine Liturgy follows the exact structure of November 11 §§ 2–3.

[^174]: Feast of Saint John Chrysostom, Archbishop of Constantinople, Sunday Concurrence, and Inter-Cohort Boundary (Violakis, pp. 212–215, November 13, §§ 1–5). November 13 marks the feast of Saint John Chrysostom (transferred from the historical date of his repose on September 14 due to the Feast of the Universal Exaltation of the Precious Cross). At Great Vespers (§ 1), the First Stasis of the Psalter (*Blessed is the man*, Pss. 1–3 LXX) is sung; at "Lord, I have cried", 3 Prosomoia are doubled to 6 (*Τὴν χρυσήλατον Σάλπιγγα*), followed by three Old Testament lections (Proverbs 10:7, 6; 3:13–16; 8:6 etc.; Proverbs 10:31–11:12; Wisdom 4:7–15); the Dismissal Troparion is the celebrated hierarchal troparion: *Ἡ τοῦ στόματός σου καθάπερ πυρσὸς ἐκλάμψασα χάρις* ("The grace of thy mouth shining forth like a torch hath enlightened the universe"). At Matins (§ 2), the Prokeimenon is Psalm 48:4 LXX (*Τὸ στόμα μου λαλήσει σοφίαν* / "My mouth shall speak wisdom"), the Gospel is John 10:9–16, and the Praises Doxastikon is *Χρυσέοις ἔπεσι*. At the Divine Liturgy (§ 3), the Epistle is Hebrews 7:26–8:2 (pericope 318, "For such an high priest became us") and the Communion Verse is Psalm 111:6 LXX (*Εἰς μνημόσυνον αἰώνιον ἔσται δίκαιος*). When occurring on a Sunday (§§ 4–5), the Resurrection office is combined with that of the Hierarch.
