Wednesday of the First Week of Great Lent: Paragraph 24 codifies the order of the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts on the first Wednesday of the Great Fast: At Vespers, after the Introductory Psalm (Ps. 103 LXX) and the Eighteenth Kathisma of the Psalter ("Unto the Lord in my distress...", Pss. 119–133 LXX), at "Lord, I have cried" (Ps. 140 LXX), six Stichera are chanted: the Idiomelon of the day from the Triodion ("Fasting, O brethren, bodily, let us also fast spiritually; let us loose every bond of iniquity...", Nēsteusantes adelphoi sōmatikōs) twice, the Martyrikon of the occurring Tone once, three Prosomia of the sequence from the Triodion, and four Prosomia from the Menaion for the Saint of the following day; "Glory... Both now and ever...": Prosomion Theotokion from the Menaion. The Entrance is made with the censer, followed by "O Gladsome Light" and the two appointed Old Testament Readings of the day from Genesis (Gen. 1:24–2:3 LXX) and Proverbs (Prov. 2:1–22 LXX). Immediately following the readings, "Let my prayer arise in Thy sight as incense" (Ps. 140:2 LXX) is chanted with its verses, followed by the Great Litany of Fervent Supplication, the Litanies of the Catechumens and for Those Preparing for Holy Illumination, and the prayer of the Presanctified Gifts. At the solemn Entrance of the Presanctified Gifts, instead of the Cherubic Hymn, the ancient hymn "Now the powers of heaven do invisibly minister with us..." (Nyn hai dynameis) is chanted, followed by the Litany of Completion, the Lord's Prayer ("Our Father..."), and the elevation: "The Presanctified Holy Things for the holy!" Friday of the First Week of Great Lent: Paragraph 25 codifies the order of the Presanctified Liturgy on Friday of the First Week, which serves as the festal eve of the commemoration of the Great Martyr Theodore the Tyro (the miracle of the Kolyva): At Vespers of the Presanctified, after the Introductory Psalm (Ps. 103 LXX) and the Eighteenth Kathisma, at "Lord, I have cried", ten Stichera are chanted: the Idiomelon of the day ("Come, O faithful, let us work the works of God in the light...", Deute pistoi epergasōmetha) twice, the four Martyrika of the Tone from the end of the Triodion, and four Stichera for Saint Theodore; "Glory...": Doxastikon of Saint Theodore ("Having used the apostate ruler as an instrument...", Organō chrēsamenos); "Both now and ever...": the first Dogmatic Theotokion of the occurring Tone of the week. Friday Evening Small Compline and the First Stasis of the Akathist: Paragraph 26 opens the venerable Constantinopolitan rubrical order for Friday evening of the First Week: In the evening, Small Compline (To mikron Apodeipnon) is served; after the hymn "It is truly meet" (Axion estin hōs alēthōs), the Canon to the Most Holy Theotokos ("I shall open my mouth, and it shall be filled with the Spirit...", Anoixō to stoma mou) is chanted with six Troparia in each Ode. At the conclusion of Ode 9 of the Canon, the festal Kontakion of the Akathist: "To Thee, the Champion Leader, do we, thy servants, dedicate a hymn of victory..." (Tē hypermachō stratēgō ta nikētēria) is chanted slowly (argōs) in Tone 8, during which the First Stasis of the Oikoi of the Most Holy Theotokos (Oikoi 1–6: "An archangel was sent from heaven...") is solemnly recited by the presiding Hierarch or Priest before the icon of the Mother of God. At the conclusion of the First Stasis, "To Thee, the Champion Leader" is chanted again at a rapid tempo (syntomon). Footnote 19 records the patriarchal ceremonial protocol: While "To Thee, the Champion Leader" is being sung, the Ecumenical Patriarch (or presiding Hierarch, if present) descends to venerate the holy icon of the Theotokos, and then blesses the two Choirs while they chant "For many years, Master" (Eis polla etē, Despota). The Patriarch stands not upon the patriarchal cathedra (throne), but rather at the Parathronion (the small side throne adjacent to the solea), vested without the patriarchal mandyas (mantle), holding merely the chazranion (the ebony pastoral walking stick). Following this ceremony, the Reader recites the Trisagion Prayers and begins the Kontakion of Saint Theodore the Tyro: "Having received the faith of Christ into thy heart as a breastplate...".

Akathist Conclusion and Gospel Pericope: Paragraph 26 concludes the Friday evening Akathist service: Following the chanting of the Kontakion of the Great Martyr Theodore ("Having received the faith of Christ into thy heart as a breastplate, and trampled down the hostile powers, O much-suffering champion, thou hast been crowned with an eternal heavenly crown", Pistin Christou hōsei thōraka), the Reader recites "Lord, have mercy" 40 times, the prayer "Thou Who at all times and at every hour...", and immediately, omitting the penitential prostrations, the Canonarchs recite the evening prayers: "O spotless, undefiled, incorrupt, all-chaste, pure Lady..." and "And grant unto us, O Master, as we depart to sleep...". The Priest exclaims: "And that we may be accounted worthy to listen to the Holy Gospel, let us pray to the Lord our God", and reads the appointed Gospel pericope. Immediately following the reading of the Gospel, the Priest pronounces the Dismissal: "Glory to Thee, O God, our hope, glory to Thee", and the Patriarch or Hierarch blesses the congregation while the Choirs chant "Our Master and Hierarch, O Lord, preserve for many years!" (Ton Despotēn kai Archierea). The Priest recites the litany of supplication: "Let us pray for the peace of the world...", at the conclusion of which the Second Choir chants the venerable Troparion: "Gabriel was rapt in amazement as he beheld the beauty of thy purity and the exceeding radiance of thy virginity: what worthy praise can I offer unto thee? By what name shall I call thee? I am lost in wonder and dismay: wherefore as commanded, I cry unto thee: Rejoice, O full of grace!" (Tēn hōraiotēta tēs parthenias sou). The service concludes with the final exclamation: "Through the prayers of our holy Fathers, O Lord Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us." Footnote 20 establishes the general rule for the remaining Fridays of Great Lent: On all subsequent Friday evenings throughout Great Lent (unless a Saint of Polyeleos or Doxology rank falls on the following Saturday), after the Trisagion Prayers, the Kontakion for the Dead is recited: "As first-fruits of our nature, O Lord, the world offers unto Thee the God-bearing Martyrs..." (Hōs aparchas tēs physeōs). Footnote 21 codifies the liturgical calendar rule: This exact rubrical order of Friday Small Compline with the Stasis of the Akathist is observed throughout the first five weeks of Great Lent up to Friday evening of the Fifth Week; however, the reading of the Gospel pericope is appointed strictly and solely for the First Friday. Furthermore, in ecclesiastical jurisdictions where the Akathist Hymn is customarily chanted at Matins on Saturday mornings, Friday Small Compline is read together with the Canon of the Tone for the Departed (Nekrosimon), except on the First Friday of Lent, on which, in place of the Canon for the Departed, the proper Canon of Saint Theodore the Tyro from the Triodion is chanted: "Thee, Theodore, lover of God, with hymns do I praise..." (Theou se, Theodore erastēn). Saturday of the First Week (Saint Theodore the Tyro and the Miracle of the Kolyva): Paragraph 27 codifies the festal order for the first Saturday of Great Lent, celebrating the miraculous deliverance of the Christians of Constantinople from the defilement of Julian the Apostate through the intervention of Saint Theodore and the distribution of boiled wheat (kolyva): At Matins, following the Midnight Office, the Six Psalms are recited.
