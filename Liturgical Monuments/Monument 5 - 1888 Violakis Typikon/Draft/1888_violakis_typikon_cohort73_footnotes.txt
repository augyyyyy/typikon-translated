Footnote Note [*]: Violakis records an important textual discordance in footnote [*]: In the General Introduction (Protheoria), § 19, Violakis specifies that at Matins on Lenten Saturdays, unless a celebrated Saint occurs, "Alleluia" is chanted instead of "God is the Lord", sung twice without verses and twice with the two memorial verses: "Blessed are they whom Thou hast chosen and taken unto Thyself, O Lord" (Ps. 64:5 LXX) and "And their memorial is from generation to generation" (Ps. 101:13 LXX). The liturgical book Triodion prescribes this exact same order for the Saturday of the Second Week. In § 34, however, likely through an editorial oversight, Violakis reverts to the common order of "God is the Lord", which is typical of festal Matins. (See the commentary in Diptycha 1994, p. 71, and in the Imerologion of the Ecumenical Patriarchate 2010, p. 128, n. 36).

Matins of the Cross Continued: Paragraph 39 details the festal Matins: After the Second Stichologia of the Psalter, the Kathisma of the Cross is chanted: "In paradise of old the wood stripped me bare, for by tasting thereof the enemy brought in death; but the Wood of the Cross, bearing the garment of life unto men, has been planted on earth, and the whole world is filled with all manner of joy..." (En paradeisō men to prin) twice. Footnote 25 establishes that the Resurrection Evlogitaria are omitted because of the solemn festal character of the Cross. The Hypakoe, the Hymns of Ascent [Anavathmoi] of the occurring Tone, and the Resurrection Prokimenon follow. The Canon of the Triodion is chanted for eight: first the Heirmos: "The most divine Moses prefigured of old the Cross..." (Ho theiotatos proetypōsen); before the first Troparion of each Ode, the refrain is: "Glory to Thy holy Resurrection, O Lord" (Doxa tē hagia Anastasei sou); before the remaining Troparia: "Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee" (Doxa soi ho Theos hēmōn, doxa soi); before the last two: "Glory...", "Both now and ever...". After Ode 3, the Kathismata of the Triodion in order; after Ode 6, the Kontakion of the Cross: "No longer does the fiery sword guard the gate of Eden, for an awesome bond has come upon it: the Wood of the Cross! The sting of death and the victory of Hades are trodden down..." (Ouketi phloginē romphaia) and the Oikos, the Menologion from the Menaion, and the Memorial/Synaxarion [Hypomnema] of the Triodion recounting how the Precious and Life-giving Cross is elevated at the midpoint of the Great Fast to refresh, comfort, and encourage the faithful amidst their ascetic labors. The Katavasiai: "The most divine Moses prefigured of old..."; the complete order of the Resurrection Matins Gospel; after "More honorable than the Cherubim", the Resurrection Exaposteilarion, the Exaposteilarion of the Cross, and the Theotokion. Praises and Patriarchal Custom: At the Praises (Lauds), eight Stichera: four Resurrection Stichera and four Prosomia of the Cross; "Glory...": Doxastikon of the Cross: "The elevated high-aspiring disposition of the wicked Pharisees, the Lord and King of all things has exposed..." (Tēn hypsēlophrona gnōmēn); "Both now and ever...": "Most blessed art thou, O Virgin Theotokos..."; the Great Doxology. Footnote 26 notes that the Patriarch (or presiding Hierarch) does not venerate the Holy Icons at this point according to the usual Sunday custom, because he will shortly venerate the Precious Cross itself. Solemn Procession of the Precious Cross to the Tetrapod: At the conclusion of the Great Doxology, the Choirs chant the slow, melismatic Asmatikon: "Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, have mercy on us" (Hagios ho Theos). The Choirs descend from their stalls and process to the North Door of the Sanctuary singing the Asmatikon. The Priest, fully vested in all his priestly vestments, censes the Holy Table and the Precious Cross, which is placed on the Holy Table upon a tray (diskos) adorned with flowers and three lighted candles. Having completed the censing, he raises the tray upon his head and exits through the North Door of the Sanctuary, preceded by candle-bearers, the six-winged fans [hexapteryga], two censers, and the Primikerios holding the Divamvoulon. When the Priest reaches the center of the nave, where the Tetrapod stands already adorned, he circumambulates it three times; then, facing Eastward, he proclaims: "Wisdom! Let us attend!" (Sophia, orthoi), and places the tray with the Cross upon the Tetrapod. He censes around the Tetrapod in a cross-wise manner while chanting the Dismissal Troparion: "Save, O Lord, Thy people, and bless Thine inheritance..." (Sōson Kyrie ton laon sou), which is repeated in turn by each of the two Choirs. The Patriarch then descends from the throne, and standing before the Tetrapod, venerates the Precious Cross while chanting: "Thy Cross we worship, O Master, and Thy holy Resurrection we glorify!" (Ton Stavron sou proskynoumen, Despota, kai tēn hagian sou Anastasin doxazomen) once; each of the Choirs repeats this chant once. Then the Patriarch blesses the people while the Choirs chant: "For many years, Master!" (Eis polla etē, Despota), after which he ascends again unto the throne.

Veneration Protocol and Flower Distribution: Paragraph 39 concludes the solemn ceremonial of the Veneration of the Cross: Following the Patriarch, the Hierarchs approach two by two in order of seniority to venerate the Precious Cross and receive blessed flowers from the hands of the Patriarch, followed by the Offikion holders (clergy and patriarchal lay dignitaries). Footnote 27 records the pastoral rubric for the congregation: One of the Priests carries the Precious Cross among the congregation so that all the faithful, both men and women, may venerate it and receive flowers from his hands.
