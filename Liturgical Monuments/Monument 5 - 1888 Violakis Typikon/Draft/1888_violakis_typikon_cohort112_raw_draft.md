**SCALE** (*κλῖμαξ, ἡ*; *μουσικὴ κλῖμαξ, ἡ*): In music theory and acoustics, a scale is a structured sequence of musical tones arranged in ascending or descending pitch order. Scales are determined by the fundamental frequencies of their constituent pitches, providing a calibrated measure of musical intervals. The interval between two successive pitches is designated a scale step.

In acoustics, musical pitches are measured by vibrational frequency, expressed in cycles per second (cps) or Hertz (Hz). In liturgical chant, musical tone is distinguished from noise by regular, periodic acoustic vibrations producing a distinct pitch, accompanied by dynamic amplitude (volume) and timbral timbre (vocal or instrumental color).

Diverse modal scales have developed across sacred musical cultures. In the sixth century B.C., Pythagoras formulated the mathematical ratios of the fundamental intervals: the octave (2:1), fourth (4:3), and fifth (3:2). In Christian antiquity, the ancient Greek tonal system was adapted into the eight ecclesiastical modes—the Byzantine **Oktoechos** (*᾿Οκτώηχος*, Tones 1 through 8) and Western church modes.

In Western ecclesiastical practice, the modal framework was gradually transformed by polyphony into the diatonic major and minor scales. The diatonic major scale comprises five whole tones (t) and two semitones (s) in the pattern: t–t–s–t–t–t–s (exemplified by the white keys of the keyboard from C to C′), which served as the foundation of classical Western musical architecture.[^670]
