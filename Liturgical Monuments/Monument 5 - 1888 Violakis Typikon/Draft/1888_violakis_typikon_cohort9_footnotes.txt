[^82]: The Liturgical Stichologia of the Psalter and the Polyeleos vs. Amomos Distinction (Violakis, pp. 66–67, §20). In §20, Violakis details the ancient monastic and cathedral discipline regarding the Psalter at Matins. Traditionally, the appointed Kathismata of the Psalter are chanted in verses (*stichologountai*) prior to the Sessional Hymns (Kathismata) of each of the two (or three) stichologiae. By pastoral economy (*oikonomia chronou*), contemporary parish practice often transfers the reading of the prescribed Kathismata of the Psalter, the Polyeleos, or the Amomos (Psalm 118 LXX) before the beginning of Matins. Violakis delineates the scriptural structure of the Polyeleos (*Πολυέλεος*): for Feasts of the Lord, Psalms 134 and 135 LXX (**"Praise the Name of the Lord"** and **"Give thanks unto the Lord, for He is good"**); for Feasts of the Mother of God, Psalm 44 LXX (**"My heart hath poured forth a good word"**); and for celebrated Saints, proper festal selections from the *Eklogarion* (or Psalms 134–135 LXX as default). On the Pre-Lenten Meatfare and Cheesefare Sundays, Psalm 136 LXX (**"By the rivers of Babylon"** [*Ἐπὶ τῶν ποταμῶν Βαβυλῶνος*]) is added to evoke the sorrow of exile from Paradise. The annual distribution mandates two Kathismata daily and three during Great Lent, culminating in completing the Psalter twice during the First Week of Lent, and once between Palm Sunday Vespers and Holy Wednesday. In accordance with universal Byzantine typika, psalmody ceases entirely from Holy Thursday until Saturday of Bright Week.

[^83]: The Architecture of Sessional Hymns (Kathismata) at Matins (Violakis, pp. 66–69, §21). Paragraph 21 establishes the rules governing the hymnographic Kathismata (*Καθίσματα*, Sessional Hymns) that follow the Psalter stichologiae at Matins. On Sundays coinciding with Marian Feasts, the first two stichologiae combine two Resurrection Kathismata and one Festal Kathisma, displacing the ordinary Octoechos Theotokia; the third stichologia is reserved exclusively for the Festal Kathisma following the Polyeleos. On Afterfeast Sundays and Apodoses, the third stichologia Kathisma is suppressed, and festal troparia replace the Octoechos Theotokia at the first two stichologiae. On commemorations of celebrated Saints with Polyeleos (the Holy Apostles, the Forerunner, the Three Hierarchs, Great Martyrs), the Saint's Kathisma is intercalated into each of the first two stichologiae after the Resurrection Kathisma and before the Theotokion, and the full third stichologia is chanted following the Polyeleos.

[^84]: The Sunday Resurrectional Complex: Evlogitaria, Hypakoe, Anavathmoi, and Matins Gospel (Violakis, pp. 68–69, §22). In §22, Violakis codifies the core resurrectional sequence of Sunday Matins: following the Kathismata, the Resurrectional Evlogitaria (**"Blessed art Thou, O Lord, teach me Thy statutes"** [*Εὐλογητὸς εἶ, Κύριε*], Psalm 118:12 LXX) are chanted, followed by the Little Litany, the sacerdotal exclamation **"For blessed is Thy Name..."**, the Hypakoe (*Ὑπακοή*) of the Tone, the Gradual Antiphons (*Ἀναβαθμοί*), the Sunday Prokimenon, and the Canons. If a Feast of the Mother of God coincides with Sunday, the Evlogitaria are omitted, and the order transitions directly to the Little Litany, Hypakoe, Gradual Antiphons, and the Festal Prokimenon. In this festal order, the Matins Gospel of the Feast is proclaimed, followed by **"Having beheld the Resurrection of Christ"** (*Ἀνάστασιν Χριστοῦ θεασάμενοι*), Psalm 50 LXX read *chuma* (smoothly/non-melodically), **"Through the intercessions of the Theotokos"**, and the festal Idiomelon. On Afterfeasts and Apodoses of Dominical or Marian Feasts falling on Sunday, however, the resurrectional Eothinon (Morning) Gospel of the weekly cycle takes precedence and is strictly preserved.

[^85]: Resurrectional Primacy of the Octoechos Canons and Seasonal Exceptions (Violakis, pp. 68–71, §23). Paragraph 23 sets forth the inviolable canonical principle that the Sunday Resurrection Canons of the Octoechos are never wholly omitted, even when coinciding with Marian Feasts or Apodoses. Violakis catalogs the specific historical exceptions: the Third Sunday of Lent (Veneration of the Holy Cross) and the Sunday before Christmas (Holy Fathers/Genealogy), where the Canons of the day replace the Octoechos Canons, but their initial troparia following the Heirmos are resurrectional and intoned with the verse **"Glory to Thy holy Resurrection, O Lord."** Similarly, on the four Sundays following Thomas Sunday (Myrrhbearers, Paralytic, Samaritan Woman, Blind Man), the Octoechos Canons are superseded by the Paschal Canon of St. John of Damascus with its two Theotokia. When a Marian Feast or Apodosis falls on Sunday, the Octoechos Canon is chanted first to four verses, followed by the festal Canon (or Canons to twelve verses). For weekday commemorations of Saints, the Small Paraklesis Canon of the Theotokos is chanted alongside the Saint's Canon, except for twelve major feast days of Apostles, Evangelists, and Hierarchs which possess proper Marian Canons or Pentecostarion/festal antecedents.

[^86]: From Biblical Canticles to Troparion Refrains: The Evolution of Canon Stichologia (Violakis, pp. 70–73, §24). Violakis documents the liturgical transition from the ancient practice of chanting the Nine Biblical Odes (*αἱ ἐννέα Ὠδαί*)—the canticles of Moses, Hannah, Habakkuk, Isaiah, Jonah, the Three Youths, the Magnificat, and the Benedictus—to the abbreviated modern usage where the biblical verses are replaced by repetitive stichic refrains prefixed to each troparion. The canonical refrains are: **"Glory to Thy holy Resurrection, O Lord"** for Resurrection Canons; **"Most Holy Theotokos, save us"** for Marian Canons; **"Saints of God, intercede for us"** for Saints' Canons; **"Have mercy on me, O God, have mercy on me"** for the Great Penitential Canon of St. Andrew of Crete; and selected verses from Psalms 64, 24, and 101 LXX for funeral troparia. On Great Feasts of the Lord, because the biblical canticles are omitted entirely, no verses are sung before the troparia, save for **"Glory..."** and **"Now and forever..."** before the final two troparia. Throughout Afterfeasts of Dominical Feasts, the refrain **"Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee"** is prefixed to the festal troparia.

[^87]: Liturgical Articulation at the Third Ode: Kathisma and Kontakion Precedence (Violakis, pp. 72–73, §25). The conclusion of the Third Ode of the Canon marks an ancient liturgical junction at Matins. Following the Little Litany and the priestly exclamation, the Kathisma (Sessional Hymn) and Theotokion of the Saint are chanted. If a Feast of the Mother of God falls on Sunday, the Kontakion and Ikos of the Octoechos are transferred to the Third Ode and read prior to the Festal Kathisma, ensuring that the resurrectional commemoration is preserved while granting the post-ode sessional hymnody to the Feast. The same transfer rule governs all Sundays of the Triodion and commemorations of major celebrated Saints. On Forefeast and Afterfeast Sundays, the Kathisma of the Saint is chanted first, followed by that of the Forefeast or Afterfeast.

[^88]: The Climax of the Canon: The Sixth Ode Kontakion and Ikos Transfer Rules (Violakis, pp. 72–75, §26). Paragraph 26 defines the placement of the Kontakion and Ikos at the conclusion of the Sixth Ode of Matins, preceded by the Little Litany and the exclamation **"For Thou art the King of peace..."** (*Ὅτι Σὺ εἶ ὁ Βασιλεὺς τῆς εἰρήνης*). On ordinary Sundays, the Kontakion and Ikos of the Octoechos are read. Violakis catalogs the extensive system of festal exceptions where the Octoechos Kontakion is displaced by that of the feast or seasonal commemoration: Marian Feasts, Triodion Sundays (except the 2nd, 4th, and 5th Sundays of Lent, which retain the Octoechos), the Pentecostarion Sundays, the Ancestral and Pre-Nativity Sundays (Forefathers, Sunday before/after Nativity), the Synods of the Holy Fathers (Nicaea I, Chalcedon, Nicaea II), and major saints whose feasts transfer the Resurrection Kontakion to the Third Ode. Furthermore, during the Pentecostarion, the Kontakia of Saints George, John the Theologian, and Constantine and Helen replace the Resurrection Kontakion at the Sixth Ode whenever their feasts coincide with a Sunday (excluding Thomas Sunday, Pentecost, and All Saints).

[^89]: The Katavasia Cycle: "I Shall Open My Mouth" and the Seasonal Typikon Stations (Violakis, pp. 74–75, §27). In §27, Violakis outlines the annual distribution of the Katavasiae (*Καταβασίαι*)—the concluding heirmoi sung by both choirs descending (*katabainein*) to the center of the nave. The normative Marian Katavasiae in Tone 4, **"I shall open my mouth, and it shall be filled with the Spirit"** (*Ἀνοίξω τὸ στόμα μου*), serve as the foundational default for all non-festal intervals of the liturgical year: (1) from the Apodosis of the Universal Exaltation of the Holy Cross (September 21) until the Forefeast of the Entry of the Theotokos (November 21); (2) from the Apodosis of the Meeting of the Lord (February 9) until the beginning of the Triodion, as well as on the 2nd, 4th, and 5th Sundays of Lent; and (3) from the Sunday of All Saints until July 27. On Sundays within Forefeasts and Afterfeasts, proper festal Katavasiae supersede the general cycle. On the first four Sundays of the Triodion and the First Sunday of Lent, the Triodion Katavasiae are chanted. On weekdays of the Triodion when a Saint occurs, the Katavasiae **"I shall open my mouth"** are chanted, and the Marian Kontakion **"O Protection of Christians unashamed"** (*Προστασία τῶν Χριστιανῶν*) is appointed for the Divine Liturgy.
