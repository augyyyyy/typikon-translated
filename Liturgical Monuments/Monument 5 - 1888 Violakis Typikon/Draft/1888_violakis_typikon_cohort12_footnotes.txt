[^103]: Seasonal Eucharistic Kontakia, Ordinary Ferias, and the Triodion Cycle (Violakis, pp. 96–97, §40 & Note 13). Paragraph 40 establishes the seasonal and weekly hierarchy of the concluding post-Entrance Kontakion (*Κοντάκιον*) at the Divine Liturgy. Outside festal seasons—namely during the general periods of the year (from the Sunday of All Saints until September 1, and from September 22 until November 7)—the universal concluding Kontakion at the Divine Liturgy is the general Marian protectress hymn in Tone 4 (or Tone 2): **"O Protection of Christians unashamed, mediation unto the Creator unshakeable, despise not the prayerful voices of sinners; but in thy goodness hasten to assist us who faithfully cry unto thee: Speed to intercession, and hasten to supplication, O Theotokos, who dost ever protect those who honor thee"** (*Προστασία τῶν Χριστιανῶν ἀκαταίσχυντε*). During Forefeasts, Feasts, and Afterfeasts of Dominical and Marian solemnities, the Kontakion of the feast is intoned at the conclusion of all troparia (e.g., Nativity of the Theotokos, Exaltation of the Precious Cross, Presentation, Nativity of Christ, Theophany, Meeting of the Lord, Annunciation, etc.). In Note 13, Violakis specifies the seasonal distribution of Kontakia during the Triodion: on the preparatory Sundays (Publican and Pharisee, Prodigal Son, Meatfare, Cheesefare) and throughout the Sundays of Great Lent (Sunday of Orthodoxy, St. Gregory Palamas, Veneration of the Precious Cross, St. John Climacus, St. Mary of Egypt), the proper Kontakion of the Triodion (or of the Cross) is chanted as the final Kontakion. On ferias of Great Lent, Saturdays of Great Lent, and Akathist Saturday, the celebrated hymn to the Theotokos **"To Thee, the Champion Leader and Victorious, a hymn of thanksgiving for deliverance from perils do I, Thy city, dedicate unto Thee, O Theotokos..."** (*Τῇ ὑπερμάχῳ στρατηγῷ τὰ νικητήρια*) is appointed. On Lazarus Saturday, Palm Sunday, Holy Thursday, and Holy Saturday, proper Kontakia are appointed. From Bright Week through the Apodosis of Pascha, the Paschal Kontakion **"Though Thou didst descend into the grave, O Immortal One, yet didst Thou destroy the power of Hades..."** (*Εἰ καὶ ἐν τάφῳ κατῆλθες, Ἀθάνατε*) displaces all others. On Ascension, Pentecost, and their Afterfeasts, the respective festal Kontakion is sung.

[^104]: Weekday Daily Kontakia and Annunciation Intersections (Violakis, pp. 100–101, §40 cont.). Paragraph 40 continues with the distribution of daily weekday Kontakia on ferias outside festive seasons. After the Dismissal Troparion of the day and of the commemorated Saint, and that of the Patron Saint of the local temple: on Monday (the Holy Angels), the Kontakion of the Bodiless Powers (**"O Archangels of God, ministers of divine glory..."**); on Tuesday (the Holy Forerunner), the Kontakion of the Baptist (**"O Prophet of God and Forerunner of grace..."**); on Wednesday and Friday (the Precious Cross), the Kontakion of the Cross (**"O Thou Who wast lifted up upon the Cross of Thine own will..."**); on Thursday (the Holy Apostles and St. Nicholas), the Kontakion of the Apostles (**"The sure and divinely inspired heralds..."**) and of St. Nicholas (**"In Myra, O Holy One, thou wast proved a true priest..."**); and on Saturday (the Departed and All Saints), the Kontakion of the Departed (**"With the Saints grant rest, O Christ, to the souls of Thy servants..."**) and that of All Saints (**"As first-fruits of our nature to the Planter of creation..."**). On all ordinary ferias, the sequence concludes with **"O Protection of Christians unashamed..."** (*Προστασία τῶν Χριστιανῶν*). Violakis further details the complex intersection when the Annunciation of the Theotokos (March 25) coincides with days of Great Lent, Lazarus Saturday, Palm Sunday, Holy Week, or Pascha (Kyriopascha): on Lazarus Saturday or Palm Sunday, the festal Kontakion of the day is combined with that of the Annunciation (*Τῇ ὑπερμάχῳ*); on Pascha and throughout Bright Week, the Paschal Kontakion (*Εἰ καὶ ἐν τάφῳ*) and the Annunciation Kontakion are coordinated according to the precedence of the Resurrection.

[^105]: The Trisagion and Its Baptismal and Stauropegic Substitutions (Violakis, pp. 100–101, §41). Paragraph 41 codifies the liturgical chants accompanying the Little Entrance before the Scriptural lections (Epistle and Gospel). The normative hymn of the Byzantine Eucharistic liturgy is the Thrice-Holy Hymn (Trisagion): **"Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, have mercy on us"** (*Ἅγιος ὁ Θεός, Ἅγιος Ἰσχυρός, Ἅγιος Ἀθάνατος, ἐλέησον ἡμᾶς*). On ancient baptismal days—the Great Feasts of the Lord where corporate catechumen baptisms were historically performed—the Trisagion is displaced by the Pauline baptismal hymn (Galatians 3:27): **"As many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ: Alleluia"** (*Ὅσοι εἰς Χριστὸν ἐβαπτίσθητε, Χριστὸν ἐνεδύσασθε. Ἀλληλούϊα*). These designated days are: (1) The Nativity of Christ (December 25); (2) The Theophany of the Lord (January 6); (3) Lazarus Saturday; (4) Holy and Great Saturday (at the Vesperal Liturgy of St. Basil the Great); (5) Holy Pascha (Easter Sunday) and every day of Bright Week through Saturday; and (6) The Great Day of Pentecost (Sunday of Holy Pentecost). On feasts dedicated to the Adoration of the Holy Cross—namely the Universal Elevation of the Precious and Life-Giving Cross (September 14) and the Third Sunday of Great Lent (Veneration of the Precious Cross)—the Trisagion is replaced by the hymn of veneration: **"Thy Cross do we adore, O Master, and Thy holy Resurrection do we glorify"** (*Τὸν Σταυρόν σου προσκυνοῦμεν, Δέσποτα, καὶ τὴν ἁγίαν σου Ἀνάστασιν δοξάζομεν*).

[^106]: Precedence and Structure of the Scriptural Lectionary (Violakis, pp. 102–103, §42). Paragraph 42 governs the order, selection, and pairing of the Scriptural Lections at the Divine Liturgy, comprising the Prokimenon, Epistle (*Apostolos*), Alleluia verses, and Gospel (*Evangelion*). Great Dominical Feasts possess absolute lectionary priority: on such days, only the proper readings of the Feast are proclaimed, suppressing both Sunday and weekday lections. On ordinary Sundays, the Sunday Resurrection Prokimenon, Epistle, Alleluia, and Gospel take primacy; when a Feast of the Theotokos, an Afterfeast, or a celebrated Saint coincides with Sunday, a dual reading structure is enacted: the Sunday Epistle is read first, followed by the Epistle of the Saint, preceded by their respective Prokimena (the Sunday Prokimenon intoned first, followed by the Prokimenon of the Saint). The Gospel readings mirror this succession: first the Sunday Gospel lection according to the Matthean or Lucan cycle, followed by the Gospel lection of the Saint. On ordinary weekdays with a celebrated Saint, either both the daily feria lection and the Saint's lection are proclaimed in succession, or the Saint's reading supplants the feria if the commemoration is of higher rubrical rank. On Saturdays, a distinctive dual lectionary structure is maintained, pairing the Saturday cycle with commemorations of martyrs or the departed.

[^107]: Post-Epistolary Alleluia, the Holy Saturday Prokimenon, and 1888 Greek Typographical Galley Defect (Violakis, pp. 104–105, §43). Paragraph 43 regulates the responsorial chant sung between the Epistle and the Gospel. The universal Byzantine norm is the chanting of the Alleluia in the appointed Tone with its scriptural psalm verses. On ordinary Sundays, the Alleluia of the Sunday Tone is intoned with two verses; on Feasts and celebrated Saints, proper festal Alleluia verses are proclaimed. At the Vesperal Liturgy of St. Basil on Holy and Great Saturday, the Alleluia is suppressed; in its place, the Reader intones the great Resurrectional Prokimenon in Tone 7: **"Arise, O God, judge the earth, for Thou shalt inherit among all nations"** (Psalm 81:8 LXX: *Ἀνάστα, ὁ Θεός, κρῖνον τὴν γῆν, ὅτι σὺ κατακληρονομήσεις ἐν πᾶσι τοῖς ἔθνεσι*), during which the clergy exchange their dark Lenten vestments for bright white vestments and scatter laurel leaves and flower petals throughout the church. Textual and Epigraphic Note: In the 1888 editio princeps, an egregious typographical compositor defect occurred across leaves p117–p120 (printed pages 102–105). At the bottom of p. 102 (leaf p117), §43 abruptly broke off mid-sentence: `...εἰς τὸ τέλος ἑκάστου τῶν Ἀποστόλων τῆς` (*"...at the end of each Apostolos of this..."*). At the top of p. 104 (leaf p119), the printer mistakenly inserted a duplicate galley slug from p. 106 (`...τοῦ ἑορταζομένου Ἁγίου μνήμη τῷ Σαββάτῳ τῆς Ἀποδόσεως...`). Fortunately, the facing English translation on p. 105 (leaf p120) preserves the genuine conclusion of §43: *"together with its verses — and the Choirs are obliged to sing it."* The original Greek is restored herein from parallel Byzantine liturgical rubrics: `[ἑβδομάδος ταύτης εὕρηται γεγραμμένον τὸ Ἀλληλούϊα τρὶς μετὰ τῶν στίχων αὐτοῦ, καὶ ὀφείλουσιν οἱ Χοροὶ ψάλλειν αὐτό]`, resolving this historical printing error.

[^108]: The Cherubic Hymn and Its Seasonal Holy Week Substitutions (Violakis, pp. 104–105, §44). Paragraph 44 governs the Cherubic Hymn (*Χερουβικὸς Ὕμνος*) intoned during the Great Entrance with the Holy Gifts at the Divine Liturgy. The universal norm of the Byzantine Rite is the classic hymn in the Tone of the day (or chosen melody): **"We who mystically represent the Cherubim, and sing the Thrice-Holy Hymn to the Life-Giving Trinity, let us now lay aside all earthly care. That we may receive the King of all, escorted invisibly by ranks of angels: Alleluia, alleluia, alleluia"** (*Οἱ τὰ Χερουβεὶμ μυστικῶς εἰκονίζοντες...*). On Holy and Great Thursday, the Cherubic Hymn is displaced by the Eucharistic troparion in Tone 6: **"Of Thy Mystical Supper, O Son of God, receive me today as a communicant; for I will not speak of Thy Mystery to Thine enemies, neither will I give Thee a kiss as did Judas, but like the thief will I confess Thee: Remember me, O Lord, when Thou comest into Thy Kingdom"** (*Τοῦ Δείπνου σου τοῦ μυστικοῦ σήμερον, Υἱὲ Θεοῦ, κοινωνόν με παράλαβε...*). On Holy and Great Saturday, at the Liturgy of St. Basil, the Cherubic Hymn is replaced by the ancient solemn chant from the Liturgy of St. James in Tone 5: **"Let all mortal flesh keep silence, and stand with fear and trembling, and ponder nothing earthly within itself; for the King of kings and Lord of lords cometh forth to be slain, and given as food to the faithful. And the ranks of angels go before Him with every principality and power, the many-eyed Cherubim and six-winged Seraphim, covering their faces and crying out the hymn: Alleluia, alleluia, alleluia"** (*Σιγησάτω πᾶσα σὰρξ βροτεία...*). At the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts, the Cherubic Hymn is replaced by: **"Now the Powers of heaven minister invisibly with us; for behold, the King of glory enters; behold, the mystical sacrifice, fully accomplished, is ushered in. In faith and love let us draw near, that we may become partakers of eternal life: Alleluia, alleluia, alleluia"** (*Νῦν αἱ Δυνάμεις τῶν οὐρανῶν...*).

[^109]: The Megalynarion and Hirmos of Ode 9 (*Axion Estin*) (Violakis, pp. 104–105, §45). Paragraph 45 defines the Eucharistic Hymn to the Mother of God (*Megalynarion* or *Hirmos of the Ninth Ode*) chanted immediately following the Epiclesis and Eucharistic consecration at the priest's exclamation: **"Especially for our most holy, pure, blessed and glorious Lady, the Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary"**. In the Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom, the standard hymn is the ancient Athonite chant: **"It is truly right to bless thee, O Theotokos, ever-blessed and most blameless and the Mother of our God. More honorable than the Cherubim, and more glorious beyond compare than the Seraphim, who without corruption gavest birth to God the Word, true Theotokos, thee do we magnify"** (*Ἄξιόν ἐστιν ὡς ἀληθῶς μακαρίζειν σε τὴν Θεοτόκον...*). In the Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great, the Megalynarion is the hymn attributed to St. John of Damascus in Tone 8: **"All of creation rejoices in thee, O full of grace: the angelic assembly and the human race; hallowed temple and rational paradise, glory of virgins, of whom God took flesh and became a child, He Who is our God before the ages. For He made thy womb a throne, and shaped thy body broader than the heavens. All of creation rejoices in thee, O full of grace: glory to thee!"** (*Ἐπὶ σοὶ χαίρει, Κεχαριτωμένη, πᾶσα ἡ κτίσις...*). On Dominical and Marian Feasts (and throughout their Afterfeasts and Apodoses), both *Axion Estin* and *Epi Soi Chairei* are replaced by the Zadostoynyk (*Ἀντὶ τοῦ Ἀξιόν ἐστιν*), consisting of the Megalynarion refrain and the Hirmos of the Ninth Ode of the festal Canon (e.g., at Pascha: **"The angel cried unto the One full of grace: Pure Virgin, rejoice! And again I say: Rejoice! Thy Son hath risen from the grave on the third day..."** followed by **"Shine, shine, O New Jerusalem, for the glory of the Lord is risen upon thee..."**; and similarly on the Annunciation, Nativity, Theophany, Ascension, Pentecost, etc.).
