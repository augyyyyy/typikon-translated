### The Relationship Between Immovable and Movable Feasts in the Gregorian Calendar

    In accordance with the Gregorian Calendar, where the Triodion period begins on 24 January (13 days later than the beginning date in the Julian Calendar) the first four immovable feasts listed in the Chart — 11, 17, 18 and 20 January — will no longer fall within the Triodion period. ## Interpretive Rules for the Chart of Feasts: Rules 1, 2, and 3 [Violakis, p. 931]

### Rules 1, 2, and 3 (*Κανόνες Α΄, Β΄, Γ΄*)

1. Holy Pascha [Easter] may occur between March 22-JC and April 25-JC [between April 4-GC and May 8-GC], a thirty-four day period [comprising 35 calendar dates inclusively]. Therefore, the beginning of the Triodion period may occur between January 11-JC and February 13-JC [between January 24-GC and February 28-GC]. The first column of the Chart indicates the beginning date of the Triodion period; the second column indicates the date of Holy Pascha [Easter]. The third column shows the date and name of each of the 18 immovable feasts and, below it, the days of the movable feasts with which each immovable feast may coincide; and the fourth column displays the paragraph and page numbers in the present work where the rubrical order of service for each concurrence is set forth in detail. We provide the following concrete example: if we desire to know in the year 1889 on which day and in which week of the Triodion period the Feast of the Meeting of the Lord (*Hypapante* / *Presentation*) occurs, we first determine the date of Pascha for that year—which in this case is April 9. We then examine the first two columns under this feast, where we find in the first column the number 29 [i.e., January 29], which represents the date of the beginning of the Triodion period in that year; in the second column we find April 9, which represents the date of Holy Pascha for that year. In the third column, directly across from those dates, the table designates the precise week and day of the Triodion period upon which this immovable feast falls—which in this case is the Thursday of the Week of the Publican and the Pharisee.

2. The immovable feasts of the Menologion that coincide within the periods of the Triodion and Pentecostarion are eighteen in number, extending from the Feast of our Venerable Father Theodosios the Cenobiarch (January 11)—the earliest date upon which the Triodion period can commence—unto the Feast of the Third Finding of the Honorable Head of the Holy Forerunner and Baptist John (May 25), which represents the latest date that can possibly fall within the Pentecostarion period. Of these eighteen immovable feasts, twelve pertain to the Triodion cycle and six pertain to the Pentecostarion cycle, as the Chart demonstrates.

3. Regarding the immovable feasts occurring between January 11-JC and February 13-JC [between January 24-GC and February 26-GC], as well as the two concluding feasts of May—namely, that of the Holy Equals-to-the-Apostles Constantine and Helen [May 21] and that of the Third Finding of the Head of the Forerunner [May 25]—these commemorations may occur either within or outside the Triodion and Pentecostarion periods respectively, depending upon the date of Holy Pascha. Consequently, whenever the columnar tables adjacent to these variable feasts do not extend beyond the designated boundary numerals, it is manifest that in those years such feasts are celebrated outside the Triodion or outside the Pentecostarion....[^532]

* *Scholarly Apparatus on the Boundaries of the Paschal Oscillation (Rules 1–3):* Rules 1 through 3 codify the mathematical architecture of the Byzantine computus as articulated in the Violakis Typikon. The 35-day inclusive oscillation of Holy Pascha (from the vernal equinox terminus of March 22 JC to the latest possible Pascha on April 25 JC) directly regulates the opening of the pre-Lenten Triodion 70 days earlier (January 11 to February 13 JC). The table identifies 18 critical intersection points with the fixed Menologion. In Rule 1, Violakis selects the year 1889—the very year following the publication of the 1888 editio princeps—as a pedagogical model: with Pascha falling on April 9, the Sunday of the Publican and the Pharisee occurred on January 29, causing the Great Feast of the Meeting of the Lord (*Hypapante*, February 2) to fall on the Thursday of the pre-Lenten Week of the Publican and the Pharisee. Rule 3 clarifies the status of boundary feasts (such as St. Constantine and Helen on May 21 and the Third Finding of the Head of the Forerunner on May 25), which are celebrated as ordinary Menologion offices outside the Pentecostarion cycle whenever Pascha occurs early.

* *Scholarly Apparatus on the Bissextile Leap-Year Triodion Discrepancy (Rule 4):* Rule 4 addresses the computistic shift introduced by bissextile intercalation (*dísekton étos* / leap year). In the Julian computus, the interval between Septuagesima (the Sunday of the Publican and the Pharisee) and Pascha Sunday is invariant at 10 weeks (70 days). However, whenever February receives an intercalary 29th day (*embolimos hēmera*), the calendar date of the Sunday of the Publican and the Pharisee must advance by one day relative to a common year sharing the same Paschal date (e.g., January 30 instead of January 29 for an April 9 Pascha).
