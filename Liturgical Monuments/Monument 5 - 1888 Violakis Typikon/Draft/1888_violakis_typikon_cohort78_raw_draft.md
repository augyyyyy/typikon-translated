# Holy Week: Great and Holy Thursday Evening (Service of the Holy Passions Continued: Gospels II–IX, Antiphons IV–XV, Procession of the Cross, and Canon) (§ 80 Continued)

## Great and Holy Thursday Evening: Service of the Holy Passions Continued (§ 80 Continued) [Violakis, p. 759]

... Sessional Hymn [Kathisma]: **"Sustaining the disciples at the supper, Thou didst say: One of you will betray Me; but Judas the transgressor did not wish to understand"** (*Ἐν τῷ δείπνῳ τοὺς μαθητάς*). After this, the Second Gospel [John 18:1–28: The Betrayal and Arrest in Gethsemane, and Jesus before Annas] is proclaimed; at the conclusion, the Second Choir chants: **"Glory to Thy forbearance, O Lord, glory to Thee!"** (*Δόξα τῇ μακροθυμίᾳ σου Κύριε δόξα σοι*) (which is repeated at the end of each of the remaining Gospels). Then begins the Fourth Antiphon until the Sessional Hymn [Kathisma]: **"What reason has made thee, O Judas, a traitor of the Savior? Did He cast thee off from the choir of the Apostles? Did He deprive thee of the gift of healings?..."** (*Ποῖός σε τρόπος Ἰούδα*). Then follows the Third Gospel [Matthew 26:57–75: Jesus before Caiaphas and the Denial of Peter]. Then the choir chants the Seventh Antiphon until the Sessional Hymn [Kathisma]: **"O how did Judas, who was once Thy disciple, devise betrayal against Thee?..."** (*Ὢ πῶς Ἰούδας ὁ ποτέ σου μαθητής*). Then the Fourth Gospel [John 18:28–19:16: Jesus before Pilate and the Condemnation to Death] is read; then the Second Choir begins the Tenth Antiphon until the Sessional Hymn [Kathisma]: **"When Thou, O God, didst stand before Caiaphas, and wast delivered unto Pilate, the Judge of all: the powers of heaven shook with fear..."** (*Ὅτε παρέστης τῷ Καϊάφᾳ ὁ Θεός*). Then the Fifth Gospel [Matthew 27:3–32: The Despair of Judas, Pilate Washes His Hands, and Simon of Cyrene Bears the Cross] is proclaimed; after this, the First Choir begins Antiphon Thirteen: **"The assembly of the Jews requested Pilate to crucify Thee, O Lord..."** (*Ὁ δῆμος τῶν Ἰουδαίων*), continuing through Antiphon Fourteen. At this point takes place the Procession of the Holy Cross, as has now become customary almost everywhere: the Priest, carrying the Holy Crucifix and preceded by candle-bearers and the hand-censer [*katzion*], exits from the Sanctuary and proceeds in solemn procession around the temple, chanting in a recitative melody: **"Today is hung upon the Tree, He Who suspended the earth upon the waters"** (*Σήμερον κρεμᾶται ἐπὶ ξύλου, ὁ ἐν ὕδασι τὴν γῆν κρεμάσας*) three times, which is repeated by the choir. The Antiphons continue until the Sessional Hymn [Kathisma]: **"Thou hast ransomed us from the curse of the Law by Thy precious Blood; having been nailed to the Cross and pierced with the spear, Thou didst pour forth immortality upon mankind. O our Savior, glory to Thee!"** (*Ἐξηγόρασας ἡμᾶς ἐκ τῆς κατάρας τοῦ νόμου*); then the Sixth Gospel [Mark 15:16–32: The Mocking by the Soldiers and the Crucifixion at Golgotha] is read. Then the First Choir begins the Beatitudes [Makarismoi], at the conclusion of which is chanted the Prokimenon in Tone 4 three times: **"They divided My garments among them, and for My vesture did they cast lots"** (*Διεμερίσαντο τὰ ἱμάτιά μου ἑαυτοῖς*, Ps. 21:18 [LXX]), and the Seventh Gospel [Matthew 27:33–54: The Crucifixion, the Darkness, and the Death of Jesus] is read. Then Psalm 50 [LXX], and the Eighth Gospel [Luke 23:32–49: The Penitent Thief and the Death of Christ] is read; then the First Choir begins the Canon of the Triodion: **"Early in the morning my soul rises unto Thee, O Word of God..."** (*Πρὸς σὲ ὀρθρίζω*) by Saint Cosmas the Melodist, with four Troparia at each Ode, without biblical verses, and at the end of each Ode the Heirmos is chanted slowly as Katavasia. After Ode 5 is sung, the Small Litany, the Kontakion in Tone 8: **"Come, let us all hymn Him Who was crucified for us; for Mary beheld Him upon the Tree and said: Even though Thou dost endure the Cross, Thou art my Son and my God!"** (*Τὸν δι' ἡμᾶς σταυρωθέντα*), the Ikos, the Menologion, and the Memorial [Hypomnema] of the Triodion. Then Ode 8 of the Canon, at the end of which the Deacon exclaims: **"The Theotokos and Mother of the Light let us magnify in hymns!"** (*Τὴν Θεοτόκον καὶ μητέρα τοῦ φωτός*), and the Ninth Ode is sung. The Small Litany, the Exaposteilarion in Tone 3: **"The wise thief in a single hour Thou didst account worthy of Paradise, O Lord; enlighten me also by the wood of the Cross, and save me!"** (*Τὸν λῃστὴν αὐθημερὸν*) three times. The Ninth Gospel [John 19:25–37: Jesus Commends His Mother to John, "It Is Finished", and the Piercing of His Side] is then read. At the Praises: the three Idiomela with their verses are sung, repeating the first: Verse 1: **"Praise Him for His mighty acts, praise Him according to the multitude of His greatness!"** (*Αἰνεῖτε αὐτὸν ἐπὶ ταῖς δυναστείαις αὐτοῦ*, Ps. 150:2 [LXX]); Idiomelon 1: **"Two evil deeds has My first-born son Israel committed: he has forsaken Me, the fountain of living water, and has dug for himself a broken cistern..."** (*Δύο καὶ πονηρὰ ἐποίησεν*). Verse 2: **"Praise Him with the sound of the trumpet, praise Him with lute and harp!"** (*Αἰνεῖτε αὐτὸν ἐν ἤχῳ σάλπιγγος*, Ps. 150:3 [LXX]); Idiomelon 1 repeated. Verse 3: **"Praise Him with timbrel and dance, praise Him with strings and pipes!"** (*Αἰνεῖτε αὐτὸν ἐν τυμπάνῳ καὶ χορῷ*, Ps. 150:4 [LXX]); Idiomelon 2: **"Every member of Thy holy flesh endured dishonor for our sake: the head with thorns, the face with spittings, the cheeks with buffetings, the mouth with vinegar mingled with gall, the ears with impious blasphemies, the back with scourgings, the hand with the reed, the whole body with extension upon the Cross, the limbs with nails, and the side with the spear. O Thou Who didst suffer for us and didst free us from passions, Who didst condescend to us in Thy love for mankind and didst raise us up: O Almighty Savior, have mercy on us!"** (*Ἕκαστον μέλος τῆς ἁγίας σου σαρκός*)...[^446]

# Holy Week: Great and Holy Friday (The Royal Hours: First and Third Hours) (§ 81)

## Great and Holy Friday: The Royal Hours (First and Third Hours) (§ 81) [Violakis, p. 763]

**GREAT AND HOLY FRIDAY**

**THE ROYAL HOURS**

81.    Around the First Hour of the morning [7:00 a.m.], the Priest having given the blessing, the Superior [Proestos] says: **"O Heavenly King, Comforter, the Spirit of truth..."** (*Βασιλεῦ οὐράνιε*); the Reader: the Trisagion Prayers; the Priest: **"For Thine is the kingdom..."**; the Reader: **"Lord, have mercy"** (12 times), **"Glory... Both now and ever..."**, **"O come, let us worship and fall down before our King and God..."** (3 times), and he reads the Psalms of the First Hour: Psalm 5 [LXX]: **"Give ear to my words, O Lord..."**; Psalm 2 [LXX]: **"Why do the nations rage..."**; and Psalm 21 [LXX]: **"O God, my God, attend to me; why hast Thou forsaken me?..."**. Then "Glory...": **"When Thou wast crucified, O Christ, the tyranny was destroyed, and the power of the enemy was trampled underfoot; for neither an angel nor a man, but the Lord Himself saved us. Glory to Thee!"** (*Σταυρωθέντος σου Χριστέ*); "Both now and ever...": **"What shall we call thee, O full of grace?..."** (*Τί σε καλέσωμεν*). The First Choir begins chanting the first Idiomelon in Tone 8: **"Today the veil of the temple is rent in twain for the rebuke of the lawless, and the sun hides its rays beholding the Master crucified"** (*Σήμερον τοῦ ναοῦ τὸ καταπέτασμα*); the Second Choir chants the second Idiomelon in Tone 8: **"As a sheep led to the slaughter wast Thou brought, O Christ King, and as an innocent lamb nailed to the Cross by lawless men on account of our sins, O Lover of mankind"** (*Ὡς πρόβατον ἐπὶ σφαγὴν*), preceded by the verse: **"Why do the nations rage, and the peoples plot in vain?"** (*Ἵνα τί ἐφρύαξαν ἔθνη*, Ps. 2:1 [LXX]); and again the First Choir chants "Glory... Both now and ever...": **"Unto the transgressors who seized Thee, O Christ, Thou didst cry aloud: What harm did I do unto you, or wherein did I provoke you?..."** (*Τοῖς συλλαβοῦσί σε παρανόμοις*). Next, the Prophecy: Zechariah 11:10–13 [LXX] (The Thirty Pieces of Silver Cast into the Furnace in the House of the Lord); the Epistle: Galatians 6:14–18 (Glorying only in the Cross of our Lord Jesus Christ); and the Gospel: Matthew 27:1–56 (The Trial before Pilate, the Mocking, the Crucifixion, and the Death of Jesus). Directly the Reader says: **"Order my steps according to Thy word, and let no iniquity have dominion over me..."** (*Τὰ διαβήματά μου*, Ps. 118:133 [LXX]), the Trisagion Prayers, the Kontakion in Tone 8: **"Come, let us all hymn Him Who was crucified for us; for Mary beheld Him upon the Tree and said: Even though Thou dost endure the Cross, Thou art my Son and my God!"** (*Τὸν δι' ἡμᾶς σταυρωθέντα*), **"Lord, have mercy"** (40 times), the Prayer of the Hours: **"Thou Who at all times and at every hour, in heaven and on earth, art worshipped and glorified, O Christ God..."** (*Ὁ ἐν παντὶ καιρῷ*), and the Superior [Proestos] says the concluding prayer: **"O Christ, the True Light, Who enlightenest and sanctifiest every man that cometh into the world..."** (*Χριστὲ τὸ φῶς*), and the Third Hour begins.⁴⁵

At the Third Hour, the service begins as at the First Hour, then Psalms 34, 108, and 50 [LXX]. "Glory...": **"O Lord, the Jews condemned Thee to death, the Life of all..."** (*Κύριε, κατέκριναν σε Ἰουδαῖοι θανάτῳ*); "Both now and ever...": **"O Theotokos, thou art the true vine that blossomed for us the fruit of life..."** (*Θεοτόκε, σὺ εἶ ἡ ἄμπελος*). The Second Choir begins the first Idiomelon in Tone 6: **"Through fear of the Jews, Peter Thy friend and companion denied Thee, O Lord..."** (*Διὰ τὸν φόβον τῶν Ἰουδαίων*); then the First Choir chants the second Idiomelon in Tone 6: **"Before Thy precious Cross, when the soldiers mocked Thee, O Lord, the angelic hosts were struck with dread..."** (*Πρὸ τοῦ τιμίου σου Σταυροῦ*), preceded by the verse: **"Give ear to my words, O Lord; understand my cry"** (*Τὰ ῥήματά μου ἐνώτισαι Κύριε*, Ps. 5:1 [LXX]). Then the Second Choir chants "Glory... Both now and ever...": **"Drawn upon the Cross, Thou didst cry aloud, O Lord: For which deed do the Jews wish to stone Me? Because I healed their paralytics?..."** (*Ἑλκόμενος ἐπὶ Σταυροῦ*). The Prophecy: Isaiah 50:4–11 [LXX] (The Obedient Servant Gives His Back to Scourges); the Epistle: Romans 5:6–10 (While We Were Still Sinners, Christ Died for Us); then the Gospel: Mark 15:16–41 (The Soldiers Mock Christ, the Crucifixion, and His Death on the Cross). The Reader: **"Blessed is the Lord God, blessed is the Lord day by day..."** (*Εὐλογητὸς Κύριος ὁ Θεός*), and the rest as above: the Trisagion Prayers, the Kontakion: **"Come, let us all hymn Him Who was crucified for us..."**, **"Lord, have mercy"** (40 times), **"Thou Who at all times..."**, the Prayer of Saint Mardarios: **"O Master God, Father Almighty, Lord, Only-Begotten Son Jesus Christ, and Holy Spirit, one Divinity, one Power, have mercy on me a sinner..."** (*Δέσποτα Θεέ*), and the Sixth Hour begins.[^448]

---
45. At this Hour, as also at the two following Hours, one Deacon in turn according to the order of seniority, vested in the customary mandya, censes with the hand-censer [*katzion*].
