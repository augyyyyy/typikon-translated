[^200]: Feast of the Nativity of Christ (December 25, Case 1: Friday Occurrence, §§ 6–7). Physical leaf p281 (printed page 266) and facing English leaf p282 (printed page 267) provide the ordotikon for Great Vespers joined with the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great on the Eve (Paramony) of the Nativity, followed by the opening of Matins on the Feast itself. Following the Ninth Hour, Great Vespers opens with the Priest in phelonion blessing: *Εὐλογημένη ἡ Βασιλεία...* ("Blessed is the Kingdom..."); after the Prooemiac Psalm [Psalm 103 LXX] and Great Synapte, at "Lord, I have cried" are chanted 8 Stichira Idiomela in Tone 2 (*Δεῦτε ἀγαλλιασώμεθα τῷ Κυρίῳ*, *Τὰ κατ' εἰκόνα καὶ καθ' ὁμοίωσιν*, etc.), repeating the first two to make 8, concluding at "Glory... Both now and ever" with *Αὐγούστου μοναρχήσαντος ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς* ("When Augustus reigned alone upon the earth"). Following the Entrance with the Gospel and *Φῶς ἱλαρόν* ("O Gladsome Light"), 8 Old Testament Prophecies are chanted with their linking Troparia and refrain verses: Genesis 1:1–13 (Troparion: *Λαθὼν ἐτέχθης ὑπὸ τὸ σπήλαιον* / "In secret wast Thou born beneath the cave", with refrain *Μεθ' ἡμῶν ὁ Θεός*); Isaiah 9:6–7; Jeremiah (Baruch 3:35–4:4); Daniel 2:31–36, 44–45; Isaiah 7:10–16; 8:1–4, 9–10; and others (Troparion: *Ἀνέτειλας, Χριστέ, ἐκ Παρθένου* / "Thou hast shone forth, O Christ, from the Virgin", with refrain *Δόξα σοι, Κύριε*). The Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great continues with the Trisagion; the Prokeimenon in Tone 1 (*Κύριος εἶπε πρός με* / "The Lord said unto Me", Psalm 2:7 LXX); the Epistle (Hebrews 1:1–12); and the Gospel (Luke 2:1–20). Note 4 clarifies that after communion, the Troparion of the Feast (*Ἡ γέννησίς σου, Χριστὲ ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν*) is chanted instead of *Εἴδομεν τὸ φῶς* ("We have seen the true light"), because Vespers has concluded. Matins (§ 7) opens at the early dawn; at "God is the Lord", the Troparion of the Feast is chanted thrice.

[^201]: Feast of the Nativity of Christ: Matins Concluded (§ 7 concl.). Physical leaf p283 (printed page 268) and facing English leaf p284 (printed page 269) continue the Festal Matins. Following the Kathismata (*Δεῦτε ἴδωμεν πιστοί* / "Come, ye faithful, let us see" and *Τί θαυμάζεις Μαριάμ* / "Why art thou amazed, O Mary"), the Polyeleos (Psalms 134 and 135 LXX) is chanted, followed by the Kathisma *Ὁ ἀχώρητος παντί* ("He Whom naught can contain"). The First Antiphon of the Anavathmoi in Tone 4 (*Ἐκ νεότητός μου* / "From my youth") is followed by the Prokeimenon in Tone 4: *Ἐκ γαστρὸς πρὸ ἑωσφόρου ἐγέννησά σε* ("From the womb before the morning star have I begotten Thee", Psalm 109:3 LXX); the Festal Gospel is Matthew 1:18–25; and after Psalm 50 [LXX] the Idiomelon *Δόξα ἐν ὑψίστοις Θεῷ* ("Glory to God in the highest") is chanted. Two festal canons are chanted with their Heirmonirmic Katavasiai: the First Canon by Saint Cosmas the Melodist (*Χριστὸς γεννᾶται, δοξάσατε* / "Christ is born, glorify Him") and the Second (Iambic) Canon by Saint John Damascene (*Ἔσωσε λαόν, θαυματουργῶν Δεσπότης* / "The Master, working wonders, saved His people"). After Ode 3, the Hypakoe *Τὴν ἀπαρχὴν τῶν ἐθνῶν* ("The first-fruits of the Gentiles") is recited; after Ode 6, the Kontakion by Saint Romanos the Melodist (*Ἡ Παρθένος σήμερον τὸν ὑπερούσιον τίκτει* / "Today the Virgin giveth birth unto the Transcendent One") and Ikos (*Τὴν Ἐδὲμ Βηθλεὲμ ἤνοιξε* / "Bethlehem hath opened Eden") are chanted with the Synaxarion. At Ode 9, the Megalynaria are chanted (*Μεγάλυνον, ψυχή μου, τὴν τιμιωτέραν... Μυστήριον ξένον* / "Magnify, O my soul, her that is more honorable... A strange and most glorious mystery"); followed by the Exapostilarion *Ἐπεσκέψατο ἡμᾶς* ("The Dayspring from on high hath visited us") thrice. At the Praises, 4 Stichira Idiomela (*Εὐφράνθητε δίκαιοι*, *Ὁ Πατὴρ εὐδόκησεν*, etc.) are sung.

Physical leaf p285 (printed page 270) and facing English leaf p286 (printed page 271) conclude the Praises with the Doxastikon in Tone 2 (*Ὅτε καιρὸς τῆς ἐπὶ γῆς παρουσίας σου* / "When the time of Thy coming upon earth") and "Both now and ever": *Σήμερον ὁ Χριστὸς ἐν Βηθλεὲμ γεννᾶται ἐκ Παρθένου* ("Today Christ is born of a Virgin in Bethlehem"), transitioning into the Great Doxology and the Apolytikion of the Feast (*Ἡ γέννησίς σου, Χριστὲ ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν*). [^203]: Feast of the Nativity Concluded (Liturgy & Dodekahemeron Fasting Rule, § 8 concl.) and Feast of the Synaxis of the Most Holy Theotokos (December 26, §§ 9–10). Physical leaf p287 (printed page 272) and facing English leaf p288 (printed page 273) complete the Divine Liturgy of the Nativity (§ 8 concl.) with the Third Antiphon from Psalm 109 LXX (*Εἶπεν ὁ Κύριος τῷ Κυρίῳ μου* / "The Lord said unto my Lord") with the Festal Dismissal Troparion; the Entrance Verse (*Ἐκ γαστρὸς πρὸ ἑωσφόρου ἐγέννησά σε...* / "From the womb before the morning star have I begotten Thee..."); the Baptismal Trisagion replacement *Ὅσοι εἰς Χριστὸν ἐβαπτίσθητε* ("As many of you as have been baptized into Christ", Galatians 3:27); the Prokeimenon in Tone 8 (*Πᾶσα ἡ γῆ προσκυνησάτωσάν σοι* / "Let all the earth worship Thee", Psalm 65:4 LXX); the Epistle (Galatians 4:4–7); and the Gospel (Matthew 2:1–12). Note 6 sets forth the canonical fast dispensation of the Dodekahemeron (Twelve Days): from December 25 through January 4, there is a total fast dispensation for all foods on all days, including Wednesday and Friday. The text then introduces the Synaxis of the Most Holy Theotokos (December 26, §§ 9–10): at Great Vespers (§ 9), at "Lord, I have cried", 6 Stichira are chanted (3 of the Nativity and 3 of the Synaxis), concluding with the Great Prokeimenon in Tone 7 (*Τίς Θεὸς μέγας ὡς ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν* / "Who is so great a god as our God?", Psalm 76:14 LXX). At Matins (§ 10), at "God is the Lord", the Troparion of the Nativity is chanted twice and "Glory... Both now and ever" of the Synaxis (*Τὴν διακονίαν σου ταύτην* / "This ministry of thine").

[^204]: Synaxis of the Most Holy Theotokos Concluded (Matins & Liturgy, §§ 10 concl., 11) and Sunday after the Nativity of Christ with Saint Stephen the Protomartyr (December 26/27, § 12). Physical leaf p289 (printed page 274) and facing English leaf p290 (printed page 275) conclude the Synaxis of the Theotokos: at Matins (§ 10 concl.), after Psalm 50 [LXX], the First Canon of the Nativity (*Χριστὸς γεννᾶται*) and the Canon of the Synaxis (*Τῷ παντάνακτος*) are chanted; at the Praises, 4 Stichira are chanted; and at the Divine Liturgy (§ 11), the Regular Antiphons are sung with the Beatitudes, the Prokeimenon of the Theotokos in Tone 3 (*Μεγαλύνει ἡ ψυχή μου τὸν Κύριον* / "My soul doth magnify the Lord", Luke 1:46–48), the Epistle (Hebrews 2:11–18; noting that if December 26 falls on a Sunday, the Epistle is 1 Timothy 6:11–16, rectifying the typographical erratum in the printed Greek which reads "Titus"), and the Gospel (Matthew 2:13–23). Paragraph 12 then articulates the rubrics for when the Sunday after the Nativity of Christ falls on December 26 or 27, commemorating the Holy and Righteous Ancestors of God (David the King, Joseph the Betrothed, and James the Brother of the Lord) together with Saint Stephen the Protomartyr and Archdeacon.
