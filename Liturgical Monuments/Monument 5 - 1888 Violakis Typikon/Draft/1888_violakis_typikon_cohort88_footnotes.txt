[^495]: Service of the Holy Passion Concluded: Gospels 7–11, Canon, and Praises (§ Appendix 9). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p871 (printed page 856) and facing English leaf p872 (printed page 857). Sacerdotal action and liturgical order of the Passion Gospels: Following Gospel 6 and the Beatitudes with their Troparia, the Prokeimenon in Tone 4 ("They parted My garments among them...") is chanted and Gospel 7 (Matt. 27:33–54) is read. The Patriarch recites Psalm 50 LXX recto tono, followed by Gospel 8 (Luke 23:32–49). Choir 1 chants the Triodion Canon (Kosmas of Maiuma, Ode 5: "Unto Thee I rise early at dawn..."), with four Troparia per Ode with the refrain "Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee!". Following Ode 5, the Small Litany is intoned, and the Kontakion in Tone 8 ("Come, let us all sing the praise..."), Ikos, Menologion, and Great Friday Synaxarion are read. Choir 2 sings Ode 8, the Deacon exclaims "The Theotokos and Mother of the Light...", and the Patriarch intones the Magnifications of Ode 9 ("More honorable than the Cherubim..."). Following the Small Litany, the Exapostilarion ("The good thief didst Thou account worthy of Paradise...") is sung three times, during which the Primikirios takes his station. Gospel 9 (John 19:25–37) is read, followed by the Praises ("Let every breath..."). At the verse "Praise Him, all His angels...", the Patriarch descends from the Throne, bows and venerates the Crucifix, blesses the congregation while Choir 1 sings "For many years, Master!", and returns to the Throne. The Choirs sing the 4 Idiomela of the Praises, followed by the Doxastikon ("I gave My back to scourgings...") and Gospel 10 (Mark 15:43–47). The Patriarch recites the Small Doxology recto tono, the Great Archimandrite intones the Morning Litany, and Gospel 11 (John 19:38–42) is read. As the Aposticha begin, the Great Archdeacon receives the blessing from the Patriarch, vests in the Bema, and at the Doxastikon ("Already the pen of sentence is dipped in ink...") brings forth the Gospel Book to the Patriarch for the blessing of the proclaimer.

[^496]: Service of the Holy Passion Concluded: Gospel 12, Dismissal, and Patriarchal Acclamation (§ Appendix 10). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p873 (printed page 858) and facing English leaf p874 (printed page 859). Final liturgical rites of the Holy Passion service: The Patriarch blesses the Great Archdeacon: "May God, through the intercessions of the holy and glorious Apostle and Evangelist Matthew...", who ascends the Ambo. At the Royal Doors, the Great Archimandrite exclaims "Wisdom! Stand upright! Let us hear the Holy Gospel!", and the Archdeacon proclaims Gospel 12 (Matt. 27:62–66). After the reading, the Patriarch blesses the congregation while the Choirs sing "For many years, Master!", and the Archdeacon brings the Gospel Book to the Patriarch to venerate. The Patriarch says "It is a good thing to give praise unto the Lord...", the Reader recites the Trisagion Prayers, and the Choirs chant the Dismissal Troparion in Tone 4 ("Thou hast redeemed us from the curse of the Law by Thy precious Blood...") three times. The Archimandrite intones the Augmented Litany and the solemn Dismissal: "He Who endured spitting and scourgings, buffetings and the Cross and death, for our salvation, Christ our true God...". During the Dismissal, the Patriarch blesses the people as the Choirs chant "Our Master and Chief Priest, O Lord, preserve and keep for many years!". The Patriarch descends, signs himself three times, and departs in procession while the Priest exclaims "Through the prayers of our holy Master...". In the Narthex, the Patriarch removes the Mandyas, turns, and blesses as the Great Primikirios proclaims his Polychronion ("May the Lord God grant many years unto our most holy and venerable Master, the Ecumenical Patriarch..."). Ascending to the balcony of the Chancellory (Protosynkellia), the Patriarch blesses the crowds in the courtyard one final time before retiring to his chambers.

[^497]: Order of Service for the Consecration of a Church: Situations 1 and 2 (§ Consecration Rubrics 1–2). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p875 (printed page 860) and facing English leaf p876 (printed page 861). Foundational rubrics for the Consecration (Enkainia) of a Christian Temple: Situation 1 governs the Consecration occurring on the commemoration of a celebrated Saint: At Vespers ("Lord, I have cried"), 10 Stichera: 4 of the Consecration, 3 of the celebrated Saint, and 3 of the Patron Saint of the consecrated church; Glory of the Saint, Now and forever of the Consecration; 3 Old Testament Readings (2 of Consecration, 1 of Saint); Aposticha of the Saint, Glory of the Patron Saint of the church, Now and forever of the Consecration; Dismissal Troparia of the Saint, Patron Saint of the church, and Consecration. At Matins, Kathismata of the Saint and Consecration; Canons of Consecration, Saint, and Patron Saint to 4 each (total 12); Praises: 8 Stichera (2 Consecration, 3 Saint, 3 Patron Saint of church); Glory of Patron Saint of church (unless an Apostle, Evangelist, or Forerunner is celebrated, where the celebrated Saint takes precedence), Now and forever of Consecration; Great Doxology and Dismissal Troparion of Consecration. Situation 2 governs Consecration occurring on a Sunday: At Vespers ("Lord, I have cried"), 10 Stichera: 4 of the Resurrection, 3 of the Consecration, and 3 of the Patron Saint of the church; Glory of Patron Saint of church, Now and forever 1st Dogmatic Theotokion of Tone of week; Readings of Consecration; Resurrection Aposticha, Glory of Patron Saint of church, Now and forever of Consecration.

Consecration of a Temple on Marian, Despotic, and Sunday-Marian Confluences: Continuation of Situation 4 (Marian Feast): Readings: 2 of Feast, 1 of Consecration; Aposticha of Feast, Glory of Patron Saint of church, Now and forever of Feast; Dismissal Troparia: Feast, Consecration, Patron Saint of church, and again Feast.
