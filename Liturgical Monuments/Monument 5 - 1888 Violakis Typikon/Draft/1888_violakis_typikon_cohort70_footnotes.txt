[^405]: Great Lent: Monday of the First Week of the Fast; Order of Matins, Vespers, and Liturgy of the Presanctified (§§ 18–19 Opening). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p691 (printed page 676) and facing English leaf p692 (printed page 677). Great Lent (First Week of the Fast): Paragraph 18 details the solemn opening of Matins on Clean Monday (the first day of Great Lent): The Priest enters the Holy Sanctuary, puts on the Epitrachilion, and taking the censer stands before the Holy Table. Censing cross-wise, he proclaims the initial blessing: "Blessed is our God always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen." The Reader recites the Trisagion Prayers, the Priest gives the Exclamation: "For Thine is the kingdom...", the Reader recites "Lord, have mercy" 12 times, and immediately the Priest proclaims: "Glory to the holy, consubstantial, life-creating, and undivided Trinity..." Then the Proestos (Superior or Leader) reads the Six Psalms [Exapsalmos] in recitativo, while the faithful listen in complete silence and devout reverence. Paragraph 19 establishes the general weekday order observed on Tuesday and all subsequent weekdays of Great Lent (excluding Saturdays and Sundays): The Priest begins from outside the Sanctuary: "Blessed is our God...", "Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee", "O Heavenly King, Comforter..."; the Reader recites the Trisagion Prayers, "For Thine is the kingdom", "Lord, have mercy" 12 times, "Glory... Both now and ever...", "Come, let us worship" thrice, Psalm 50 [LXX], and the Midnight Office according to the Horologion. Following the Midnight Office, the Penitential Troparia are recited: "Have mercy on us, O Lord, have mercy on us...", the Litany of Fervent Supplication by the Priest, and the Small Dismissal with mutual forgiveness: "Let us pray for the peace of the world..." and "Through the prayers of our holy Fathers..." Then the Priest re-enters the Sanctuary to begin the Royal Office and Matins proper.

[^406]: Great Lent: Weekday Matins Order Continued (§ 19 Continued: Alleluia, Kathismata, and Biblical Odes). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p693 (printed page 678) and facing English leaf p694 (printed page 679). Weekday Matins Order: Paragraph 19 continues the order of weekday Matins: The Priest enters the Sanctuary and exclaims: "Blessed is our God." The Reader recites the Trisagion Prayers, the Priest: "For Thine is the kingdom...", and the Reader recites the three Troparia of the Royal Office: "O Lord, save Thy people", "Glory...": "O Thou Who wast lifted up upon the Cross of Thine own free will", "Both now and ever...": "A protection dread and unassailable". The Priest intones the fervent petitions for the civil authorities, the Hierarch, and the people, concluding with the Exclamation: "For Thou art a merciful God, and lovest mankind..." Reader: "In the name of the Lord, bless, father." Priest: "Glory to the holy, consubstantial, life-creating, and undivided Trinity..."; and the Six Psalms are recited. Following the Six Psalms and the Great Litany, the "Alleluia" is chanted in the Tone of the week four times, thrice after each of the four scriptural verses: "My soul yearns for Thee in the night, O God..." [Isa. 26:9 LXX] etc., followed by the Triadic Hymns in the Tone of the week. Footnote 6 of the Greek edition notes that at the conclusion of each first Triadic Hymn, mention is made of the Saint of the day; at the end of each second Triadic Hymn, of the Patron Saint of the Church; and at the end of each third Triadic Hymn, of the Theotokos, exactly as noted below for the Photagogikon (see Footnote 10). Then the appointed Kathismata of the Psalter are recited, and after the Small Litany, the Penitential Sessional Hymns of the Tone from the First Psalter Stasis [Kathisma] (from the end of the Triodion), followed by the Sessional Hymns of the day from the Second and Third Psalter Stases. Then Psalm 50 [LXX], "O God, save Thy people...", "Lord, have mercy" 12 times, the Priest's Exclamation: "Through the mercy and compassion...", and the recitation of the Nine Biblical Odes. The rule for the Biblical Odes establishes that only those Odes possessing corresponding Triodia in the Triodion for that day are recited in full; of the remaining Odes, only the first verse and the concluding two verses with "Glory... Both now and ever..." are recited. However, the Eighth Ode (of the Three Holy Children in Babylon) and the Ninth Ode of Zacharias (the song of the Theotokos being reserved for "More honorable than the Cherubim") are chanted in their entirety every day. Thus, on Monday, the First Ode ("Let us sing unto the Lord") is recited in full; the Third, Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, and Seventh Odes are recited with opening and closing verses only; and the Eighth and Ninth Odes in full. On Tuesday, the First Ode has opening and closing verses only; the Second Ode ("Give ear, O heavens", Deut. 32 LXX) is recited in its entirety...

[^407]: Great Lent: Weekday Distribution of the Nine Biblical Odes and Order of Canons (§ 19 Continued). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p695 (printed page 680) and facing English leaf p696 (printed page 681). Weekday Matins Order: Paragraph 19 details the distribution of the Nine Biblical Odes for the remainder of the week: On Wednesday, the First Ode has opening and closing verses; the Third Ode ("My heart is established in the Lord", 1 Sam. 2 LXX) is recited in full; the Fourth, Fifth, Sixth, and Seventh Odes have opening and closing verses; and the Eighth and Ninth Odes are recited in full. On Thursday, the First and Third Odes have opening and closing verses; the Fourth Ode ("O Lord, I have heard Thy report, and was afraid", Hab. 3 LXX) is recited in full; the Fifth, Sixth, and Seventh Odes have opening and closing verses; and the Eighth and Ninth Odes in full. On Friday, the First, Third, and Fourth Odes have opening and closing verses; the Fifth Ode ("My soul yearns for Thee in the night", Isa. 26 LXX) is recited in full; on Cheesefare Friday only, the Sixth and Seventh Odes are recited in full, whereas on all other Fridays of Great Lent they have opening and closing verses only; and the Eighth and Ninth Odes are recited in full on every Friday. The rubric clarifies that by "opening verse" of an Ode is meant the scriptural verse printed at its incipit (e.g., Ode 1: "Let us sing unto the Lord, for He hath triumphed gloriously"; Ode 3: "Holy art Thou, O Lord, and my spirit praiseth Thee"; Ode 4: "Glory to Thy power, O Lord"). Order of the Canons: During Cheesefare week, the Canons of the Octoechos and Menaion precede the Triodia. Footnote 7 notes that both Canons of the Octoechos are chanted in those Odes lacking Triodia, but where Triodia exist the Second Canon is omitted; if the Triodion has two Heirmoi (e.g. Wednesday and Friday), both Octoechos Canons and the Menaion Canon are omitted. During the Forty-day Fast [Lent], the Octoechos Canons are omitted. On Monday, after the First Ode of the Menaion, follow the Triodia of the First Ode with the Verse: "Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee". Footnote 8 notes that strictly speaking no verse is required before the Triodia since the preceding Biblical Ode supplies the verses; only the Second Ode in the Horologion assigns the verse "Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee" because "Give ear, O heavens" lacks verses; nevertheless, priests have customarily prefixed "Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee" before all Triodia. Then follows the Third Ode of the Menaion, the Heirmos of the First Ode of the Triodion, the Small Litany, the Sessional Hymn of the Menaion with its Theotokion, and Odes 4, 5, and 6 of the Menaion.

[^408]: Great Lent: Canons Order Continued, Daily Photagogika Endings, and Aposticha (§ 19 Continued). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p697 (printed page 682) and facing English leaf p698 (printed page 683). Weekday Matins Order: Paragraph 19 continues the reading of the Canons: After the Heirmos of the Sixth Ode of the Menaion, the Small Litany is recited, followed by the Kontakion of the Saint (if one is appointed); otherwise, the Martyrikon of the Tone following the Sessional Hymns (found at the end of the Triodion) is recited, along with the Menologion of the day. Footnote 9 confirms that if the Saint has a Kontakion, it is said after Ode 6, while the Martyrikon is appended to the Sessional Hymns after the Kathismata. Then Odes 7 and 8 of the Menaion and the Triodia of Ode 8 of the Triodion; Ode 9 of the Menaion and the Triodia of Ode 9; the Heirmos of Ode 8 of the Triodion with "We praise, we bless, and we worship the Lord"; and the chanting of "More honorable than the Cherubim". On Tuesday: Ode 1 of the Menaion; Ode 2 of the Triodion; Ode 3 of the Menaion and Heirmos of Ode 2; Small Litany; Sessional Hymns of the Menaion; Odes 4, 5, and 6 of the Menaion; Heirmos of the Menaion; Small Litany; Martyrikon; Menologion; Odes 7 and 8 of the Menaion and Triodia of Ode 8; Ode 9 of the Menaion and Triodia of Ode 9. This proportion is observed on all weekdays through Friday throughout Great Lent. On Wednesday and Friday of Cheesefare week, because the Triodion has Canons for all Odes, the Heirmos of Ode 6 is taken from the Triodion rather than the Menaion. At Ode 9, we chant "We praise, we bless..." and the Heirmos of the Second Canon of Ode 8 of the Triodion; "More honorable than the Cherubim"; the Heirmos of Ode 9 of the Second Canon of the Triodion, followed by "It is truly right to call thee blessed"; the Small Litany and Exclamation: "For all the powers of heaven praise Thee..."; the Photagogikon [Hymn of Light] of the Tone thrice; the Small Doxology: "To Thee belongs glory..."; and the Litany of Fervent Supplication [Ektenis]. Footnote 10 provides the exact daily endings for the Photagogika: On Monday: 1st Photagogikon: "Through the intercessions of the Bodiless Powers, and save me"; 2nd Photagogikon: "Through the intercessions of Thy Saint [of the Church], and save me"; 3rd Photagogikon: "Through the intercessions of the Theotokos, and save me". On Tuesday: "Through the intercessions of the Forerunner, and save me". On Wednesday and Friday: "By the power of Thy Cross, and save me". On Thursday: "Through the intercessions of the Apostles, and save me". On Saturday: the Exaposteilaria for the dead: "O Thou Who rulest over the dead and the living" and "In Thee do we boast"; or according to exact rubrics, the Photagogikon of the Tone twice with "Through the intercessions of Thy Saints" and "Through the intercessions of the Theotokos", followed by "O Thou Who rulest over the dead and the living".

[^409]: Great Lent: Weekday Aposticha Concl., Hours, Beatitudes / Typika at Ninth Hour, and Transition to Vespers (§ 19 Concluded). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p699 (printed page 684) and facing English leaf p700 (printed page 685). Weekday Matins, Hours, and Typika Order: Paragraph 19 concludes weekday Matins: Following the Litany of Fervent Supplication and the Exclamation: "For Thine it is to have mercy on us and to save us...", the Idiomelon of the Matins Aposticha is chanted twice, with the Verses: "We were filled in the morning with Thy mercy, O Lord..." [Ps. 89:14 LXX] and "And let the brightness of the Lord our God be upon us..." [Ps. 89:17 LXX]; then the Martyrikon once; "Glory... Both now and ever...": Theotokion (or Stavrotheotokion on Wednesday and Friday); "It is good to give praise unto the Lord..." [Ps. 91:2 LXX]; the Trisagion Prayers; the Dismissal Troparion: "Standing in the temple of Thy glory, we seem to stand in heaven, O Theotokos..."; "Lord, have mercy" 40 times; "Glory... Both now and ever..."; "More honorable than the Cherubim"; "In the name of the Lord, bless, father"; the Priest: "He Who is blessed, Christ our God, always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen."; the Reader: "O Heavenly King, strengthen the faith..."; followed by three great prostrations with the Prayer of Saint Ephraim the Syrian. The Hours (First, Third, Sixth, and Ninth) are read according to the Triodion and Horologion. At the Ninth Hour, following the Kontakia ("When the thief beheld the Author of life...", etc.), "Lord, have mercy" 40 times, "More honorable than the Cherubim", "In the name of the Lord, bless, father", the Priest says "Through the prayers of our holy Fathers...", and three great prostrations are made. Immediately, the Beatitudes [Makarizmoi] are chanted by the two choirs: "In Thy Kingdom remember us, O Lord, when Thou comest into Thy Kingdom"; the Troparia: "The heavenly choir praiseth Thee..."; the Symbol of Faith: "I believe in one God..."; "Remit, forgive, pardon, O God, our offenses..."; the Lord's Prayer: "Our Father..."; and the daily Kontakia: "On the mountain wast Thou transfigured, O Christ God..."; the Kontakion of the day (Footnote 11 lists: Monday: "O Archistrategi of God"; Tuesday: "O Prophet of God and Forerunner of grace"; Wednesday and Friday: "O Thou Who wast lifted up upon the Cross"; Thursday: "The sure and God-proclaiming heralds" and St. Nicholas: "In Myra, O holy one..."); the Kontakion of the Patron Saint of the Church; the Martyrikon of the Tone (Seventh Ode Martyrikon from the end of the Triodion); "Glory...": "With the Saints give rest..."; "Both now and ever...": "Steadfast protectress of Christians..."; "Lord, have mercy" 40 times; the prayer: "Thou Who at all times and at every hour..."; "Lord, have mercy" thrice; "More honorable than the Cherubim"; "In the name of the Lord, bless, father". The Priest exclaims: "May God be merciful unto us, and bless us, and cause His face to shine upon us, and have mercy on us" [Ps. 66:2 LXX]; three great prostrations with the Prayer of Saint Ephraim; and the Proestos immediately begins reading the Introductory Psalm: "Bless the Lord, O my soul" [Ps. 103 LXX], which opens Vespers.
