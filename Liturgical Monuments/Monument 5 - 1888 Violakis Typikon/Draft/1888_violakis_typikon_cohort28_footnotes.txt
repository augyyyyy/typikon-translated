[^195]: Sunday before the Nativity of Christ (The Holy Fathers, Case 2: December 20–23). Physical leaf p271 (printed page 256) and facing English leaf p272 (printed page 257) set forth the liturgical rubrics when the Sunday before the Nativity of Christ occurs between December 20 and 23. In this occurrence, the dominical Resurrection office is combined with both the Fathers and the Forefeast of the Nativity (which commences on December 20). At Saturday Great Vespers (§ 4), at "Lord, I have cried", 4 Resurrection Stichira are joined with 3 Forefeast Prosomoia (*Παρθένε πανάμωμε Θεοῦ* / "O all-blameless Virgin of God") and 3 of the Fathers (*Τὰ τῶν Πατέρων σήμερον πιστοί* / "Today, ye faithful, let us celebrate the memory of the Fathers"), concluding at "Glory" with the Doxastikon in Tone 6 *Δανιὴλ ἀνὴρ ἐπιθυμιῶν* ("Daniel, a man of desires") and at "Both now and ever" with the First Dogmatic Theotokion of the Tone; three Old Testament composite readings of the Fathers are read (Genesis 14:14–20; Deuteronomy 1:8–11, 15–17; Deuteronomy 10:14–21); at the Aposticha, the Resurrection Stichira conclude at "Glory" with *Χαίρετε, προφῆται τίμιοι* ("Rejoice, ye honored Prophets") and at "Both now and ever" with the Forefeast Theotokion *Ἰδοὺ καιρὸς ἤγγικε* ("Behold, the time is at hand"); and the Dismissal Troparia combine the Resurrection, the Fathers (*Μεγάλα τὰ τῆς πίστεως κατορθώματα*), and the Forefeast (*Ἑτοιμάζου Βηθλεέμ*). At Sunday Matins (§ 5), after Psalm 50 [LXX], the Triadic Canon is followed at the Lite by "Glory": *Βολίδες ἀστράπτοντες* ("Flashing like lightning bolts") and "Both now and ever": *Ἄκουε οὐρανέ* ("Hear, O heaven"); at the Kathismata following the Polyeleos, two Resurrection Kathismata are joined with one Forefeast Kathisma (*Τῶν προφητῶν αἱ ῥήσεις* / "The sayings of the Prophets") at the first stichologia, and two Resurrection with another Forefeast Kathisma (*Αὐλῶν ποιμενικῶν* / "While shepherds were piping") at the second stichologia; the Canon is the Menaion Canon of the Fathers (*Κύματι θαλάσσης*); after Ode 3, the Hypakoe *Ἄγγελος Παίδων ἐδρόσισε κάμινον* and Resurrection Kontakion are recited; after Ode 6, the Forefeast Kontakion *Εὐφραίνου Βηθλεέμ* ("Be glad, O Bethlehem") and Ikos *Τῆς σῆς, Παρθένε, κυήσεως* ("Of Thy conception, O Virgin") are chanted with the dual Synaxarion; and at the Praises, 4 Resurrection Stichira are combined with 4 of the Fathers. Leaf p272 concludes mid-sentence at § 6 ("After the Little Entrance,"), resolving on physical leaf p273.

[^196]: Sunday before the Nativity of Christ (Case 2 Concluded & Case 3: December 24 Eve Concurrence). Physical leaf p273 (printed page 258) and facing English leaf p274 (printed page 259) complete the Eucharistic Liturgy of Case 2 (§ 6), specifying the entrance Apolytikia (Resurrection, Forefeast, Fathers, Temple Patron), the Kontakion *Ἡ Παρθένος σήμερον* ("Today the Virgin cometh unto the cave"), and the appointed Epistle (Hebrews 11:9–10, 32–40) and Gospel (Matthew 1:1–25). The text then articulates Case 3 (§§ 7–8): when the Sunday before the Nativity falls on December 24 itself (the Paramony / Eve of the Nativity). Here Violakis formulates a crucial liturgical exception: because December 24 is the final and most intensive day of the Forefeast, the dominical Resurrection hymns are entirely suppressed at Saturday Great Vespers (§ 7), and the service is sung exclusively from the Menaion for the Fathers and the Forefeast. At "Lord, I have cried", 4 Prosomoia of the Fathers (*Τὰ τῶν Πατέρων σήμερον πιστοί*) are combined with 4 of the Forefeast (*Προεορτάσωμεν λαοὶ Χριστοῦ τὰ γενέθλια* / "Let us celebrate beforehand, O ye peoples, the Nativity of Christ", transferred from Dec 20); at "Glory": *Δανιὴλ ἀνὴρ ἐπιθυμιῶν*; at "Both now and ever": *Σπήλαιον εὐτρεπίζου* ("Prepare thyself, O cave"); at the Aposticha, 3 Forefeast Prosomoia (*Παρθένε πανάμωμε Θεοῦ*) are chanted with Habakkuk psalm verses (*Ὁ Θεὸς ἀπὸ Θαιμὰν ἥξει* / "God shall come from Teman" and *Κύριε, εἰσακήκοα τὴν ἀκοήν σου* / "O Lord, I have heard Thy report"); and the Dismissal Troparia are those of the Fathers (*Μεγάλα τὰ τῆς πίστεως κατορθώματα*) and the Forefeast (*Ἀπεγράφετό ποτε* / "Mary once, with aged Joseph, went to be enrolled"). At Sunday Matins (§ 8), instead of the Triadic Canon after Psalm 50 [LXX], the Canon *Κύματι θαλάσσης* appointed after Vespers of the 24th is sung; at "God is the Lord", the Resurrection Dismissal Troparion is restored alongside those of the Fathers and Forefeast; and the Kathismata integrate the Resurrection with Forefeast hymnography. Leaf p274 concludes mid-sentence at "then the Litany and the Hypakoe of the Tone, the...", resolving on physical leaf p275.

[^197]: Sunday before the Nativity Concluded (Case 3, §§ 8–9) and Feast of the Nativity of Christ (December 25, Case 1: Friday Occurrence, §§ 1–2). Physical leaf p275 (printed page 260) and facing English leaf p276 (printed page 261) complete Case 3 of the Sunday before the Nativity. At Sunday Matins (§ 8 concl.), two canons are combined: the Canon of the Fathers (*Κύματι θαλάσσης*) and the Forefeast Canon (*Χριστὸν σαρκὶ νηπιάσαντα* / "Christ being an infant in the flesh"); after Ode 3, the Hypakoe *Ἄγγελος Παίδων ἐδρόσισε κάμινον* is recited; after Ode 6, the Kontakion of the Three Children (*Χειρόγραφον εἰκόνα μὴ σεβασθέντες* / "Refusing to worship the graven image") and Ikos (*Ἔκτεινόν σου τὴν χεῖρα* / "Stretch out Thy hand") are chanted; and at the Praises, 4 Resurrection Stichira are joined with 4 Troparia of the Fathers (*Ἆρόν σου τὴν φωνὴν ἀληθῶς* / "In truth lift up thy voice, O Zion"). In the Divine Liturgy (§ 9), the Beatitudes combine Troparia from Ode 3 of the Fathers' Canon and Ode 6 of the Forefeast Canon. The Typikon then opens the solemn Feast of the Nativity according to the Flesh of our Lord, God, and Savior Jesus Christ (December 25). Case 1 (§ 1) governs when the Feast of the Nativity occurs on a Friday. In this occurrence, Thursday (December 24) is the Paramony (Eve): in the morning, following daily Matins, the Great Hours (Royal Hours: First, Third, Sixth, and Ninth) are chanted, immediately followed by Great Vespers joined with the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great. [^198]: Feast of the Nativity of Christ: The Royal Hours (First Hour Concluded & Third Hour, §§ 2–3). Physical leaf p277 (printed page 262) and facing English leaf p278 (printed page 263) provide the ceremonial ordotikon for the First and Third Royal Hours of the Nativity Eve when the Feast falls on Friday. At the First Hour (§ 2 concl.), the Patriarch or presiding Hierarch descends into the nave and begins the opening blessing (*Δόξα σοι, ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν... Βασιλεῦ Οὐράνιε...*); three special psalms are read (Psalms 5, 44, and 45 LXX); following the Troparion *Ἀπεγράφετό ποτε* and Theotokion, three idiomela are chanted antiphonally by the two choirs (*Βηθλεὲμ ἑτοιμάζου*, *Νῦν προφητικὴ πρόρρησις*, and *Τάδε λέγει Ἰωσήφ*); the First Canonarch reads the Prophecy (Micah 5:2–4, prophesying the birth in Bethlehem Ephrathah) and the Epistle (Hebrews 1:1–12, proclaiming the divine Sonship of Christ superior to the angels); and the Priest proclaims the Holy Gospel (Matthew 1:18–25, the Virginal Conception and birth of Emmanuel) before the Holy Doors. In an important rubrical note, Violakis specifies that during the chanting of the idiomela of the Third Hour, the Great Archdeacon (or Deacon, or Priest who read the Gospel) comes forth from the North Door dressed in a black mandyas, bearing a katzion (hand-censer with bells) preceded by a taper-bearer (Lambadouchos), and censes the people, a ceremony repeated at the Sixth and Ninth Hours by other deacons (in the absence of a Hierarch, the Priest censes robed in a phelonion). At the Third Hour (§ 3), following the psalms (Psalms 28, 66, and 50 LXX) and the Troparion of the Forefeast, the choirs chant the idiomela *Οὗτος ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν* ("This is our God"), *Πρὸ τῆς γεννήσεως τῆς σῆς* ("Before Thy birth, O Lord"), and *Ἰωσὴφ εἰπὲ ἡμῖν* ("Tell us, Joseph"). [^199]: Feast of the Nativity of Christ: The Royal Hours Concluded (Third Hour Concl., Sixth and Ninth Hours, §§ 3–5). Physical leaf p279 (printed page 264) and facing English leaf p280 (printed page 265) conclude the Royal Hours of the Nativity. At the Third Hour (§ 3 concl.), the Second Canonarch proclaims the Prophecy (Baruch 3:35–4:4, Christ appearing upon earth and conversing with men) and the Epistle (Galatians 3:23–29, the Law as tutor unto Christ, and adoption as sons through faith), and the Priest reads the Gospel (Luke 2:1–20, the Caesar Augustus census, the manger, and the Angelic Gloria heard by the shepherds). At the Sixth Hour (§ 4), after Psalms 71, 131, and 90 LXX, the idiomela are *Δεῦτε πιστοὶ ἐπαρθῶμεν ἐνθέως* ("Come, ye faithful, let us be inspired divinely"), *Ἄκουε, οὐρανέ* ("Hear, O heaven, and give ear, O earth"), and *Δεῦτε, χριστοφόροι λαοί* ("Come, ye Christ-bearing peoples"); the Prophecy is Isaiah 7:10–16; 8:1–4, 9–10 (the virgin conceiving Emmanuel), the Epistle is Hebrews 2:11–18 (Christ partaking of flesh and blood to destroy the devil), and the Gospel is Matthew 2:1–12 (the Adoration of the Magi). At the Ninth Hour (§ 5), after Psalms 109, 110, and 85 LXX, the idiomela are *Ἐξεπλήττετο ὁ Ἡρώδης* ("Herod was struck with amazement") and *Ὅτε Ἰωσήφ, Παρθένε* ("When Joseph, O Virgin"); then the Canonarch stands in the midst of the nave facing east and solemnly chants the celebrated idiomelon *Σήμερον γεννᾶται ἐκ Παρθένου ὁ δρακὶ τὴν πᾶσαν ἔχων κτίσιν* ("Today He Who holds all creation in the hollow of His hand is born of a Virgin") in Gospel recitative style thrice, continuing with the strophes, repeating thrice *Προσκυνοῦμέν σου τὴν γέννησιν, Χριστέ* ("We worship Thy Nativity, O Christ"), and once *Δεῖξον ἡμῖν καὶ τὰ θεῖά σου Θεοφάνεια* ("Show us also Thy divine Theophany"), whereupon the choirs repeat the idiomelon melodiously; the Prophecy is Isaiah 9:6–7 (Unto us a Child is born), the Epistle is Hebrews 2:1–10 (Jesus crowned with glory and honor), and the Gospel is Matthew 2:13–23 (the Flight into Egypt and Slaughter of the Holy Innocents). In Note 3, Violakis explains that historically the Typika were read after the Ninth Hour, but in modern practice are omitted to transition directly to Great Vespers with the Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great.
