[^220]: Eve of Holy Theophany (January 5) Concluded: Great Vespers Joined to the Divine Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great, Old Testament Readings, and Great Blessing of the Waters (§ 2); and Feast of Holy Theophany (January 6): Friday Matins Opening (§ 3). Physical leaf p321 (printed page 306) and facing English leaf p322 (printed page 307) govern the festive eve and opening of Holy Theophany when January 6 falls on a Friday. Great Vespers joined to the Divine Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great (§ 2) begins following the Dismissal of the Ninth Hour: the Deacon intones the opening blessing (*Εὐλόγησον, Δέσποτα* / "Bless, Master"), the Priest gives the doxological exclamation *Εὐλογημένη ἡ βασιλεία τοῦ Πατρός* ("Blessed is the kingdom of the Father..."), and the Patriarch (or presiding Hierarch) reads the Introductory Psalm [Ps. 103 LXX], followed by the Great Litany. At "Lord, I have cried" (*Κύριε ἐκέκραξα*), 6 Stichira are chanted: the 4 Idiomela of the Feast (*Τὸν φωτισμὸν ἡμῶν* / "Our illumination") repeating the first two; "Glory... Both now and ever...": *Ὑπέκλινας κάραν τῷ Προδρόμῳ* ("Thou hast bowed Thy head unto the Forerunner"). The Little Entrance is made with the Holy Gospel Book, followed by *Φῶς ἱλαρόν* ("O Gladsome Light") and the thirteen Old Testament Readings of the Eve. Following Reading 3 (Genesis 1:1–13: *Ἐν ἀρχῇ ἐποίησεν ὁ Θεός* / "In the beginning God created"), the First Choir intones the Troparion *Ἐπεφάνης ἐν τῷ κόσμῳ* ("Thou hast appeared in the world"), and the Second Choir responds with the choral refrain *Ἵνα φωτίσῃς τοὺς ἐν σκότει καθημένους, Φιλάνθρωπε, δόξα σοι* ("To give light to those who sit in darkness; O Lover of mankind, glory to Thee!"), sung alternately between the verses of Psalm 66 LXX until the Canonarch concludes. Following Reading 6 (4 Kingdoms 2:6–14 LXX / 2 Kings 2:6–14: *Εἶπεν Ἠλίας τῷ Ἐλισσαιέ* / "And Elijah said unto Elisha"), the Second Choir intones the Troparion *Ἁμαρτωλοῖς καὶ τελώναις* ("Unto sinners and tax-collectors"), and the choral refrain *Ποῦ γὰρ εἶχε τὸ φῶς σου λάμψαι* ("Where indeed should Thy light have shone...") is alternated between the verses of Psalm 92 LXX. Following Reading 13 (4 Kingdoms 5:1–14 LXX / 2 Kings 5:1–14: *Παραγένετο Νεεμὰν βασιλέως Ἀσσυρίων* / "So Naaman, captain of the host of the king of Syria [printed Ἀσσυρίων]"), the Small Litany is intoned, the Trisagion Hymn is chanted, followed by the Epistle (1 Corinthians 9:19–27), "Alleluia", and the Holy Gospel (Luke 3:1–18) proclaimed from the ambo, followed by the Divine Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great. The Communion Verse is Psalm 148:1 LXX (*Αἰνεῖτε τὸν Κύριον* / "Praise the Lord from the heavens"), followed by *Εἴδομεν τὸ φῶς τὸ ἀληθινόν* ("We have seen the true light"). After the Prayer behind the Ambon, the clergy proceed to the solea for the solemn Great Blessing of the Waters (*Μέγας Ἁγιασμός*): the choirs chant the Idiomela *Φωνὴ Κυρίου ἐπὶ τῶν ὑδάτων* ("The voice of the Lord is upon the waters"), and the office is celebrated according to the Menaion; during the sprinkling of the faithful, the Second Choir sings the Idiomelon *Ἀνυμνήσωμεν οἱ πιστοί* ("Let us, the faithful, praise"), followed by the solemn festal Dismissal with its proper characteristic incipit: *Ὁ ἐν Ἰορδάνῃ ὑπὸ Ἰωάννου βαπτισθῆναι καταδεξάμενος διὰ τὴν ἡμῶν σωτηρίαν, Χριστὸς ὁ ἀληθινὸς Θεὸς ἡμῶν* ("May He Who deigned to be baptized by John in the Jordan for our salvation, Christ our true God..."). The rubrics prescribe xerophagy (*ξηροφαγία*, strict fast without wine or oil) at table on this eve. Paragraph 3 opens Friday Matins for the feast: following Psalm 50 [LXX], the Lite and the Idiomela of the Aposticha (omitted on the eve due to the vigil Liturgy) are chanted; then the Trisagion Prayers, the Dismissal Troparion of Theophany *Ἐν Ἰορδάνῃ βαπτιζομένου σου, Κύριε* ("When Thou, O Lord, wast baptized in the Jordan"), and the Hexapsalmos; at "God is the Lord", the Dismissal Troparion is chanted thrice, followed by the Psalter Kathismata and the festal Polyeleos (*Αἰνεῖτε τὸ ὄνομα Κυρίου*, Psalm 134 LXX; the printed English column incorrectly labels this Psalm 135 LXX by confusing Masoretic numbering).

[^221]: Feast of Holy Theophany (January 6) Continued: Friday Matins Gospel Order, Canons, and Festive Megalynaria of the Ninth Ode (§ 3 cont.). Physical leaf p323 (printed page 308) and facing English leaf p324 (printed page 309) provide the rubrical sequence of Friday Matins from the Kathismata through the Ninth Ode. Following the 3 Kathisma sedalia, the Anavathmoi of Tone 4 (First Antiphon *Ἐκ νεότητός μου* / "From my youth") are sung, followed by the Prokeimenon from Psalm 113:3 LXX (*Ἡ θάλασσα εἶδε καὶ ἔφυγεν, ὁ Ἰορδάνης ἐστράφη εἰς τὰ ὀπίσω* / "The sea saw and fled, Jordan was driven back"). After *Πᾶσα πνοὴ αἰνεσάτω τὸν Κύριον* ("Let everything that hath breath praise the Lord"), the Matins Gospel is proclaimed (Mark 1:9–11), followed by Psalm 50 [LXX] read simply; "Glory": *Τὰ σύμπαντα σήμερον ἀγαλλιάσθω* ("Today let all things greatly rejoice"); "Both now and ever": the same; Verse: *Ἐλέησόν με, ὁ Θεός* (Ps. 50:3 LXX) and the Idiomelon *Θεὸς λόγος ἐπεφάνη ἐν σαρκί* ("God the Word appeared in the flesh"). Both Canons of the Feast are chanted with 4 troparia each, without verses except for "Glory... Both now and ever..." in each Ode: the First Canon by Saint Cosmas the Melodist in Tone 2 (*Βυθοῦ ἀνεκάλυψε πυθμένα* / "He uncovered the bottom of the deep") and the Second Iambic Canon by Saint John Damascene in Tone 2 (*Στείβει θαλάσσης* / "The Lord, working wonders"). After Ode 3, the Hypakoe *Ὅτε τῇ ἐπιφανείᾳ σου* ("When at Thy manifestation"); after Ode 6, the Kontakion *Ἐπεφάνης σήμερον τῇ οἰκουμένῃ* ("Thou hast appeared today to the universe"), the Ikos, and the Menologion. Both Katavasiai are sung in full at each Ode. At Ode 9, the Magnificat (*Τὴν τιμιωτέραν*) is not sung; instead, the festive Megalynaria (*Μεγαλυνάρια*) are appointed for each troparion. For the First Canon, four primary Megalynaria are given: (1) *Μεγάλυνον ψυχή μου, τὴν τιμιωτέραν τῶν ἄνω στρατευμάτων* ("Magnify, O my soul, Her that is more honorable than the heavenly hosts"); (2) *Μεγάλυνον ψυχή μου, τὸν ἐν Ἰορδάνῃ ἐλθόντα βαπτισθῆναι* ("Magnify, O my soul, Him Who hath come to be baptized in the Jordan"); (3) *Μεγάλυνον ψυχή μου, τὸν ὑπὸ Προδρόμου τὸ βάπτισμα αἰτοῦντα [λαβόντα]* ("Magnify, O my soul, Him Who asketh [receiveth] baptism from the Forerunner"); (4) *Μεγάλυνον ψυχή μου, τὸν ἐκ τῆς Πατρῴας φωνῆς μαρτυρηθέντα* ("Magnify, O my soul, Him to Whom the voice of the Father bore witness"). Three additional bracketed Megalynaria marked with asterisks are preserved from the modern printed Athenian Menaion tradition (ed. Phos, 1960; Apostoliki Diakonia, 1991), although absent from the classical textus receptus of the Violakis Typikon: (5) *Μεγάλυνον ψυχή μου, τὸν ἕνα τῆς Τριάδος, κλίναντα τὸν αὐχένα, καὶ βάπτισμα λαβόντα* ("Magnify, O my soul, One of the Trinity Who bowed His head and received baptism"); (6) *Προφῆτα, δεῦρο πρός με, ἔκτεινον τὴν χεῖρα, καὶ βάπτισόν με τάχος* ("O Prophet, come unto Me: stretch out thy hand and baptize Me swiftly"); (7) *Προφῆτα, ἄφες ἄρτι, καὶ βάπτισόν με θέλων· πληρῶσαι καὶ γὰρ ἦλθον, πᾶσαν δικαιοσύνην* ("O Prophet, suffer it to be so now, and baptize Me willing: for I have come to fulfill all righteousness"). For the Second Iambic Canon, five Megalynaria are provided: (1) *Σήμερον ὁ Δεσπότης, κλίνει τὸν αὐχένα, χειρὶ τῇ τοῦ Προδρόμου* ("Today the Master bows His head beneath the hand of the Forerunner"); (2) *Σήμερον Ἰωάννης, βαπτίζει τὸν Δεσπότην, ἐν ῥείθροις Ἰορδάνου* ("Today John baptizes the Master in the streams of the Jordan"); and three bracketed Menaion additions: (3) *Σήμερον ὁ Δεσπότης, νάμασιν ἐνθάπτει, βροτῶν τὴν ἁμαρτίαν* ("Today the Master buries in the waters the sin of mortal man"); (4) *Σήμερον ὁ Δεσπότης, ἄνωθεν μαρτυρεῖται, Υἱὸς ἠγαπημένος* ("Today the Master receives testimony from on high, that He is the beloved Son"); (5) *Σήμερον ὁ Δεσπότης, ἦλθεν ἁγιάσαι, τὴν φύσιν τῶν ὑδάτων* ("Today the Master has come to sanctify the nature of the waters").

[^222]: Feast of Holy Theophany (January 6) Continued: Conclusion of Matins Ode 9, Praises, Great Doxology, and Great Blessing of the Waters Rite (§ 3 concl.). Physical leaf p325 (printed page 310) and facing English leaf p326 (printed page 311) conclude the Ninth Ode and detail the Great Blessing of the Waters celebrated at the climax of the Great Doxology. Ode 9 concludes with the final Megalynaria: *Σήμερον ὁ Δεσπότης, τὸ βάπτισμα λαμβάνει, ὑπὸ χειρὸς Προδρόμου* ("Today the Master receives baptism at the hand of the Forerunner"); "Glory": *Μεγάλυνον ψυχή μου, τῆς τρισυποστάτου, καὶ ἀδιαιρέτου, θεότητος τὸ κράτος* ("Magnify, O my soul, the power of the Godhead in three Persons and undivided"); "Both now and ever": *Μεγάλυνον ψυχή μου, τὴν λυτρωσαμένην, ἡμᾶς ἐκ τῆς κατάρας* ("Magnify, O my soul, Her who hath delivered us from the curse"). Matins continues with the Exapostilarion chanted thrice; at the Praises: 6 Idiomela (*Φῶς ἐκ φωτός* / "Light from Light"); "Glory": *Νάματα Ἰορδάνεια* ("O Savior, Who clothedst Thyself with the waters of Jordan"); "Both now and ever": *Σήμερον ὁ Χριστός* ("Today Christ"), and the Great Doxology. In the Great Doxology, an ancient Constantinopolitan rubrical custom replaces the customary opening verse *Δόξα σοι τῷ δείξαντι τὸ φῶς* ("Glory to Thee Who hast shown us the light") with the concluding refrain of the Theotokion: "We who have been enlightened cry aloud: Glory to God made manifest, Who hath appeared upon earth and brought light unto the world." Immediately following the third repetition of the Trisagion (*Ἅγιος ἀθάνατος, ἐλέησον ἡμᾶς*) at the end of the Doxology, the clergy and choirs transition directly into the solemn Great Blessing of the Waters: the choirs chant the 3 Idiomela of Saint Sophronius of Jerusalem (*Φωνὴ Κυρίου ἐπὶ τῶν ὑδάτων* / "The voice of the Lord is upon the waters"), "Glory... Both now and ever...": *Πρὸς τὴν φωνὴν τοῦ βοῶντος ἐν τῇ ἐρήμῳ* ("At the voice of one crying in the wilderness"). The Priest exits through the Holy Doors carrying the Precious Cross on his head, preceded by candle-bearers and Deacons; the Patriarch (or presiding Hierarch), vested in epitrachilion and small omophorion, descends from his Throne to the center of the temple where the water vessel has been prepared. The Canonarch reads the three Prophecies (Isaiah 35:1–10; 55:1–13; 12:3–6), followed by the Epistle (1 Corinthians 10:1–4) and the Gospel (Mark 1:9–11) proclaimed by the Patriarch. Following the polychronion (*Εἰς πολλὰ ἔτη*), the Great Archdeacon intones the Great Litany of the Blessing while the Patriarch recites silently the secret prayer *Κύριε Ἰησοῦ Χριστέ* ("O Lord Jesus Christ"), culminating in the aloud exclamation *Τριὰς ὑπερούσιε* ("O Trinity transcending essence") and the great festive prayer *Μέγας εἶ, Κύριε, καὶ θαυμαστὰ τὰ ἔργα σου* ("Great art Thou, O Lord, and marvelous are Thy works"), repeated thrice with the choral acclamation *Δόξα σοι, Κύριε, δόξα σοι!* ("Glory to Thee, O Lord, glory to Thee!"). A critical apparatus note in both Greek and English facsimile columns addresses a numbering error in the 1888 edition, where Violakis misnumbered this footnote as footnote 4 instead of 1; the note documents the pastoral practice emerging in parish churches of delaying the Great Blessing of the Waters until after the Divine Liturgy to prevent disorder and accommodate late-arriving faithful.

[^223]: Feast of Holy Theophany (January 6) Continued: Conclusion of the Great Blessing of the Waters and Divine Liturgy Antiphons 1 & 2 (§ 4 opening). Physical leaf p327 (printed page 312) and facing English leaf p328 (printed page 313) conclude the solemn sanctification of waters and commence the festal Divine Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom. Following the thrice-repeated prayer *Μέγας εἶ, Κύριε*, the celebrant immerses the Precious Cross into the water vessel three times with the sprinkler (*ἁγιαστήρ*), chanting each time the Dismissal Troparion of Theophany: *Ἐν Ἰορδάνῃ βαπτιζομένου σου, Κύριε* ("When Thou, O Lord, wast baptized in the Jordan"), repeated by each choir. He then sprinkles the congregation cruciformly with the newly consecrated water while chanting the Kontakion *Ἐπεφάνης σήμερον τῇ οἰκουμένῃ* ("Thou hast appeared today to the universe"). As the Patriarch ascends the Throne amidst choral acclamations (*Εἰς πολλὰ ἔτη*), he sprinkles the concelebrating Hierarchs and clergy; during this sprinkling, the choirs chant slowly and melodiously the Troparion from Ode 8 of the Canon: *Τριάδος ἡ φανέρωσις* ("The manifestation of the Trinity"). The Second Choir then chants the Dismissal Troparion *Ἐν Ἰορδάνῃ*, and the Divine Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom commences immediately. At the Divine Liturgy (§ 4), the special festive Antiphons of Theophany are chanted in Tone 2: First Antiphon verses from Psalm 113:1, 2, 3, 5 LXX (*Ἐν ἐξόδῳ Ἰσραὴλ ἐξ Αἰγύπτου, οἴκου Ἰακώβ* / "When Israel went out of Egypt, the house of Jacob from a barbarian people"; *Ἐγενήθη Ἰουδαία ἁγίασμα αὐτοῦ, Ἰσραὴλ ἐξουσία αὐτοῦ* / "Judea became His sanctuary, and Israel His dominion"; *Ἡ θάλασσα εἶδε καὶ ἔφυγεν· ὁ Ἰορδάνης ἐστράφη εἰς τὰ ὀπίσω* / "The sea saw and fled; the Jordan turned back"; *Τί σοί ἐστι θάλασσα, ὅτι ἔφυγες; καὶ σὺ Ἰορδάνη, ὅτι ἐστράφης εἰς τὰ ὀπίσω;* / "What ailed thee, O sea, that thou didst flee? And thou, Jordan, that thou didst turn back?"); refrain: *Ταῖς πρεσβείαις τῆς Θεοτόκου, Σῶτερ, σῶσον ἡμᾶς* ("Through the intercessions of the Theotokos, Savior, save us"). Second Antiphon verses from Psalm 114:1, 2, 3, 5 LXX (*Ἠγάπησα, ὅτι εἰσακούσεται Κύριος τῆς φωνῆς τῆς δεήσεώς μου* / "I am well pleased that the Lord will hear the voice of my prayer"; *Ὅτι ἔκλινε τὸ οὖς αὐτοῦ ἐμοί, καὶ ἐν ταῖς ἡμέραις μου ἐπικαλέσομαι* / "For He hath inclined His ear unto me, and in my days will I call upon Him"; *Περιέσχον με ὠδῖνες θανάτου, κίνδυνοι ᾅδου εὕροσάν με* / "The pangs of death compassed me, the perils of Hades found me; I found tribulation and sorrow"; *Ἐλεήμων ὁ Κύριος καὶ δίκαιος, καὶ ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν ἐλεεῖ* / "Merciful is the Lord, and righteous; yea, our God is merciful").

Physical leaf p329 (printed page 314) and facing English leaf p330 (printed page 315) conclude the festal Divine Liturgy of Theophany and commence the post-festal Synaxis of the Forerunner celebrated on the following day. At the Divine Liturgy (§ 4 concl.), the Second Antiphon concludes with the refrain *Σῶσον ἡμᾶς Υἱὲ Θεοῦ, ὁ ἐν Ἰορδάνῃ ὑπὸ Ἰωάννου βαπτισθείς, ψάλλοντάς σοι, Ἀλληλούϊα* ("Save us, O Son of God, Who wast baptized by John in the Jordan, who sing to Thee: Alleluia"), followed by *Ὁ Μονογενὴς Υἱὸς καὶ Λόγος τοῦ Θεοῦ* ("Only-begotten Son and Immortal Word of God").
