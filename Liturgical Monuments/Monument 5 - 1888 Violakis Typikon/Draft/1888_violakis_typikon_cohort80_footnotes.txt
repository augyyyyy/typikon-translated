[^455]: Great and Holy Saturday: Vespers with the Divine Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great Opening (§ 86 Opening). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p791 (printed page 776) and facing English leaf p792 (printed page 777). Rubrics of Paragraph 86: The Archdeacon exclaims: "Bless, Master!" (Eulogeson Despota); the Priest intones the Kingdom Doxology: "Blessed is the kingdom of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen." (Eulogemene he basileia tou Patros); and the Patriarch recites the Introductory Psalm [Psalm 103 LXX: "Bless the Lord, O my soul..."] plainly. At "Lord, I have cried" (Kyrie ekekraxa), eight stichoi are set, and the choirs chant four Resurrectional Stichera in Tone 1 and four Idiomela of the day in Tone 8: 1. Tone 1: "Accept our evening prayers, O holy Lord, and grant us remission of sins, for Thou alone art He Who hast manifested the Resurrection unto the world" (Tas hesperinas hemon euchas); 2. Tone 1: "Surround Zion, O ye peoples, and encompass her, and give glory within her unto Him Who is risen from the dead; for He is our God, Who hath delivered us from our iniquities" (Kyklosate laoi Sion); 3. Tone 1: "Come, ye peoples, let us hymn and worship Christ, glorifying His Resurrection from the dead; for He is our God, Who hath delivered the world from the error of the enemy" (Deute laoi hymnesomen); 4. Tone 1: "By Thy Passion, O Christ, we have been set free from passions, and by Thy Resurrection we have been delivered from corruption: O Lord, glory to Thee!" (To pathei sou Christe); 5. & 6. Tone 8: "Today Hades groaning cries aloud: Woe is me, if only I had not received Him Who was born of Mary; for coming upon me He has dissolved my power, and broken the gates of bronze; as God, He has raised up the souls that I held fast of old! Glory to Thy Cross and Thy Resurrection, O Lord!" (Semeron ho hades stenon boa) (twice); 7. Tone 8: "Today Hades groaning cries aloud: My power is dissolved; I received a dead Man as one of the dead, but could not hold Him at all, for with Him I lose those over whom I had ruled; all those whom I held fast for ages I have vomited forth! But He Who was crucified has raised up the dead! Glory to Thy Cross and Thy Resurrection, O Lord!" (Semeron ho hades stenon boa: Katelythe mou he exousia); 8. Tone 8: "Today Hades groaning cries aloud: My dominion is swallowed up; the Shepherd has been crucified, and has raised up Adam; those over whom I reigned are taken from me; whom I swallowed down in my might, I have surrendered utterly; the Crucified One has emptied the graves; the might of death has no power! Glory to Thy Cross and Thy Resurrection, O Lord!" (Semeron ho hades stenon boa: Katepothe mou to kratos). "Glory...": Doxastikon in Tone 6: "Moses the great mystically prefigured this present day, saying: And God blessed the seventh day; for this is the blessed Sabbath, this is the day of rest, whereon the Only-Begotten Son of God rested from all His works..." (Ten semeron mystikos ho megas Moyses); "Both now and ever...": Dogmatic Theotokion in Tone 1: "Let us praise the Virgin Mary, the glory of the whole world, who sprang forth from mankind and gave birth to the Master, the gate of heaven..." (Ten pankosmion doxan). The Entrance is made with the Holy Gospel Book; "O Gladsome Light" (Phos hilaron) is chanted; there is no Prokimenon, but directly the three Old Testament Readings are proclaimed: 1. Genesis 1:1–13 LXX (The Creation of the World: Heaven and Earth, the Light, the Firmament, the Earth, the Seas, and Plant Life); 2. Jonah 1:1–4:11 LXX (The Entire Book of the Prophet Jonah: The Three Days and Three Nights Prefiguring the Burial and Resurrection of the Lord); 3. Daniel 3:1–23 LXX (The Three Holy Youths in the Babylonian Furnace of Fire), followed by the Prayer of Azariah [Daniel 3:26–45 LXX / Apocrypha]: "Blessed art Thou, O Lord, God of our fathers, and praised and glorified is Thy Name unto the ages...", and the Hymn of the Three Youths [Daniel 3:52–90 LXX / Song of the Three Holy Children] through verse 56: "Blessed art Thou in the firmament of heaven, and praised and glorified unto the ages" (Eulogemenos ei en to stereomati tou ouranou). At this point, all stand and begin chanting in Tone 1 the remainder of the Hymn of the Three Youths [from verse 57]: "Bless the Lord, all ye works of the Lord; hymn and exalt Him above all forever!" (Ton Kyrion hymneite kai hyperypsoute eis pantas tous aionas), with the Canonarch intoning the intermittent verses through verse 88: "Bless the Lord, Hananiah, Azariah, and Mishael; hymn and exalt Him above all forever!", the choir repeating after each verse the refrain: "Hymn and exalt Him above all forever!" (Hyperypsoute eis pantas tous aionas), concluding with the verses for the Apostles, Prophets, and Martyrs, and the final Doxology: "We bless the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit: the Lord!" (Eulogoumen Patera, Yion kai Hagion Pneuma, ton Kyrion).

[^456]: Great and Holy Saturday: Vespers with the Divine Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great Continued (§ 86 Continued). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p793 (printed page 778) and facing English leaf p794 (printed page 779). Concluding cadences of the Hymn of the Three Youths: "We hymn, we bless, and we worship the Lord! Hymn and exalt Him above all forever!". The Little Litany is recited by the Deacon, with the Priest's exclamation: "For Holy art Thou, our God, and unto Thee we send up glory: to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.". Instead of the Trisagion Hymn, the choirs chant the Baptismal Hymn: "As many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ. Alleluia" (Hosoi eis Christon ebaptisthete, Christon enedysasthe. Allelouia, Gal. 3:27). The Epistle of the day is proclaimed: Romans 6:3–11 (Baptized into Christ's Death, Buried with Him through Baptism, That as Christ was Raised from the Dead We Also Should Walk in Newness of Life). At the conclusion of the Epistle, "Alleluia" is omitted. Instead, the Priest emerges from within the Sanctuary and intones in Tone 7 (Grave Tone) the triumphant Verse: "Arise, O God, judge the earth; for Thou shalt inherit among all the nations!" (Anasta ho Theos krinon ten gen, hoti sy katakleronomeseis en pasi tois ethnesi, Ps. 81:8 LXX), while proceeding down the solea and nave, scattering fragrant laurel leaves (bay leaves) throughout the entire temple as an epigraphic symbol of the Lord's victory over death and the shattering of Hades. The choirs repeat this verse after each of the six stichoi of Psalm 81 LXX intoned by the Canonarch: 1. "God standeth in the assembly of gods; in the midst He judgeth gods" (Ps. 81:1 LXX); 2. "How long will ye judge unjustly, and accept the persons of the wicked?" (Ps. 81:2 LXX); 3. "Judge the orphan and the poor; do justice to the humble and needy" (Ps. 81:3 LXX); 4. "Rescue the poor and needy; deliver them out of the hand of the sinner" (Ps. 81:4 LXX); 5. "They have not known, neither have they understood; they walk on in darkness; let all the foundations of the earth be shaken" (Ps. 81:5 LXX); 6. "I said: Ye are gods, and all of you the children of the Most High; but ye die as men, and fall as one of the princes" (Ps. 81:6–7 LXX). After the final stichos, "Arise, O God" is chanted slowly and majestically. The Holy Gospel: Matthew 28:1–20 (The Resurrection of Christ, the appearance of the angel to the women, and the Great Commission: "In the end of the Sabbath, as it began to dawn toward the first day of the week..." until "even unto the end of the world") is proclaimed from the ambo. The Divine Liturgy of Saint Basil the Great continues in its regular order. Instead of the customary Cherubic Hymn, the ancient Liturgical Troparion of the Liturgy of Saint James in Tone 5 is chanted: "Let all mortal flesh keep silence, and stand with fear and trembling, and ponder nothing earthly within itself; for the King of kings and Lord of lords cometh forth to be slain, and to be given as food to the faithful. Before Him go the choirs of angels with every principality and power," [during which the solemn Great Entrance is made with the Holy Gifts] "the many-eyed Cherubim and the six-winged Seraphim, covering their faces and singing the hymn: Alleluia, Alleluia, Alleluia!" (Sigasato pasa sarx broteia).

Violakis notes the foundational canonical reference to General Provisions [Protheoria], Paragraph 16, and the official Report of the Typikon Committee published in the Preface of the 1888 Typikon.

[^458]: Pentekostarion: The Sunday of Holy Pascha (The Midnight Office Opening). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p797 (printed page 782) and facing English leaf p798 (printed page 783). Major structural division: Ritual Order of the Services of the Pentekostarion: The Sunday of Pascha (Mesonyktikon / Midnight Office). At around the Fifth Hour of the night (11:00 p.m.), the clergy and faithful enter into the temple. The Priest intones the opening blessing: "Blessed is our God always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen." (Eulogetos ho Theos); the Patriarch says: "Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee. O Heavenly King, Comforter, the Spirit of truth..." (Basileu ouranie); the Reader recites the Trisagion Prayers, "Lord, have mercy" (12 times), "Glory... Both now and ever...", "Come, let us worship" (thrice), and Psalm 50 LXX plainly. The choirs chant the monumental Holy Saturday Canon in Tone 6: "Of old the surge of the sea covered the pursuing tyrant beneath the waves..." (Kymati thalasses) for six Troparia, each preceded by the refrain: "Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee"; after each Ode, the Heirmos is chanted slowly as Katavasia; the Sessional Hymn [Kathisma] is omitted. At the Fourth Ode, the deacons, vested in their sacred vestments, exit from the Sanctuary bearing the two-branched and three-branched candlesticks [dikirotrikira]. The Patriarch, having venerated the holy icons and prepared himself according to custom for the Divine Liturgy, enters the Sanctuary and vests in all his hierarchical vestments, as do all the concelebrating Hierarchs. Following the Ninth Ode of the Canon, the Trisagion Prayers are recited from within the Sanctuary; then the Dismissal Troparion in Tone 2: "When Thou didst descend unto death, O Life Immortal, then didst Thou slay Hades with the brilliance of Thy Divinity..." (Hote katelthes pros ton thanaton); the Augmented Litany and the Dismissal of the Midnight Office are given. The Patriarch, preceded by the concelebrating Hierarchs, exits from the Sanctuary, chanting slowly with them the Sticheron in Tone 6: "Thy Resurrection, O Christ Savior, the angels hymn in the heavens: enable us also on earth to glorify Thee with a pure heart" (Ten Anastasin sou Christe Soter), which is repeated also by the choirs until the procession reaches the appointed outdoor platform [dais / ambo]. Printed Footnote 1 of Violakis: Records the widespread local custom wherein the presiding Hierarch stands before the Beautiful Gate holding the two-branched and three-branched candlestick [dikirotrikiron] lit from the sleepless lamp upon the Holy Table, chanting: "Come, receive the light from the unwaning Light, and glorify Christ Who is risen from the dead!" (Deute labete phos ek tou anesperou photos), and summons the congregation to light their tapers. A translator's scholarly reference cites Alkiviadis C. Calivas's liturgical treatise *Great Week and Pascha in the Greek Orthodox Church* (pages 97–121) regarding the historical evolution of Holy Week and Paschal services.

[^459]: Pentekostarion: The Sunday of Holy Pascha (Paschal Matins: The Resurrection Service and Canon). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p799 (printed page 784) and facing English leaf p800 (printed page 785). Continuation of the Paschal Service at the outdoor platform: The Archdeacon exclaims: "And that we may be accounted worthy to hear the Holy Gospel, let us pray unto the Lord God" (Kai hyper tou kataxiothenai hemas). The Patriarch proclaims the Holy Gospel: "In the end of the Sabbath, as it began to dawn toward the first day of the week..." (Opse sabbaton te epiphoskouse) [Matthew 28:1–20]. The choirs sing: "Many years to you, Master!" (Eis polla ete, Despota). Printed Footnote 2 of Violakis: Documents the liturgical practice in large cathedrals where this Gospel is proclaimed collaboratively by multiple Hierarchs and Priests, dividing the pericopes at verse 10 and continuing at verse 16 (omitting verses 11–15); it also notes the alternative tradition observed in various monasteries and parishes of proclaiming the Second Eothinon Gospel: "When the Sabbath was past" (Diagenomenou tou Sabbatou, Mark 16:1–8). The Patriarch takes the censer, censes the Holy Gospel Book three times, and intones the solemn Paschal Doxology: "Glory to the holy, and consubstantial, and life-creating, and undivided Trinity, always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen." (Doxa te hagia kai homoousio kai zoopoio kai adiaireto Triadi). The Patriarch sings the Paschal Troparion in Tone 5: "Christ is risen from the dead, trampling down death by death, and upon those in the tombs bestowing life!" (Christos aneste ek nekron) three times; the choirs repeat it six times, while the Patriarch censes and intones the four Paschal Verses of Psalm 67 LXX: 1. "Let God arise, and let His enemies be scattered; let them also that hate Him flee before Him!" (Ps. 67:2 LXX); 2. "As smoke vanisheth, so let them vanish; as wax melteth before the fire!" (Ps. 67:3 LXX); 3. "So let the wicked perish at the presence of God; and let the righteous be glad!" (Ps. 67:3 LXX); 4. "This is the day which the Lord hath made; let us rejoice and be glad in it!" (Ps. 117:24 LXX), followed by "Glory... Both now and ever...". The Patriarch sings the Troparion once more while censing the clergy and people. The Archdeacon recites the Great Litany: "In peace let us pray unto the Lord" (En eirene tou Kyriou deetnomen), and the Patriarch sings the Exclamation: "For unto Thee is due all glory, honor, and worship: to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen." (Hoti prepei soi pasa doxa). The clergy and people process back into the temple in solemn order. The Patriarch begins chanting the Paschal Canon of Saint John of Damascus in Tone 1: "It is the day of Resurrection: let us be radiant, O ye peoples!" (Anastaseos hemera) with the concelebrating Hierarchs, with six Troparia in each Ode preceded by the verse: "Glory to Thy holy Resurrection, O Lord!" (Doxa te hagia Anastasei sou, Kyrie); the final two Troparia are preceded by "Glory..." and "Both now and ever...". After each Ode, the Katavasia is chanted, followed by the Little Litany and the appointed Exclamations: Ode 1: "For Thine is the dominion..."; Ode 3: "For Thou art our God..."; Ode 4: "For Thou art a good God, and lovest mankind..."; Ode 5: "For sanctified and glorified is Thine all-honorable and majestic Name..."; Ode 6: "For Thou art the King of peace, and the Savior of our souls..."; Ode 7: "Blessed and glorified be the might of Thy kingdom..."; Ode 8: "For blessed is Thy Name, and glorified is Thy kingdom..."; Ode 9: "For all the powers of the heavens praise Thee...". After the 3rd Ode, the Ypakoe: "Anticipating the dawn, Mary and the women found the stone rolled away from the tomb..." (Prolabousai ton orthron) is read plainly. After the 6th Ode, the Paschal Kontakion, Ikos, and Synaxarion Memorial of the day are recited; then the Resurrectional hymn: "Having beheld the Resurrection of Christ, let us worship the Holy Lord Jesus, the only sinless One..." (Anastasin Christou theasamenoi) is chanted three times (once by the Patriarch and twice by the concelebrating Hierarchs), followed by "Jesus having risen from the tomb, as He foretold, hath given us eternal life and great mercy" (Anastas ho Iesous apo tou taphou). Odes 7 and 8 follow; the Great Archdeacon exclaims: "The Theotokos and Mother of the Light let us magnify in hymns!", and the 9th Ode is chanted with the appointed Paschal Megalynaria.
