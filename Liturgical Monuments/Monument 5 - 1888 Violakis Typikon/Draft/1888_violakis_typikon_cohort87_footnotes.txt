[^490]: Service of the Akathist Hymn Concluded (§ Appendix 4). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p861 (printed page 846) and facing English leaf p862 (printed page 847). Concluding rubrics of the solemn Patriarchal Service of the Akathist Hymn: Following the Fourth Stasis and the entrance with the Divamvulon, the Great Ecclesiarch, Priests, and Deacons take their stations across from and beside the Patriarchal Throne. The Reader-Priest recites "To the Champion General" (*Τῇ ὑπερμάχῳ*), the Forty Lord Have Mercies, and the Prayer of the Canonical Hours: "Thou Who at all times and at every hour..." (*Ὁ ἐν παντὶ καιρῷ*). The Patriarch recites "May God be merciful unto us and bless us..." (*Ὁ Θεὸς οἰκτιρήσαι ἡμᾶς*), and the Canonarchs chant the Compline prayers: "O Spotless, Undefiled, Incorrupt..." (*Ἄσπιλε, ἀμόλυντε*) and "And grant unto us, O Master..." (*Καὶ δὸς ἡμῖν, Δέσποτα*). The Priest intones the Small Dismissal and the Fervent Supplication for the peace of the world (*Εὐξώμεθα ὑπὲρ εἰρήνης τοῦ κόσμου*). The Choirs sing the slow Kathisma in Tone 3: "Seeing the beauty of thy virginity..." (*Τὴν ὡραιότητα τῆς παρθενίας σου*), during which the Patriarch descends from the Throne, blesses the congregation, and departs, while the Priest concludes with the solemn Patriarchal Dismissal: "Through the prayers of our holy Master, Lord Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us. Amen." (*Δι' εὐχῶν τοῦ ἁγίου Δεσπότου ἡμῶν...*). [Printed Note: At the Dismissal, when the Patriarch himself concludes or prior to the priest's exclamation, the Patriarch proclaims: "Through the prayers of our Holy Fathers, Lord Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us. Amen."].

[^491]: Order of the Service of the Holy Passion in the Patriarchal Church: Procession, Vesting, and Entourage (§ Appendix 5). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p863 (printed page 848) and facing English leaf p864 (printed page 849). Patriarchal ceremonial for the Twelve Passion Gospels (*Τῶν Ἁγίων Παθῶν*) on Holy and Great Thursday evening: Bells peal at the Twelfth Hour [6:00 p.m.]. His All-Holiness the Ecumenical Patriarch descends with his entourage half an hour later. The procession is led by four staff-bearing ushers (*κλητῆρες ῥαβδοῦχοι*) proclaiming the Patriarch's arrival, followed by all parish clergy and the Great Archimandrite, and the Great Ecclesiarch bearing the pastoral staff (*πατριαρχικὴ ποιμαντικὴ ῥάβδος* / *Pateritsa*). The Patriarch walks bearing a plain, unadorned walking staff (*χαζράνιον* / *Chazranion*), flanked by the Great Protosynkellos on his right and the Great Archdeacon on his left, followed by the junior deacons. At the Great Gate of the church, the Patriarch is vested with the Mandyas by the Great Archdeacon, and the pastoral staff is ceremonially transferred to him by the Protosynkellos. The procession enters the nave led by the Great Primikirios carrying the large double-branched candlestick (*διβάμβουλον* / *Divamvulon*). In the center of the nave, the Patriarch blesses the congregation while the Choirs sing "For many years, Master!" (*Εἰς πολλὰ ἔτη, Δέσποτα*). [Printed Note: The *Chazranion* is a plain cane devoid of ornamentation, uniquely carried during the solemnity of Great and Holy Week].

[^492]: Order of the Service of the Holy Passion: Opening Rubrics, Hexapsalmos, and Troparion (§ Appendix 6). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p865 (printed page 850) and facing English leaf p866 (printed page 851). Sacerdotal action and introductory Matins order for the Twelve Gospels: The Patriarch ascends the Throne; the Great Archimandrite, Great Archdeacon, and Second Deacon receive Keron (*καιρόν*), followed by the junior deacons. The Great Archimandrite, vested in Epitrachilion at the Holy Table, intones the opening blessing: "Blessed is our God always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen." (*Εὐλογητὸς ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν*), and the Patriarch recites the Trisagion Prayers. The Reader-Priest reads the introductory Royal Psalms (Psalms 19 and 20 LXX), during which the Archimandrite, vested in Phelonion and preceded by a candle-bearer, censes the whole church using a hand censer (*Κατζί* / *Katzion*). Following the Trisagion exclamation and customary royal troparia, the Archimandrite intones the augmented petition and exclamation: "Glory to the holy, consubstantial, life-creating, and undivided Trinity, always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.", whereupon the Patriarch recites the Six Psalms (*Ἑξάψαλμος*). After the Great Litany, the Choirs chant "Alleluia" four times with the appointed verses from Isaiah 26:9, 11, 15 (LXX), followed by the Troparion in Tone 8: "When the glorious disciples were enlightened at the washing of the feet..." (*Ὅτε οἱ ἔνδοξοι μαθηταί*), sung twice slowly and once moderately fast (*ἀργοσυντόμως*). During this hymn, the concelebrating Hierarchs exit the Sanctuary led by the Great Ecclesiarch to take their stations, and the Archdeacon enters the Bema to vest. [Editorial Note: Violakis accidentally transposed the third and fourth verses of the Alleluia in the Greek text].

[^493]: Order of the Service of the Holy Passion: Gospels 1 and 2, Antiphons, and Kathismata (§ Appendix 7). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p867 (printed page 852) and facing English leaf p868 (printed page 853). Proclamation of the Passion Gospels and hierarchical rotations: The vested Deacons exit the Royal Doors holding Dikerotrikera and flank the Patriarchal Throne. As "When the glorious disciples..." is sung for the third time, the Patriarch descends, enters the Sanctuary, vests with Epitrachilion and Omophorion, and the Holy Gospel is placed upon the Triskelion before the Royal Doors. Following the liturgical dialogue intoned by the Great Archdeacon, the Patriarch proclaims the First Passion Gospel (John 13:31–18:1). After blessing the faithful with the Trikirion, the Patriarch unvests in the Bema and returns to his Throne. The Choirs sing the 15 Antiphons of Holy Friday Matins. During the Second Antiphon, the Great Ecclesiarch invites the senior Hierarch, who receives Keron from the Patriarch, vests in the Bema, and upon the conclusion of the Kathisma in Tone 7 ("While feeding the disciples at the Supper..."), reads the Second Passion Gospel (John 18:1–28) at the Triskelion. At each Gospel reading, the Choirs alternate singing rapidly: "Glory to Thy forbearance, O Lord, glory to Thee!" (*Δόξα τῇ μακροθυμίᾳ σου, Κύριε, δόξα σοι*).

[^494]: Order of the Service of the Holy Passion: Gospels 3–6, Antiphons 4–15, and the Procession of the Crucified (§ Appendix 8). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p869 (printed page 854) and facing English leaf p870 (printed page 855). Succession of Gospel readings and the dramatic Solemn Procession of the Crucified Cross: Officiating Hierarchs read successive Gospels in order of seniority; remaining Gospels are proclaimed by the Great Protosynkellos, Grand Chancellor-Secretary (*Ἀρχιγραμματεύς*), Great Archimandrite, and Synkellos. After Gospel 2, Choir 2 sings Antiphons 4–6 and Kathisma in Tone 7 ("What manner of way, O Judas..."), followed by Gospel 3 (Matt. 26:57–75). Choir 1 sings Antiphons 7–9 and Kathisma in Tone 8 ("Oh, how did Judas..."), followed by Gospel 4 (John 18:28–19:16). Choir 2 sings Antiphons 10–12 and Kathisma in Tone 8 ("When Thou, O God, stoodest before Caiaphas..."), followed by Gospel 5 (Matt. 27:3–32). Choir 1 sings Antiphons 13–15 through the Theotokion ("Rejoice, thou who through the Angel..."). The Great Archimandrite, vested in Epitrachilion and Phelonion, lifts the Crucifix (*Ἐσταυρωμένος*), circumambulates the Holy Table, and intones in a sorrowful recitative the Idiomelon in Tone 6 [Tone 2 Plagal]: "Today He Who suspended the earth upon the waters is suspended upon the Tree!" (*Σήμερον κρεμᾶται ἐπὶ ξύλου*). Preceded by the Primikirios, candle-bearers, and censing Deacons, he carries the Cross into the center of the Nave and erects it in its floor socket. Choir 1 repeats the Idiomelon, and Choir 2 sings the concluding Stichera of Antiphon 15 and Kathisma in Tone 4 ("Thou hast redeemed us from the curse of the Law..."), leading into Gospel 6 (Mark 15:16–32) and the Beatitudes. [Printed Note 1: Historical note by George Violakis noting that the Procession of the Crucified was introduced into the Patriarchal Church under Ecumenical Patriarch Sophronios IV (1864) and had not existed in earlier printed Triodia or ancient Typika, but was sanctioned to meet the intense piety of the faithful].
