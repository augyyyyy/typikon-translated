[^153]: Strict Fast Discipline, Equal Rank to Great Friday, and Concluding Liturgical Propers of the Universal Exaltation of the Precious Cross (Violakis, pp. 176–177, September 14, §7 concl., Rubrical Notes, and September Saturday after Exaltation, §8). The rubrical constitution of the Byzantine rite elevates the Feast of the Universal Exaltation of the Precious and Life-Creating Cross to an exceptional ascetical standing: it is explicitly designated as “bearing the equal rank of Holy and Great Friday” (*φερούσῃ τὰ ἴσα τῆς ἁγίας καὶ Μ. Παρασκευῆς*). Consequently, a strict total fast (*νηστεία*) is canonically enjoined upon the entire body of the Church on September 14 regardless of the day of the week on which it falls, excluding all flesh-meat, dairy products, eggs, and fish. In accordance with Apostolic Canon 64 and the ancient canonical tradition that forbids strict fasting (xerophagy) on the Sabbath or the Lord's Day, an ascetical relaxation or dispensation (*κατάλυσις*) for wine and olive oil is permitted only when September 14 concurs with a Saturday or a Sunday. At Vespers on the evening of the Feast, the Great Prokeimenon *Ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν ἐν τῷ οὐρανῷ καὶ ἐν τῇ γῇ* (“Our God is in heaven and on earth: all things whatsoever He willed, He hath done”, Psalm 113:11 LXX) is solemnly chanted with its verses, an invariable mark of Great Feasts of the Lord. The concluding propers of the Divine Liturgy (§7 concl.) mirror the high festal dignity of the Cross: The proper Festal Isodikon (*Ὑψοῦτε Κύριον τὸν Θεὸν ἡμῶν*, Psalm 98:5 LXX) incorporates the proper christological refrain *Σῶσον ἡμᾶς, Υἱὲ Θεοῦ, ὁ σαρκὶ σταυρωθείς*; the Trisagion is replaced by the hymn of solemn adoration *Τὸν Σταυρόν σου προσκυνοῦμεν, Δέσποτα, καὶ τὴν ἁγίαν σου Ἀνάστασιν δοξάζομεν* (“We venerate Thy Cross, O Master, and we glorify Thy holy Resurrection”); the festive Epistle pericope (1 Corinthians 1:18–24: “For the preaching of the cross is to them that perish foolishness; but unto us which are saved it is the power of God”) and Gospel reading (John 19:6–11, 13–20, 25–28, 30–35, the solemn Passion account of the Crucifixion) bear witness to the salvific triumph of Golgotha; the festal Megalynarion [Zadostoinyk] is the Heirmos of Ode 9 of the First Canon of the Cross: *Μυστικὸς εἶ, Θεοτόκε, παράδεισος* (“O Theotokos, Thou art a mystical paradise”, Tone 8); and the Communion Verse is Psalm 4:7 LXX (*Ἐσημειώθη ἐφ’ ἡμᾶς τὸ φῶς τοῦ προσώπου σου, Κύριε*). At §8, the order for the Saturday after the Exaltation (*Σαββάτῳ μετὰ τὴν Ὕψωσιν*) establishes the post-festal weekday structure: the Canons of the Feast and of the occurring Saint are recited in direct sequence (*χῦμα*), the Magnificat (*Τὴν τιμιωτέραν*) is chanted, the festal Antiphons and Entrance Hymn are maintained at the Divine Liturgy, and the appointed scriptural readings are 1 Corinthians 1:26–29 and John 8:21–30.

[^154]: Sunday after the Universal Exaltation of the Precious Cross and Concurrence with the Great-Martyr Euphemia (Violakis, pp. 178–179, September Sunday after Exaltation, §§9–11, and September 16, §§12–14). The Sunday after the Exaltation (*Κυριακὴ μετὰ τὴν Ὕψωσιν*, §§9–11) holds a primary structural position within the autumnal post-festal cycle of the Byzantine Menologion. While the ordinary Sunday Resurrectional order reasserts its primacy over the liturgical structure (governing the Tone of the week, the Resurrection Stichira, the Sunday Evlogitaria, the Sunday Morning Gospel, and the Great Doxology dismissal troparion *Σήμερον σωτηρία*), the post-festal presence of the Holy Cross permeates every service. At Saturday Great Vespers (§9), 6 Resurrection Stichira are joined to 4 post-festal Stichira of the Cross, crowned by the festal Doxastikon. At Sunday Matins (§10), both the Resurrection Canon and the Canon of the Cross are chanted, accompanied by the Katavasiai *Σταυρὸν χαράξας*. At the Divine Liturgy (§11), the Beatitudes combine four of the Tone with four from Ode 6 of the Canon of the Feast, and the scriptural pericopes provide the theological core of the post-festal season: the Epistle pericope (Galatians 2:16–20: “I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me”) and the Gospel pericope (Mark 8:34–9:1: “Whosoever will come after Me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow Me”). At §§12–14, the Typikon addresses the concurrence of the Holy Glorious Great-Martyr and All-Praised Euphemia (September 16). Although Saint Euphemia was martyred under Diocletian at Chalcedon (A.D. 304), her liturgical office is especially renowned for the Miracle of the Fourth Ecumenical Council of Chalcedon (A.D. 451), commemorated on July 11, in which the rival Tomes of the Orthodox and the Monophysites were placed in her reliquary, and upon reopening, the Orthodox Tome of Pope Saint Leo was found held in her right hand while the heretical Tome was cast at her feet. When her feast falls on Sunday within the afterfeast of the Cross (§§13–14), the ten Stichira at “Lord, I have cried” are partitioned into 4 Resurrection, 3 post-festal of the Cross, and 3 of the Saint, with the Doxastikon assigned to Saint Euphemia (*Ἡ διηνθισμένη ταῖς ἀρεταῖς*) and “Both now” to the First Dogmatic Theotokion of the Tone; at Matins, three canons are chanted (Resurrection, Cross, and Saint Euphemia), and the Kathisma of the Saint is sung after Ode 3.

[^155]: Sunday Concurrence with the Holy Great-Martyr Eustathius and the Architecture of Post-Festal Weekday/Sunday Offices (Violakis, pp. 180–181, September 16 concl., §15, and September 20, §16). Section 15 concludes the Divine Liturgy for the Sunday concurrence of Saint Euphemia within the afterfeast: the Beatitudes combine 4 of the Tone with 4 from Ode 6 of the Canon of Saint Euphemia; following the Little Entrance, the dismissal troparia are sung in sequence (Resurrection, Feast of the Cross, Saint Euphemia, and the Patron Saint of the local temple), followed by the festal Kontakion *Ὁ ὑψωθεὶς ἐν τῷ Σταυρῷ*, while the Epistle and Gospel remain those of the Sunday after the Exaltation. Section 16 governs the feast of the Holy Great-Martyr Eustathius (Placidus), his wife Theopiste, and their sons Agapius and Theopistus (September 20), who suffered martyrdom by being enclosed in a heated bronze bull under Emperor Hadrian (c. A.D. 118). Falling on the penultimate day of the post-festal period of the Exaltation, the concurrence of Saint Eustathius on Sunday provides a canonical template for integrating a Polyeleos-rank Saint within an Afterfeast: at Saturday Great Vespers, the Stichira at “Lord, I have cried” are apportioned 4 of the Resurrection, 3 of the Afterfeast of the Cross, and 3 of Saint Eustathius; at the Aposticha, the Doxastikon is assigned to the Saint, followed by “Both now” and the post-festal Doxastikon of the Cross; the dismissal troparia consist of the Resurrection Troparion, that of Saint Eustathius (*Οἱ μάρτυρές σου, Κύριε*), and that of the Feast (*Σῶσον, Κύριε, τὸν λαόν σου*). At Matins, the three canons are combined (Resurrection, Cross, and Saint Eustathius), with the Kathisma of the Saint sung after Ode 3; at the Divine Liturgy, the troparia of the Resurrection, the Cross, Saint Eustathius, and the temple Patron Saint are chanted after the Little Entrance, crowned by the festal Kontakion *Ὁ ὑψωθεὶς ἐν τῷ Σταυρῷ*, while the Epistle and Gospel remain those of the Sunday after the Exaltation.

[^156]: The Apodosis (Leave-Taking) of the Universal Exaltation of the Precious Cross and the Autumnal Katavasia Transition (Violakis, pp. 182–183, September 21, §§17–20, and Rubrical Note). The Apodosis (*Ἀπόδοσις*) of the Universal Exaltation of the Precious Cross on September 21 (§§17–20) brings the eight-day post-festal cycle to its liturgical consummation. When falling on a weekday (§17), the service replicates the principal day of the feast in all its hymnography, with the rubrical omission of the Old Testament readings at Vespers and the festal additions at Matins (the Liti, the Matins Gospel, and the Polyeleos); at the Divine Liturgy, the daily Epistle and Gospel are read. When the Apodosis coincides with Sunday (§§18–20), the Sunday Resurrectional office and the festal Apodosis merge: Great Vespers (§18) appoints 4 Resurrection and 6 Festal Stichira at “Lord, I have cried”, while the Aposticha assigns both “Glory” and “Both now” to the Feast. At Sunday Matins (§19), the Resurrection Dismissal Troparion is chanted once and the Festal Troparion twice; the Resurrection Canon is combined with the Canon of the Cross, culminating in the solemn chanting of the Ninth Ode of the Feast with both of its Katavasiai (*Μυστικὸς εἶ, Θεοτόκε* and *Ὁ διὰ βρώσεως τοῦ ξύλου*). At the Divine Liturgy (§20), the proper Festal Antiphons are retained: at Antiphon 2, the refrain is *Ὁ σαρκὶ σταυρωθείς*; at the Little Entrance, the festal Isodikon combines the festal verse with the Sunday refrain *Ὁ ἀναστὰς ἐκ νεκρῶν*; and in place of *Axion Estin*, the festal Zadostoinyk is chanted: *Ὁ διὰ βρώσεως τοῦ ξύλου* (the Heirmos of Ode 9 of the Second Canon of the Cross by St. John of Damascus). The concluding rubrical note defines the structural boundary of the Menologion: beginning on the first Sunday after the Apodosis of the Cross, the appointed Katavasiai transition to the Marian canon *Ἀνοίξω τὸ στόμα μου* (“I shall open my mouth”, Tone 4) through November 21 (the Feast of the Entry of the Most Holy Theotokos into the Temple), while the seasonal Kontakion becomes *Προστασία τῶν Χριστιανῶν ἀκαταίσχυντε* (“O Protection of Christians that cannot be put to shame”, Tone 2) through November 8 (the Synaxis of the Archangel Michael and the Bodiless Hosts).

[^157]: The Conception of the Honorable Forerunner, Text-Critical Rectification of Stichira Count, and the Theotokos of Kythera with St. Thecla (Violakis, pp. 184–185, September 23, §§1–3, and September 24, §1). September 23 marks the Feast of the Conception of the Honorable and Glorious Prophet, Forerunner, and Baptist John (*Ἡ Σύλληψις τοῦ τιμίου Προδρόμου*, §§1–3), which celebrates the supernatural intervention of God in the conception of John by the elderly and barren Elizabeth following the angelic annunciation to the Priest Zacharias in the Holy of Holies (Luke 1:5–25). When this feast concurs with Sunday (§§2–3), Great Vespers appoints ten Stichira at “Lord, I have cried”. A significant text-critical divergence exists between the editio princeps of the Greek text and its original English facing page: the Greek text definitively specifies six Resurrection Stichira and four of the Forerunner (`Ἀναστάσιμα ς΄ καὶ τοῦ Προδρόμου δ΄`), completing the requisite decuple; the English translation facing leaf in the 1888 edition erroneously recorded “3 Stichira of the Forerunner”, which would have left the decuple incomplete with only nine Stichira. This critical edition rectifies the erratum in conformity with the primary Greek witness and the authoritative Constantinopolitan Menaion. At Matins (§3), the Kathisma *Θυμιῶντι ἐν Ναῷ* (“As Zacharias was offering incense in the temple”) and the Theotokion *Κατεπλάγη Ἰωσήφ* (“Joseph was struck with wonder”) follow Ode 3; at the Divine Liturgy, the Epistle is assigned to the Forerunner (Galatians 4:22–31, allegorizing the barren Sarah and the child of promise Isaac), while the Gospel is that of the Sunday. Section 1 of September 24 details the concurrence on Sunday of the Wonderworking Icon of the Most Holy Theotokos of Kythera (*Παναγία ἡ Μυρτιδιώτισσα*, “Our Lady of the Myrtle Tree”, commemorating the miraculous discovery of the holy icon amidst myrtle branches on the Aegean island of Kythera in the 14th century and the healing of a paralytic) with the Holy Protomartyr and Equal-of-the-Apostles Thecla of Iconium (first century), spiritual daughter and disciple of the Holy Apostle Paul.
