[^158]: The Apolytikia of Saint Thecla, Patriarchal Compositions (Cyril VI and Constantius I), and Folio Continuity (Violakis, pp. 186–187, September 24, §§2–3 concl., and Troparia). September 24 concludes the combined commemoration of the Wonderworking Icon of the Most Holy Theotokos Myrtidiotissa of Kythera (*Παναγία ἡ Μυρτιδιώτισσα*) and the Holy Protomartyr and Equal-to-the-Apostles Thecla of Iconium. The liturgical rubrics at §2 (Sunday Matins) appoint the Dismissal Troparion of the Theotokos (*Τῆς Μυρτιδιωτίσσης*, Tone 4) followed by that of Saint Thecla. At §3, the Divine Liturgy appoints the Sunday Epistle and the Gospel for the Theotokos (Luke 10:38–42; 11:27–28). When the feast falls on a weekday, the entire service of the Menaion is sung. Following the rubrics, the Typikon incorporates two historically significant proper Dismissal Troparia for Saint Thecla composed or retrieved by two 19th-century Ecumenical Patriarchs of Constantinople: First, the Dismissal Troparion in Tone 3, *Θείου κήρυκος διδασκαλίας* ("Inflamed with desire for Thy Creator through the teaching of the divine preacher"), composed in 1813 by Patriarch Cyril VI (reigned 1813–1818; martyred in Adrianople at the outbreak of the Greek War of Independence in 1821; glorified as a hieromartyr). Second, an alternative Dismissal Troparion in Tone 2, *Ὡς τῷ Παύλῳ μαθητευθεῖσα* ("Having been taught by Paul, O all-praised one"), transcribed in 1832 by Patriarch Constantius I of Sinai (reigned 1830–1834; renowned scholar, hymnographer, and former Archbishop of Sinai) from an ancient parchment manuscript (*εὑρεθὲν ἐν μεμβράνῃ*). In the printed 1888 edition, the introductory rubric for Constantius I's troparion appears at the foot of printed page 186 (leaf p201 / English facing leaf p202), while the troparion text itself continues across the leaf boundary onto the top of printed page 188 (leaf p203 / English facing leaf p204).

[^159]: Feast of the Metastasis of Saint John the Theologian, Ceremonial Standing for Paremiae, and Folio Transition (Violakis, pp. 188–189, September 26, §§1–3 incipit). September 26 marks the solemn commemoration of the Falling Asleep (Metastasis) of the Holy, Glorious Apostle and Evangelist John the Theologian (*Ἡ μετάστασις τοῦ ἁγίου ἐνδόξου Ἀποστόλου καὶ Εὐαγγελιστοῦ Ἰωάννου τοῦ Θεολόγου*). At Great Vespers (§1), following the First Stasis of the First Kathisma of the Psalter (Psalms 1–3 LXX), three Prosomia are sung at "Lord, I have cried" doubled unto 6, followed by the Entrance with the censer (*Φῶς ἱλαρόν*) and the daily Prokeimenon. The three festal Old Testament readings (Paremiae) are unique in the Byzantine liturgical corpus: rather than being drawn from the Old Testament prophets or wisdom literature, they are excerpted from the New Testament Catholic Epistles of Saint John himself (1 John 3:21–4:6; 1 John 4:11–16; and 1 John 4:20–5:5). Because these readings derive from apostolic canonical Scripture, the Great Church of Constantinople prescribes an exceptional ceremonial rubric: *(καθ’ ἅ ἡμεῖς ἱστάμεθα, ὁ δὲ Διάκονος ἐκφωνεῖ Πρόσχωμεν, Σοφία, Πρόσχωμεν)* ("during which we stand upright, as the Deacon exclaims: Let us be attentive! Wisdom! Let us be attentive!"). This elevates the Paremiae to a liturgical honor normally reserved exclusively for the Holy Gospel and apostolic lections. At Matins (§2), the order includes the Liti, the Polyeleos, the First Antiphon of the Anavathmoi of Tone 4 (*Ἐκ νεότητός μου*), the festal Prokeimenon (Psalm 18:5 LXX: *Εἰς πᾶσαν τὴν γῆν*), and the Eleventh Morning Gospel (John 21:15–25, detailing Christ's dialogue with Peter regarding the beloved disciple). [^160]: Sunday of the Holy Fathers of the Seventh Ecumenical Council (Nicaea II) and Syntactical Leaf Continuation (Violakis, pp. 192–193, October 11–17, §§1–3 incipit). The service of the Holy God-bearing Fathers of the Seventh Ecumenical Council (held in Nicaea in A.D. 787 under Empress Irene and Patriarch Tarasius to anathematize iconoclasm and define the Orthodox veneration of holy icons) is celebrated annually on the Sunday falling between October 11 and 17 inclusive. At Saturday Great Vespers (§1), 6 Resurrection Stichira are joined to 4 Stichira of the Holy Fathers; the Old Testament Paremiae comprise three classic conciliar readings: Genesis 14:14–20 (Melchizedek blessing Abraham), Deuteronomy 1:8–11, 15–17 (Moses establishing wise and righteous judges over the tribes), and Deuteronomy 10:14–21 (Moses recounting the majesty and commandments of the Lord). At Sunday Matins (§2), following the Resurrection Canons, the Canon of the Fathers is sung, culminating in the Katavasiai *Ἀνοίξω τὸ στόμα μου*. At the Divine Liturgy (§3), the Beatitudes combine 4 of the Tone with 4 from Ode 6 of the Canon of the Fathers; the appointed Epistle reading is Titus 3:8–15 (*Τέκνον Τίτε, πιστὸς ὁ λόγος*), exhorting the clergy to maintain good works and avoid foolish questions, genealogies, and strivings about the law; and the Gospel reading is Luke 8:5–15 (the Parable of the Sower, appointed for the Fourth Sunday of Luke). [^161]: The Tripartite Annual Commemoration of the Ecumenical Councils in the Constantinopolitan Typikon (Violakis, pp. 194–195, October 11–17, Concluding Rubrical Note). Appended to the rubric of the Seventh Ecumenical Council on printed page 194 is a fundamental didascalic note (*Σημείωσις*) summarizing the three annual corporate celebrations dedicated to the Holy Fathers of the Ecumenical Councils in the Byzantine liturgical year: (1) The First Ecumenical Council (Nicaea I, A.D. 325, which formulated the initial clauses of the Nicene Creed and anathematized Arius), commemorated on the Seventh Sunday of Pascha (the Sunday after the Ascension of our Lord), with the appointed Gospel pericope from Saint John (John 17:1–13, the High Priestly Prayer of Christ for the unity of the Church); (2) The Fourth Ecumenical Council (Chalcedon, A.D. 451, which confessed the two unconfused and undivided natures of Christ, celebrated jointly with the First Six Ecumenical Councils), commemorated on the Sunday falling between July 13 and 19 inclusive, with the appointed Gospel pericope from Saint Matthew (Matthew 5:14–19, "Ye are the light of the world... Whosoever shall do and teach them, the same shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven"); and (3) The Seventh Ecumenical Council (Nicaea II, A.D. 787, which restored the veneration of the holy icons), commemorated on the Sunday falling between October 11 and 17 inclusive, with the appointed Gospel pericope from Saint Luke (Luke 8:5–15, the Parable of the Sower, from the Fourth Sunday of Luke). This note provides liturgical coordinators and cantors with an authoritative comparative key to the three major patristic celebrations that anchor the movable and fixed cycles of the Constantinopolitan typikon.

[^162]: Feast of the Holy Apostle and Evangelist Luke, Sunday Concurrence, and Inter-Cohort Transition to Cohort 22 (Violakis, pp. 194–195, October 18, §§1–3 incipit). October 18 commemorates the Holy Apostle and Evangelist Luke, companion of Saint Paul and author of the Third Gospel and the Acts of the Apostles. When the feast occurs on a weekday (§1), the order of the Menaion is sung plain. When the feast concurs with Sunday (§§2–3), Great Vespers on Saturday evening appoints 6 Resurrection Stichira and 4 Stichira of the Evangelist at "Lord, I have cried", followed by the Entrance and the daily Prokeimenon; at the Aposticha, the Doxastikon is assigned to the Evangelist (*Πάνσοφε ἁλιεῦ*) and the Dismissal Troparion is *Ἀπόστολε ἅγιε καὶ εὐαγγελιστὰ Λουκᾶ*. At Sunday Matins (§3), the order combines the Resurrection Kathismata and the 17th Kathisma (Psalm 118 LXX, the Amomos), followed by the Canons (Resurrection and Saint Luke) and the Katavasiai *Ἀνοίξω τὸ στόμα μου*.
