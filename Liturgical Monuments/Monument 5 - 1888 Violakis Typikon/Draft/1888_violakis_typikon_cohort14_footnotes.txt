[^117]: The Conclusion of the Daily Midnight Office (*Mesonyktikon*), the Litany of Peace, Mutual Forgiveness, and the Royal Enarxis of Matins (Violakis, pp. 116–117, §2 cont.). This section details the ritual bridge connecting the nocturnal Midnight Office (*Μεσονυκτικόν*) to the morning service of Matins (*Ὄρθρος*). In classical Byzantine horological practice, the Midnight Office culminates in the solemn penitential Litany of Supplication (*Ἐλέησον ἡμᾶς ὁ Θεός...*), followed by the sacerdotal prayer: **"Hear us, O God our Savior, the hope of all the ends of the earth..."** (*Ἐπάκουσον ἡμῶν ὁ Θεὸς ὁ Σωτὴρ ἡμῶν*), and the preliminary Dismissal. The Priest then descends one step from the Sanctuary (*Bema*) toward the solea to intone the ancient Litany for Peace: **"Let us pray for the peace of the world"** (*Εὐξώμεθα ὑπὲρ εἰρήνης τοῦ κόσμου*), during which the choirs chant **"Lord, have mercy"** continuously without pause through all petitions. Upon concluding with the triple Kyrie eleison (*"Let us also say for ourselves: Lord, have mercy [thrice]"*) and the universal seal *Di' euchōn*, the clergy and people exchange the ancient mutual Rite of Forgiveness (*συγχώρησις*, Slavonic *Proshchenie*). Having received mutual absolution from the community, the Priest re-enters the Sanctuary, takes his station before the Holy Table, and inaugurates the Royal Beginning (*Βασιλικὴ Ἔναρξις*) of Matins: **"Blessed is our God, always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages"**, followed by the Trisagion Prayers, the Royal Troparia (*Σῶσον Κύριε*, *Ὁ ὑψωθεὶς ἐν τῷ Σταυρῷ*, *Προστασία φοβερά*), and the Royal Litany. Following the choir's response **"In the Name of the Lord, Father, bless"** (*Ἐν ὀνόματι Κυρίου εὐλόγησον, Πάτερ*), the Priest intones the great Trinitarian doxology: **"Glory to the Holy, consubstantial, life-creating, and undivided Trinity..."** (*Δόξα τῇ Ἁγίᾳ καὶ ὁμοουσίῳ καὶ ζωοποιῷ καὶ ἀδιαιρέτῳ Τριάδι...*), which directly introduces the Six Psalms (*Ἑξάψαλμος*, Psalms 3, 37, 62, 87, 102, 142 LXX).

[^118]: Weekday Matins Canon Scheme, Scriptural Refrains (*Stichologia*), and the Magnificat (Violakis, pp. 116–119, §2 cont.). This paragraph delineates the complete hymnographic progression of weekday Matins outside festal periods. Following the Psalter kathisma of the day and Psalm 50 LXX, the Canons from the Parakletike and the Menaion are chanted with specific scriptural and intercessory refrains (*stichoi*): (1) For the penitential troparia (*Katanyktika*) of the primary Canon: **"Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee"** (*Δόξα σοι ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν, δόξα σοι*); (2) For the martyric troparia (*Martyrika*): **"Holy martyrs of God, intercede for us"** (*Ἅγιοι τοῦ Θεοῦ πρεσβεύσατε ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν*); (3) For the second Canon of the Parakletike, according to the weekday angelic and saintly dedication: Monday (Archangels: *Ἀρχάγγελοι τοῦ Θεοῦ*), Tuesday (the Forerunner: *Προφῆτα τοῦ Χριστοῦ*), Wednesday and Friday (the Precious Cross: *Ὑπεραγία Θεοτόκε σῶσον ἡμᾶς*), Thursday (Apostles and St. Nicholas: *Ἀπόστολοι τοῦ Χριστοῦ* and *Ἅγιε τοῦ Θεοῦ*), and Saturday (Martyrs and the Departed, utilizing the psalm verses Psalm 24:13 LXX: *Αἱ ψυχαὶ αὐτῶν ἐν ἀγαθοῖς αὐλισθήσονται* and Psalm 64:5 LXX: *Μακάριοι οὓς ἐξελέξω καὶ προσελάβου, Κύριε*); (4) For the Canon of the Saint from the Menaion: **"Holy saint of God, intercede for us"** (*Ἅγιε τοῦ Θεοῦ πρέσβευε ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν*). Structural landmarks within the Canon include: the Heirmos and Kathisma of the Saint after Ode 3; the Heirmos, Kontakion of the day, and Menologion [Synaxarion] after Ode 6; the biblical verse **"We praise, we bless, and we worship the Lord"** (*Αἰνοῦμεν, εὐλογοῦμεν καὶ προσκυνοῦμεν τὸν Κύριον*) before the Heirmos of Ode 8; the Magnificat (**"More honorable than the Cherubim"**, *Τὴν τιμιωτέραν*) with the biblical Song of the Theotokos (Luke 1:46–55); and the Heirmos of Ode 9 from the Menaion. Following the weekday Exapostilaria, the service moves into the Lesser Doxology (*Σοὶ δόξα πρέπει*), the Morning Litany of Supplication (*Πληρώσωμεν τὴν ἑωθινήν*), the Parakletike Aposticha with weekday psalm verses (Psalm 89:14 and 89:17 LXX), **"It is good to give praise unto the Lord"** (Psalm 91:2 LXX), the Trisagion Prayers, the Dismissal Theotokion, and the immediate transition into the Divine Liturgy.

[^119]: The Daily Antiphon System of the Byzantine Divine Liturgy and Scriptural Versification (Violakis, pp. 118–121, §3). Paragraph 3 establishes the authoritative Constantinopolitan standard for the three Daily Antiphons (*καθημερινὰ Ἀντίφωνα*) of the Divine Liturgy on ordinary weekdays throughout the ecclesiastical year: (1) Antiphon One is drawn from Psalm 91 LXX (*Ἀγαθὸν τὸ ἐξομολογεῖσθαι*, Psalm 92 MT), celebrating divine benevolence and constant faithfulness, paired with the universal Marian refrain: **"Through the intercessions of the Theotokos, Savior, save us!"** (*Ταῖς πρεσβείαις τῆς Θεοτόκου, Σῶτερ, σῶσον ἡμᾶς*). (2) Antiphon Two is drawn from Psalm 92 LXX (*Ὁ Κύριος ἐβασίλευσεν*, Psalm 93 MT), celebrating the cosmic sovereignty of God and the beauty of His sanctuary, paired with the refrain: **"Through the prayers of Thy Saints, save us, O Lord!"** (*Πρεσβείαις τῶν Ἁγίων σου, σῶσον ἡμᾶς, Κύριε*), and concluding with the 6th-century Christological hymn of Emperor Justinian: **"O Only-begotten Son and Word of God"** (*Ὁ μονογενὴς Υἱὸς καὶ Λόγος τοῦ Θεοῦ*). (3) Antiphon Three is drawn from Psalm 94 LXX (*Δεῦτε ἀγαλλιασώμεθα*, Psalm 95 MT), the great invitatory psalm of adoration, paired with the weekday refrain: **"Save us, O Son of God, Who art wonderful in Thy Saints, who sing to Thee: Alleluia!"** (*Σῶσον ἡμᾶς Υἱὲ Θεοῦ, ὁ ἐν Ἁγίοις θαυμαστός, ψάλλοντάς σοι Ἀλληλούϊα*). At the Little Entrance, this refrain directly provides the text for the Entrance Hymn (*Εἰσοδικόν*): **"O come, let us worship and fall down before Christ! Save us, O Son of God, Who art wonderful in Thy Saints..."** Note on Septuagint versification: Under MTS-1, all psalm citations strictly reflect Septuagint (LXX) chapter and verse numbering, avoiding the Masoretic divergence found in modern Western printings (e.g. RSV, KJV).

[^120]: The Weekday Kontakion Rotation and the Universal Marian Alternative (*Prostasia*) (Violakis, pp. 122–123, §3 & Note 2). This rubric governs the post-Entrance dismissal hymnody at the weekday Divine Liturgy. Following the Little Entrance, the choir chants: (1) The Dismissal Troparion of the Saint of the day; (2) The Dismissal Troparion of the Patron Saint of the Church temple; and (3) The Kontakion of the day of the week, following the classical Byzantine weekly cycle: Monday (Bodiless Powers: *Ἀρχιστράτηγοι Θεοῦ*, Tone 2); Tuesday (the Forerunner: *Προφῆτα Θεοῦ καὶ πρόδρομε*, Tone 2); Wednesday and Friday (the Precious Cross: *Ὁ ὑψωθεὶς ἐν τῷ Σταυρῷ*, Tone 4); Thursday (the Holy Apostles: *Τοὺς ἀσφαλεῖς καὶ θεοφθόγγους*, Tone 2); Saturday (All Saints and the Faithful Departed: *Ὡς ἀπαρχὰς τῆς φύσεως*, Tone 8). In Note 2, Violakis records an important customary divergence prevalent in 19th-century Greek parish practice: *"Some chant on every day the Kontakion: Protection of Christians"* (*Τινὲς ψάλλουσι καθ' ἑκάστην Κοντάκιον Προστασία τῶν Χριστιανῶν*, Tone 2: **"O Protection of Christians unashamed, Mediatrix before the Creator unfailing..."**). While monastic typika preserve the strict weekly rota of patristic Kontakia, modern parish use frequently substitutes *Prostasia tōn Christianōn* as a daily general Marian Kontakion on weekdays lacking a proper festal Kontakion.

[^121]: The Typika and First Hour Synthesis when the Divine Liturgy is Omitted (Violakis, pp. 122–123, §3 cont.). Paragraph 3 outlines the liturgical adaptation required whenever the Divine Liturgy does not follow weekday Matins—a common occurrence in non-monastic parish churches where daily Eucharistic celebration was not customary. Instead of an abrupt dismissal, the service seamlessly integrates the First Hour (*Α΄ Ὥρα*) and elements of the Reader's Typika (*Τυπικά*, Slavonic *Izobrazitel'ny*): immediately after the reading of **"It is good to give praise unto the Lord"** (*Ἀγαθὸν τὸ ἐξομολογεῖσθαι*), the Trisagion Prayers are recited, followed by the Dismissal Troparion of the Saint; the First Hour is conjoined directly via the triple invitatory **"O come, let us worship..."**; after the First Hour, the liturgical scriptural lections appointed for the day—the Epistle [Apostolos] and Gospel—are proclaimed; the Priest then intones the Litany of Supplication (*Ἐλέησον ἡμᾶς ὁ Θεός*), the ecphonesis, the ancient dialogue **"Wisdom! ... He Who is, blessed is Christ our God..."**, the choir's petition **"Establish, O Lord God, the holy Orthodox Faith..."**, and the Priest's Great Dismissal with *Di' euchōn*. This ensures that the biblical and liturgical cycle of the daily lectionary is proclaimed even in the absence of the Eucharistic synaxis.

[^122]: Typikon of Forefeasts (*Proeortia*) and Afterfeasts (*Metheorta*) with a Celebrated Saint: Vesperal and Matins Synthesis (Violakis, pp. 122–125, §4). Paragraph 4 codifies the precise combinatory mechanics applied when a day of preparation (*Προεόρτιον*) or afterfeast (*Μεθέορτον*) of a Dominical or Marian Feast coincides with the commemoration of a Saint possessing proper Doxastika (*ἔχων Δοξαστικά*) in the Menaion. At Vespers, six Stichira are chanted at *Lord, I have cried*: three of the season (Forefeast or Afterfeast) and three of the Saint; the Saint's hymnographic dignity is prioritized at *Doxa*, while the festal season is retained at *Kai nyn*. The same balanced synthesis occurs at the Aposticha. A crucial canonical distinction is codified regarding the sacerdotal Dismissal (*Ἀπόλυσις*): On Afterfeast days, the Priest must pronounce the distinctive festal dismissal incipit characteristic of the feast (e.g. at the Transfiguration: **"May He Who was transfigured in glory before His holy disciples and apostles upon Mount Tabor, Christ our true God..."**); on Forefeast days, however, the Priest is strictly forbidden from using the festal incipit, retaining instead the standard daily dismissal formula, because the historical saving event commemorated by the feast has not yet arrived in liturgical time.

[^123]: Eucharistic Antiphons and Entrance Refrains during Afterfeasts: Festal vs. Daily Antiphon Practice (Violakis, pp. 124–125, §5 & Note 3). Paragraph 5 and Footnote 3 address the Eucharistic psalmody and sanctuary movement on Afterfeasts of Dominical and Marian Feasts. Violakis prescribes that on days of Afterfeasts, the Divine Liturgy appoints the proper festal Antiphons of the feast with their respective refrains, and the Little Entrance hymn (*Eisodikon*) incorporates the feast's characteristic refrain (e.g. **"Save us, O Son of God, Who wast baptized in the Jordan by John..."** or **"Who wast transfigured on Mount Tabor..."**). Footnote 3 documents an authentic liturgical variant: *"Some chant the daily Antiphons even on Afterfeast days"* (*Τινὲς καὶ εἰς τὰς μεθεόρτους ἡμέρας ψάλλουσι τὰ καθημερινὰ Ἀντίφωνα*). In earlier monastic typika (and throughout the traditional Ruthenian and Slavic recensions), festal antiphons are strictly reserved for the day of the Feast itself and its Apodosis, while ordinary afterfeast weekdays employ either the Daily Antiphons (Psalms 91, 92, 94 LXX) or the Beatitudes / Typika with troparia from the Canons. Violakis records both practices, demonstrating the coexistence of the older monastic use with the 19th-century Constantinopolitan parochial simplification.
