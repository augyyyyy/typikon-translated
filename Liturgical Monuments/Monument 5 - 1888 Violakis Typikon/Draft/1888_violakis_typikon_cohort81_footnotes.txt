Concluding rubrics of Paschal Matins: At the Ninth Ode of the Paschal Canon of Saint John of Damascus in Tone 1, the appointed Megalynaria are chanted with their Troparia: 1. "Magnify, O my soul, Him Who suffered willingly, and was buried, and arose from the grave on the third day!" (Megalynon psyche mou ton ethelousios pathonta) with the Heirmos: "Shine, shine, O new Jerusalem, for the glory of the Lord is risen upon thee! Dance now and be glad, O Zion, and do thou, O pure Theotokos, rejoice in the Resurrection of Him Whom thou didst bear!" (Photizou, photizou he nea Ierousalem); 2. "Magnify, O my soul, Christ the Giver of life, Who arose from the grave on the third day!" (Megalynon psyche mou ton exanastanta trihemeron ek taphou Christon ton zoodoten) with the same Heirmos repeated; 3. "Christ is the new Pascha, the living sacrificial Victim, the Lamb of God Who taketh away the sin of the world!" (Christos to kainon Pascha) with the Troparion: "O Thy divine, O Thy beloved, O Thy sweetest voice!..." (O theias, o philes); 4. "Today the whole creation is glad and does rejoice, for Christ has risen, and Hades has been despoiled!" (Semeron pasa ktisis agalletai kai chairei) with the same Troparion repeated; "Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit": "Magnify, O my soul, the dominion of the Undivided Godhead of Three Hypostases!" (Megalynon psyche mou, tes Trisypostatou kai adiairetou Theotetos to kratos) with the Troparion: "O great and most sacred Pascha, Christ! O Wisdom, and Word of God, and Power!..." (O Pascha to Mega kai hierotaton, Christe); "Both now and ever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen": "Rejoice, O Virgin, rejoice; rejoice, O blessed one; rejoice, O most glorified one, for thy Son has arisen from the grave on the third day!" (Chaire Parthene, chaire, chaire, eulogemene) with the same Troparion repeated. The Katavasia is chanted: "The Angel cried unto her who is full of grace: O pure Virgin, rejoice, and again I say rejoice; for thy Son has arisen from the grave on the third day. Shine, shine, O new Jerusalem..." (Ho Angelos eboa te Kecharitomene). The Little Litany [Petition] is recited, and the Exaposteilarion in Tone 2: "Having slept in the flesh as a mortal, O King and Lord, on the third day Thou didst rise again, raising up Adam from corruption and abolishing death: O Pascha of incorruption, salvation of the world!" (Sarki hypnosas hos thnetos) is sung three times. At the Praises, eight stichoi are set, chanting the four Resurrectional Stichera in Tone 1 ("We hymn Thy saving Passion, O Christ...", "Thou Who didst endure the Cross...", "Thou Who didst despoil Hades...", "Glorifying Thy divine condescension...") and the four Stichera of Pascha in Tone 5 with their verses of Psalm 67 LXX ("Let God arise...", "A sacred Pascha is shown forth unto us today...", "As smoke vanisheth...", "Come from that vision, O women...", "So let the wicked perish...", "The myrrh-bearing women...", "This is the day which the Lord hath made...", "A Pascha of delight! Pascha, the Lord's Pascha!..."). "Glory... Both now and ever...": Paschal Doxastikon in Tone 5: "It is the day of Resurrection: let us be radiant for the festival, and let us embrace one another. Let us say, Brethren, even to those who hate us; let us forgive all things at the Resurrection, and thus let us cry:". At the words "and thus let us cry", the Second Choir sings the customary melody of "Christ is risen from the dead, trampling down death by death, and upon those in the tombs bestowing life!" (Christos aneste); then the First Choir chants the same, and again the Second Choir repeats it, and the Divine Liturgy begins. Printed Footnote 3 of Violakis: Documents that while this Doxastikon is chanted, the solemn veneration of the Holy Gospel Book takes place in all reverence and due order; in certain local traditions, however, the veneration is performed immediately at the beginning of the Paschal Canon for greater convenience of both the clergy and the congregation.

Paragraph 2: The Divine Liturgy of Holy Pascha: The Archdeacon exclaims: "Bless, Master!" (Eulogeson Despota); the Priest intones the Kingdom Doxology: "Blessed is the kingdom of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen." (Eulogemene he basileia), and the choirs respond: "Amen". Directly from within the Sanctuary, the celebrant chants the Paschal Troparion in Tone 5: "Christ is risen from the dead, trampling down death by death, and upon those in the tombs bestowing life!" (Christos aneste ek nekron) three times; the choirs then sing it six times with the Paschal Verses of Psalm 67 LXX ("Let God arise...", "As smoke vanisheth...", "So let the wicked perish...", "This is the day which the Lord hath made..."), and the final repetition is intoned again from within the Sanctuary. The Festal Antiphons of Pascha are chanted antiphonally with their appointed refrains: Antiphon 1: Psalm 65 LXX ("Shout joyfully unto the Lord, all the earth; chant now unto His Name, give glory unto His praise", "Say unto God: How awesome are Thy works! In the multitude of Thy power shall Thine enemies lie unto Thee", "Let all the earth worship Thee, and chant unto Thee; let them chant unto Thy Name, O Most High!") with the refrain: "Through the intercessions of the Theotokos, Savior, save us!" (Tais presbeiais tes Theotokou, Soter, soson hemas); Antiphon 2: Psalm 66 LXX ("May God be compassionate unto us, and bless us; may He cause His face to shine upon us, and have mercy on us", "That we may know Thy way upon the earth, Thy salvation among all nations", "Let the peoples give thanks unto Thee, O God; let all the peoples give thanks unto Thee!") with the refrain: "Save us, O Son of God, Who art risen from the dead, who sing to Thee: Alleluia!" (Soson hemas Yie Theou, ho anastas ek nekron, psallontas soi, Allelouia); Antiphon 3: Psalm 67 LXX ("Let God arise, and let His enemies be scattered...", "As smoke vanisheth, so let them vanish...", "So let the wicked perish at the presence of God; and let the righteous be glad!", "This is the day which the Lord hath made; let us rejoice and be glad in it!") with the refrain: "Christ is risen from the dead, trampling down death by death, and upon those in the tombs bestowing life!". At the Little Entrance, the clergy chant the Entrance Hymn [Isodikon]: "In the congregations bless ye God, the Lord from the springs of Israel!" (En ekklesiais eulogeite ton Theon, Kyrion ek pegon Israel, Ps. 67:27 LXX).

[^462]: Pentekostarion: The Sunday of Holy Pascha (The Divine Liturgy Concluded) (§ 2 Concluded) and Paschal Vespers Opening (§ 3 Opening). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p805 (printed page 790) and facing English leaf p806 (printed page 791). Concluding rubrics of Paragraph 2 (The Divine Liturgy of Pascha): The Entrance Hymn is completed with the Paschal refrain: "Save us, O Son of God, Who art risen from the dead, who sing to Thee: Alleluia!". Directly after the Entrance: the Troparion "Christ is risen from the dead...", the Ypakoe in Tone 4: "Anticipating the dawn, Mary and the women found the stone rolled away from the tomb..." (Prolabousai ton orthron), and the Kontakion in Tone 8: "Though Thou didst descend into the grave, O Immortal One, yet didst Thou destroy the power of Hades..." (Ei kai en tapho katelthes). In place of the Trisagion Hymn, the Baptismal Hymn: "As many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ. Alleluia" (Hosoi eis Christon ebaptisthete, Gal. 3:27) is chanted. The Epistle is proclaimed: Acts 1:1–8 (The Acts of the Apostles: The Promise of the Father and the Ascension); the "Alleluia" with its verses; and the Holy Gospel: John 1:1–17 (The Paschal Prologue: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God..."). In place of "It is truly right" [Axion Estin / Zadostoinyk], the choirs chant the Ninth Heirmos with its Megalynarion: "The Angel cried unto her who is full of grace: O pure Virgin, rejoice, and again I say rejoice; for thy Son has arisen from the grave on the third day. Shine, shine, O new Jerusalem, for the glory of the Lord is risen upon thee! Dance now and be glad, O Zion, and do thou, O pure Theotokos, rejoice in the Resurrection of Him Whom thou didst bear!" (Ho Angelos eboa... Photizou, photizou). The Communion Hymn is chanted: "Receive the Body of Christ; taste of the immortal Fountain. Alleluia!" (Soma Christou metalabete, peges athanatou geusasthe. Allelouia). After the Prayer behind the Ambo, the Troparion "Christ is risen from the dead..." is intoned three times plainly, and the Patriarch reads from the ambo the monumental Catechetical Sermon of Saint John Chrysostom: "If any be pious and a lover of God, let him enjoy this fair and radiant triumph! If any be a wise servant, let him rejoicing enter into the joy of his Lord!..." (Ei tis eusebes kai philotheos). At its conclusion, the Dismissal Troparion of Saint John Chrysostom in Tone 8 is chanted: "Grace shining forth from thy mouth like a radiant torch has enlightened the universe; it has disclosed to the world the treasures of poverty, and demonstrated to us the height of humility; but as thou dost instruct us by thy words, Father John Chrysostom, intercede with Christ God that our souls may be saved!" (He tou stomatos sou kathaper pyrsos eklampsasa charis); the blessing of the Lord is given, and the solemn Dismissal is pronounced by the Patriarch, who exclaims three times: "Christ is risen!", the people responding thrice: "Truly He is risen!". The Patriarch exclaims: "Glory to His holy third-day Resurrection!", the people responding: "We worship His third-day Resurrection!", and the Patriarch concludes by singing the entire Troparion "Christ is risen from the dead...". Following the Dismissal, the Patriarch and concelebrating Hierarchs in full vestments, preceded by taper-bearers, processional fans [hexapteryga], and church officers, ascend to the Great Synodikon while the choirs chant "Christ is risen from the dead...", where the Polychronismos of the Patriarch is chanted according to custom. Paragraph 3 Opening: The Vespers of Pascha [Agape Vespers]: Around the Third Hour of the day [9:00 a.m.], the Patriarch and Hierarchs assemble in the Great Synodikon and vest in their hierarchical vestments, attended by priests and deacons already vested. Printed Footnotes 4–8 of Violakis: Footnote 4: The Entrance Hymn "In the congregations" is chanted daily at the Divine Liturgy until the Apodosis of Pascha; Footnote 5: "As many of you as have been baptized into Christ" replaces the Trisagion until Saturday of Bright Week and on the Apodosis; Footnote 6: Chanting "Christ is risen" three times after the Prayer behind the Ambo continues until Saturday of Bright Week, whereas from Thomas Sunday onward "Blessed be the name of the Lord" is sung; Footnote 7: In ancient Typika, Chrysostom's Catechetical Sermon was read after the Praises before the Divine Liturgy as preparation for Holy Communion, but later practice permanently fixed it after the Communion Hymn or Prayer behind the Ambo; Footnote 8: From Holy Pascha until Saturday of Bright Week, and on the Apodosis, the entire liturgical cycle of the Hours (First, Third, Sixth, Ninth Hours), Compline, and Midnight Office is replaced by the Paschal Service: "Blessed is our God always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.", "Christ is risen from the dead" (thrice), "Having beheld the Resurrection of Christ" (thrice), the Ypakoe "Anticipating the dawn...", the Kontakion "Though Thou didst descend into the grave...", the Troparia "In the tomb bodily...", "Glory...", "As life-bearing...", "Both now and ever...", "That which is sanctified by the Most High...", "Lord, have mercy" (40 times), "Glory... Both now and ever...", "More honorable than the Cherubim", "Through the prayers of our holy fathers...", and "Christ is risen from the dead" (thrice). This service is repeated three times daily in place of the regular offices.

[^463]: Pentekostarion: The Sunday of Holy Pascha (Paschal Agape Vespers Concluded) (§ 3 Concluded). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p807 (printed page 792) and facing English leaf p808 (printed page 793). Concluding rubrics of Paragraph 3 (The Vespers of Love / Agape Vespers): While the Patriarch and concelebrating Hierarchs vest in the Great Synodikon, the choirs chant the slow Paschal Heirmos in Tone 1: "It is the day of Resurrection: let us be radiant, O ye peoples!" (Anastaseos hemera argon). A solemn procession forms: led by candle-bearers, processional fans [hexapteryga], the Primikirios, and the choirs chanting "Christ is risen from the dead...", followed by priests, Hierarchs, deacons holding the two-branched and three-branched candlesticks [dikirotrikira], and finally the Patriarch bearing his pastoral staff and the Holy Gospel Book, escorted by the Grand Logothete, ambassadors of Orthodox nations, and civil dignitaries. Processing down into the church, the Patriarch and clergy enter directly into the Sanctuary. The Patriarch censes three times before the Holy Table and intones the opening doxology: "Glory to the holy, and consubstantial, and life-creating, and undivided Trinity, always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.", singing "Christ is risen from the dead..." according to the order of Paschal Matins. The Great Litany is recited by the Archdeacon, and the Patriarch intones the exclamation: "For unto Thee is due all glory, honor, and worship...". At "Lord, I have cried" (Kyrie ekekraxa), six Resurrectional Stichera in Tone 2 are chanted in order: "Before the ages, begotten of the Father, God the Word incarnate of the Virgin Mary, come, let us worship; for having endured the Cross, He was delivered over to burial, as He Himself willed, and having risen from the dead, He saved me, the wandering man!" (Ton pro aionon ek Patros gennethenta), while the Patriarch, preceded by the Primikirios bearing the two-branched candle [divamvoulon], censes the icons and congregation, and ascends the throne. The First Choir sings "Glory...": "The saving hymn we sing with our mouths: Come, let us all worship in the house of the Lord..." (Ton soterion hymnon); the Second Choir sings "Both now and ever...": Dogmatic Theotokion in Tone 2: "The shadow of the law passed away when grace arrived..." (Parelthen he skia tou nomou). The Little Entrance is made with the Holy Gospel Book; "O Gladsome Light" (Phos hilaron) is chanted slowly and entirely outside the Sanctuary. The Patriarch enters the Sanctuary singing the Great Prokimenon in Tone 7: "What God is as great as our God? Thou art the God Who workest wonders!" (Tis Theos megas hos ho Theos hemon; sy ei ho Theos ho poion thaumasia, Ps. 76:14 LXX) with its three verses (Ps. 76:15, 11, 12 LXX), repeated antiphonally by the choirs. The Patriarch exclaims: "And that we may be accounted worthy to hear the Holy Gospel, let us pray unto the Lord God", and proclaims the Holy Gospel: John 20:19–25 ("When it was evening on that day, the first day of the week..." until "Except I shall see in His hands the print of the nails... I will not believe"), proclaimed in successive pericopes and translated/echoed in multiple languages by the concelebrating Hierarchs, priests, and deacons. After the choirs chant "Many years to you, Master!" (Eis polla ete), the Augmented Litany, "Vouchsafe, O Lord, to keep us this evening without sin", and the Evening Litany are recited. After the exclamation: "Blessed and glorified be the might of Thy kingdom: of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.", the First Choir chants the Resurrectional Apostichon Sticheron in Tone 2: "Thy Resurrection, O Christ Savior, hath enlightened the whole universe and recalled Thine own creation: O almighty Lord, glory to Thee!" (He Anastasis sou Christe Soter), followed by the Stichera of Pascha: "A sacred Pascha is shown forth unto us today..." (Pascha hieron), "Glory... Both now and ever...": "It is the day of Resurrection...", and "Christ is risen from the dead..." (three times). During this chanting, the Patriarch and clergy process back up to the Great Synodikon, where the Patriarch gives the solemn Dismissal while the choirs chant his Polychronismos; thus concluding the Agape Vespers.

Paragraph 4: Monday of Bright Week Matins: Following the Paschal replacement service for the Midnight Office (Paragraph 2, Note 8), the Priest, fully vested in all priestly vestments, stands before the Holy Table with the censer, censes, and intones: "Glory to the holy, and consubstantial, and life-creating, and undivided Trinity, always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen."; he then chants "Christ is risen from the dead..." three times with the Paschal Verses. The Litany is recited with the exclamation: "For unto Thee is due all glory, honor, and worship...". The Paschal Canon of Saint John of Damascus in Tone 1 is chanted without variation, except that after Ode 6 only the Kontakion ("Though Thou didst descend into the grave...") is said, followed directly by "Having beheld the Resurrection of Christ..." (once). After Ode 8, the Deacon intones: "The Theotokos and Mother of the Light let us magnify in hymns!", and Ode 9 is sung with the Megalynaria. The Exaposteilarion: "Having slept in the flesh as a mortal..." is sung twice according to ancient typika. At the Praises, four Resurrectional Stichera in Tone 2 ("Let every breath and all creation glorify Thee, O Lord...") and the Stichera of Pascha ("A sacred Pascha...") are chanted with their verses; "Glory... Both now and ever...": "It is the day of Resurrection...", "Christ is risen from the dead..." (three times), and the Divine Liturgy begins. Paragraph 5: Monday of Bright Week Divine Liturgy: The Antiphons are celebrated as on Pascha Sunday. In place of the Trisagion, "As many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ. Alleluia" (Gal. 3:27) is sung; the Epistle (Acts 1:12–17, 21–26) and Holy Gospel (Luke 24:12–35: The Road to Emmaus) of the day are proclaimed. In place of "It is truly right", the Ninth Heirmos with Megalynarion: "The Angel cried unto her who is full of grace... Shine, shine, O new Jerusalem..." is chanted. Communion Hymn: "Receive the Body of Christ; taste of the immortal Fountain. Alleluia!". After the Prayer behind the Ambo, "Christ is risen from the dead..." is intoned three times plainly, followed by the Dismissal. General Rubric for Bright Week: This identical order of service is to be strictly observed on each day throughout the entirety of Bright Week at Vespers, Matins, and the Divine Liturgy, with the sole exception of the Gospel reading proclaimed at the Vespers of Pascha Sunday afternoon. Paragraph 6: Friday of Bright Week: The Feast of the Most Holy Lady Theotokos of the Life-Giving Fountain: On Thursday evening at "Lord, I have cried" (Kyrie ekekraxa), ten Stichera are chanted: six Resurrectional Stichera in Tone 3: "Holding the victory, O Christ, over Hades, Thou didst ascend upon the Cross, that Thou mightest raise up with Thyself those who sat in the darkness of death: O Free among the dead, Who pourest forth life from Thine own light, almighty Savior, have mercy on us!" (Niken echon Christe) and four of the Life-Giving Fountain in Tone 3, repeating the first: 1. & 2. "Who can declare thy mighty wonders, O Fountain? For flowing with streams of wonders, thou dost pour forth rivers of healings..." (Tis lalesei tas dynasteias sou Pege). "Glory...": Doxastikon of the Fountain in Tone 6: "Who can tell of thy mighty acts, O undefiled Fountain? For abundant in miracles and lavish in grace, thou dost ever pour forth healings..." (Tis lalesei tas dynasteias sou Pege); "Both now and ever...": Dogmatic Theotokion in Tone 3: "Who would not admire, who would not praise thy childbirth without seed, O all-pure Virgin? (Ten Anastasin sou Christe Soter) is chanted.
