Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p701 (printed page 686) and facing English leaf p702 (printed page 687). Great Lenten Vespers (Aliturgical Order): Paragraph 20 codifies the structure of Great Lenten Vespers on days when no Presanctified Liturgy is celebrated (observed on Monday, Tuesday, and Thursday evenings of Great Lent when not superseded by a feast): At "Lord, I have cried" (Ps. 140 LXX), six Stichera are chanted: three Prosomia from the Triodion and three of the Saint of the following day from the Menaion; "Glory... Both now and ever...": Prosomion Theotokion (or Stavrotheotokion on Tuesday or Thursday evenings). The Entrance with the censer is omitted. "O Gladsome Light" is read in recitativo (or chanted simply). The Prokimena and two Old Testament Readings of the day from Genesis and Proverbs follow. Then "Vouchsafe, O Lord", the Evening Litany ("Let us complete our evening prayer"), and the priestly Exclamation: "Blessed and glorified be the majesty of Thy kingdom, of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen." At the Aposticha, the Idiomelon of the day is chanted twice, the Martyrikon of the Tone once, and "Glory... Both now and ever...": Theotokion (or Stavrotheotokion). Footnote 12 specifies the psalm verses for the Aposticha: before the second singing of the Idiomelon, the Verse: "Unto Thee have I lifted up mine eyes, O Thou Who dwellest in the heavens! Behold, as the eyes of servants look unto the hands of their masters, and as the eyes of a maiden unto the hands of her mistress, so our eyes look unto the Lord our God, until He have mercy upon us" (Ps. 122:1–2 LXX); and before the Martyrikon, the Verse: "Have mercy on us, O Lord, have mercy on us; for we are utterly filled with contempt: our soul is exceedingly filled with the reproach of the prosperous, and with the contempt of the proud" (Ps. 122:3–4 LXX). Then "Now Thou dost dismiss Thy servant", the Trisagion Prayers, and the festive Troparia chanted with melody: "Rejoice, O Virgin Theotokos, Mary full of grace, the Lord is with thee; blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, for thou hast borne the Savior of our souls" with a great prostration; "O Baptizer of Christ, remember us all, that we may be delivered from our transgressions, for unto thee is given grace to intercede for us" with a prostration; "Glory...": "Intercede for us, holy Apostles and all Saints, that we may be delivered from perils and sorrows, for we have acquired you as fervent advocates before the Savior" with a prostration; "Both now and ever...": "Beneath thy tender compassion we take refuge, O Theotokos; despise not our supplications in adversity, but deliver us from perils, O only pure and blessed one", read rapidly in recitativo (chuma) without prostration. "Lord, have mercy" 40 times; "Glory... Both now and ever..."; "More honorable than the Cherubim"; "In the name of the Lord, bless, father"; Priest: "He Who is, Christ our God, is blessed..."; Reader: "O Heavenly King, strengthen our faithful civil authorities..."; followed by the three great prostrations with the Prayer of Saint Ephraim the Syrian. Then the Trisagion Prayers again, "Lord, have mercy" 12 times, the prayer: "All-Holy Trinity, one-natured Might, undivided Kingdom, Cause of all good, be well pleased with me also, the sinner; confirm, instruct my heart, and cleanse away all my defilement; enlighten my mind, that I may ever glorify, hymn, and worship Thee, saying: One is holy, One is the Lord, Jesus Christ, to the glory of God the Father. Amen."; "Blessed be the name of the Lord" thrice; "Glory... Both now and ever..."; Psalm 33 LXX ("I will bless the Lord at all times"), and the Dismissal. Concluding Rubric & Presanctified Transition: Paragraph 20 concludes by contrasting this aliturgical order with the days when the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts is celebrated: on Presanctified days, after the prayer "All-Holy Trinity, one-natured Might...", the Priest pronounces the Dismissal of Matins and immediately intones the solemn opening of the Divine Liturgy: "Blessed is the kingdom of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen." Paragraph 21 initiates the Presanctified Liturgy: the Deacon (if serving) exclaims "Bless, Master!", the Priest proclaims the Kingdom, and the Proestos reads the Introductory Psalm (Ps. 103 LXX).

[^411]: Great Lent: Order of Presanctified Liturgy Continued (§ 21 Continued: Eighteenth Kathisma, "Lord, I have cried", Entrance, Old Testament Readings, "Let my prayer arise", and Great Entrance). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p703 (printed page 688) and facing English leaf p704 (printed page 689). Presanctified Liturgy Structure: Paragraph 21 continues the order of the Presanctified Liturgy: After the Introductory Psalm, the Eighteenth Kathisma of the Psalter ("Unto the Lord in my distress...", Pss. 119–133 LXX, the Gradual Psalms or Songs of Ascents) is appended. Footnote 13 provides the historical and liturgical background: the formal order of the Typikon requires "Unto the Lord" (Ta Pros Kyrion) to be chanted on every weekday of Great Lent; however, in recent centuries, general custom has restricted its chanting exclusively to days when the Presanctified Liturgy is served, though certain traditional priests continue to recite it on every Monday of Great Lent. At "Lord, I have cried" (Ps. 140 LXX), ten Stichera are chanted: the Idiomelon of the day twice, the Martyrikon once, three Prosomia in sequence from the Triodion, and four Prosomia from the Menaion for the Saint of the following day. Footnote 14 records the Friday evening variation: on Friday at Vespers (on the eve of the Saturday of the Dead), the Idiomelon is sung twice, followed by the four Martyrika of the Tone from the end of the Triodion, four Stichera for the Saint; "Glory...": Nekrosimon Doxastikon (Hymn for the Dead); "Both now and ever...": Theotokion of the Tone of the week. On other days: "Glory... Both now and ever...": Prosomion Theotokion (or Stavrotheotokion on Tuesday and Thursday evenings). The Entrance is made with the censer (or with the Gospel if a Gospel pericope is appointed for the Saint), followed by "O Gladsome Light" and the two Old Testament Readings of the day from Genesis and Proverbs. Footnote 15 describes the venerable ceremonial of the Presanctified light: after the first Reading (and the intervening second Prokimenon), the Reader exclaims "Command!" (Keleuson); the Priest, holding the censer in his right hand and a lighted candle in his left, turns toward the holy doors and exclaims: "Wisdom! Let us attend!"; then, turning toward the people, he elevates the candle and censer cross-wise, proclaiming: "The light of Christ enlightens all!" The faithful perform a full prostration. After the second Reading, the Priest exclaims to the Reader: "Peace be unto thee, reader", and the Deacon adds: "Wisdom!" Immediately the Priest, holding the censer and censing lightly before the Holy Table, intones: "Let my prayer arise in Thy sight as incense, and the lifting up of my hands as an evening sacrifice" (Ps. 140:2 LXX). The two Choirs alternate chanting the four verses four times, while the faithful remain kneeling, and finally the Priest censes the people while singing "Let my prayer arise" up to the words "in Thy sight as incense", which the Choir concludes. Following "Let my prayer arise" and the three great prostrations, the Great Litany of Fervent Supplication is intoned, along with the litanies for the Catechumens and for Those Preparing for Holy Illumination. After the Exclamation: "According to the gift of Thy Christ, with Whom Thou art blessed, together with Thine all-holy, good, and life-creating Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.", instead of the Cherubic Hymn, the ancient entrance hymn is chanted: "Now the powers of heaven do invisibly minister with us; for lo, the King of glory enters. Behold, the mystical sacrifice, fully accomplished, is ushered in. With faith and love let us draw near, that we may become partakers of eternal life. Alleluia." Footnote 16 details the Great Entrance of the Presanctified: the chanter halts the melody mid-sentence; the Priest, preceded by the Deacon censing, exits the Sanctuary bearing the Presanctified Holy Gifts upon his head in complete silence and profound solemnity, omitting all audible commemorations, because the sacrifice is already perfected and the gifts carried are the very Body and Blood of our Lord Jesus Christ. The people prostrate themselves in reverent adoration and remain bent down until the Priest re-enters the Sanctuary and places the Holy Gifts upon the Holy Table; then the chanter resumes the hymn from "Behold, the mystical sacrifice...". The Litany of Completion follows: "Let us complete our evening prayer unto the Lord", the priestly Exclamation: "And make us worthy, Master...", and the Lord's Prayer ("Our Father..."). The Deacon exclaims: "Let us attend!", and the Priest proclaims: "The Presanctified Holy Things for the holy!"

Presanctified Liturgy Conclusion: Paragraph 21 concludes the Presanctified Liturgy: Following the elevation of the Presanctified Gifts, the Communion Hymn [Koinonikon] is chanted: "Taste and see that the Lord is good. Alleluia" (Ps. 33:9 LXX). The Deacon exclaims: "With the fear of God, faith, and love, draw near", and the post-communion hymns are sung: "I will bless the Lord at all times; His praise shall be ever in my mouth" and "Taste of the heavenly Bread and the Cup of Life, and see that the Lord is good. Alleluia, Alleluia, Alleluia." The Priest recites the Prayer behind the Ambo: "O Master Almighty, Who hast created all creation in wisdom..."; then: "Blessed be the name of the Lord, henceforth and forever" thrice. The Priest exclaims: "The blessing of the Lord and His mercy be upon you...", and gives the Dismissal (during which "The Master and Chief Priest of us all, O Lord, preserve for many years!" is chanted if a bishop presides). After the Dismissal, Psalm 33 LXX ("I will bless the Lord at all times") and Psalm 144 LXX ("I will extol Thee, my God, my King") are read during the distribution of the Antidoron, concluding with "Through the prayers of our holy Fathers...". Feast Days in Great Lent: Paragraph 22 codifies the rubrics for an officially celebrated Saint (Polyeleos or Doxology rank) falling on fasting weekdays: 1) If on Wednesday: at Vespers, ten Stichera at "Lord, I have cried": the day's Idiomelon twice, the Martyrikon once, three Prosomia from the Triodion sequence, and four Prosomia for the celebrated Saint (not the Saint of the following day); "Glory...": of the Saint; "Both now and ever...": Prosomion Theotokion in the same Tone as the Doxastikon. Entrance with the Gospel. After "Let my prayer arise", the Prokimenon, Epistle [Apostolos], and Gospel of the celebrated Saint are read, and the Koinonikon of the Saint is chanted: "The righteous shall be in everlasting remembrance. Alleluia" (Ps. 111:6 LXX). 2) If on Friday: at "Lord, I have cried", the day's Idiomelon twice, the four Martyrika of the Tone, and four Stichera for the Saint; "Glory...": of the Saint; "Both now and ever...": the first Theotokion of the Tone of the week. Two exceptions are noted: a) On Friday of the First Week of Lent (commemoration of the Great Martyr Theodore the Tyro and the miracle of the Kolyva), after the Idiomelon twice, four Stichera of Saint Theodore and four of the celebrated Saint are chanted; b) On Friday of the Fifth Week of Lent (eve of the Akathist Saturday), after the Idiomelon twice, four Stichera of the Theotokos ("Counsel eternal", Boulen proaionion) and four of the celebrated Saint are chanted. 3) If on Monday, Tuesday, or Thursday: at "Lord, I have cried", the Idiomelon of the Matins Aposticha of the day is chanted twice, the Martyrikon once, three Prosomia of the sequence from the Triodion, and four Stichera of the celebrated Saint; "Glory...": of the Saint; "Both now and ever...": Prosomion Theotokion; and the remainder of the Presanctified Liturgy proceeds as above. Great Compline Opening: Paragraph 23 initiates Great Compline (To Mega Apodeipnon): On Monday evening of the First Week of the Fast, the Priest exclaims: "Blessed is our God always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen.", "Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee", "O Heavenly King, Comforter, Spirit of Truth...".

[^413]: Great Lent: Order of Great Compline Continued (§ 23 Continued: Hexapsalmos of Compline, "God is with us", Creed, Penitential Verses, Prayer of Manasseh, Great Canon of St. Andrew, and "Lord of hosts"). Detailed epigraphic synthesis of physical leaf p707 (printed page 692) and facing English leaf p708 (printed page 693). Great Compline Structure: Paragraph 23 details the order of Great Compline: The Reader recites the Trisagion Prayers, the Priest exclaims: "For Thine is the kingdom...", and the Reader recites "Lord, have mercy" 12 times, "Glory... Both now and ever...", "Come, let us worship" thrice, and begins the Six Psalms of Great Compline (Psalms 4, 6, 12, 24, 30, and 90 LXX). After Psalm 90, "Glory... Both now and ever... Alleluia, alleluia, alleluia, glory to Thee, O God" thrice, "Lord, have mercy" thrice, "Glory... Both now and ever...". Immediately the two Choirs chant the antiphonal verses of "God is with us: understand, O ye nations, and submit yourselves, for God is with us" (Isa. 8:9 LXX), the First Choir repeating the first verse in full at the end. The Reader recites the three evening Troparia in recitativo (chuma): "The day is past, I give Thee thanks, O Lord...", and the Second Choir chants: "The bodiless nature of the Cherubim glorifies Thee with unceasing hymns...", down to: "That escaping from the deceits of the evil one, we may sing the angelic hymn!". The Reader recites: "Holy, Holy, Holy, Lord God of Sabaoth, heaven and earth are full of Thy glory!", followed by the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Symbol of Faith: "I believe in one God...". The Second Choir chants the verses of supplication: "All-holy Lady, Theotokos, intercede for us sinners" (thrice), followed by the invocations of the Bodiless Powers, the Apostles, Martyrs, and Saints (twice), and concluding with: "O Lord, God, forgive us our sins" (thrice), "and have mercy on us". The Trisagion Prayers follow, after which the Reader recites the Prayer of Manasseh, King of Judah: "O Lord Almighty, God of our fathers Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob...". Following another Trisagion, the penitential Troparia are chanted in a subdued voice: "Have mercy on us, O Lord, have mercy on us...". Then the Small Doxology ("Glory to God in the highest...") and the prayer "Vouchsafe, O Lord, to keep us this night without sin" (Kataxioson). The Great Penitential Canon of Saint Andrew of Crete is chanted: Heirmos "He is my helper and protector unto salvation" (Exod. 15:2 LXX), with the refrain before each troparion: "Have mercy on me, O God, have mercy on me". Footnote 17 notes that the Great Canon is chanted in four sections on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday evenings of the First Week of Great Lent; on all subsequent weekdays of Great Lent until Thursday evening of the Sixth Week, Canons to the Theotokos from the Theotokarion are chanted in its place. After Ode 6 of the Canon, the Kontakion "My soul, my soul, arise! Why sleepest thou?" is chanted with melody; after Ode 9, the Heirmos "Without seed of man, by the Holy Spirit..." is chanted, followed by the Trisagion Prayers. Then the hymn "O Lord of hosts, be with us, for we have no other helper in distress but Thee; O Lord of hosts, have mercy on us!" is chanted antiphonally with its six scriptural verses (Ps. 150 LXX), the first choir repeating the refrain after "Praise God in His Saints" and the second choir after "Praise Him in the firmament of His power".

Great Compline Conclusion: Paragraph 23 concludes the order of Great Compline: Following the verses of "O Lord of hosts", the Reader recites: "Glory...": "O Lord, if we had not Thy Saints as intercessors...", "Both now and ever...": "Great is the multitude of mine offenses, O Theotokos...". The First Choir chants: "All-holy Theotokos, forsake me not during the days of my life...", and the Second Choir chants: "All my hope I place in thee, O Mother of God; keep me under thy protection". The Reader recites "Lord, have mercy" 40 times, the prayer "Thou Who at all times and at every hour...", "Lord, have mercy" thrice, "Glory... Both now and ever...", "More honorable than the Cherubim", "In the name of the Lord, bless, father". The Priest exclaims: "May God be merciful unto us, and bless us, and cause His face to shine upon us, and have mercy on us" (Ps. 66:2 LXX). Three great prostrations with the Prayer of Saint Ephraim the Syrian, and "Lord, have mercy" 12 times are performed. The two Canonarchs alternate the concluding prayers: the right Canonarch intones the Prayer of Paul of Evergetis: "O spotless, undefiled, incorrupt, all-chaste, pure Lady, Bride of God..."; the left Canonarch intones the Prayer of Antiochus the Monk: "And grant unto us, O Master, as we depart to sleep, repose of body and soul..."; followed by the antiphonal invocations: "Most glorious ever-virgin Mary...", "The Father is my hope...", and "All my hope I place in thee...". Then the Priest exclaims: "And that we may be counted worthy to listen to the Holy Gospel, let us pray to the Lord our God", and reads the appointed Gospel pericope of the First Week. The Bishop (or Priest) says: "Peace be unto all", the Priest exclaims: "Let us bow our heads unto the Lord", and the Hierarch (or Priest) recites the prayer of bowing heads: "O Master abundant in mercy, Lord Jesus Christ our God...". The Priest intones the litany of supplication: "Let us pray for the peace of the world...", and all the brethren and faithful receive mutual forgiveness from the Hierarch or Superior [Proestos], kissing his right hand. While this veneration is taking place, the Second Choir chants the venerable Theotokion: "Thou protectest all, O good one, who with faith flee unto thy strong hand; for we sinners have no other advocate before God in dangers and afflictions, being weighed down with many sins: O Mother of God Most High, therefore we bow before thee: deliver thy servants from every distress!" (Panton prostateueis, agathe). Footnote 18 records that on Tuesday and Thursday evenings, the Stavrotheotokion "When the Virgin beheld Thine unjust slaughter, O Christ..." (Sphagen sou ten adikon, Christe) is chanted in its place. The service concludes with the final exclamation: "Through the prayers of our holy Fathers, O Lord Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us." Concluding Statutory Rubric: George Violakis establishes the general rule that this exact form and order of Great Compline is to be observed daily throughout all of Great Lent until Great and Holy Tuesday evening, with the sole exception that the Gospel readings are appointed strictly and exclusively for the First Week. On Great and Holy Monday and Great and Holy Tuesday evenings, instead of the Canon of the Theotokos from the Theotokarion, the proper Triodia of Compline appointed in the Triodion are chanted, concluding with the hymn "When the Virgin beheld Thine unjust slaughter, O Christ."
