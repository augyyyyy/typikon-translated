[^581]: Physical leaf p1031 (printed page [1016] / TD-37), presenting entries for the octave rotation of Bright Week (*Διακαινήσιμος ἑβδομάς*), the *Canon* (*κανών*), the office of *Canonarch* (*κανονάρχης* / Slavonic *Kanonarkh*), and the introduction to the Biblical *Canticle* (*ᾠδή*). In Byzantine hymnography, the Canon developed as a structured nine-ode poetic cycle modeled upon the nine scriptural canticles, with each ode consisting of a series of troparia preceded by an overarching Heirmos. The canonarch, tonsured to the minor orders, ensured precise choral execution by announcing each phrase in recitative cadence before the choir chanted.

[^582]: Physical leaf p1032 (printed page [1017] / TD-38), detailing the Nine Biblical Canticles appended to the liturgical Psalter: (1) First Song of Moses (Exodus 15:1–19); (2) Second Song of Moses (Deuteronomy 32:1–43); (3) Prayer of Hannah (1 Kingdoms 2:1–10 LXX [1 Sam 2:1–10]); (4) Prayer of Habakkuk (Habakkuk 3:2–19 LXX); (5) Prayer of Isaiah (Isaiah 26:9–20 LXX); (6) Prayer of Jonah (Jonah 2:2–9 LXX); (7) Prayer of the Three Holy Children in the Furnace (Daniel 3:26–56 LXX); (8) Song of the Three Holy Children (*Benedicite*, Daniel 3:57–88 LXX); and (9) Song of the Theotokos (Magnificat, Luke 1:46–55). Odes 7 and 8 are preserved in the canonical Septuagint text of Daniel.

[^583]: Physical leaf p1033 (printed page [1018] / TD-39), concluding the treatment of the Biblical Canticles (the Benedictus of Zechariah, Luke 1:68–79, and the omission of Ode 2 outside Great Lent) and defining the liturgical *Cassock* (*ράσον* / Slavonic *Podryasnik* and *Ryasa*). The baptismal troparion cited (**“A robe of divine light bestow upon me, O Thou that for vesture arrayest Thyself with Light”**) employs the ancient biblical terms *chiton* (*χιτών*) and *himation* (*ἱμάτιον*), sung as the newly baptized candidate is clothed in the white garment of regeneration.

[^584]: Physical leaf p1034 (printed page [1019] / TD-40), concluding the baptismal hymn and presenting entries for the *Celebrant* (*ἱεροτελεστής*), the liturgical *Censer* (*θυμιατόν* / *θυμιατήριον* / Slavonic *Kadilo*), the hand censer (*Katzion* / *κατζίον*), and *Ceremony* (*τελετή*). Incense symbolizes both the purification and sweet fragrance of the Holy Spirit and the ascending prayers of the Christian faithful (cf. Psalm 140:2 LXX; Revelation 8:3–4).

[^585]: Physical leaf p1035 (printed page [1020] / TD-41), presenting liturgical entries for *Chasmodia* (*χασμῳδία*, forbidden discordant singing under Canon XV of the Council of Laodicea), *Chazranion* (pastoral walking stick), *Chief Secretary* (*Ἀρχιγραμματεύς*), *Choneftirion* (the sanctuary sacrarium or piscina for reverent disposal of sacred elements), *Chorostaton Archierefs* (hierarch presiding in the choir), the *Chromatic* mode in Byzantine octoechos theory, and Holy *Chrism* (*Χρῖσμα* / *Holy Myron*).

[^586]: Physical leaf p1036 (printed page [1021] / TD-42), continuing the composition of Holy Chrism according to the Constantinopolitan formula of 64 fragrant botanical ingredients based on Exodus 30:22–25, and providing the architectural exposition of the *Church* (*ἐκκλησία* / *ναός*). The entry details the construction and dedication of Hagia Sophia under Justinian I (December 27, 537) and the theological symbolism of the tripartite division of the temple (Narthex, Nave, and Sanctuary).

[^587]: Physical leaf p1037 (printed page [1022] / TD-43), concluding the architectural entry with the famous testimony of Prince Vladimir's envoys from the *Primary Chronicle* regarding the divine beauty of Hagia Sophia, and presenting entries for the rubric *Chyma* (*χῦμα*, continuous recitative reading or chanting), the transition from scroll to *Codex* (*κῶδιξ*) in early Christian biblical transmission, and the introduction to *Commemoration* (*μνημόσυνον*).

[^588]: Physical leaf p1038 (printed page [1023] / TD-44), defining liturgical *Commemoration* (*μνημόσυνον* / Slavonic *Pominaniye*), the evening office of *Compline* (*ἀπόδειπνον* / Slavonic *Povecheriye*), and the hagiographical rank of *Confessor* (*ὁμολογητής*). Compline exists in two distinct orders—Great Compline and Small Compline—incorporating a canon to the Mother of God in the Tone of the week, transferred commemorations from the *Menaion*, and the mutual asking of forgiveness.

[^589]: Physical leaf p1039 (printed page [1024] / TD-45), defining the *Creed* (*Σύμβολον τῆς Πίστεως* / Slavonic *Simvol Very*) from its origin in early baptismal interrogations to the anti-Arian formulation of the First Ecumenical Council in Nicaea (325). The Symbol of Faith represents the dogmatic consensus of the Church affirming the consubstantiality (*homoousion*) and eternal divinity of the Son of God.

[^590]: Physical leaf p1040 (printed page [1025] / TD-46), setting forth the canonical text of the Nicene Creed and the pneumatological completion ratified by the Second Ecumenical Council in Constantinople (381). The resulting Nicene-Constantinopolitan Symbol of Faith was adopted as the universal baptismal profession and the central dogmatic confession chanted by the faithful at every celebration of the Divine Liturgy.
