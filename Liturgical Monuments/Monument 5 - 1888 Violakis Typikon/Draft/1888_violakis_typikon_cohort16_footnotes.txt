[^130]: The Magnificat (*He Timiotera*), the Ninth Ode of the Matins Canon, and the Morning Exapostilarion (Violakis, pp. 136–137, §2 cont.). This rubric codifies the climax of the Sunday Matins Canon at the Ninth Ode. Prior to chanting the Ninth Ode, the Deacon emerges before the icon of the Theotokos (or from before the Holy Doors) and intones the venerable censing acclamation: *"The Theotokos and Mother of the Light let us magnify in hymns"* (*Τὴν Θεοτόκον καὶ Μητέρα τοῦ φωτὸς ἐν ὕμνοις τιμῶντες μεγαλύνωμεν*). In response, the choirs chant the Canticle of the Theotokos (the *Magnificat*, Luke 1:46–55) punctuated after each of its six biblical stichoi by the megalynarion composed in the eighth century by St. Cosmas the Melodist: *"More honorable than the Cherubim, and beyond compare more glorious than the Seraphim, who without corruption gavest birth to God the Word, the very Theotokos, thee do we magnify"* (*Τὴν τιμιωτέραν τῶν Χερουβίμ...*). The irmos of the Ninth Ode is sung as the Katavasia in the designated Tone of the day. Following the Ninth Katavasia, the Little Litany is intoned, culminating in the Sunday exclamation: *"For all the powers of heaven praise Thee, and unto Thee do we ascribe glory: to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages"* (*Ὅτι σὲ αἰνοῦσι πᾶσαι αἱ δυνάμεις τῶν οὐρανῶν...*). Immediately thereafter, the choirs chant the Paschal acclamation: *"Holy is the Lord our God"* (*Ἅγιος Κύριος ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν*, Psalm 98:5, 9 LXX) twice, followed by the verse *"Exalt ye the Lord our God, and worship at His footstool, for He is holy."* This introduces the Resurrection Exapostilarion (*Ἐξαποστειλάριον*, Photogogic / Hymn of Light) corresponding to the appointed Eothinon Gospel of the Sunday, drawn from the eleven-hymn cycle composed by Emperor Constantine VII Porphyrogenitus (905–959) and his father Leo VI the Wise. If a celebrated Saint from the Menaion has an appointed Exapostilarion, it is appended after the Resurrection hymn, sealed by the proper Theotokion.

[^131]: The Sunday Praises (*Ainoi* / Lauds), Psalmody of Creation, and the Resurrection Stichira (Violakis, pp. 136–139, §2 cont.). The Praises (*Αἶνοι*, Latin *Laudes*) constitute the cosmic morning psalmody of the Byzantine Horologion, comprising Psalms 148, 149, and 150 LXX. The First Choir inaugurates the psalmody with the solemn antiphonal incipit: *"Let every breath praise the Lord. Praise the Lord from the heavens, praise Him in the highest: to Thee is due praise, O God"* (*Πᾶσα πνοὴ αἰνεσάτω τὸν Κύριον*, Psalm 148:1 LXX), and the Second Choir responds with *"Praise Him, all His angels: praise Him, all His hosts: to Thee is due praise, O God"* (*Αἰνεῖτε αὐτὸν πάντες οἱ ἄγγελοι αὐτοῦ*, Psalm 148:2 LXX). On Sundays, eight Resurrection Stichira are appointed, beginning at the verse *"This glory shall be to all His holy ones"* (*Δόξα αὕτη ἔσται πᾶσι τοῖς ὁσίοις αὐτοῦ*, Psalm 149:9 LXX). Under ordinary Sunday order, four Stichira are taken from the Octoechos and four Anatolian (Eastern) Stichira of the Tone. If a Saint commemorated from the Menaion possesses Praises, the distribution is four of the Resurrection and four of the Saint, substituting the specific festal verses of the Saint for the concluding penitential verses of Psalm 9:33 and 9:2 LXX. In a famous editorial note, Violakis observes that earlier editions printed "two" Resurrection stichira instead of four, correcting the typikon text to ensure the full eightfold octoechal count is preserved.

[^132]: The Great Doxology (*Megale Doxologia*), Morning Litanies, and the Sunday Dismissal Troparia (Violakis, pp. 138–139, §2 cont.). At the conclusion of the Praises and the Eothinon Doxastikon, the choir chants the Sunday morning Theotokion: *"Most blessed art thou, O Virgin Theotokos; for through Him Who became incarnate of thee, Hades is bound, Adam is recalled, the curse is annulled, Eve is set free, death is slain, and we are brought to life..."* (*Ὑπερευλογημένη ὑπάρχεις, Θεοτόκε Παρθένε...*). The Priest or Superior then intones the ancient liturgical acclamation: *"Glory to Thee Who hast shown us the light!"* (*Δόξα σοι τῷ δείξαντι τὸ φῶς*), and the choirs chant the Great Doxology (*Μεγάλη Δοξολογία*, the *Gloria in Excelsis Deo*) in the Tone of the preceding Doxastikon. During the chanting of the Doxology, the Deacon recites the Litany of Fervent Supplication (*Ἐλέησον ἡμᾶς ὁ Θεός*) and the Morning Morning Supplication (*Πληρώσωμεν τὴν ἑωθινὴν δέησιν ἡμῶν*), followed by the Prayer of the Inclination of Heads and the Great Dismissal. Following the concluding Trisagion of the Doxology, the Second Choir chants the proper Sunday Resurrection Troparion according to the canonical alternation of Tones: in Tones 1, 2, 3, and 4, the hymn **"Today salvation has come into the world: let us sing to Him Who arose from the tomb and the Author of our life; for having destroyed death by death, He hath given us the victory and great mercy"** (*Σήμερον σωτηρία τῷ κόσμῳ γέγονεν...*) is sung; whereas in Tones 5, 6, 7, and 8, the hymn **"Having risen from the tomb and burst the bonds of Hades, Thou hast destroyed the condemnation of death, O Lord..."** (*Ἀναστὰς ἐκ τοῦ μνήματος...*) is appointed.

[^133]: The Enarxis of the Divine Liturgy, the Typika Antiphons, and the Beatitudes (*Makarismoi*) (Violakis, pp. 138–141, §3). The Divine Liturgy opens with the solemn Trinitarian enarxis: *"Blessed is the Kingdom of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages"* (*Εὐλογημένη ἡ βασιλεία...*), followed by the choral Amen, the Great Litany (*Μεγάλη Συναπτή*), and the three Antiphons. Violakis codifies the traditional monastic and Byzantine cathedral order of the Typika (*Τὰ Τυπικά*) for the Lord's Day: (1) First Antiphon: Psalm 102 LXX (*Εὐλόγει ἡ ψυχή μου τὸν Κύριον*, "Bless the Lord, O my soul") chanted in Tone 8 Plagal [Tone 8]; (2) Second Antiphon: Psalm 145 LXX (*Αἴνει ἡ ψυχή μου τὸν Κύριον*, "Praise the Lord, O my soul") chanted in Tone 2, concluding with the fifth-century hymn of Emperor Justinian: **"Only-begotten Son and Word of God"** (*Ὁ μονογενὴς Υἱὸς καὶ Λόγος τοῦ Θεοῦ*); (3) Third Antiphon: The Beatitudes (*Οἱ Μακαρισμοί*, Matthew 5:3–12), introduced by the penitent thief's prayer: *"In Thy Kingdom remember us, O Lord, when Thou comest into Thy Kingdom"* (*Ἐν τῇ βασιλείᾳ σου μνήσθητι ἡμῶν, Κύριε*). Beginning at the verse *"Blessed are the merciful, for they shall obtain mercy"* (Matthew 5:7), eight Stichira of the Beatitudes from the Octoechos are intercalated with the remaining verses. If the occurring Saint is celebrated with a Polyeleos or Doxology, only the first four Resurrection Stichira are sung, followed by four troparia from the Sixth Ode of the Saint's Canon from the Menaion, concluding with *Glory* and *Both now* to the Trinity and the Theotokos.

[^134]: The Little Entrance with the Holy Gospel (*Mikra Eisodos*), the Entrance Hymn (*Eisodikon*), Dismissal Troparia Order, and the Trisagion Hymn (Violakis, pp. 142–143, §3 cont. & Note 3). During the chanting of the Beatitudes (or the Third Antiphon), the Little Entrance (*Μικρὰ Εἴσοδος*) is executed: the Priest and Deacon, preceded by candlebearers, process through the nave carrying the Book of the Holy Gospels, symbolizing the Epiphany of Christ and the proclamation of the Gospel to the cosmos. At the center of the solea, the Deacon elevates the Gospel Book, proclaiming: *"Wisdom, stand upright!"* (*Σοφία, ὀρθοί*), and the choirs chant the Entrance Hymn (*Εἰσοδικόν*): **"O come, let us worship and bow down before Christ: save us, O Son of God, Who art risen from the dead, who sing unto Thee: Alleluia!"** (*Δεῦτε προσκυνήσωμεν καὶ προσπέσωμεν Χριστῷ· σῶσον ἡμᾶς Υἱὲ Θεοῦ, ὁ ἀναστὰς ἐκ νεκρῶν, ψάλλοντάς σοι· Ἀλληλούϊα*). Immediately thereafter follow the Dismissal Troparia (*Ἀπολυτίκια*) in strict hierarchical sequence: (1) the Resurrection Dismissal Troparion of the Tone; (2) the Troparion of the occurring Saint (if celebrated); (3) the Troparion of the Patron Saint of the temple/parish; and (4) the seasonal Kontakion (*Κοντάκιον*). The sacerdotal prayer of the Trisagion follows, leading into the choral chanting of the thrice-holy hymn: **"Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, have mercy on us"** (*Ἅγιος ὁ Θεός, Ἅγιος Ἰσχυρός, Ἅγιος Ἀθάνατος, ἐλέησον ἡμᾶς*), chanted thrice, followed by *Glory... Both now...*, the concluding verse, and the *Dynamis* (*Δύναμις*).

[^135]: The Liturgy of the Word: Epistle (*Apostolos*), Alleluia, and the Gospel Proclamation from the Ambo (Violakis, pp. 142–143, §3 cont. & Note 4). Following the Trisagion Hymn, the Liturgy of the Word commences with the Prokeimenon of the Apostle chanted by the Reader. The Deacon intones *"Wisdom! Let us attend!"* (*Πρόσχωμεν, σοφία, πρόσχωμεν*), and the Reader proclaims the appointed pericopes from the Acts of the Apostles or the Epistles (*Ὁ Ἀπόστολος*). At the conclusion of the reading, the Priest blesses the Reader with *"Peace be unto thee that readest"* (*Εἰρήνη σοι τῷ ἀναγινώσκοντι*), and the Second Choir chants the threefold *Alleluia* (*Ἀλληλούϊα*) in Tone 2 with its biblical psalm verses. The solemn Gospel sequence follows: the Priest or Deacon stands before the Royal Doors proclaiming *"Wisdom, stand upright! Let us hear the Holy Gospel: Peace be unto all!"*, and the Deacon ascends the Ambo (*Ἄμβων*) to proclaim the Holy Gospel (*Τὸ Εὐαγγέλιον*). Violakis's mention of the Ambo reflects the classical architectural heritage of Constantinople, where the ambo was an elevated platform in the nave from which scripture was chanted. At the conclusion of the Gospel proclamation, the First Choir sings the acclamation: *"Glory to Thee, O Lord, glory to Thee!"* (*Δόξα σοι, Κύριε, δόξα σοι*), leading directly into the Litany of Fervent Supplication (*Ἐκτενὴς Ἱκεσία*) and the Litany for the Catechumens.

[^136]: The Cherubic Hymn (*Cheroubikon*), the Great Entrance (*Megale Eisodos*), and the Kiss of Peace (Violakis, pp. 142–143, §3 cont.). After the dismissal of the catechumens and the two Litanies of the Faithful, the Priest chants the ecphonesis: *"That being always guarded under Thy dominion, we may send up glory unto Thee: to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages"* (*Ὅπως ὑπὸ τοῦ κράτους σου πάντοτε φυλαττόμενοι...*). The choirs then begin the Cherubic Hymn (*Χερουβικὸς Ὕμνος*), instituted under Emperor Justin II in 573 AD: **"We who mystically represent the Cherubim, and sing the thrice-holy hymn to the Life-creating Trinity, let us now lay aside all earthly cares: That we may receive the King of all, invisibly escorted by the angelic hosts. Alleluia, alleluia, alleluia"** (*Οἱ τὰ Χερουβὶμ μυστικῶς εἰκονίζοντες...*). During the hymn, the Great Entrance (*Μεγάλη Εἴσοδος*) takes place: the Holy Gifts (the Diskos bearing the Lamb and particles, and the Chalice containing the Wine and Water, covered by the Aer) are carried in solemn procession from the Table of Preparation (*Prothesis*) through the nave to the Holy Table (*Hagia Trapeza*), symbolizing Christ going forth to His voluntary Passion, Crucifixion, and Burial. The transfer concludes with the Litany of Supplication (*Πληρωτικά*), the greeting of peace (*Εἰρήνη πᾶσι*), and the Deacon's call to love: *"Let us love one another, that with one mind we may confess"* (*Ἀγαπήσωμεν ἀλλήλους, ἵνα ἐν ὁμονοίᾳ ὁμολογήσωμεν*), to which the choir responds: *"Father, Son, and Holy Spirit: the Trinity, consubstantial and undivided"* (*Πατέρα, Υἱόν, καὶ Ἅγιον Πνεῦμα, Τριάδα ὁμοούσιον καὶ ἀχώριστον*).

[^137]: The Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed, the Holy Anaphora, Words of Institution, Epiclesis, and the Diptychs (Violakis, pp. 144–145, §3 cont.). Following the ancient command *"The doors! The doors! In wisdom let us attend!"* (*Τὰς θύρας, τὰς θύρας· ἐν σοφίᾳ πρόσχωμεν*), the Superior (or the whole congregation) recites the Symbol of Faith (the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed). The Deacon then calls the assembly to Eucharistic reverence: *"Let us stand well; let us stand with fear; let us attend, that we may offer the Holy Anaphora in peace"* (*Στῶμεν καλῶς...*), to which the choir responds: *"A mercy of peace, a sacrifice of praise"* (*Ἔλεον εἰρήνης, θυσίαν αἰνέσεως*). The Priest inaugurates the Anaphora with the Pauline blessing (2 Cor. 13:14): *"The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God the Father, and the communion of the Holy Spirit, be with you all"*, followed by the ancient Eucharistic dialogue: *"Lift up your hearts"* (*Ἄνω σχῶμεν τὰς καρδίας*), *"Let us give thanks unto the Lord"* (*Εὐχαριστήσωμεν τῷ Κυρίῳ*), and the angelic Triumphal Hymn: **"Holy, Holy, Holy, Lord Sabaoth: heaven and earth are full of Thy glory..."** (*Ἅγιος, Ἅγιος, Ἅγιος Κύριος Σαββαώθ...*). The Priest then recites the Eucharistic Prayer of Thanksgiving, proclaiming the Words of Institution: **"Take, eat: this is My Body, which is broken for you for the remission of sins"** and **"Drink of this all of you: this is My Blood of the New Testament, which is shed for you and for many for the remission of sins."** Following the Anamnesis (**"Thine own of Thine own we offer unto Thee, on behalf of all and for all"**), the choir chants the solemn Epiclesis hymn: **"We hymn Thee, we bless Thee, we give thanks unto Thee, O Lord, and we pray unto Thee, our God"** (*Σὲ ὑμνοῦμεν, σὲ εὐλογοῦμεν, σοὶ εὐχαριστοῦμεν, Κύριε, καὶ δεόμεθά σου ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν*), while the Priest invokes the Holy Spirit upon the Gifts to accomplish the transformation of the Bread and Wine into the Precious Body and Blood of Christ. The Anaphora concludes with the commemoration of the Mother of God, accompanied by the Megalynarion **"It is truly meet to bless thee, O Theotokos, ever-blessed and most pure, and the Mother of our God..."** (*Ἄξιόν ἐστιν ὡς ἀληθῶς...*) chanted in Tone 2, followed by the Diptychs commemorating the Hierarch and the royal priesthood of the faithful.
