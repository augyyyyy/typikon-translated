Paragraph 66 Concluded: On Palm Sunday evening at the rite of Mutual Forgiveness during Compline, the Stavrotheotokion is chanted: "When the Virgin beheld Thy unjust slaughter, O Christ..." (Sphagēn sou tēn adikon); Dismissal: "Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee... May the Lord, coming unto His voluntary Passion for our salvation, Christ our true God..." Footnote 37 notes that this exact Dismissal formula is maintained throughout Passion Week until Great and Holy Wednesday at Matins and at the Divine Liturgy. Paragraph 67: Great and Holy Monday at Matins (The Service of the Bridegroom / Nymphios): After the initial blessing: "Blessed is our God always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages", the Superior [Proestos] says: "Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee. O Heavenly King, Comforter...", the Reader recites the Trisagion Prayers, the Priest gives the exclamation: "For Thine is the kingdom...", the Reader recites "Lord, have mercy" (12 times), "Glory... Both now and ever...", "O come, let us worship and fall down before our King and God..." (3 times), and the Royal Psalms: Psalm 19 LXX ("The Lord hear thee in the day of trouble...") and Psalm 20 LXX ("The king shall rejoice in Thy strength, O Lord..."). While these Psalms are being read slowly, the Priest censes the people with the hand-censer [katzion]. After the Trisagion Prayers and "For Thine is the kingdom...", the Troparia: "Save, O Lord, Thy people, and bless Thine inheritance...", "Glory...": "Thou Who wast voluntarily lifted up upon the Cross, O Christ God...", "Both now and ever...": "O awesome and unashamed protection...". The Dean or presiding Reader [Proistamenos] recites the Six Psalms. After the Great Litany, the Great Alleluia is chanted with its appointed verses from Isaiah 26:9 LXX: "From night until morning my spirit seeketh Thee early, O God, for Thy commandments are a light upon the earth", twice in slow melody and twice in brief melody. Then the solemn Dismissal Troparion of the Bridegroom in Tone 8: "Behold, the Bridegroom cometh in the middle of the night, and blessed is the servant whom He shall find watching..." (Idou ho Nymphios erchetai) three times (twice slowly and once rapidly). At the conclusion of the first time we say: "...through the protection of the Bodiless Hosts, save us!" (Prostasiais tōn Asōmatōn sōson hēmas); Footnote 38 notes that on Great Tuesday this ending is "...through the intercessions of the Forerunner, save us", and on Great Wednesday "...by the power of Thy Cross, save us". At the second time we join the commemoration of the Patron Saint of the temple: "...through the intercessions of Saint [N.] (or: the Saints), save us!"; and at the third time: "...through the Theotokos, have mercy on us!". Then the Kathismata of the Psalter: Footnote 39 records that while the daily Psalter Kathismata are commonly omitted in most parish churches during these Bridegroom Matins services either for the sake of brevity or because five Kathismata are assigned each day, they are read privately in the morning by the Priest; nevertheless it is devoutly to be wished that at least a portion of the five Kathismata be read publicly for the spiritual edification of the faithful, as the Psalter is the basis of church prayer (as noted in the Introductory Chapter [Protheoria] § 3, footnote 3). Following the Psalter, the three appointed Sessional Hymns [Kathismata] of the Triodion are chanted slowly in sequence: the first two once each, and the third twice. Immediately the Priest exclaims: "And that we may be accounted worthy to hear the Holy Gospel...".

Paragraph 67 Concluded: The Priest reads the Matins Gospel pericope: Matthew 21:18–43 (The Cursing of the Barren Fig Tree and the Parable of the Wicked Vinedressers). Footnote 40 details the patriarchal ceremonial during the Praises: at the singing of "Praise ye" [Aineite], the Ecumenical Patriarch or presiding Hierarch descends from his throne to venerate the holy Icons of the Passion of Christ, blesses the people while the choirs chant "Many years to you, Master!" (Eis polla etē), and ascends the small side-throne [parathronion]. Small Doxology: "To Thee belongs glory, O Lord our God..." read plain. After the litany: "Let us complete our morning prayer unto the Lord", the First Choir begins the Aposticha: "O Lord, the mother of the sons of Zebedee, unable to understand the ineffable mystery of Thy dispensation, requested of Thee the honor of a temporal kingdom for her sons..." with verses from Psalm 89 LXX. Then: "It is good to give praise unto the Lord...", the Trisagion Prayers, the Kontakion: "Jacob lamented the loss of Joseph...", "Lord, have mercy" (12 times); Footnote 41 references footnote 31 regarding the liturgical usage of this response. The Priest: "Blessed is He Who is, Christ our God...", the Reader: "Confirm, O God, the holy Orthodox faith...", the Prayer: "O Heavenly King, strengthen our faithful civil authorities...", the three Great Prostrations with the Prayer of Saint Ephrem the Syrian, and the solemn Passion Week Dismissal: "May the Lord, coming unto His voluntary Passion for our salvation, Christ our true God...". Footnote 42 records that this identical order of Matins is observed on Great Tuesday and Great Wednesday, whether celebrated in the morning or in the evening as Bridegroom Matins; if celebrated in the evening, the Psalter Kathismata are postponed to the morning.
