[^190]: Feast of the Conception of Saint Anna Concluded (Violakis, pp. 246–247 § 4) and the Sunday of the Holy Forefathers (§§ 1–2). Physical leaf p261 (printed page 246) and facing English leaf p262 (printed page 247) conclude the feast of the Conception by Saint Anna of the Most Holy Theotokos (December 9) by completing the Divine Liturgy propers (§ 4), assigning 4 Beatitudes to the Tone and 4 to Ode 6 of the Canon of Saint Anna, the Kontakion *Ἑορτάζει σήμερον ἡ Οἰκουμένη* ("Today the universe celebrateth"), and the Epistle Galatians 4:22–31. The text then establishes the liturgical cursus for the Sunday of the Holy Forefathers (the Ancestors of Christ according to the flesh, celebrated on the Sunday falling between December 11 and 17). This solemn dominical observance honors all the righteous patriarchs, righteous men, and holy women of the Old Covenant from Adam, Abel, Seth, Enosh, Enoch, Noah, and Melchizedek, through Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, the Twelve Patriarchs, Moses, Aaron, Joshua, Samuel, David, and all the Holy Prophets down to Saint John the Forerunner. At Saturday Great Vespers (§ 1), following the Psalter, at "Lord, I have cried" 6 Resurrection Stichira are joined with 4 of the Forefathers, concluding at "Glory" with the celebrated idiomelic Doxastikon in Tone 6 *Τοὺς πρὸ τοῦ νόμου Πατέρας* ("The Fathers before the Law"), at "Both now and ever" with the First Dogmatic Theotokion of the Tone, at the Aposticha with the Doxastikon *Τῶν Προπατόρων τὸ σύστημα* ("The assembly of the Forefathers"), and the Dismissal Troparion in Tone 2 *Ἐν πίστει τοὺς Προπάτορας ἐδικαίωσας* ("Through faith Thou didst justify the Forefathers"). At Sunday Matins (§ 2), the canons combine the Resurrection Canon with that of the Forefathers composed by Saint Germanus of Constantinople (*Πατράσιν αἶνον προσάξωμεν* / "Let us offer praise to the Fathers"); after Ode 3, the Hypakoe *Εἰς δρόσον τοῖς παισὶ* ("Unto dew for the Children") and the Resurrection Kontakion are recited; after Ode 6, the Kontakion of the Forefathers (*Χειρόγραφον εἰκόνα μὴ σεβασθέντες* / "Refusing to worship the graven image") and Ikos are chanted together with the Menaion Synaxarion and the special Hypomnema of the Sunday of the Forefathers; the Katavasiai of the Nativity (*Χριστὸς γεννᾶται* / "Christ is born, give ye glory") are sung; and at the Praises, 4 Resurrection Stichira are combined with 4 of the Forefathers (*Τὴν τῶν σεπτῶν* / "The memory of the venerable"), concluding with the idiomelon *Δεῦτε ἅπαντες πιστοὶ πανηγυρίσωμεν* ("Come, all ye faithful, let us feast").

The text then presents the feast of our Father among the Saints Spyridon the Wonderworker, Bishop of Trimythous in Cyprus (ca. 270–348), one of the 318 Holy Fathers of the First Ecumenical Council of Nicaea (325). When December 12 falls on a weekday (§ 1), the service is sung according to the Menaion. When it coincides with the Sunday of the Holy Forefathers (§§ 2–3), a complex rubrical integration is executed: Saturday Great Vespers assigns 4 Resurrection Stichira, 3 of the Forefathers, and 3 of Saint Spyridon at "Lord, I have cried", followed at "Glory" by the Doxastikon of the Forefathers (*Τοὺς πρὸ τοῦ νόμου Πατέρας*) and at "Both now and ever" by the First Dogmatic Theotokion of the Tone. Violakis notes that while the Great Church Typikon omits Old Testament lections for this concurrence, the printed Menaia recommend chanting the three readings of Saint Nicholas (from Dec 6). At the Aposticha, the Resurrection Stichira conclude at "Glory" with the Saint's Doxastikon *Ἱεραρχῶν τὸ θεῖον κειμήλιον* ("The divine treasure of hierarchs") and at "Both now and ever" with *Τῶν Προπατόρων τὸ σύστημα* ("The assembly of the Forefathers"). Physical leaf p267 (printed page 252) and facing English leaf p268 (printed page 253) detail the rubrics when December 20 coincides with the Sunday before the Nativity of Christ (the Sunday of the Holy Fathers). In this occurrence, the commemoration of Hieromartyr Ignatius is transferred and sung in advance on Saturday, December 19, combined with the commemoration of the Martyr Boniface of Tarsus. On Friday evening at Great Vespers (§ 2), 3 Prosomoia of Saint Ignatius and 3 of Saint Boniface are chanted at "Lord, I have cried", followed at "Glory" by *Θεοφόρε Ἰγνάτιε* ("God-bearing Ignatius") and at "Both now and ever" by *Ὁ Βασιλεὺς τῶν οὐρανῶν* ("The King of heaven"). Here Violakis inserts an important rubrical commentary in parenthetical text, observing that *Ὁ Βασιλεὺς τῶν οὐρανῶν* is the First Dogmatic Theotokion of Tone Plagal 4 (Tone 8) from Saturday Great Vespers, and highlighting the liturgical anomaly of why this specific Tone 8 Theotokion was prescribed in the Constantinopolitan tradition rather than the Theotokion of the Tone of the current week (as mandated by the General Rubrics in Protheoria § 8). On Saturday morning (§ 3), Matins and the Eucharistic Liturgy of the Saturday before Nativity are celebrated with the propers of Saint Ignatius and Saint Boniface (Galatians 3:8–12 and Luke 13:19–29). The text then introduces the Sunday before the Nativity of Christ (the Sunday of the Holy Fathers, celebrated between December 18 and 24, focusing on the genealogy of Christ and the Old Testament righteous), articulating Case 1 (§ 1) for when this Sunday falls early on December 18 or 19. [^194]: Sunday before the Nativity of Christ (The Holy Fathers, Case 1, §§ 2–3 concluded). Physical leaf p269 (printed page 254) and facing English leaf p270 (printed page 255) conclude Case 1 of the Sunday before the Nativity of Christ (when occurring on December 18 or 19). At Saturday Great Vespers (§ 1 concl.), the Doxastikon at "Lord, I have cried" is the idiomelic hymn in Tone 6 *Δανιὴλ ἀνὴρ ἐπιθυμιῶν* ("Daniel, a man of desires"), three Old Testament composite readings are read (Genesis 14:14–20, Melchizedek blessing Abram; Deuteronomy 1:8–11, 15–17, Moses appointing judges; and Deuteronomy 10:14–21, the majesty of God and circumcision of the heart), and the Dismissal Troparion in Tone 2 is *Μεγάλα τὰ τῆς πίστεως κατορθώματα* ("Great are the accomplishments of faith"). At Sunday Matins (§ 2), after the Polyeleos, the canon is the renowned Menaion composition *Κύματι θαλάσσης* ("By the wave of the sea"), borrowing the incipit and acrostic of the Great Saturday Canon. Violakis specifies a rare stichological directive: before the first Troparion following each Heirmos, the singers chant the Resurrection verse *Δόξα τῇ ἁγίᾳ Ἀναστάσει σου, Κύριε* ("Glory to Thy holy Resurrection, O Lord"), while before all subsequent Troparia they chant the hagiographical verse *Ἅγιοι τοῦ Θεοῦ, πρεσβεύσατε ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν* ("Saints of God, intercede for us"), concluding with "Glory" and "Both now and ever". After Ode 3, the Hypakoe is *Ἄγγελος Παίδων ἐδρόσισε κάμινον* ("An Angel cooled the furnace of the Children"); after Ode 6, the Kontakion and Ikos of the Fathers are sung; and at the Praises, 4 Resurrection Stichira are joined with 4 of the Fathers, concluding at "Glory" with *Τῶν νομικῶν διδαγμάτων ὁ σύλλογος* ("The assembly of legal teachings"). At the Divine Liturgy (§ 3), the Epistle is Hebrews 11:9–10, 32–40 (the cosmic catalogue of the faith of the Old Testament Saints, Abraham dwelling in tents, Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthae, David, Samuel, and the Prophets who through faith subdued kingdoms and stopped the mouths of lions) and the Gospel is Matthew 1:1–25 (the historical and theological Genealogy of Jesus Christ from Abraham through forty-two generations, culminating in the Virginal Conception and Birth of the Incarnate Word).
