[^525]: Physical leaf p932 (printed page 917), rubrics governing the concelebration of multiple presbyters during the Funeral Evlogitaria and the beginning of the Eight Idiomela of Saint John of Damascus (Tones 1–4). In Constantinopolitan practice, the deacon intones a petition after each troparion of the Evlogitaria, and each concelebrating priest takes turns pronouncing the exclamation according to seniority of ordination. The idiomela that follow constitute the classical Byzantine hymnographic meditation on mortality, vanity, and resurrection.

[^526]: Physical leaf p934 (printed page 919), concluding strophes of the Funeral Idiomela of Saint John of Damascus (Tones 5 through 8) and the Doxastikon in Tone 8 (*Ὁ θάνατός σου, Κύριε*). The hymns integrate biblical citations from the Prophets (Job 30:19; Genesis 18:27: *“I am but dust and ashes”*) with patristic theology regarding the composite constitution of human nature and the despoiling of Hades through the death and burial of Christ.

[^527]: Physical leaf p936 (printed page 921), rubrics for the Funeral Absolution prayers, the Paschal Funeral during Bright Week (Renewal Week), and the universal application of the funeral rite across all ecclesiastical and social orders. During Bright Week, mourning is superseded by the Paschal Canon and Paschal Stichera (*“It is the day of Resurrection”*). For deceased infants, the Damascene idiomela are omitted and replaced by the special euchological prayers for infants.

[^528]: Physical leaf p938 (printed page 923), mystical theology of Memorial Services (*Mnemosyna*) according to Saint Symeon of Thessalonica (3rd, 9th, 40th days, and 3-, 6-, 9-month commemorations), followed by the solemn colophon marking the conclusion of the 1888 Violakis Typikon text (*“ΤΕΛΟΣ ΚΑΙ Τῼ ΘΕῼ ΔΟΞΑ”* / *“The End and to God be Glory”*). Commemorations on the 3rd day honor the Holy Trinity; the 9th day invokes the nine angelic orders; the 40th day points to the Ascension and the General Resurrection.

[^529]: Physical leaf p940 (printed page 925), editorial introduction by Demos and Bogdanos to the perpetual Paschalion and Table of Feasts (*Πίναξ τοῦ Ἑορτοδρομίου*). The original 1888 edition of George Violakis calculated all movable feasts, Pascha, and fasting periods according to the Julian Calendar. Following the calendar reform of the 1920s, twentieth-century editors provided dual concordance dates for both the Julian Calendar (JC) and Gregorian Calendar (GC) to preserve the functional utility of the Typikon tables.
