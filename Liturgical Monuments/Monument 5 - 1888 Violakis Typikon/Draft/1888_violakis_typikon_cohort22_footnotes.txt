[^163]: Feast of the Holy Apostle and Evangelist Luke, Liturgical Propers, and Inter-Cohort Transition (Violakis, pp. 196–197, October 18, § 3 concl.). Physical leaf p211 (printed page 196) and facing English leaf p212 (printed page 197) begin Cohort 22 with the concluding propers of the Divine Liturgy for the feast of Saint Luke the Evangelist when celebrated on a Sunday, continuing directly across the cohort boundary from printed page 195 (leaf p210, Cohort 21). Following the Little Entrance, the dismissal troparia, and the Kontakion *Protection of Christians*, the appointed lections are: Epistle: Colossians 4:5–11, 14–18 (pericope 260, honoring Luke "the beloved physician"); Gospel: Luke 10:16–21 (pericope 51, the mission and return of the Seventy Disciples); at the Megalynarion: *It is truly meet* (*Ἄξιόν ἐστιν*); and the festal Communion Verse: Psalm 18:5 LXX (*Εἰς πᾶσαν τὴν γῆν ἐξῆλθεν ὁ φθόγγος αὐτῶν* / "Their sound hath gone forth into all the earth, and their words unto the ends of the world"). The service concludes with *We have seen the true light* and the customary dismissal.

[^164]: Commemoration of St. Artemios, St. Matrona of Chios, and St. Gerasimos of Cephalonia, Special Festal Booklets, and Sunday Concurrence (Violakis, pp. 196–199, October 20, §§ 1–4). October 20 unites the general Byzantine commemoration of the Holy Great-Martyr Artemios (beheaded under Julian the Apostate in A.D. 363) with two highly venerated post-Byzantine monastic saints whose offices developed in regional pamphlets (*ἰδιαίτεραι φυλλάδες*): the Righteous Mother Matrona of Chios (d. 1462, whose office was composed and popularized across the Aegean islands) and our Righteous Father Gerasimos the New Ascetic of Cephalonia (d. 1579, whose incorrupt relics in Omala form the monastic heart of the Ionian Islands). When October 20 falls on a weekday (§ 1), the first three Stichira at "Lord, I have cried" are sung for Saint Artemios from the general Menaion, followed by three Stichira for Saint Matrona or Saint Gerasimos from their respective booklets, with the Doxastikon assigned to whichever local patron is celebrated. When occurring on a Sunday (§§ 2–4), Saturday Great Vespers prescribes 4 Resurrection Stichira, 3 of Saint Artemios, and 3 of Saint Matrona or Saint Gerasimos; at Matins, the Liti is taken from the booklet of the righteous saint, followed by the Triadic hymns (*Ἄξιόν ἐστιν*) and the third Kathisma reading assigned to the saint; at the Divine Liturgy, the Beatitudes combine 4 of the Tone and 4 from Ode 6 of the Canon of the righteous saint, with the Epistle taken from the saint's office (Galatians 3:23–4:5 for a female ascetic or Galatians 5:22–6:2 for a venerable monk) and the Sunday Gospel.

[^165]: Feast of the Holy Apostle James, Brother of the Lord (Adelphotheos), Sunday Concurrence, and Apolytikion (Violakis, pp. 198–201, October 23, §§ 1–4). October 23 commemorates Saint James the Brother of the Lord (*Ἰάκωβος ὁ Ἀδελφόθεος*), first Bishop of Jerusalem, author of the Catholic Epistle of James, and martyr under the High Priest Ananus II in A.D. 62. On a weekday (§ 1), the office is sung plain from the Menaion. When the feast occurs on a Sunday (§§ 2–4), Great Vespers on Saturday evening appoints 6 Resurrection Stichira and 4 Stichira of the Apostle at "Lord, I have cried"; the Doxastikon is *Αἵματι τοῦ μαρτυρίου* ("With the blood of martyrdom"); at the Aposticha, the Doxastikon is *Τοῦ Ἀρχιποίμενος Χριστοῦ* ("Of Christ the Chief Shepherd"); and the Dismissal Troparion is the ancient troparion of Saint James: *Ὡς τοῦ Κυρίου μαθητὴς ἀνεδέξω, δίκαιε, τὸ Εὐαγγέλιον· ὡς μάρτυς ἔχεις τὸ ἀπαράτρεπτον· τὴν παρρησίαν ὡς ἀδελφόθεος· τὸ πρεσβεύειν ὡς ἱεράρχης* ("As a disciple of the Lord thou didst receive the Gospel, O righteous one; as a martyr thou hast that which cannot be changed; boldness as the Brother of God; and intercession as a hierarch"). At Matins (§ 3), after the Amomos (Psalm 118 LXX) and the Canons, the Katavasiai *Ἀνοίξω τὸ στόμα μου* are sung; at the Divine Liturgy (§ 4), the Beatitudes combine 4 of the Tone with 4 from Ode 6 of the Canon of the Apostle, the Epistle is Galatians 1:11–19 (Saint Paul recounting his apostolic calling and his meeting in Jerusalem with "James the Lord's brother"), and the Sunday Gospel is appointed.

[^166]: Feast of the Holy Great-Martyr Demetrios the Myrrh-Streamer and the Commemoration of the Great Earthquake at Constantinople (Violakis, pp. 200–205, October 26, §§ 1–8). October 26 is one of the most solemn feast days of the Byzantine calendar, combining the major martyrdom of Saint Demetrios of Thessalonica (martyred ca. A.D. 306 under Maximian Galerius) with the civil and liturgical commemoration of the cataclysmic earthquake that struck Constantinople on October 26, A.D. 740, under Emperor Leo III the Isaurian (the Violakis heading parenthetically cites 791, reflecting a recensional chronological discrepancy found in certain post-Byzantine liturgical printed synaxaria). At Vespers (§ 1), 6 festal Stichira of Saint Demetrios are sung at "Lord, I have cried", followed by three Old Testament lections (Prophecies: Isaiah 63:15–64:5, 8–9; Jeremiah 2:2–12; and Wisdom of Solomon 3:1–9); at the Aposticha, the penitential Idiomela of the Earthquake are sung, followed by the Doxastikon of the Saint (*Ἔχει μὲν ἡ θειοτάτη σου ψυχή*) and the Troparion of the Earthquake *Ὁ ἐπιβλέπων ἐπὶ τὴν γῆν καὶ ποιῶν αὐτὴν τρέμειν* (Psalm 103:32 LXX) substituted in place of the Theotokion. At Matins (§ 2), two canons are appointed: the Canon of the Earthquake in Tone 6 (by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer, with the penitential refrain *Δόξα σοι, ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν, δόξα σοι*) and the second Canon of the Saint in Tone 4 (by Saint Theophanes the Branded, having the acrostic *Μερίς μου, Δημήτριε*); the Troparion of the Earthquake replaces the Theotokion after Ode 3. At the Divine Liturgy (§ 3), the Epistle is 2 Timothy 2:1–10 ("Thou therefore, my son, be strong in the grace that is in Christ Jesus... Endure hardness, as a good soldier of Jesus Christ") and the Gospel is John 15:17–16:2 ("These things I command you, that ye love one another..."). Sections 4–8 provide detailed rubrics for Saturday and Sunday concurrences.

[^167]: Textual Criticism of the Praises Doxastikon of Saint Demetrios and Violakis's Editorial Defense (Violakis, pp. 204–205, October 26, § 7 note 1). This footnote reproduces in full the crucial critical annotation printed at the foot of pages 204–205 in the 1888 edition: "In the Typikon of Constantine [the Byzantios, Protopsaltes of the Great Church], published in 1851, a note appears here correcting the ending of this Doxastikon [at the Praises: *Τὸν λόγχαις κληρωσάμενον* / 'Pierced by spears'], which reads: 'the protector of the Thessalonians in many and frequent dire perils' (*τὸν ἐν πολλοῖς καὶ πολλάκις κινδύνοις χαλεποῖς τῶν Θεσσαλονικέων προϊστάμενον*), altering it to: 'the protector of all the pious in many and dire perils' (*τὸν ἐν πολλοῖς καὶ... χαλεποῖς πᾶσι τοῖς εὐσεβέσι προϊστάμενον*). But because this correction neither succeeds grammatically, nor is it permitted to alter hymns arbitrarily according to one's liking—especially those in which mention is made of events possessing historical significance—for these reasons it is recommended to the chanter that he preserve with all exactitude the text as it is found originally." George Violakis here establishes a primary methodological principle of Constantinopolitan liturgical editorial practice: ancient hymnographic texts recording historical topoi (in this case, Saint Demetrios's specific historical role as miraculous defender and patron of Thessalonica against barbarian sieges) must not be bowdlerized or universalized to satisfy modern stylistic taste.
