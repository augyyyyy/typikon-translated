[^110]: The Byzantine Eucharistic Communion Hymn System, Weekly and Festal Lections, and LXX Versification (Violakis, pp. 106–109, §46). Paragraph 46 codifies the comprehensive cyclical and hierarchy of the Eucharistic Communion Hymn (*Κοινωνικόν*, Slavonic *Prichasten*) chanted during the communion of the clergy and the faithful at the Divine Liturgy. In classical Byzantine euchology, the Communion Hymn represents an ancient responsorial psalmody whose scriptural verses accompany the distribution of the Holy Mysteries, culminating in the triple Alleluia. Violakis delineates four primary lectionary tiers: (1) Sunday Ordinary: The universal Dominical Communion Hymn is Psalm 148:1 LXX: **"Praise the Lord from the heavens, praise Him in the highest: Alleluia"** (*Αἰνεῖτε τὸν Κύριον ἐκ τῶν οὐρανῶν, αἰνεῖτε αὐτὸν ἐν τοῖς ὑψίστοις. Ἀλληλούϊα*), appointed on all ordinary Sundays, on the Feast of the Circumcision (January 1), and on the Vesperal Liturgies of Christmas and Theophany Eve conjoined with St. Basil. (2) Dominical and Marian Feasts: Great Feasts of the Lord suppress the Sunday lection in favor of proper Dominical verses (e.g. Pascha: **"Receive the Body of Christ; taste of the Fountain of immortality"**; Ascension: Psalm 46:6 LXX; Pentecost: Psalm 142:10 LXX; Exaltation: Psalm 4:7 LXX). For Feasts of the Theotokos and the Indiction (September 1), the standard Communion Hymn is Psalm 115:4 LXX: **"I will take the cup of salvation, and call upon the Name of the Lord: Alleluia"** (*Ποτήριον σωτηρίου λήψομαι*); on the Annunciation (March 25), this is replaced by Psalm 131:13 LXX: **"The Lord hath chosen Zion; He hath desired it for His habitation: Alleluia"** (*Ἐξελέξατο Κύριος τὴν Σιών*). (3) Celebrated Saints on Sunday: When major commemorations coincide with Sunday, proper verses replace *Aineite*: Forerunner: Psalm 111:6b LXX (**"The righteous shall be in everlasting remembrance"**); Apostles, Evangelists, and Hierarchs: Psalm 18:5 LXX (**"Their sound is gone out into all the earth..."**); Archangels (November 8): Psalm 103:4 LXX (**"Who maketh His angels spirits..."**); Mid-Lent and August 1: Psalm 4:7 LXX (**"The light of Thy countenance hath been signed upon us, O Lord"**); the four Sundays after Thomas: **"Receive the Body of Christ"**; All Saints: Psalm 32:1 LXX (**"Rejoice in the Lord, O ye righteous"**). (4) Weekday Feria Cycle: Monday (Angels, Ps. 103:4 LXX); Tuesday (Forerunner, Ps. 111:6b LXX); Wednesday (Theotokos, Ps. 115:4 LXX); Thursday (Apostles, Ps. 18:5 LXX); Friday (Holy Cross, Ps. 73:12 LXX: **"Thou hast wrought salvation in the midst of the earth, O Christ God"**); Saturday (Departed and All Saints, Ps. 64:5 LXX: **"Blessed are they whom Thou hast chosen and taken to Thyself, O Lord..."**). At the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts, the proper Communion chant is Psalm 33:9 LXX: **"O taste and see that the Lord is good: Alleluia"** (*Γεύσασθε καὶ ἴδετε ὅτι χρηστὸς ὁ Κύριος*), unless displaced by a celebrated Saint's verse.

[^111]: Post-Communion Hymnody (*Eidomen to Phos*) and Dominical Substitutions (Violakis, pp. 108–109, §47). Paragraph 47 defines the post-communion hymnody executed immediately after the faithful have received the Holy Mysteries, following the Deacon's invitation **"With fear of God, with faith and with love draw near"** (*Μετὰ φόβου Θεοῦ, πίστεως καὶ ἀγάπης προσέλθετε*) and the Priest's solemn blessing over the congregation: **"O God, save Thy people, and bless Thine inheritance"** (*Σῶσον, ὁ Θεός, τὸν λαόν σου, καὶ εὐλόγησον τὴν κληρονομίαν σου*). The normative hymn of the Byzantine Eucharistic liturgies of St. John Chrysostom and St. Basil the Great is the ancient Pentecostarion troparion in Tone 2: **"We have seen the true Light, we have received the Heavenly Spirit, we have found the true Faith, worshipping the Undivided Trinity: for He hath saved us"** (*Εἴδομεν τὸ φῶς τὸ ἀληθινόν, ἐλάβομεν Πνεῦμα ἐπουράνιον, εὕρομεν πίστιν ἀληθῆ, ἀδιαίρετον Τριάδα προσκυνοῦντες· αὕτη γὰρ ἡμᾶς ἔσωσεν*). Violakis establishes the liturgical displacements of this hymn across the liturgical year: (1) On Dominical Feasts and their Apodoses, on the Third Sunday of Great Lent (Veneration of the Cross), and on Lazarus Saturday, the Dismissal Troparion of the Feast is chanted instead. (2) On Holy and Great Thursday, the choir chants recitatively (*chyma*) the Troparion of the Mystical Supper: **"Of Thy Mystical Supper, O Son of God, accept me today as a communicant..."** (*Τοῦ δείπνου σου τοῦ μυστικοῦ*). (3) On Holy and Great Saturday, the choir chants in Tone 2 irmologically the Troparion of the Penitent Thief: **"Remember us also, O Compassionate One, as Thou didst remember the thief in the Kingdom of Heaven"** (*Μνήσθητι εὔσπλαγχνε καὶ ἡμῶν καθὼς ἐμνημόνευσας τοῦ λῃστοῦ ἐν τῇ βασιλείᾳ τῶν οὐρανῶν*). (4) Throughout Paschaltide (from Easter Sunday until its Apodosis), the Paschal Troparion **"Christ is risen from the dead..."** (*Χριστὸς ἀνέστη*) is sung daily. (5) From Ascension until its Apodosis, the festal Troparion **"Thou hast ascended in glory, O Christ our God..."** (*Ἀνελήφθης ἐν δόξῃ*) is sung. (6) On the Saturday before Pentecost (Soul Saturday), the troparion of the departed **"O Thou Who by the depth of wisdom..."** (*Ὁ βάθει σοφίας*) is sung. (7) On Pentecost Sunday, the Troparion **"Blessed art Thou, O Christ our God..."** (*Εὐλογητὸς εἶ Χριστέ*); *Eidomen to Phos* resumes on Monday of the Holy Spirit. (8) At the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts, *Eidomen to Phos* is never sung; the Reader or choir recites Psalm 33:2 LXX: **"I will bless the Lord at all times; His praise shall continually be in my mouth"** (*Εὐλογήσω τὸν Κύριον ἐν παντὶ καιρῷ*).

[^112]: The Post-Ambo Acclamation *Eiē to Onoma Kyriou* and Bright Week Displacement (Violakis, pp. 108–111, §48). Paragraph 48 governs the responsorial acclamation following the Prayer behind the Ambo (*Ὀπισθάμβωνος Εὐχή*): **"Blessed be the Name of the Lord, from this time forth and unto the ages"** (Psalm 112:2 LXX: *Εἴη τὸ ὄνομα Κυρίου εὐλογημένον ἀπὸ τοῦ νῦν καὶ ἕως τοῦ αἰῶνος*). In all three Byzantine Eucharistic Liturgies (Chrysostom, Basil, and Presanctified), this scriptural verse is chanted thrice in Tone 2 in recitative style (*chyma*). During Bright Week (from Easter Sunday through Bright Saturday) and on the Apodosis of Pascha, this scriptural acclamation is suppressed and replaced by the triple singing of the Paschal Troparion **"Christ is risen from the dead, trampling down death by death, and upon those in the tombs bestowing life"** (*Χριστὸς ἀνέστη*), likewise executed in Tone 2 *chyma*.

[^113]: Sacerdotal Benediction (*Eulogia Kyriou*) and Artoklasia Conjunction (Violakis, pp. 110–111, §49). Paragraph 49 highlights the solemn sacerdotal blessing imparted immediately before the Dismissal of the Divine Liturgy: **"The blessing of the Lord and His mercy come upon you, through His grace and love for mankind, always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages"** (*Εὐλογία Κυρίου καὶ ἔλεος ἔλθοι ἐφ’ ἡμᾶς, τῇ αὐτοῦ χάριτι καὶ φιλανθρωπίᾳ πάντοτε, νῦν καὶ ἀεὶ καὶ εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας τῶν αἰώνων*). Violakis notes that this same solemn apostolic benediction concludes the rite of the Blessing of the Loaves (*Artoklasia*) at Great Vespers. The text is derived from ancient Byzantine euchological formulas connecting priestly intercession with divine beneficence. Note on the Father Paul Doxology Standard: The Greek original concludes with the traditional liturgical doxology *πάντοτε, νῦν καὶ ἀεὶ καὶ εἰς τοὺς αἰῶνας τῶν αἰώνων*, which in the English facing text was translated loosely as "now, and ever, and unto ages of ages"; under MTS-1, this is standardized to the canonical UGCC form: *"always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages."*

[^114]: The Universal Liturgical Seal (*Di' Euchōn*) and Hierarchical Acclamation (Violakis, pp. 110–111, §50). Paragraph 50 treats the final liturgical seal (*ἐπισφράγισμα*) of Byzantine liturgical prayer: **"Through the prayers of our holy Fathers, Lord Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us"** (*Δι’ εὐχῶν τῶν ἁγίων Πατέρων ἡμῶν, Κύριε Ἰησοῦ Χριστὲ ὁ Θεός, ἐλέησον ἡμᾶς*). This formula concludes every liturgical office, private prayer rule, and Eucharistic Liturgy in the Byzantine tradition. Violakis details two critical rubrical distinctions: (1) Bright Week Paschal Variant: Throughout Bright Week, *Di' euchōn* is suppressed at both Vespers and the Liturgy. The Priest instead proclaims the triple Paschal greeting: **"Christ is risen!"** (*Χριστὸς ἀνέστη!*), the faithful responding: **"Truly He is risen!"** (*Ἀληθῶς ἀνέστη!*). The Priest then exclaims: **"Glory to His third-day Resurrection!"** (*Δόξα τῇ αὐτοῦ τριημέρῳ ἐγέρσει!*), the people answering: **"We worship His third-day Resurrection!"** (*Προσκυνοῦμεν αὐτοῦ τὴν τριήμερον ἔγερσιν!*), followed by the complete chanting of *Christos anesti*. (2) Hierarchical Rubric: Whenever an Archbishop, Metropolitan, or Patriarch presides at the service, the formula changes from the plural *holy Fathers* (*ἁγίων Πατέρων*) to the singular hierarchical invocation: **"Through the prayers of our holy Master [Bishop], Lord Jesus Christ our God, have mercy on us. Amen"** (*Δι’ εὐχῶν τοῦ ἁγίου Δεσπότου ἡμῶν, Κύριε Ἰησοῦ Χριστὲ ὁ Θεός, ἐλέησον ἡμᾶς. Ἀμήν*), acknowledging the presiding archpastor as the apostolic icon of Christ within the synaxis.

[^115]: Typikon of Weekday Daily Vespers and the Four Combination Rules (Violakis, pp. 112–115, §1 & Note 1). This section opens the second major division of the 1888 Violakis Typikon: the Order of Daily Services throughout the year (*Τυπικὴ Διάταξις τῆς καθ' ἡμέραν Ἀκολουθίας*). Paragraph 1 codifies the standard structure of weekday Vespers outside festal periods: (1) The Ninth Hour: Footnote 1 notes that the Ninth Hour precedes Vespers, referring back to Pre-theory §1 where its conjunction with Vespers was established. (2) Structure at *Kyrie ekekraxa*: A total of six Stichira: two from the first group of three in the Parakletike (Tone of the week), one from the second group of three in the Parakletike, and three of the commemorated Saint from the Menaion. Doxastikon / Theotokion / Stavrotheotokion (on Tuesday/Thursday evening). (3) The Four Rules (α΄–δ΄): Rule α΄: Saint with 6 stichira in Menaion without Doxastikon: 3 stichira of the Saint are omitted, and 3 stichira of the Parakletike precede the remaining 3 of the Saint. Rule β΄: Saint with 2 Doxastika: only the Saint's stichira are sung (each repeated twice to make 6), completely omitting the Parakletike. Rule γ΄: Two Saints commemorated with proper stichira: each Saint receives 3 stichira, omitting the Parakletike. Rule δ΄: Friday evening Vespers for Saturday: If the Saturday Saint has no Doxastikon, at *Doxa Kai nyn* we chant the First Dogmatic Theotokion of the Tone of the week. If the Saint has a Doxastikon, *Doxa* of the Saint and *Kai nyn* the First Dogmatic Theotokion. If the Saint has no Doxastikon, the Dismissal Troparion is *Apostles, Martyrs, and Prophets*, *Doxa: Remember O Lord*, *Kai nyn: Holy Mother*. (4) Vesting and Sanctuary Movement: At weekday Vespers, no Little Entrance is made (unless it is an Apodosis of a Dominical/Marian Feast). The Royal Doors remain closed throughout. The priest vests only in the epitrachilion, enters through the south deacon's door into the Sanctuary, and exits through the north door to cense during the chanting of *Kateuthyntheto*.

[^116]: Daily Midnight Office Conjunction with Matins and the Solemn Fortyfold Kyrie Intercession (Violakis, pp. 114–115, §2). Paragraph 2 governs the commencement of weekday Orthros and its seamless conjunction with the Midnight Office (*Mesonyktikon*): (1) Initial Enarxis: The Priest gives the opening blessing **"Blessed is our God..."**, followed by **"Glory to Thee, our God..."**, **"O Heavenly King..."**, the Trisagion Prayers, the ecphonesis **"For Thine is the Kingdom..."**, **"Lord, have mercy"** (twelve times), the Doxology (*Doxa, Kai nyn*), the triple invitation to worship (**"O come, let us worship..."**), Psalm 50 LXX, and the Midnight Office according to the Horologion. (2) Forefeast / Afterfeast Adaptations: On days of Forefeasts and Afterfeasts, the nocturnal Troparion **"Behold, the Bridegroom cometh at midnight"** (*Ἰδοὺ ὁ Νυμφίος*) is replaced by the Troparion of the Forefeast or Feast, and the concluding Troparia (*Mnesthēti Kyrie*) are replaced by the festal Kontakia. (3) Penitential Troparia and Temple Action: Immediately following the Mesonyktikon, the choir chants the Penitential Troparia (*Eleēson hēmas Kyrie*, *Doxa: Kyrie eleēson hēmas*, *Kai nyn: Tēs eusplanchnias tēn pylēn*). During the chanting of *Tēs eusplanchnias*, the curtain (*parapetasma*) behind the Royal Doors is drawn aside. The priest, vested in his epitrachilion, emerges from the Sanctuary, stands before the closed Holy Doors facing east, and intones the Great Litany of Supplication (*Ἐλέησον ἡμᾶς ὁ Θεός...*). (4) The Fortyfold Kyrie Eleison: At the petition for preservation from wrath, famine, pestilence, earthquake, flood, and foreign invasion, both choirs alternate in chanting **"Lord, have mercy"** in four sets of ten (forty times in total: 10 by First Choir, 10 by Second Choir, 10 by First Choir, 10 by Second Choir), reflecting the ancient penitential monastic discipline of the Byzantine Horologion.
