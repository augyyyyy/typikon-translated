[^143]: The Nativity of the Most Holy Mother of God, September 8: Festal Rank, Vigil, and Typikon Synthesis (Violakis, pp. 156–157, September 8, §§4–5). The Nativity of the Most Holy Theotokos (*Τὸ Γενέσιον τῆς Ὑπεραγίας Θεοτόκου*) on September 8 is the first of the Twelve Great Feasts of the Byzantine ecclesiastical liturgical year, commemorating the birth of the Virgin Mary to the righteous ancestors of God Joachim and Anna in fulfillment of ancestral prophecies. Under the patriarchal reform of George Violakis, the feast is ranked among the great feasts of the Mother of God celebrated with Great Vespers, Litē (*Λιτή*), and festive Matins. When celebrated on a weekday, Great Vespers appoints the First Stasis of the First Kathisma of the Psalter (*Blessed is the man*, Psalm 1 LXX), followed at "Lord, I have cried" by six Idiomela Stichira in Tone 6 composed by St. Sergius of Jerusalem and St. Stephen the Sabaite, doubled with "Glory... Both now...". The three Old Testament lessons (Genesis 28:10–17, Jacob's ladder; Ezekiel 43:27–44:4, the shut gate seen by the prophet; Proverbs 9:1–11, Wisdom hath builded Her house) provide the primary typological framework for Marian veneration in the Byzantine rite. At Matins, the celebration incorporates the Midnight Office, the Festal Litē, and the Polyeleos taken from Psalm 44 LXX (*Ἐξηρεύξατο ἡ καρδία μου λόγον ἀγαθόν*, "My heart overfloweth with a good word"), which constitutes the royal epithalamium typifying the Mother of God as the Queen seated at the King's right hand. Following the Anavathmoi (Tone 4, Antiphon 1) and the Prokeimenon (Psalm 44:18 LXX), the Matins Gospel is Luke 1:39–49, 56 (the Visitation of Mary to Elizabeth and the Magnificat), followed by the post-Gospel Idiomelon in Tone 2 (*Αὕτη ἡ ἡμέρα Κυρίου*). The hymnography features two canons: the First Canon composed by St. John of Damascus (Tone 2, Heirmos: *Δεῦτε λαοί*) and the Second Canon composed by St. Andrew of Crete (Tone 8, Heirmos: *Ἁρματηλάτην Φαραώ*), punctuated by the Katavasiai of the Exaltation of the Holy Cross (*Σταυρὸν χαράξας*). At Ode 9, the biblical canticles give way to the proper festal megalynaria and the Ninth Ode Heirmos (*Μυστικὸς εἶ Θεοτόκε*).

[^144]: The Divine Liturgy of the Nativity of the Theotokos: Proper Festal Antiphons and Zadostoinyk (Violakis, pp. 158–159, September 8, §6). On the Feast of the Nativity of the Theotokos, the Divine Liturgy replaces the ordinary weekday psalms and Typika with three proper Festal Antiphons derived primarily from Psalm 131 LXX and Psalm 45 LXX. The first antiphon incorporates verses emphasizing David's vow to build a sanctuary for God (Psalm 131:1, 6; Psalm 86:3; Psalm 45:6 LXX) with the Marian refrain: "Through the intercessions of the Theotokos, O Savior, save us." The second antiphon presents the messianic oath made unto David regarding the royal heir to his eternal throne (Psalm 131:11, 17, 13 LXX) with the christological refrain: "Save us, O Son of God, Who art wonderful in the saints, who sing unto Thee: Alleluia!" (which in Ruthenian and Slavic practice is alternatively rendered: "Who art wonderful in the saints" or "Through the prayers of the Theotokos"). The third antiphon proclaims the eternal habitation of God in Sion (Psalm 131:14; Psalm 45:5; Psalm 64:5 LXX), interspersed with the festal Dismissal Troparion: "Thy Nativity, O Theotokos Virgin..." (*Ἡ γέννησίς σου Θεοτόκε*, Tone 4). At the Little Entrance, the clergy and choirs chant the festal Entrance Verse: "O come, let us worship and bow down before Christ: Save us, O Son of God, Who art wonderful in the saints, who sing unto Thee: Alleluia!". In place of the ordinary Marian hymn *Axion Estin*, the Liturgy appoints the Zadostoinyk (*Ἀντὶ τοῦ Ἄξιόν ἐστιν*), consisting of the festal megalynarion and the Heirmos of the Ninth Ode of the Second Canon (Tone 8): "Virginity is foreign to mothers, and childbearing strange to virgins; yet in thee, O Theotokos, both were accomplished: wherefore all we generations of the earth unceasingly magnify thee" (*Ἀλλότριον τῶν μητέρων ἡ παρθενία*). The Communion Verse is Psalm 115:4 LXX: "I will take the cup of salvation, and I will call upon the name of the Lord. Alleluia" (*Ποτήριον σωτηρίου λήψομαι*).

[^145]: Sunday Concurrence with the Nativity of the Theotokos and Precedence of the Sunday before the Exaltation (Violakis, pp. 160–161, September 8, §§7–9). When September 8 falls on the Lord's Day, a complex liturgical concurrence occurs between the Resurrectional service of the prevailing Octoechos Tone, the Feast of the Nativity of the Theotokos, and the Sunday before the Exaltation of the Precious Cross (*Κυριακὴ πρὸ τῆς Ὑψώσεως*). At Saturday Great Vespers, the ten Stichira at "Lord, I have cried" are divided: four of the Resurrection and six Idiomela of the Feast, with the festal Doxastikon (*Σήμερον ὁ τοῖς νοεροῖς θρόνοις*) chanted at "Glory... Both now...". The Old Testament readings of the Feast are retained. At the Aposticha, the Resurrection Stichira take precedence, concluded by the festal Doxastikon: "Come, all ye faithful" (*Δεῦτε ἅπαντες πιστοί*). At Matins, the Polyeleos of the Feast is appended to the Psalter Kathismata, and the Sunday Anavathmoi are followed by the festal Prokeimenon and the festal Matins Gospel (Luke 1:39–49, 56). The Sunday chant *Anastasin Christou theasamenoi* is sung, followed by the festal Idiomelon at Psalm 50. In the hymnographic canon, the Resurrection Canon precedes both Canons of the Feast, and the Katavasiai remain those of the Precious Cross (*Σταυρὸν χαράξας*). At the Divine Liturgy, the proper Festal Antiphons are sung with the Sunday second-antiphon refrain: "Save us, O Son of God, Who art risen from the dead...". Crucially, Violakis specifies that the Epistle (Galatians 6:11–18: "God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ") and the Gospel (John 3:13–17: "No man hath ascended up to heaven, but He that came down from heaven...") are taken exclusively from the Sunday before the Exaltation, while the Zadostoinyk and Communion Verse remain those of the Nativity of the Theotokos.

[^146]: Commemoration of the Holy and Righteous Ancestors of God Joachim and Anna on Sunday and Afterfeast Progression (Violakis, pp. 162–163, September 9, §§10–13). September 9 observes the Synaxis of the Holy and Righteous Ancestors of God Joachim and Anna (*Σύναξις τῶν ἁγίων καὶ δικαίων Θεοπατόρων Ἰωακεὶμ καὶ Ἄννης*), celebrating the parents of the Mother of God on the day following her Nativity, according to the ancient patristic principle whereby the primary human instruments of a divine mystery are venerated on the morrow of the feast. On weekdays, their office is joined directly with the hymns of the Afterfeast from the Menaion. When September 9 coincides with Sunday (§§10–12), the service of the Lord's Day combines six Resurrection Stichira with four of the Ancestors of God (*Δεῦτε νῦν χορεύσωμεν*), crowned by their Doxastikon: "O blessed couple, Joachim and Anna" (*Ὦ μακαρία δυάς*), and the First Dogmatic Theotokion of the Tone. At the Aposticha, the Resurrection Stichira are sealed by the Doxastikon of the Saints: "They who from barren loins" (*Οἱ ἐξ ἀκάρπων λαγόνων*), and the Dismissal Troparia include the Resurrection Troparion, the Troparion of the Saints (*Οἱ ἐν χάριτι δικαιωθέντες* or their proper dismissal troparion), and the festal Troparion of the Nativity. At Matins, the Kathismata of the Feast replace the ordinary Theotokia, and the Canons join the Resurrection Canon with the Canon of the Saints, sealed by the Katavasiai of the Cross. At the Liturgy, the Beatitudes combine four of the Tone and four from Ode 6 of the Canon of the Saints, and the readings are those of the Sunday before the Exaltation. Paragraph 13 establishes the general rubric for the "Sunday after the Feast" (*Κυριακὴ μετὰ τὴν ἑορτήν*), when the Afterfeast continues through September 12.

[^147]: The Apodosis of the Nativity of the Theotokos on Weekdays and Sunday (Violakis, pp. 164–165, September 12, §§15–17). The liturgical cycle of the Nativity of the Theotokos culminates on September 12 with its Apodosis (*Ἀπόδοσις*), concluding the four-day Afterfeast period prior to the inauguration of the Forefeast of the Universal Exaltation of the Precious Cross on September 13. When the Apodosis falls on a weekday (§15), the entire service of September 8 is repeated verbatim with specific omissions reflecting its non-initiatory character: at Vespers, the Kathisma of the Psalter and the three Old Testament readings are omitted; at Matins, the Litē, the Polyeleos, and the festal Matins Gospel are omitted, while the full festal canons, Katavasiai, and praises are sung; at the Divine Liturgy, the festal antiphons and Zadostoinyk are repeated, but the Epistle and Gospel are appointed from the regular weekday cycle. When the Apodosis coincides with Sunday (§§16–17), it merges with the Sunday before the Exaltation. At Saturday Great Vespers, four Resurrection Stichira combine with six of the Feast, and the Aposticha join the Resurrection stichira with the festal aposticha. At Sunday Matins, the Resurrection Kathismata are intercalated with those of the Feast, the Canons combine the Resurrection and the Feast, and the Katavasiai of the Cross are chanted. The Liturgy unites the Sunday Beatitudes with Ode 6 of the Feast, the Epistle and Gospel of the Sunday before the Exaltation, and the Zadostoinyk and festal Communion Verse of the Nativity.
