[^55]: Prince Sviatoslav Davydovych (monastic name Nikola Sviatosha, c. 1080–1143), Prince of Lutsk and Chernihiv, son of Davyd Sviatoslavych and grandson of Grand Prince Sviatoslav II of Kyiv. In 1106 he renounced his princely rank and entered the Kyiv-Pechersk Monastery, becoming the first East Slavic prince to embrace monasticism. He served as gatekeeper, kitchen laborer, and orchardist, and funded the construction of the Holy Trinity Gate Church (Troitska Nadbramna tserkva) above the monastery gates.
[^56]: The three Old Testament readings for a Venerable Father (Wisdom 3:1–9; Wisdom 5:15–6:3; Wisdom 4:7–15) are indexed to folio 30 verso of the 1785 print (physical leaf p44), where common festal readings are printed in full.
[^57]: Slavonic typographical error in the 1785 Lavra print: *па́пене* (papene) appears on Folio 18 recto as an epigraphic misprint for *прпⷠне* (prepodobne, "O venerable").
[^58]: Saint Joasaph of India: The protagonist of the Christianized tale of Barlaam and Josaphat (*Повѣсть о Варлаамѣ и Іоасафѣ*), traditionally attributed to Saint John Damascene, who renounced his royal inheritance to pursue asceticism in the desert, serving as the quintessential patristic exemplar for Christian royalty renouncing secular rule.
[^59]: Prince Nikola Sviatosha served for three continuous years in the monastery kitchen (*пова́рня*) chopping wood and preparing meals for the brethren; when his brothers Princes Iziaslav and Vladimir urged him to cease such menial labor, he requested permission to remain for an additional year.
[^60]: Saint Nikola Sviatosha served for three continuous years as the monastery gatekeeper, sitting by the outer gates of the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra, strictly regulating entry and greeting all visitors with deep prostrations, regardless of their social rank.
[^61]: Following his three years as gatekeeper, Prince Nikola was assigned to the monastery refectory (*трапеза*), where he washed dishes, carried water, and set out food for the monks.
[^62]: At the end of his cycle of menial obediences, the superior and brethren directed Saint Nikola to work in the monastery garden and orchard (*вертоград*), where he tended trees and produced fruits for the community.
[^63]: The Syrian or Greek physician Peter, who had served as court physician to Prince Nikola before his tonsure, was sent by Nikola's brother Prince Iziaslav to admonish him to stop degrading himself through menial labor and harsh fasting; Saint Nikola instead converted Peter to monastic life.
[^64]: Saint Nikola Sviatosha had excavated a burial niche for himself in the Near Caves of Saint Anthony; when the former physician Peter fell mortally ill and wept that he had not prepared a tomb, Saint Nikola ceded his own prepared burial place to Peter.
[^65]: Codicological and hagiographical witness from the *Kyiv-Pechersk Paterikon*: Following the tonsure and death of the monk Peter, Saint Nikola Sviatosha lived an additional thirty years in the monastery without leaving its walls, falling asleep in the Lord around 1143.
[^66]: The contact relics of Saint Nikola Sviatosha: his wooden headrest, brass pectoral cross, and hairshirt (*власяница*). According to the Paterikon, during an illness of Prince Iziaslav, Saint Nikola sent him his hairshirt, which immediately restored the prince to health; Iziaslav later wore it into battle as an invulnerable armor.
[^67]: Saint Nestor the Chronicler (c. 1056–1114), monk of the Kyiv-Pechersk Monastery, disciple of Saint Theodosius, and author of the fundamental Ruthenian chronicle *The Tale of Bygone Years* (*Повѣсть временныхъ лѣтъ* / *Primary Chronicle*), as well as the *Life of Saint Theodosius* and the *Passion of Saints Boris and Gleb*.
[^68]: Saint Nestor entered the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra at the age of seventeen during the abbacy of Saint Theodosius, under whose spiritual direction he was trained in monastic ascetical discipline, later receiving the diaconate from Abbot Stephen.
[^69]: Reference to the miraculous foundation of the Great Lavra Church of the Dormition (1073): according to the *Paterikon*, dew fell upon the entire region except the foundation site, and fire fell from heaven to consume the dew and trace the foundation boundaries, an event witnessed by Saint Nestor as a young novice.
[^70]: Saint Nestor was ordained to the holy diaconate by Abbot Stephen (successor of Saint Theodosius), as recorded in his *Life* in the *Kyiv-Pechersk Paterikon*.
[^71]: In August 1091, under Abbot John, Saint Nestor was appointed to excavate and uncover the incorrupt relics of Saint Theodosius from the Far Caves, subsequently participating in their solemn translation into the Great Dormition Church on August 14.
[^72]: Saint Barlaam, first Abbot of the Kyiv-Pechersk Monastery (c. 1020–1065), son of the Kyivan boyar Ivan Yanovych; tonsured by Saint Anthony despite the fierce opposition of Grand Prince Iziaslav and his father; later appointed by Iziaslav as superior of the Monastery of Saint Demetrius in Kyiv. While returning from a pilgrimage to Constantinople and Mount Athos, he fell asleep in the Lord at the Holy Mountain Monastery in Volodymyr-Volynskyi; his relics were later translated to the Near Caves of Saint Anthony.
