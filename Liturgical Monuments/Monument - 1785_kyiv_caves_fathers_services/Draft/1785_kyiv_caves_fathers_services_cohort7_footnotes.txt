[^207]: The Archangel Michael, Supreme Commander (*Archistrategos*) of the Heavenly Hosts. In 1089, Bishop Ephraim of Pereyaslav consecrated the great stone Cathedral of Saint Michael the Archangel in Pereyaslav, renowned for its architectural magnificence, frescoed walls, and accompanying stone bath-house and infirmary.
[^208]: Saint Prochor the Lebednik (Orache-Eater / Goosefoot-Eater), monk of the Kyiv Caves Monastery (d. February 10, 1107). During the severe famine in Rus' brought on by Polovtsian raids, he gathered wild orache (*Atriplex*, Church Slavonic *loboda*), rubbed it into flour with his hands, and baked loaves to feed the starving inhabitants of Kyiv; during the salt embargo imposed by Prince Svyatopolk Izyaslavich, he miraculously transformed wood ashes into pure salt. See Kyiv Caves Paterikon, Discourse 31.

[^218]: Saint Isaac the Recluse of the Caves (d. February 14, ca. 1090). Formerly a wealthy merchant of Toropets named Chern, he gave away his wealth to the poor and monasteries, came to the Kyiv Caves, and was tonsured by Saint Antony. He entered strict reclusion in a tiny cave cell; after seven years, the adversary appeared to him disguised as Christ surrounded by demons in angelic splendor. Deceived into prostrating before the demon, Isaac fell gravely ill and paralyzed for two years, lovingly nursed by Saints Antony and Theodosius. Upon his miraculous recovery, he adopted holy folly (*yurodstvo*), endured the severe cold of winter, and gained complete power over the demons. See Kyiv Caves Paterikon, Discourse 36.
