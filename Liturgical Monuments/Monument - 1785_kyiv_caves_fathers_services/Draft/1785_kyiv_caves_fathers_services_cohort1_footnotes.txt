[^1]: *Poluchnik* (получникъ): 19th-century Ukrainian/Ruthenian administrative term denoting a pensioner or recipient of an allowance, here identifying the codex's private owner, Yevstafiy Savych, who recorded his ownership as a parental blessing on February 17, 1836.
[^2]: Catherine II ('the Great'), Empress of Russia (reigned 1762–1796). The imperial family members enumerated are the Grand Duke Paul Petrovich (later Emperor Paul I), his wife Maria Feodorovna (Sophie Dorothea of Württemberg), and their children Alexander (later Emperor Alexander I), Constantine, Alexandra, and Elena.
[^3]: Archimandrite Zosima (Valkevich), Archimandrite of the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra from 1762 to 1786. Under his tenure, the Lavra printing press issued numerous landmark liturgical editions.
[^4]: Engraver monogram (СФНМ) in Cyrillic, identifying the master engraver of the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra workshop who executed the Dormition copperplate/woodcut.
[^5]: The feast of the Synaxis of the Venerable Fathers of the Kyiv Caves who rest in the Near Caves (St. Anthony's Caves) is celebrated on the first Saturday following the Apodosis of the Elevation of the Holy Cross (September 21), which places it between September 22 and September 28 in the Julian calendar.
[^6]: *The bell-ringer strikes the klepalo* (Клиентъ звонитъ): The physical rubric uses *klient* (a Lavra monastic colloquialism/corruption of *klepalo*, semantron or bell-ringer) directing the ringer to sound the klepalo/bells for Great Vespers. The 20th Kathisma antiphon refers to the final kathisma of the liturgical Psalter (Psalms 134–142, "O give thanks unto the Lord"), prescribed for Friday evening Vespers.
[^7]: *Ruthenian land* (рѡссійская земле): The historical Kyivan recension applies this term to the lands of Kyivan Rus' and Ukraine, here celebrated as the spiritual cradle of the Caves monastics.
[^8]: The rubric directs the choir to the Dismissal Theotokia (*Otpostitelny*) printed at the end of the codex on folio 151 (physical leaf p155).
[^9]: The three festal Paremias for venerable monastics are taken from the Septuagint text of the Wisdom of Solomon: Wisdom 3:1–9; Wisdom 5:15–6:3; Wisdom 4:7–15.
[^10]: The melody model *Tone 3, Podoben: "Thou Art the Trinity"* (Троица еси) governs the Sessional Hymn after the first Kathisma.
[^11]: The Gospel reading for monastic hierarchs and ascetics: Matthew 4:23–5:1 (Slavic Pericope 10), recounting Christ teaching and healing throughout Galilee, followed by the ascent unto the mountain before the Sermon on the Mount.
[^12]: Saint Barlaam, first Abbot of the Kyiv Caves Monastery (commemorated November 19), who left boyar rank and wealth to accept the monastic tonsure under Saint Anthony.
[^13]: Saint Isaiah of the Caves, later Bishop of Rostov (commemorated May 15, d. 1090), and Saint Simon, Bishop of Vladimir and Suzdal (commemorated May 10, d. 1226), one of the principal epistolary compilers of the Kyiv Caves Paterikon.
[^14]: Saint Ilya of Murom (St. Elijah of Murom, commemorated December 19, d. c. 1188), the epic warrior of Kyivan Rus' who took monastic vows in the Kyiv Caves Monastery; his incorrupt relics in the Near Caves bear an unhealed spear wound in the left hand and chest.
[^15]: Saint Nikon the Dry (commemorated December 11, early 12th century), captured by the Polovtsians and held captive for three years under severe torment, refusing ransom and bearing torture with extraordinary endurance until he was miraculously delivered.
[^16]: Saint Prokhor the "Orache-Eater" (*Lebednik*, commemorated February 10, d. 1107), who gathered wild orache (*chenopodium*) to bake bread for the starving populace during the severe Kyiv famine and miraculous transformed wood ashes into pure white salt.
[^17]: Saint Mercurius, Bishop of Smolensk (commemorated August 7, d. 1239), whose body according to the Kyiv Caves tradition miraculously floated up the Dnieper River in a wooden coffin to the Lavra, where his relics were laid to rest in the Near Caves.
[^18]: Saint Nestor the Chronicler (commemorated October 27, c. 1056–1114), monk of the Kyiv Caves and renowned chronicler, author of the *Primary Chronicle* (Tale of Bygone Years) and the hagiographical lives of Saints Boris and Gleb and Saint Theodosius of the Caves.
[^19]: Hieromartyr Eustratius of the Caves (commemorated March 28, d. 1096), captured during a Polovtsian raid on Kyiv, sold into slavery in Chersonese (Crimea), and crucified on Pascha for refusing to renounce Christ, compared in the hymn unto the Prophet Elijah ascending to heaven in a fiery chariot.
