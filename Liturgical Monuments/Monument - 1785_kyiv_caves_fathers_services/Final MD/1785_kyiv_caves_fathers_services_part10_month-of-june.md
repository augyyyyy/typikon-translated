# MONTH OF JUNE

*On the 1st Day:*
*Service to the Venerable Agapitus of the Caves, Unmercenary Physician:*[^372]
*At Vespers, at "Lord, I have cried", Stichera: Tone 4, Podoben: "As one valiant" (Я́кѡ до́бля):*[^373]
Having abundantly received from on high the gift of spiritual grace, O venerable Agapitus, thou dost water all as with river streams, and by thy prayers thou grantest healing unto all that flee unto thee with faith;

*(Right page — Folio 90 recto, running header: TO THE UNMERCENARY PHYSICIAN OF THE CAVES:)*
therefore we also, taking courage, bow down before the reliquary of thy relics, and pray: grant thou healing unto our souls and bodies!
Subduing the irrational dart by purity of mind and by unceasing standings in prayer unto the spirit, O all-blessed one, thou didst receive the grace of healing, and dost lavishly grant help unto all that ask of thee; therefore we also, honoring thy falling asleep, pray thee fervently: as an unmercenary physician, grant healing unto our souls and bodies!
The reliquary of thy relics, O father, venerable Agapitus, is shown forth as a radiant heaven, displaying wonders like light-bearing stars, and granting grace-filled healing unto those that flee unto it with hope; therefore having hope, we also pray thee fervently: grant assistance in passions unto our souls and bodies!
*Glory... Tone 2:*
Having received the grace of healing from on high while yet living in this brief life, O all-blessed Agapitus, thou didst grant healing unstintingly unto all that asked; having now been accounted worthy of greater grace, standing before the throne of the Most High, send down fiery gifts upon us who bow down before thy many-wonderworking relics, and who honor thy memory with praises, that through thy prayers unto God, O father, we may receive healing for our infirmities!
*Both now... Theotokion, or Stavrotheotokion.*
*At the Aposticha, from the Oktoechos:*
*Glory... Tone 3:*
Gushing forth streams of healings from spiritual gifts, O glorious one, unto those held by many passions who require thy protection, grant swift help from on high, refreshing with streams of thy wonders and driving away the diseases brought on by the evil one, that drawing healings, we may all glorify the Heavenly Father, and magnify His Co-beginningless Son with His Co-eternal Spirit, and call thee all-blessed, O Father Agapitus!
*Both now... Theotokion, or Stavrotheotokion.*
*Troparion, Tone 5:*
Emulating the humble-mindedness of the God-bearing Anthony, as with medicine Thou didst heal the sick with edible herbs, O venerable Agapitus;[^374] thereby having also convinced the unbelieving physician, Thou didst guide him unto the path of salvation: heal also our infirmities, and pray unto the Lord on behalf of those who hymn thee![^375]

*(Left page — Folio 90 verso, running header: SERVICE TO THE VENERABLE AGAPITUS:)*
*Canon to the Venerable One, Tone 6.*
*Ode 1. Heirmos:*
Traversing the deep on foot as on dry land, and seeing the pursuer Pharaoh drowned, Israel cried: Let us sing a song of victory unto God![^376]
*Troparia:*
As Thou didst valiantly enable the mute to speak uprightly by a touch, so also, O my Christ, loose Thou my tongue and open my mind, that hymning with love, I may offer praise unto the memory of Thy favorite!
I cannot hymn thy grace, O venerable one, unless thou by thy prayers grant me help, that hymning in praise, I may glorify the Good One Who gave thee the grace of healings!
By foreknowledge wast thou called, O father, to walk in the footsteps of thy Master; and following Him, having left all the beautiful things of the world, in love thou didst come unto great Anthony, and didst live with him, singing unceasing praise unto God![^377]
*Glory...*
Following the earthly angel and heavenly man, thou thyself didst acquire the angelic life, O venerable father; therefore we honor thy righteous deeds, and singing a hymn unto Him concerning thee, we glorify the Master of all!
*Both now... Theotokion:*
O Virgin ever praised by earthly and heavenly hosts, preserve from all assaults those who dare to offer praise unto thee on this present day, that we may ever hymn thee, O woman full of the grace of God!

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*
There is none holy as Thou, O Lord my God, Who hast exalted the horn of Thy faithful, O Good One, and established us upon the rock of Thy confession![^378]
*Troparia:*
Through many years thou didst pass through manifold labors, O Agapitus, struggling in vigil and fasting, until thou wast shown forth of one mind with thine instructor, and wast established by him upon the rock of a steadfast life.
Possessing nothing else than poverty, following Christ Who impoverished Himself for our sake, O father, and keeping His commandments, thou didst serve the sick, visiting and giving them rest, and granting them health by thy prayer.[^379]
Having lived with the venerable, thou didst acquire holiness, O praiseworthy Agapitus, and wast accounted worthy of gifts of healing, so that by prayer and edible herbs thou didst grant health unto the sick; therefore thou wast also called an unmercenary physician!
*Glory...*

*(Right page — Folio 91 recto, running header: TO THE UNMERCENARY PHYSICIAN OF THE CAVES:)*
Thou didst glorify God in thy members, O venerable one; therefore God also glorified thee with the grace of healing, wherewith heal also our infirmities of soul and body, that we may be established upon the rock of the confession of God!
*Both now... Theotokion:*
O most certain living Tree: by the fruit thereof make us who are dead through sin to live again, and grant unto us, O pure Mother, a steadfast confession of thy goodness!

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 3:*
Freely having received the gift of healing from God, thou grantest healing freely unto those that suffer, who flee unto thee with faith; therefore we also, bowing down before the reliquary of thy relics, O venerable father, pray to receive from thee healing for our infirmities!
*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*
Fleeing unto thy loving-kindness in sorrows, we pray thee, O all-immaculate one: for the sake of thy venerable one, grant swift healing unto us who are held by infirmities!

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*
Christ is my power, God and Lord, the honorable Church divinely sings, crying aloud from a pure mind, keeping festival in the Lord![^380]
*Troparia:*
Fleeing this world, Christ chose thee to put the wise to shame; which also came to pass in thee, O all-blessed father: for by grace and not by cunning didst thou put the most wise physician to shame; therefore keeping festival in the Lord, we glorify thee![^381]
He was deceived who formerly deceived the sick, predicting unto them the day and hour of death; whom reproving, O venerable one, thou didst heal the sick man by prayer and edible herbs, saying: "Give glory unto God, Who holds the key of life and death!"[^382]
Wounded by the arrow of envy, the wicked Armenian reviled thee, O holy one; and having sent a condemned man, he commanded him to be slain before thee by deadly herbs; but thou, having healed him by prayer and herbs, didst put to shame the heretic who wished to put thee to shame![^383]
*Glory...*
The grace given unto thee became a stumbling block for the falling, O all-blessed one: for the wicked Armenian, arming himself against thee most of all, plotted to give thee poison; but though thou didst drink a deadly thing, thou wast unharmed, for the grace of God preserved thee for greater wonderworking![^384]
*Both now... Theotokion:*
Rejoice, O vessel that didst bear the myrrh that healed the human race! Give life unto me who am slain by the poison of sin, that having lived sound for the rest of my time, I may praise thee, O Virgin!

*(Left page — Folio 91 verso, running header: SERVICE TO THE VENERABLE AGAPITUS:)*
*Ode 5. Heirmos:*
With Thy divine light, O Good One, illuminate with love the souls of those who rise early unto Thee, I pray, to know Thee, O Word of God, as the true God, Who callest them from the darkness of sin![^385]
*Troparia:*
Having received the grace of healing from God, Thou didst heal the prince,[^386] who though he was treated by the Armenian physician, obtained no relief; but having tasted of thine herb, he was delivered from his infirmity!
The monastics beholding it were stirred unto the virtuous endurance of the God-bearing father, seeing that with the renunciation of the world he renounced his own will, and never went outside the monastery gates; seeing which, emulate him!
Having one love toward all, and not regarding persons, whether prince or commoner came unto thee, O venerable one, thou didst grant healing freely unto all that came, seeking reward from God alone.
*Glory...*
The prince having received healing through thee, O all-blessed one, understood that thou art a true servant of God, and desired to see thee; therefore he came unto thee with gifts; but thou, having God alone, didst not desire to receive anything from those gifts![^387]
*Both now... Theotokion:*
We praise thee, O most pure Virgin, for thou openest unto us the doors of thy mercy, and grantest unto all an unstinting fountain of healing, delivering from the poison of sin!

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*
Beholding the sea of life surging with the storm of temptations, and fleeing unto Thy calm haven, I cry unto Thee: Lead up my life from corruption, O Most Merciful One![^388]
*Troparia:*
Having forsaken all corruptible things of this world, thou didst cast out the gold brought unto thee before the doors of thy cell, O blessed one, fleeing the snares of the money-loving enemy, and didst crush his soul-destroying head![^389]
Having acquired the one pearl of great price, Jesus, thou didst account all things as refuse, O father, and thereby didst purchase for thyself a dwelling in the heavens, which we also pray to obtain by thy prayers, O father![^390]
Fasting through many years, Thou didst grow weak in body, O venerable one, yet in spirit Thou didst remain steadfast like a pillar, enduring all things brought upon thee, and giving thanks unto God, unto Whom Thou didst cry: Lead up my life from corruption, O Most Merciful One!
*Glory...*
Sick in body, in spirit thou didst contend with the wicked Armenian, who foretold death for thee after three days; whom thou, O blessed one, didst overcome, having announced thy departure unto God after three months![^391]

*(Right page — Folio 92 recto, running header: TO THE UNMERCENARY PHYSICIAN OF THE CAVES:)*
*Both now... Theotokion:*
The Word Who dwelt in thy womb, O all-immaculate Virgin, settled the human race in the heavenly courts: entreat Him to translate me from the corruption of this life, whither the saints feed upon the stream of delight!

*Kontakion, Tone 6:*[^392]
Being an excellent physician, O marvelous one, displaying thy unquestioned faith, and reproving the Armenian's unbelief, and bringing him unto piety: when thou thyself wast near unto death, thou didst ask of God a lengthening of life, and having convinced him by this wonder, didst bring him unto Christ; before Whom standing in gladness, pray for us, O venerable one!

*Ode 7. Heirmos:*
An angel made the furnace dew-dropping for the venerable youths, but the command of God consuming the Chaldeans persuaded the tyrant to cry: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers![^393]
*Troparia:*
Having the gift from on high to heal infirmities, O venerable one, heal me who am sick through negligence of good deeds, as once thou didst heal him that was brought in sickness, granting him health by prayer, and didst sing: Blessed is the God of our fathers!
The Creator worked wonders through thy holy deeds: suddenly He set thee sound before the wicked one, who seeing it was amazed at thy swift change from sickness unto health: lead me who am tormented by the wounds of shameful deeds unto change of correction, through thy visitation from on high, O father, that I may sing: Blessed is the God of our fathers!
Thy sickness and the edible herb compelled the physician to confess his impiety, whom thou didst reproach for daring to condemn the saints: behold, I also dare to approach unto thee, O father; but I pray thee, cast me not away in my necessities, that I may sing: Blessed is the God of our fathers!
*Glory...*
Having gathered the fruits of thy labors with gladness like a good husbandman, by a blessed end thou didst depart unto the higher things, to receive reward from Christ God, and didst receive unending joy from Him, O many-wonderworking Agapitus!
*Both now... Theotokion:*
Fleeing unto thy haven, O Virgin Bride of God, tossed by the waves of worldly passions, do thou calm them, that we may sing unto thee: Rejoice, haven of worldly voyagers!

*(Left page — Folio 92 verso, running header: SERVICE TO THE VENERABLE AGAPITUS:)*
*Ode 8. Heirmos:*
Thou didst cause dew to drop from the flame for the venerable ones, and didst consume the sacrifice of the righteous with water; for Thou doest all things, O Christ, merely by willing them: Thee do we supremely exalt unto all ages![^394]
*Troparia:*
Thou wast a most marvelous physician, O father, healing bodily diseases while living in the flesh in this life, and after thy falling asleep granting spiritual medicine, as thou didst show forth this upon the Armenian, granting healing unto his soul and instruction unto piety![^395]
Having seen thee in a marvelous vision, O holy one, the Armenian confessed clearly that thou art holy and venerable; and having turned from unbelief unto the faith, he became a monk by thy guidance: of which deprive not us who hymn the Lord unto the ages![^396]
Having a great gift, thou didst work wonders, healing the sick; and having now been accounted worthy of greater gifts, apply a plaster unto the wounds of my soul, that having obtained relief, I may supremely exalt the Lord unto the ages!
*Let us bless the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit, the Lord.*
O Christ God, Who grantest streams of grace of flame-like light unto the saints, quench the passions that burn me with the dew of Thy mercies, driving away the darkness of passions: the venerable one entreats Thee with us, the Lord Who art supremely exalted by all unto the ages!
*Both now... Theotokion:*
O thou who didst enlighten the world by thy most pure childbearing, dispel the darkness of my evildoing, pouring out upon me the rich mercy of thy compassions unto well-doing, that I may supremely exalt thee, the Mother of God, unto the ages!

*Ode 9. Heirmos:*
It is impossible for men to see God, upon Whom the angelic ranks dare not gaze; but through thee, O all-pure one, the Word was made manifest unto men incarnate: Whom magnifying, with the heavenly hosts we call thee blessed![^397]
*Troparia:*
The people possessing a divinely-gifted healer, let no one go unto vain physicians who harm property and health; but let him that is in need come with faith unto the reliquary of the God-bearing father, and he shall receive healing freely!
Noetic robbers have now wounded me, having truly incurable wounds: with unhesitating faith we fall down before thy many-wonderworking reliquary, O venerable one, and pray: grant thou healing unto those that need, that we may all praise and magnify thee!
Having ascended with thy soul unto the higher things, into the all-radiant mansions, thou didst leave the reliquary of thy relics in the cave, O all-blessed one:

*(Right page — Folio 93 recto, running header: TO THE UNMERCENARY PHYSICIAN OF THE CAVES:)*
bowing down wherebefore, we pray: grant healing unto our infirmities, that we may magnify thee in hymns!
*Glory...*
Despise not those who ask help of thee in infirmities, but by thy visitation grant healing of soul together with body, O all-blessed Agapitus, as an excellent physician, that we may ever magnify thee!
*Both now... Theotokion:*
Despise not those who require help from thee, O Virgin, but grant it unto those who flee unto thee with faith; for thou art an unfailing fountain of healings, that with the heavenly powers we may ever magnify thee!

*Exapostilarion:*
Having forsaken the corruptible things of the world, and been accounted worthy to receive incorruptible things, thou didst hasten to exchange the worse for the better, having been impoverished through passionate love of possessions, but enriched with the gift of healings; and grant these things unto us who honor thy memory this day, O all-blessed Agapitus!
*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*
Saved through thee, we confess thee to be the pure Mother; for thou didst bring forth Him Who destroyed death by the Cross, and ascended on high, drawing all things unto Himself, and granting great mercy unto us!

*At the Praises, Stichera: Tone 1, Podoben: "Thou hast given a sign" (Да́лъ єсѝ зна́менїе):*[^398]
Having withered the passionate uprisings of the flesh by ascetic labors, and ascended unto the height of virtues, thou wast shown forth brighter than the sun, shining with the grace of healings, and enlightening with rays those who come unto thee; therefore we pray thee, O venerable one, grant healing of infirmities unto those who lovingly honor thy memory!
Having received from on high a rich gift from the One Lover of mankind, thou didst distribute it unstintingly unto those in need, granting them healing; but now also send down healing in infirmities upon those who fall down before the reliquary of thy relics, as a true physician, O all-blessed one, and intercessor for our souls!
Having assembled in faith and love in memory of the worthy-blessed father, let us praise him with voices of song, that we may be accounted worthy of healing in our weaknesses from him; for he has *(Left page — Folio 93 verso, running header: SERVICE TO THE VENERABLE AGAPITUS:)*
...for he has swift compassion, and grants healing as an unmercenary physician and swift helper in sorrows!
*Glory... Tone 2:*
Endless is Thy grace, O Christ the King, and great is the gift which Thou hast bestowed upon Thy venerable one; for not only while living in the body did he heal infirmities, but even after death by Thy divine power the reliquary of his unhidden relics grants healing unto those that come with faith. Therefore we pray Thee, O Good Master, bringing Thy saint unto propitiation, according to Thy great goodness deliver us from infirmities of soul and body, and save our souls, as the Lover of mankind!
*Both now... Theotokion.*
*Great Doxology, and Dismissal.*

### Notes

[^372]: Saint Agapitus the Unmercenary Physician of the Kyiv Caves (d. ca. 1095), disciple of Saint Anthony, commemorated June 1. His relics lie incorrupt in the Near Caves (St. Anthony's Caves).
[^373]: Melodic model heading: *Tone 4, Podoben: "As one valiant" (Я́кѡ до́бля)*.
[^374]: Agapitus gathered the wild herbs that served him for food, boiled them, and gave them to the sick as "medicine," concealing the miracle of healing behind ordinary food.
[^375]: Troparion of Saint Agapitus of the Caves, Tone 5.
[^376]: Heirmos of Ode 1, Tone 6 (Exodus 14:21–31).
[^377]: Agapitus (Greek Ἀγαπητός, "beloved") was tonsured by Saint Anthony of the Caves.
[^378]: Heirmos of Ode 3, Tone 6 (1 Kings / 1 Samuel 2:2).
[^379]: Discourse 27 of the Kyiv Caves Paterikon relates that Agapitus never accepted any gold, silver, or gifts from those he healed, distributing everything to the poor.
[^380]: Heirmos of Ode 4, Tone 6 (Habakkuk 3:1–19).
[^381]: 1 Corinthians 1:27. Referring to the Armenian physician in Kyiv who was famous for diagnosing terminal illnesses by feeling a patient's pulse.
[^382]: Revelation 1:18. When the Armenian predicted that a sick boyar would die within eight days, Agapitus gave the boyar herbs from his cell and healed him.
[^383]: When the Armenian sent a man condemned to death to be given lethal poison, Agapitus gave him herbs and healed him, exposing the Armenian's plot.
[^384]: Mark 16:18: "And if they drink any deadly thing, it shall not hurt them." The Armenian tried to poison Agapitus himself, but Agapitus drank the poison unharmed.
[^385]: Heirmos of Ode 5, Tone 6 (Isaiah 26:9).
[^386]: Prince Vladimir Vsevolodovich Monomakh (1053–1125), then Prince of Chernihiv, fell mortally ill; the Armenian physician could not cure him, but Saint Agapitus sent him herbs from the Caves, and the prince was immediately healed.
[^387]: Vladimir Monomakh came in person to the Kyiv Caves Lavra to thank Agapitus and brought him rich gifts of gold and silver, but Agapitus refused to see him or accept any gifts.
[^388]: Heirmos of Ode 6, Tone 6 (Jonah 2:1–10).
[^389]: A boyar whom Agapitus healed brought him a large sum of gold; Agapitus threw it outside the door of his cell, refusing to keep any worldly wealth.
[^390]: Matthew 13:45–46; Philippians 3:8.
[^391]: When Saint Agapitus fell ill, the Armenian visited him, took his pulse, and stated he would die within three days, pledging to become an Orthodox monk if Agapitus survived; Agapitus told him the Lord revealed he would live three more months, which came to pass.
[^392]: Kontakion of Saint Agapitus of the Caves, Tone 6.
[^393]: Heirmos of Ode 7, Tone 6 (Daniel 3:49–50).
[^394]: Heirmos of Ode 8, Tone 6 (Daniel 3:57–88; 1 Kings / 1 Samuel 18:38).
[^395]: After Saint Agapitus reposed, he appeared in a vision to the Armenian physician, which brought about his conversion.
[^396]: The Armenian physician came to Hegumen John of the Caves, confessed his sins, asked for baptism and the monastic tonsure, and lived out his life as a monk in the Caves.
[^397]: Heirmos of Ode 9, Tone 6 (Luke 1:46–55).
[^398]: Melodic model heading: *Tone 1, Podoben: "Thou hast given a sign" (Да́лъ єсѝ зна́менїе)*.
