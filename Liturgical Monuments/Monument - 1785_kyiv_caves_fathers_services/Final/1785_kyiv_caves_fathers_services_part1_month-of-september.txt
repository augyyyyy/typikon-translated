# MONTH OF SEPTEMBER

*Service to the Venerable Fathers of the Kyiv Caves Who Rest in the Near Caves:*
Which is chanted after the Apodosis of the Feast of the Elevation of the Precious Cross of the Lord, on the first Saturday.[^5]

*On Friday evening, the bell-ringer strikes the klepalo.[^6] The 20th Kathisma antiphon.*
*On "Lord, I Call," 6 Stichera, Tone 6, Podoben: "Having Despaired" (Всю̀ ѿложи́вше):*

Dancing in choir with the singing of spiritual songs, we summon all the assemblies of feast-lovers to praise the union of labor-loving, God-bearing fathers, and here we lay as foundation the great and marvelous Anthony, and Theodosius the divinely wise; for their sake, and through the prayers of all the fathers of the Caves, we entreat Thy love for mankind, O Christ, King of all: grant grace unto Thy people, and save from perils the souls of those who faithfully praise Thee at their memorial.

Let us all reverently sing with hymns Anthony of the Caves, the supreme among the fathers and beginner of the whole synaxis, and together let us honor with praises the bright ray and unsluggish emulator of his virtues, the God-bearing Barlaam, and let us cry out with a voice of joy, saying: O our most blessed venerable fathers, entreat Christ God that mercy be obtained for those who perform your memorial with love!

Rejoice, O fruitful Ruthenian land[^7], that hast nurtured citizens of the Jerusalem on high, *(Left page — Folio 1 verso, running header: SERVICE TO THE VENERABLE OF THE CAVES:)*
Sing the celebration with a sound of gladness; and thou, O marvelous Cave hill, that hast assembled warriors unto the Heavenly King, and hast shown forth steadfast men of spiritual desires, unto whom let us now cry out, saying: O God-bearing fathers, noetic luminaries, enlighten with the light of your grace the souls of those who honor your synaxis with love!

*Glory... Tone 8:*
Who shall tell the mighty acts of the Lord, or what tongue of earthborn men shall utter your marvelous life, O God-bearing fathers of ours of the Caves! How ye endured sufferings, and what ascetic labors ye bore, wearing down your flesh with vigilance, fasting, and tears, and through prayers and humility crushing the pride of the enemy; and having shone forth in a mortal body like angels, ye were translated unto heavenly mansions; therefore visit on this present day those who praise in song your honorable, fasting assembly in God, and ask of Christ God that we may obtain great mercy!

*Both now... Theotokion according to the tone. Seek at the end of this book (see fol. 151).[^8] Then, the Entrance: Prokeimenon of the day. And 3 Readings for the Venerable:[^9]*

*Reading from the Wisdom of Solomon (Wisdom 3:1–9):*
The souls of the righteous are in the hand of God, and no torment shall touch them. In the eyes of the foolish they seemed to die, and their departure was accounted to be misery, and their going from us to be utter destruction, but they are in peace. For though they be punished in the sight of men, yet is their hope full of immortality. And having been a little chastised, they shall be greatly rewarded, for God proved them, and found them worthy for Himself. As gold in the furnace hath He tried them, and received them as a whole burnt offering. And in the time of their visitation they shall shine, and run to and fro like sparks among the stubble. They shall judge nations, and have dominion over peoples, and the Lord shall reign in them unto the ages. They that put their trust in Him shall understand the truth, and such as be faithful in love shall abide with Him; for grace and mercy is in His venerable ones, and visitation among His elect.

*Reading from the Wisdom of Solomon (Wisdom 5:15–6:3):*
The righteous live unto the ages, and their reward is in the Lord, and the care of them is with the Most High. Therefore shall they receive a kingdom of glory, and a diadem of beauty from the hand of the Lord; for with His right hand shall He cover them, and with His arm shall He shield them. He shall take to Him His jealousy for complete armor, and make the creation His weapon for the vengeance of His enemies. He shall put on righteousness as a breastplate, and shall put on unfeigned judgment as a helmet.

*(Right page — Folio 2 recto, running header: OF THE NEAR CAVES:)*
He shall take invulnerable holiness for a shield. His severe wrath shall He sharpen for a sword, and the world shall fight with Him against the unwise. Then shall the right-aiming thunderbolts go abroad, and from the clouds, as from a well-drawn bow, shall they fly to the mark. And hailstones full of wrath shall be cast as out of a stone bow, and the water of the sea shall rage against them, and the rivers shall harshly drown them. Yea, a mighty wind shall stand up against them, and like a whirlwind shall it blow them away; thus wickedness shall lay waste the whole earth, and ill dealing shall overthrow the thrones of the mighty. Hear therefore, O ye kings, and understand; learn, ye that be judges of the ends of the earth. Give ear, ye that rule the people, and glory in the multitude of nations; for power is given you from the Lord, and sovereignty from the Most High.

*Reading from the Wisdom of Solomon (Wisdom 4:7–15):*
Though the righteous be overtaken with death, yet shall he be in rest. For honorable age is not that which stands in length of time, nor that is measured by number of years. But wisdom is the gray hair unto men, and an unspotted life is ripe old age. He pleased God, and was beloved of Him; so that living among sinners he was translated. Yea, speedily was he taken away, lest wickedness should alter his understanding, or deceit beguile his soul. For the bewitching of wickedness obscures things that are good, and the wandering of concupiscence undermines the simple mind. He, being made perfect in a short time, fulfilled a long time; for his soul pleased the Lord, therefore hasted He to take him away from among the wicked. This the people saw, and understood it not, neither laid they up this in their minds: that His grace and mercy is with His venerable ones, and that He hath respect unto His chosen.

*At the Aposticha, Stichera, Tone 2, Podoben: "As One Noble" (Ꙗ҆́кѡ до́блѧ):*
Come, O people, let us anticipate with hymns the foremost choir of God-bearing fathers who shone forth in the cave by fasting as good armor-bearers of Christ; for behold, from noetic Zion they swiftly come unto us, taking thought for their fulfillment and dispensing grace abundantly unto those who honor their honorable synaxis!

*Verse: The righteous cried, and the Lord gave ear to them (Ps. 33:18 [LXX 33:18]).*
Having assembled today in this holy temple, O ye people, bearing torches meet the monastic assembly that, like noetic stars, hath illuminated the honorable firmament upon the sensible sphere, and cry out with supplicatory voices, saying: O God-bearing and most blessed ones, pray for those who remember your fellowship with love!

*(Left page — Folio 2 verso, running header: SERVICE TO THE VENERABLE OF THE CAVES:)*
*Verse: Be glad in the Lord, and rejoice, ye righteous (Ps. 31:11 [LXX 31:11]).*
Rejoice, O choirs of Christ-loving people, assemble with those celebrating today with us, and weaving wreaths of praise, greet the synaxis of God-bearing fathers, invoking all by name together, who shone forth in the cave by fasting, and illuminate with the rays of their grace the souls of those who magnify them with love and faithfully perform their memorial!

*Glory... Tone 6:*
Enriched from the glory of the Lord, arming themselves with His Cross as with a spear, the assembly of ascetics came together, and the host of God-marked venerable fathers stood upon the hill of the Caves, bearing an invincible weapon against the enemy, whereby they completely cast down the demonic battle-arrays; and having attained unto the heavenly mansion, they received crowns of victory from Christ God and our Savior, and pray unceasingly for our souls.

*Both now... Theotokion:*
O Theotokos, thou art the true vine that didst bud forth for us the Fruit of life: we entreat thee, intercede, O Lady, together with the venerable fathers, that He may have mercy on our souls.

*Troparion, Tone 4:*
As a fiery pillar and radiant sun that shone forth upon the hill of the Caves, we bring before Thee, O Christ, the great Anthony unto prayer together with the whole synaxis of the God-bearing fathers; for their sake we pray: grant grace unto our monastery, and great mercy unto our souls.

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*
The Mystery hidden from all ages and unknown unto the angels was made manifest to those on earth through thee, O Theotokos: God incarnate in an unconfused union, willingly taking upon Himself the Cross for our sake, whereby He raised up the first-fashioned and saved our souls from death.

Through many afflictions ye traversed the path of this life and attained the goal of noetic desire, taking up your abode in the chamber of divine glory; and now rejoicing with the King of all, God, pray that we also may become partakers of your gladness, we who honor your synaxis, O most blessed fathers!

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*
O thou who art full of grace, through thy mediations and prayers entreat for our souls a multitude of compassions and cleansing of transgressions, for all who sing of thee, the honorable Mother and Virgin!

*After the 2nd Kathisma, Sessional Hymn, Tone 3:*
Having heard of the marriage of the Lamb in the heavenly chamber, and the invitation of the King on high having gone forth unto all, ye left behind all worldly attachments, O God-bearing fathers; and having cleansed your garments with streams of tears, ye honorably entered into the banquet of Christ God: but before the doors of that entrance are shut, pray that we also, having entered through repentance, may become partakers with you!

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*
Beholding the chamber adorned with everlasting glory, we have the desire of the Most High to enter therein, but having no wedding garment, we are ashamed lest we be bound by the angels and cast out; O Virgin Mary, who alone art the garment of boldness for the naked, adorn us and lead us into the kingdom of Christ!

*The Canon of the Theotokos with the Heirmos, to 6; and of the Venerable, to 8.*
*Tone 8, Ode 1. Heirmos:*
Let us send up a song, O ye people, unto our marvelous God, Who delivered Israel from servitude, singing a song of victory and crying aloud: let us sing unto Thee, the only Master!

*Troparia:*
At the memorial of Thy venerable ones, grant, O my Christ, help from on high, and guide me, giving me wisdom through Thy Holy Paraclete, that I may offer a hymn of praise unto Thee, the only Master.

The synaxis of God-bearing fathers hath assembled in God; come, O people, in worship, bearing with yourselves living sacrifices, for behold, they summon us that we may offer glory unto the King of all, the only Master.

Embracing the fruits of those whom the East and the Ruthenian land brought forth, and whom the Cave hill reared up like trees planted by God, assemble, O people, to honor them, and let us offer a hymn unto the Master.

Thou art a noetic paradise, O holy Cave hill, that hast multiplied noetic trees, the blessed fathers, whom we cannot enumerate one by one; therefore together for them all we offer praise and song unto the only Master.

A fruitful tree and olive branch, brought forth from that tree unto this hill is the great Anthony, with whose fruits cheering our souls, we offer a song unto Thee, O Christ, Master of all.

Stephen, full of faith and piety, builder of the church founded by God, and Nikon, who adorned it with icons, have shone forth like great luminaries; honoring them, we offer a song unto Thee alone, O Christ, Master of all.

*Glory...*
Offering praise unto Thee, O Most Holy Father, we bring forward as intercessor the marvelous Barlaam,[^12] who despised the glory of boyar lineage; for his sake turn my glory in this world into bitterness, that there I may find glory from Thee, the Master of all.

*Both now... Theotokion:*
O thou who didst bring the human race unto its primal nobility through thy holy childbearing: lead me, who have been dishonored by the deceits of demons, unto noetic glory, that I may unceasingly sing unto thee: Rejoice, O glory of the Orthodox, pure Virgin Mother!

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*
There is none holy as the Lord, and none righteous as our God, of Whom all creation sings: there is none righteous above Thee, O Lord.

*Troparia:*
Though there is no beauty of song upon my lips, yet do I bring praise unto thee, O God-bearing Ephraim; for having ceased to serve an earthly prince,

*(Right page — Folio 4 recto, running header: OF THE NEAR CAVES:)*
prince, thou didst follow the Heavenly King and didst receive reward from the hand of the Lord.

How shall I speak of Isaiah, loud-voiced in the gift of miracles?[^13] Entreating him at least as holding the hierarchic throne of Rostov, may he through his prayers unite me, a straying sheep, unto the flock of the Shepherd on high, and lead me into His fold.

In the beauty of the most radiant and among healers I will praise the blessed Damian; for burning with love toward all, he healed through oil and prayer: through his remedy heal me also, O Master and Lover of mankind!

It is meet to honor thee with praises, O Jeremiah, for honored from on high with the gift of prophecy, thou alone by the revelation of God wast a seer of the hidden things of men: remember me, who am in the oblivion of countless sins, O blessed one, and be a guide unto my repentance.

O Matthew, who didst manifestly behold the assaults of demons and didst correct those who gave cause unto passionate suggestions: as one seeing my uncorrected state with noetic eyes, guide me, that I may unceasingly magnify thee, O venerable one!

O good shepherd and luminary of Suzdal, Simon, together with Nikita the Recluse: as lovers of the great Lavra and zealots for monastic rectitude, correct me who have fallen, O fathers, through your chastening!

*Glory...*
Preserving Thy glory in their soul in this life, O Holy Father, the venerable ones were thereby counted worthy of everlasting glory from Thee; that we also may not be deprived thereof, we present their synaxis unto Thee today in prayer.

*Both now... Theotokion:*
O Lady, who by the Word art the ineffable dwelling of the venerable, make me a partaker of their joy and deprive me not of the glory of the saints, for all things are possible for thee in answer, and thou doest whatsoever thou desirest, O Mary, Bride of God!

*Sessional Hymn, Tone 8:*
I venture to honor the synaxis of the God-bearing fathers, the noetic stars that illuminate the Ruthenian land: but having a soul darkened by passions, I am at a loss in my mind; dispel the darkness of inconstant thoughts, that I may honorably sing a hymn unto you, O most blessed fathers!

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion:*
Rejoice, O Mother of the unsetting Star and dawn of the mystical day, for through thee have we beheld the Light and Sun of true righteousness, illuminated by whose rays we unceasingly sing of thee, O Maiden, who alone art blessed!

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*
The Prophet in divine vision perceived Thee, O Word, intending to become incarnate from the overshadowed mountain, the only Theotokos, and with fear glorified Thy power.

From the hill, according unto the foreknowledge of God, the God-bearing fathers appeared as luminaries upon earth, and illuminated the Ruthenian land with the brightness of their life: honoring which, we are filled with wonder, and glorify Thy power, O Christ!

We marvel how, being in the flesh, ye took upon yourselves an ascetic labor beyond nature and became like unto the angels; for having winged your souls with the love of God, ye preferred nothing in the world above it, whence even in weak flesh ye received Thy power, O Christ!

Stephen was steadfast in fasting, and Anastasius in boldness toward God, for in their petition they obtained all things; honoring their love of labor, I glorify Thy power, O my Christ!

Isaac, deceived in light by darkness, yet having overthrown the darkness by the light of rectitude, so that even now the sons of darkness fear his name: for our deliverance from such temptation Thy venerable one prays, O Christ, that we may unceasingly glorify Thy power!

I venture to honor the ascetic labors of Ilya of Murom, the invincible warrior:[^14] who having in His hand a wound from a weapon, yet having love toward Thee, O Christ, in His heart, which wrought a yet deeper wound within him: he is crowned all the more, glorifying Thy power.

We praise the steadfastness of Nikon in temptation,[^15] for he endured much from the infidels; but through patience he acquired even more humility and salvation: through his prayers may we also be saved, O Almighty Christ, as we glorify Thy power!

*Glory...*
In one essence we glorify the Tri-hypostatic Godhead, Father, and Son, and Most Holy Spirit; and we sing of Thee as the one God, and bowing before Thee at the memorial of our venerable fathers, we glorify Thy power!

*Both now... Theotokion:*
Having hope in thee, I fear not the assaults of demons; and having gained boldness in thee, I withstand the terrors of Satan, knowing that thou art the Good One Who through thunder terrifies the enemies.

*Ode 5. Heirmos:*
Delivering me from the gloom of passions as from deep night, make my spirit worthy to keep early vigil, I pray Thee, in the light of the day of Thy commandments, O Christ!

*Troparia:*
Having cast aside all the gloom of passions and harmful lust, Thy venerable ones, O Lord, ran with patience unto the ascetic struggle set before them; therefore were they illumined with grace from Thee, walking in the light of Thy commandments, O Savior!

We glorify the memory of Titus the Priest, who through humility trampled down the demon of anger, and Laurence the Recluse, who possessed boldness against the subtle

spirits; for they kept Thy commandments and are glorified in Thy light, O Savior!

Though I venture to praise him who shone sacredly in fasting, yet I cannot beatify him worthily; therefore unto Thee alone do I flee: grant me strength from Thy gifts, that I may praise Thy favorite, O Savior!

Theophilus, eminent in wonders, is praised in song, for while yet in this life he astonished many through the working of miracles; and now having received a greater gift to assist others, because he kept Thy commandments, O Savior!

We honor Basil and Theodore with the martyrs, for slandered by a demon, they innocently received torment from the prince: the one an end by an arrow, the other by fire and smoke; but together they are glorified in Thy kingdom, O Savior!

Prokhor is praised in his working of miracles and in his labors,[^16] for like a bird he fed upon unsown herbs, and we learn of him as the maker of bread from orache, who also gave salt transformed from ashes: marveling at his working of miracles, we glorify Thee, O Savior!

*Glory...*
Guided by the Spirit, named a son of the Most High, John was illuminated through fasting; for he was nourished by the Word of God rather than by food: therefore lightly, as if winged, did he pass through this life, receiving Thy glory from Thee, O Savior!

*Both now... Theotokion:*
All thy glory is within, O King's Daughter: adorned and arrayed, thou standest before the most pure throne; whence shining brighter than the sun, look down upon us with the rays of thy goodness, that we may unceasingly sing of thy power, O Virgin Mother!

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*
Encompassed with many sins, receive me, O Lover of mankind, who fall down before Thy compassions, as Thou didst the Prophet, O Lord, and save me!

*Troparia:*
I am constrained by love to praise your synaxis, O God-bearing fathers; for ye are praised by God and men, because in this life ye despised the glory of this world, that ye might fulfill the commandments of Christ.

What tongue shall tell your glory, O venerable ones, which ye acquired through many labors and brought unto perfection through grace! And these signs make manifest your marvelous benefactions and healings, wherewith despise not us who are weak, that we may be able to fulfill the commandments of Christ!

O Agapitus rich in miracles, who dispensest healings abundantly, and venerable Helladius with the other Abraham: as possessing much boldness before the Master of all, despise not your servants, but pray *(Left page — Folio 5 verso, running header: SERVICE TO THE VENERABLE OF THE CAVES:)*
pray that we may be delivered from the snares of the enemy, and ever do the will of God!

O John of many virtues, who even after death by obedience unto thy brother didst show forth an image of love; and Theophilus, who didst wash away the defilement of vainglory with tears: as ye are undivided both here and there, pray together unto God, that we also may have true love toward Him and unfeigned love toward our neighbor!

O Damian, who through prayer and oil didst heal bodily passions, with Sabbas the Wonderworker, despise not us who are exhausted by spiritual passions; but from the healing gift of your prayers cure our wounds of heart, that we may offer acceptable prayers unto God!

O Nectarius the Obedient, with Sergius of one mind with thee: having yielded your souls under the yoke of the obedience of Christ, bow down our stubbornness through your prayer under the yoke of Jesus Who sent you forth, that we may diligently fulfill His commandments!

*Glory...*
The glory of the city of Smolensk, Mercurius the Shepherd,[^17] who well pastured the sheep of his Chief Shepherd, and in spirit was exalted on high to be inseparably of the choir of the Caves venerable ones: therefore after death he miraculously floated in a wooden coffin, and in the holy cave rests with his body, while in his soul he rejoices with the venerable ones unto eternal glory.

*Both now... Theotokion:*
Having no ascent from good works unto the dwelling of the venerable, I flee unto thee, O Good Theotokos: for thou art the door of contemplation and the ladder beheld by the prophets; according unto thy goodness, therefore, grant an ascent in my heart, and open unto me the doors of thy mercy!

*Kontakion, Tone 2:*
Beholding with noetic eyes the choir of ascetics gathered in mind, come, O people, let us honor them with praises, and bowing before their relics, offer gifts of love; for they are intercessors before God and pleaders for our souls.

*Ikos:*
Who can praise Thy saints one by one, O Good One? I will number them and they shall be multiplied above the sand; but do Thou Thyself, O Master Christ, Who countest the multitude of the stars and callest them all by name, receive the prayers of them all for us, and show Thy compassions upon Thy people: for we know that they have much boldness before Thy majesty, and intercede with Thee as unceasing pleaders for our souls!

*Ode 7. Heirmos:*
O Hebrew children in the furnace, Who didst preserve for her who bore Thee her virginity after childbearing: blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!

*Troparia:*

O Pimen the Long-Ailing, who not only didst pass through this life in illness with thanksgiving, but didst also grant relief in sicknesses unto others through prayer: do thou, who art now in endless rest, relieve me from sickness, that I may sing: Blessed is the God of our fathers!

Pure gold and silver tried sevenfold in the gifts, O righteous Eustathius: thou wast a righteous worker, and didst cross over into the working of noetic virtues; wherewith having adorned thy soul, thou hast presented an offering as gold, and singest: Blessed is the God of our fathers!

O glorious Nestor,[^18] who by thy writing didst declare unto us the times and years of the most precious Church, and the lives and virtues of the first fathers, and wast a diligent emulator of their deeds: together with them do we now beatify thee, singing: Blessed is the God of our fathers!

O Eustratius, beauty of martyrs,[^19] thou didst appear as a second Elijah the charioteer: but he was taken up as unto heaven, while thou into the very heavenly gates didst enter on a fiery chariot, a voice summoning thee from the majesty of glory, where with a voice of joy thou singest: Blessed is the God of our fathers!

O glorious and blessed fathers, Helladius and Luke the Steward, renowned in gracious miracles: having been good laborers of the commandments of the Master, ye have now received recompense from the torrent of delight, and joyfully sing: Blessed is the God of our fathers!

*Glory...*
The Holy Spirit assists our spirit, O God-bearing fathers, for ye are the children of God and heirs of Christ; and Him Whom ye served, with Him are ye also glorified, singing: Blessed is the God of our fathers!

*Both now... Theotokion:*
The Holy Spirit came upon Thee, and the power of the Most High overshadowed Thee, showing Thee to be an honorable Mother even after childbearing: delivered through whom from the servitude of the stranger, all generations bless Thee, for the blessed God of our fathers hath beatified Thee!

*Ode 8. Heirmos:*
Him Who was glorified on the holy mountain, and in the bush revealed unto Moses the mystery of the Ever-Virgin through fire: praise the Lord, and supremely exalt Him unto all ages!

*Troparia:*
Those who lived upon the holy Cave hill, you, the great luminaries, O God-bearing fathers, whence ye stretched out rays of grace and illuminated the whole Ruthenian land with miracles: therefore we praise the Lord, and supremely exalt Him unto all ages!

Not the stormy sea, but the holy cave reveals your relics *(Left page — Folio 6 verso, running header: SERVICE TO THE VENERABLE OF THE CAVES:)*
relics as precious pearls, O favorites of God, and enrich with outpourings of miracles all who come unto you with faith, and supremely exalt the Lord with praises unto the ages.

Matthew the clairvoyant of things to come,[^20] and John, radiant above others in many sufferings, with Moses, the mirror of purity, as precious stones hidden in the cave, are revealed through miracles unto all who come unto them with faith, and supremely exalt the Lord with praises unto the ages.

Ephraim the Eunuch, a noetic dove,[^21] who brought the olive branch of the Studite Rule into this monastery, and who was exceedingly adorned with elder-like manners, together with Jeremiah the clairvoyant, let them be praised, who taught all to glorify the Lord unto the ages.

Mark the Grave-Digger was shown forth as a mirror unto lovers of labor,[^22] an instructor unto the repentant, a raiser of the dead: whose word they obeyed with fear as the roar of a lion; and even now he is dreadful unto demons, for he lives with the Lord unto the ages.

Gregory was shown forth as a wonderworker, not from Caesarea, but from the Pechersk Lavra,[^23] who through prayer held the thieves motionless with the fruits for three days and three nights; and having ended his life by drowning in the water, he now rejoices with the venerable Sergius by the waters of rest, singing unto the Lord unto the ages.

*Let us bless the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit: the Lord.*
Glory unto Erasmus alone, who adorned the Pechersk church with icons, and unto Onesimus of renowned profit,[^24] unto whom is numbered Matthew, who in our day possessed the prophetic gift: in these three the name of the Trinity is glorified unto all ages.

*Both now... Theotokion:*
Whosoever will not ever praise thee, O pure Virgin, is a stranger unto his salvation; for thy glory hath reached out unto the salvation of all who glorify thee, O Mary, Bride of God.

*Ode 9. Heirmos:*

### Notes

[^5]: The feast of the Synaxis of the Venerable Fathers of the Kyiv Caves who rest in the Near Caves (St. Anthony's Caves) is celebrated on the first Saturday following the Apodosis of the Elevation of the Holy Cross (September 21), which places it between September 22 and September 28 in the Julian calendar.
[^6]: *The bell-ringer strikes the klepalo* (Клиентъ звонитъ): The physical rubric uses *klient* (a Lavra monastic colloquialism/corruption of *klepalo*, semantron or bell-ringer) directing the ringer to sound the klepalo/bells for Great Vespers. The 20th Kathisma antiphon refers to the final kathisma of the liturgical Psalter (Psalms 134–142, "O give thanks unto the Lord"), prescribed for Friday evening Vespers.
[^7]: *Ruthenian land* (рѡссійская земле): The historical Kyivan recension applies this term to the lands of Kyivan Rus' and Ukraine, here celebrated as the spiritual cradle of the Caves monastics.
[^8]: The rubric directs the choir to the Dismissal Theotokia (*Otpostitelny*) printed at the end of the codex on folio 151 (physical leaf p155).
[^9]: The three festal Paremias for venerable monastics are taken from the Septuagint text of the Wisdom of Solomon: Wisdom 3:1–9; Wisdom 5:15–6:3; Wisdom 4:7–15.
[^10]: The melody model *Tone 3, Podoben: "Thou Art the Trinity"* (Троица еси) governs the Sessional Hymn after the first Kathisma.
[^11]: The Gospel reading for monastic hierarchs and ascetics: Matthew 4:23–5:1 (Slavic Pericope 10), recounting Christ teaching and healing throughout Galilee, followed by the ascent unto the mountain before the Sermon on the Mount.
[^12]: Saint Barlaam, first Abbot of the Kyiv Caves Monastery (commemorated November 19), who left boyar rank and wealth to accept the monastic tonsure under Saint Anthony.
[^13]: Saint Isaiah of the Caves, later Bishop of Rostov (commemorated May 15, d. 1090), and Saint Simon, Bishop of Vladimir and Suzdal (commemorated May 10, d. 1226), one of the principal epistolary compilers of the Kyiv Caves Paterikon.
[^14]: Saint Ilya of Murom (St. Elijah of Murom, commemorated December 19, d. c. 1188), the epic warrior of Kyivan Rus' who took monastic vows in the Kyiv Caves Monastery; his incorrupt relics in the Near Caves bear an unhealed spear wound in the left hand and chest.
[^15]: Saint Nikon the Dry (commemorated December 11, early 12th century), captured by the Polovtsians and held captive for three years under severe torment, refusing ransom and bearing torture with extraordinary endurance until he was miraculously delivered.
[^16]: Saint Prokhor the "Orache-Eater" (*Lebednik*, commemorated February 10, d. 1107), who gathered wild orache (*chenopodium*) to bake bread for the starving populace during the severe Kyiv famine and miraculous transformed wood ashes into pure white salt.
[^17]: Saint Mercurius, Bishop of Smolensk (commemorated August 7, d. 1239), whose body according to the Kyiv Caves tradition miraculously floated up the Dnieper River in a wooden coffin to the Lavra, where his relics were laid to rest in the Near Caves.
[^18]: Saint Nestor the Chronicler (commemorated October 27, c. 1056–1114), monk of the Kyiv Caves and renowned chronicler, author of the *Primary Chronicle* (Tale of Bygone Years) and the hagiographical lives of Saints Boris and Gleb and Saint Theodosius of the Caves.
[^19]: Hieromartyr Eustratius of the Caves (commemorated March 28, d. 1096), captured during a Polovtsian raid on Kyiv, sold into slavery in Chersonese (Crimea), and crucified on Pascha for refusing to renounce Christ, compared in the hymn unto the Prophet Elijah ascending to heaven in a fiery chariot.
[^20]: Saint Matthew the Clairvoyant (commemorated October 5), monk of the Kyiv Caves, endowed with the prophetic gift of seeing demonic temptations and hidden thoughts.
[^21]: Saint Ephraim the Eunuch, Bishop of Pereyaslav (commemorated January 28), who traveled to Constantinople and Mount Athos and brought back the Studite Typikon for Saint Theodosius.
[^22]: Saint Mark the Grave-Digger (commemorated December 29), who dug graves in the Near Caves for thirty years and at whose command corpses obeyed his voice.
[^23]: Saint Gregory the Wonderworker of the Caves (commemorated January 8, d. 1093), drowned in the Dnieper River by order of Prince Rostislav Vsevolodovich after prophesying the prince's defeat and death.
[^24]: Saint Erasmus (commemorated February 24), who gave his wealth to adorn the Lavra Dormition Church with icons, and Saint Onesimus the Recluse (commemorated October 4).
[^25]: Saint Spyridon and Saint Nicodemus the Prosphora-Bakers (commemorated October 31), who for thirty years baked prosphora while continually chanting the Psalter.
[^26]: Saint Alipius the Iconographer (commemorated August 17) and Saint Gregory the Iconographer, who learned icon painting from the Byzantine masters who decorated the Great Lavra Church.
[^27]: Saint Pimen the Faster (commemorated May 8) and Saint Ephraim the Priest.
[^28]: Saint Onuphrius the Silent (commemorated July 21), Saint Isaiah the Recluse, and Saint Sylvester the Chronicler (commemorated January 2).
[^29]: The foursome of princely ascetics: Saint Vladimir the Great, Equal-to-the-Apostles (commemorated July 15); the passion-bearers Boris and Gleb (baptized Roman and David, commemorated May 2 and July 24); and Prince Sviatoslav of Chernihiv, tonsured as Monk Nikola Sviatosha (commemorated October 14).
[^30]: Saint Barbara the Great-Martyr (commemorated December 4), whose relics were brought to Kyiv in 1108; Saint Euphrosyne, Princess of Polotsk (commemorated May 23, d. 1173 in Jerusalem, whose incorrupt body was kept in the Kyiv Caves until 1910); and Righteous Juliana, Princess of Olshansk (commemorated July 6, c. 1540).
[^31]: The assembly of the Hierarchs of Rus' enumerated in the Exapostilarion includes the Metropolitans of Kyiv and Moscow: Michael I, Hilarion, Peter, Alexis, Jonah, Photius, and Cyprian; Bishops Ephraim of Pereyaslav, Nicholas of Tmutarakan, and Nikita of Novgorod; Macarius, Metropolitan of Kyiv killed by the Tatars in 1497; Leontius, Bishop of Rostov; and Simon, Bishop of Vladimir and Suzdal.
[^32]: Epistle reading for the feast: 2 Corinthians 11:21–12:9 (Pericope 176), recounting the Apostle Paul's ascetic labors, persecutions, visions, and the thorn in the flesh.
[^33]: Gospel reading for monastics: Matthew 11:27–30 (Pericope 43), the invitation of Christ: "Come unto Me, all ye that labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."
[^34]: Saint Michael I, traditionally revered as the first Metropolitan of Kyiv (reigned c. 988–992), sent from Constantinople to baptize Prince Vladimir and the people of Rus'.
[^35]: *Tsargrad* (Ца́рьгра́дъ): Constantinople, imperial capital of the Byzantine Empire and source of the baptism and ecclesiastical hierarchy of Kyivan Rus'.
[^36]: The prophecy of the Apostle Andrew the First-Called, who according to the *Primary Chronicle* stood upon the hills of Kyiv, planted a cross, and prophesied that great churches would arise there and grace would shine forth upon the land.
[^37]: The rubric prescribes the common festal Paremias for a holy hierarch from Proverbs 10, Proverbs 10–11, and Wisdom 4, located in the general festal apparatus at folio 151.
[^38]: A dramatic prosopopoeia depicting the lament and terror of the pagan demonic idols when Saint Michael arrived in Kyiv, cast down the statues of Perun, and established the Christian faith.
[^39]: Fulfilling the aforementioned prophecy of Saint Andrew the First-Called upon the Kyivan hills.
[^40]: Gospel reading at Matins: John 10:9–16 (Pericope 36), the Good Shepherd pericope: "I am the door: by Me if any man enter in, he shall be saved..."
[^44]: The destruction of the idol of Perun upon the hills of Kyiv in 988, as recorded in the *Primary Chronicle* (Tale of Bygone Years): the wooden statue with a silver head and gold mustache was tied to a horse's tail, dragged down the Borychiv descent, beaten with rods, and cast into the Dnieper River.
[^41]: Typological parallel to the fall of the walls of Jericho before the Ark of the Covenant under Joshua (Joshua 6), prefiguring the collapse of Kyivan idols before the Cross and Saint Michael.
[^42]: The gold neck-chain (*hryvnia*, гривна) worn by nobility in Kyivan Rus', here employed as a metaphor for the holy Gospel adorning the neck of the baptized city of Kyiv.
[^43]: Saint Michael is likened to a second Moses leading the new Israel out of Egyptian idolatry, citing the prophetic verse of Psalm 71:16 (LXX 71:16): "There shall be an establishment of faith in the earth, upon the top of the mountains."
[^45]: The relics of Saint Michael originally rested in the Church of the Tithes (*Desiatynna*) built by Prince Vladimir, and were later translated to the Kyiv Caves Lavra Dormition Cathedral, where they rested incorrupt.
[^46]: The feast of Saint Michael on September 30 falls at the conclusion of September, the first month of the Byzantine liturgical year (Indiction), hence celebrated as the "first rotation of the new year."
[^47]: Prince Vladimir the Great is here designated by his Christian baptismal name Basil (*Vasiliy*), after his imperial sponsor Emperor Basil II of Byzantium.
[^48]: Woodcut engraving of Saint Damian the Presbyter and Healer (commemorated October 5, d. 1071), first physician and healer of the Kyiv Caves Monastery, who ministered to the sick with prayer and sanctified oil. In the lower register, Saint Theodosius appears to him on his deathbed in an angelic vision promising him the Kingdom of Heaven.
