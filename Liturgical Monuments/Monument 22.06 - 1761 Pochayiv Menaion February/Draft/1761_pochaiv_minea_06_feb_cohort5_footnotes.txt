[^117]: In the Byzantine-Ruthenian Typikon, Cheesefare Saturday commemorates All Holy Ascetic Fathers (Commemoration of All the Righteous Monks and Ascetics). When the Afterfeast of the Meeting of the Lord coincides with Cheesefare Saturday, the services of the feast and the ascetic Fathers are combined as specified in these rubrics.
[^118]: February 3 commemorates the Holy and Righteous Symeon the God-Receiver and Anna the Prophetess, who met the Christ Child in the Temple on the 40th day after His Nativity (Luke 2:22–38). In the liturgical cycle, this day functions as the Synaxis of the Meeting of the Lord.
[^119]: Attributed to St. Germanus, Patriarch of Constantinople (c. 634–733 or 740), author of numerous festal stichera and liturgical hymns.
[^120]: Attributed to St. John the Monk (St. John of Damascus, c. 676–749).
[^121]: Acrostic in the Theotokia and troparia of the Saint's Canon: "Receive the song, O Symeon the God-Receiver. By Joseph" (St. Joseph the Hymnographer, c. 816–886).
[^122]: The 1761 Pochaiv Menaion reads diplomatically 'and strengthen the king whom Thou hast loved' (*и оукрѣпи короля егоже возлюбилъ*), reflecting the political sovereignty of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth under King Augustus III of Poland (reigned 1734–1763), where the Pochaiv Basilian press operated, unlike Russian Synodal editions which read *христоименитых людей* or *императора*.
