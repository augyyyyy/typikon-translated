[^322]: In his famous Antiochian sermons, St. Meletius demonstrated the Holy Trinity by holding up three fingers and then bringing them together as one, thereby avoiding both the tritheism/subordinationism of Arius and the modalistic monarchianism of Sabellius.
[^323]: Macedonius I of Constantinople, heresiarch who denied the divinity of the Holy Spirit (Pneumatomachianism / Macedonians), condemned at the Second Ecumenical Council under the presidency of St. Meletius.

[^325]: The *Pneumatomachians* ('Spirit-fighters') or Macedonians, who denied the full divinity of the Holy Spirit, opposed by St. Meletius.
[^326]: St. Martinian of Caesarea in Palestine (5th c.), ascetic who struggled from youth in the wilderness, resisted the temptation of the courtesan Zoe (whom he converted), and spent years upon an isolated sea rock before wandering until his holy repose in Athens.
[^327]: When the woman Zoe sought to seduce St. Martinian in his hermitage, he rekindled the fire of charcoal and stepped barefoot into the burning embers to vanquish carnal passion with the remembrance of eternal fire.
[^328]: Following his encounter with Zoe, St. Martinian retreated to an uninhabited rocky reef in the sea, dwelling under the open sky without a roof, until he survived a shipwrecked maiden, whom he left on the rock while casting himself into the sea to preserve his chastity, miraculously brought ashore by dolphins.
