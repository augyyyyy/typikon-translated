[^329]: Alluding to the prophecy of the Righteous Simeon in Luke 2:35 ("Yea, a sword shall pierce through thine own soul also"), fulfilled at the foot of the Cross.
[^330]: Referring to Zoe, the woman sent by the adversary to tempt St. Martinian in his desert cell, whom he converted to repentance, directing her to a monastery in Bethlehem where she lived out her days in asceticism.
[^331]: St. Martinian, feeling the temptation of the flesh, stepped barefoot into burning coals, saying to himself: "If thou canst not bear this temporary fire, Martinian, how wilt thou endure the eternal fire of Gehenna?"
[^332]: The Slavonic text reads literally: *И҆сплени́лъ твоѝ гдⷭ҇и во поще́нїи свое́мъ по́двиги показа́въ...* (a Pochaiv print corruption for *Τὰς σὰς ὁσίας ἐν νηστείαις δείξας πράξεις*), addressing Christ concerning the ascetic struggles of St. Martinian.
[^333]: St. Martinian retreated to an isolated sea reef/rock in the Mediterranean, living under the open sky for ten years without any shelter from sun or storm.
[^334]: After saving the shipwrecked maiden Photina and leaving the sea rock to preserve his chastity, St. Martinian spent his remaining years as a homeless wanderer (*strannik*), passing through numerous cities until his repose in Athens.
[^335]: When St. Martinian cast himself into the sea from the rock to flee temptation, two dolphins miraculously bore him upon their backs to the shore.
[^336]: St. Martinian foresaw his approaching death, entered a cathedral church in Athens, lay down upon the floor, and having asked the local bishop to bury his remains, commended his soul to God.
[^337]: Referring to Photina, the maiden shipwrecked upon St. Martinian's rock, whom he left to pursue asceticism there while he plunged into the sea. She lived on the rock for six years in prayer and fasting until her holy repose.
[^338]: St. Auxentius of Bithynia (5th c.), former courtier under Emperor Theodosius the Younger, who renounced worldly rank to become a solitary ascetic on Mount Oxia and Mount Sinope (later called Mount of Auxentius). He played an influential role at the Fourth Ecumenical Council (Chalcedon, 451) in defending the Orthodox dogma against Monophysitism.
[^339]: The Greek acrostic reads: *Αὐξέντιον τὸν θεῖον ᾄδω σοι, μάκαρ* ("Unto thee, O blessed one, I sing of divine Auxentius"), composed by St. Theophanes the Branded (c. 778–845).
[^340]: Referring to the Orthodox dogma affirmed at the Fourth Ecumenical Council of Chalcedon (451) in which St. Auxentius participated, confessing Christ in two unconfused natures and Mary as truly *Theotokos*.
[^341]: St. Onesimus of the Seventy Apostles, disciple of the Holy Apostle Paul (commemorated in the Epistle to Philemon), formerly a runaway slave of Philemon in Colossae, who was baptized by Paul in Rome, sent back as a beloved brother, and later served as bishop (traditionally of Ephesus or Byzantium) before suffering martyrdom by stoning in Rome under Emperor Trajan or Domitian.
