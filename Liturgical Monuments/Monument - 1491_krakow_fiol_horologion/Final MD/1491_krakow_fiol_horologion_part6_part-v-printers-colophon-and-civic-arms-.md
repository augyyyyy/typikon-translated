# PART V: PRINTER'S COLOPHON AND CIVIC ARMS OF KRAKOW (1491)[^1354]

[Woodcut: The Civic Arms of the Royal Capital City of Krakow — an embattled masonry city wall with three towers, an open gateway with raised portcullis and gate-leaves bearing heraldic fleurs-de-lis; flanked by scrolled banderoles inscribed with the printer's initials and name "S V F I O L" (Schweipolt Fiol) and merchant-printer's orb-and-cross device.][^1353]

This book was completed in the great city of Krakow, under the reign of the great King of Poland, Casimir;[^1355] and it was completed by the Krakow burgher Schweipolt Fiol,[^1356] of the German nation, a Frank;[^1357] and it was finished after the Nativity of God in the one thousand four hundred and ninety-first year.[^1358]

---

### Notes

[^1354]: Concluding landmark for the landmark publication edition of the Krakow Horologion (*Chasoslov*), the earliest printed Cyrillic liturgical monument, printed at Krakow in the printing shop of Schweipolt Fiol in 1491.
[^1353]: Architectural woodcut device of Schweipolt Fiol (Szwajpolt Fiol, ca. 1460–1525/1526): The heraldic coat of arms of the City of Krakow (*herb Krakowa*), featuring a fortified brick wall with three towers, an open double portal with portcullis, framed by scrolled Renaissance banderoles displaying his monogram initials `S` (top left), `V` (top right), and name `FIOL` (lower ribbon), supported by a four-footed printer's orb-and-cross mark.
[^1355]: Casimir IV Jagiellon (*Kazimierz IV Jagiellończyk*, 1427–1492), Grand Duke of Lithuania (from 1440) and King of Poland (from 1447 to 1492). Church Slavonic: `при державѣ великаго короля полскаго казимира` ("under the realm/dominion of the great King of Poland, Casimir").
[^1356]: Church Slavonic: `мѣщаниномь краковьскымь шваиполтомь , фѣоль` ("by the Krakow burgher Schweipolt Fiol"). Fiol, a master goldsmith and printer originally from Neustadt an der Aisch (Franconia), acquired burgher citizenship in Krakow in 1479 and established the first Cyrillic printing press with the support of the Krakow merchant and mining patrician Jan Turzon (Johann Thurzó).
[^1357]: Church Slavonic: `из нѣмець немецького родоу , франкъ` ("of the Germans, of German stock, a Frank / Franconian"). Denotes his geographical origin in the historic Duchy of Franconia (*Frankenland*), Germany.
[^1358]: Church Slavonic: `и скончашася по божиємь нароженне • д҃і сътъ • девѧтьдесятъ и а҃ лѣто •` Diplomatic rendering of the numeral system: `д҃і сътъ` (14 hundreds = 1400) + `девѧтьдесятъ и а҃ лѣто` (ninety and first year) = the year 1491 after the Nativity of Christ.
