# TONE 1: TRINITY HYMNS AND PHOTAGOGICA

*Trinity Hymns and Photagogica according to the Tones. Tone 1.*[^4]

*Troparion:*  
By bodily representations being led by the bodiless Powers unto the noetic and immaterial mind, having received the radiance of the three-hypostatic Godhead through the thrice-holy hymn, cherub-like we cry aloud: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God![^5]

*Troparion:*  
With the majestic angelic powers, the cherub-like cry aloud in the highest, sending up praise unto the Trinity: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God!

*Troparion:*  
Arisen from sleep, we fall down before Thee, O Good One, and cry aloud unto Thee the angelic hymn, O Mighty One: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God, through the Theotokos have mercy on us!

*Photagogicon:*  
O Lord Who hast caused the light to shine, cleanse my soul from every sin by the intercession of the bodiless powers, and save me![^6]

*It is proper to say likewise: [On Monday:] Through the intercession of the bodiless powers; on Tuesday: By the prayers of Thy Forerunner; on Wednesday and on Friday: By the power of Thy Cross; on Thursday: By the prayers of Thy holy Apostles, have mercy on us.*[^7]

*Tone 2:*

*Troparion:*  
O uncreated Nature, Creator of all, open Thou our lips, that we may proclaim Thy praise, crying aloud: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God!

*Troparion:*  
Emulating the powers on high here upon earth, we offer Thee the triumphal song, O Good One, crying aloud: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God!

*Troparion:*  
From the bed of sleep Thou hast raised me up, O Lord; enlighten my mind and heart, and open my lips to sing of Thee, O Holy Trinity: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God!

*Photagogicon:*  
Send forth Thy co-eternal Light, O Lord, and enlighten the most secret eyes of my heart, [through the intercession of the bodiless powers...][^8]

*Tone 3:*

*Troparion:*  
The Father without beginning, and the Son equally without beginning, and the co-eternal Spirit, one Godhead: cherub-like taking courage, we say: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou!

*Troparion:*  
O Trinity consubstantial and undivided, O Monad three-hypostatic and ever-existing, unto Thee as God we cry the angelic hymn: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God, through the Theotokos have mercy on us![^9]

*Glory... Both now... Theotokion. If there be none for the saint, then the hymn for the Martyrs is said at the 7th Ode, after the third Kathisma of the Octoechos.[^10]*

*Then the ecphonesis:*  
**Priest:** For blessed and glorified is Thy most honorable and majestic Name, [of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.]

*If it be Sunday, after both Kathismata, and after the Amomos [Kathisma 17], and after the Polyeleos ("Mnogomilostive"), the diaconal petitions and ecphonesis take place, and the prayer is said before the Gospel, then the Hypakoe or the sessional hymn to the saint.[^11]*

*If there be a Gospel of that day at Matins, the Polyeleos and the Songs of Ascents [Anabathmoi] are sung. And after the Prokeimenon:*  
**Deacon:** Let us pray to the Lord.  
**Priest:** *Ecphonesis:* For holy art Thou, O our God, and unto Thee do we send up glory, [to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.]  

*Then:* Let every breath praise the Lord [LXX Ps. 150:6].[^12] *Verse:* Praise God in His saints [LXX Ps. 150:1].  
**Deacon:** And that we may be accounted worthy to give ear to the Holy Gospel, in peace let us pray to the Lord God.  
**Priest:** Wisdom! Stand upright! Let us give ear to the Holy Gospel. Peace be unto all.  
**Reader:** And to thy spirit.  
**Priest:** The reading from the Holy Gospel according to [Name].  
**Choir:** Glory to Thee, O Lord, glory to Thee.  
**Deacon:** Let us attend!  

*After the Gospel and after Psalm 50 [LXX Ps. 50], this prayer is said:*

**Priest:** Save, O God, Thy people, and bless Thine inheritance; visit Thy world with mercy and compassions; exalt the horn of Christians, and send down upon us Thy rich mercies; through the intercessions of our all-pure Lady, the Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary; by the power of the precious and life-creating Cross; through the protection of the honorable and heavenly bodiless Powers; of the honorable and glorious Prophet, Forerunner, and Baptist John; of the holy, glorious, and all-lauded Apostles; of our venerable and God-bearing Fathers; of the holy and righteous Ancestors of God, Joachim and Anna; and of Saint [Name], the patron of this temple; and of all Thy saints; by their prayers, O most merciful Lord, give ear unto us sinners who pray unto Thee, and have mercy on us![^13]

*And the choir:*  
**Choir:** Lord, have mercy *(twelve times).*

*Then the ecphonesis:*  
**Priest:** Through the mercy and compassions and love for mankind of Thine Only-Begotten Son, [with Whom Thou art blessed, together with Thine all-holy, good, and life-creating Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.]

*Rubric: If it be [a feast], the odes are recited, then the canon. But if not, or if it be a feast of an appointed saint, then...*[^14]

*When the [Biblical] Odes are not recited, the canons alone are sung.*

*After the 3rd Ode, the Little Litany and ecphonesis:*  
**Priest:** For Thou art our God, and unto Thee do we send up glory, [to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.]  
*Sessional hymn to the saint.*

*After the 6th Ode, the Little Litany and ecphonesis:*  
**Priest:** For Thou art the King of peace, and unto Thee do we send up glory, [to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.][^15]  
*Kontakion of the day and the ikos thereof, or of the saint. If it be a fast, the hymn for the Martyrs is recited.*

*Then at the 9th Ode, we chant the Magnificat ("More honorable than the Cherubim") with its verses [LXX Lk. 1:46–55]. And after the canon, we sing: "It is truly meet to bless thee, O Theotokos..." Then the Little Litany and ecphonesis:*  
**Priest:** For all the heavenly powers praise Thee, and unto Thee do we send up glory, [to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and ever, and unto ages of ages. Amen.]

*If it be Sunday, the reader says:*  
**Reader:** Holy is the Lord our God [LXX Ps. 98:9].  
*Verse:* For holy is the Lord our God [LXX Ps. 98:9]. Holy is the Lord our God.

*Then the Resurrectional Photagogica are recited. Or if it be a feast of the Lord, say the festal photagogicon; if of an appointed saint, the photagogicon of the day is recited.*[^16]

*Glory to the saint, Both now... Theotokion. But if it be for a simple saint and there be no photagogicon, then say for the saint this photagogicon: "Thou Who hast adorned the heaven with stars..." Theotokion: "O sweetness of the angels..." But if not, the photagogicon following the Trinity hymns is sung.*

### Notes

[^4]: Folio 1r opens with a decorative calligraphic ligatured heading in cinnabar vermilion ink: 'ТРОИЧНА СВѢТИЛНИ ГЛАСОВОМЬ. ГЛАСЪ 1.' ('Trinity Hymns and Photagogica according to the Tones. Tone 1').
[^5]: In the Midnight Office according to the archaic Studite and early Jerusalem Horologia, the Trinity Hymns (Троичны) of the Eight Tones form the core nocturnal prayer cycle.
[^6]: The photagogicon concludes with the archaic rubric indicating the daily variable refrains according to the day of the week.
[^7]: The rubric establishes the daily intercessory endings for the Midnight photagogicon across the liturgical week (Monday: Angels; Tuesday: Forerunner; Wednesday/Friday: Cross; Thursday: Apostles).
[^8]: The photagogicon text concludes with an abbreviated cue 'застѹпленіе', referencing the weekday refrain formula established on fol. 1r (p2): 'through the intercession of the bodiless powers' (on Monday), or the respective patronal refrain of the day.
[^9]: Folio 1v concludes with the second troparion of Tone 3. In the surviving bound codex, fol. 2r (p4) does not continue the Nocturn Trinity troparia of Tones 3–8, but binds directly into quire 11 (signature 'а҃і'), containing the order of Matins rubrics following the Kathismata.
[^10]: The physical leaf bears the Cyrillic signature 'а҃і' (11) in the bottom right corner, marking the beginning of the eleventh quire of the codex. The text contains the rubric for concluding the third kathisma of the Octoechos at Matins.
[^11]: The rubric specifies the Sunday Matins order after the two regular Psalter kathismata, followed either by the 17th Kathisma (the 'Amomos' or Непорочны) or the Polyeleos (designated by its refrain 'Многомилостиве' / 'For His mercy endures forever', Pss. 134–135).
[^12]: The Matins Gospel dialogue follows the archaic pre-Nikonian Byzantine-Slavonic order: the deacon intones the petition for hearing the Gospel ('миромъ Господа Бога молимъ') and the priest proclaims 'Wisdom! Stand upright!' directly before the Gospel pericope.
[^13]: This prayer after Psalm 50 at Matins ('Save, O God, Thy people...') represents the standard Byzantine-Slavonic intercessory litany, invoking the Theotokos, the Cross, the Angels, the Forerunner, Apostles, Venerable Fathers, Joachim and Anna, and the patron saint of the temple.
[^14]: The rubric at the foot of fol. 2v introduces the order of the Matins Canons and Biblical Odes, governing whether the Scriptural Odes (пѣсни) are sung in full or superseded by the festal canon.
[^15]: The ecphonesis after Ode 6, 'Ты бо еси Царь мира...' ('For Thou art the King of peace...'), reflects the ancient liturgical ecphonesis common to early Ruthenian and Byzantine Horologia before standard late-Muscovite harmonization.
[^16]: The rubrics on fol. 3r govern the concluding structure of the Matins Canon: the Little Litanies after Odes 3, 6, and 9; the chanting of the Magnification ('Честнѣйшую') and 'Достойно есть' at Ode 9; and the intonation of 'Holy is the Lord our God' (Святъ Господь Богъ нашъ) preceding the Resurrectional Exapostilaria.
