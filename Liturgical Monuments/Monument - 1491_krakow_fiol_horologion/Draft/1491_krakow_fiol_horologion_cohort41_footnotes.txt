[^463]: The three Old Testament readings (*Paroimiai*) prescribed for the Transfiguration are: Exodus 24:12–18 (Moses ascents Mount Sinai to receive the tablets of stone); Exodus 33:11–23 (Moses beholds the glory of God in the cleft of the rock); and 3 Kings 19:3–16 (Elijah's vision of God on Mount Horeb in a still small voice).
[^464]: Physical leaf p402 directs reading from the homilies of St. John Chrysostom on the Gospel of Matthew (Homilies 56 and 57 on the Transfiguration), and designates the two canonical festive Canons: the first by St. Cosmas the Melodist (*Хоры Израильтестии*, Tone 4) and the second by St. John of Damascus (*Воду прошед*, Tone 8).
[^465]: The Kontakion of the Transfiguration (*На горѣ преобразился еси...*, Tone 7, Idiomelon / Automelon) encapsulates the theological purpose of the manifestation on Mount Tabor: that the disciples, beholding Christ crucified, might perceive His voluntary Passion and proclaim unto the world that He is truly the Radiance of the Father.
[^466]: Liturgical pericope references for the Divine Liturgy of the Transfiguration: Epistle 2 Peter 1:10–19 (Catholic lection 65); Gospel Matthew 17:1–9 (lection 70).
[^467]: Physical leaf p403 records the ancient Byzantine and monastic ritual of the Blessing of Grapes (*стафиліе* / σταφυλή) at the conclusion of the Divine Liturgy on August 6, recited following the distribution of the antidoron (*дора* / ἀнτίδωрон).
[^468]: Physical leaf p404 preserves the ancient Ruthenian recension of the *Prayer for the Blessing of Grapes* (*Молитва на благословеніе гроздія*), invoking God Who adorned the earth with flowers and ripens fruits through the dew, the gentle breeze, and the heat of the sun, that the tasting of these earthly fruits may lead unto spiritual joy and thanksgiving through the Blood and Body of Christ.
[^469]: Physical leaf p405 prescribes the strict monastic penance (*епитимія*) barring any brother or vinedresser who prematurely tastes grapes prior to August 6 from eating grapes for the entire remainder of August. It further specifies the dietary dispensation for grapes at the refectory thrice weekly at the midday meal.
[^470]: August 7 commemorates the Holy Venerable-Martyr Dometius of Persia and his two disciples (d. 363 under Emperor Julian the Apostate).
[^471]: Physical leaf p406 marks August 14: the Forefeast of the Falling Asleep of the Most Holy Theotokos, joined with the commemoration of the Holy Prophet Micah (one of the Twelve Minor Prophets, 8th c. BC).
[^472]: Physical leaf p407 reflects a physical signature misbinding in the 1491 Krakow exemplar. Folio 210 (leaves p407 and p408) preserves the conclusion of the ritual order for August 1 (Procession of the Precious Wood of the Life-Giving Cross and Small Blessing of Waters), which was initiated on physical leaves p394 and p397. Leaf p408 (the verso of folio 210) textually immediately precedes leaf p407 (the recto), as marked by the connecting rubrical cross (`+`). In strict compliance with Rule 15 and MTS-1, the codex text is transcribed and translated in exact physical leaf pagination order.
[^473]: The monastic ritual of lustrating the monastery with freshly blessed water and the Precious Cross on August 1: the priest in full vestments, accompanied by chanting brethren, processes throughout all monastic quarters—the Igumen's cell, refectory (*трапеза*), cellar (*келарня*), kitchen (*поварня*), bakery (*пекленица*), granary (*житница*), cellars (*погребы*), and all individual monks' cells.
[^474]: Liturgical pericope references for August 1 (Procession of the Cross and the Seven Maccabee Martyrs): Epistle 1 Cor. 1:18–24 (lection 125); Gospel John 19:6–11, 13–20, 25–28, 30–35 (lection 60). For the Maccabees: Epistle Hebrews 11:33–40 (lection 330 / to All Saints); Gospel Matthew 10:32–33, 37–38; 19:27–30 (lection 38).
[^475]: Physical leaf p408 records the rubric for bringing forth the Precious Cross from the altar onto a prepared table in the nave during the chanting of the Trisagion at the Great Doxology, followed by the Small Blessing of Waters and the solemn veneration of the Cross (*Поклоненіе честному древу*).
[^476]: Physical leaf p409 opens the commemoration for August 15: the Great Feast of the Falling Asleep (Dormition) of the Most Holy Lady Theotokos and Ever-Virgin Mary (*Успеніе Пресвятыя Богородицы*), celebrated with an All-Night Vigil.
[^477]: The Troparion of the Dormition (*Въ рождествѣ дѣвство сохранила еси...*, Tone 1) professes the central mystery of the feast: in giving birth She preserved Her virginity, and in falling asleep She did not forsake the world.
[^478]: Physical leaf p410 details the liturgical order for Dormition Matins, prescribing the two canonical festive Canons: the first by St. Cosmas of Maiuma (*Пособитницею*, Tone 1) and the second by St. John of Damascus (*Преукрашенная божественною славою*, Tone 4), along with a reading from the Life of the Mother of God by Symeon Metaphrastes (*Метафрастъ*).
