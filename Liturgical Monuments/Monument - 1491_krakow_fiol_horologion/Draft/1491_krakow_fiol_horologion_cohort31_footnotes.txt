[^350]: Leaf p301 details the Sixth Royal Hour (*шестый часъ*) on the Eve of Theophany (January 5). At the 4th hour of the day (mid-morning), two tolls of the semantron signal the assembly. The priest and deacon vest in full sacred vestments to celebrate the Sixth and Ninth Royal Hours together. Following the opening blessing, Trisagion, and introductory prayers, the Sixth Hour appoints Psalm 73 (*O God, why hast Thou cast us off unto the end?* [LXX Ps. 73:1]), Psalm 76 (*With my voice have I cried unto God* [LXX Ps. 76:2]), and Psalm 90 (*He that dwells in the help of the Most High* [LXX Ps. 90:1]). Following the Great Litany, the two choirs alternately chant the appointed Theophany Sixth Hour troparia: Tone 8 *Thus says the Lord unto John* (*Си глаголетъ Господь къ Іоанну: Пророче, прииди, крести Мене*); then with the refrain from LXX Ps. 41:7 (*Therefore will I remember Thee*), the Tone 6 Troparion *Today the prophetic scripture is fulfilled* (*Днесь пророческое сбывается писаніе*); concluding with the Tone 5 Doxastikon *What turnest thou back, O Jordan, thy waters?* (*Что возвращаешися, Іордане*), sung by both choirs together. The lectionary comprises the Prokeimenon from LXX Ps. 28:3, 1 (*The voice of the Lord is upon the waters*), and the Prophecy of Isaiah 55:1–13 (*Ho, everyone that thirsts, come ye to the waters*).

[^351]: Leaf p302 concludes the lections of the Sixth Royal Hour: the Epistle of Great Saturday (Romans 6:3–11, St. Paul's fundamental discourse on baptism into Christ's death and resurrection) and the Gospel from Matthew 3:13–17 (*Then comes Jesus from Galilee to Jordan unto John, to be baptized of Him*). Following the Litany of Supplication, the Forefeast Troparion *The river Jordan once turned back*, the verse *Let Thy tender mercies speedily prevent us* (LXX Ps. 78:8), and the Kontakion *In the streams of Jordan*, the leaf introduces the Ninth Royal Hour (*девятый часъ*). After a brief pause, the Ninth Hour opens with Psalm 92 (*The Lord reigns, He is clothed with majesty* [LXX Ps. 92:1]), Psalm 113 (*In the exodus of Israel out of Egypt* [LXX Ps. 113:1]), and Psalm 85 (*Bow down Thine ear, O Lord, and hear me* [LXX Ps. 85:1]). The choral interchange features the Tone 7 Troparion *Terror was it to behold the Creator of heaven and earth* (*Ужасъ бѣ видѣти Творца небесе и земли*), paired with the refrains from LXX Ps. 41:7 and Ps. 76:17 and the Tone 2 Troparion *When the Forerunner came unto Him* (*Егда прииде къ нему Предтеча*).

[^352]: Leaf p303 concludes the Ninth Royal Hour and the Typika (*Изобразительныя*) on the Eve of Theophany. The choral climax of the Ninth Hour features the Tone 5 Doxastikon *Thy hand which touched the immaculate head of the Master* (*Руку твою прикоснувый пречистѣй версѣ Владычнѣ*). The lectionary comprises the Prokeimenon from LXX Ps. 26:1 (*The Lord is my light and my savior*), the Prophecy of Isaiah 49:8–15 (*In an acceptable time have I heard thee*), the Epistle from Titus 2:11–14 and 3:4–7, and the Gospel from Luke 3:1–18. At the 10th hour of the day (late afternoon), the great klepalo summons the community for Great Vespers conjoined with the Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great.

[^353]: Leaf p304 sets forth Great Vespers conjoined with the Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great on the Eve of Theophany (January 5). Following the opening blessing, Trisagion, and Psalm 103 (*Bless the Lord, O my soul* [LXX Ps. 103:1]), the Great Litany is intoned. At *Lord, I have cried*, while the deacon censes, the priest prepares the Proskomide at the Table of Oblation. Eight stichera are chanted (4 festal idiomela repeated, alongside the saint's stichera), culminating in the famous Tone 2 Doxastikon *Thou bowedst Thy head unto the Forerunner* (*Преклонилъ еси главу Предтечи*). The Little Entrance is made with the Gospel book, followed by the daily Prokeimenon and the prescribed Old Testament lections (the 13 Paremias of Theophany). Following the Paremias and Little Litany, the priest recites the Trisagion prayers, the Trisagion is chanted, and the Divine Liturgy lections are proclaimed: Prokeimenon from LXX Ps. 26:1 (*The Lord is my light*), Epistle from 1 Corinthians 9:19 (*For though I be free from all men...*), Alleluia from LXX Ps. 44:2 (*My heart hath poured forth a good word*), and Gospel from Luke 3:1–18. St. Basil's Liturgy is celebrated according to its order. At the conclusion of the Liturgy, the rubrics record the solemn preparation for the Great Blessing of Waters: the priest places the Holy Cross upon a vessel/dish upon his head, accompanied by candles and censers, and processes to the water font.

[^354]: Leaf p305 details the Great Blessing of Waters (*Великое освященіе воды*) on the Eve of Theophany. The clergy process to the baptismal font or water vessel preceded by candles and incense, singing the Tone 8 Troparia of St. Sophronius of Jerusalem (*The voice of the Lord is upon the waters*, *Today the nature of the waters is sanctified*, and *Thus says the Lord unto John*). The Scriptural lectionary features the three Isaiah Prophecies (Isa. 35:1–10; 55:1–13; 12:3–6), Prokeimenon from LXX Ps. 26:1, Epistle from 1 Corinthians 10:1–4 (the typological crossing of the Red Sea as baptism into Moses), Alleluia from LXX Ps. 28:3, 1, and the Gospel from Mark 1:9–11 (the Baptism of Jesus). The deacon intones the solemn consecratory litany for the sanctification of the waters with its special petitions (*яже о священныхъ*). While the deacon chants the litany, the priest recites the silent prayer of humble self-abasement (*Господи Іисусе Христе, Единородный Сыне*); then, at the deacon's concluding cue, the priest intones aloud the majestic consecratory prayer of St. Sophronius: *Great art Thou, O Lord, and marvelous are Thy works* (*Велій еси, Господи, и чудна дѣла Твоя*). A precise rubrical rubric is provided for when a priest serves without a deacon: he recites the secret prayer while the Old Testament lections are being read, so that he is ready to begin the great audible consecration immediately after the litany.

[^355]: Leaf p306 records the core hieratic moment of the Great Blessing of Waters on Theophany Eve: the solemn recitation of the great prayer of St. Sophronius (*Great art Thou, O Lord, and marvelous are Thy works* / *Велій еси, Господи, и чудна дѣла Твоя*), the prayer of the bowing of heads (*Incline Thine ear, O Lord* / *Приклони, Господи, ухо Твое*), and the threefold blessing and vertical immersion of the Precious Cross into the water. As the Cross is immersed, the Festal Troparion in Tone 1 is sung in full: *When Thou wast baptized in the Jordan, O Lord, the worship of the Trinity was made manifest: for the voice of the Father bore witness unto Thee, naming Thee His beloved Son, and the Spirit in the form of a dove confirmed the certainty of the word: O Christ God, Who hast appeared and enlightened the world, glory to Thee!* (*Во Іорданѣ крещающути ся, Господи, Троическое явися поклоненіе...*). As the faithful are sprinkled with the newly sanctified Jordan water, the Troparion is chanted thrice. The clergy and people process back into the temple singing the Tone 6 Doxastikon *Let us praise, ye faithful* (*Воспоимъ, вѣрніи, еже о насъ Божія благодѣянія величество*) and Psalm 33 (*I will bless the Lord at all times* [LXX Ps. 33:2]).

[^356]: Leaf p307 provides liturgical and dietary regulations for the Eve of Theophany. Following the Great Blessing of Waters, antidoron (*преломленіе*) is distributed, the full dismissal is given, the festal Troparion and Kontakion are sung, and the community partakes of a fasting meal seasoned with olive oil (*древяное масло*), strictly abstaining from dairy and eggs (*сыръ и яйца*). A crucial Typikon statute governs the calendar collision when January 5 falls on a Saturday or a Sunday: the strict Paramony fast is relaxed (*поста нѣсть*), the Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom is celebrated in the morning at the normal hour, and the faithful consume blessed bread and a cup of wine in the church to sustain themselves. On such years, the Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great is transferred to the feast day itself (January 6). In the afternoon, the semantron is struck for Great Vespers (celebrated independently of Liturgy); if on Saturday evening, the first Kathisma (*Blessed is the man* [LXX Ps. 1]) is chanted, followed by 8 festal stichera on *Lord, I have cried*, the Doxastikon *Thou bowedst Thy head*, and the Little Entrance with the Gospel.

[^357]: Leaf p308 continues the rubrics for Great Vespers when Theophany Eve falls on a Saturday or Sunday. After the entrance and Paremias, the Little Litany is intoned, followed by the Prokeimenon from LXX Ps. 26:1. The Trisagion hymn (*Святый Боже*) is explicitly omitted because the Divine Liturgy is not joined to Vespers on this day (*не совершеныя ради литургіа*). The Epistle (1 Cor. 9:19) and Gospel are read immediately at Vespers, followed by the Litany of Supplication. The clergy then process to the water vessel chanting *The voice of the Lord is upon the waters*, performing the Great Blessing of Waters in its complete order (Paremias, Epistle, Gospel, prayer, immersion of the Cross, and sprinkling). Returning to the temple, they chant *Let us praise, ye faithful*, the Augmented Litany (*Помилуй насъ, Боже*), and the dismissal, followed by a meal with oil. The leaf then introduces the rubrics for when the Feast of Theophany itself (January 6) coincides with a Sunday (*въ неделю*).

[^358]: Leaf p309 provides instructions for the coincidence of Theophany with Sunday (*Недѣля*). In striking contrast to standard Sunday Typikon rules where the Resurrectional octoechos elements dominate, on Theophany the Resurrectional material is almost entirely suppressed in favor of the Despotic Feast (*на все праздниково*): no Resurrectional stichera, hypakoai, gradual hymns (*степенны*), Psalm 118 (*Блажени непорочніи*), Resurrectional Gospel, nor *Having beheld the Resurrection of Christ* (*Воскресеніе Христово видѣвше*) are sung; only three Psalter kathismata are chanted, and the faithful simply venerate the Gospel book. At the 2nd hour of the night (the opening of the All-Night Vigil), Great Compline (*павечерница*) is chanted. Following the Minor Doxology (*Glory to God in the highest*), five festal idiomela in Tone 4 are sung, with the Tone 8 Doxastikon *O Lord, wishing to fulfill what Thou hadst ordained* (*Господи, исполнити хотя*) and Theotokion *Today creation is enlightened*. The community then processes into the nave for Litia (*Литія*), chanting the Tone 2 idiomela (*In the river Jordan*, *Today Christ comes to be baptized*, and *What ailed thee, O sea*) with their fixed psalm refrains (LXX Ps. 76:17 and Ps. 113:3, 5), concluding with the Tone 6 Doxastikon *He that clothes Himself with light as with a garment*, the blessing of the five loaves, wheat, wine, and oil (*благословеніе хлѣбомъ*), and the festal Troparion thrice.

[^359]: Leaf p310 details the structure of festal Matins (*Утреня*) for the Feast of the Holy Theophany (January 6). Following the Anavathmoi (Tone 4, 1st Antiphon *From my youth*), the Matins Prokeimenon is proclaimed from LXX Ps. 113:3, 5 (*The sea saw it and fled*), followed by the Matins Gospel, Psalm 50, and the festal Doxastikon.
