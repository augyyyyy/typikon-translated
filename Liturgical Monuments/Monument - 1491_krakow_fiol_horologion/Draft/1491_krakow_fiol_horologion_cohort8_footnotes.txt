[^120]: Folio 35v witnesses the concluding verses of Psalm 83 (LXX Ps. 83:7–13), celebrating the blessedness of them that dwell in the house of God and go from strength to strength, followed by the opening of Psalm 84 (LXX Ps. 84:1–4; 'Благоволилъ еси, Господи, землю Твою...'), appointed at the Ninth Hour.
[^121]: Folio 36r completes Psalm 84 (LXX Ps. 84:5–14), featuring the messianic and incarnational verses on mercy and truth meeting, and righteousness looking down from heaven ('Милость и истина срѣтостася, правда и миръ облобызастася...'; Greek Ἔλεος καὶ ἀλήθεια συνήντησαν, δικαιοσύνη καὶ εἰρήνη κατεφίλησαν...). It introduces the third psalm of the Ninth Hour, the Prayer of David, Psalm 85 (LXX Ps. 85:1–2; 'Приклони, Господи, ухо Твое, и услыши мя...').
[^122]: Folio 36v continues Psalm 85 (LXX Ps. 85:2–13), preserving the humble supplications for Divine mercy and guidance, the confession of God's solitary wonder-working power above all false gods ('яко велій еси Ты и творяй чудеса, Ты еси Богъ единъ'), and thanksgiving for deliverance from the lowest hades.
[^123]: Folio 37r concludes Psalm 85 (LXX Ps. 85:14–17), records the concluding metanias and daily troparion rubrics, and introduces the solemn penitential Troparia of the Ninth Hour appointed during the Fasts in Tone 8 ('Иже въ девятый часъ насъ ради плотію смерти вкусивый...'; Greek Ὁ ἐν τῇ ἐννάτῃ ὥρᾳ δι' ἡμᾶς σαρκὶ θανάτου γευσάμενος...), interspersed with verses from Psalm 118 (LXX Ps. 118:169, 170).
[^124]: Folio 37v provides the Christological Theotokion of the Ninth Hour ('Иже насъ ради рождейся отъ Дѣвы, и распятіе претерпѣвъ, Благій...'; Greek Ὁ δι' ἡμᾶς γεννηθεὶς ἐκ Παρθένου, καὶ σταύρωσιν ὑπομείνας, Ἀγαθέ...), the prayer of Azariah from the furnace of Babylon (Dan. 3:34–35 / LXX Pr. Azar. 11–12), forty repetitions of 'Lord, have mercy', and the Lenten Troparion of the Good Thief in Tone 8 ('Видя разбойникъ Начальника жизни на крестѣ висяща...').
[^125]: Folio 38r completes the Stavrotheotokion of the Ninth Hour ('Ягньца и Пастыря и Спаса міру на крестѣ зрящи...'), followed by forty repetitions of 'Lord, have mercy', the Prayer of the Hours ('Иже на всякое время...'), the Prayer of Saint Ephrem the Syrian with three great prostrations and twelve metanias, and introduces the ancient Mid-Hour of the Ninth Hour (Междочасіе 9-го часа / Interhorium Nonae) with three prostrations and Psalm 112 (LXX Ps. 112:1–4; 'Хвалите, отроцы, Господа...').
[^126]: Folio 38v completes Psalm 112 (LXX Ps. 112:4–9), and introduces the second psalm of the Mid-Hour of the Ninth Hour, Psalm 137 (LXX Ps. 137:1–4; 'Исповѣдаюся Тебѣ, Господи, всѣмъ сердцемъ моимъ...').
[^127]: Folio 39r reveals an epigraphic textual lacuna between fol. 38v and fol. 39r in this surviving exemplar: following the opening four verses of Psalm 137 on fol. 38v, fol. 39r opens directly with the concluding lines of the Mid-Hour supplication to Christ the Good Shepherd ('...грѣшныя, да не отлучиши мене стада Твоего, Пастырю добрый...'), followed by the Mid-Hour troparia ('Яко разбойникъ исповѣдаяся...' and 'Насъ ради распеншагося...'), thirty repetitions of 'Lord, have mercy', the Prayer of St. Ephrem, and the opening lines of the canonical Prayer of St. Basil the Great ('Владыко Господи Іисусе Христе Боже нашъ, долготерпѣливый о нашихъ согрѣшеніихъ...'; Greek Δέσποτα Κύριε Ἰησοῦ Χριστὲ ὁ Θεὸς ἡμῶν, ὁ μακροθυμήσας ἐπὶ τοῖς ἡμῶν πλημμελήмаσι...).
[^128]: Folio 39v continues the Prayer of Saint Basil the Great at the Mid-Hour of the Ninth Hour, preserving its profound penitential theology of the Crucifixion, the admission of human fragility and straying from the divine path, and the supplication for the mortification of carnal mind and the putting on of the new man created in righteousness.
[^129]: Folio 40r completes the Prayer of Saint Basil the Great with its Trinitarian doxology ('...и Тебѣ славу возсылаемъ, со Безначальнымъ Ти Отцемъ, и съ Пресвятымъ и Благимъ и Животворящимъ Ти Духомъ, нынѣ и присно и во вѣки вѣкомъ. Аминь'). It introduces the monumental office of Vespers with an ornate cinnabar title ('ЧИНЪ ВЕЧЕРНИ ПОЕМЫЯ') and detailed 15th-century monastic Typikon rubrics governing the sounding of the great semantron or bell by the lamp-lighter (кандилаптъ / candelaptes) following the hegumen's blessing.
