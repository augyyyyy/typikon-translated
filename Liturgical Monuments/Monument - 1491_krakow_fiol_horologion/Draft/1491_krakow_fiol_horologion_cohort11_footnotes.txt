[^150]: Folio 50v witnesses the conclusion of the introductory Trisagion prayers of Great Compline (Великое повечерие) and the recitation of Psalm 69 (LXX Ps. 69; 'Боже, въ помощь мою воньми...'), followed by the crucial rubric for the Great Penitential Canon of St. Andrew of Crete (Канонъ великій святаго Андрея Критскаго). In ancient Studite and early Jerusalem Horologia, such as Fiol's 1491 witness, the Great Canon is divided across the first four days of Clean Week (Monday through Thursday) and chanted at Great Compline. The text explicitly directs: 'аще есть недѣля первая святаго поста, поемъ великій канонъ святаго Андрея Критскаго, раздѣляющи на 4 части, куюждо пѣснь, яко съвръшити въ 4 дни куюждо часть'.

[^151]: Folio 51r concludes the Lenten rubric for the Great Canon with the standard post-ode Marian hymnody ('Достойно есть' and 'Честнѣйшую Херувимъ') and initiates the first station of Great Compline with Psalm 4 (LXX Ps. 4; 'Внегда воззвахъ, услыша мя, Боже правды моея...'). The leaf concludes with the inscription for Psalm 6, exhibiting the epigraphic witness 'въ конецъ пѣніи о семомь' (concerning the eighth / octave, an archaic East Slavic scribal rendering or phonetic confusion for 'о осмомъ', Greek ὑπὲρ τῆς ὀγδόης). The quire signature or leaf coordination marks this as the opening of folio 51.

[^152]: Folio 51v contains the complete text of Psalm 6 (LXX Ps. 6; 'Господи, да не яростію Твоею обличиши мене...'), the first of the seven classical Penitential Psalms, expressing the soul's groaning, nocturnal weeping, and bodily languor in the face of spiritual death and divine judgment. The leaf closes with the rubricated cinnabar heading for Psalm 12 (LXX Ps. 12; 'Псаломъ Давыдовъ, 12').

[^153]: Folio 52r witnesses the complete text of Psalm 12 (LXX Ps. 12; 'Доколѣ, Господи, забудеши мя до конца...'), followed by the liturgical closure of the first tri-psalm unit of Great Compline: the Lesser Doxology ('Слава... И нынѣ...'), the threefold Alleluia with three prostrations ('Аллилуіа трижды, и дѣле три'), and three Kyrie eleisons ('Господи, помилуй, трижды'). It then commences the second tri-psalm group of the first station with Psalm 24 (LXX Ps. 24:1–3; 'Къ Тебѣ, Господи, воздвигохъ душу мою...'), preserving the opening acrostic petition of trust and redemption.

[^154]: Folio 52v continues Psalm 24 (LXX Ps. 24:4–14), setting forth David's supplicatory plea for divine guidance ('Пути Твоя, Господи, скажи ми...'), forgiveness of the sins of youth and ignorance ('Грѣхъ юности моея и невѣдѣнія моего не помяни...'), and the covenantal blessing upon the meek and God-fearing. The orthography consistently maintains archaic Ruthenian/Middle East Slavic features, including nasal reflexes and jers.

[^155]: Folio 53r witnesses the conclusion of Psalm 24 (LXX Ps. 24:15–22; 'Очи мои выну ко Господу...'), concluding with the national redemption of Israel ('Избави, Боже, Израиля отъ всѣхъ скорбей его'). It immediately introduces the third Psalm of this unit, Psalm 30 (LXX Ps. 30; 'Псаломъ Давыдовъ о исступленіи, тридесятый', Greek ψалмὸς τῷ Δαυΐδ, ἐκστάσεως, λ҃), beginning with David's cry of refuge: 'На Тя, Господи, уповахъ, да не постыжуся въ вѣкъ...' (LXX Ps. 30:1–3).

[^156]: Folio 53v continues Psalm 30 (LXX Ps. 30:3–11), featuring the celebrated Christological and Compline surrender verse: 'Въ руцѣ Твои предамъ душу мою; избавилъ мя еси, Господи, Боже истины' (Luke 23:46; Ps. 30:6). The text articulates the acute physical and psychological suffering of the psalmist ('изнеможе нищетою крѣпость моя, и кости моя смятошася'), underscoring the deep penitential ethos of Great Compline.

[^157]: Folio 54r continues Psalm 30 (LXX Ps. 30:12–20), chronicling the psalmist's alienation ('паче всѣхъ врагъ моихъ быхъ поношеніе... быхъ яко сосудъ погубленъ'), the conspiracy against his life, and his renewed confession of faith ('Азъ же на Тя, Господи, уповахъ: рѣхъ: Ты еси Богъ мой; въ руку Твоею жребіи мои'). It concludes with the encomium of God's abundant goodness reserved for those who fear Him.

[^158]: Folio 54v witnesses the conclusion of Psalm 30 (LXX Ps. 30:20–25; 'Благословенъ Господь, яко удиви милость Свою въ градѣ обьстоянія... Мужайтеся, и да крѣпится сердце ваше, вси уповающеи на Господа'). It then introduces Psalm 90, the climactic protective canticle of Compline, headed by the cinnabar title: 'Хвала пѣсни Давыдовы, 90' (Greek Αἶνος ᾠδῆς τῷ Δαυΐδ, ϟ҃), transcribing the opening declaration of divine defense under the wings of the Almighty (LXX Ps. 90:1–4).

[^159]: Folio 55r continues Psalm 90 (LXX Ps. 90:4–14), embodying the premier nocturnal apotropaic prayer of the Byzantine rite against the terror of night, the flying arrow, the pestilence, and the noonday demon ('отъ срѣща и бѣса полуденнаго'). It contains the angelic guardianship promise ('яко Ангеломъ Своимъ заповѣсть о тебѣ...') cited in the Temptation in the Wilderness (Matt. 4:6; Luke 4:10–11) and concludes with the opening phrase of the divine oracle: 'Яко на Мя упова, и избавлю и' (LXX Ps. 90:14).
