[^190]: Folio 70v completes the Tone 5 Resurrectional Troparion ('и въскр҃снти оу́мръшаꙗ въ славно́е въскрсеніе свое́'). It provides the Tone 5 Resurrectional Theotokion ('Ра́доуисѧ двери гн҃ꙗ непроходимаꙗ...') and the Hypakoe ('А́гг҃льскыимь ꙁрако́мь оу́мь съмѫщающе...'). Lines 14–19 introduce Tone 6 with its Resurrectional Troparion ('А́гг҃льскыꙗ силы на гробѣ тво́емь...'), concluding with the catchword incipit in cinnabar for the Tone 6 Theotokion ('Иже бл҃ве'). Red marginal letterings identify the structural genres ('б҃' for Theotokion, 'ѵ' for Hypakoe).

[^191]: Folio 71r concludes the Tone 6 Resurrectional Theotokion ('...ннаꙗ нарекыисѧ себѣ мт҃рь · пріиде на съдѫрть вѡльндыь съвѣтѡмь...'). The text displays distinctive archaic Middle Ukrainian orthography ('съдѫрть' / 'сѫдръть' for 'страсть'; 'вѡльндыь' for 'вольнымь'). Lines 6–10 give the Tone 6 Hypakoe ('Вл҃ною животворною ти съ́мрътїю х҃е...'). Lines 10–15 witness Tone 7: the Resurrectional Troparion ('Раꙁдроуши кр҃стѡмь свои́мь съ́мрътъ...') and the Dogmatic Theotokion ('Ꙗко нашемоу въскр҃нію сокровище...').

[^192]: Folio 71v opens with the Tone 7 Hypakoe ('И́же нашь ꙁракъ въспріе́млыи...'). Lines 3–7 contain the Tone 8 Resurrectional Troparion ('Съ вышинѣ съниде мл҃рде...'), followed by the Dogmatic Theotokion ('И́же на́ ради роже́исѧ ѡ дв҃ы...') and the Hypakoe ('Мѵ́роносица живодавномоу прѣстоаще гро́боу...'). The leaf concludes with the cinnabar rubric directing the singing of the two Sunday dismissal troparia after the Great Doxology ('сіа, тро́рѣ · по славословіи велицѣ , въ нп҃елѧ · сеже гл҃и').

[^193]: Folio 72r contains the two solemn Sunday troparia chanted following the Great Doxology: the troparion for odd tones (1, 3, 5, 7), 'Днесь спасение миру бысть...' (the opening words of which are trimmed at the top margin), and the troparion for even tones (2, 4, 6, 8), 'Въскр҃сь ѡ́ гроба, и́ оу́ꙁы растрѫꙁа...'. The bottom half of the leaf inaugurates a rare medieval calendar table recording the so-called 'Egyptian' or non-working days for September through January ('мц҃ь септе · а҃ до · ѕ҃ до · и҃ ве...'), noting whether the prohibition applies until noon ('до' / 'до полоудна'), in the evening ('ве'), or for the entire day ('весь'). The leaf bears the printed quire signature 'к҃ꙁ' (quire 27).

[^194]: Folio 72v completes the calendar table of Egyptian and fasting days for February through August. In lines 2–4, an extraordinary cinnabar monastic statute is embedded: 'вı же ѡтъ лаꙁарѧ въ в҃ · въ вт҃рѫ · срѣдоу · четвертокъ · пѧтокъ · всю · по клѧтвою ст҃ыхъ ѡц҃ь · ктѡ не празнуе' (Likewise from Lazarus Saturday: on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, all week, under the curse of the holy fathers, whosoever does not keep it as a feast). The second half of the folio opens a new hymnographic division under the monumental vyaz headpiece: 'БГ҃ОРОДИЧНЫ ПРОТИВО ГЛ҃ОУ ТРОРЕН КР҃Тꙋ И БЦ҃И' (Theotokia according to the Eight Tones: Troparia to the Cross and Theotokos), providing Tone 1.

[^195]: Folio 73r continues the dismissal Theotokia and Stavrotheotokia of the Eight Tones: Tone 2 ('Мт҃и ст҃аа прч҃тго свѣта...' and 'Чт҃ныи кр҃тъ сн҃а твое́го...'), Tone 3 ('Кождо и́де же сп҃саетсѧ...' and 'Жезлъ силы стяжавше кр҃то...'), and Tone 4 ('Стѣна непобѣдимаꙗ хрстіано́мъ є́си...'). Lines 17–19 begin the Tone 4 Stavrotheotokion in cinnabar ink ('На древѣ висѧще воꙁвѣи на сн҃а твоего...').

[^196]: Folio 73v completes the Tone 4 Stavrotheotokion ('...двы въсіꙗвыи въсемоу мироу х҃е б҃е...'). It provides Tone 5 Stavrotheotokion ('Кр҃тѡмь сы́на твоего · бг҃обл҃годатнаа...'), Tone 6 Theotokion ('Прѣславноу́ю х҃воу мт҃рь...') and Stavrotheotokion ('Тебе рожьшіа х҃е пр҃но дв҃аа...'), and opens Tone 7 with the Theotokion ('Радоуисѧ тебе въмѣстилаго на нб҃сехь въ оу́тробѣ...').

[^197]: Folio 74r concludes the Tone 7 Theotokion and gives its Stavrotheotokion ('Ра́доуисѧ бц҃е чт҃аа пр҃но дв҃о · на кр҃тѣ ꙁрящи...'). It then gives Tone 8: the Theotokion ('Тѧ стѣноу и пристанище и́мамы...') and Stavrotheotokion ('А́гнца и пастыря и сп҃са мироу...'). Lines 14–18 mark a monumental transition in the Fiol Horologion: the opening of the Menologion (Part III) under the decorative cinnabar vyaz title: 'МЦ҃Ь СЕПТЕВРИИ ИМЯ дній · л҃', marking the New Year / Indiction and the memory of St. Symeon the Stylite (September 1). The folio bears quire signature 'к҃ѳ' (quire 29).

[^198]: Folio 74v records the complete calendar commemorations for September 1 (St. Aeithalas, the 40 Holy Women and Deacon Ammoun, St. Callistus, Evodius, and Hermogenes, Joshua son of Nun, and the Great Fire of Constantinople) and sets forth the complete rubrics for Great Vespers of the Indiction and St. Symeon: Stichera at 'Lord, I have cried' (to the Indiction 3, to St. Symeon 3 podobny and 4 automela), Doxasticon, Old Testament Paremias from Isaiah (61:1–9), Leviticus (26:3–12), and Wisdom (4:7–15), litanies, and the Aposticha.

[^199]: Folio 75r completes the Aposticha stichera and Psalm versicles (LXX Ps. 64:2, 5, 12) for September 1. It provides the Dismissal Troparion of the Indiction, Tone 2 ('И́же въсеꙗ твари съдѣтелю...'), the Troparion of St. Symeon the Stylite, Tone 1 ('Тръпѣнію стлъпь бысть...'), and the Dismissal Theotokion ('Ра́доуисѧ бл҃гослове́наа бц҃е дв҃о...').
