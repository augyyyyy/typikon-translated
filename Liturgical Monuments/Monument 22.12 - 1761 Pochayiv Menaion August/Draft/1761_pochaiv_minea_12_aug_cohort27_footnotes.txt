[^840]: The hymn refers to the tradition in the Life of the Hieromartyr Eutyches (August 24) where a lioness loosed against him in the amphitheater was miraculously endowed with human speech, praising the God of the Christians before the assembled crowd.
[^841]: Slavonic: *мункѡвъ*, a printer's mechanical contraction/error for *мученикѡвъ* (of martyrs).
[^842]: August 25 in the Byzantine calendar commemorates both the Translation/Return of the Relics of the Holy Apostle Bartholomew (to the Lipari Islands) and the Memory of the Holy Apostle Titus, Bishop of Gortyna in Crete.
[^843]: Slavonic: *му́нкѡмъ*, another recurring contraction in this edition for *мученикѡмъ* (martyrs), referring to the relics of the four martyrs (Lucian, Gregory, Acacius, and Eugene) whose sarcophagi accompanied that of Bartholomew.
[^844]: The Slavonic acrostic of the first Canon (to St. Bartholomew) by St. Joseph the Hymnographer reads: *Пѣ́сньми почита́ю до́браго моего̀ предста́теля* ("With hymns I honor my good protector").
[^845]: The Slavonic acrostic of the second Canon (to St. Titus) by St. Theophanes Graptos reads: *Па́ѵлова оучени́ка ті́та пою̀ премꙋ́дреннѡ* ("Wisely do I sing of Paul's disciple Titus").
[^846]: Slavonic: *бг҃обо́жїа*, an apparent misprint for *безбо́жїа* (godlessness) or *многобо́жїа* (polytheism).
[^847]: Ode 4 Heirmos of the Canon of St. Titus is based upon the theophany of Habakkuk (Habakkuk 3:3; Septuagint: "God shall come from Teman...").
[^848]: Ode 5 Heirmos of the Canon of St. Titus draws upon Isaiah 26:9-10 ("With my soul have I desired Thee in the night...").
[^849]: The Ikos of St. Bartholomew recounts the tradition of his leaden reliquary being cast into the sea by pagans in Anastasiopolis/Albanopolis and miraculously sailing westward across the Mediterranean Sea to Lipari.
[^850]: Ode 8 Heirmos of St. Bartholomew (*Ѻ́троки благочести̑выя въ пещѝ...*) is the standard Tone 4, Ode 8 Heirmos from the Heirmologion.
