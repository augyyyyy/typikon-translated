[^851]: The Slavonic text reads "плещи̑ дбо́ ны́нѣ мнѣ̀ дае́тъ", where *дбо́* is a printed contraction/misprint for *дѣ́во* (O Virgin). The phrase *плещи̑ дае́тъ* (literally "gives [its] shoulders/back") translates the Greek idiom νῶτα δίδωσι ("turns its back / flees"), referring to the turning flaming sword of Genesis 3:24 withdrawing from the faithful through the Incarnation.
[^852]: Slavonic: *свере́тника* (variant form of *све́рстника* / *сверѣ́тника*, peer or spouse of equal standing). Saint Natalia, though a woman in the eyes of the pagan magistrates, spiritually fortified her husband Saint Adrian during his interrogation and torture.
[^853]: Slavonic: *свѣ́тства*, a typographical error in the 1761 Pochaiv print for *сѣ́тства* (snares/entanglements) or *сꙋ́етства* (vanities). The standard Church Slavonic Menaion reads *сѣ́тства* (*сѣ́тей*, snares).
[^854]: Slavonic: *десѧ́тица сꙋгꙋ́баѧ и҆ тро́ица мꙋ́ченикъ* (literally "a double decade and a triad of martyrs"). This refers to the twenty-three Christians in Nicomedia whose unwavering confession under torture moved Saint Adrian to profess Christ and join them in martyrdom.
[^855]: According to the hagiographical account, Saint Natalia secretly retrieved Saint Adrian's severed hand after his martyrdom, wrapped it in costly cloths, and kept it with her until her peaceful repose in Argyropolis.
[^856]: Slavonic: *ѡ҆боже́нїѧ* (deification / theosis). The hymnographic tradition identifies the fall of man in Genesis 3 as the serpent's deceitful envy of man's calling to likeness unto God.
[^857]: Slavonic: *безстра́стїе ѻ҆́гненно* (fiery dispassion/apatheia). In the ascetic vocabulary of the Desert Fathers and Saint Poemen the Great, *apatheia* is not passive stoicism or cold apathy, but the luminous, burning purity of love purged of all disordered passions by the divine fire.
