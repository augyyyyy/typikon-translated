The fact noted by us of the dependence of the Typikon of the Athonite Hilandar Monastery upon that of Evergetis will become completely understandable to us if we recall in what relations to the Evergetis Monastery stood the ktetor of the Hilandar Monastery, Archbishop Sava, son of Stefan Nemanja, Grand Župan of Serbia. In the Life of this saint, "written by Domentijan, hieromonk of Hilandar on the Holy Mount Athos, disciple of Saint Sava", the Evergetis Monastery is twice termed "his own" [^77]; in this monastery the Venerable Sava stopped each time he came on business to Constantinople [^78]; from the God-given gold pointed out to him by "a certain woman of goodly countenance and devout", Saint Sava "gave one part in Tsargrad to the monastery of the Most Holy Theotokos Evergetis" [^79]; hither, to this monastery, Archbishop Sava "first having come made obeisance" [^80] upon his second return from his journey to Palestine, and so forth. All this speaks to us of the fact that the Venerable Sava of Serbia had special and vivid ties with the Constantinopolitan Evergetis Monastery, which render completely natural the fact of the dependence of the Hilandar Typikon upon that of Evergetis.

To Chapter 8 "On Vigils" of the Hilandar Typikon corresponds Chapter 8 "On vigils, when they ought to take place" (*περὶ τῶν ἀγρυπνιῶν, πότε ὀφείλουσι γίνεσθαι*) of the Evergetis Typikon, which literally reads thus: "And vigils also according to the due pattern ought to take place both on every Sunday feast and on commemorations of the other saints, which we shall learn from the Synaxarion" (*«Καὶ τὰς ἀγρυπνίας δὲ κατὰ τὸν τύπον ὀφειλόμενον γίνεσθαι ἔν τε πάσῃ κυριακῇ ἑορτῇ καὶ ἐν λοιπῶν ἁγίων μνήμαις, ἃς ἐκ τοῦ Συναξαρίου μαθησόμεθα»*) (p. 624). An indirect, but at the same time sufficiently weighty foundation for the proposition that the Hilandar monastery until the 14th century was completely unacquainted with the orders of the Jerusalem divine service and in its liturgical practice was guided by its own special Typikon, can be found in the manuscript Nikodim Typikon of the Belgrade Library. From the extensive preface to it, written in the year 1319, we learn that the Serbian Archbishop Nikodim, who prior to that was hegumen of the Hilandar monastery, for the first time became acquainted with the Jerusalem Ordo during his stay in Constantinople during the patriarchate of Niphon I (1313–1315), through the Patriarchs of Jerusalem Athanasius and of Antioch, "holding the Typikon according to the custom of the holy city of Jerusalem, according to the Ordo of Saint Sabbas the Desert-Dweller". Upon his accession to the archiepiscopal throne of the Serbian Church, Archbishop Nikodim conceived the plan to make a translation of this Ordo into the Serbian language and introduce it into the liturgical practice of his Church. "I sent to the imperial city to the monastery of the holy Forerunner and Baptist John," says Archbishop Nikodim, "and there was brought to me this Jerusalem Typikon, and I translated it into our tongue from the letters of the Greek language" [^84]. If at Hilandar they had known of the Jerusalem orders already during the life of the Venerable Sava, then there would have been no need anew to translate the Jerusalem Ordo, and moreover by a person who was well acquainted with the orders of the Serbian Athonite Hilandar Monastery. In its turn, there would have been no need afterwards, in the year 1331, for Gervasius, hegumen of the Hilandar Monastery, to give an order to a certain Roman [^85] to copy the Nikodim manuscript of the Typikon for use in the liturgical practice of this monastery.
