[^186]: Sessional Hymn (Kathisma) in Tone 7 chanted after Antiphon 3 and the First Passion Gospel. The accompanying rubric specifically directs: *(Не сѣди́мъ же на сицевь́хъ сѣда́льнахъ, за є҆́же сщ҃е́нникꙋ кади́ти ст҃ы́й ѻ҆лта́рь, но стоя́ще пое́мъ ѧ҆̀)* — "We do not sit at such sessional hymns, because the priest censes the holy altar, but standing we sing them." In the liturgical structure of the Passion Matins, sessional hymns follow every three antiphons (after the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, and 5th groups of antiphons), accompanied by a censing of the holy table and the entire nave, while the faithful remain standing holding lighted candles. The hymn meditates on the Mystical Supper, where Christ nourished His disciples while convicting Judas of his covert treachery.
[^187]: The Second Passion Gospel: John 18:1–28 (section 58). This pericope covers the departure of Jesus and His disciples across the brook Cedron into the garden of Gethsemane; the arrival of Judas with the cohort of soldiers and temple officers bearing lanterns, torches, and weapons; Christ's majestic self-revelation ("I am He") causing them to draw back and fall to the ground; Peter striking the high priest's servant Malchus; Christ's arrest and binding; His being led first to Annas; Peter's first denial outside the high priest's court; the interrogation of Jesus concerning His disciples and doctrine; the officer striking Jesus with the palm of his hand; and Christ being sent bound unto Caiaphas the high priest.
[^188]: Sessional Hymn in Tone 7 chanted after Antiphon 6 and the Second Passion Gospel (*Кі́й тѧ̀ ѻ҆́бразъ І҆ꙋ́до, преда́телѧ Сп҃сꙋ содѣ́ла* / *Ποῖός σε τρόπος Ἰούδα, προδότην τοῦ Σωτῆρος εἰργάσατο*). A celebrated patristic and hymnographic interrogation of Judas's motives, contrasting the singular privileges granted to him as an Apostle—sharing in the Apostolic choir, the charismatic gift of performing healings, reclining at the Mystical Supper, and having his feet washed by the Creator—with his base ingratitude and avarice. The hymn concludes by magnifying Christ's boundless long-suffering and great mercy.
[^189]: The Third Passion Gospel: Matthew 26:57–75 (section 109). Christ is brought before Caiaphas the high priest, where the scribes and elders are assembled; false witnesses seek testimony against Him; Caiaphas adjures Him by the living God to declare whether He is the Christ, the Son of God; Jesus confesses His divine identity and session at the right hand of Power; the high priest rends his clothes and condemns Him for blasphemy; the council spits in His face, buffets Him, and strikes Him with the palms of their hands; simultaneously, Peter sits in the courtyard, where he is recognized three times and denies the Lord with an oath; the cock crows, Peter remembers the word of the Lord, and he goes out weeping bitterly.
[^190]: Sessional Hymn in Tone 8 chanted after Antiphon 9 and the Third Passion Gospel (*Ѽ ка́кѡ І҆ꙋ́да и҆ногда̀ тво́й ᲂу҆чн҃къ* / *Ὦ πῶς Ἰούδας ποτὲ σοῦ ὁ μαθητής*). The hymn contrasts Judas's former fellowship as an Apostle with his treacherous bargain with the chief priests, whom he approached with hypocritical zeal, claiming to deliver Him Who "hath broken the Law and defiled the Sabbath."
[^191]: The Fourth Passion Gospel: John 18:28–19:16 (section 54). Christ is led early from Caiaphas unto the Roman praetorium before Pontius Pilate; the Jews refuse to enter the praetorium lest they be defiled; Pilate interrogates Jesus within concerning His kingship; Christ delivers His classic declaration that His Kingdom is not of this world, and that He came into the world to bear witness unto the truth; Pilate utters his cynical question "What is truth?" and attempts to release Jesus according to the Passover custom; the crowd demands Barabbas the robber; Pilate scourges Jesus and the soldiers crown Him with thorns, clothe Him in a purple robe, and strike Him; Pilate presents Him to the multitude with the immortal words "Behold the Man!" (*Ἴδε ὁ ἄνθρωπος* / *Сѐ Человѣ́къ*); the chief priests cry "Crucify Him!"; Pilate washes his hands of His blood; and upon the chief priests' declaration "We have no king but Caesar," Pilate delivers Him over to be crucified.
[^192]: Greek recensional variant troparion in Antiphon 10 (*Оу҆мирѝ мі́ръ, ѿ Дв҃ы и҆зво́ливый Гдⷭ҇и* / *Εἰρήνευσον τὸν κόσμον, ὁ ἐκ Παρθένου ἑλόμενος Κύριε*). In the 1907 Lviv Stauropegion edition, this troparion is printed with the explicit editorial rubrical heading `[ѿ Гре́ческагѡ.]` ("From the Greek") enclosed in brackets. This witnesses to the historical scholarly and recensional collation performed by the Stauropegion editors, incorporating Greek Triodion hymnographic witnesses into the Church Slavonic textus receptus.
[^193]: Sessional Hymn in Tone 8 chanted after Antiphon 12 and the Fourth Passion Gospel (*Е҆гда̀ предста́лъ є҆сѝ Каїа́фѣ Бж҃е* / *Ἡνίκα τῷ Καϊάφᾳ παρίστασο ὁ Θεός*). The hymn depicts the cosmic dread of the heavenly powers at the sight of the Supreme Judge standing before earthly tribunals (Caiaphas and Pilate), and His willing ascent upon the Cross between two thieves, numbered among the transgressors (Isaiah 53:12) to save fallen mankind.
[^194]: The Fifth Passion Gospel: Matthew 27:3–32 (section 111). Judas repents, confesses that he has betrayed innocent blood, casts down the thirty pieces of silver in the temple, and goes and hangs himself; the chief priests purchase the potter's field with the blood-money, fulfilling the prophecy of Zechariah/Jeremiah (Matthew 27:9–10); Jesus stands before Pilate the governor and remains silent; Pilate's wife sends word of her dream warning him to have nothing to do with that just man; the multitude chooses Barabbas and cries "Let Him be crucified!"; the Roman soldiers mock Christ in the praetorium, stripping Him, arraying Him in a scarlet robe, plaiting a crown of thorns upon His head, and placing a reed in His right hand; and leading Him away to Golgotha, they compel Simon of Cyrene to bear His cross.
[^195]: The Fifteenth Antiphon of Great Friday Matins, Tone 6 (*Дне́сь ви́ситъ на дре́вѣ* / *Σήμερον κρεμᾶται ἐπὶ ξύλου*). This hymn is universally recognized as the poetic and theological masterpiece of Great Friday hymnography. Composed in a sequence of six majestic parallel antitheses contrasting Christ's divine sovereignty with His kenotic humiliation: (1) He Who suspended the earth upon the waters hangs upon the Tree; (2) the King of angels is crowned with thorns; (3) He Who wraps the heavens in clouds is wrapped in a mock purple robe; (4) He Who freed Adam in the Jordan receives blows upon the face; (5) the Bridegroom of the Church is transfixed with nails; and (6) the Son of the Virgin is pierced with a lance. The antiphon concludes with the celebrated triple prostration: "We worship Thy Sufferings, O Christ! / We worship Thy Sufferings, O Christ! / We worship Thy Sufferings, O Christ! / Show us also Thy glorious Resurrection!"
[^196]: The Sessional Hymn (Kathisma / Apolytikion) in Tone 4 chanted after the 15th Antiphon and prior to the Sixth Passion Gospel (*И҆скꙋпи́лъ ны̀ є҆сѝ ѿ кла́твы зако́нныѧ* / *Ἐξηγόρασας ἡμᾶς ἐκ τῆς κατάρας τοῦ νόμου*). Based directly upon Galatians 3:13 ("Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us: for it is written, Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree"), this troparion summarizes the soteriological doctrine of the Atonement: by Christ's voluntary Crucifixion, piercing with the lance, and shedding of His precious Blood upon Golgotha, mankind is delivered from the condemnation of the Mosaic Law and endowed with divine immortality.
