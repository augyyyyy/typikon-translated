*Verses:*
*Standing in the midst of the feast, He teaches:*
*Christ the Messiah in the midst of the doctors.*

On Wednesday of the Paralytic, we celebrate the feast of Mid-Pentecost, on account of the honor of the two great feasts: of Pascha, I say, and of Pentecost, as connecting and uniting both. And this came to pass thus: after Christ had wrought the supernatural wonder upon the paralytic, the Jews, being scandalized concerning the paralytic because it was done on the Sabbath, sought to kill Him. He therefore departed into Galilee, and staying in the mountains there, He worked a miracle with five loaves and two fish, feeding five thousand men besides women and children. Afterwards, when the feast of Tabernacles was at hand—and that also was a great feast among the Jews—He went up to Jerusalem and walked secretly. And in the midst of this feast, entering into the sanctuary, He taught. [Now the sanctuary is a sacred place for God alone, wherein love and peace, wherein faith and chastity dwell. Therefore also the bishop, doing good unto God, ought to be peaceful and meek, not contentious, not deceitful, not a lover of money, that first he may cause God to dwell in his own soul, and afterwards may unite the people after his own manner and path. And the soul of every faithful person may likewise be called a sanctuary, having been exceedingly purified: for I will dwell in you and walk in you, says the Lord.][^621] And all marveled at His teaching, and envying Him they said: How does this man know letters, having never learned? For being the New Adam, He knew even as the first Adam, and was filled with all wisdom, and as God. Again, all murmured, and were all intent on murdering Him. But Christ, employing refutations against them, as they were contending concerning the Sabbath, said: Why seek ye to kill Me? And they said: Hast thou a demon? Who seeks to kill thee? But He again addresses what went before, saying: If ye contend concerning the Law, why are ye angry with Me, because I made an entire man whole on the Sabbath? If Moses gave this commandment, he bids you to break the Sabbath for the sake of circumcision. Having spoken many things unto them concerning this, He showed Himself to be the Giver of the Law and equal unto the Father. And especially on the last great day of the feast, He had stones cast at Him by them, but the stone touched Him not at all; and passing by from thence, He found a man blind from his birth, and granted him sight. Now it is to be known that the Jews had three very great feasts: The first is the Passover, which is celebrated in the first month, having a commemoration of the crossing of the Red Sea. The second is Pentecost, commemorating the sojourn in the wilderness after the passing through of the Red Sea: for they spent fifty days in the wilderness until they received the Law of Moses; and also in honor of the number seven, which was held in honor among them. And the third feast was the veneration of the tents and tabernacles, making commemoration of the Tabernacle which Moses saw in the cloud on the mountain, and commanded Bezaleel, the chief craftsman, to construct; which was celebrated for seven days, having a remembrance of the ingathering of the fruits and of the resting in the wilderness. When that feast was celebrated, Jesus stood and cried with a loud voice: If any man thirst, let him come unto Me and drink! Thus in His teaching Christ the Messiah showed Himself so great, being our Mediator and Reconciler with His eternal Father. Therefore, having this present celebration and naming it Mid-Pentecost, we hymn Christ the Messiah, and set forth the honor which comes from both of the two great feasts. For this reason also, I believe, the feast of the Samaritan Woman is celebrated after it, because there also many things were spoken concerning Christ the Messiah, and concerning water and concerning thirst, as here in John the Evangelist; and the Blind Man stands before the Samaritan Woman.[^622] By Thine immeasurable mercy, Christ our God, have mercy on us. Amen.

Always ungrateful, they that lived in the wilderness provoked the Benefactor to anger through envy, blaspheming, moving unrighteous tongues, and meditating vain things.

*Let us bless the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit: the Lord!*
*Triadicon:*
One God is the Trinity: the Father having not passed over into sonship, nor the Son turned into procession; but the proper properties of each: Light, God, Three, do I glorify for ever!

*Both now and for ever and unto ages of ages. Amen. Theotokion:*
Tell us, how didst thou give birth unto Him Who timelessly shone forth from the Father, and is hymned with the Holy Spirit? Even as the One Light Who was well pleased to be born of thee, O Theotokos!

*Then: We praise, we bless, and we worship the Lord...*
*Katavasia:*
Angels and heavens, bless, sing, and exalt above all unto all ages Him Who sits upon the throne of glory and is unceasingly glorified as God!

*More honorable than the Cherubim is not sung.*
*Ode 9. Heirmos:*[^627]
A cornerstone was cut out from thee, O Virgin, the uncut mountain, not by the hand of man: Christ, Who joined together the sundered natures: therefore rejoicing, we magnify thee, O Theotokos!

*N. Troparion:*
Having been instructed by Christ in a new and pure manner of life, let us all zealously strive to preserve it profitably unto the end, that we may receive the coming of the Holy Spirit.

*E. Troparion:*
Having been called back unto heavenly citizenship by the power of mediation of Him Who emptied Himself even unto the form of a servant and exalted us: Him let us worthily magnify!
