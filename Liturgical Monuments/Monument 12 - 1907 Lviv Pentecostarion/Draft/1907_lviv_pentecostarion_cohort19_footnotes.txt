[^346]: Liturgical time and structure of Holy Saturday Vespers: The service is appointed for the tenth hour of the day (approximately 4:00 PM), marking the transition toward the Paschal night. It unites Vespers with the Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great.
[^347]: Sacerdotal vesting and Proskomedia timing: Unlike the typical Byzantine order where the Proskomedia is completed before the Liturgy begins, on Holy Saturday (as on the eve of the Nativity and Theophany) the Proskomedia is performed during the singing of "Lord, I have cried" while the deacon and concelebrants vest, allowing the extensive Old Testament readings to serve their ancient baptismal catechetical purpose.
[^348]: The Doxastikon of Holy Saturday (*Дне́шнїй де́нь та́йнѡ*): Attributed to the Studite or early Byzantine hymnographers, this hymn parallels Genesis 2:2-3 with Christ's Sabbath rest in the tomb, concluding with His return through the Resurrection to life and eternal glory.
[^349]: Sacerdotal Entrance with the Holy Gospel: Because the Liturgy of St. Basil the Great follows immediately after the Old Testament readings, the Little Entrance of the Liturgy is combined with the Vespers entrance, made with the Gospel Book rather than the censer.
[^350]: The Fifteen Old Testament Lections of Holy Saturday: The ancient Byzantine and Jerusalem cathedral rite appointed these extended readings during the paschal baptismal vigil, while the catechumens were being baptized, chrismated, and led in procession into the temple for the Paschal Eucharist.
[^351]: Typology of the Paschal Lamb: Exodus 12:46 prescribes that no bone of the Paschal lamb shall be broken, fulfilled at Christ's Crucifixion (John 19:36), establishing the central Paschal typology of Christ as the true Lamb of God.
[^352]: The Sign of the Prophet Jonah: Chanted in its entirety across chapters 1 through 4, the Book of Jonah serves as the preeminent Scriptural type of Christ's three-day burial and descent into hades, as declared by Christ Himself in Matthew 12:40.
[^353]: The Cessation of Manna and the Archistrategos: Joshua 5 prefigures the cessation of the Old Testament types (the manna) upon entering the Promised Land and eating the unleavened bread, followed by the Christophany of the Commander (Archistrategos) of the Host of the Lord, venerated by Joshua as holy ground (parallel to Exodus 3:5).
[^354]: The Canticle of Moses at the Red Sea (Exodus 15:1-19): Chanted antiphonally with the refrains "Let us sing unto the Lord: for He hath been gloriously glorified!" following the Sixth Reading, standing as the universal Christian canticle of baptismal illumination, resurrection victory over hades, and deliverance from spiritual Pharaoh.
[^355]: The Prophecy of Zephaniah on the Day of the Resurrection: In Zephaniah 3:8, the LXX text explicitly reads: *ὑπόμεινόν με, λέγει Κύριος, εἰς ἡμέραν ἀναστάσεώς μου εἰς μαρτύριον* ("Wait for Me, saith the Lord, unto the day of My resurrection as a witness"), celebrated as a direct prophetic witness of Christ's resurrection.
[^356]: Elijah, the Widow of Zarephath, and the Inexhaustible Cruse: The miracle of the jar of flour and the cruse of oil that did not fail, followed by the resurrection of the widow's son by Elijah breathing threefold upon him, serves as an Old Testament type of both the inexhaustible Eucharistic gifts and the universal resurrection from the dead.
[^357]: The Robe of Salvation and the Nuptial Joy of the Church: Isaiah 61:10–62:5 provides the baptismal vesting verse ("My soul shall rejoice in the Lord: for He hath clothed me with the robe of salvation...") and celebrates the mystical marriage of the Church, redeemed and adorned for her Bridegroom.
[^358]: The Binding of Isaac (the Akedah): Genesis 22:1–15 stands as the supreme Old Testament sacrifice prefiguring the Father offering His Only-begotten Son on the Cross, carrying the wood of the sacrifice to Mount Moriah (Golgotha), and the ram caught in the thicket by its horns sparing Isaac.
