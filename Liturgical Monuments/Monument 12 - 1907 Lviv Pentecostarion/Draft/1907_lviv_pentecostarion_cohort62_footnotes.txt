[^2773]: The Cross-Resurrectional troparion highlights Christ stretching out His hands upon the Cross to heal the unrestrained reaching forth of Adam's hand in Eden (Genesis 3:6).
[^2774]: In the Canon of All Saints, Ode 6 begins with the letter Ω of the acrostic word *ΠΟΛΥΩΝΥΜΑ*, meditating on Christ as the precious cornerstone laid in Sion (Isaiah 28:16, 1 Peter 2:6).
[^2775]: Physical leaf p612 corresponds to printed folio 606 (`х҃ѕ`). Ode 6 of All Saints concludes with the letters N, Y, and M, followed by the Kontakion and Ikos of All Saints in Tone 8 and the reading of the Synaxarion.
[^2776]: The Kontakion of All Saints in Tone 8 *Ꙗ҆́кѡ нача́тки є҆стества̀* ("As the first-fruits of nature, the universe offereth unto Thee, O Lord, the God-bearing Martyrs") is attributed to St. Romanos the Melodist, serving as one of the most famous kontakia of the Byzantine liturgical year.
[^2777]: The Synaxarion for the Sunday of All Saints expounds three theological reasons why the Church established this feast immediately following Holy Pentecost: first, to demonstrate the universal fruit of the Holy Spirit's descent; second, to honor the countless anonymous saints whose names are known only to God across all lands; and third, to unite all the righteous into one common celebration since they struggled in one Christ.
[^2778]: Physical leaf p613 corresponds to printed folio 607 (`х҃з`). The Synaxarion recounts the historical dedication of the Church of All Saints in Constantinople by Emperor Leo VI the Wise (886–912) and provides an overview of the Triodion's liturgical journey from the fall of Adam to the heavenly gathering of all the saints.
[^2779]: Emperor Leo VI originally intended to dedicate the grand church to his pious first wife, Empress Theophano (d. 893/897), but when the Church hierarchy objected to immediate imperial canonization, Leo wisely dedicated the temple to All Saints of the whole world, noting: "Even if Theophano is holy, let her be numbered with all the saints."
[^2780]: The nine ranks of sanctity enumerated in the Synaxarion mirror the nine celestial angelic choirs: Forefathers, Patriarchs, Prophets, Apostles, Martyrs, Hierarchs, Hieromartyrs and Venerable Martyrs, Venerables and Righteous, and holy Women, with the Most Holy Theotokos exalted above all creation.
[^2781]: The Resurrectional heirmos of Ode 7 in Tone 8 *Бж҃їѧ снизхожде́нїа* ("God's condescension") is based on the Canticle of the Three Holy Children in the Babylonian furnace (Daniel 3:26-56 LXX).
[^2782]: Physical leaf p614 corresponds to printed folio 608 (`х҃и`). Ode 7 of the Canon of All Saints incorporates the letters T, A, Γ, M, A, T, A of the acrostic phrase *ΤΑΓΜΑΤΑ ΜΕΛΠΩ* ("I sing the ranks"), followed by Ode 8 of the four canons.
[^2783]: The Resurrectional heirmos of Ode 8 in Tone 8 *Седмери́цею пе́щь* ("The Chaldean tyrant furiously heated the furnace sevenfold") continues the hymnography of Daniel 3:57-88 LXX (The Song of the Three Children).
[^2784]: The Trinitarian troparion of Ode 8 articulates the orthodox dogma of the one uncreated essence (*є҆диноесте́ственно*) and three distinct Hypostases (*трїѵпоста́снагѡ є҆стества̀*): the unoriginate Father, the co-essential Word, and the co-reigning Spirit of one essence.
[^2785]: The Marian troparion implores the Virgin to quench the fiery darts of the adversary (Ephesians 6:16).
[^2786]: Physical leaf p615 corresponds to printed folio 609 (`х҃ѳ`). Ode 8 of All Saints concludes with the letters M, E, Λ, and Π, followed by the Magnificat rubric and Ode 9 of the Resurrectional and festive canons.
[^2787]: The troparion of All Saints in Ode 8 affirms that by communion with God's deifying energies and illumination by His uncreated light, the saints become "gods by grace" through drawing near unto God (*бо́зи быва́ете, къ Бг҃ꙋ приближе́нїемъ*), reflecting classical patristic theosis (divinization; cf. Psalm 81:6 LXX, John 10:34-35).
[^2788]: The Resurrectional heirmos of Ode 9 in Tone 8 *Оу҆жасе́ся ѡ҆ се́мъ нб҃о* ("Heaven was dismayed thereat") is based on the Canticle of the Theotokos (Magnificat, Luke 1:46-55) and Jeremiah 2:12 LXX.
[^2789]: The Christological troparion of Ode 9 confesses Christ in two natures unconfusedly and indivisibly united (*во двою̀ сꙋщество̀ нераздѣ́льнѡ и҆ неслїѧ́ннѡ*), directly expressing the Chalcedonian dogmatic definition.
[^2790]: Physical leaf p616 corresponds to printed folio 610 (`х҃і`). Ode 9 concludes with the final troparia of All Saints (letters E, Λ, Π, and Ω, completing the acrostic *Τῶν ἁγίων πάντων πολυώνυμα τάγματα μέλπω*), the Katavasia of the Akathist, and the first Sunday Resurrectional Exapostilarion.
[^2791]: The Katavasia of Ode 9 *Вся́къ земноро́дный* ("Let every earth-born mortal leap for joy, enlightened in spirit") is taken from the Canon of the Annunciation / Akathist Hymn (Tone 4).
[^2792]: The 1st Resurrectional Exapostilarion *Со оу҆чени́ки взы́демъ на го́рꙋ Галїле́йскꙋю* ("Let us go up with the disciples into the mountain of Galilee") corresponds to the 1st Matins Gospel reading (Matthew 28:16-20), recalling Christ's Great Commission to baptize all nations in the name of the Holy Trinity.
[^2793]: Physical leaf p617 corresponds to printed folio 611 (`х҃аі`). Matins proceeds with the Exapostilarion of All Saints, its Theotokion, and the Praises (Lauds, *На хвали́техъ*), comprising 4 Resurrectional stichera in Tone 8, 1 Anatolian sticheron in Tone 8, and 3 stichera of All Saints in Tone 4.
[^2794]: The Exapostilarion of All Saints *Крⷭ҇ти́телѧ и҆ Предте́чꙋ* ("The Baptist and Forerunner, the Apostles, Prophets...") is chanted to the melodic model *Tone 2, Podoben: "Give ear, ye women" (Жены̀ оу҆слы́шите)*, although the incipit of the automelon is omitted in the printed heading of the 1907 edition.
[^2795]: The melodic model *Tone 4, Podoben: "As one valiant among martyrs" (Ꙗ҆́кѡ до́блѧ)* corresponds to the canonical Greek automelon *Ὡς γενναῖον ἐν μάρτυσιν*, sung for the praises of the holy martyrs and righteous ones.
[^2796]: Physical leaf p618 corresponds to printed folio 612 (`х҃в`). Matins concludes with the final sticheron of All Saints, the 1st Matins Gospel Doxasticon in Tone 1 *На го́рꙋ Оу҆ченикѡ́мъ и҆дꙋ́щымъ*, the Great Doxology, and the First Hour with the Catechesis of St. Theodore the Studite; followed by the opening of the Divine Liturgy.
[^2797]: The 1st Matins Gospel Doxasticon in Tone 1 *На го́рꙋ Оу҆ченикѡ́мъ и҆дꙋ́щымъ* ("Unto the disciples going into the mountain...") is the work of Emperor Leo VI the Wise, meditating on Christ's Ascension and the promise of His perpetual presence.
[^2798]: At the Divine Liturgy of All Saints, the Beatitudes (Makarismoi) are taken from Tone 8 (4 troparia) and from Ode 6 of the Canon of All Saints (4 troparia).
[^2799]: The Epistle for the Sunday of All Saints is Hebrews 11:33–12:2 (Pericope 330), recounting the heroic faith and sufferings of the Old Testament saints and martyrs who "through faith subdued kingdoms, wrought righteousness, obtained promises."
[^2800]: Physical leaf p619 corresponds to printed folio 613 (`х҃гї`). The Divine Liturgy concludes with the Gospel according to Matthew 10:32-33, 37-38; 19:27-30 (Pericope 38), the Communion Hymn (LXX Ps 148:1 and 32:1), and a rubric governing the celebration when a temple is dedicated to All Saints, featuring a woodcut engraving of the Mother of God and Child.
[^2801]: The rubric notes that at the agape meal (*на трапе́зѣ*), consolation is given to the brethren in anticipation of the Apostles' Fast (*гото́вѧщымся къ по́стномꙋ по́двигꙋ*), which customarily begins on the Monday following All Saints Sunday (or the second Monday after Pentecost in ancient Ruthenian monastic Typika).
[^2802]: For a parish or monastery dedicated to All Saints, the Typikon directs a full Vigil service with Polyeleos, Megalynarion (*Велича́емъ ва́съ А҆́пⷭ҇ли*), and Selected Psalm (LXX Ps 111:1 *Бл҃же́нъ мꙋ́жъ боя́йся Гдⷭ҇а*).
[^2803]: The woodcut engraving depicts the Most Holy Mother of God holding the divine Infant Christ in an oval mandorla of glory, symbolizing the Church of the Firstborn who surround the Queen of Heaven.
[^2804]: Physical leaf p620 corresponds to printed folio 614 (`х҃ді`). Opening of the Office of the Feast of the Most Holy Mystery of the Eucharist (Corpus Christi), celebrated on Thursday after the Sunday of All Saints, adorned with an elaborate headpiece engraving of the Eucharistic mystery.
[^2805]: The celebration of the Feast of the Most Holy Eucharist (Corpus Christi / *Боже́ственнаго Тѣ́ла*) on the Thursday following All Saints Sunday is a distinctive monument of the Ruthenian Greco-Catholic liturgical recension, formally confirmed for the Ruthenian Church at the Synod of Zamość (1720) and preserved in the 1907 Lviv Stauropegion Pentecostarion.
[^2806]: The opening stichera at "Lord, I have cried" in Tone 6 meditate on the institution of the Holy Mystery of the Eucharist at the Mystical Supper (Matthew 26:26-28; 1 Corinthians 11:23-26), calling Christ the "Living Bread" (*Хлѣ́бъ живо́тный*) and "Heavenly Bread" (*Хлѣ́бъ сы́й нбⷭ҇ный*).
[^2807]: The fourth sticheron juxtaposes the earthly manna eaten by the Israelites in the wilderness with the true Bread from Heaven (John 6:49-51, 58), the Angelic Bread (*Хлѣ́бъ А҆́гг҃льскїй*) that imparts everlasting life.
[^2808]: The Doxasticon in Tone 6 summons all lovers of the feasts to rejoice without the "old leaven of malice" (1 Corinthians 5:7-8), partaking of the Lamb of God Who takes away the sins of the world (John 1:29).
