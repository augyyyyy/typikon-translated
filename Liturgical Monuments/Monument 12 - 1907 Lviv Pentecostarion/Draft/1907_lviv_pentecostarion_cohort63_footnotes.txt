[^2810]: The first Old Testament Reading is Exodus 19:10–19, recounting the Lord's descent upon Mount Sinai in fire and smoke to sanctify Israel, prefiguring the awe and moral purification required before partaking of the Holy Mysteries.
[^2811]: The second Old Testament Reading is 1 Kingdoms (1 Samuel) 21:1–6, in which Ahimelech the priest gives the holy Showbread (Bread of Presentation) to David and his companions after ascertaining their ritual purity, prefiguring the Eucharist as the Holy Bread reserved for the faithful.
[^2812]: The third Old Testament Reading is 3 Kingdoms (1 Kings) 17:1–9, wherein ravens miraculously nourish the Prophet Elijah at the brook Cherith with bread and meat, pointing forward to the miraculous food from heaven.
[^2813]: Physical leaf p622 corresponds to printed folio 616 (`х҃ѕі`). Conclusion of the third reading (3 Kingdoms 17:10–16, Elijah and the widow of Zarephath with the unfailing jar of meal and cruse of oil), followed by the Litiya stichera in Tone 6, the Doxasticon in Tone 2, and the opening of the Aposticha in Tone 1.
[^2814]: The first Litiya sticheron in Tone 6 evokes King David leaping and dancing before the Ark of the Covenant (2 Kingdoms / 2 Samuel 6:14–16), contrasting the shadow of the old Ark (which contained the manna and tables of stone) with Christ Himself, the living Lawgiver and incarnate God concealed in the Eucharistic bread.
[^2815]: The second Litiya sticheron recalls the Ark of the Covenant captured by the Philistines and set in the temple of Dagon (1 Kingdoms / 1 Samuel 5:2–4), where the idol fell prostrate and was shattered before the glory of the Lord.
[^2816]: The third and fourth Litiya stichera beseech Christ, the Lamb of God and supernatural Bread, not to consume the communicant as an idol or sinner, but to deify and enlighten through His Body and Blood.
[^2817]: Physical leaf p623 corresponds to printed folio 617 (`х҃зі`). Stichera at the Aposticha (with psalm verses LXX Ps. 77:25 and 77:24), Aposticha Doxasticon in Tone 8, Festal Troparion in Tone 7, Dismissal of Vespers, and the opening of Matins.
[^2818]: The Aposticha Doxasticon in Tone 8 *Ѡ҆ пресла́вное чꙋ́до* ("O marvelous wonder! The Living and Divine Bread...") cites St. Paul's injunction that each examine himself before eating and drinking (1 Corinthians 11:28).
[^2819]: The Festal Troparion in Tone 7 *Хлѣ́бъ преесте́ственный* ("The supernatural Bread, which Angels and Archangels desire to behold with trembling...") is the primary apolytikion of the Feast of Corpus Christi in the Ruthenian recension.
[^2820]: The Dismissal of Vespers commemorates the institution of the Eucharist at the Mystical Supper ("giving His own Body to eat and His true Blood to drink for our salvation") alongside the twelve holy Apostles.
[^2821]: Physical leaf p624 corresponds to printed folio 618 (`х҃иі`). Sessional Hymn after the 2nd Kathisma in Tone 4, Polyeleos with Megalynarion and Selected Psalm verses, Sessional Hymn after the Polyeleos, and the 1st Antiphon of the Songs of Ascents in Tone 4.
[^2822]: The Sessional Hymn after the 2nd Kathisma is sung to the melodic model *Tone 4, Podoben: "Speedily anticipate" (Ско́рѡ предварѝ)*, quoting Christ's Eucharistic discourse in John 6:54: "Whoso eateth My Flesh, and drinketh My Blood, hath eternal life; and I will raise him up at the last day."
[^2823]: The Megalynarion for the Feast of the Most Holy Eucharist: *Велича́емъ тѧ̀ Живодв҃че Хрⷭ҇тѐ* ("We magnify Thee, O Christ Giver of life, Who for our sake didst change bread into Thy Body and wine into Thy Blood...").
[^2824]: The Sessional Hymn after the Polyeleos is chanted to the melodic model *Tone 4, Podoben: "The beauty of thy virginity" (Красотѣ̀ дѣ́вства)*, meditating on the transition from the Old Passover to the New Mystical Passover in the Upper Room.
[^2825]: Physical leaf p625 corresponds to printed folio 619 (`х҃ѳі`). Matins Prokeimenon in Tone 4 (quoting John 6:54 and LXX Ps. 77:25), Matins Gospel (John 6:55–58, Pericope 24), Psalm 50, Post-Gospel Sticheron in Tone 6, and Ode 1 of the Festive Canon in Tone 2.
[^2826]: The Matins Gospel pericope is John 6:55–58: "For My Flesh is food indeed, and My Blood is drink indeed..."
[^2827]: The Post-Gospel Sticheron in Tone 6 *Дне́сь А҆́ггли оу҆жаса́ются* describes the angels filled with awe beholding the incarnate God hidden within the Eucharistic species and received by mortals.
[^2828]: The Canon of the Feast is composed in Tone 2 to the heirmos *Грядѣ́те лю́дїе* ("Come, O ye peoples, let us sing a song unto Christ God, Who divided the sea..."), with the festal refrain: *Сла́ва Хрⷭ҇тѐ Пло́ти твое́й и҆ Кро́ви* ("Glory, O Christ, to Thy Body and Blood!").
[^2829]: Physical leaf p626 corresponds to printed folio 620 (`х҃к`). Ode 3 of the Canon, Sessional Hymn in Tone 1 with its Theotokion, and Ode 4 (opening).
[^2830]: The Sessional Hymn after Ode 3 is sung to the melodic model *Tone 1, Podoben: "The stone being sealed" (Ка́мени запеча́танꙋ)*, praying that communion of the Holy Mysteries be not unto judgment or condemnation (cf. 1 Corinthians 11:29).
[^2831]: Ode 4 begins with the heirmos *Прише́лъ є҆сѝ ѿ Дв҃ы* ("Thou didst come forth from the Virgin, neither an envoy nor an angel..."), based on the Canticle of Habakkuk (Habakkuk 3).
[^2832]: In the third troparion of Ode 4, the communicant is portrayed standing with fear and trembling, eyes fixed on the Crucified Savior Whose outstretched hands bestow divine communion.
[^2833]: Physical leaf p627 corresponds to printed folio 621 (`х҃ка`). Conclusion of Ode 4, Ode 5 with heirmos *Просвѣще́нїе во тмѣ̀ лежа́щихъ*, and Ode 6 with heirmos *Въ бе́зднѣ грѣхо́внѣй валя́ѧся*.
[^2834]: The heirmos of Ode 5 *Просвѣще́нїе во тмѣ̀ лежа́щихъ* ("O illumination of them that lie in darkness...") is based on the Canticle of Isaiah (Isaiah 26:9–20). The second troparion invokes the burning coal of Isaiah 6:6–7 (*оу҆́гль*) to purge the darkened soul.
[^2835]: The heirmos of Ode 6 *Въ бе́зднѣ грѣхо́внѣй валя́ѧся* ("Wallowing in the abyss of sin...") reflects the Canticle of Jonah (Jonah 2).
[^2836]: The third troparion of Ode 6 implores Christ to grant the communicant to approach His Divine Mysteries with fear and trembling, receiving His true and holy Body and His true, holy, and precious Blood.
[^2837]: Physical leaf p628 corresponds to printed folio 622 (`х҃кв`). Ode 6 Theotokion, Katavasia, Kontakion and Ikos in Tone 2, and the beginning of the historical Synaxarion for the Feast of the Most Holy Eucharist.
[^2838]: The Kontakion in Tone 2 *Сѐ предлежи́тъ Хрⷭ҇то́съ на пи́щꙋ всѣ̑мъ* ("Behold, Christ is set forth as food for all...") implores the Lord that the reception of the Mysteries not burn the unworthy, but consume sins and purify souls.
[^2839]: The Synaxarion for Corpus Christi is a major historical and canonical monument of the Ruthenian Greek Catholic Church, detailing how the local Synod of Zamość (1720) formally adopted this solemn feast from the Roman Church to glorify the Holy Eucharist and confirm the faithful in Catholic dogma. Pope Urban IV instituted the universal feast of Corpus Christi in 1264 through the bull *Transiturus de hoc mundo*.
[^2840]: The Synaxarion cites St. John Chrysostom's Eucharistic homilies (on 1 Corinthians 10 and Matthew 26) regarding how Christ feeds believers with His own Body and Blood like a mother nursing her infant, and references the Council of Trent's decrees (Session XIII, Decree on the Eucharist, Canon 6) approving Eucharistic processions (*літі́а* / *processio*) through public squares and streets.
[^2841]: Physical leaf p629 corresponds to printed folio 623 (`х҃кг`). Conclusion of the Synaxarion, followed by Ode 7 and Ode 8 of the Festive Canon.
[^2842]: The heirmos of Ode 7 *Тѣ́лꙋ злато́мꙋ на по́ли Деи́рѣ слꙋжи́мꙋ* ("When the golden image was worshiped on the plain of Dura...") is based on the Canticle of the Three Holy Youths (Daniel 3:1–23).
[^2843]: The heirmos of Ode 8 *Ѽ подо́бїи зла́тѣ небре́гше* ("Disregarding the golden image, the thrice-blessed youths...") continues the theme of the Three Holy Children in the furnace (Daniel 3:57–88).
[^2844]: The third troparion of Ode 8 expresses the tremulous mystery of communion: "I shudder receiving Fire, lest I be burned as wax and as grass... How do I, who am clay, partake of the Divine Body and Blood, and am made incorruptible?"
[^2845]: Physical leaf p630 corresponds to printed folio 624 (`х҃кд`). Katavasia of Ode 8, special Festal Megalynaria (refrains) for Ode 9, Ode 9 Heirmos and troparia, Katavasia of Ode 9, and the Exapostilarion (Photogogic) with its Theotokion.
[^2846]: Special refrains are prescribed for Ode 9: *Велича́й дꙋшѐ моѧ̀ Гдⷭ҇а...* and *Велича́й дꙋшѐ моѧ̀ Хрⷭ҇та̀ Бг҃а на́шего...* The heirmos of Ode 9 in Tone 2 *Ве́сь є҆сѝ жела́нїе, ве́сь сла́дость* ("Thou art all desire, Thou art all sweetness, O Word of God...") is based on the Song of Songs (Canticle of Canticles 5:16).
[^2847]: The Exapostilarion *Оу҆чени́кѡмъ сѣдѧ́щымъ на Та́йнѣй Ве́чери* ("While the disciples were sitting at the Mystical Supper...") recalls Christ lifting His eyes to heaven to God the Father, blessing, breaking, and giving the bread to His disciples.
[^2848]: The Theotokion of the Exapostilarion meditates on the incarnational continuity of the Eucharist: the very same Christ now worshiped and received in the Holy Mysteries is He Whom the Virgin Mary bore in her maternal womb and held in her arms.
