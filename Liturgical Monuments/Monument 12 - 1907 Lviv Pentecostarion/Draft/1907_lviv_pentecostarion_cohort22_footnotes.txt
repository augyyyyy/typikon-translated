[^391]: The Paschal Synaxarion for the Holy and Great Sunday of Pascha, traditionally attributed to Nikephoros Kallistos Xanthopoulos (c. 1256–1335). It expounds the Hebrew etymology of "Pascha" as "passage" (transitus / διαβατήρια), harmonizes the four resurrectional Gospel accounts of the appearances to the Myrrh-Bearing Women and Apostles, and presents two distinct liturgical computations for Christ's "three days in the tomb" (the triduum) based on the Hebrew reckoning of a day-and-night (nychthemeron).
[^392]: The ancient resurrectional hymn *Resurrectionem Christi cernentes* (Воскресе́нїе Хрⷭ҇то́во ви́дѣвше). Unlike throughout the rest of the liturgical year where it is chanted once during Sunday Matins, during the Paschal season it is chanted three times in succession at Matins, at the Paschal Hours, and at the Paschal Typika.
[^393]: Ode 7 Heirmos of the Paschal Canon: "He Who delivered the youths from the furnace..." (Ѻ҆́троки ѿ пе́щи И҆зба́вивый). Based on the Song of the Three Holy Children in the Babylonian furnace (Daniel 3), this hymn contemplates the supreme mystery of the Incarnation: Christ, clothed in our human nature, voluntarily undergoes mortal suffering in order to vest our mortality with the beauty of incorruption.
[^394]: Ode 8 Heirmos of the Paschal Canon: "This chosen and holy day..." (Се́й нарече́нный и҆ ст҃ы́й де́нь). Echoing Gregory the Theologian's Paschal Oration, this hymn proclaims the Day of the Resurrection as the "one of the Sabbaths" (mia ton sabbaton), the King and Lord of days, the "Feast of feasts and Triumph of triumphs."
[^395]: The Paschal Ode 9 Megalynarion: "The angel cried aloud unto Her that is full of grace..." (А҆́ггелъ вопїѧ́ше Бл҃года́тнѣй). This renowned ninth-ode refrain combines the Archangel Gabriel's greeting to the Mother of God with the joyful announcement of the third-day Resurrection of Her Son, bidding the human race to rejoice in the destruction of death.
[^396]: Ode 9 Heirmos of the Paschal Canon: "Shine, shine, O New Jerusalem..." (Свѣти́сѧ свѣти́сѧ но́вый І҆ерꙋсали́ме). Drawing upon Isaiah 60:1 ("Arise, shine, for thy light is come, and the glory of the Lord is risen upon thee") and Revelation 21:2, this triumphant hymn celebrates the Church as the New Jerusalem and calls upon the Mother of God to exult in the bodily rising of Her Son.
[^397]: The Paschal Exapostilarion *Carne cum obdormivisses* (Пло́тїю оу҆сну́въ ꙗ҆́кѡ ме́ртвъ), an Automelon (having its own unique melody, traditionally assigned to Tone 3). It employs the poetic scriptural idiom of sleep for Christ's voluntary bodily death in the sepulchre, through which the forefather Adam is raised and mortality abolished.
[^398]: The Paschal Stichera in Tone 5: "A sacred Pascha is revealed unto us today..." (Па́сха свѧще́ннаѧ на́мъ дне́сь показа́ся). Traditionally ascribed to Saint John of Damascus, these festive troparia are intercalated with the prophetic verses of Psalm 67 [68], concluding with the majestic Paschal Doxasticon ("It is the day of Resurrection...") commanding universal reconciliation and mutual forgiveness in the light of Christ's victory.
[^399]: The Paschal Kiss of Peace and Rite of Mutual Forgiveness (Цѣлова́нїе). Standing before the holy doors, the superior, clergy, monastic brethren, workmen, and laity exchange the joyful Paschal greeting ("Christ is risen! Truly He is risen!"), venerating the precious Cross, the Holy Gospel, and the icons, embodying in liturgical action the spiritual pardon proclaimed in the Doxasticon: "Let us forgive all things by the Resurrection."
[^400]: The Paschal Catechetical Oration of Saint John Chrysostom (*Sermo Catecheticus in Pascha*, PG 59:721-724). Universally appointed to be read at Paschal Matins while the entire congregation stands holding candles, this homily invites all believers without exception—whether they fasted or neglected the fast, whether they labored from the first, third, sixth, ninth, or eleventh hour—to partake of the rich banquet of faith, culminating in the triumphant Pauline cry: "O death, where is thy sting? O hades, where is thy victory?" (1 Cor. 15:55).
[^401]: The Apolytikion of Saint John Chrysostom in Tone 8: "The grace shining forth from thy mouth like a torch..." Chanted immediately following the reading of his Paschal Catechetical Oration, this hymn expresses filial gratitude to the Golden-mouthed Archbishop of Constantinople for his theological illumination of the universe.
[^402]: The Order of the Paschal Hours (Часы̀ Па́схи). Throughout Bright Week, the entire ordinary Psalmody of the Daily Office (the First, Third, Sixth, and Ninth Hours, as well as Compline and the Midnight Office) is replaced by this concise, joyful sequence composed of the Paschal Troparion, the Hypakoe, the Kontakion, and resurrectional troparia, omitting all penitential prayers and prostrations.
[^403]: The Order of Typika (Изобразительны / Obednitsa) during Bright Week. Appointed to be read in place of the Divine Liturgy in the absence of a priest, this rite replaces the typical psalms with the Paschal Troparion and integrates the Beatitudes taken from Odes 3 and 6 of the Paschal Canon, together with the Epistle and Gospel pericopes of the day.
[^404]: The Order of the Paschal Moleben (Supplicatory Canon). Celebrated during Paschaltide, this festive service incorporates the Paschal Canon, the Paschal Stichera, the resurrectional Prokeimenon, and the reading of the appearance of the Risen Christ on the road to Emmaus (Luke 24:12-35, pericope 113), concluding with the blessing of the faithful with the precious Cross.
[^405]: The Opening of the Paschal Divine Liturgy. Celebrated early in the morning following Matins and the Paschal Hours, the Liturgy begins with the solemn triadic exclamation ("Blessed is the Kingdom..."), followed by the thrice-repeated chanting of the Paschal Troparion by the clergy within the sanctuary and by the choirs in the nave, inaugurating the Eucharistic celebration of Bright Week.
[^406]: The Three Paschal Antiphons. Composed of selected verses from Psalms 65, 66, and 67 [LXX 65, 66, 67 / MT 66, 67, 68] with the refrains "Through the prayers of the Mother of God, O Savior, save us" (Antiphon 1) and "Save us, O Son of God, Who art risen from the dead..." (Antiphon 2), followed by the Hymn of the Incarnation ("Only-begotten Son and Word of God...").
[^407]: The Paschal Eisodikon (Introit) and Baptismal Hymn. The Little Entrance is made with the verse: "In the assemblies bless ye God, the Lord from the fountains of Israel" (Ps. 67:27 [68:26]). In place of the Trisagion, the ancient baptismal proclamation from Galatians 3:27 is chanted: "As many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ. Alleluia" (Є҆ли́цы во Хрⷭ҇та̀ крⷭ҇ти́стеся...), testifying to the early Christian practice of administering baptism to catechumens during the Paschal Vigil.
[^408]: The Opening Pericope of the Acts of the Apostles (Acts 1:1-8, pericope 1). The liturgical reading of the Acts of the Apostles begins on the Holy and Great Sunday of Pascha and continues daily throughout the fifty days of the Pentecostarion, proclaiming the witness of the Holy Apostles to the Resurrection of Jesus Christ and the descent of the Holy Spirit.
