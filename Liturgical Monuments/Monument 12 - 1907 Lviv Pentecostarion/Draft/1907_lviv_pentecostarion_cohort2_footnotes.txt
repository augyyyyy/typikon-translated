[^19]: The second reading at Vespers on Friday of Palm Week is the Praise of the Valiant Woman (LXX Proverbs 31:8–31 / MT 31:10–31), opening with the counsel to open one's mouth in defense of the poor and weak, prefiguring the pious women (Martha and Mary) who served the Lord and received Lazarus back from the tomb.
[^20]: Rubrical note on leaf p12: From this day forward (Friday of Palm Week), neither the Martyrikon (*Мꙋ́чениченъ*), nor the Theotokion (*Бг҃оро́диченъ*), nor the Octoechos (*Ѻ҆смогла́сникъ*) is chanted until the Saturday of Saint Thomas (Antipascha), marking the complete cessation of the weekly Octoechos cycle for the duration of Passion Week and Bright Week.
[^21]: The liturgical rubric notes the divergence between the general Byzantine monastic practice appointing Great Compline (*Повече́рїе вели́кое*) and the specific Ruthenian regional usage established at the Synod of Lviv (1891), which appointed Small Compline (*Повече́рїе ма́лое*) together with the Canon of Saint Andrew of Crete for the eve of Lazarus Saturday.
[^22]: Saint Andrew of Crete (ca. 660–740), Archbishop of Gortyna and author of the Great Canon of Repentance, is surnamed "the Hierosolymite" (*І҆еросолими́тъ*) in reference to his monastic tonsure and early ascetic formation at the Lavra of Saint Sabbas the Sanctified in Jerusalem.
[^23]: Lazarus Saturday (*Суббѡ́та ст҃а́гѡ и҆ пра́веднагѡ Ла́зарѧ*) commemorates the raising of the four-days-dead Lazarus of Bethany (John 11:1–45) by Our Lord Jesus Christ as the solemn pledge and prefiguration of the universal resurrection before His own voluntary Passion and death.
[^24]: The Midnight Office rubrics for Lazarus Saturday prescribe the festal troparion (*Confirming the common resurrection...*) and kontakion (*Christ, the joy of all...*), omitting the customary penitential prayers for the departed (*Помянѝ, Го́споди...*).
[^25]: The chanting of the Resurrectional Troparia of the Blameless (*Еѵлогїта́рїа* / *А҆́ггельскїй собо́ръ*, "Blessed art Thou, O Lord: teach me Thy statutes") on a Saturday is a unique liturgical occurrence found only on Lazarus Saturday and Great and Holy Saturday, testifying to the intensely resurrectional character of the day.
[^26]: The Matins Gospel is omitted on Lazarus Saturday except in a church dedicated to Saint Lazarus. The appointed patristic reading consists of the homiletic commentary on John 11 by Saint John Chrysostom (*Homilies 62 and 63 on the Gospel of John*, designated in the Slavonic text as chapters 33 and 34 of the commentary).
[^27]: The Matins canon combines two compositions: the first by Saint Theophanes the Branded (*Theophanes Graptos*, ca. 778–845), Metropolitan of Nicaea; the second by Saint John Damascene. From Ode 6, the office incorporates the four-ode canon (*четверопѣ́снецъ*) of Saint Cosmas of Maiuma.
[^28]: "John the Monk" (*І҆ѡа́ннъ мона́хъ*) is the standard liturgical designation for Saint John of Damascus (Damascene, ca. 676–749), monk of the Lavra of Saint Sabbas and Doctor of the Church.
