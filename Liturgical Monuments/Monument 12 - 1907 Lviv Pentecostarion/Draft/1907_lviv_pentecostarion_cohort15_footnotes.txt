[^264]: The solemn Vespers of Holy and Great Friday, marking the descent from the Cross and the burial of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. In Ruthenian liturgical practice (both Kyivan and Lviv recensions), this service culminates in the solemn procession with the Plashchanytsia (Holy Shroud) to the mystical tomb (sepulchre) positioned in the center of the nave for veneration.
[^265]: Liturgical timekeeping: the tenth hour of the day corresponds to approximately 4:00 PM (16:00), marking the late afternoon when Joseph of Arimathea obtained permission from Pontius Pilate to take down the Body of the Lord from the Cross.
[^266]: Psalm 21:19 (LXX 21:18, *Раздѣли́ша ри̑зы моѧ̑ себѣ̀*), the prophetic Passion psalm cited directly in Matthew 27:35 and John 19:24. The verse (*Стї́хъ*) is Psalm 21:2 (LXX 21:1, *Бж҃е Бж҃е мо́й, вонми́ ми, вскꙋ́ю ѡ҆ста́вилъ мѧ̀ є҆сѝ*), Christ's fourth word from the Cross (*Eli, Eli, lema sabachthani*).
[^267]: The First Old Testament Reading of Great Friday Vespers: Exodus 33:11–23. Moses's intimate dialogue with God in the Tabernacle of Witness and the divine theophany on Mount Sinai. Moses desires to see God's glory, but God declares that no man can see His face and live; God hides Moses in a cleft of the rock and covers him with His hand, revealing His back parts as He passes by. Typologically interpreted by the Holy Fathers as prefiguring Christ's burial in the rock tomb and the mystery of the Incarnate Godhead revealed in His suffering humanity.
[^268]: Psalm 34:1–2 (LXX 34:1–2, *Сꙋдѝ Гдⷭ҇и ѡ҆би́дѧщымъ мѧ̀*), David's prayer for divine deliverance against unrighteous oppressors, typifying the innocent Christ before the Sanhedrin and Pilate.
[^269]: The Second Old Testament Reading of Great Friday Vespers: Job 42:12–17 with the Septuagint epilogue (*та́кѡ толкꙋ́етсѧ ѿ сѷ́рскїѧ кни́ги* / "thus is it interpreted from the Syriac book"). The restoration of Job after his deep suffering, the naming of his three daughters (Day, Cassia, and Amalthea's Horn), his longevity and vision of four generations, and the explicit prophecy of his resurrection with those whom the Lord raiseth up. The Septuagint genealogical appendix identifies Job with Jobab, the fifth generation from Abraham through Esau (Genesis 36:33).
[^270]: The Third Old Testament Reading of Great Friday Vespers: Isaiah 52:13–54:1. The Fourth Servant Song of Isaiah, prophesying Christ's vicarious suffering, silent endurance, and ultimate vindication.
[^271]: Physical Leaf p130 = Printed Folio 124 + 5. The printed text abbreviates the reading of Isaiah 52:13–54:1 with the internal cross-reference rubric: *"Look in the 6th Hour, folio 124"* (*И҆щѝ на ѕ҃-мъ часѣ̀, ли́стъ рк҃д*).
[^272]: Prokeimenon of the Apostle, Tone 6: Psalm 87:7, 2 (LXX 87:6, 1, *Положи́ша мѧ̀ въ ро́вѣ преисподнѣ́йшемъ*), the most desolate psalm of the Psalter, expressing total abandonment in the grave, directly applied to Christ's descent into the tomb and hades.
[^273]: The Epistle of Great Friday Vespers: 1 Corinthians 1:18–2:2 (selection 125). St. Paul's fundamental discourse on the theology of the Cross: the word of the Cross as the power and wisdom of God against the worldly wisdom of Greeks and the seeking of signs by Jews.
[^274]: Alleluia of Great Friday Vespers, Tone 1: Psalm 68:2, 21, 24 (LXX 68:1, 20, 23, *Спаси́ мѧ Бж҃е, ꙗ҆́кѡ внидо́ша во́ды до дꙋшѝ моеѧ̀*), the Messianic Passion psalm describing overwhelming waters, bitter reproach, and the blinding of persecutors.
[^275]: The Gospel of Great Friday Vespers: Matthew 27:1–38; Luke 23:39–43; Matthew 27:39–54; John 19:31–37; Matthew 27:55–61 (selection 110). A composite Passion Gospel harmonization spanning Christ's trial before Pilate, the suicide of Judas, the release of Barabbas, the crowning with thorns, the Crucifixion between two thieves, the repentance of the thief, the cosmic signs at His death, the piercing of His side, the deposition by Joseph of Arimathea, and the burial in the rock tomb opposite the women.
[^276]: Canonical 3-layer melodic model: Tone 2, Podoben: "When from the Tree" (*Є҆гда̀ ѿ дре́ва* / *Ὅτε ἐκ τοῦ ξύλου*), attributed to St. Theophanes Graptos. The famous Aposticha stichera of Great Friday Vespers depicting the burial of Christ by Joseph of Arimathea and Nicodemus.
[^277]: Small Compline on Holy and Great Friday: prescribed to be recited in the cells (*въ ке́ллїахъ*), featuring the monumental Canon of the Crucifixion and the Lament of the Most Holy Theotokos (*Канѡ́нъ ѡ҆ Распѧ́тїи Гдⷭ҇ни, и҆ на пла́чь прест҃ы́ѧ Бцⷣы*).
[^278]: Authorship of the Lament of the Theotokos: attributed to Symeon the Metaphrast (Logothete, 10th century), one of the supreme masterpieces of Byzantine hymnographic mariology, meditating on the Mother of God's profound maternal grief, dialogue with her crucified and dead Son, and the promise of the Resurrection.
[^279]: Kontakion of the Lament of the Theotokos, Tone 2: *На́съ ра́ди Распѧ́таго прїиди́те всѝ воспои́мъ* ("Come, let us all hymn Him Who was crucified for our sake"). An epitome of the maternal grief of the Theotokos standing at Golgotha.
[^280]: Ikos of the Lament of the Theotokos: *Своего̀ А҆́гнца А҆́гница зрѧ́щи* ("Seeing her own Lamb drawn unto the slaughter..."). Reminiscent of the classical Kontakion of St. Romanos the Melodist on the Passion (*Mary at the Cross*), asking whether there is another marriage in Cana of Galilee.
[^281]: The Prayer of Compline to the Most Holy Theotokos by Paul the Monk of the Monastery of the Benefactress (Evergetis): *А҆́спиле, а҆мо́лѷнте, а҆́фѳоре, а҆́хранте, а҆́гнὴ Па́рѳене* (*Нескве́рнаѧ, небла́знаѧ, нетлѣ́ннаѧ, пречи́стаѧ, неискꙋсобра́чнаѧ* / "O Spotless, undefiled, incorrupt, immaculate, pure Virgin Bride of God").
[^282]: Codicological rubric: the Midnight Office of Holy and Great Saturday (*Полно́щницꙋ же пое́мъ въ ке́ллїахъ*) is read privately in the cells, without public ecclesiastical chanting, before the solemn nocturnal or dawn Matins of Holy Saturday.
[^283]: Codicological ornament: a decorative woodcut/typographical vignette depicting an ornamental scroll inscribed with the Greek letters Alpha and Omega and the cross (*A + Ω*), symbolizing Christ as the First and the Last, the Beginning and the End (Revelation 1:8, 22:13), placed at the conclusion of Great Friday Compline before Great Saturday.
