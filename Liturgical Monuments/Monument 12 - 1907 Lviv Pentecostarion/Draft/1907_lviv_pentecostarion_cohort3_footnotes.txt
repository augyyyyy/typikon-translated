[^29]: The Sessional Hymn model *Tone 8, Podoben: "The Wisdom..." (Премꙋ́дрости)* is the celebrated poetic incipit *Τὴν Σοφίαν καὶ Λόγον ἐν σῇ γαστρὶ συλλαβοῦσα* ("Having conceived the Wisdom and the Word in thy womb"), attributed to Saint Theodore the Studite or Saint John Damascene, widely utilized across the Triodion and Pentecostarion for festive sessional compositions.
[^30]: Saint Cosmas the Melodist, Bishop of Maiuma (ca. 675–752), foster brother of Saint John Damascene, composed the four-ode canon (*четверопѣ́снецъ*) spanning Odes 6 through 9, which forms the concluding portion of the Matins canon for Lazarus Saturday.
[^31]: The Kontakion model *Tone 2, Podoben: "Seeking the things on high" (Вы́шнихъ и҆щѧ̀)* (*Τὰ ἄνω ζητῶν*) is Saint Romanos the Melodist's classical prooimion model, here applied to Lazarus Saturday to proclaim Christ as the universal Joy, Truth, and Resurrection of all.
[^32]: The liturgical Synaxarion for Lazarus Saturday from the historical compendium of Nikephoros Kallistos Xanthopoulos (ca. 1256–1335). It synthesizes patristic traditions concerning Lazarus's life after his resurrection, including his flight to Kition in Cyprus to escape the Sanhedrin, his thirty-year episcopate, the hand-woven omophorion gifted him by the Theotokos, and the translation of his relics to Constantinople under Emperor Leo VI the Wise (reigned 886–912) in 899.
[^33]: In place of the Trisagion (*Свѧты́й Бж҃е*), the baptismal hymn from Galatians 3:27 (*"As many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ. Alleluia"*) is sung at the Divine Liturgy on Lazarus Saturday. In the ancient cathedral usage of Constantinople and Jerusalem, this day was one of the primary baptismal vigils of the liturgical year, when catechumens were solemnly baptized into Christ prior to Holy Week.
[^34]: Monastic dietary mitigation on Lazarus Saturday: the Great Fast rule of xerophagy is mitigated to permit cooked food with olive oil (*варе́нїемъ со є҆ле́емъ*) and wine. Furthermore, the consumption of fish roe/caviar (*и҆кра̀*) is explicitly permitted up to three ounces (*трѝ ѻ҆́нгїи*), while whole fish remains strictly prohibited until Palm Sunday.
[^35]: The rubrical statute for a parish or monastery temple dedicated to Saint Lazarus directs the chanting of the Polyeleos at Matins, the First Antiphon of the Anavathmoi (Hymns of Ascent) in Tone 4, and the full patristic reading of the Life of Saint Lazarus composed by Nikephoros Kallistos Xanthopoulos.
[^36]: Small Vespers on the Eve of Palm Sunday (*на ма́лѣй вече́рни въ сꙋббѡ́тꙋ ва́їй ве́чера*) is celebrated before sunset in monastic communities, preceding the solemn festal Great Vespers. Its stichera anticipate the Entry of the Lord into Jerusalem riding upon the foal of an ass.
[^37]: Great Vespers on Saturday Evening begins the Great Feast of the Palm Sunday of the Lord's Entry into Jerusalem (*Вхо́дъ Гдⷭ҇ень въ І҆ерꙋсали́мъ* / *Недѣ́лѧ Ва́їй*), one of the Twelve Great Feasts (*Dodekaorton*). The opening sticheron, *"Today the grace of the Holy Spirit hath gathered us together..."* (*Дне́сь бл҃года́ть ст҃а́гѡ Дх҃а на́съ собра̀*), is one of the most renowned processional idiomela in Byzantine hymnography.
