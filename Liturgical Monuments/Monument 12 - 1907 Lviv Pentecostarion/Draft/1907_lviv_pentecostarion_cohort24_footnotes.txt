[^431]: The Epistle reading for Bright Tuesday: Acts 2:14-21 (pericope 4). Proclaiming Saint Peter's inaugural sermon on the day of Pentecost, applying the prophecy of the Prophet Joel (Joel 2:28-32 [3:1-5]) concerning the outpouring of the Holy Spirit upon all flesh in the last days, the celestial wonders and signs on earth, and the salvation of whosoever shall call upon the Name of the Lord.
[^432]: The continuous Paschal Kinonikon (Communion Hymn): "Receive ye the Body of Christ; taste ye of the immortal Fountain" (Тѣ́ло Хрⷭ҇то́во прїими́те, и҆сто́чника безсме́ртнагѡ вкꙋси́те). Chanted at every Divine Liturgy throughout Bright Week. In addition, this leaf marks an irregularity in the 1907 Lviv printer's folio coordinates: physical leaf p231 bears printed folio скѕ (fol. 226), followed on subsequent leaves by typographical misprints in the Cyrillic folio headings (repeating скз, ски, and сл҃а across fols. 227-234) before stabilizing.
[^433]: Bright Tuesday Vespers (for Bright Wednesday). In accordance with the Bright Week progression through the Octoechos, the liturgical choir advances to Tone 4, chanting the resurrectional stichera and Anatolian hymns attributed to Patriarch Anatolius of Constantinople, praising Christ's harrowing of hades and the smashing of the gates of death.
[^434]: The Dogmatic Theotokion (*Dogmatikon*) of Tone 4: "David the prophet, who through thee became the forefather of God..." (И҆́же тебѐ ра́ди, Бг҃ооте́цъ проро́къ Дв҃дъ). Attributed to Saint John of Damascus, this hymn interprets King David's messianic psalm (Psalm 44:10 [45:9], "The Queen stood at Thy right hand") as a prophetic type of the Virgin Theotokos, and weaves together the parable of the lost sheep (Luke 15:4-7) with the dogma of the Incarnation, wherein the Good Shepherd shoulders fallen humanity and presents it restored unto the Father.
[^435]: Bright Wednesday Matins and Divine Liturgy (Въ Сре́дꙋ свѣ́тлыѧ Седми́цы на Оу҆́трени). Following Matins, monastic typika prescribe a solemn Litiya and procession outside the monastery precincts (*внѣ̀ ѻ҆би́тели*), accompanied by the ringing of bells and the singing of Paschal stichera. At the Liturgy, the Epistle (Acts 2:22-36, pericope 5) continues Saint Peter's proclamation of Christ's resurrection and ascension to the right hand of God, citing Psalm 15 [16] and Psalm 109 [110], while the Gospel (John 1:35-51, pericope 6) records the call of Andrew, Peter, Philip, and Nathanael.
[^436]: Bright Wednesday Vespers (for Bright Thursday). The daily cycle advances to Tone 5 of the Octoechos. The Tone 5 Dogmatic Theotokion, "In the Red Sea there was once depicted an image of the unwedded Bride..." (Въ Чермнѣ́мъ мо́ри, неискꙋсобра́чныѧ Невѣ́сты), provides a typology contrasting Moses parting the Red Sea for the dryshod passage of Israel (Exodus 14) with Gabriel announcing the virginal, seedless conception of Christ the Emmanuel.
[^437]: Bright Thursday Matins and Divine Liturgy (Въ Четверто́къ свѣ́тлыѧ Седми́цы). Matins features Tone 5 resurrectional stichera highlighting the confusion of the guards and the mystery of the sealed tomb. At the Liturgy, the Epistle (Acts 2:38-43, pericope 7) records the baptism of three thousand souls on Pentecost, and the Gospel (John 3:1-15, pericope 8) presents Christ's nocturnal discourse with Nicodemus regarding regeneration of water and the Spirit.
[^438]: Bright Thursday Vespers (for Bright Friday). Advancing to Tone 6 of the Octoechos, the service features the solemn chanting of the Great Prokeimenon in Tone 7: "I will love Thee, O Lord, my strength: the Lord is my foundation and my refuge!" (Ps. 17:2-3a [18:1-2a]), accompanied by three responsory verses from Psalm 17 [18], celebrating God's triumphant deliverance of His servant from death and the nethermost pit.
[^439]: Bright Friday Matins and Divine Liturgy (Въ Пѧто́къ свѣ́тлыѧ Седми́цы). In Byzantine and Slavic tradition, Bright Friday is also widely observed with the commemoration of the Life-Giving Spring (*Живоно́сный Исто́чникъ*) of the Mother of God. The Pentecostarion text appoints the Tone 6 resurrectional stichera at Praises, followed at Liturgy by the Epistle (Acts 3:1-8, pericope 7), describing the miraculous healing of the lame man at the Beautiful Gate of the Temple, and the Gospel (John 2:12-22, pericope 7), recounting the cleansing of the Temple in Jerusalem and Christ's prophecy concerning the temple of His Body.
[^440]: Bright Friday Vespers (for Bright Saturday). The liturgical cycle reaches the final mode of the week, Tone 8 of the Octoechos. The stichera at "Lord, I have cried" celebrate the harrowing of hades, concluding with the Dogmatic Theotokion of Tone 8: "The King of heaven out of love for mankind appeared on earth..." (Цр҃ь нб҃ный за чл҃вѣколю́бїе на землѝ ꙗ҆ви́сѧ), which articulates the Chalcedonian confession of Christ's two natures in one hypostasis without confusion or separation.
[^441]: Bright Saturday Matins (Въ Сꙋббѡ́тꙋ свѣ́тлыѧ Седми́цы на Оу҆́трени). The concluding morning service of Bright Week, bringing the week-long succession through the Octoechos tones to completion in Tone 8. At the conclusion of the Divine Liturgy on this day, the ancient prayer for the breaking and distribution of the Artos (*раздроблѐнїе А҆́ртоса*) is recited, and the Royal Doors of the iconostasis are closed prior to the celebration of the Ninth Hour and Thomas Sunday Vespers.
