[^38]: The Old Testament Paremias at Palm Sunday Great Vespers: Genesis 49:1-2, 8-12 (the Patriarch Jacob's dying blessing upon Judah, prophesying the perpetual royal sceptre, the expectation of the nations, and the King binding His foal unto the vine and washing His garments in the blood of the grape); Zephaniah 3:14-19 (the joyous advent of the King of Israel in Zion, removing judgment and casting out enemies); and Zechariah 9:9-15 (the messianic prophecy of the meek King entering Jerusalem mounted upon the foal of an ass, cutting off chariot and war-bow, and establishing universal peace).
[^39]: The festal dismissal troparia of Palm Sunday: The primary troparion, *Tone 1: "Giving assurance of the general resurrection before Thy Passion, Thou didst raise Lazarus from the dead, O Christ our God; wherefore we also, like the children, carrying the tokens of victory, cry aloud to Thee, the Vanquisher of death: Hosanna in the highest! Blessed is He that cometh in the name of the Lord"* (chanted twice), and the baptismal troparion, *Tone 4: "Having been buried with Thee through baptism, O Christ our God, we have been made worthy of immortal life through Thy Resurrection; and chanting we cry aloud: Hosanna in the highest! Blessed is He that cometh in the name of the Lord"* (chanted once).
[^40]: Physical Leaf p26 (fol. 21r).
[^41]: The solemn Prayer for the Blessing of Palms (*Моли́тва на бл҃гослове́нїе ва́їа*) recited by the priest at Matins after the chanting of the festal Gospel and Psalm 50, followed by the ceremonial distribution of blessed palms and willow branches (*ва́їа и҆ вѣ́тви древе́съ*) to the clergy and faithful.
[^42]: The Canon of Palm Sunday Matins composed by Saint Cosmas the Melodist, Bishop of Maiuma (ca. 675–752). The Greek acrostic formed by the initial letters of the troparia across Odes 1 through 9 reads: *Ὡσαννὰ Χριστὸς εὐλογημένος Θεός* ("Hosanna, Christ is blessed God").
[^43]: The Ikos of Palm Sunday draws a theological contrast between the slaughter of the Innocents in Bethlehem at Christ's Nativity and His voluntary self-offering upon the Cross for all humanity, both young and old.
[^44]: The Synaxarion for Palm Sunday (*Вхо́дъ Гдⷭ҇ень въ І҆ерꙋсали́мъ* / *Ἡ Κυριακὴ τῶν Βαΐων*) by Nikephoros Kallistos Xanthopoulos (ca. 1256–1335). It expounds the historical circumstances of the Lord's royal entry into Jerusalem six days before the Passover, the typology of the palms (*ва́їа*) as emblems of victory over death, and the foal of an ass as the prefiguration of the Gentile nations called from idolatrous irrationality into the Church of God.
[^45]: At Ode 9 of the Matins Canon on Palm Sunday, the biblical canticle of the Theotokos (*Magnificat* / *Честнѣ́йшꙋю*) is omitted in accordance with the rubrics for Master Feasts of the Lord (*Владычніи праздники*), proceeding immediately to the festal heirmos.
[^46]: The Festive Antiphons of Palm Sunday at the Divine Liturgy are taken from Psalms 114, 115, and 117 (LXX). Each antiphon features its own proper festal refrain celebrating Christ's entry upon the foal.
[^47]: The Festal Entrance Hymn (*Входное*) at the Little Entrance of the Divine Liturgy is taken from Psalm 117:26-27a: *"Blessed is He that cometh in the name of the Lord; we have blessed you out of the house of the Lord; God is the Lord, and hath appeared unto us."*
[^48]: Monastic dietary mitigation on Palm Sunday: Although occurring within Great Lent, because the Entry of the Lord into Jerusalem is one of the Twelve Great Feasts, the typikon mitigates the strict Lenten fast to permit the consumption of fish, wine, and oil (*ꙗ҆ди́мъ ры́бы, и҆ вїно̀ пїе́мъ*).
[^49]: Palm Sunday Evening Vespers opens Great and Holy Week and marks the liturgical transition from the festal triumph of the Entry into Jerusalem to the solemn, sober anticipation of the Lord's Sufferings and the Bridegroom Matins services (*Служба Жениха*).
