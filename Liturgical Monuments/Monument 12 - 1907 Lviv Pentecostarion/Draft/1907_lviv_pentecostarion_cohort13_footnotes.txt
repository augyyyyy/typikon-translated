[^221]: The Order of the Royal Hours of Holy and Great Friday (*Сказа́нїе часѡ́въ ст҃а́гѡ и҆ вели́кагѡ Пѧтка̀*), traditionally attributed in the liturgical title to St. Cyril, Archbishop of Alexandria (c. 376–444). The service combines the First, Third, Sixth, and Ninth Hours into an extended monumental office of the Passion, celebrated with the striking of the klepalo, the priest vesting in epitrachelion and phelonion, and the solemn chanting of special psalms, stichera idiomela, Old Testament prophecies, Epistles, and Gospels.
[^222]: Psalm 5 (LXX 5): *Глаго́лы моѧ̑ внꙋшѝ Гдⷭ҇и* ("Give ear to my words, O Lord"). Appointed as the opening psalm of the First Hour, emphasizing morning prayer and the righteous appeal to God against the assembly of deceitful, boastful, and bloodthirsty adversaries.
[^223]: Psalm 2 (LXX 2): *Вскꙋ́ю шата́шасѧ ꙗ҆зы́цы* ("Why have the heathen raged"). A foundational Davidic messianic psalm appointed for the First Royal Hour, prophesying the kings of the earth and the rulers taking counsel together against the Lord and against His Anointed (Christ), directly fulfilled in the Sanhedrin, Herod, and Pontius Pilate's tribunal.
[^224]: Psalm 21 (LXX 21): *Бж҃е, Бж҃е мо́й, вонми́ ми, вскꙋ́ю ѡ҆ста́вилъ мѧ̀ є҆сѝ* ("O God, my God, attend unto me; why hast Thou forsaken me?"). The preeminent prophetic psalm of the Passion, whose opening cry was uttered by Christ upon the Cross (Matt. 27:46, Mark 15:34), detailing the mockery of the bystanders, the piercing of His hands and feet, the dividing of His garments, and the casting of lots.
[^225]: Troparion of the First Hour in Tone 1: *Распе́ншꙋсѧ тебѣ̀ Хрⷭ҇тѐ, поги́бе мꙋчи́тельство* ("When Thou wast crucified, O Christ, the tyranny was destroyed"). Commemorating the voluntary Passion of the Savior which vanquished the hostile power of the devil without angelic or human intervention.
[^226]: Theotokion of the First Hour: *Что́ тѧ нарече́мъ, ѽ Ѽбра́дованнаѧ;* ("What shall we call thee, O thou full of grace?"). A classic Marian theological hymn celebrating the Mother of God under the typological titles of Heaven, Paradise, Virgin, and Pure Mother.
[^227]: The Stichera Idiomela of the First Hour: three stichera selected from the twelve solemn troparia of the Passion chanted across the Royal Hours. Chanted responsively between the two choirs with Psalm verses: Tone 8 (*Дне́сь церко́внаѧ завѣ́са*), Tone 8 (*Ꙗ҆́кѡ ѻ҆вча̀ на заколе́нїе*), and Tone 8 (*Є҆́мшымъ тѧ̀ беззако́ннымъ*).
[^228]: Old Testament Prophecy of the First Hour: Zechariah 11:10–14; 12:10; 13:7; 14:6–9, 20–21. A composite prophetic pericope containing Zechariah's remarkable messianic prophecies: the thirty pieces of silver cast into the furnace in the house of the Lord, the piercing of the Messiah ("they shall look upon Him Whom they have pierced"), the striking of the Shepherd and scattering of the sheep, and the cosmic darkening of the sun on that unique day known only to the Lord.
[^229]: Epistle of the First Hour: Galatians 6:14–18 (section 215 from the half). St. Paul's triumphant apostolic confession: *"God forbid that I should boast, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, by Whom the world is crucified unto me, and I unto the world."*
[^230]: Gospel of the First Hour: Matthew 27:1–56 (section 110). The monumental Passion narrative according to Matthew: the council of the chief priests and elders; Judas's remorse, return of the thirty pieces of silver, and death; the trial before Pontius Pilate; the release of Barabbas; the scourging and crowning with thorns; the journey to Golgotha with Simon of Cyrene; the Crucifixion between two thieves; the derision; the darkness from the sixth to the ninth hour; Christ's death; the tearing of the temple veil; the resurrection of many saints; and the confession of the centurion.
[^231]: Concluding Prayer of the First Hour: *Хрⷭ҇тѐ свѣ́те и҆́стинный* ("O Christ, the true Light, Who enlightenest and sanctifiest every man that cometh into the world").
[^232]: Psalm 34 (LXX 34): *Сꙋдѝ Гдⷭ҇и, ѡ҆би́дѧщымъ мѧ̀* ("Judge them, O Lord, that wrong me"). The first appointed psalm of the Third Royal Hour, praying for divine vindication against false witnesses and unrighteous persecutors who reward evil for good.
[^233]: Psalm 108 (LXX 108): *Бж҃е, хвалы̀ моеѧ̀ не премолчѝ* ("O God of my praise, hold not Thy peace"). A severe imprecatory and prophetic psalm directly applied in the New Testament (Acts 1:20) to Judas the Iscariot and the deicidal persecutors of Christ: *"Let his days be few, and his bishopric let another take."*
[^234]: Psalm 50 (LXX 50): The universal penitential psalm of King David (*Поми́лꙋй мѧ̀ Бж҃е* / "Have mercy on me, O God"), chanted at the Third Hour to evoke profound contrition and inward cleansing before the mystery of the Cross.
[^235]: Troparion of the Third Hour in Tone 6: *Гдⷭ҇и, ѡ҆сꙋди́ша тѧ̀ І҆ꙋде́е на сме́рть* ("O Lord, the Jews condemned Thee unto death, Who art the Life of all"). Contrasting Israel's miraculous deliverance through the Red Sea and water from the rock with their handing over of Christ to death and offering Him gall and vinegar.
[^236]: The Stichera Idiomela of the Third Hour: Tone 8 (*Стра́ха ра́ди І҆ꙋде́йскагѡ*), Tone 8 (*Пре́жде чⷭ҇тна́гѡ твоегѡ̀ крⷭ҇та̀*), and Tone 5 (*Влекомь на крⷭ҇тъ си́це вопїѧ́лъ є҆сѝ Гдⷭ҇и*), recounting Peter's denial and repentance, the mocking by the Roman soldiers, and Christ's sorrowful reproaches to the Jewish people.
[^237]: Old Testament Prophecy of the Third Hour: Isaiah 50:4–11. The Third Servant Song of Isaiah: *"I gave My back to the smiters, and My cheeks to blows; I hid not My face from the shame of spitting."*
[^238]: Epistle of the Third Hour: Romans 5:6–10 (section 118 from the half). St. Paul's theological discourse on divine reconciliation: while we were yet sinners and enemies, Christ died for the ungodly, and we are justified by His Blood.
[^239]: Gospel of the Third Hour: Mark 15:16–41 (sections 67 and 68 in series). St. Mark's account of the Roman soldiers mocking Christ in the praetorium, Simon of Cyrene carrying the Cross, the Crucifixion at the third hour, the superscription, the mockery of the priests and scribes, the darkness at the sixth hour, Christ's death at the ninth hour, the tearing of the veil, and the centurion's confession.
[^240]: The Prayer of Saint Mardarios: *Влⷣко Бж҃е Ѻ҆́ч҃е Вседержи́телю* ("O Master God, Father Almighty, O Lord the Only-begotten Son, Jesus Christ, and Holy Spirit: one Godhead, one Power, have mercy on me a sinner; and by the judgments which Thou knowest, save me Thine unworthy servant, for Thou art blessed unto ages of ages. Amen.").
[^241]: The Order of the Sixth Hour of Holy and Great Friday: opening with the royal psalms Psalm 53 (LXX 53, *Бж҃е, во и҆́мѧ твоѐ спаси́ мѧ*), Psalm 139 (LXX 139, *И҆зми́ мѧ Гдⷭ҇и, ѿ человѣ́ка лꙋка́ва*), and Psalm 90 (LXX 90, *Живы́й въ по́мощи Вы́шнѧгѡ*), focusing on the midday hour of the Crucifixion when Christ was nailed to the Cross between the two thieves.
