[^242]: Troparion of the Sixth Hour, Tone 2: *Спасе́нїе содѣ́лалъ є҆сѝ посредѣ̀ землѝ Хрⷭ҇тѐ Бж҃е* ("Thou hast wrought salvation in the midst of the earth, O Christ God"). Commemorating Christ stretching out His most pure hands upon the Cross to gather all the nations into unity.
[^243]: Stichera Idiomela of the Sixth Hour: Tone 8 (*Сїѧ̑ глаго́летъ Гдⷭ҇ь І҆ꙋде́ѡмъ*), Tone 8 (*Законополо́жницы І҆и҃левы, І҆ꙋде́е и҆ фарісе́е*), and Tone 5 (*Прїиди́те Хрⷭ҇тоно́снїи лю́дїе*). The divine reproaches contrasting the wonders of the Exodus (manna, water from the rock) with the vinegar and gall, and Christ's prayer for His crucifiers: *"Father, forgive them this sin."*
[^244]: Old Testament Prophecy of the Sixth Hour: Isaiah 52:13–54:1. The Fourth Servant Song of Isaiah: the suffering, humiliation, and vicarious atonement of the Servant of the Lord (*"He was wounded for our sins, and tormented for our iniquities; the chastisement of our peace was upon Him, and by His stripe we are healed"*).
[^245]: Epistle of the Sixth Hour: Hebrews 2:11–18 (section 306). St. Paul's theological discourse on the Incarnation: Christ took part of flesh and blood that through death He might destroy the devil who had the power of death, becoming a merciful and faithful High Priest.
[^246]: Gospel of the Sixth Hour: Luke 23:32–49 (section 111). St. Luke's account of the Crucifixion between two malefactors, the prayer of Christ for His executioners, the salvation of the prudent thief (*"Today shalt thou be with Me in Paradise"*), the darkness over all the earth from the sixth to the ninth hour, and Christ's final commendation into the hands of the Father.
[^247]: Liturgical patristic reading after the Gospel of the Sixth Hour: customarily from the Catechetical Lectures of St. Cyril of Jerusalem (Catechesis 13) or St. John Chrysostom's Homily on the Cemetery and the Cross.
[^248]: The Prayer of the Sixth Hour by Saint Basil the Great: *Бж҃е и҆ Гдⷭ҇и си́лъ, и҆ всеѧ̀ тва́ри Содѣ́телю* ("O God and Lord of hosts, and Maker of all creation"), beseeching God to nail our flesh to His fear, wound our souls with His love, and guide us into His unapproachable and everlasting light.
[^249]: The Order of the Ninth Hour of Holy and Great Friday: marking the solemn hour of 3:00 PM when our Lord Jesus Christ, having tasted the vinegar and cried with a loud voice, gave up the ghost upon the Cross.
[^250]: Psalm 68 (LXX 68, *Спаси́ мѧ Бж҃е, ꙗ҆́кѡ внидо́ша во́ды до дꙋшѝ моеѧ̀*), the paramount Messianic Passion psalm, directly prophesying Christ's thirst, the gall and vinegar (*"They gave Me gall for My food, and in My thirst they gave Me vinegar to drink"*), and the reproaches of those that reviled God falling upon Him.
[^251]: Psalm 69 (LXX 69, *Бж҃е, въ по́мощь мою̀ вонмѝ*), David's urgent cry for divine aid against mockers and persecution, traditional to the opening of the Ninth Hour.
[^252]: Psalm 85 (LXX 85, *Приклонѝ Гдⷭ҇и ᲂу҆́хо твоѐ, и҆ ᲂу҆слы́ши мѧ̀*), a prayer of profound humility and trust in God's great mercy, foretelling the worship of all nations before the true God.
[^253]: Troparion of the Ninth Hour in Tone 8: *Ви́дѧ разбо́йникъ Нача́льника жи́зни на крⷭ҇тѣ̀ ви́сѧща* ("Seeing the Author of life hanging upon the Cross, the thief said..."), praising the faith of the thief who recognized the crucified Lord as God through the cosmic signs in creation.
[^254]: The Stichera Idiomela of the Ninth Hour: Tone 7 (*Оу҆́жасъ бѣ̀ ви́дѣти*), Tone 2 (*Є҆гда̀ на крⷭ҇тѣ̀ пригвозди́ша беззако́ннїи*), and Tone 6 (*Дне́сь ви́ситъ на Дре́вѣ* / "Today He Who hung the earth upon the waters is hung upon the Tree"). The 15th Antiphon of Great Friday Matins incorporated into the Royal Hours, culminating in the threefold veneration of the Passion and prayer for the Resurrection.
[^255]: Old Testament Prophecy of the Ninth Hour: Jeremiah 11:18–23; 12:1–5, 9–11, 14–15. Commemorating the Prophet Jeremiah as a type of the innocent Lamb led to the slaughter (*"I was like a gentle lamb led to the slaughter; and I knew not that they had devised devices against me"*).
[^256]: Physical Leaf p79 = Printed Folio 74 + 5. The printed text abbreviates the Ninth Hour reading of Jeremiah with the rubric: *"Look in Great Thursday at the 1st Hour, folio 74"* (*И҆щѝ въ Четверто́къ вели́кїй на а҃-мъ часѣ̀, ли́стъ ѻ҃д*).
[^257]: Epistle of the Ninth Hour: Hebrews 10:19–31 (section 324). St. Paul on entering the Holy of Holies through the veil of Christ's flesh by His Blood, warning against counting the Blood of the covenant an unholy thing or outraging the Spirit of grace.
[^258]: Gospel of the Ninth Hour: John 18:28–19:37 (sections 61 and 62). St. John's Passion Gospel: the trial before Pilate, the scourging and crowning with thorns, *Ecce Homo*, the Crucifixion at Golgotha, the commending of the Mother of God to the beloved disciple, Christ's death, the piercing of His side with a lance, and the witness of blood and water.
[^259]: Liturgical patristic reading after the Gospel of the Ninth Hour: *The Homily on the Cross* (*Сло́во крⷭ҇тное*), traditional to the Sabaite and Studite Royal Hours, drawn from St. John Chrysostom or St. Cyril of Alexandria.
[^260]: The Prayer of the Ninth Hour: *Влⷣко Гдⷭ҇и І҆и҃се Хрⷭ҇тѐ Бж҃е на́шъ, долготерпѣ́вый ѡ҆ на́шихъ согрѣше́нїихъ* ("O Master, Lord Jesus Christ our God, Who hast long-suffered over our transgressions"), commemorating Christ's opening of Paradise to the prudent thief and petitioning for the mortification of carnal mind and renewal of the inner man.
[^261]: The Typika (Beatitudes) chanted quickly at the end of the Royal Hours, followed by the heavenly choir hymn, the Creed, the Kontakion of Great Friday, Psalm 33, and the final dismissal.
[^262]: Sabaite Typikon rubric for Great and Holy Friday: total aliturgical day (neither Presanctified nor Divine Liturgy is permitted) and strict fast without common meal or cooked food. Citing the Apostolic Constitutions (Book V, ch. 18) and the Canonical Epistle of St. Dionysius the Great of Alexandria (canon 1, PG 10:1272–1277) on the duration of the Paschal fast.
[^263]: Full-page copperplate engraving facing Great Friday Vespers: The Crucifixion of Christ with the Most Holy Mother of God and the Holy Apostle and Evangelist John the Theologian standing at the foot of the Cross upon Mount Golgotha, with the titulus *I.N Ц.I.*, the skull of Adam, and the caption: *"For God so loved the world..."* (John 3:16).
