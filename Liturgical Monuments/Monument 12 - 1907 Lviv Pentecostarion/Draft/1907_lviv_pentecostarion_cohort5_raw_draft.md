=== LEAF p47 ===
*[Running header: At Matins / p. 39]*

...And Joseph is first taken as a type of this. For he was the youngest son of the patriarch Jacob, born to him of Rachel, envied by his own brethren on account of certain dreams. First he was hidden in an excavated pit, and stolen from his father by his children with a deceitfully blood-stained coat, as though he had been devoured by beasts. Then he was sold for thirty pieces of silver unto the Ishmaelites, who in turn sold him unto Potiphar, chief of the eunuchs of Pharaoh, king of Egypt. And when his mistress raged against the chastity of the youth, because he would not commit lawlessness, he left his garment and fled; but she slandered him unto her master, and bonds and prison received him. Then by the interpretation of dreams he was brought out, and presented unto the king, and appointed ruler over all the land of Egypt. And again by the distribution of corn he was made known unto his brethren, and having spent all his life well, he died in Egypt, known as great in chastity alongside his other virtues. And he is a type of Christ: for Christ also is envied by the Jews of His own tribe, and sold by His disciple for thirty pieces of silver, and shut up in the dark and gloomy pit of the tomb; and bursting forth thence by His own sovereign power, He reigns over Egypt—that is, over every sin, and vanquishes it unto the end—and rules over the whole world, and as the Lover of mankind redeems us by the mystical distribution of corn, since He gave Himself for us, and feeds us with heavenly bread, His life-bearing Flesh. In accordance with this discourse, therefore, Joseph the All-Comely is commemorated this day.

Here also we make commemoration of the withered fig tree: for the divine Evangelists, namely Matthew and Mark, record after the narrative concerning the palms: "On the morrow, when they were come from Bethany, He hungered" [Mark 11:12]. And the other says: "Now in the morning, as He returned into the city, He hungered; and seeing a fig tree having only leaves, for the time of figs was not yet, He came unto it, and when He found no fruit thereon, He said: Let no fruit grow on thee henceforward for ever; and presently the fig tree withered away" [Matt. 21:18-19]. The fig tree is the Jewish synagogue, wherein the Savior found not fitting fruit, but only the shadow of the Law; and this He took away from them, making it utterly void. But if anyone say: For what reason did an inanimate tree become dry, taking a curse having not sinned? Let him know that the Jews, seeing Christ ever doing good unto all and causing no one any grief, objected that He had power only to do good, but not to do evil. And the Master, being the Lover of mankind, was not pleased to show this upon a man, lest it so happen; therefore, that He might convince the ungrateful people that He hath also sufficient power for vengeance, yet as the Good One desires it not, upon an inanimate and insensate nature He works vengeance. Together with this, there is also a certain ineffable saying that hath come down unto us from the most wise elders, even as Isidore of Pelusium says:[^60] that this was the tree of transgression, whose leaves the transgressors used for a covering; therefore also it was cursed by Christ as the Lover of mankind, since it did not suffer then, that it might no longer bear fruit that was guilty of sin. And that sin is likened unto a fig is praiseworthy: for it hath that which sweetens with pleasure, the stickiness of sin, and which hardens afterwards and torments the conscience. Nevertheless, from these things the narrative of the fig tree is set forth here for moral instruction, even as for Joseph because he bore the type of Christ. For every soul is a fig tree that is destitute of every spiritual fruit; unto which in the morning, immediately after the present life, the Lord finding no rest therein, dries it up with a curse, and sends it into eternal fire; and it stands as a withered pillar, terrifying them that bring not forth the fitting fruit of virtues.

By the prayers of Joseph the All-Comely, O Christ God, have mercy on us. Amen.

And their faces and their wings were stretched upward on the four; two wings of every one were joined one to another, and two covered their bodies above; and they went every one straight forward: whither the spirit was to go, they went; and they turned not as they went. And in the midst of the living creatures was an appearance like burning coals of fire, and like the appearance of torches turning back and forth in the midst of the living creatures; and the fire was bright, and out of the fire went forth lightning. And the living creatures ran and returned as the appearance of a flash of lightning. Now as I beheld, behold, one wheel upon the earth by the four living creatures. The appearance of the wheels and their workmanship was like unto the appearance of beryl, and they four had one likeness; and their work was as it were a wheel in the middle of a wheel. When they went, they went upon their four sides; and they turned not when they went. As for their rings, they had both height; and I beheld them, and the rings of all four were full of eyes round about. And when the living creatures went, the wheels went by them; and when the living creatures were lifted up from the earth, the wheels were lifted up. Whithersoever the cloud was to go, there was also the spirit to go; the living creatures went, and the wheels were lifted up with them; for the spirit of life was in the wheels.
