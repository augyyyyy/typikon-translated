*Synaxarion for the Sunday of Saint Thomas.*

*Verses:*
*If the seals or keys of the tomb hindered not / Thy course, O Savior, how should the locks of the doors hinder it?*

On this Sunday we celebrate the Renewal of Christ's Resurrection, and the Touching of the holy Apostle Thomas. For it was an ancient custom that renewals should take place: when a certain notable event, as years roll by, occurred on that day on which it had been accomplished, they kept the memory of that thing active, lest those great deeds should fall into oblivion; and in manifold ways, as was done of old with the festival of Tabernacles, and others, not to speak of each one in detail; and the Lord's Resurrection itself—a deed more excellent and higher than all things pertaining unto life, and surpassing all thought—we not only celebrate and renew each year, but continually also after eight days: for the first renewal of this is the present Sunday, which is truly called both the eighth and the first: the eighth, indeed, as being from Pascha; and the first, as the beginning of the others; and again the eighth, as being set forth as an image of that unending day in the age to come, which shall be the first and altogether one, not divided by night. These things, then, concerning renewals.

Now that which concerns Thomas came about thus: when Christ appeared late unto the disciples on the very day on which He arose, Thomas was not there; for he had not gathered together with the others because of the fear of the Jews. Then, having arrived a little later, and having learned of Christ's appearance, he did not believe—not only that the disciples had seen Him risen, but that the Lord had risen at all; and he being one of the Twelve, God, Who is marvelous in wisdom, taking such care for even one, and together with this by a greater dispensation that the Resurrection might be even more confirmed for those who should come after, permitted eight days to elapse: that He might thoroughly sharpen his love, and that he who had disbelieved more than all might furnish unto all a more certain assurance of the Resurrection; He comes again, and the doors being shut as before, Thomas also being present, He enters; and having imparted the customary peace, thereafter He stretches forth unto Thomas, and says: "Bring hither thy finger, and behold My hands; and bring thy hand, and thrust it into My Side; and be not unbelieving, but believing: for since thou didst seek to be confirmed not only by sight, holding the common things of vision to be a phantom, but being forward, didst also demand touch"; and by these words He showed that when Thomas had spoken these things unto the disciples, He had heard him: "Thrust thy hand into My Side"; and it shows that the wound upon the Side was broad, so as even to admit a hand within it. And Thomas, having examined carefully and received faith through touch—[for it was permitted him to see these things and to do them, even unto a Body altogether incorruptible and deified]—cried aloud at that examination: "My Lord and my God!"—the one because of the flesh, and the other because of the Godhead. And Christ says unto Thomas: "Because thou hast seen Me, thou hast believed; blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed."

Now Thomas is called the Twin: either because he was born with another, or because of his doubting concerning the Resurrection, or because by nature two fingers of his right hand were conjoined together—namely, the middle finger and that which is before it, called the forefinger—as one might say, because he was to doubt and to touch with them; and others say, which is more certain, that the name Thomas is interpreted "Twin". This was the second appearance of Christ; the third was at the Sea of Tiberias at the catching of the fish, when He also partook of food baked by divine fire, as He Himself knows, prefiguring the most certain Resurrection; then He appears at Emmaus; the fifth time in Galilee unto the Eleven, as they say; and He continued to appear until He ascended, working many and supernatural signs before the disciples after the Resurrection; which things, having shown them unto not many, the Evangelists omitted to write down all of them, since it was not possible for many men living in the world to hear them, being exceedingly supernatural. By the prayers of Thine Apostle Thomas, O Christ our God, have mercy upon us. Amen.[^451]

Not in vain did Thomas doubt concerning Thy Rising: he was not cast down, but was zealous to show this beyond doubt unto all nations, O Christ; whence, having assured all through his disbelief, he taught them to say: Thou art the Lord God of our fathers, Who art supremely exalted, blessed art Thou!

*Troparion:*

Having with fear thrust his hand into Thy life-giving Side, O Christ, Thomas with trembling perceived the twofold operation, O Savior, unconfusedly united in Thee in two natures; and in faith he cried aloud, saying: Thou art the Lord God of our fathers, Who art supremely exalted, blessed art Thou!
