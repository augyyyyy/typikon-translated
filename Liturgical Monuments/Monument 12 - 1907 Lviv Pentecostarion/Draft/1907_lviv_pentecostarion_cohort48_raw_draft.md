*Synaxarion on the Ascension of the Lord God and Savior Jesus Christ:*[^1661]

*Verses:*  
*Thou didst sit at the right hand of the Father, O Word,*  
*Showing unto the Disciples a most sure faith.*[^1662]

On this same day, on Thursday of the Sixth Week after Pascha, we celebrate the Ascension of our Lord God and Savior Jesus Christ. For while He was with the Disciples before the Passion, He promised the coming of the All-Holy Spirit, saying: It is expedient for you that I go away; for if I go not away, the Comforter will not come. And again: When He is come, He will teach you all truth. And after His Resurrection from the dead, appearing unto them during forty days, not continually, but being present separately, eating and drinking with them, and presenting His most certain Resurrection. Finally, having promised many things concerning the Kingdom of God, He commanded them not to depart from Jerusalem, but to remain there, awaiting the coming of the Most Holy Spirit, and to be baptized therewith: for your baptism formerly from John was with water and single, although afterwards Epiphanius of Cyprus related that John the Theologian baptized the Mother of God, and Peter baptized him and the other Apostles. And He commanded them to remain in Jerusalem, so that the preaching of the Gospel might first be confirmed there, lest going to foreign places they might easily be slandered, and as was meet, that as certain soldiers they should from thence be prepared with the weapon of the Spirit, and so go forth to battle against the enemies of Christ. But when the time of the Ascension arrived, He led them out unto the Mount of Olives: and it is so called because it was planted with very many olive trees. And having spoken unto them concerning the preaching unto the end of this age, and also concerning His incomprehensible Kingdom in the age to come, seeing that they were wishing to hold Him back, His all-pure Mother also being there, He appointed Angels for them, showing them His ascent into heaven. And while they looked on, He was taken up from the midst of them, a cloud having received Him; and thus being sent up by the Angels, who commanded one another the lifting up of the heavenly doors, and marveled at His flesh being crimson from the blood, He ascended and sat at the right hand of the Father: having deified the flesh, and—I dare to say—having made it truly divine, whereby we have been reconciled, the ancient enmity being abolished. And Angels in the form of men stood before the Apostles, saying: Ye men of Galilee, why stand ye marveling and gazing into heaven? This Jesus, Whom ye see as God in the flesh, this same shall come again manifestly in the flesh; yet not as before, in poverty and silence, but with great glory, as ye now see Him escorted by Angels. Then the Apostles, leaving off their gazing, returned from the Mount of Olives, which is near Jerusalem, being distant two thousand and forty foot-paces: this is a Sabbath-day’s journey. For so it was ordained by Moses to walk so many paces on the Sabbath, because the Tabernacle of the Covenant also stood at that number of paces distant from the camp of the Jews. For it was permitted for anyone to walk that far on the Sabbath to worship, and not to extend their journey further; for this reason it was called a Sabbath-day’s journey. Hence it seems to some that Christ’s Ascension took place on the Sabbath, but they are mistaken. And having returned, the Apostles entered into the upper room, where they had gathered together with the Myrrh-bearing women and the Mother of the Word, occupying themselves in fasting, and prayers, and supplication, and awaiting the descent of the All-Holy Spirit, according as was the promise.[^1663]
