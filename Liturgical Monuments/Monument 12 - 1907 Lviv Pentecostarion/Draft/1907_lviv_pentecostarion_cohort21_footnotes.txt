[^375]: Ode 6 Heirmos of Holy Saturday Compline: "Jonah was caught, but not held fast..." In this heirmos, the Prophet Jonah's three-day deliverance from the belly of the sea monster prefigures Christ's bodily rest in the tomb and His triumph over death. The concluding phrase quotes Jonah 2:9 (LXX: "They that observe vanities and lies have forsaken their own mercy"), addressed here directly to the Roman guard ("custodia") keeping watch over Christ's sepulchre.
[^376]: The Holy Saturday Kontakion and Ikos in Tone 6: "He Who shut the abyss is beheld dead..." Traditionally ascribed to Saint Romanos the Melodist, this hymn contemplates the supreme theological antinomy of the Creator resting bodily in the tomb while hades groans below and the Jews plot to slander His Resurrection. The refrain proclaims Holy Saturday as the "most blessed Sabbath" (prefigured in Genesis 2:2-3), wherein Christ rests from all His works of recreation.
[^377]: The Continuous Reading of the Acts of the Apostles: Preserving the ancient Jerusalem nocturnal vigil before Paschal Matins, the Typikon directs that after Compline the reader immediately begins reading the Acts of the Apostles, the biblical account of the Resurrection proclaimed to the nations, continuing until the fourth hour of the night (approx. 10:00 PM) when the bells are struck for the Paschal Midnight Office.
[^378]: The Paschal Midnight Office (Nocturns): Celebrated late on Saturday night before the midnight Paschal vigil, the Midnight Office comprises the repetition of the Great Saturday Matins Canon ("He Who in ancient times concealed the pursuing tyrant...", Tone 6). During Ode 9, the clergy transfer the Holy Shroud (Plashchanytsia) from the tomb in the center of the nave to the holy table in the sanctuary, where it remains for forty days until the Leave-taking of Pascha.
[^379]: The Paschal Patristic Homily of Saint Epiphanius of Cyprus: Entitled *In sancto et magno Sabbato* (PG 43:439-464), this classic patristic lection opens with the renowned words: "What is this? Today there is a great silence upon the earth, a great silence and stillness, for the King sleepeth." It portrays Christ descending into hades to awaken Adam and Eve and all the righteous of the Old Covenant.
[^380]: The Extended Litany at the Midnight Office: Includes seasonal agricultural petitions according to weather conditions (supplications for rain during drought or for temperate dry weather during unseasonable rains), reflecting the ancient agrarian liturgical cycle embedded in the Slavic Trebnik and Typikon traditions.
[^381]: The Concluding Dismissal of the Holy Saturday Midnight Office: The final liturgical dismissal of the Lenten Triodion period, commemorating Christ Who "voluntarily was laid in the grave in the Flesh and trod down hades." Immediately following this dismissal, the church lights are extinguished in anticipation of the Paschal midnight procession.
[^382]: The Frontispiece Engraving of the Resurrection of Christ (p206): Designed and executed after a sketch under the direction of Evlogy (E. V.) Voronets in 1901 for the Stauropegion typography, this full-page plate depicts the triumphant Risen Christ ascending above the open tomb surrounded by radiant beams of light and angelic choirs, with the biblical caption from Luke 24:34: "Truly the Lord is risen..."
[^383]: The Master Inscription of the Pentecostarion: "Beginning with God the Holy. The Pentecostarion, that is, the Festal Fifty Days..." This formal incipit marks the boundary between the Lenten Triodion (which concluded with the Midnight Office) and the Pentecostarion proper, which encompasses the liturgical period from the dawn of Holy Pascha through the Sunday of All Saints.
[^384]: The Paschal Midnight Procession and Outdoor Matins Opening: At midnight the church bells peal, the temple is filled with incense, and the clergy and faithful process out of the dark sanctuary through the northern doors into the narthex (or outside around the temple) with tapers, icons, and the Holy Gospel, while chanting the Sticheron in Tone 6 ("Thy Resurrection, O Christ Savior..."). The western doors are shut to symbolize the sealed sepulchre of Christ.
[^385]: The Paschal Troparion in Tone 5: "Christ is risen from the dead, trampling down death by death, and upon those in the tombs bestowing life." Chanted for the first time before the closed doors of the temple, this core hymnographic proclamation is repeated three times by the superior and three times by the choir, inaugurating the bright Paschal celebration.
[^386]: The Paschal Verses: Drawn from Psalm 67:1-3 [68:1-3] ("Let God arise, and let His enemies be scattered...") and Psalm 117:24 [118:24] ("This is the day which the Lord hath made..."), these verses are chanted antiphonally between the superior and the choir before the temple doors are struck with the Cross and flung open, symbolizing Christ shattering the gates of brass and breaking the bars of iron in hades.
[^387]: The Paschal Canon of Saint John of Damascus in Tone 1: Known as the "Golden Canon" or the "Queen of Canons," this hymnographic masterpiece of the 8th century is celebrated with dynamic censing of the whole temple and mutual Paschal greetings ("Christ is risen! Truly He is risen!") at every ode, accompanied by the repeated refrain "Christ is risen from the dead."
[^388]: The Paschal Hypakoe in Tone 4: "When they that were with Mary came before the dawn..." Commemorating the holy Myrrh-Bearing Women arriving at the sepulchre before the dawn, where the angel announces to them that Christ is risen and bids them proclaim the glad tidings to the Apostles.
[^389]: The Paschal Reading from Saint Gregory the Theologian: Oration 45 (The Second Paschal Oration, PG 36:623-664), opening with the citation of Habakkuk 2:1: "Upon my watch will I stand, and sit upon the rock..." The prophet Habakkuk stands upon his watch, beholding the light-bearing angel proclaiming the Resurrection of Christ.
[^390]: The Paschal Kontakion in Tone 8: "Though Thou didst descend into the grave, O Immortal One..." Ascribed to Saint Romanos the Melodist, this hymn crystallizes the dogma of the Resurrection: Christ's bodily descent into the grave destroyed the dominion of hades, while His rising brought the apostolic greeting of joy ("Rejoice!") to the Myrrh-Bearers and divine peace to the disciples.
