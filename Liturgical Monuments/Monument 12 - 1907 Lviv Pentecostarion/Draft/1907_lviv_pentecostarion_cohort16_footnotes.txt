[^284]: Codicological woodcut plate: A monumental woodcut headpiece engraving positioned at the opening of Holy and Great Saturday, depicting the Entombment of Christ (*Положе́нїе во гро́бъ* / *Epitaphios Threnos*). The dead Christ, wrapped in the winding-sheet (*плащани́ца*), is borne to the sepulchre by Joseph of Arimathea and Nicodemus, attended by the Virgin Theotokos, Saint John the Theologian, and the Myrrh-bearing women before a rock cave, flanked on both sides by quadruple vertical floral strapwork columns.
[^285]: Liturgical office opening: The Matins of Holy and Great Saturday (*Во святу́ю и вели́кую Суббо́ту на Ѹ҆́трени*), historically known as the Burial of Christ or the Lamentations at the Tomb (*Свяще́нный Пла́чь* / *Ἐπιτάφιος Θρῆνος*), one of the supreme climaxes of the Byzantine liturgical year.
[^286]: Liturgical time rubric: "At the seventh hour of the night" (*въ седмы́й ча́съ но́щи*), designating approx. 1:00 AM by Byzantine/monastic seasonal reckoning, prescribing that this service be celebrated as an all-night nocturnal vigil keeping watch over the buried Lord.
[^287]: The Resurrection and Entombment Troparia at "God is the Lord": The noble Joseph (*Благообра́зный І҆ѡ́сифъ*), When Thou didst descend unto death (*Е҆гда̀ снизше́лъ є҆сѝ къ сме́рти*), and Unto the myrrh-bearing women (*Мѵроно́сицамъ жена́мъ*), summarizing the mystery of the Crucified, buried, and descending Life-giver.
[^288]: Codicological rubric: The Parrecclesiarch (*Параекклисїа́рхъ* / candle-lighter and sacristan) distributes unlit or lighted candles to all the brethren, symbolizing the light of the Resurrection already dawning within the nocturnal darkness of the tomb.
[^289]: The Amomos / Kathisma 17 (*Блаже́ни непоро́чнїи* / Psalm 118): The longest psalm in the Psalter, the funeral psalm of the Hebrew and Christian traditions, interpreted typologically as the psalm of the spotless Lamb of God lying in the tomb.
[^290]: The Enkomia / Lamentations at the Tomb: Prescribed in three Staseis (*трѝ Статі̑и*). The First Stasis is chanted in Tone 5 (*гла́съ є҃*), the Second in Tone 2 (*гла́съ в҃*), and the Third in Tone 3 (*гла́съ г҃*). Each psalm verse is intercalated with a short hymnographic sticheron/troparion meditating on Christ's death and descent into hades.
[^291]: Rubric on the initial censing: The Superior (*И҆гꙋ́менъ*) stands before the Shroud (*Плащани́ца*) in the center of the nave (*посредѣ̀ Це́ркве*), sings the opening refrain, and censes cruciformly (*покади́въ крⷭ҇тоѡбра́знѡ*), followed by censing the altar and the whole church.
[^292]: Theological typology: Directly juxtaposing the messianic glory of Psalm 44:3 ("Fair in beauty beyond all the sons of men" / *Кра́сный добро́тою па́че всѣ́хъ человѣ́къ*) with the Suffering Servant of Isaiah 53:2 ("He hath no form nor comeliness; and when we shall see Him, there is no beauty" / *ꙗ҆́кѡ беззра́ченъ ме́ртвъ ꙗ҆влѧ́етсѧ*).
[^293]: Christ as the Rock of Life (*Живота̀ Ка́мень*): Evoking the miraculous rock struck in Horeb (Exodus 17:6, 1 Corinthians 10:4) and the stone cut out of the mountain without hands (Daniel 2:34, 45). Swallowing this living Rock, hades is forced to vomit up all the righteous dead held captive since Adam.
[^294]: Scriptural typology: The dying grain of wheat (*я́коже пшени́чное зе́рно*), echoing Christ's discourse in John 12:24 ("Truly, truly, I say unto you, Except a corn of wheat fall into the ground and die, it abideth alone: but if it die, it bringeth forth much fruit"), by His death yielding an abundant harvest of resurrected souls.
[^295]: Pauline Adam-Christ typology: Christ as the New Adam (*но́вый во пло́ти ꙗ҆вле́йсѧ А҆да́мъ*), reversing the death brought by the first Adam through envy and disobedience (Romans 5:14-19, 1 Corinthians 15:22, 45).
[^296]: Messianic lion typology: The sleeping lion (*я́коже ле́въ ꙋ҆снꙋ́въ пло́тїю*), drawn from the patriarchal blessing of Jacob over Judah in Genesis 49:9 ("Judah is a lion's whelp; from the prey, my son, thou art gone up: he stooped down, he couched as a lion, and as an old lion; who shall rouse him up?"), interpreted by the Fathers as Christ sleeping in the tomb in His humanity while invincible in His divinity.
[^297]: Patristic Eve-Church typology: As Eve was fashioned from the side of the sleeping Adam in Eden (Genesis 2:21-22), so the Church was born and the sacraments of water (Baptism) and blood (Eucharist) poured forth from the pierced side of Christ asleep on the Cross (John 19:34).
[^298]: Messianic imagery of Eden: The flaming sword placed at the gate of Paradise to guard the Tree of Life (Genesis 3:24) is blunted and turned back by the sacrifice of Christ, granting redeemed humanity re-entry into Eden.
[^299]: Hymnographic mariology: The Mother of God as the Ewe (*А҆́гница*) weeping over her Lamb (*А҆́гнецъ*) led to the slaughter, directly continuing the poetic paradigm of St. Romanos the Melodist's Kontakion *Mary at the Cross*.
[^300]: Physical witnesses of the Resurrection: The empty grave clothes—the linen winding-sheet (*плащани́ца* / *σινδών*) and the napkin that was about His head (*суда́рь* / *σουδάрион*, John 20:6-7)—left behind in the tomb as silent, irrefutable testimony against the slanders of the chief priests.
[^301]: Direct apostrophe to Judas Iscariot: Indicting his feigned concern for the poor at the anointing in Bethany (John 12:4-6) and his covetous betrayal of the uncontainable divine Myrrh for thirty pieces of silver.
[^302]: Christ as the Spiritual Rock: Drawing upon Numbers 20:11 and 1 Corinthians 10:4 ("and that Rock was Christ"), struck to pour forth living waters of salvation in the wilderness.
[^303]: The sacramental dual stream: The "two-fold river" (*сугу́бая рѣка́*) of blood and water flowing from Christ's pierced side (John 19:34), the font of Baptism and the cup of the Holy Eucharist granting immortal life.
[^304]: Second Stasis of the Praises (*Статїа втора́я*): Chanted in Tone 2 (*гла́съ в҃*), beginning with the left choir singing the opening refrain: "It is truly meet to magnify Thee, O Christ the Life-giver..." (*Досто́йно є҆́сть велича́ти тѧ̀, Живода́вче Хрⷭ҇тѐ*).
[^305]: Christ's Sabbath rest: Meditating upon the Lord's physical death as a "natural life-giving sleep" (*естественноживо́тный со́нъ*), wherein He rests on the Great Saturday from all His works of redemption, even as God rested on the seventh day from creation (Genesis 2:2-3, Hebrews 4:9-10).
[^306]: The Mother of God's sorrow: Contrasting her painless virginal birthgiving (*кромѣ̀ болѣ́зней*, preserving her perpetual virginity) with the excruciating maternal birth-pangs (*болѣ́зни нестерпи̑мыя*) endured at the foot of the Cross, fulfilling Simeon's prophecy (Luke 2:35).
[^307]: Cosmic signs: The rending of the temple veil from top to bottom (Matthew 27:51) and the darkening of the sun (Luke 23:44-45), signaling the passing of the Old Covenant and creation's shuddering at the burial of its Creator.
[^308]: The Paschal Salutation: Anticipating the risen Christ's first word to the Myrrh-bearing women in the garden: "Rejoice!" (*Χαίρετε* / *Ра́дуйтеся*, Matthew 28:9).
