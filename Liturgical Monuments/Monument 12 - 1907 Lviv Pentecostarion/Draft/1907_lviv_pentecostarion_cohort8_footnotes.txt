[^101]: The celebrated Idiomel of Saint Kassiani the Nun (Kassia, ca. 810–865), chanted at Holy Wednesday Matins and Presanctified Vespers: Tone 8, *"Lord, the woman who had fallen into many sins..."* (*Господи, яже во многїя грѣхи впадшая жена*). One of the most famous liturgical compositions of the Byzantine and Slavonic traditions, it poignantly portrays the weeping sinful woman anointing Christ's feet in anticipation of His burial, contrasting her repentance with Eve's terror in the Garden of Eden.
[^102]: Codicological cross-reference to Great Monday (Physical Leaf p46 = Printed Folio 41 + 5), where the full liturgical order of the Hours (Third, Sixth, and Ninth Hours) with kathisma psalmody, Gospel readings, and the Prayer of Saint Ephraim the Syrian is detailed in full.
[^103]: The Sixth Hour Old Testament reading of Holy and Great Wednesday: Ezekiel 2:1–3:3, recounting the prophet Ezekiel's divine vocation to preach repentance to the rebellious and stiff-hearted house of Israel, and his eating of the scroll inscribed with lamentation and woe, which tasted in his mouth sweet as honey.
[^104]: The Gospel reading at the Sixth Hour on Holy and Great Wednesday: John 12:17–50, concluding the third of the four daily Gospel lessons of Passion Week.
[^105]: The Ninth Hour of Holy and Great Wednesday marks the definitive conclusion of the reading of the Four Gospels (*четвероевангелїе*) that began on Great Monday. The final reading covers the Passion narrative up to the betrayal.
[^106]: The concluding monastic prayer of the Ninth Hour: *"O Master of great mercy, Lord Jesus Christ our God, Who through Thine ineffable love for mankind..."* (*Владыко многомилостиве*), during which the entire monastic brotherhood remains prostrated on the ground, culminating in the solemn mutual Rite of Forgiveness (*Чинъ прощенїя*) that formally marks the conclusion of the Forty Days of Great Lent.
[^107]: The reciprocal monastic forgiveness formula: each monk approaches the Superior and fellow brethren two by two, bowing to the earth and asking: *"Forgive me, holy father, a sinner, wherein I have sinned against thee in word, deed, or thought."*
[^108]: Codicological and typikon distinction: In monasteries, the brethren disperse to their cells after the mutual forgiveness to maintain absolute prayerful silence until Vespers; in cathedral and parish churches, however, the faithful remain assembled as the bells ring a festive chime (*трезвонъ*) signaling the immediate transition to Presanctified Vespers.
[^109]: The ten stichera on *"Lord, I have cried"* at Presanctified Vespers of Holy and Great Wednesday: five idiomel stichera repeated to make ten (including repetitions of the Matins Praises and Aposticha), focusing intensely on the moral and spiritual contrast between the repentant sinful woman who offered costly spikenard and Judas Iscariot who bartered his Master for thirty pieces of silver.
[^110]: The solemn repetition of Saint Kassiani's Idiomel at Vespers, Tone 8, serving as the Doxastikon (*Слава, и нынѣ*) during the Entrance with the Holy Gospel at the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts.
[^111]: The Entrance with the Holy Gospel (*Входъ со Евангелїемъ*) at Presanctified Vespers: prescribed on Holy Wednesday because of the solemn Gospel reading (Matthew 26:6–16) appointed for the Presanctified Liturgy.
[^112]: The first Old Testament reading at Holy Wednesday Presanctified Vespers: Exodus 2:11–22, recounting Moses slaying the Egyptian taskmaster who was beating a Hebrew, his flight into Midian, his defense of the daughters of Jethro (Reuel) at the well, and his marriage to Zipporah.
[^113]: The second Old Testament reading at Holy Wednesday Presanctified Vespers: Job 2:1–10 (spanning physical leaves p75 and p76), recounting the second celestial assembly, Satan obtaining permission to afflict Job's flesh with grievous boils, and Job sitting upon the ashes outside the city.
[^114]: Septuagintal and recensional note on Job 2:9 LXX: The Slavonic printed text preserves the extended Greek Septuagint reading of the desperate lamentation of Job's wife (*"How long wilt thou endure... speak some word against the Lord, and die!"*), which is absent from the Masoretic Hebrew text. The bracketed clause `[и не даде безумїя Богу]` represents the canonical LXX ending of Job 2:10 (*"and he did not charge God foolishly"* / *οὐδὲ ἔδωκεν ἀφροσύνην τῷ Θεῷ*).
[^115]: The Presanctified Gospel of Holy and Great Wednesday: Matthew 26:6–16 (section 150), detailing the anointing of Christ by the woman in the house of Simon the leper in Bethany, and Judas Iscariot negotiating His betrayal with the chief priests for thirty pieces of silver.
[^116]: The liturgical cessation of prostrations: The three great prostrations made after the transfer of the Presanctified Gifts mark the solemn cessation of all full prostrations (*метанія великїя*) within the church temple until the Kneeling Prayers of Pentecost Vespers (with the sole exception of prostrations before the Holy Epitaphios and the Cross). In private cell devotions, prostrations continue until Great Friday.
[^117]: Small Compline on Holy Wednesday evening features a tri-ode (*трипѣснецъ*) composed by Saint Andrew of Crete (ca. 660–740), consisting of Odes 4, 8, and 9, dedicated to the mysteries of Great Thursday: the preparation of the Upper Room, the Mystical Supper, the Washing of the Feet, and the contrast between the true disciples and the betrayal of Judas.
[^118]: The Sessional Hymn at Compline, Tone 4, Podoben: "Thou hast appeared today" (*Явился еси днесь* / *Ἐπεφάνης σήμερον*), meditating on the institution of the Holy Eucharist as the mystical prefiguration of Christ's sacrifice on the Cross.
[^119]: Typikon rubric on the Midnight Office: From Holy Wednesday evening onward, the Midnight Office (*Полунощница*) is entirely omitted from public church celebrations throughout Passion Week and Bright Week, resuming only on the Sunday of Saint Thomas (Antipascha).
[^120]: Codicological and iconographic description of the full-width woodcut engraving on fol. 75r (Physical Leaf p80) depicting the Washing of the Feet (Mandatum / *Умовенїе ногъ*): Christ, kneeling and girded with a linen towel, washes the feet of Saint Peter, while the other Apostles sit or stand around the table in the Upper Room of Mount Sion.
[^121]: The nocturnal striking of the semantron (*параекклисїархъ клеплетъ*) in the seventh hour of the night (approximately 1:00 AM) marks the ancient monastic schedule for Matins of Holy and Great Thursday.
[^122]: The solemn Troparion of Holy and Great Thursday Matins, Tone 8: *"When the glorious disciples were enlightened at the washing of the Supper..."* (*Егда славнїи ученицы на умовенїи Вечери просвѣщахуся*), chanted thrice with Alleluia instead of "God is the Lord," summarizing the central themes of the Day: the illumination of the Apostles, the dark madness of Judas's avarice, and Christ's righteous judgment.
[^123]: The Matins Gospel of Holy and Great Thursday: Luke 22:1–39 (beginning in section 108 and concluding within section 109), recounting the preparation of the Passover meal, the institution of the Holy Eucharist, Christ's prophecy of Peter's denial, and His departure to the Mount of Olives.
[^124]: The Canon of Holy and Great Thursday, composed by Saint Cosmas the Melodist, Bishop of Maiuma (ca. 675–752), set in Tone 6. The 1907 Lviv edition preserves the original Greek iambic acrostic: *Τῇ μακρᾷ πέμπτῃ μακρὸν ὕμνον ᾄδω* (*Тѝ макра̀ пе́мити макро́нъ Ѵ҆́мнонъ ᾄδᾄδω*, literally: "On Great Thursday I sing a long hymn"), rendered into Church Slavonic as *"Въ великїй Четвертокъ долгую пѣснь пою."*
