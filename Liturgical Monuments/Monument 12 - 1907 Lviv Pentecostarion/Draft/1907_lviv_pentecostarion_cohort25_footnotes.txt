[^442]: Bright Saturday Epistle and Gospel: Acts 3:11-16 (pericope 8), proclaiming Saint Peter's address in Solomon's Porch after the healing of the lame man, testifying that the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob hath glorified His Servant Jesus, Whom the Jews delivered up, but God raised from the dead; and John 3:22-33 (pericope 11), recounting Saint John the Baptist's final testimony unto Christ in Judea ("He that hath the bride is the bridegroom... He must increase, but I must decrease"). In addition, this leaf codifies the monastic typikon for the solemn elevation, distribution, and consumption of the Artos (*раздроблѐнїе А҆́ртоса*) and the elevation of the Panagia bread at the refectory trapeza concluding Bright Week.
[^443]: Antipascha or Sunday of Saint Thomas (*Недѣ́лѧ А҆нтїпа́схи, є҆́же є҆́сть ѡ҆пѧза́нїе ст҃а́гѡ сла́внагѡ А҆пⷭ҇ла Ѳѡмы̀* / Ἀντίπασχα), the Octave of Pascha, also designated Renewal Sunday (*Но́ваѧ Недѣ́лѧ* / Dominica in albis), commemorating the second appearance of the risen Christ unto the Eleven gathered behind closed doors eight days after His Resurrection, and the touching of His side and nail-prints by Saint Thomas (John 20:19-31).
[^444]: The Festal Apolytikion (Troparion) of Thomas Sunday in Tone 7: "Though the tomb was sealed, Thou didst shine forth Life from the tomb, O Christ God..." (Запеча́танꙋ гро́бꙋ, живо́тъ ѿ гро́ба возсїѧ́лъ є҆сѝ). Chanted at Small and Great Vespers, Matins, and Divine Liturgy throughout the post-festive week until the Apodosis of the Feast, this hymn connects Christ's emergence from the sealed sepulchre with His passage through closed doors, bestowing the Holy Spirit upon the disciples for the renewal of humanity according to His abundant mercy.
[^445]: The Festal Idiomela at "Lord, I have cried" for the Sunday of Renewal (*Но́ваѧ Недѣ́лѧ*), authored by Saint John of Damascus ("John the Monk"). The hymnody focuses upon the physiological and empirical reality of the resurrected Body of Christ: entering through locked doors without breaching their physical integrity, yet offering tangible flesh, bones, and wounds to be touched, thereby proving that He was neither a disembodied phantom nor an ordinary mortal.
[^446]: The Festal Litiya hymn in Tone 4, singing of Christ's twofold nature (*твоѐ сꙋгꙋ́бое сꙋщество̀*): "O Lord, by the unendurable radiance of Thy Divinity..." Thomas's tactile examination of the Savior's pierced Side, hands, and feet serves as a dogmatic witness to the unconfused union of the divine and human natures in the one Hypostasis of the Incarnate Word.
[^447]: The Aposticha idiomelon in Tone 4 comparing Saint John the Evangelist and Saint Thomas: "O most marvelous wonder! Grass, touching fire, was saved... John reclined upon the breast of the Word, and Thomas was counted worthy to touch His Side..." While John drew forth the sublime theology of the pre-eternal Word at the Mystical Supper, Thomas obtained the physical proof of the bodily Resurrection, providing an irrefutable apologetic witness for subsequent generations.
[^448]: The Polyeleos Megalynarion (Magnification) of Thomas Sunday: "We magnify Thee, O Christ Life-giver, Who for our sake didst descend into hades, and hast raised all with Thyself" (Велича́емъ тѧ̀ Живода́вче Хрⷭ҇тѐ). The eight appointed psalm verses are drawn from Psalm 92:1a, Psalm 106:16, Psalm 106:14, Psalm 77:65, Psalm 67:2 [68:1], Psalm 117:24 [118:24], Psalm 9:33 [10:12], and Psalm 29:13 [30:12].
[^449]: The Canon of Thomas Sunday in Tone 1, composed by Saint John of Damascus ("John the Monk"), chanted with the heirmoi of the Paschal Canon ("The Day of Resurrection! Let us be radiant, O ye peoples...") as Katavasia. Following Ode 3 and the Hypakoe in Tone 6, the rubrics appoint the Patristic reading from Saint Gregory the Theologian: Oration 44, *In novam Dominicam* (PG 36:608-621; incipit: *Ѡ҆бновле́нїемъ чти́тисѧ дрѐвнїй зако́нъ* / "To honor renewals is an ancient law").
[^450]: The Kontakion and Ikos of Thomas Sunday in Tone 8, composed by Saint Romanos the Melodist. The Ikos employs vivid theological imagery (*Кто̀ сохранѝ ᲂу҆чени́чꙋ дла́нь тогда̀ неѡпали́мꙋ...*): "Who preserved the disciple's hand unburnt at that time, when he approached the fiery Side of the Lord?", likening Thomas's hand to Moses' burning bush (Exodus 3:2) and the Three Holy Youths in the Babylonian furnace (Daniel 3), protected by divine power while touching the incandescent Godhead.
[^451]: The Synaxarion of the Sunday of Saint Thomas from the Byzantine Menologion, attributed to Nikephoros Kallistos Xanthopoulos (c. 1256–1335). It expounds the theological significance of Renewal Sunday as the eighth day (*ѻ҆сми́ца*) prefiguring the unending, nightless Day of eternity in the age to come, explains the divine pedagogy in allowing Thomas to doubt for eight days, and explores the etymology of Didymus ("Twin", as doubting, born a twin, or possessing conjoined fingers).
