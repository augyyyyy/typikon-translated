*Synaxarion on the Fourth Sunday after Pascha, of the Paralytic:*[^575]

*Verses:*
*The word of Christ was strength unto the paralytic;*
*Thus this word alone was his healing.*

On this same day, the Fourth Sunday after Pascha, we commemorate the Paralytic, and as is meet, we celebrate this miracle of our Lord, God, and Savior Jesus Christ. It was placed here because Christ performed this during the Jewish Pentecost; for having entered Jerusalem for the feast, and being in the porch of five porticoes which Solomon built, which was also named the Sheep Pool, because they washed the entrails of the sheep slaughtered in the sanctuary for sacrifice there; or because whoever entered there first, whenever the water was troubled once a year by an Angel, was shown to be healed. He finds there a man who had been ill for thirty-eight years, and who was in perplexity as to who might cast him, lying there, into the water; from whom also we are instructed how great a good are waiting and patience, and that since baptism was to be given which purges away every sin, God ordained that miracles should be wrought through water in the Old Dispensation, so that when that baptism should come, it might be easily accepted. Jesus comes to this Paralytic, who was so named, and asks him; and he presents his lack of anyone to assist him. And Christ, knowing that the man had languished in weakness for so long, says: Take up thy bed and walk. And immediately he is shown to be healed, and taking his bed upon his shoulder, lest the deed be thought an apparition, he carried it to his home. Since it was the Sabbath, he was forbidden by the Jews to walk. But he pointed to Him Who had healed him, as having told him to walk on the Sabbath, not knowing who He was; for Jesus, he says, had slipped away and hidden Himself, as a crowd had gathered there. Afterward Jesus found him in the sanctuary, and says to him: Behold, thou hast been made whole; sin no more, lest a worse thing come upon thee; Christ showed that the infirmity of paralysis had happened to him on account of sins. Yet not every sickness is from sins, but also comes upon us from natural illness, from gluttony, from neglect, and from many other causes. The paralytic man, having realized that it was Jesus Who had made him whole, makes this known to the Jews. And they, being provoked to anger, seek to kill Christ, because He was breaking the Sabbath. But He spoke many things to them,

=== LEAF p345 ===
establishing that it is righteous to do good even on the Sabbath, and that He Himself is the One Who commanded the Sabbath to be kept, that He is equal to the Father, and even as the Father works hitherto, so also He works. It is to be known that this Paralytic is distinct from the one in Matthew; for that one was performed in a house, with men ministering to him, and he heard: Thy sins are forgiven thee; but this one was performed in a portico, and he had no man, as the Holy Gospel says, though he also took up his bed like that one. It is celebrated now because this also occurred during Pentecost, as did the accounts of the Samaritan Woman and the Blind Man. For Thomas and the Myrrh-bearers we celebrate for the assurance of Christ’s Resurrection from the dead; and the rest until the Ascension because He variously performed these during the time of the Jewish Pentecost, and because John briefly recorded them thus.

Christ has arisen from the dead, the first-fruits of those who have fallen asleep, the First-born of creation, and the Creator of all things that have been made; He has renewed in Himself the corrupted nature of our race: no longer dost thou hold dominion, O death, for the Master of all has destroyed thy power!
