[^480]: The 1907 Lviv Stauropegion edition retains the statutory Austro-Hungarian imperial prayer (*І҆мпера́тора*), invoking divine protection for Apostolic Emperor Franz Joseph I (reigned 1848–1916), under whose imperial patronal sanction the Stauropegion Institute operated.
[^481]: *Tone 1, Podoben: "While the soldiers were guarding Thy tomb" (Гро́бъ тво́й Сп҃се)* is the classical Resurrectional Sessional melody *Τὸν τάφον σου Σωτήρ*, sung throughout the Paschal season.
[^482]: The Church Slavonic reading *не невѣ́рꙋй мѝ во́лче* ("do not disbelieve Me, O wolf-like one" or "O impetuous one") translates the Greek *μὴ ἀπίστει μοι λύκε* or represents a recensional variant addressing Thomas's fierce and relentless questioning, which Christ converts from predatory skepticism into apostolic witness.
[^483]: Theological comparison between Saint John the Theologian and Saint Thomas: John reclined upon Christ's breast at the Mystical Supper (John 13:23–25) to draw forth sublime theology, while Thomas was counted worthy to touch the resurrected ribs (John 20:26–28) to confirm the physical reality of the bodily Resurrection for the entire Church.
[^484]: *Tone 2, Podoben: "O House of Ephratha" (До́ме Еѵ҆фра́ѳовъ)* is the venerable Prosomoion melody *Οἶκος τοῦ Ἐφραθᾶ*, originally composed for the Prefeast of the Nativity of Christ and widely used for concise festal stichera across the Triodion and Pentecostarion.
[^485]: The Greek acrostic *Ὑμνῳδία Ἰωσήφ* ("The Hymnody of Joseph"), preserved in the printed Slavonic edition through marginal Greek and Slavonic letters marking the successive strophes of Odes 4, 8, and 9 of the Thursday Triodion composed by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer (c. 816–886).
[^486]: *Tone 1, Podoben: "Joy of the heavenly hosts" (Небе́сныхъ чино́въ)* is the classical Prosomoion melody *Τῶν οὐρανίων ταγμάτων*, sung for festal stichera throughout the liturgical year.
[^487]: *Tone 1, Podoben: "When the stone had been sealed" (Ка́мени запеча́танꙋ)* is the venerable Prosomoion melody *Τοῦ λίθου σφραγισθέντος*, adapted here for the Friday Sessional Hymn of the Precious Cross.
[^488]: A Stavrotheotokion (*Крестобогоро́диченъ*) expressing the maternal lament of the Virgin Mary at the foot of the Cross, contemplating the paradox of the Creator hanging lifeless upon the Tree to effect mankind's salvation.
[^489]: *Tone 4, Podoben: "As noble among the martyrs" (Ꙗ҆́кѡ до́блѧ)* is the widely celebrated Prosomoion melody *Ὡς γενναῖον ἐν μάρτυσιν*, originally composed for the Great Martyr George and adapted here for the post-festal Paschal stichera of Thomas Week.
[^490]: The Greek acrostic *Δόξα τῷ Θεῷ ἀμήν* ("Glory to God, Amen"), preserved in the printed Slavonic edition through marginal Greek and Slavonic letters marking the successive strophes of Odes 5, 8, and 9 of the Friday Triodion composed by Saint Joseph the Hymnographer.
[^491]: A profound dogmatic formulation of the hypostatic union: touching the wounded side whence issued blood and water (John 19:34), Thomas confesses Christ to be neither "bare God" (*Бо́га не на́га*, without true human nature) nor "mere man" (*человѣ́ка не про́ста*, without true divinity), but the Incarnate Word, true God and true Man.
[^492]: The solemn Dogmatic Theotokion of Tone 1, *Всемі́рную сла́ву* ("The glory of all the world"), composed by Saint John of Damascus, celebrating the Virgin Mary as the heavenly gate, the temple of the Godhead, and the champion through whom the barrier of enmity was overthrown.
