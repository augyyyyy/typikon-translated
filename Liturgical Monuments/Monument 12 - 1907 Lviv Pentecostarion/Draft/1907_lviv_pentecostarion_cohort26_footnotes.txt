[^452]: *Tone 1, Podoben: "Thou Who didst adorn heaven with stars" (Ꙗ҆́кѡ ѕвѣзда́ми)* is the classical festive Exaposteilarion model melody (*Ὁ οὐρανὸν τοῖς ἄστροις κατακοσμήσας*), here abbreviated by its Slavonic incipit *Ꙗ҆́кѡ ѕвѣзда́ми* rather than the full *Не́бо ѕвѣзда́ми оу҆краси́вый*.
[^453]: The monastic catecheses (*oglasheniya*) of Saint Theodore the Studite (759–826), abbot of the Studion Monastery in Constantinople, traditionally read in the narthex during the Paschaltide processions (*litiya*) before the First Hour.
[^454]: The liturgical readings for Sunday of Saint Thomas Liturgy: the Apostle reading from the Acts of the Apostles, pericope 14 (Acts 5:12–20), and the Holy Gospel according to John, pericope 65 (John 20:19–31), recording Christ's appearance on the eighth day and the confession of Thomas.
[^455]: The Great Prokeimenon *Who is so great a god as our God?* (Ps. 76:14–15, 11, 12) is chanted in Tone 7 at festive Vespers on Pascha Sunday evening and on the evening of the Sunday of Antipascha (Saint Thomas Sunday).
[^456]: Saint Joseph the Hymnographer (c. 816–886), prolific Byzantine melodist and hymnographer, author of the weekday triodia and tetraodia included in the Pentecostarion across the Paschal season.
[^457]: The monastic rubric notes that the Paschaltide Midnight Office (*Polunochnitsa*) was commonly recited individually in cells (*v keliyakh*) during the festive octave rather than chanted in common choir in the nave.
[^458]: The Patristic homily assigned at Matins of Thomas Week: readings from Saint John Chrysostom's *Commentary on the Gospel of Saint John* (Homilies 86 and 87, expounding John 20:19–31).
[^459]: The Inter-Hours (*Mesoria*, Church Slavonic *Mezhdochasie*) are the four supplementary mid-hour offices inserted between the First, Third, Sixth, and Ninth Hours, resumed on the Monday of Saint Thomas Week following the Bright Week suspension.
[^460]: The liturgical readings for Monday of Thomas Week Liturgy: Acts pericope 8 (Acts 3:19–26) and John pericope 6 (John 2:1–11, the Marriage at Cana).
[^461]: Rubrical and recensional note documenting the liturgical variance between Ruthenian/Slavonic editions (which print the weekday Triodia of Saint Joseph in the Pentecostarion for Liturgy/Matins) and Byzantine Greek usage (which assigns them to Compline together with the Theotokion Canon).
[^462]: The Greek alphabetical acrostic *ᾄδει δὲ σὲ Ἰωσήф* ("Joseph also hymns Thee"), preserved in the printed Slavonic edition with individual marginal letters (*А, Д, Є, І, Д, Є, С, Є, І, Ѡ, С, И, Ф*) marking the successive troparia across Odes 1, 8, and 9 of the Triodion.
[^463]: *Tone 4, Podoben: "As one valiant among martyrs" (Ꙗ҆́кѡ до́блѧ)* is the widely used Prosomoion melody *Ὡς γενναῖον ἐν μάρτυσιν* (*Я́кѡ до́бля въ мꙋ́ченицѣхъ*), originating from the feast of Saint George the Great-Martyr.
