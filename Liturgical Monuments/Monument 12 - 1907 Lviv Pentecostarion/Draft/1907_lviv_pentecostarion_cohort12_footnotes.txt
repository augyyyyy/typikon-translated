[^197]: The Sixth Passion Gospel: Mark 15:16–32 (section 67). This pericope recounts the mocking of Christ by the Roman cohort in the praetorium, where they clothe Him in purple, crown Him with thorns, strike Him upon the head with a reed, spit upon Him, and bow their knees before Him in derision; His procession to Golgotha, with Simon of Cyrene compelled to bear His cross; His refusal of the wine mingled with myrrh; His crucifixion at the third hour; the division of His garments by casting lots; the superscription of His accusation ("The King of the Jews"); His crucifixion between two thieves; the mockery of the passers-by, chief priests, and scribes ("He saved others; Himself He cannot save... Let Christ the King of Israel descend now from the cross, that we may see and believe"); and the reviling of the thieves crucified with Him.
[^198]: Psalm 21:19 (LXX 21:18): *Раздѣли́ша ри̑зы моѧ̑ себѣ̀, и҆ ѡ҆ ѻ҆де́жди мое́й мета́ша жре́бїй* ("They parted My garments among them, and upon My vesture they cast lots"). In the liturgical structure of Passion Matins, this prokeimenon in Tone 4 introduces the Seventh Passion Gospel, highlighting the direct fulfillment of Davidic messianic prophecy in the crucifixion narrative.
[^199]: The Seventh Passion Gospel: Matthew 27:33–54 (section 113). The arrival at Golgotha; offering of vinegar mingled with gall; the crucifixion and casting lots for His garments; the setting up of the accusation ("This is Jesus the King of the Jews"); the railing of the passers-by and elders; the darkness over all the land from the sixth unto the ninth hour; Christ's loud cry at the ninth hour: *"Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani"*; the offering of the sponge filled with vinegar on a reed; Christ crying again with a loud voice and yielding up the ghost; the tearing in twain of the temple veil from top to bottom; the earthquake and rending of rocks; the opening of the graves and resurrection of many bodies of the saints; and the confession of the centurion and those watching Jesus: *"Truly this was the Son of God."*
[^200]: Rubric for patristic reading: *(И҆ полага́етсѧ чте́нїе)* — "And a reading is appointed." Following the Seventh Passion Gospel and preceding Psalm 50, traditional liturgical typika (including the Evergetis, Studite, and Ruthenian Stauropegion traditions) appoint a solemn patristic homily on the Lord's Passion, typically selected from St. John Chrysostom, St. Ephrem the Syrian, or St. Cyril of Alexandria.
[^201]: The Eighth Passion Gospel: Luke 23:32–49 (section 114). The leading away of two evildoers to be put to death with Jesus at Calvary; Christ's prayer for His executioners: *"Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do"*; the dividing of His garments; the derision of the rulers and soldiers offering vinegar; the superscription in letters of Greek, Latin, and Hebrew; the dialogue of the two thieves, wherein the repentant thief confesses Christ's innocence and cries: *"Lord, remember me when Thou comest into Thy kingdom"*, receiving the Lord's divine promise: *"Verily I say unto thee, Today shalt thou be with Me in paradise"*; the darkness over all the earth until the ninth hour; the sun darkened and veil rent; Christ's final commendation: *"Father, into Thy hands I commend My spirit"*; and the centurion glorifying God and declaring: *"Certainly this was a righteous man."*
[^202]: The Three-Ode Canon (Triodion / Tripesnets) of Great Friday Matins, composed by St. Cosmas the Melodist (Bishop of Maiuma, c. 675–752). Comprising Odes 5, 8, and 9 in Tone 6, the canon directly reflects Byzantine hymnographic structure for the Holy and Great Friday vigil. The rubric directs the heirmoi to be chanted twice and the troparia twelve times (interpolating the stichological refrains between the troparia), concluding with a joint katavasia by both choirs chanting the heirmos together.
[^203]: The acrostic (*краегране́сїе*) of St. Cosmas's Three-Ode Canon: *Проса́вватонъ те* (Greek: Προσάββατόν τε), signifying "And the eve of the Sabbath" (the day before the Sabbath, i.e., Friday). Across Odes 5, 8, and 9, the twelve troparia spell out this exact Greek acrostic in their original Byzantine verses, preserved here faithfully in Church Slavonic translation with the acrostic letters explicitly indicated in the margins: Ode 5 (`П`, `Р`, `О`), Ode 8 (`С`, `А`, `В`, `В`, `А`), and Ode 9 (`Т`, `О`, `Н`, `Т`, `Є`).
[^204]: The Kontakion of Holy and Great Friday in Tone 8 (*На́съ ра́ди Распѧ́таго, прїиди́те всѝ воспои́мъ* / *Τὸν δι' ἡμᾶς σταυρωθέντα, δεῦτε πάντες ὑμνήσωμεν*), composed by St. Romanos the Melodist (6th century). This kontakion introduces Romanos's celebrated poetic dialogue between the Virgin Mary and her Son on His way to Golgotha, emphasizing that Mary's confession of faith transcends the shame of the Cross: *"Though Thou dost endure crucifixion, Thou art my Son and my God!"*
[^205]: The Ikos of Holy and Great Friday (*Своего̀ А҆́гнца А҆́гница зрѧ́щи* / *Τὸν ἴδιον Ἄρνα θεωροῦσα ἡ Ἀμνάς*), forming the strophe of St. Romanos's kontakion. The Virgin Mary, seeing her Lamb led to slaughter, addresses Him with maternal pathos, asking whether He is hastening to another wedding in Cana of Galilee to turn water into wine, and begging the divine Logos not to pass her by in silence, but to speak a word of comfort to the Mother who bare Him.
[^206]: The Synaxarion of Holy and Great Friday, compiled by the 14th-century Byzantine ecclesiastical historian Nicephorus Callistus Xanthopoulos. The opening distich verses on the Crucifixion (*Живы́й є҆сѝ Бж҃е ты̀, и҆ ᲂу҆мерщвле́нный на дре́вѣ* / *Ζωὸς πέφυκας, ὦ Θεός, νεκρὸς δὲ σύ· Ὦ νεκρὲ γυμνέ, καὶ Θεοῦ ζῶντος Λόγε*) and on the thief (*Заключе́ннаѧ ѿве́рзе Е҆де́мскаѧ врата̀* / *Κεκλεισμένας ἤνοιξε τῆς Ἐδὲμ πύλας, Βαλὼν ὁ λῃστὴς κλεῖδα, τὸ Μνήσθητί μου*) encapsulate the cosmic paradox of the Creator dying in the flesh and opening paradise through the repentant thief's prayer. The narrative prose recounts the full chronological progression of Christ's trials before Annas, Caiaphas, and Pilate, the release of Barabbas, the scourging and crowning with thorns, the crucifixion at Golgotha, the supernatural portents, the piercing of the side, and the burial by Joseph and Nicodemus.
[^207]: *Lithostrotos* (Greek: Λιθόστρωτον; Hebrew/Aramaic: Gabbatha, John 19:13): the elevated stone pavement where Pilate sat on the judgment seat. The Synaxarion connects this biblical site with the Jewish and early Christian theological tradition regarding Solomon covering Adam's skull with a mound of stones, whence the name of the place was derived.
[^208]: The patristic theological exposition of the Crucifixion in the Synaxarion: St. Nicephorus Callistus Xanthopoulos expounds the typology of the Passion: Christ is crucified on Friday (the 6th day) and at the 6th hour because man was created on the 6th day and Adam stretched forth his hand to the tree at the 6th hour; the garden of the tomb corresponds to the Garden of Eden; the bitter drink heals the tasting of the forbidden fruit; the buffeting confers freedom; the spittings restore human honor; the crown of thorns removes the curse of the earth; the scarlet robe replaces the coats of skins; the nails arrest the movement of sin; the pierced side heals the wound of Adam's rib from which Eve was formed; the lance wards off the cherubim's flaming sword; the water from the side prefigures baptism; and the blood poured forth upon Adam's skull beneath Golgotha signifies the mystical baptism and redemption of fallen humanity by the New Adam.
[^209]: The Exapostilarion of Holy and Great Friday (*Разбо́йника бл҃горазꙋ́мнаго во є҆ди́номъ часѣ̀ ра́еви сподо́билъ є҆сѝ Гдⷭ҇и* / *Τὸν λῃστὴν αὐθημερὸν τοῦ παραδείσου ἠξίωσας, Κύριε*). Chanted thrice with great solemnity at the conclusion of the Three-Ode Canon, this hymn is one of the most beloved masterpieces of the Passion services, begging the Savior to enlighten the believer by the wood of the Cross even as He received the repentant thief into paradise.
[^210]: The Ninth Passion Gospel: John 19:25–37 (section 61). Standing by the Cross: His Mother, Mary the wife of Cleopas, and Mary Magdalene; Christ commending His Mother unto the beloved disciple John: *"Woman, behold thy son!"* and to the disciple: *"Behold thy mother!"*; Christ's cry: *"I thirst"*; the sponge filled with vinegar put upon hyssop and placed to His mouth; Christ's triumph: *"It is finished"*; bowing His head and giving up the ghost; the soldiers breaking the legs of the first and the other thief, but finding Jesus already dead, they broke not His legs, fulfilling Scripture (*"A bone of Him shall not be broken"*, Ex. 12:46, Ps. 33:21); one of the soldiers piercing His side with a lance, and forthwith blood and water coming out; and the testimony of the eyewitness disciple, fulfilling Zechariah 12:10 (*"They shall look on Him Whom they pierced"*).
[^211]: The Doxastikon (Glory) of the Praises (Lauds) in Tone 6 (*Совлеко́ша съ менѐ ри̑зы моѧ̑* / *Ἐξέδυσάν με τὰ ἱμάτιά μου*). A prophetic monologue of the suffering Messiah, drawing directly upon Psalm 2:9 (*"Thou shalt dash them in pieces like a potter's vessel"*) and the gospel accounts of the Roman soldiers stripping Him, placing the scarlet robe upon Him, crowning Him with thorns, and placing the reed in His hand.
[^212]: The Theotokion / Sticheron (Both now) of the Praises in Tone 6 (*Плещи̑ моѧ̑ да́хъ на ра̑ны* / *Τὸν νῶτόν μου ἔδωκα εἰς μάστιγας*). Drawing directly upon the Third Servant Song of Isaiah (Isaiah 50:6: *"I gave My back to the smiters, and My cheeks to them that plucked off the hair: I hid not My face from shame and spitting"*), this hymn confesses Christ's voluntary endurance of the tribunal of Pilate and the cross for the salvation of the world.
[^213]: The Tenth Passion Gospel: Mark 15:43–47 (section 69). Joseph of Arimathea, an honorable counselor who waited for the Kingdom of God, goeth boldly unto Pilate and craveth the body of Jesus; Pilate marveleth if He were already dead, and calling the centurion, asketh whether He had been any while dead; upon the centurion's confirmation, Pilate giveth the body to Joseph; Joseph buyeth fine linen, taketh Him down, wrappeth Him in the linen, and layeth Him in a sepulchre hewn out of a rock, rolling a stone unto the door; and Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses behold where He was laid.
[^214]: The Eleventh Passion Gospel: John 19:38–42 (section 62). Joseph of Arimathea, being a disciple of Jesus, but secretly for fear of the Jews, beseecheth Pilate that he might take away the body of Jesus; Pilate granteth permission; Nicodemus cometh also, bringing a mixture of myrrh and aloes, about an hundred pound weight; they take the body of Jesus and wind it in linen clothes with the spices, as the manner of the Jews is to bury; in the place where He was crucified there was a garden, and in the garden a new sepulchre wherein was never man yet laid; there they lay Jesus because of the Jews' preparation day, for the sepulchre was nigh at hand.
[^215]: Psalm 68:22 (LXX 68:21): *Да́ша въ снѣ́дь мою̀ же́лчь, и҆ въ жа́ждꙋ мою̀ напои́ша мѧ̀ ѻ҆́цта* ("They gave Me gall for My food, and for My thirst they gave Me vinegar to drink"). A messianic stichos chanted before the third aposticha sticheron of Great Friday.
[^216]: Psalm 73:12 (LXX 73:12): *Бг҃ъ же Цр҃ь на́шъ, пре́жде вѣ́ка содѣ́ла спасе́нїе посредѣ̀ землѝ* ("But God is our King of old, He hath wrought salvation in the midst of the earth"). Appointed as the final stichos of the Aposticha, confessing the cosmic and eternal scope of the Redemption accomplished on Golgotha.
[^217]: The Twelfth Passion Gospel: Matthew 27:62–66 (section 114). The final Passion Gospel, chanted immediately after the Aposticha at the conclusion of Great Friday Matins. On the morrow after the preparation day, the chief priests and Pharisees gather before Pilate, recalling Christ's prophecy: *"After three days I will rise again"*; they request that the sepulchre be made secure until the third day, lest His disciples come by night, steal Him away, and say unto the people: *"He is risen from the dead"*; Pilate granteth them a guard (*"Ye have a watch: go your way, make it as sure as ye can"*); and they go and make the sepulchre sure, sealing the stone and setting a watch.
[^218]: The Troparion of Holy and Great Friday in Tone 4 (*И҆скꙋпи́лъ ны̀ є҆сѝ ѿ кла́твы зако́нныѧ* / *Ἐξηγόρασας ἡμᾶς ἐκ τῆς κατάρας τοῦ νόμου*). Rooted in Galatians 3:13 ("Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us"), this hymn summarizes the entire theological drama of the Twelve Passion Gospels: by Christ's voluntary Crucifixion and piercing with the lance, the curse of the Law is dissolved and divine immortality is poured forth upon mankind.
[^219]: The solemn Great Friday Passion Dismissal: following the exclamation of the litany, the priest pronounces the special kenotic dismissal commemorating Christ's voluntary humiliation: *"May He Who endured spittings, and scourgings, and buffetings, the Cross and death, for our salvation, Christ our true God..."*
[^220]: The concluding Typikon rubric of Great Friday Matins: *(Пе́рвый же ча́съ не совокꙋплѧ́емъ ны́нѣ, но ѡ҆ второ́мъ часѣ̀ днѐ)* — "The First Hour we do not join now, but at the second hour of the day." In normal daily liturgical practice, the First Hour is attached directly to the end of Matins. On Holy and Great Friday, however, the First Hour is separated from Matins and chanted later in the morning (at the second hour, approximately 8:00 AM) together with the Third, Sixth, and Ninth Royal Hours (*Царскіе Часы*), which form a single extended monumental liturgical office with special psalms, prokeimena, Old Testament prophecies, Epistles, and Gospels.
