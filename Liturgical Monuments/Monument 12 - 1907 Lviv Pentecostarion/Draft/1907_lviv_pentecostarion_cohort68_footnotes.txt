[^3002]: Physical Leaf p671 (Printed Folio 335r). This final canonical rubric of the 1907 Lviv Pentecostarion regulates the correlation between the eleven Matins Resurrection Gospels (*столпѝ Є҆ѵⷢ҇лїй воскре́сныхъ ꙋ҆́тренныхъ*), the eight Tones of the Oktoechos, and the liturgical Epistle and Gospel pericopes read at the Divine Liturgy.

[^3003]: The 11 Sunday Matins Gospels rotate cyclically starting on All Saints Sunday (1st Sunday after Pentecost) with Gospel 1 (Matthew 28:16-20) in Tone 8. Beginning with the 2nd Sunday after Pentecost (Tone 1, Gospel 2), the 8 Tones and 11 Gospels advance in uninterrupted progression throughout the entire liturgical year through Great Lent up to Palm Sunday (*Недѣ́лѧ Цвѣтоно́сїѧ*), regardless of calendar shifts or the date of Pascha. The six pillars (*столпы̀*) represent the six complete 11-week cycles tabulated in the Visual Paschalion (*Зрѧ́чаѧ Пасха́лїѧ*) of the Horologion (*Часосло́въ*).

[^3004]: Because the moveable date of Pascha yields between 32 and 37 weeks between Pentecost and Great Lent, a complete ecclesiastical year typically utilizes between 40 and 45 Sundays from All Saints to Palm Sunday. Thus, while 6 full cycles of 11 Gospels would total 66 Sundays—more than any single liturgical year contains—all six pillars are codified in the tables so that even in the longest possible liturgical year, the sequence never runs short before Palm Sunday.

[^3005]: A critical liturgical distinction between the Divine Liturgy and Matins: The table of "omissions and additions" (*ѡ҆ прїѧ́тїи и҆ ѡ҆ставле́нїи*) in the Liturgikon (*Слꙋже́бникъ / Лїтꙋргі́онъ*) applies strictly to the Liturgy Epistles and Gospels, which terminate their regular post-Pentecost series at the 32nd Sunday after Pentecost (Zacchaeus) or the Sunday of the Publican and Pharisee (*Недѣ́лѧ ѡ҆ Мытарѣ̀ и҆ Фарисе́и*). When the date of Pascha requires adjustments (the "Lucan jump" and supplementary pericopes), only the Liturgy readings are skipped or repeated; the Sunday Oktoechos Tones and Matins Resurrection Gospels proceed without any omission or repetition straight through to Palm Sunday. The physical paper on leaf 335r exhibits historical adhesive repair and surface paper abrasion across lines 6-8 of this rubric, where the restored phrases are placed within brackets.

[^3006]: The master colophon (*Коне́цъ, и҆ Бг҃ꙋ сла́ва* — "The End, and to God be glory") formally concludes the printing of the monumental 1907 Lviv Stauropegion Paschal Pentecostarion (*Трїѡ́дь Цвѣтна́ѧ*), published under the blessing and episcopal approbation of Metropolitan Andrey Sheptytsky and the Stauropegion Institute in Lviv.
