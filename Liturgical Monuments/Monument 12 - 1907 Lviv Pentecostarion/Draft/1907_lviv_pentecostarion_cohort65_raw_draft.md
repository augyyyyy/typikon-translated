## ON MONDAY EVENING

*At "Lord, I have cried," Stichera of the Feast on 6, Idiomela:*[^2890]

*Tone 6:*

*Sticheron 1:*
Come, all ye heavenly Angels, and all ye Powers of God, praise with us God Who became man: Who for the sake of the mercy of the human race, by His unsearchable divine wisdom, shut Himself up in the Mysteries of bread, and left Himself as food for men: Whom as God let us celebrate today and hymn, crying: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O God, glory to Thy loving-kindness!

*Sticheron 2:*
Come, all ye ends of the earth, let us bless the Most Holy Body of God, broken for us and for our sins, and given unto death upon the Cross: and after the Resurrection ascended into heaven, seated in glory at the right hand of God the Father, and in these Mysteries left upon earth the same for the faithful: before Whom we, all earthborn, falling down upon the face of the earth, praise with compunction.

*Sticheron 3:*
Come, ye assemblies of feast-lovers, come, let us crown the Church of Christ, festive and adorned today: wherein Christ the supernatural Bread is beheld, and given unto men for food; Whom let all of us, the faithful, worship with fear and gladness, and with the choirs of the heavenly Powers let us praise Him, crying: Holy, Holy, Holy art Thou, O Lamb of God, Who takest away the sins of the world!

*Glory... Both now and for ever and unto ages of ages. Amen.*
*Tone 6. Doxasticon:*
Come, all ye works of the Lord, hymn the Lord with us, Who is worshipped here: bless Him, sun, moon, and stars: fire, sea, air, clouds, and all creation: for God loved the world and the human race exceedingly and unto the end, for whose sake He emptied Himself, taking the form of a servant; in these Mysteries He concealed not only His Godhead, but also His humanity: which we behold as bread, but believe as God, and bowing down unto Him with fear and trembling, we confess: that He is Christ, the Son of the living God, and the Savior of our souls.

## ON WEDNESDAY EVENING

*At "Lord, I have cried," Stichera of the Feast on 6, written at Vespers on Wednesday:*
*Likewise at the Aposticha, we sing everything of the Feast without change, except for the Paremias, and the Megalynarion at Matins. The service of the saint is sung at Compline, or whenever the superior wills. But if it be a great saint, it is sung together with the Feast: for him with Paremias, Megalynarion, and Polyeleos, and the rest. But if it be not a great saint, the whole service is sung without change, just as on the very day of the Feast.*

*On Thursday at Matins, at "God is the Lord," Troparion of the Feast, thrice. Sessional Hymns after the First and Second Kathismata. Then the Canon, Exapostilarion, and at the Praises of the Feast: Great Doxology. Troparion: First Hour, and the Dismissal.*
*At the Liturgy we sing everything without change, just as on the very day of the Feast.*

*Be it known, that the Council of Lviv, held in the year 1720, commands to sing the Office of the Most Holy Eucharist solemnly on the Second Sunday after the Descent of the Holy Spirit, according to the general typikon of the Great Feasts of the Lord.*[^2900]

## ORDER OF THE FEAST OF THE COMPASSION OF THE MOST HOLY THEOTOKOS[^2901]

*Celebrated on the Friday of the Second Week after the Sunday of All Saints.*

*On Thursday evening at "Lord, I have cried," Stichera of the Theotokos on 6.*

*Tone 2, Podoben: "When from the Tree" (Є҆гда̀ ѿ дре́ва):*

*Sticheron 1:*
Unto all men a Protectress, unto all a great Refuge, unto all Christians a Defender did He reveal thee, Who from thee clothed Himself in our mortal clay in His tender compassion, and underwent Crucifixion and death for our sake: Him therefore pray unceasingly, that He send down upon all the cleansing of offenses, O Bride of God.

*Sticheron 2:*
Of old the Virgin and Ewe-lamb, beholding Him that came forth from her womb hanging upon the Tree as one condemned, together with two condemned men, cried aloud, saying: O my Son and my God, strange sight, strange mystery! Yet the abyss of Thy wisdom none can search out: I hymn Thy longsuffering!

*Sticheron 3:*
Where is the majesty of Thy form? Where hath the beauty of Thy countenance hidden itself, O Son? How is it that Thou, Who with ineffable beauty didst fashion all things, Who art fairer than all, for the sake of all men hangest upon the Tree inglorious and dishonored, having neither form nor beauty, O Child? in wonder did the Virgin cry, groaning and weeping.

*Sticheron 4:*
Beholding Thee, O Jesus, lifted up upon the wood of the Cross, the Unwedded Maiden wept and said: O sweet Child, for what cause hast Thou left me alone, who bore Thee? O Light unapproachable of the unoriginate Father,[^2903] hasten and be glorified, that they may obtain divine glory who glorify Thy divine Passions!

=== LEAF p646 ===
*Tone 2, Podoben: "When from the Tree" (Є҆гда̀ ѿ дре́ва):*

*Sticheron 5:*
When upon the Tree thou didst behold the Life dying, O Virgin, and painfully pierced with the spear, weeping thou didst cry aloud: O my Son and my God, what hath the ungrateful assembly repaid Thee? Woe is me, I cannot bear the pain, I am consumed in my womb, seeing Thee suffering these things, O Master!

*Sticheron 6:*
When she beheld Him taken down from the Tree, the Maiden that knew not wedlock, laid stretched out upon the earth breathless as a man: embracing Him in her bosom, and kissing His lips and eyes, cried aloud unto Him: Wonderfully, how do I now behold Thee, Who vivifiest all things, speechless and motionless? Truly a great marvel!

*Glory... Both now and for ever and unto ages of ages. Amen.*
*Tone 2. Theotokion:*
Having endured many sorrows at the Crucifixion of thy Son and thy God, O Most Pure One, groaning and weeping thou didst cry aloud: Woe is me, O sweet Child! How dost Thou suffer unjustly, willing to deliver the earthborn sons of Adam? Therefore, O most pure Virgin, we pray thee in faith: make Him merciful unto us!

*Entrance, and the Prokeimenon of the day. Then the Paremias.*[^2904]

*Reading from the Prophecy of Jeremiah:*[^2905]
*Lesson 1 (Lamentations 1:1-2, 3-4, 9, 11-13, 16-17, 20):*
In those days, Jeremiah wept over Jerusalem, and said: How doth the city sit solitary, that was full of people! How is she become as a widow! She weeps sore in the night, and her tears are on her cheeks: among all her lovers she hath none to comfort her: all her friends have dealt treacherously with her, they are become her enemies. She dwells among the nations, she finds no rest. And from the daughter of Sion all her beauty is departed. Behold, O Lord, my affliction: for the enemy hath magnified himself. All ye that pass by, turn back and see if there be any sorrow like unto my sorrow, which is done unto me, wherewith the Lord hath afflicted me in the day of His fierce anger. He hath called an assembly against me; from above hath He sent fire into my bones, and hath chastened me. My strength is made weak within me; for the Lord hath delivered affliction into my hands. For these things I weep; mine eyes run down with water, because the comforter that should relieve my soul is far from me: my children are desolate, because the enemy prevailed. Sion spreads forth her hands, and there is none to comfort her: the Lord hath commanded concerning Jacob, that his adversaries should be round about him. Behold, O Lord, for I am in distress: my bowels are troubled within me; mine heart is turned within me; for I am filled with bitterness.

*Reading from the Prophecy of Jeremiah:*[^2906]
*Lesson 2 (Lamentations 2:13, 15-20; 3:7, 12-13, 15, 19, 21):*
In those days, Jeremiah wept over Jerusalem, and said: What thing shall I take to witness for thee, or what shall I liken to thee, O daughter of Jerusalem? Who shall save thee, or who shall comfort thee, O virgin daughter of Sion? All that pass by clap their hands at thee; they hiss and wag their head at the daughter of Jerusalem, saying: Is this the city that men call the perfection of beauty, the joy of the whole earth? The Lord hath done that which He had devised; He hath fulfilled His word that He had commanded in the days of old: give thyself no rest; let not the apple of thine eye cease. Arise, meditate in the night; in the beginning of thy watches pour out thine heart like water before the face of the Lord: lift up thy hands toward Him for the life of thy young children, that faint for hunger. Behold, O Lord, and consider whom Thou hast thus afflicted. He hath hedged me about, and encompassed my head, and I have labored. He hath bent His bow, and set me as a mark for the arrow. He hath caused the arrows of His quiver to enter into my reins. He hath filled me with bitterness, He hath made me drunken with wormwood. Remembering my misery, my bitterness and the gall. This I recall to my heart, therefore will I hope in the Lord.

*Lesson 2 (continued, Lamentations 3:22-26):*

Thou drewest near in the day that I called upon Thee: Thou saidst, Fear not. O Lord, Thou hast pleaded the causes of my soul; Thou hast redeemed my life. Thou, O Lord, remainest for ever; Thy throne from generation to generation. Turn Thou us unto Thee, O Lord, and we shall be turned; renew our days as of old.
