# Lviv Stauropegion Paschal Pentecostarion (Lviv, 1907) <a id="lviv-stauropegion-paschal-pentecostarion-lviv-1907"></a>

*[Ornamental decorative headpiece frame with floral pattern across the top of the leaf]*

*NOTICE TO THE READERS.*[^1]

[Unto the glory of the Holy, Consubstantial, Life-Creating, and Undivided Trinity, of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit,] by the good pleasure, assistance, and completion of God glorified in the Holy Trinity, the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit, this Book, the Flowery Triodion, was completed in the year 1907.[^2]

We pray you therefore in Christ, brethren who read this Book, if anything should happen to be stumbled or unknown, do you, who have been accounted worthy of so many gifts from the Holy Spirit, make correction.[^3]

Moreover, bless those who labor in this soul-saving work and curse not, that you yourselves may be accounted worthy of forgiveness and blessing from God Almighty, by Whose assistance this Book also was begun and completed; to Whom be honor, glory, and dominion, unto endless ages of ages. Amen.

[^2]: Church Slavonic year ,ац҃з corresponds numerically to 1907 (,а = 1000, ц = 900, з = 7).
[^3]: An epigraphic formula standard in Ruthenian and Slavic Cyrillic printings since the 16th century (cf. the editions of Ivan Fedorov and the Lviv Brotherhood), entreating clerical readers and chanters to correct typesetting lapses with Christian meekness rather than condemnation.
[^4]: The master title page on leaf p2 demonstrates the historical bilingual title: Church Slavonic *Triod Tsvitnaya* (*ТРІѠ́ДЬ ЦВѢ́ТНАЯ*) glossed by its canonical Greek origin *Pentekostarion* (*ПЕНТИКОСТА́РЇОНЪ*), defined in the vernacular as *Piatdesiatnytsia* (*ПЯТДЕСЯ́ТНИЦЯ*, the Fiftieth Day Book / Pentecostarion).
[^5]: The Church of the Dormition of the Most Holy Mother of God (the "Wallachian" or Dormition Church) in Lviv served from the 16th through the 20th century as the primary temple, institutional heart, and publishing seat of the Stauropegion Brotherhood and Institute.
[^6]: Physical leaf p3 features a full-page architectural plate depicting the Dormition Church complex in Lviv, showcasing Paolo Romanus's Renaissance facade (1591–1629), the Chapel of the Three Hierarchs (1578–1591), and Petro Krasovsky's 66-meter Korniakt Bell Tower (1572–1578).
[^7]: Pope Saint Pius X (Giuseppe Melchiorre Sarto), Supreme Pontiff of the Catholic Church from August 4, 1903, until his death on August 20, 1914. Emperor Franz Joseph I of Austria and Apostolic King of Hungary reigned over the Austro-Hungarian Empire from 1848 to 1916.

[^9]: Metropolitan Sheptytsky held statutory dignities within the Cisleithanian Austrian administration, including Privy Councillor (*Geheimer Rat*), Member of the Austrian Imperial House of Lords (*Herrenhaus*), and Deputy Marshal (*Vizelandmarschall*) of the Galician Provincial Diet (*Sejm Krajowy*).
[^10]: Bishop Konstantyn Chekhovych (1847–1915), Ukrainian Greek-Catholic Bishop of the Eparchy of Peremyshl, Sambir, and Sanok from 1896 until his arrest and martyrdom during the Russian occupation of Galicia in 1915.

[^13]: Codicological Note on the Table of Contents: This 1907 Lviv Stauropegion edition of the Flowery Triodion follows the traditional Galician/Ruthenian recension by incorporating the full liturgical services of the eve of Lazarus Saturday (Friday of Palm Week), Lazarus Saturday, Palm Sunday, and Great and Holy Passion Week (pages 1–197), followed on page 198 by the Pentecostarion proper (*Начало Пентикостаріа сирѣчь Пятдесятницы*).
[^14]: The Feast of the Most Holy Mystery of the Eucharist (Corpus Christi, instituted for the Ruthenian Church at the Synod of Zamość in 1720) and the Feast of the Compassion of the Most Holy Theotokos were standard proper feasts included in Galician editions of the Pentecostarion following All Saints Sunday.
[^15]: Iconographic note on leaf p8: The engraving of the Dormition depicts the ancient tradition of the Archangel Michael intervening when the Jewish priest Jephonias (or Athonios) insolently reached out to topple the bier of the Mother of God during her burial procession in Gethsemane; upon repenting and confessing Christ, his severed hands were miraculously restored.
