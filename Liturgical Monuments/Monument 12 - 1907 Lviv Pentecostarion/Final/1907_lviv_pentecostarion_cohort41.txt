...the five books of Moses did they accept, rejecting the Prophets and the rest of the Scripture.[^833] These were also called Samaritans, as being from Mount Somor; and they were hated by the Hebrews returning from captivity, because they Judaized only in part and would not eat with them, abominating them unyieldingly; therefore also they often called Christ a Samaritan, as one who despised and broke certain things of the Law even as they did.[^834]

The Lord therefore comes into Sychar, and being weary from the journey, He sat down about the sixth hour of the day. And a certain woman from the city came to draw water, the disciples having gone away to buy food; Jesus therefore asks water. But she proposes not to touch Him, having recognized Him both from His speech and from His apparel. But He leads her upward, bringing spiritual water into the midst, which He shows to be inexhaustible and cleansing; for the Spirit is ever likened unto water and fire. But the woman, persisting importunately, that He had no such water, so as not to carry a vessel for drawing, and that the well belonged to Jacob, she replies; furthermore she also raises word concerning the forefather Jacob, that he himself dug the well, and he and his sheep drank of it, setting forth the rich abundance of the springs, and that the well was otherwise well-suited.[^835]

Christ therefore does not make Himself greater than Jacob, lest He terrify the woman, but speaks concerning the water, setting forth the abundance thereof prefiguratively: for he who drinks of that water shall by no means thirst.[^836] And the woman asks for the water. But He tells her to summon her husband, as she was in need of understanding more solid words. But she denies that she has a husband. And He Who knows all things says: Thou hast well said, for five husbands hast thou had, as the Law commands, and he whom thou now hast as the sixth, because thou cohabitest with him unlawfully, is not thy husband.[^837]

[Some indeed have thought the five husbands to be the five books of Moses, which the Samaritans accepted, and the sixth to be those words of Christ, which were not yet spoken, for grace was not yet poured out. Others say they were the five laws given by God: that in paradise, and that after the expulsion; that in the time of Noah; that in the time of Abraham; that in the time of Moses; while the sixth is the Gospel, which she did not yet possess. There are also those who say they are the five senses.][^838]

The woman answers Him, calling Him a Prophet. Then she also questions Him concerning the mountain, where it is proper to worship: in Somor or in Jerusalem? For the Samaritans did not deem that God is completely everywhere, but that God abides only there where they also worshiped, on Mount Gerizim, because the blessing was given there from God; or because there Israel first built an altar unto God; even as the Jews said in turn: In Jerusalem it is proper to worship the one God; therefore also those from all quarters gathered there for the feast.[^839]

And Christ answers: Salvation for the world, He says, is of the Jews; yet He said: God is immaterial, and those deemed worthy to worship Him are no longer to worship in sacrifices, but in spirit and in truth; or they shall know God thus: not as solitary, but in the Holy Spirit and the Son, for He is the Truth.[^840]

The woman says again: We hear from the Scriptures that Messiah comes, Who is called Christ. And Jesus says: I am He; having understood the noble disposition of the woman.[^841]

And the Samaritans knew also concerning the Messiah from the books of Moses, and especially from this: A Prophet shall the Lord God raise up unto you; and from many other passages.[^842]

And as the conversation was ending, the disciples also come, and they marvel exceedingly at His condescension, how He was speaking with a woman. Nevertheless they entreat Him to eat, both because He had toiled and because of the heat of the season. But He speaks unto them of eternal food, that is the salvation of mankind, and that they ought to reap the labors of the Prophets.[^843]

And when the woman reached the city and related what had befallen her, all were stirred up and came unto Christ, having believed; for the woman would not have put herself to shame unless she had understood something great. And having entreatingly besought Him to abide with them, they persuade Him to stay for two days. And having tarried, He wrought very many miracles, which indeed by reason of their multitude were not written in the Gospel.[^844]

And this is that Samaritan woman, who was afterward named Photina by Christ, who also was crowned with the wreath of martyrdom under Nero Caesar, together with her seven sons, after much dragging about, scraping, cutting off of breasts, crushing of hands, inserting of sharp reeds under fingernails, pouring of molten lead, and countless other torments, and at the end was tied to two trees and torn asunder.[^845]

Be it known also that the well-curb of that well, that is, its mouth, Emperor Justinian honorably brought thence, and placed it in the Great Church of the Word of God, Hagia Sophia; and the stone also whereon Christ sat as He conversed with the Samaritan woman; and both abide unto this day before the narthex from the east, on the left side to those entering the temple, healing every disease, and especially those who happen to suffer from burning fevers and are tossed with the ague, and they cleanse and drive away enchantments that are wrought.[^846]

By the intercessions of Thy martyr Photina, O Christ God, have mercy on us. Amen.

Thou hast come, O King, O great Wisdom, whereby the Father in wisdom created the world with the Holy Spirit.[^906]

*Glory... Both now and for ever and unto ages of ages. Amen. Tone 8:*  
Having cleansed the treasuries of our thoughts, and having brightened the spiritual lamps, let us also behold the Life, Christ, Who came into the sanctuary through an exceeding abundance of goodness, that He might conquer the enemy and save our race by the Passion of the Cross and the Resurrection; unto Whom let us cry aloud: O Incomprehensible Lord, glory to Thee![^907]

*On Monday of the Samaritan Woman, the Three-Ode Canon,*  
*whose acrostic is: Doxa to Theo hemon [Δόξα τῷ Θεῷ ἡμῶν], that is: Glory to our God.*[^908]

*Tone 4. Ode 1. Heirmos:*  
O Thou Who wast born of the Virgin, drown, I pray Thee, the three parts of my soul in the depth of dispassion, even as the mighty captains; that unto Thee, as upon a timbrel, in the mortification of the body, I may chant a song of victory![^909]

*Troparion [Acrostic letter D]:*  
Having bound the destroyer's hostile power with bonds, which Thou didst endure for our sake, O Lover of mankind: by Thy Cross hast Thou made peace, O Lord, among those who were scattered, and destroyed the enmity of the middle wall; therefore we hymn Thee![^910]

*Troparion [Acrostic letter O]:*  
The Lord, even as He foretold before the Passion, arose with power, having as God plundered the realms of hades, and raised up all that slept there from the ages: unto Him that magnify Him![^911]

*Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit.*  
*Troparion [Acrostic letter X]:*  
Marveling at the vision and the appearance, the choir of the Myrrh-bearing women learned from the divine minister: Behold, Christ is risen! Go, proclaim unto the disciples who mourn and weep![^912]

*Both now and for ever and unto ages of ages. Amen. Theotokion [Acrostic letter A]:*  
Thou didst shine forth, O Pure One, as a bridegroom marvelously fair from the dead, He Who was incarnate from thy womb through goodness beyond understanding; therefore, having beheld Him with the Apostles, thou didst glorify His third-day Resurrection![^913]
