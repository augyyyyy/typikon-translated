On this same day we celebrate the resurrection of the holy and righteous friend of Christ, Lazarus of four days. This man was by descent a Hebrew, of the sect of the Pharisees, and the son, as is found somewhere, of the Pharisee Simon, originating from the village of Bethany. When Our Lord Jesus Christ was walking upon earth accomplishing the economy for the salvation of our race, He thus united Himself in friendship with them. For because Christ often conversed with Simon, as also he himself had promised to believe in the resurrection of the dead, and often came unto his house, Lazarus was joined in friendship with Him: not he alone, but also his two sisters, Martha and Mary.

When the saving Passion was already drawing nigh, wherein it was fitting for the mystery of the resurrection to be more firmly confirmed, Jesus was abiding beyond the Jordan, having previously raised the daughter of Jairus and the son of the widow from the dead. And His friend Lazarus, being seized with a grievous disease, died. Jesus therefore, not being there, said unto the disciples: Our friend Lazarus hath fallen asleep; and after a little, standing before them again, He said: Lazarus is dead. He comes therefore unto Bethany, having left the Jordan, being summoned by his sisters. Now Bethany was distant from Jerusalem about fifteen stadia. And the sisters of Lazarus met Him, saying: Lord, if Thou hadst been here, our brother had not died; but even now, if Thou desirest, raising him, Thou art able. Jesus asked the people: Where have ye laid him? And straightway all went forward unto the tomb. And when they removed the stone, Martha said: Lord, by this time he stinks, for he hath been four days in the tomb. Jesus prayed, and shed tears over him that was lying there, and cried with a loud voice: Lazarus, come forth! And straightway the dead man came forth; and being loosed, he departed unto his house.

Beholding this strange wonder, the Hebrew people were moved with envy against Christ, and they were incensed against Him; but Jesus departed again. And the chief priests took counsel also to put Lazarus to death, because many beholding him joined themselves unto Christ. But he, knowing their thoughts, fled unto the island of Cyprus, and abiding there, was afterward manifested by the Apostles as Bishop of the city of Kition, and having lived well and in a manner pleasing to God, thirty years of time having passed after his resurrection, he fell asleep again, and is buried there, having wrought many wonders. It is said that he ate nothing without sweetening. And the Most Pure Mother of God, having fashioned his omophorion with her own hands, gave it unto him.

His precious and holy relics the most pious Emperor Leo the Wise, having seen in a divine vision, transferred them from thence unto Constantinople, unto the church built in the name of the same saint, having honorably and at great cost built it, on the right side of the church as one goes toward the facing holy altar table: where even now his relics lie, working ineffable fragrant grace.

This resurrection was appointed by our holy and God-bearing Fathers to be celebrated on this present day, because after the fasting of the forty days for cleansing, desiring to behold the Holy Passions of Our Lord Jesus Christ, since this wonder was found to be the beginning and cause especially of the Jewish madness against Christ, for this reason they placed this supernatural wonder here. For the Evangelist John alone hath recorded this, as the other Evangelists omitted it: since Lazarus was yet living and visible. It is said, indeed...

...that also for this very cause the rest of the Gospel was written: and that nothing concerning the unoriginate Nativity of Christ did the other Evangelists mention. For this was the thing sought to be confirmed: that Christ was the Son of God and God, and that He arose, and that there shall be a resurrection of the dead: which by Lazarus is above all confirmed. Lazarus said nothing of what was in Hades: either because it was not permitted him to see what was there, or having seen, he was commanded to be silent concerning these things. From that time forth, every man who hath died is now called Lazarus, and the funeral shroud is likewise named "Lazaritsa", hinting at the word that we should come to the remembrance of the first Lazarus. For if he by the word of Christ arose, and lived again, so also this man, though he die, having arisen at the last trumpet, shall live for ever. *Sticheron 3:*
Come, let us also today, the entire new Israel, the Church called from the gentiles, cry aloud with the Prophet Zechariah: Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Sion; proclaim aloud, O daughter of Jerusalem: for behold, thy King comes unto thee, meek and bringing salvation, sitting upon a colt, the foal of an ass; celebrate as the children, and holding branches in thy hands, praise Him: Hosanna in the highest! Blessed is He that comes, the King of Israel!

*Sticheron 4:*
Prefiguring unto us Thy precious Resurrection, O Good One, by Thy command Thou didst raise from the tomb the breathless dead man, Thy friend Lazarus, who was four days dead and stinking; therefore also, mounting upon a foal figuratively as though borne upon a chariot, Thou tamest the gentiles, O Savior; for this cause also beloved Israel offers praise out of the mouth of sucklings and innocent infants, beholding Thee, O Christ, entering into the Holy City six days before Pascha.

*Sticheron 5:*
Six days before Pascha, Jesus came unto Bethany, and His disciples came unto Him, saying unto Him: Lord, where wilt Thou that we prepare for Thee to eat the Passover? And He sent them, saying: Go ye into the village opposite, and ye shall find a man carrying an earthen pitcher of water; follow him, and say unto the master of the house: The Teacher saith: With thee will I keep the Passover with My disciples.
