## ON HOLY AND GREAT FRIDAY, AT VESPERS[^264]

*At the tenth hour of the day the klepalo is struck.*[^265]

*And the priest having given the blessing, the Lamp-lighting [Psalms] are begun; at "Lord, I have cried," we set 6 verses:*

*And we sing idiomel stichera.*

*Tone 1:*

All creation was altered by fear, beholding Thee hanging upon the Cross, O Christ: the sun was darkened, and the foundations of the earth were shaken; all things suffered with the Creator of all. O Thou Who didst endure willingly for our sake, Lord, glory to Thee! *(Twice.)*

*Tone 2:*

Ye wicked and lawless people, why do ye meditate on vain things? Why did ye condemn to death the Life of all? A great wonder! That the Creator of the world is delivered into the hands of the lawless, and the Lover of mankind is lifted up upon the Tree, that He may free the prisoners in hades, who cry: O long-suffering Lord, glory to Thee!

Today the blameless Virgin, seeing Thee lifted up upon the Cross, O Word, weeping with a mother's bowels, was bitterly wounded in her heart; and groaning sorrowfully from the depths of her soul, tearing her face and hair, and beating her breast, she cried out piteously: Woe is me, divine Child! Woe is me, Light of the world! Why hast Thou set from mine eyes, O Lamb of God? Therefore the hosts of the bodiless were held with trembling, saying: O incomprehensible Lord, glory to Thee!

Beholding Thee hanging upon the Tree, O Christ, the Creator and God of all, she who bore Thee without seed cried bitterly: My Son, whither has the beauty of Thy countenance departed? I cannot bear to behold Thee...

=== LEAF p142 ===

...unjustly crucified: make haste therefore to arise, that I also may see Thy three-day Resurrection from the dead!

*Tone 6:*

Today the Master of creation stands before Pilate, and to the Cross is delivered the Creator of all: as a sheep led of His own will, He is nailed with nails, and in His side is pierced, and with a sponge is given drink by He Who rained down manna; upon the cheek is buffeted the Redeemer of the world, and by His own servants is mocked the Creator of all. O Master's love for mankind! For them that crucified Him He prayed to His Father, saying: Father, forgive them this sin; for the lawless know not what injustice they do.

*Glory, in the same tone [Tone 6]:*

O how the lawless assembly condemned to death the King of creation! Not being ashamed of the benefits which He, putting them in remembrance, had confirmed before, saying unto them: My people, what have I done unto you? Have I not filled Judea with wonders? Have I not raised the dead with a single word? Have I not healed every disease and sickness? What then do ye render unto Me? Why do ye not remember Me? In return for healings, ye have laid wounds upon Me; instead of life, ye put Me to death; hanging upon the Tree as an evildoer the Benefactor, as a transgressor the Lawgiver, as a condemned man the King of all! O long-suffering Lord, glory to Thee!

*Both now, in the same tone [Tone 6]:*

A fearsome and most glorious mystery is seen accomplished today: He Who is untouchable is held; He Who loosed Adam from the curse is bound; He Who tris the hearts and reins is unjustly tried; He Who shut up the abyss is shut up in prison; before Pilate stands He before Whom heavenly powers stand with trembling; with the hand of the creature the Creator is buffeted; to the Tree is condemned He Who judges the living and the dead; in the tomb is enclosed the Destroyer of hades! O Thou Who didst endure all things mercifully, and hast saved all from the curse, O innocent Lord, glory to Thee!

*Entrance with the Gospel.*

*O gladsome Light:*

*Prokeimenon, Tone 6:*

They divided my garments among them, and upon my vesture they cast lots.[^266]

*Verse: O God, my God, attend to me: why hast Thou forsaken me?*

*The Reading from Exodus (Chapter 33):*[^267]

The Lord spoke unto Moses face to face, as if one should speak unto his friend; and he returned into the camp; but his minister Joshua, the son of Nun, a young man, departed not out of the tabernacle. And Moses said unto the Lord: Behold, Thou sayest unto me: Bring up this people; yet Thou hast not shown me whom Thou wilt send with me. But Thou hast said unto me: I know thee above all, and thou hast found grace with Me. If therefore I have found grace in Thy sight, manifest Thyself unto me, that I may clearly see Thee, that I may find grace in Thy sight, and that I may know that this great nation is Thy people. And the Lord said unto him: I Myself will go before thee, and I will give thee rest. And Moses said unto Him: If Thou Thyself go not...

=== LEAF p143 ===

...with us, lead me not up hence. And how shall it be known truly that I and Thy people have found grace with Thee, unless Thou go with us? And so shall I and Thy people be glorified beyond all the nations that are upon the earth. And the Lord said unto Moses: I will do this word also that thou hast spoken; for thou hast found grace before Me, and I know thee above all. And Moses said: Show me Thy glory. And the Lord said unto Moses: I will go before thee in My glory, and I will call upon the name of the Lord before thee; and I will have mercy on whom I will have mercy, and I will have compassion on whom I will have compassion. And He said: Thou canst not see My face; for man shall not see My face, and live. And the Lord said: Behold, there is a place by Me, and thou shalt stand upon the rock; and when My glory passes by, I will put thee in a cleft of the rock, and will cover thee with My hand over thee, until I have passed by. And I will take away My hand, and then shalt thou see My back; but My face shall not be revealed unto thee.

*Prokeimenon, Tone 4:*

Judge, O Lord, them that wrong me; fight against them that fight against me.[^268]

*Verse: Take hold of arms and shield, and arise to my help.*

*The Reading from Job (Chapter 42):*[^269]

The Lord blessed the latter days of Job more than the former; and he had fourteen thousand sheep, six thousand camels, a thousand yoke of oxen, and a thousand she-asses of the pasture. And there were born unto him seven sons and three daughters. And he called the name of the first, Day; and the second, Cassia; and the third, Amalthea's Horn. And there were not found women so fair as the daughters of Job in all that is under heaven; and their father gave them an inheritance among their brethren. And Job lived after his affliction a hundred and seventy years; and all the years he lived were two hundred and forty-eight. And Job saw his sons, and his sons' sons, unto the fourth generation. And Job died, an old man, and full of days. And it is written that he shall rise again with those whom the Lord raises up: thus is it interpreted from the Syriac book. He dwelt in the land of Ausitis, on the borders of Idumaea and Arabia; and his name before was Jobab. And taking an Arabian wife, he begat a son whose name was Enon. And he was the son of his father Zaretha, a son of the sons of Esau, and of his mother Bosorra; so that he was the fifth from Abraham.

*The Reading from the Prophecy of Isaiah (Chapter 52):*[^270]

*Thus saith the Lord: Behold, My Servant shall understand... The End: ...Than she that hath a husband.*

*(see fol. 124).*[^271]

*And immediately:*

*Prokeimenon of the Apostle, Tone 6:*

They laid me in the lowest pit, in dark places and in the shadow of death.[^272]

*Verse: O Lord God of my salvation, I have cried day and night before Thee.*

*The Reading from the Epistle of the Holy Apostle Paul to the Corinthians (Selection 125):*[^273]

Brethren: The word of the cross is foolishness unto them that perish, but unto us who are being saved it is the power of God. For it is written: I will destroy the wisdom of the wise, and will reject the understanding of the prudent. Where is the wise? Where is the scribe? Where is the disputer of this age? Hath not God made foolish the wisdom of this world? For after that in the wisdom of God the world...

=== LEAF p144 ===

...by wisdom knew not God, it pleased God by the foolishness of preaching to save them that believe. For the Jews require signs, and the Greeks seek after wisdom; but we preach Christ crucified, unto the Jews a stumbling block, and unto the Greeks foolishness; but unto them that are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God. Because the foolishness of God is wiser than men, and the weakness of God is stronger than men. For see your calling, brethren, that not many wise men after the flesh, not many mighty, not many noble, are called. But God hath chosen the foolish things of the world to put to shame the wise; and God hath chosen the weak things of the world to put to shame the mighty; and base things of the world, and things that are despised, hath God chosen, and things that are not, to bring to naught things that are; that no flesh should boast before God. But of Him are ye in Christ Jesus, Who of God was made unto us wisdom, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption: that, according as it is written: He that gloris, let him glory in the Lord. And I, brethren, when I came to you, came not with excellency of speech or of wisdom, declaring unto you the testimony of God. For I determined not to know anything among you, save Jesus Christ, and Him crucified.

*Alleluia, Tone 1:*

*Save me, O God, for the waters are come in even unto my soul.*[^274]

*Verse: My soul looked for reproach and affliction.*

*Verse: Let their eyes be darkened, that they see not.*

*The Holy Gospel according to Matthew (Selection 110):*[^275]

*At that time, all the chief priests took counsel... The End: ...opposite the tomb.*

*Then, the customary Litany: Let us all say with our whole soul... And afterwards: Vouchsafe, O Lord:*

*Let us complete our evening prayer... and the rest.*

*And after the exclamation we sing at the Aposticha stichera, 4 prosomoia.*

*Tone 2, Podoben: "When from the Tree" (Є҆гда̀ ѿ дре́ва):*[^276]

When from the Tree the Arimathean took Thee down dead, the Life of all, he wrapped Thee, O Christ, in fine linen with myrrh, and was moved by love to kiss with heart and lips Thy incorrupt Body; yet held by fear, he cried unto Thee rejoicing: Glory to Thy condescension, O Lover of mankind!

*Verse: The Lord is King, He is clothed with majesty.*

When in a new tomb Thou wast laid for all, O Redeemer of all, hades, laughing mockingly, seeing Thee was terrified: the bars were crushed, the gates were broken, the tombs were opened, the dead arose! Then Adam thankfully and rejoicing cried unto Thee: Glory to Thy condescension, O Lover of mankind!

*Verse: For He hath established the world, which shall not be moved.*

When in the tomb according to the flesh Thou didst will to be enclosed, O Thou Who in the nature of Godhead remainest uncircumscribed and boundless, Thou didst shut up the treasuries of death, and didst empty all the kingdoms of hades, O Christ: then also didst Thou vouchsafe this Sabbath of divine blessing and glory, and of Thy radiance!

=== LEAF p145 ===

*Verse: Holiness becomes Thy house, O Lord, unto length of days.*

When the Powers beheld Thee, O Christ, slandered by the lawless as a deceiver, they were astounded at Thine ineffable long-suffering, and at the stone of the tomb sealed by the hands of those who had pierced Thy spotless side; yet rejoicing in our salvation, they cried unto Thee: Glory to Thy condescension, O Lover of mankind!

*Glory, both now, Tone 5:*

Thee Who clothest Thyself with light as with a garment, Joseph with Nicodemus took down from the Tree; and beholding Thee dead, naked, and unburied, he took up a compassionate lamentation, and weeping said: Woe is me, sweetest Jesus! Whom a little while ago the sun, seeing hanging upon the Cross, wrapped itself in darkness, and the earth quaked with fear, and the veil of the temple was rent in twain; but lo, now I behold Thee, for my sake willingly undergoing death! How shall I bury Thee, my God? Or with what winding-sheet shall I wrap Thee? With what hands shall I touch Thine incorrupt Body? Or what songs shall I sing for Thy departure, O Compassionate One? I magnify Thy Passions, I hymn also Thy Burial with the Resurrection, crying: Lord, glory to Thee!

*Then: Now lettest Thou Thy servant depart. Trisagion.*

*After Our Father:*

*Troparion, Tone 2:*

The noble Joseph, taking down Thy most pure Body from the Tree, wrapped it in clean linen and spices, and laid it in a new tomb.

*Another Troparion:*

Standing by the tomb, the Angel cried unto the myrrh-bearing women: Myrrh is fitting for the dead, but Christ hath shown Himself a stranger to corruption.

*Wisdom, and*

*The Dismissal:*

May He Who for the sake of us men and for our salvation, endured in the flesh the dread Passions, and the life-creating Cross, and the voluntary Burial, for the sake of our salvation, Christ our true God, through the prayers of His most pure Mother, and of all the Saints, have mercy on us and save us, as He is good and the Lover of mankind.

*Small Compline is sung in the cells.*

## ON HOLY AND GREAT FRIDAY, AT COMPLINE

*Which we sing in the cells, whereon we sing:*

*The Canon on the Crucifixion of the Lord, and on the Lament of the Most Holy Theotokos.*[^277]

*The Heirmoi twice, troparia to 6; and at the end the Heirmos by both choirs together.*

*The work of Symeon the Metaphrast.*[^278]

*Tone 6. Ode 1. Heirmos:*

Traversing the deep on foot as if on dry land, Israel, seeing their pursuer Pharaoh drowned, cried out: Let us sing a song of victory unto God!

*Refrain: Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee.*

*Troparion:*

When the pure Virgin saw her Son and Lord suspended upon the Cross, she was torn with grief and cried bitterly, groaning with the other women, and saying:

*Troparion:*

I see Thee now, my beloved Child and darling, hanging upon the Cross, and I am bitterly wounded in heart, said the pure Virgin; but give a word, O Good One, unto Thine handmaid.

*Glory:*

*Troparion:*

Willingly, my Son and Creator, dost Thou endure cruel death upon the Tree, the Virgin said, standing by the Cross with the beloved disciple.

*Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

Now am I deprived of my hope, joy, and gladness, of my Son and Lord: woe is me, I suffer sorrow of heart, said the pure Virgin, weeping.

*Ode 3. Heirmos:*

There is none holy like Thee, O Lord my God, Who hast exalted the horn of Thy faithful, O Good One, and established us upon the rock of Thy confession.

*Troparion:*

For fear of the Jews Peter hid himself, and all the faithful fled, forsaking Christ, the Virgin said, lamenting.

*Troparion:*

At Thy dread and strange Nativity, my Son, I was magnified above all mothers; but woe is me, now seeing Thee upon the Tree, I burn within my bowels.

=== LEAF p147 ===

*Glory:*

*Troparion:*

I see my bowels taken down from the Tree into the hands wherewith I held Thee as an Infant, said the pure Virgin; but no one, woe is me, hath granted this.

*Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

Lo, my sweet Light, my Hope and good Life, my God is extinguished upon the Cross, and I burn within my bowels, the Virgin said, groaning.

*Ode 4. Heirmos:*

Christ is my power, God and Lord, the venerable Church sings in godly manner, crying from a pure mind, keeping festival in the Lord.

*Troparion:*

O unwaning Sun, eternal God, and Creator of all creation, O Lord, how dost Thou endure Passion upon the Cross? the pure Virgin said, weeping.

*Troparion:*

Weeping, the unwedded Virgin said unto the noble counselor: Make haste, Joseph, go to Pilate, and beg to take down from the Tree thy Teacher.

*Glory:*

*Troparion:*

Seeing the All-Pure shedding bitter tears, Joseph was troubled, and weeping went to Pilate, crying with tears: Give me the Body of my God!

*Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

Seeing Thee wounded, and without glory, naked upon the Tree, my Child, I burn within my bowels, lamenting as a Mother, the Virgin uttered.

*Ode 5. Heirmos:*

With Thy divine light, O Good One, illumine with love the souls of them that rise early unto Thee, I pray, that they may know Thee, O Word of God, the true God, Who callest them from the darkness of sin.

*Troparion:*

Torn with grief, weeping, and wondering together with Nicodemus, Joseph took Thee down, and kissing the most pure Body, he wept and groaned, singing of Him as God.

*Troparion:*

Receiving Him with tears, the unwedded Mother laid Him upon her knees, praying unto Him with tears, and kissing Him, weeping bitterly and crying out:

*Glory:*

*Troparion:*

My only Hope and Life, Master, my Son and God, the Light of mine eyes had I, Thine handmaid; but now am I deprived of Thee, my sweet Child and darling!

*Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

Pains and sorrows and sighings have found me, woe is me! the pure Virgin said, weeping bitterly, seeing Thee, my beloved Child, naked and forsaken, and anointed with spices as a corpse.

=== LEAF p148 ===

*Ode 6. Heirmos:*

Beholding the sea of life surging with the tempest of temptations, fleeing to Thy calm haven, I cry unto Thee: Lead up my life from corruption, O Most Merciful One!

*Troparion:*

I see Thee dead, O Lover of mankind, Who didst quicken the dead and holdest all things, and I am sorely wounded in my bowels; I would fain die with Thee, the All-Pure said: for I cannot bear to behold Thee dead without breath.

*Troparion:*

I marvel beholding Thee, O most good God and all-wise Lord, without glory, without breath, and formless; and weeping I hold Thee, for I hoped not, woe is me, to see Thee, my Son and God!

*Glory:*

*Troparion:*

Wilt Thou not speak a word unto Thine handmaid, O Word of God? Wilt Thou not have compassion, O Master, on her who bore Thee? said the pure Virgin, lamenting and weeping, kissing the Body of her Lord.

*Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

I consider, O Master, that no more shall I hear Thy sweet voice, nor see the beauty of Thy face, as Thine handmaid did before; for Thou hast set from mine eyes, my Son!

*Kontakion, Tone 2:*[^279]

Come, let us all hymn Him Who was crucified for our sake; for Mary beheld Him upon the Tree, and said: Though Thou endurest crucifixion, Thou art my Son and my God.

*Ikos:*[^280]

Seeing her own Lamb drawn unto the slaughter, Mary followed with hair disheveled, together with the other women, crying thus: Whither goest Thou, Child? For what cause dost Thou accomplish this swift course? Is there again another marriage in Cana, and dost Thou now hasten thither, that Thou mayest make wine for them from water? Shall I go with Thee, Child, or shall I rather wait for Thee? Give me a word, O Word; pass me not by in silence, Thou Who didst keep me pure: for Thou art my Son and my God.

*Ode 7. Heirmos:*

An Angel made the furnace sprinkle dew for the righteous youths, but the command of God consuming the Chaldeans persuaded the tyrant to cry: Blessed art Thou, O God of our fathers!

*Troparion:*

Where, my Son and God, is the good tidings of old which Gabriel spoke unto me, calling Thee King, Son, and God of the Most High? But now I see Thee, my sweet Light, a naked and wounded corpse!

*Troparion:*

O Thou Who deliverest from afflictions, now receive me with Thee, my Son and God, that I also, O Master, may descend into hades with Thee; leave me not alone, for I can no longer bear to live without seeing Thee, my sweet Light!

=== LEAF p149 ===

*Glory:*

*Troparion:*

Weeping bitterly with the other myrrh-bearing women, and seeing Christ borne away, the Blameless Virgin said: Woe is me, what do I see! Whither goest Thou now, my Son, and leavest me alone?

*Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

Fainting and weeping, the Blameless Virgin said unto the myrrh-bearing women: Mourn with me, and weep bitterly with me; for behold, my sweet Light and your Teacher is delivered unto the tomb!

*Ode 8. Heirmos:*

Out of the flame didst Thou drop dew upon the righteous, and the sacrifice of the just didst Thou consume with water; for Thou doest all things, O Christ, merely by willing: Thee do we exalt unto all the ages!

*Troparion:*

Seeing the Virgin weeping, Joseph was completely rent with grief, and cried bitterly: How shall I, O my God, now bury Thee, Thy servant? With what winding-sheets shall I wrap Thy Body?

*Troparion:*

Beyond all understanding surpasses Thy strange vision, O Lord Who upholdest all creation: therefore Joseph, carrying Thee as a dead man in his hands, with Nicodemus bears Thee and buris Thee.

*Glory:*

*Troparion:*

A strange and most glorious mystery do I see, the Virgin cried unto her Son and Lord: how art Thou laid in a poor tomb, O Thou Who raisest up the dead in the tombs by Thy command?

*Both now:*

*Theotokion:*

Neither will I arise from Thy tomb, my Child, nor will I cease shedding tears, Thine handmaid, until I also descend into hades; for I cannot bear separation from Thee, my Son!

*Ode 9. Heirmos:*

God cannot be seen of men, upon Whom the ranks of Angels dare not gaze; but through thee, O All-Pure, was the Word incarnate made visible unto men: Whom magnifying, with the heavenly hosts we call thee blessed.

*Troparion:*

Joy shall never touch me henceforth, the Blameless Virgin said, weeping: my Light and my Joy is departed into the tomb; but I will not leave Him alone, here will I die, and be buried with Him!

*Troparion:*

Heal now the wound of my soul, my Child, the All-Pure cried with tears: arise, and assuage my pain and sorrow; for Thou canst do whatsoever Thou willest, O Master, even though Thou hast been buried willingly.

*Glory:*

*Troparion:*

O how the abyss of compassion is hidden from thee! the Lord said secretly unto His Mother: for willing to save My creation, I have chosen to die; but I will also arise, and will magnify thee, as God of heaven and earth.

*Theotokion:*

I will hymn Thy mercy, O Lover of mankind, and I worship the riches of Thy compassion, O Master: for willing to save Thy creation, Thou hast undergone death, said the All-Pure; but by Thy Resurrection, O Savior, have mercy on us all.
