*Resurrection stichera, Tone 6:*  
Holding victory over hades, O Christ, Thou didst ascend upon the Cross, that Thou mightest raise up with Thyself those who sat in the darkness of death, Thou Who art free among the dead, pouring forth life from Thine own light: O Almighty Savior, have mercy on us![^1846]

Today Christ, having trampled down death as He said, arose, and granted rejoicing unto the world, that all crying aloud might say this song: O Fountain of life, unapproachable Light, Almighty Savior, have mercy on us![^1847]

Whither shall we sinners flee from Thee, O Lord, Who art throughout all creation? In heaven Thou dwellest; in hades Thou hast trampled down death; into the depths of the sea? There is Thy hand, O Master! Unto Thee do we flee, falling down before Thee we pray: O Thou Who didst rise from the dead, have mercy on us![^1848]

*Other Idiomelon stichera of the Ascension, the same Tone:*  
The Lord ascended unto the heavens, that He might send the Comforter unto the world; the heavens prepared His throne, the clouds His ascent; the Angels marvel, beholding a man higher than themselves; the Father awaits Him Whom He hath co-eternal in His bosom; and the Holy Spirit commands all His Angels: Lift up your gates, O ye princes; clap your hands, all ye nations, for Christ is gone up where He was before![^1849]

O Lord, at Thy Ascension the Cherubim were astonished, beholding Thee, God, ascending upon the clouds, sitting upon them; and we glorify Thee, for Thy mercy is good: glory to Thee![^1850]

Beholding Thy Ascension upon the holy mountains, O Christ, the Radiance of the Father's glory, we hymn the light-fashioned appearance of Thy countenance; we bow down in worship before Thy Passions, we honor Thy Resurrection, glorifying Thy glorious Ascension: have mercy on us![^1851]

*Other stichera of the Holy Fathers.*  
*Tone 6, Podoben: "Despairing on account of her life" (Ѿча́ѧннаѧ):*  
Thou wast born from the womb before the morning star, motherless of the Father before the ages (Ps. 109:3 LXX): even though Arius glorifies Thee as a creature and not God, foolishly and daringly confounding Thee the Creator with creatures, treasuring up for himself the fuel of eternal fire; but the council which assembled in Nicaea proclaimed Thee to be the Son of God, O Lord, co-enthroned with the Father and the Spirit.[^1852]

Who tore Thy garment, O Savior? Arius, Thou hast said, who cut the equally honored dominion of the Trinity into divisions: he denied Thee to be One of the Trinity; he also teaches Nestorius not to say "Theotokos": but the council which assembled in Nicaea proclaimed Thee to be the Son of God, O Lord, co-enthroned with the Father and the Spirit.[^1853]

Into the precipice of sin falls Arius, having hated to see the light, and by a divine hook is torn asunder in his bowels, forcibly pouring out all his substance and his soul, becoming another Judas in character and in manner: but the council which assembled in Nicaea proclaimed Thee to be the Son of God, O Lord, co-enthroned with the Father and the Spirit.[^1854]

The foolish Arius cut the monarchia of the Most Holy Trinity into three unlawful and unjoined substances; therefore the God-bearing Fathers, coming together zealously, kindled with zeal like Elijah the Tishbite, with the spiritual sword cut down the blasphemer who taught shame, even as the Spirit spoke.[^1855]
