*The woman, pouring myrrh upon the body of Christ,*  
*Anticipates the myrrh and aloes of Nicodemus.*

On Holy and Great Wednesday, the most divine Fathers commanded that commemoration be made of the woman, a harlot, who anointed the Lord with myrrh, because this took place shortly before the saving Passion. For when Jesus came to Jerusalem and was in the house of Simon the leper, the woman approached Him, and poured that costly myrrh upon His head. It was arranged to be placed here for this reason: that according to the Savior's word, that most fervent deed of hers should be proclaimed everywhere and unto all. From what motive then did she come? Because she saw Christ's compassion and His approachability toward all, and now especially when she saw Him enter the house of that leper, whom the Law commanded to be unclean and cast out of fellowship. The woman therefore considered that even as He healed the other's leprosy, so also would He heal her spiritual sickness. And while He was reclining at supper, she pours myrrh upon the crown of His head, being of the value of three hundred denarii, which is sixty assaria, ten unciae, or three pieces of silver. And the disciples rebuked her, and especially Judas Iscariot; but Christ defended her, that they should not cut short her good intention. Then He also recalls His burial, turning Judas away from betrayal and vouchsafing the woman the honor that her good deed should be preached throughout the whole inhabited world. Be it known, however, that this woman seems to some to be one and the same among all the Evangelists, yet it is not so; but among three of them, as the divine Chrysostom says (Homily on Matthew [Homily 80]), she is one and the same who was also called a harlot; but in John she is no longer the same, but another marvelous woman possessing an honorable life, Mary the sister of Lazarus, who, not being as a harlot, was beloved of Christ.[^96] Which Mary, indeed, six days before Pascha, in her own house in Bethany, while He was reclining at supper, performed the anointing with myrrh, pouring it upon the extremities of His feet, and wiping them with the hair of her head, rendering double honor unto Him and offering myrrh as unto God. For she knew well that oil was offered unto God in sacrifices, and priests were anointed with myrrh (Exodus, chap. 30), and Jacob of old anointed a pillar unto God (Genesis 28 and 35). And she brought this, offering it as a gift unto Him as Teacher and as God, for the sake of the revivification of her brother. Therefore no reward is promised unto her, when Judas himself murmurs as a lover of money. But the other woman, being a harlot, two days before Pascha, while Christ was still in the same Bethany, and having been at the house of Simon the leper at supper, and then reclining, pours that costly myrrh upon His head, even as holy Matthew and Mark related. And concerning this harlot the disciples also were indignant, clearly foreseeing Christ's zealous care for almsgiving; and unto her also was the reward given, that her good deed should be glorified everywhere throughout the world. Some therefore say she is one; but the Golden-mouthed says that there are two women. There are also some who say that there are three: two indeed aforementioned, when the Passion of Christ was drawing near to the doors, and a third, another before these, or rather the first, who did this near the middle of the Gospel preaching, who was both a harlot and a sinner, inside the house not of the leper, but of Simon the Pharisee, pouring myrrh on the feet of Christ Himself and alone; when also one Pharisee was scandalized, unto whom the Savior gives the reward of the remission of sins. And concerning this woman alone does the divine Luke relate in his Gospel (section 33), near the middle, as has been said. And after the account concerning this harlot, he straightway adds these words: "And it came to pass afterward, that He went throughout every city and village, preaching and showing the glad tidings of the Kingdom of God," from which it is evident that this was not at the time of the Passion. It seems, therefore, from the time, and from those who received Him, and from the place, and persons, and houses, and moreover from the manner of the anointing with myrrh, that these women were three: two indeed were harlots, but the third was Mary the sister of Lazarus, celebrated for an honorable life. And there was one house of Simon the Pharisee, and another of Simon the leper in Bethany, and yet another house of Mary and Martha the sisters of Lazarus, in the same Bethany, even as from these things it is to be understood: that there were also two suppers for Christ, and both in Bethany: one indeed six days before Pascha in the house of Lazarus, when Lazarus also ate with Him, as the son of thunder relates, saying thus: (John, section 31): "Then Jesus six days before the Passover came to Bethany, where Lazarus was which had been dead, whom He raised from the dead. There they made Him a supper, and Martha served; but Lazarus was one of them that sat at the table with Him. Then took Mary a pound of ointment of spikenard, very costly, and anointed the feet of Jesus, and wiped His feet with her hair." But the other supper was for Him two days before Pascha, while Christ was still in Bethany in the house of Simon the leper, when the harlot also came unto Him, pouring out the costly myrrh, even as sacred Matthew relates, saying as from Christ to the disciples: (Matthew, section 108): "Ye know that after two days is the feast of the Passover." And shortly thereafter he again added: "Now when Jesus was in Bethany, in the house of Simon the leper, there came unto Him a woman having an alabaster box of very precious ointment, and poured it on His head, as He sat at meat." Wherewith Mark also agrees, saying: (Mark, section 63): "After two days was the feast of the Passover, and of unleavened bread; and being in Bethany in the house of Simon the leper, as He sat at meat, there came a woman..." and the rest thereafter. But those who contend and say that the woman who anointed the Lord with myrrh was one and the same among the four Evangelists, consider Simon the Pharisee and the leper to be one and the same; whom some also gave out to be the father of Lazarus and of his sisters Mary and Martha; and they think, not well, that there was one and the same supper, and the same one house of his in Bethany, in which also the furnished upper room was prepared, and that it was the Mystical Supper. For these two suppers were outside Jerusalem in Bethany, six days and two days [as has been said] before the legal Passover, when the women brought myrrh unto Christ in different manners. But the Mystical Supper and the furnished upper room were adorned inside the city of Jerusalem, one day before the legal Passover and the day of Christ's Passion. Some say it was in the house of an unknown man, but others say [as they assert] in the house of the beloved disciple John on holy Zion, where also the disciples were hidden for fear of the Jews, and where the touching of Thomas took place after the Resurrection, and the coming of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost, and certain other ineffable and mystical things were accomplished.[^98] Therefore this indeed

=== LEAF p69 ===
seems to me, and in truth, to be more certain according to the Golden-tongued: that these two women are accounted: one indeed, as has been said, among three Evangelists, being a harlot and a sinner, who poured myrrh upon Christ's head; and the other in John, Mary the sister of Lazarus, who brought and poured it upon the divine feet of Christ themselves. Other suppers were those in Bethany, and another was the Mystical Supper. And clearly from those things after the account concerning this harlot, the Savior sends the disciples to the city to prepare the Passover: "Go," saying, "into the city to such a man, and say: The Master says, With thee I will keep the Passover with My disciples." And again: "And there shall meet you a man bearing a pitcher of water, and he will show you a large upper room furnished; there make ready for us." And they having gone, he says, found even as He had commanded them, and made ready the Passover, as the legal Passover was near at hand, which He also came and accomplished with the disciples, as the divine Chrysostom says. Thereafter, when the supper was taking place, that is, the Mystical Supper, and the divine washing had been accomplished in the midst, having reclined again, He delivers our Pascha upon the table itself, even as John the Golden-mouthed relates; and these things are thus. But the divine John, and with him Mark, the divine Evangelists, also added the kind of myrrh, naming it genuine [pistic] and costly. For they thought to call it pistic, that is, genuine, unadulterated and unmingled, and trusted unto purity; or perhaps it was a certain name of the most excellent and first myrrh. Mark also adds that from haste the woman broke the vessel, as being narrow-mouthed, which he also calls an alabaster box; and this vessel is of glass, as sacred Epiphanius says, made without a handle, which is also called a bikion.[^97] And that myrrh was composed of many kinds, but especially of these: myrrh, fragrant cinnamon flower, that is, aromatic reed, and oil. But do Thou, Who wast anointed with spiritual myrrh, O Christ God, free us from the passions that assault us, and have mercy on us, for Thou alone art holy and the Lover of mankind. Amen.

*At the Praises, we set 4 verses, and we sing these idiomel stichera, Tone 1:*[^99]  
Knowing Thee, the Son of the Virgin, to be God, the harlot said, praying in weeping as one who had wrought deeds worthy of tears: "Loose my debt, even as I have loosed my hair; love her that loves Thee, who am justly hated, and near the publicans will I proclaim Thee, O Benefactor and Lover of mankind!"

The harlot mingled costly myrrh with tears, and poured it out upon Thy most pure feet, kissing them; straightway Thou didst justify her, and unto us grant forgiveness, O Thou Who didst suffer for us, and save us!

When the sinful woman was offering the myrrh, then was the disciple agreeing with the all-lawless; the one indeed rejoiced, pouring out the costly myrrh, but the other made haste to sell the Priceless One; she recognized the Master, but he parted from the Master; she was set free, but Judas became a slave of the enemy. A grievous thing is slothfulness, but great is repentance, which grant Thou unto me, O Savior Who didst suffer for us, and save us!

O the misery of Judas! He beheld the harlot kissing the footsteps, and he craftily plotted the kiss of betrayal; she loosed her hair, but he was revealed in fury, bearing evil-smelling malice instead of myrrh; for envy knows not how to prefer what is profitable. O the misery of Judas! from which deliver our souls, O God!

*Glory, Tone 2:*  
The sinful woman ran to buy the costly myrrh, wherewith to anoint the Benefactor, and she cried unto the seller of myrrh: "Give me the myrrh, that I also may anoint Him Who cleansed all my sins!"

*Both now, Tone 6:*  
She that was sunken in sin found Thee as a harbor of salvation; pouring out myrrh with tears, she cried unto Thee: "Behold, Thou that awaitest the repentance of those that sin! But, O Master, save me from the wave of sin, for the sake of Thy great mercy!"

*At the Aposticha, the idiomel stichera, Tone 6:*[^100]  
Today Christ comes into the house of the Pharisee, and the sinful woman, drawing near unto His feet, prostrated herself, crying aloud: "Behold her that is sunken in sin, despaired of because of her deeds, yet not abhorred by Thy goodness; and grant me, O Lord, remission of evils, and save me!"

*Verse: We were filled in the morning with Thy mercy, O Lord [Ps. 89:14 LXX].*  
The harlot spread out her hair unto Thee, the Master; Judas spread out his hands unto the lawless: the one indeed to receive remission, but the other to take silver pieces. Therefore we cry aloud unto Thee Who wast sold, and Who hast freed us: O Lord, glory be to Thee!

*Verse: And let the brightness of the Lord our God be upon us [Ps. 89:17 LXX].*
