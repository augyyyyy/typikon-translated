*Synaxarion on the Compassion of the Most Holy Theotokos:*[^2927]
On this same Friday, we celebrate the memory of the Compassion wherewith the All-Holy and Spotless Mother Virgin Mary suffered together with her Son Jesus Christ at the time of His world-saving Passions. And this Feast in the beginning was instituted by the local Council of Cologne in the year 1423[^2928] (according to the testimony of the most holy Benedict, fourteenth Pope of Rome of that name),[^2929] in rebuttal of the senseless audacity of certain heretics,[^2930] who raged exceedingly against the Icon of the most glorious Mother of God suffering at the Crucifixion. From that time therefore, in the territory only of the Roman Church (as he says), it was venerated on Friday of the sixth week of the holy Great Fast; then in time this pious veneration spread among them also unto other lands. But among us it was initiated from the local Council which was in the city of Zamość,[^2931] for the following reasons: Because of old in many places of the Ruthenian borders the Christ-loving people were accustomed piously to revere the Friday which is the tenth after Pascha;[^2932] therefore unto this their veneration, our Ruthenian Church—which has its entire order from those whom all the Teachers of the Western Church are accustomed to call *Hyperphileotheotokos*,[^2933] that is to say: lovers of the Mother of God above all, or Fathers surpassing in love towards the Mother of God, from the Greeks, I say—out of exceedingly great love toward the Most Holy Theotokos, joined unto that day of theirs, that is, unto the tenth Friday after the Resurrection of Christ, to revere with devotion that which, apart from the daily Services, two days in each week, is manifoldly commemorated generally, but by its own feast was not venerated,

=== LEAF p653 ===
although every reason of Christian piety always made it right to anticipate it: namely, the suffering of the All-Praised and Most Blessed Ever-Virgin Mary, which in reality, at the time of the voluntary Passion of her Son Christ, fulfilling the prophecy of the venerable God-receiver Simeon concerning herself: *Yea, a sword shall pierce through thine own soul also;*[^2934] she unspeakably endured. For if the wounds and torments of the martyrs endured in their bodies for Christ, in the tribulations of their salvation, are exceedingly revered by us, how much more is it worthy to revere the grief of her who, together with Christ, for the common redemption of us all, suffered anguish not now in body, but in soul? Therefore rightly, by Saint Augustine[^2935] and the other Fathers, the most holy Mother is accounted to have suffered more than all the martyrs. But in so far as that love which she bare toward her natural Son surpassed the love of the saints who surrendered their lives for Christ—Whom they had not seen nor known so intimately—by so much the Compassion of her who saw and knew Him to be God, so cruelly dishonored and slain for the sins of men, is exalted above those sufferings. This Compassion of the Most Holy and Most Blessed Virgin Mother of God we therefore now revere with such goodwill, as we are bound to be thankful unto her, not only for that she bare for us the Author and Finisher of faith, Jesus,[^2936] but also as it were co-operated with the Son to suffer for us, through which we in every way hope to obtain salvation, in Christ Jesus our Lord. Amen.
