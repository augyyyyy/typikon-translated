On Holy and Great Tuesday, we make commemoration of the Parable of the Ten Virgins. For such parables the Lord, going up to Jerusalem and journeying unto His Passion, spoke unto His disciples, and there are parables which He extended also unto the Jews. The Parable of the Ten Virgins, turning it unto almsgiving, He spoke, at the same time teaching that all should be prepared before the end. For He told them much concerning virginity, and concerning eunuchs; and virginity hath great glory, for it is truly great. But lest anyone, fulfilling this work, should neglect other virtues, and especially almsgiving, whereby the lamp of virginity is enlightened, the Holy Gospel introduces this parable. And five it calls wise, as having joined with virginity also the abundant and rich oil of almsgiving; but five it calls foolish, as they also indeed possessed virginity, but had an incomparable lack of almsgiving. On this account they are called foolish, because having accomplished the greater, they neglected the lesser, and differed in nothing from harlots: for the latter indeed were overcome by the flesh, but the former by possessions. And the night of this present life passing away, all the virgins slumbered, that is to say, they died: for sleep is called death. And while they were sleeping, a cry arose at midnight; and those indeed who had brought oil in abundance, the doors being opened, entered with the Bridegroom; but the foolish, having an insufficient supply of oil, sought it after their sleep. But the wise, though wishing to give, were unable, and before entering answered, saying: "Lest perhaps there should not be enough for us and you, go you to them that sell, that is to say, to the poor, and buy; but with difficulty, for after death this is impossible." Which also Abraham shows concerning the rich man and Lazarus, saying: "There is an impassable gulf, so that they cannot cross over from hence thither." But the foolish, having come unenlightened, cry aloud thus, knocking at the gate: "Lord, Lord, open unto us!" But the Lord Himself through Himself gives that dread answer: "Depart," saying, "I know you not; for how will you see the Bridegroom, not having the bridal gift, even almsgiving itself?" On account of this figure, therefore, the Parable of the Ten Virgins was appointed by the God-bearing Fathers to be placed here, instructing us ever to watch and to be prepared for the meeting of the true Bridegroom, by good works, and especially by almsgiving; because the day and hour of the end are unknown: even as through Joseph we acquire chastity, and through the fig tree we ever bring forth spiritual fruit. For he who accomplishes one single and greatest deed, but neglects others, and especially almsgiving, shall not enter with Christ into eternal rest, but returns back put to shame. For nothing is more mournful, and more full of disgrace, than virginity overcome by possessions.[^79] But, O Christ the Bridegroom, unite us with the wise virgins, and number us with Thy chosen flock, and have mercy on us. Amen.

Unto us who sin beyond measure, richly forgive, O Savior, and account us worthy without condemnation to worship Thy holy Resurrection, by the intercessions of Thine all-pure Mother, O Thou Who alone art Plenteous in mercy!

*Prokeimenon, Psalm 129 [LXX], Tone 6:*  
For with the Lord there is mercy, and with Him is plenteous redemption [Ps. 129:7 LXX].
