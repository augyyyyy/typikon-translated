*Verses:*  
*Giver of light, being Light of Light,*  
*Thou dost restore the man blind from birth, O Word.*[^1253]

On this same day, the Sixth Sunday after Pascha, we celebrate the miracle of our Lord God and Savior Jesus Christ wrought upon the man blind from birth, which indeed was also performed through water, even as the miracle of the Samaritan woman and that of the paralytic.[^1254] It came to pass thus: while Christ was conversing with the Jews, and showing Himself equal to the Father, and saying: "Before Abraham was, I am," they cast stones at Him (Jn 8:58–59). But He, departing thence, found a blind man stumbling—he had been born thus, having only the appearance of eyes and sunken sockets.[^1255] The Savior, finding him thus, was asked by His disciples, who had heard what was said concerning the paralytic: "Behold, thou art made whole; sin no more" (Jn 5:14), and that the sin of parents is visited upon children: "Master, who did sin, this man, or his parents, that he was born blind?" (Jn 9:2).[^1256] Otherwise also, holding a certain Epicurean notion that souls pre-exist, and having sinned immaterially, are joined into bodies—all of which Christ refuted, saying: "Neither hath this man sinned, nor his parents: but that the works of God should be made manifest," that is, Mine (Jn 9:3). Nor is the word spoken concerning the Father, and the "that" expresses the outcome and not the cause.[^1257] And having said this, Christ spat on the ground, made clay, and anointed the place of his eyes, and commanded him to go to the Pool of Siloam and wash, to show that He Himself in the beginning took dust from the earth and created man (Gen 2:7). And since the eye is the most divine member of all in the body, He creates that which did not exist, showing that He is the One Who gives motion also to the power of the soul. Nor did He anoint with water, but with spittle, that it might be known that all grace was from the spittle of His mouth, and that He intended to send him to Siloam. And He commanded him to wash, lest anyone should attribute the healing to the earth and clay of that place.[^1258] And He sends him to Siloam, that he might have many witnesses of the healing, for many met him as he walked with his eyes anointed with clay. And some say that when he washed, the clay from the spittle was not washed away, but that that clay, by the addition of that which was better, was transformed into the structure of eyes. Now "Siloam" is interpreted "Sent" (Jn 9:7); for it was a stronghold of the city of Jerusalem. In the days of Hezekiah, when enemies besieged the city and cut off Siloam, so that water was dug everywhere, they dug out the inner wells and streams within, for the reception of water. And if anyone was sent thither by the command of the Prophet Isaiah, the water again gushed forth and was drawn; but if any of the enemies wished to draw, the springs of water were withheld, and so it happened in their case. That Christ might therefore show that He also is of God, He sends the blind man thither, and light immediately follows. Some indeed thought that Siloam was interpreted "Sent" also on account of the present blind man sent by Christ.[^1259] The blind man then returned, having washed, by an ineffable power, the mystery being unknown even to him who had suffered it. And his neighbors and those who otherwise knew him, seeing him seeing well, were in doubt. But he confessed that he had formerly been blind, and being questioned concerning the cause of his receiving sight, proclaimed Christ, the healer of the affliction.[^1260] When the Pharisees heard of the marvelous sign, the Savior was again blasphemed, as not keeping the Sabbath; for on the Sabbath, as was His custom, that which was done unto the blind man was also performed. A division therefore arose among them: some saying that Jesus was of God on account of the miracles that were done, but others that He was not of God, because He kept not the Sabbath. Those who held a good opinion of Him questioned the blind man: "What sayest thou of Him?" And he proclaimed Him to be a Prophet, which was more honorable than their view. But they again did not believe that, when he was blind, Christ granted him healing; therefore they called his parents also, not believing his neighbors, wishing rather to conceal the matter; but they made it all the more evident, since the parents confessed everything, though fearing to be put out of the synagogue, referring the deed to his own age.[^1261] But again they say unto the blind man: "Give God the praise," for thou wast healed by Him and not by Christ; for He is a sinner, because He breaks the Sabbath. But he, wishing to show by deeds that He is God, said: "I know not; one thing I know, that, whereas I was blind, now I see." Again therefore they said unto him: "How opened He thine eyes?" But he, being vexed, did not speak in detail, but affirmed that if He were not of God, He could do no such sign. And at first he was reviled by them, because he had confessed, and was His disciple, and because he said: "Since the world began was it not heard that any man opened the eyes of one that was born blind" (Jn 9:32); for others also enlightened the blind, but one born blind none at all. Having mocked him therefore, they cast him far out of the synagogue.[^1262] After this Jesus found him, and said unto him: "Dost thou believe on the Son of God?" And he, having learned Who He was that was speaking with him and was seen by him—for he knew Him not before, being blind—worshiped Him, and became His disciple, proclaiming Thanksgiving.[^1263] This was said also allegorically: For the blind man is the people from among the Gentiles, whom Christ, passing by the calumny of living upon earth and not in heaven, found—even though He came again for the sake of the Hebrew people, yet passing by, He came also unto the Gentiles. And having spat upon the ground and made clay, He anointed him: which calumny He taught before, because He came down upon earth as a drop, and was incarnate of the Holy Virgin. Then He also delivered divine Baptism, which is Siloam. Thereafter, the Christian people from the Gentiles dare all things for Christ, and are persecuted, and bear witness, and afterward are proclaimed and glorified by Him.[^1264]

## ON THE SUNDAY OF THE BLIND MAN, AT VESPERS[^1326]

*At Lord, I have cried, we set 6 verses: 3 of the feast, and 3 of the Menaion.*[^1327]

*Tone 5, Podoben: "Rejoice, O container of ascetics" (Ра́дꙋйсѧ по́стникѡвъ):*  
Being Light unto all human nature, and the Eye unto the body of this world, O God and Word, and recognized as the Maker and Fashioner of eyes: now also by the mingling of clay and spittle, Thou dost ineffably refashion the blind man, Thy fingers fashioning both sight and vision; which having attained, he who was previously known among those born as one entirely blind to the sun, sees Thee, the sweet Sun, beholding the form of Him Who created him for our sake in Thine ineffable compassion.[^1328]

Deprived of limbs, and enriched by the configuration of human bodily instrumental parts, he that was previously blind from his mother’s womb considered that he was not a complete creation on account of the lack of eyes, whence by his feet came many stumblings of the body, striking against stones: yet having acquired through Thee this wealth also, so that he sees the light of the world, he announcs Thee, the Master of lights, as God and Lord over creations, and the Maker of all the universe.[^1329]

With lips, and soul, and mind, he that was formerly blind confessed Him Who creatively gave pupils of the eyes from dust and spittle and ocular enlightenment, as the all-cosmic Overseer and Master of creatures, Who became man solely for compassion on His creature; and he proclaimed Him as almighty God, Whom the scribes, being totally unable to behold through envy and because of his converse with them, acting ever more blindly, judged worthy of expulsion from the synagogue, Him Who once gave the blind man pupils, both spiritual and bodily.[^1330]

## ON MONDAY OF THE BLIND MAN, AT MATINS[^1338]

*At God is the Lord, the Resurrection Troparion of the Tone.*[^1339]  
*After the first Stichology, Sessional Hymn.*  
*Tone 5, Podoben: "The Word co-unoriginate" (Собезна́чальное Сло́во):*  
Let us praise the Cross of the Lord, let us honor His holy Burial with hymns, and let us supremely glorify His Resurrection: for as God He raised the dead together with Himself from the tombs, having despoiled the dominion of death and the might of the devil, and shone forth light upon those in hades.[^1340]

*Verse: O Lord, rebuke me not in Thine anger, nor chasten me in Thy wrath.*[^1341]  
Let us all run victoriously and meet Christ, ye faithful, with abundance of oil and radiant lamps, that we may be accounted worthy within the bridal chamber: for he that is found outside the doors shall cry in vain unto God: Have mercy on me![^1342]

*Verse: Marvelous is God in His saints, the God of Israel.*[^1343]  
*Martyrikon:*  
O Lord, Thy passion-bearers, having emulated the cup of Thy Passion, abandoned worldly beauty and became companions of the Angels: by their supplications grant peace unto our souls, O Christ God, and great mercy.[^1344]
