# PART III: PROPER RUBRICS FOR FIXED FEASTS (Menaion Cycle)

[Book Page 133]

### Month of September

☩ **On the 1st Day: The Beginning of the Indiction, which is the New Year. [The Beginning of Christian Freedom]. And of our Venerable Father Symeon the Stylite, and of the other Holy Women.**[^146]

The office of the Indiction and of the Venerable Symeon is sung according to the rubric of the Anthologion or Menaion.  
At Vespers: stichera of the Indiction 3, and of the Venerable Father 3; then there is an Entrance and 3 readings.  
At Matins: after the Kathismata and Sessional Hymns, immediately Psalm 50 and the canons. [There is no Gospel]. The Great Doxology.  
At the Divine Liturgy: the Beatitudes from the canon of the Indiction, Ode 3, and of the saint to 4. After the Entrance: troparion first of the Indiction, and of the Theotokos: **"Rejoice, full of grace, Virgin Theotokos..."** (*Ра́дꙋйся, благода́тная...*), and of the Venerable Father; **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:"** kontakion of the saint; **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** kontakion of the Indiction. If the Temple is of the Theotokos: **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** of the Temple of the Theotokos. Prokeimenon, Epistle, and Gospel of the Indiction and of the Venerable Father. The daily Epistle and Gospel are read beforehand.  
If the Temple is of St. Symeon: at Great Vespers, at **"Lord, I have cried"** (*Го́споди воззва́хъ*): stichera of the Indiction 3, and of the Temple of Symeon to 5 (if two stichera of the Venerable Father, we sing of him to 6); **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:"** of the Venerable Father; **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** of the Indiction. Entrance, Prokeimenon of the day, and readings

[Book Page 134]

of the Indiction and of the Venerable Father.[^147]  
At the Litiya: stichera of the Temple [those at the Aposticha]; **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:"** of the Temple; **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** Theotokion according to Tone.  
At the Aposticha: stichera of the Indiction; **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:"** of the saint; **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** of the Indiction.  
At the Blessing of Loaves: troparion of the Temple twice, and **"O Virgin Theotokos, rejoice..."** (*Богоро́дице Дѣ́во, ра́дꙋйсѧ...*) once.  
At Matins: at **"God is the Lord"** (*Бо́гъ Госпо́дь*): in order as in the Anthologion. Polyeleos and Sessional Hymn of the Temple; Graduals: Tone 4, 1st Antiphon. Gospel from Matthew, Pericope 43, and sticheron of the Temple. Canon of the Indiction for the New Year with Heirmos to 6, and of the Temple to 8. Katavasia: **"Having inscribed the Cross..."** (*Кре́стъ начерта́въ...*). At the Praises: stichera of the Indiction 4, and of the Temple 4, with his evening refrains. **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:"** of the Temple; **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** of the Indiction.  
At the Divine Liturgy: Beatitudes of the Indiction to 4, and of the Venerable Father Ode 6 to 4. After the Entrance: troparion of the Indiction and of the Temple; **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:"** of the Temple; **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** of the Indiction. Prokeimenon, Epistle, Gospel, and Communion Hymn of the Indiction and of the Temple.  
☞ It is necessary to know: if the first day of the month of September occur on a Sunday, the office of the holy women is omitted, and is sung when the Superior of the church decides. The Sunday Resurrection office is sung with the service of the New Year and of the Venerable Symeon according to the Typikon which you have in the Anthologion. And then we sing at the Divine Liturgy, after the Entrance: the Resurrection Troparion and of the Indiction, and of the saint; the Resurrection Kontakion; **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:"** of the saint; **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** of the Indiction. Prokeimenon, Epistle, Gospel first of the Indiction, then Sunday Resurrection under one pericope, then of the saint. Communion Hymn of the Indiction and of the saint.

[Book Page 135]

☞ But if the Temple of St. Symeon the Stylite occur on a Sunday:  
At Little Vespers, at **"Lord, I have cried"** (*Го́споди воззва́хъ*): Sunday Resurrection stichera 4; **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:"** of the Temple, and the rest.  
At Great Vespers, at **"Lord, I have cried"**: Sunday Resurrection stichera 3, of the Indiction 3, and of the Temple 4; **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:"** of the Temple; **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** Dogmatikon of the Tone. Readings of the Indiction and of the saint.  
At the Litiya: stichera of the Temple and of the Indiction from the Aposticha; **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:"** of the Temple; **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** of the Indiction written at the Praises.  
At the Aposticha: Sunday Resurrection stichera; **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:"** of the Temple; **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** of the Indiction.  
At the Blessing of Loaves: troparion **"O Virgin Theotokos, rejoice..."** (*Богоро́дице Дѣ́во, ра́дꙋйсѧ...*) twice, and of the Temple once, and the rest.  
At Matins: Prokeimenon and Gospel of the Temple; **"Having seen the Resurrection of Christ..."** (*Воскресе́нїе Христо́во...*); sticheron of the Temple. Canon: Sunday Resurrection to 4, of the Indiction to 4, and of the saint to 6. After Ode 3: kontakion and ikos of the Indiction, and Sessional Hymn of the Temple; **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** of the Indiction. After Ode 6: kontakion and ikos of the Temple. At the Praises (*Хвали́тны*): Sunday Resurrection stichera 3, of the Indiction 3, and of the Temple 3 with his refrains; **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:"** of the Temple; **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** **"Most blessed art thou, O Virgin Theotokos..."** (*Преблагослове́нна е҆сѝ...*); and the rest. On the evening of the same day there is the Apodosis for the honor of the Temple in the customary manner.

☞ But if the Forefeast of the Nativity of the Theotokos occur on a Sunday, at the Divine Liturgy after the Entrance: Resurrection Troparion and of the Forefeast; **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:"** Resurrection Kontakion; **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** of the Forefeast. Prokeimenon, Epistle, and Gospel: first of Sunday before the Exaltation [if applicable], then of the day/cycle...

### On the 8th Day: If the Feast of the Nativity of the Theotokos Occur on the Saturday before the Exaltation

The Epistle and Gospel of Saturday before the Exaltation are read on the 13th day.[^149]  
If the Nativity of the Theotokos occur on a Sunday: you have the Typikon on leaf 94. In addition to this, at the Divine Liturgy: Prokeimenon, Epistle, and Gospel we read first of Sunday before the Exaltation, then of the cycle, after this of the Theotokos, but the Prokeimenon of the Tone is omitted. Communion Hymn of Sunday and of the feast.  
☞ It is necessary to know: on the evening of the Feast of the Nativity of the Theotokos, a Kathisma is not said at Vespers, on account of the labor of the Vigil. Thus indeed we do on every feast when there is a Vigil.

### Saturday before the Exaltation

At the Divine Liturgy: Prokeimenon, Tone 3: **"Sing to our God, sing; sing to our King, sing"** (*По́йте Бо́гу на́шему, по́йте; по́йте Царе́ви на́шему, по́йте*); Epistle and Gospel of Saturday before the Exaltation, and Epistle and Gospel of Saturday of the cycle under one pericope. Then of the saint, if he has them. Communion Hymn of the day.  
Look: If the Apodosis of the Nativity of the Theotokos occur on the Saturday before the Exaltation: Prokeimenon and Communion Hymn of the feast; Epistle and Gospel first of Saturday before the Exaltation, then of the cycle. And the Prokeimenon of Saturday before the Exaltation is omitted.

### Sunday before the Exaltation

At the Divine Liturgy: Prokeimenon, Tone 6: **"Save, O Lord, Thy people, and bless Thine inheritance"** (*Спасѝ, Го́споди, лю́ди Твоя̑, и҆ благословѝ достоя́нїе Твоѐ*); Epistle and Gospel of Sunday before the Exaltation, then of the cycle. Communion Hymn of the day.

[Book Page 137]

☞ If the Afterfeast of the Nativity of the Theotokos occur on a Sunday: it is sung according to the general Typikon on leaf 97. At the Divine Liturgy: Prokeimenon, Epistle, Alleluia, and Gospel first of Sunday before the Exaltation, then of the cycle under one pericope, and of the saint. The ordinary Prokeimenon of the cycle is omitted. Communion Hymn: **"Praise the Lord from the heavens"** (*Хвали́те Го́спода съ небе́съ*), and of the saint, if there is one.

### On the 13th Day: Commemoration of the Dedication of the Temple of the Holy Resurrection of Christ our God. And Forefeast of the Precious and Life-Giving Cross, and of the Holy Centurion Cornelius

*(To celebrate the day of dedication, in the likeness of Solomon, throughout all the churches of the inhabited world, the Holy Fathers ordained, having gathered from all lands on this day for the consecration of the church built by Constantine and his mother Helen on the Mount of Golgotha in Jerusalem, which is the mother of all churches. We celebrate therefore giving thanks to Christ God, Who by His passion and resurrection renewed the whole creation, and cleansed from idolatrous defilements His holy Church).*[^150]  
At Vespers: the customary Kathisma. At **"Lord, I have cried"** (*Го́споди воззва́хъ*): stichera to 6: of the Dedication 3, and of the saint 3; **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:"** of the Dedication; **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** of the forefeast. If on Friday evening: **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** Dogmatikon. Entrance. Readings: 3, and all the rest in order of the Anthologion.  
At the Divine Liturgy: After the Entrance: troparion of the Dedication, and of the Cross, and of the saint; **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:"** kontakion of the saint; **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"**

[Book Page 138]

of the Dedication.[^151] Prokeimenon of the Dedication: Epistle pericope 337, and Gospel pericope 37; Communion Hymn of the Dedication; then the Matins Epistle and Gospel under one pericope, of the cycle.  
☞ Be it also known: If the Exaltation of the Precious Cross occur on a Sunday: on the Saturday before the Exaltation, at the Divine Liturgy: Prokeimenon and Alleluia of the Dedication only; but the Prokeimenon of Saturday before the Exaltation is omitted. Epistle and Gospel first of the Dedication, and then of Saturday before the Exaltation. Communion Hymn of the Dedication. But the daily Epistle and Gospel of the cycle are read beforehand on another day. On the Sunday before the Exaltation, the Epistle and Gospel are read on the outgoing Sunday.  
☞ If the Dedication on the 13th day occur on a Sunday, the office of the Holy Centurion Cornelius is omitted, and is sung when the Superior of the church decides. The Sunday Resurrection office is sung with the Dedication and with the Forefeast of the Precious Cross, according to the Typikon of the Anthologion.  
At the Divine Liturgy: Beatitudes: Sunday Resurrection to 6, and of the Dedication Ode 3 to 4. After the Entrance: troparion: Sunday Resurrection, of the Dedication, and of the Forefeast; Kontakion: Sunday Resurrection; **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** of the Dedication. Prokeimenon, Epistle, Alleluia, and Gospel: first of Sunday before the Exaltation, and then of the Dedication. But the cycle under one pericope. Communion Hymn: **"Praise the Lord from the heavens"** (*Хвали́те Го́спода съ небе́съ*), and of the Dedication.

### On the 14th Day: The Universal Exaltation of the Precious and Life-Giving Cross

At the time of Vespers [in the Forefeast], at the striking of the bell for Vespers, the Priest enters into the Skeuophylakion, and places the Precious Cross with fragrant basil upon the Prothesis (Table of Preparation). And Little Vespers is sung in the customary manner according to the order of the Anthologion. After the dismissal of Little Vespers, the Priest enters to the Table of Preparation with candles, vests in epitrachelion and phelonion, and having censed the Precious Cross, says: **"Blessed is our God always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages:"** Then: the Trisagion; after **"Our Father..."**: Troparion of the Cross: **"Save, O Lord, Thy people, and bless Thine inheritance..."** (*Спасѝ, Го́споди, лю́ди Твоя̑...*); **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** Kontakion: **"He that was lifted up upon the Cross voluntarily..."** (*Вознесы́йся на Кре́стъ во́лею...*). And the Priest takes the Precious Cross upon his head, and carries It into the Holy Sanctuary, preceded by two candles. And he places the Precious Cross upon the Holy Table in the place of the Gospel, and sets the Gospel upon the High Place, and lights a candle before It for the whole night.[^152]

[Book Page 139]

☞ Such an order of the bringing out of the Cross takes place likewise on the Third Sunday of Great Lent, and on the 1st day of August.  
At Great Vespers at the Vigil: If Sunday occur, we sing **"Blessed is the man"** (*Блаже́нъ мꙋ́жъ*): the whole Kathisma. If another day, there is no Kathisma. At **"Lord, I have cried"** (*Го́споди воззва́хъ*): stichera to 8. Prokeimenon of the day. [If this feast occur on a Saturday, the Prokeimenon of the day is omitted, and there is sung the Great Prokeimenon, Tone 7: **"Our God is in heaven and on earth; He hath done whatsoever He pleased"** (*Бо́гъ на́шъ на небесѝ и҆ на землѝ, вся̑ є҆ли̑ка восхотѣ̀ сотворѝ*). Seeing that on Saturday evening the Prokeimenon is sung: **"The Lord is King, He is clothed with majesty"** (*Госпо́дь воцари́ся, въ лѣ́поту ся ѡ҆блече́*)]: and the rest according to the rubric of the Anthologion. At the Blessing of Loaves: troparion of the feast 3 times, and the rest.

*At Matins, the Gospel from John, pericope 42 from the midpoint.*[^153]  
**"Having beheld the Resurrection of Christ..."** (*Воскресе́нїе Хрїсто́во ви́дѣвше*); **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:"** **"Through the prayers of the Apostles..."** (*Моли́твами а҆по́столъ*); **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** **"Through the prayers of the Theotokos, O Merciful One..."** (*Моли́твами Богоро́дицы, Ми́лостиве*); the sticheron, and the rest.  
*During the singing of the Great Doxology, the Superior vests in all priestly vestments, and taking the censer with incense, comes to the Holy Table, and censes the Precious Cross crosswise, and takes It upon his head, and goes out by the North Door, preceded by two lamps (candles) as far as the Royal Doors, and standing there, awaits the end of the Doxology and of the Trisagion. When this is finished, the Superior proclaims in a loud voice:* **"Wisdom! Stand upright!"** (*Премꙋ́дрость, про́сти!*). *And the choir with those present begins the troparion:* **"Save, O Lord, Thy people..."** (*Спасѝ, Го́споди, лю́ди Твоя̑...*) *3 times. And the Superior, bearing the Precious Cross upon his head, comes into the midst of the church, directly opposite the Holy Royal Doors, to the tetrapod [table] prepared there, and places the Precious Cross upon it, and censes It from the four sides. Then he makes three prostrations to the earth, on whatever day it may be, and taking the Precious Cross with the fragrant basil entwined upon the foot of the Cross, he stands before the tetrapod, looking toward the east; and the deacon, if there be one, proclaims in a loud voice in the hearing of all:* **"Have mercy on us, O God, according to Thy great mercy, hear us..."** (*Поми́луй на́съ, Бо́же, по вели́цѣй ми́лости Твое́й, оу҆слы́ши...*), *as in the Anthologion. And the choir with those present begins the first hundred:* **"Lord, have mercy"** (*Го́споди, поми́луй*). *The Superior at the beginning signs with the Precious*

[Book Page 141]

*Cross three times. Then the Superior inclines his head, so that the head is about a span from the earth, and gradually is elevated upward until the completion of the second fifty,* **"Lord, have mercy"** (*Го́споди, поми́луй*). *And when it reaches ninety-seven,* **"Lord, have mercy"**, *the leader of the choir raises his voice; and the Superior standing upright awaits the end of the hundred [the hundredfold* **"Lord, have mercy"**], *and signs [blesses] with the Cross again 3 times. Thereafter he turns toward the west, and the deacon says:* **"Again we pray for the most holy universal Pontiff (name), and for our God-preserved Emperor, for his realm..."** *and the rest. And we begin the second hundred,* **"Lord, have mercy"**. *And the Superior makes the second elevation as was prescribed above. Then turning toward the south, the deacon says:* **"Again we pray for the remission of sins of our Most Reverend Metropolitan (name), and of our God-loving Bishop (name), and for all our brethren in Christ, for health, and for salvation, let us all say."** *And he begins the third hundred,* **"Lord, have mercy"**. *When that is finished, the Superior turns toward the north, and the deacon says:* **"Again we pray for every Christian soul, afflicted and distressed, and for health and salvation and remission of sins, let us all say."** *And the fourth elevation takes place. And the Superior, turning toward the east, blesses again; and the deacon says:* **"Again we pray for all who serve and have served in this holy church*

[Book Page 142]

*(or in this holy monastery), our fathers and brethren, for health and for salvation, and for the remission of their sins, let us all say."* *And we begin the fifth hundred.*[^154]  
*After the fifth elevation, we sing:* **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:"** *kontakion:* **"He that was lifted up upon the Cross voluntarily..."** (*Вознесы́йся на Кре́стъ во́лею...*). *While this is sung, the Superior places the precious Wood of the Cross upon the tetrapod, and sings this troparion in Tone 6:*  
**"To Thy Cross we bow down in worship, O Master, and Thy holy Resurrection we glorify"** (*Крестꙋ̀ Твоему̀ покланя́емся, Влады́ко, и҆ свято́е воскресе́нїе Твоѐ сла́вимъ*) *3 times. The brethren also singing the same troparion, the Superior begins to venerate, making 2 metanias before the Precious Cross, and then kisses It. After the kissing, he makes again one metania, and likewise to both choirs one metania each. Thereafter all the brethren come two by two, and likewise the people present, in order bow down and kiss the Precious Cross. And we sing the idiomela stichera until the veneration is completed. The Stichera of the Cross, Tone 2:* **"Come, O believers..."** (*Прїиди́те, вѣ́рнїи...*).  
*After the conclusion of the veneration of the Precious Cross, the tetrapod with the Precious Cross is set on the right side of the Royal Doors, and there it stands until the feast is given its Apodosis.*[^155]  
*Thereafter the priest enters into the holy Sanctuary; and the deacon says the ectene:* **"Have mercy on us, O God..."** (*Поми́луй на́съ, Бо́же...*). *Thereafter:* **"Let us complete our morning prayer..."** (*И҆спо́лнимъ оу҆́треннюю моли́тву на́шу...*). *And the Great Dismissal. The First Hour: at the First Hour, troparion of the feast; and after the Trisagion, kontakion of the feast.*

[Book Page 143]

*At the Divine Liturgy: the Antiphons of the feast. Instead of the Trisagion:* **"To Thy Cross we bow down in worship, O Master, and Thy holy Resurrection we glorify."** *In the refectory, we eat with oil. But cheese and eggs we dare by no means to touch, but we fast completely.*[^156]  
*If this feast occur on a Sunday, nothing of the Sunday Resurrection is sung (only* **"Having beheld the Resurrection of Christ"** *we say); but the entire service is of the feast.*

### On the 15th Day: The Holy Great-Martyr Nicetas

*In the evening we do not say a Kathisma, on account of the labor of the Vigil. At* **"Lord, I have cried"** (*Го́споди воззва́хъ*): *stichera to 6: of the Cross 3, and of the saint 3; "Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:" of the saint; "Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:" of the feast. Entrance. Great Prokeimenon, Tone 7:* **"Our God is in heaven and on earth; He hath done whatsoever He pleased"** (*Бо́гъ на́шъ на небесѝ и҆ на землѝ, вся̑ є҆ли̑ка восхотѣ̀ сотворѝ*), *and its verses. Thereafter the ectene:* **"Let us all say..."** (*Рце́мъ всѝ...*); *and* **"Vouchsafe, O Lord..."** (*Сподо́би, Го́споди...*); *and* **"Let us complete our evening prayer..."** (*И҆спо́лнимъ вече́рнюю моли́тву на́шу...*). [*Thus it is fitting to sing on all Dominical feasts in the evening, on which there is an Entrance and the Great Prokeimenon]. Then at the Aposticha: stichera of the feast, with its refrains; "Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:" of the saint; "Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:" of the Cross; Tone of the Prophet. After* **"Now lettest Thou"** (*Ны́нѣ ѿпꙋща́еши*): *troparion of the saint; "Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:" of the feast. Great Dismissal.*  
*Matins takes place according to the general Typikon: see if the saint occurs within the feast [in the Afterfeast]. At the Divine Liturgy: Beatitudes of the feast, Ode 3 to 4, and of the saint, Ode 6 to 4. After the Entrance:* **"Save us, O Son of God, Who wast crucified in the flesh, who sing to Thee: Alleluia."** [*Thus we sing until the Apodosis*].[^157]

[Book Page 144]

*Troparion of the feast, troparion of the saint; "Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:" kontakion of the saint; "Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:" of the feast. Prokeimenon of the feast and of the saint. Epistle and Gospel of the day, and of the saint.*

### Saturday after the Exaltation

*Epistle to the Corinthians, pericope 125 from the midpoint, and Gospel from John, pericope 30, and of the cycle.*[^158]

### Sunday after the Exaltation

*Prokeimenon of the feast. Epistle to the Galatians, pericope 203; Gospel from Mark, pericope 37, and of the cycle. Communion Hymn of the day and of the feast.*  
☞ *It is necessary to know: If the Exaltation of the Holy Cross occur on a Saturday, the Sunday after the Exaltation has its Epistle and Gospel read on the morrow of the feast on Sunday. In the incoming [approaching] Saturday, at the Apodosis of the feast, at the Divine Liturgy: Prokeimenon, Alleluia, and Communion Hymn of the feast; Epistle and Gospel first of the Saturday after the Exaltation, and thereafter of the cycle.*

### On the 21st Day: The Holy Apostle Quadratus. On This Day the Feast of the Precious Cross Is Given Its Apodosis

*At Vespers: the customary Kathisma. At* **"Lord, I have cried"** (*Го́споди воззва́хъ*): *stichera of the feast to 6, sung as on the feast itself, and the rest according to the general Typikon. At the Divine Liturgy: Beatitudes from the canon, Ode 4 to 8. After the Entrance: troparion of the feast; "Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:" kontakion of the feast. The Trisagion. (* **"To Thy Cross"** *we do not sing). Prokeimenon of the feast; Epistle and Gospel of the day.*

[Book Page 145]

☞ *After the dismissal of the Divine Liturgy: the priest goes forth with the censer, the deacon with a candle, and censes the Precious Cross; and veneration having been made to the Precious Cross, the priest takes up the Precious Cross upon his head, and carries It into the holy Sanctuary, and censes It crosswise. And thus the feast of the Precious Cross is given its Apodosis. The office of the holy Apostle is sung on the 20th day.*[^159]  
☞ *If the Apodosis of the feast of the Precious Cross occur on a Sunday: at the Divine Liturgy, Prokeimenon of the day and of the feast; Epistle and Gospel of the Sunday after the Exaltation, and of the day. Alleluia and Communion Hymn of the feast and of the day.*  
*Look: Be it known: That after the Apodosis of the feast of the Precious Cross, from the 22nd day of the month of September, we begin to recite the Psalter at Matins by 3 Kathismata, if there is no Polyeleos for the saint, on all days until the 20th day of the month of December; on Sundays at Matins by 2 Kathismata, and we add also the Polyeleos on all Sundays until the 20th of December, and the Bulgarian refrains.*[^160]

## The Month of October

### On the 1st Day: Celebration of the Protection of the Most Holy Theotokos, with the Apostle Ananias, and the Venerable Romanus the Melodist

*We sing everything in order, as in the Anthologion.*  
☞ *If the Temple be of the Protection of the Theotokos, on whatever day it occur, except Sunday: we sing the entire office of the Protection, as on another feast of the Theotokos; but the office of the saints we sing on another day.*

### On the 11th Day: Sunday of the Holy Fathers

*It is necessary to know: That after the 11th day of this month of October, on the incoming [approaching] Sunday, we celebrate the memory of the Holy Fathers who came together at the Seventh Ecumenical Council in Nicaea for the second time, for the deposition and destruction of the godless teachings of the Christ-hating iconoclasts: whom they anathematized; and to honor the Holy Icons and render them fitting veneration.*[^162]

## The Month of November

### On the 14th Day: The Holy Apostle Philip

*Be it known: That we always observe Meatfare on the 14th day, on the commemoration of the holy Apostle Philip, without change, except for the two days of Wednesday and Friday; and on the 15th we begin the Fast before the Nativity of Christ.*[^164]  
*If Wednesday or Friday occur on the 14th day, on the commemoration of the holy Apostle Philip, then we observe Meatfare on the 13th day, on the commemoration of Saint John Chrysostom; but on Wednesday or on Friday we dare by no means to make Meatfare, for it is forbidden by the holy Canons.*

[Book Page 150]

## The Month of December

### On the 11th Day: On the Sunday of the Forefathers

*It is necessary to know: That from this 11th day, from the commemoration of Daniel the Stylite, the Sunday of the Forefathers begins, and ascends until the 17th day of this same December; and it takes place in this manner:*[^165]  
*If the Nativity of Christ occur on a Sunday, then the Sunday of the Forefathers takes place on the 11th day of December, two weeks before the Nativity; and the Sunday of the Holy Fathers, which is before the Nativity of Christ, is on the 18th day of December.*  
*If the Nativity of Christ occur on a Monday, then the Sunday of the Forefathers takes place on the 17th day of December; and the Sunday of the Holy Fathers on the 24th day.*  
*If the Nativity of Christ occur on a Tuesday, then the Sunday of the Forefathers takes place on the 16th day; and the Sunday of the Holy Fathers, which is before the Nativity of Christ, on the 23rd day of December.*  
*If the Nativity of Christ occur on a Wednesday, then the Sunday of the Forefathers takes place on the 15th day of December, and the Sunday of the Holy Fathers on the 22nd day of December.*  
*If the Nativity of Christ occur on a Thursday, then the Sunday of the Forefathers takes place on the 14th day of December, and the Sunday of the Holy Fathers on the 21st day.*  
*If the Nativity of Christ occur on a Friday, the Sunday of the Forefathers takes place on the 13th day of December, and the Sunday of the Holy Fathers on the 20th day.*  
*If the Nativity of Christ occur on a Saturday, then the Sunday of the Forefathers takes place on December*

[Book Page 151]

*the 12th day, and the Sunday of the Holy Fathers on December the 19th day.*  
*Look: On the Sunday of the Forefathers, whether it occur before the 28th Sunday or afterwards, read at the Divine Liturgy the Gospel which is written for the 28th Sunday, from Luke, pericope 76:* **"A certain man made a great supper..."** (*Человѣ́къ нѣ́кїй сотворѝ ве́черю ве́лїю...*), *invariably alone. And on the 28th Sunday read the ordinary pericope of the other Sunday in which it occurs, or of the Forefathers.*[^166]

### Notice 1

*Concerning the festal Sundays occurring here, that is: concerning the Sunday of the Forefathers, the Sunday of the Fathers before the Nativity of Christ, concerning the Sunday after the Nativity, before and after Theophany, it is necessary to know: that if in any year the Sunday of the Publican occur earlier, and the ordinary Sundays before the Sunday of the Forefathers remain unfulfilled, then it is not fitting to omit them; but after the Epistles and Gospels of the aforesaid festal Sundays, read also the ordinary ones, lest they remain unread.*[^167]  
*If ever the Sunday of the Publican and the Pharisee be far removed from the Sunday after Theophany, then these ordinary Sundays are omitted here, and after the Sunday which is after Theophany they are resumed. These Sundays are omitted where there is a deferral [otstupka] for the Sundays, that is: a Paschal letter indicating which Sunday is to be taken after the 4th Sunday after Theophany. Such deferrals are found around the 24th Sunday after the Descent [of the Holy Spirit]: see in the Sluzhebniks.*

[Book Page 152]

### Notice 2

*Concerning the Sundays, that is, the liturgical Epistles and Gospels, which must sometimes be repeated after the Sunday after Theophany.*[^168]  
*If in any year the Sunday of the Publican and the Pharisee is not soon after the 1st Sunday after Theophany, and there are not enough ordinary Epistles and Gospels for reading for several weeks: then it is fitting to look in the Paschalion, or in the Sluzhebnik according to the Paschal Keys, arranged for this reason in the Menologion from the 14th of January until the 14th of February, on what date of the month the Triodion begins (that is, the Sunday of the Publican); and beginning from the 31st Sunday [for the 32nd Sunday is not repeated, but is always read before the Sunday of the Publican] count backward, and borrow as many Sundays with their weeks as is necessary to fill this deficiency.*  
☞ *But this must not be understood concerning the Tones and Matins Gospels: for the Tones and Matins Gospels follow invariably in their own order until the 5th Sunday of Lent. For the Tones and Matins Gospels thou hast six pillars in the Octoechos or in the Horologion; likewise also at the end of this book.*[^169]  
*Look: One pillar embraces 8 Tones. The first pillar begins on the 2nd Sunday after the Descent of the Holy Spirit.*

### On the 24th Day: The Eve of the Nativity of Christ

*It is necessary to know concerning the Hours on the Eve of the Nativity of Christ: If the Nativity of Christ occur on a Sunday or on a Monday, we sing the Hours beforehand on Friday. But if the Nativity of Christ occur on a Tuesday, on a Wednesday, on a Thursday, on a Friday, and on a Saturday, we sing the Hours on the Eve.*[^172]  
*The Order of the Royal or Great Hours, sung on the Eve of the Nativity of Christ:*  
*At the beginning of the 2nd hour [the 2nd hour before noon], the bell strikes. And having assembled in church, the priest vests in phelonion, and the deacon in sticharion. And the sacristan places a decorated tetrapod directly opposite the Royal Doors, and lights a candle upon the candle-stand. And the priest, taking from the altar the Holy Gospel, comes out with the Gospel into the church through the Royal Doors, preceded by the deacon with the censer. And he places*

[Book Page 158]

*the Holy Gospel upon the tetrapod; and standing before the tetrapod, makes the beginning according to custom:* **"Blessed is our God..."** (*Благослове́нъ Бо́гъ на́шъ...*); *and after the beginning the reader says:* **"Glory to Thee, our God, glory to Thee. O Heavenly King..."** (*Сла́ва Тебѣ̀, Бо́же на́шъ, сла́ва Тебѣ̀. Царю̀ Небе́сный...*), *and the rest. And after the exclamation, the priest censes the Holy Gospel on the tetrapod round about, and the other icons, and the whole church, and the Superior and the brethren; and having returned the censer, stands throughout the entire office according to the ancient Typikon before the tetrapod. And the reader reads the Psalms of the Royal Hours, as in the Horologion. Then we sing the troparia by 2 (to the self-governing tone). Thereafter the Prokeimenon. Then the 1st Paremia; the Epistle to the Hebrews, pericope 303; and the Gospel from Matthew, pericope 2. Then they read:* **"Order my steps..."** (*Стопы̀ моя̀ напра́ви...*); *the Trisagion. After* **"Our Father"**: *the kontakion:* **"The Virgin today..."** (*Дѣ́ва дне́сь...*); **"Lord, have mercy"** *40 times; and the prayer:* **"Thou Who at all times..."** (*И҆́же на вся́кое вре́мя...*). *After* **"More honorable than the Cherubim"**: *the priest:* **"God be gracious unto us..."** (*Бо́же, оу҆ще́дри ны̀...*). *Then the prayer:* **"O Christ, the True Light..."** (*Христѐ, Свѣ́те...*). *Thereafter the 3rd, 6th, and 9th Hours are sung similarly. At the 9th Hour the priest censes the whole church, as at the 1st. After the prayer of the 9th Hour:* **"O Master, Lord Jesus Christ..."** (*Влады́ко Го́споди І҆исоу́се Христѐ...*); *immediately:* **"Bless the Lord, O my soul"** (*Благословѝ, душѐ моя̀, Го́спода*); **"Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit:"** **"Praise the Lord, O my soul"** (*Хвалѝ, душѐ моя̀, Го́спода*); **"Now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. *It is necessary to know: That on the feast of the Nativity of Christ [and of Theophany], on whatever day it may occur, Great Compline is sung with Litiya in this manner: At the 10th hour of the night (that is: 4 hours after midnight), the bell tolls for a single stroke*

*It is necessary to know: If the Nativity of Christ occur on a Sunday, then there is only a single Saturday and Sunday between the Nativity of Christ and Theophany; and we sing the Sunday after the Nativity on Monday on the 26th day, on which at the Divine Liturgy we read the Epistle and Gospel of the Sunday after the Nativity of Christ; and for the Saturday after the Nativity of Christ on Saturday on the Apodosis of the feast of the Nativity of Christ; thereafter for the Saturday before Theophany. ### On the 5th Day: The Eve of the Theophany of the Lord
*The order of the Royal Hours on the Eve of Theophany or Illumination takes place according to the directive; see December the 24th day, on leaf 157. After the Hours, the departure takes place until the 6th hour of the day. Vespers takes place with the Divine Liturgy of Basil the Great at its proper time. And after the Ambo Prayer, immediately we all go forth to the blessing of water into the narthex [or to the springs/rivers] of the church with the brethren. The senior priest, if there be many, bearing upon his head the Precious Cross, preceded by candle-bearers and the deacon with the censer. And he places the Precious Cross upon a decorated table, where water is also prepared in a basin with candles standing round about, and candles are distributed to the brethren. While the choirs sing the troparia, Tone 8:* **"The voice of the Lord upon the waters..."** (*Гла́съ Госпо́день на вода́хъ...*) *3 times. At that same time the Superior censes round about the table, the icons, and the people in order, preceded by the deacon with a candle. And the blessing of water takes place, as indicated in the Trebnik and Anthologion. While the ectene of blessing is said by the deacon, the priest*

[Book Page 170]

*says the prayer:* **"O Lord Jesus Christ, Only-begotten Son..."** (*Го́споди І҆исоу́се Христѐ, Е҆диноро́дный Сы́не...*) *secretly, without exclamation; and immediately begins in a loud voice, in the hearing of all, saying this prayer:* **"Great art Thou, O Lord, and marvelous are Thy works..."** (*Ве́лїй є҆сѝ, Го́споди, и҆ чꙋ́дна дѣла̀ Твоя̑...*). *(The prayer* **"O Transubstantial Trinity"** *is not found in the ancient Trebniks). After the blessing of water, the priest turning his face toward the west: holding in his left hand the Precious Cross, and in his right the basil [sprinkler], sprinkles crosswise in all directions, and all kiss the Precious Cross. At that time the troparion is sung by all many times. We enter into the church, and sing the stichera; "Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:", Tone 6:* **"Let us sing, O believers..."** (*Воспои́мъ, вѣ́рнїи...*); *then:* **"Blessed be the name of the Lord..."** *3 times; Psalm 33 [Psalm 34]. Then antidoron is distributed by the priest, and the Dismissal. After the Dismissal, troparion and kontakion in the midst of the church. And we enter into the refectory, and eat with oil.*  
*Thus the service takes place when a fast day occurs on the Eve of Illumination.*[^185]  
☞ *If the Eve of Holy Theophany occur on a Saturday or Sunday, there is no fast. The Royal Hours are sung beforehand on Friday. On the Eve (if it be on a Saturday or Sunday), the Divine Liturgy of Chrysostom takes place at its proper time according to custom, as always in order. And after the dismissal of the Divine Liturgy we do not enter into the refectory, but tasting a little refreshment, we await the evening assembly.*  
*At the evening hour of the day: the bell tolls for a single stroke and all together, and having assembled in church, we sing*

*Vespers according to custom.* **"Blessed is the man"** (*Блаже́нъ мꙋ́жъ*); *at* **"Lord, I have cried"** (*Го́споди воззва́хъ*): *we set 8 stichera, and sing stichera of the feast, Tone 2:* **"Our Enlightener..."** (*Просвѣти́теля на́шегѡ...*). *Then the Entrance with the Gospel. Prokeimenon of the day, and the readings in their order with the troparia and with their verses. [The Trisagion we do not sing, because the Divine Liturgy is not celebrated]. After the reading of the Paremias, the Little Ectene; and after the exclamation, Prokeimenon, Epistle, and Gospel as prescribed above. Ectene:* **"Let us all say..."**; **"Vouchsafe, O Lord..."**; **"Let us complete our evening prayer..."**; *the priest says the exclamation. (There are no Aposticha). And the priest acts according to the rite, as was indicated before, while we sing:* **"The voice of the Lord upon the waters..."** *And the rest of the blessing of water takes place invariably. After the sprinkling of the holy water, we enter into the church. And we sing the sticheron; "Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:", Tone 6:* **"Let us sing, O believers..."** *And the Dismissal of Vespers. And thereafter in the midst of the church, the troparion of the feast; "Glory to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen:" the kontakion. And having drunk the holy water, we enter into the refectory, and eat fully with oil.*  
☞ *Be it known: That if the Eve of Theophany occur on a Saturday or on a Sunday: the Divine Liturgy of Basil the Great is celebrated on the very feast of Theophany without Vespers. In place of "It is truly meet", we sing the Heirmos of the 9th Ode of the feast.*[^186]

### On the 6th Day: The Very Feast of Theophany
*On whatever day it may occur, Great Compline is sung after midnight in this manner: At the 10th hour of the night, the bell strikes the great bell, and thereafter all the bells. And having assembled in church, the priest having blessed and censed, we sing Great Compline with Litiya, as was indicated on December the 24th day on leaf 160. And* **"God is with us"** *with singing, in Tone 8. After the 1st Trisagion, troparion of the feast:* **"When Thou wast baptized in the Jordan..."** (*Во І҆орда́нѣ...*). *After the 2nd Trisagion, kontakion of the feast:* **"Thou hast appeared today..."** (*Ꙗ҆ви́лся є҆сѝ...*). *After "Glory to God in the highest", the Litiya takes place into the narthex of the church according to custom: while we sing the stichera of the feast, Tone 4:* **"He that clothes Himself with light as with a garment..."** (*Ѡ҆дѣва́йся свѣ́томъ, я҆́кѡ ри́зою...*). *Then after the stichera, the prayers of the Litiya:* **"Save, O God, Thy people..."** *And having entered into the church, we sing the Aposticha of the feast with its refrains. At the blessing of the loaves, troparion of the feast 3 times.* **"Blessed be the name of the Lord..."** *3 times with singing; Psalm 33 [Psalm 34]. And the reading of the feast is set forth; and after the reading immediately:* **"Glory to God in the highest..."**; *the Six Psalms, and the rest of Matins, as on all great feasts. On this day the Divine Liturgy takes place early, because of the labor of the vigil. At the Divine Liturgy, the Antiphons of the feast. Instead of the Trisagion, we sing:* **"As many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ. Alleluia"** (*Е҆ли́цы во Хрїста̀ крести́стеся, во Хрїста̀ ѡ҆блеко́стеся. А҆ллилꙋ́їа*); *and the rest.*[^187]  
☞ *On this feast the secular folk eat meat, even if it be on a Wednesday or Friday.*

### Notes

[^146]: Part III of the Doskovsky Typikon commences the Menologion (Fixed Feasts) with September 1: the Church New Year / Indiction (*Начало Индікта*), designated historically as "The Beginning of Christian Freedom" (*Начатокъ свободы христіанскія*) in commemoration of Constantine the Great's victory, celebrated alongside St. Symeon the Stylite and the Holy Women.
[^147]: The concurrence of the Patronal Feast of St. Symeon the Stylite with the Indiction on September 1: celebrated with an All-Night Vigil harmonizing the New Year propers with the Temple patron; on Sunday, the commemoration of the Holy Women is deferred by decision of the church superior.
[^148]: September 7 Forefeast of the Nativity of the Most Holy Theotokos and Martyr Sozon: when coinciding with a Sunday, the Sunday Resurrection propers harmonize with the Forefeast, integrating the designated Sunday before the Exaltation scriptural readings.
[^149]: Pericope coordination for the Nativity of the Most Holy Theotokos (September 8) falling on the Saturday or Sunday before the Exaltation of the Holy Cross: resolving liturgical clashes by transferring the Saturday before the Exaltation pericopes to September 13 (Dedication of the Resurrection) and omitting the evening Kathisma on the feast day on account of the Vigil.
[^150]: September 13 Commemoration of the Dedication of the Church of the Holy Resurrection on Golgotha (*Память Обновленія храма*): preceded by the historical prologue recalling the dedication of the Constantinian basilica in Jerusalem (A.D. 335), celebrated together with the Forefeast of the Cross and St. Cornelius the Centurion.
[^151]: The rubrical harmonization when the Dedication of the Holy Resurrection (September 13) or the Exaltation of the Cross (September 14) coincides with Sunday: prioritizing the pericopes of the Sunday before the Exaltation and Dedication, while transferring the daily pillar cycle and deferring the memory of St. Cornelius the Centurion.
[^152]: The solemn Rite of Bringing Out the Precious Cross (*Чинъ изношенія Креста*) on the Eve of the Universal Exaltation (September 14): the preliminary translation of the Cross adorned with basil from the Skeuophylakion (*сосудохранилиница*) to the Prothesis table before Vespers; followed after Little Vespers by vesting in epitrachelion and phelonion, censing, and carrying the Cross upon the priest's head into the sanctuary to be enthroned upon the Holy Table until the Great Doxology on the morrow.
[^153]: The Gospel pericope appointment at Matins of the Universal Exaltation of the Holy Cross: John §42 from the midpoint (John 12:28–36), followed immediately by *Having beheld the Resurrection of Christ*, which is chanted invariably even when the feast occurs on a weekday.
[^154]: The solemn Rite of the Elevation of the Holy Cross (*Чинъ Воздвиженія Честнаго Креста*): celebrated at the conclusion of the Great Doxology, wherein the celebrant carries the Cross upon his head into the nave, censes It upon the tetrapod, and performs five distinct elevations facing east, west, south, north, and east again, accompanied by five petitions and 500 chants of *Lord, have mercy* (a hundred at each station), bowing low until his head is a span from the floor and slowly raising the Cross aloft.
[^155]: The enthronement and veneration of the Precious Cross in the nave: following the five elevations and the chanting of *To Thy Cross we bow down in worship, O Master* (*Кресту Твоему покланяемся, Владыко*), the faithful venerate the Cross to the singing of the Idiomela of the Cross; the tetrapod bearing the Cross is then stationed on the right side of the Royal Doors throughout the entire eight days until the Apodosis.
[^156]: The strict fasting discipline appointed for the Universal Exaltation of the Holy Cross (September 14): wine and oil are permitted at the trapeza, but cheese, eggs, dairy, and meat are strictly prohibited, even when the feast occurs on a Sunday.
[^157]: The festal Antiphon refrain (*Припѣвъ на входѣ*) prescribed for the Divine Liturgy throughout the Afterfeast of the Exaltation: *"Save us, O Son of God, Who wast crucified in the flesh, who sing to Thee: Alleluia"* (*Спаси насъ, Сыне Божій, плотію распныйся, поющія Ти: Аллилуіа*), replacing the customary seasonal entrance response until the Apodosis.
[^158]: Pericope synchronization for the Saturday and Sunday after the Exaltation of the Holy Cross: coordinating 1 Corinthians §125 (1 Cor 1:26–29) and John §30 (John 8:21–30) on Saturday, and Galatians §203 (Gal 2:16–20) and Mark §37 (Mark 8:34–9:1) on Sunday, alongside the ordinary daily cycle.
[^159]: The liturgical Apodosis (*Отданіе*) of the Universal Exaltation on September 21 (commemoration of St. Quadratus of the Seventy): the festal propers are repeated for the final time; after the dismissal of the Divine Liturgy, the celebrant and deacon solemnly cense the Cross, venerate It, and the priest bears the Cross upon his head back into the sanctuary through the Royal Doors.
[^160]: The winter distribution of the Psalter in the Ruthenian Typikon: commencing on September 22 (the morrow of the Apodosis of the Cross) and continuing until December 20, the daily Matins Psalter is increased to three Kathismata on weekdays, while on Sundays two Kathismata are chanted together with the Polyeleos and the traditional Bulgarian refrains (*припѣвы болгарскіа*).
[^161]: Concurrence of the Feast of the Protection of the Most Holy Theotokos (Pokrov, October 1) with Sunday: combining the Sunday Resurrection office with the festal propers of the Protection and the Apostle Ananias, while deferring the commemoration of St. Romanus the Melodist to another day.
[^162]: Commemoration of the Holy Fathers of the Seventh Ecumenical Council (Nicaea II, A.D. 787): observed on the Sunday falling between October 11 and October 17, celebrating the definitive victory of Orthodoxy over iconoclasm and the dogmatic restoration of the veneration of the Holy Icons.
[^163]: Concurrence of the Feast of Great-Martyr Demetrius of Thessalonica (October 26) with the Commemoration of the Great Earthquake of Constantinople (A.D. 740) on Sunday: integrating the three canons (Sunday Resurrection, Earthquake, and St. Demetrius) at Matins with Katavasia *I will open my mouth*, and alternating the commemorations across the Little Hours.
[^164]: The institution of the St. Philip Fast (Pylypivka / Nativity Fast, *Филипповка*): Meatfare is permanently fixed on November 14 (Feast of the Apostle Philip), with the forty-day fast commencing on November 15; if November 14 falls on a Wednesday or Friday, Meatfare is anticipated on November 13 (St. John Chrysostom), as eating meat on Wednesday or Friday is strictly prohibited by canonical statute.
[^165]: Calendar mechanics governing the Sunday of the Holy Forefathers (*Недѣля святыхъ Праотецъ*): movable between December 11 and December 17 (falling two Sundays prior to Christmas), followed on the penultimate Sunday before Christmas by the Sunday of the Holy Fathers (*Недѣля святыхъ Отецъ*, December 18–24).
[^166]: The Gospel of the Great Banquet (Luke §76, Luke 14:16–24): permanently appointed for the Divine Liturgy on the Sunday of the Forefathers regardless of whether it falls before or after the 28th Sunday after Pentecost, with the ordinary pericope of the Sunday either transferred or combined.
[^167]: Liturgical Notice 1 (*Примѣчаніе 1*) on the coordination of the Lucan Jump and Pre-Lenten boundaries: regulating the fulfilment of unread ordinary Sunday Epistles and Gospels when the Triodion begins early, preventing omissions between the post-Theophany cycle and the Sunday of the Publican and Pharisee.
[^168]: Liturgical Notice 2 (*Примѣчаніе 2*) on the repetition and backward calculation (*отступка*) of Sunday pericopes: when Pascha is late and the interval between the 1st Sunday after Theophany and the Sunday of the Publican is extended, ordinary pericopes are borrowed backward beginning from the 31st Sunday after Pentecost to fill the liturgical vacancy.
[^169]: The structural independence of the Octoechos 8-Tone pillar cycle (*столпы*) and Sunday Matins Gospels from the movable Lucan deferrals: the eight tones and eleven resurrectional Matins Gospels continue in strict invariable succession through the 5th Sunday of Great Lent, governed by the six perpetual pillars set forth in the Horologion.
[^170]: The Paschal Boundary Key Table (*Ключи граничные*): a 35-letter algorithmic table (A–II / А҃–Ѫ҃) mapping the calendar date of Pascha (March 22 to April 25 Old Style) to the exact Sunday reading resumed after the 1st Sunday after Theophany, coordinating the Zacchaeus pericope (Luke §94) with the onset of the Lenten Triodion.
[^171]: The liturgical suspension of the Octoechos and Polyeleos during the Winter Festal Season: from December 20 (commencement of the Forefeast of the Nativity) until January 14 (Apodosis of Theophany), the daily Octoechos is suppressed on weekdays, and the Polyeleos is omitted on Sundays unless a Great Saint coincides.
[^172]: The rubrical distribution of the Royal Hours (*Царскіе Часы*) on Christmas Eve: when the Nativity of Christ falls on Sunday or Monday, the Royal Hours are anticipated on Friday morning without a Liturgy; when Christmas falls between Tuesday and Saturday, the Royal Hours are chanted on Christmas Eve (December 24) before noon.
[^173]: The ceremonial order of the Royal Hours of Christmas Eve: celebrated in the center of the nave before the tetrapod upon which the Book of the Gospels is solemnly enthroned, with continuous censing, three Psalms at each Hour, Old Testament Paremias, Apostle and Gospel readings, concluding with the Typika (*Обидниця*).
[^174]: Christmas Eve Vespers united with the Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great: celebrated in the afternoon with eight Old Testament readings, after which the festive candle-stand is placed in the center of the nave and both choirs unite to chant the Christmas Troparion (*"Thy Nativity, O Christ our God..."*) and Kontakion, followed by a strict fast meal of cooked food with oil.
[^175]: The conclusion of Christmas Eve (December 24) when the Nativity of Christ occurs on a Sunday or Monday: the Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom is celebrated in the morning at its proper time, followed by Vespers with eight Old Testament readings at the 7th hour of the day (1:00 PM), concluding with the lighting of the candle-stand, the chanting of the festal Troparion and Kontakion, and a monastic collation of boiled wheat or kutia (kolivo) prepared with honey and olive oil.
[^176]: The solemn order of Great Compline with Litiya (*Повечеріе великое съ литіею*) on the night of the Nativity of Christ: celebrated at the 10th hour of the night (4:00 AM, four hours after midnight) with the great bell and festive chiming of all bells, the priest vesting in phelonion, the solemn chanting of *God is with us* (*Съ нами Богъ*), Litiya in the narthex with the blessing of five loaves, wheat, wine, and oil, immediately followed by the Six Psalms of Matins.
[^177]: The universal fasting dispensation of the Twelve Days (*Дванадесятодневіє* / Svyatki) extending from the Nativity of Christ (December 25) to the Eve of Theophany (January 5): laity are permitted meat and monastics dairy and eggs on every day without exception, including Wednesday and Friday, by ancient canonical decree.
[^178]: The festal Introit refrain (*Припѣвъ на входѣ*) prescribed for the Divine Liturgy throughout the Afterfeast of the Nativity of Christ: *"Save us, O Son of God, Who wast born of the Virgin, who sing to Thee: Alleluia"* (*Спаси ны, Сыне Божій, рождейся отъ Дѣвы, поющія Ти: Аллилуіа*), chanted daily until the Apodosis on December 31.
[^179]: Calendar mechanics governing the Commemoration of the Holy God-ancestors (*Святыхъ Богоотецъ* — David the King, Joseph the Betrothed, and James the Brother of the Lord): when the Nativity of Christ occurs on a Sunday, the Sunday after Nativity cannot be observed on the subsequent Sunday (January 1) because the Afterfeast has concluded and January begins; therefore, the commemoration of the God-ancestors is transferred immediately to Monday, December 26 (the morrow of the Nativity).
[^180]: The liturgical order of the Sunday after the Nativity of Christ (*Недѣля по Рождествѣ Христовомъ*): combining the Sunday Resurrection office with the Afterfeast and the commemorations of the Righteous Joseph the Betrothed, David the King, and James the Brother of the Lord (*Adelphotheos*), with their troparion *Proclaim the good news, O Joseph* (*Благовѣствуй, Іосифе*).
[^181]: Synchronizing the Epistle and Gospel pericopes for the Saturdays and Sundays between the Nativity and Theophany when Christmas occurs on a Sunday: since only a single Saturday and Sunday intervene, the Sunday after Nativity is celebrated on Monday, December 26; the Saturday after Nativity on Saturday, December 31 (the Apodosis); and the Sunday before Theophany on January 1 alongside the Circumcision and St. Basil the Great.
[^182]: The complete rubrical permutations governing the post-Nativity and pre-Theophany cycle across all days of the week: detailing the distribution of Saturday and Sunday pericopes when Christmas falls on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday/Thursday/Friday, or Saturday, preventing any liturgical collision between the two Great Feasts.
[^183]: Concurrence of the patronal temple feast of St. Stephen the Protomartyr (December 27) with the Sunday after Nativity: an All-Night Vigil is celebrated, uniting the Sunday Resurrection office, the God-ancestors, and the Protomartyr, with the Polyeleos and Megalynarion of St. Stephen, Graduals of Tone 3, and Luke §52 at Matins.
[^184]: The Feast of the Circumcision of our Lord and St. Basil the Great (January 1) coinciding with the Sunday before Theophany: celebrated with an All-Night Vigil, combining the Sunday Resurrection office with the Circumcision and St. Basil, singing the Liturgy of St. Basil the Great with the megalynarion *In thee rejoices* (*О тебѣ радуется*).
[^185]: The ceremonial order of the Great Blessing of Water (*Великое водоосвященіе*) on the Eve of Theophany (January 5): celebrated in the narthex or at external water springs/rivers following the Ambo Prayer of the Divine Liturgy, with the senior priest bearing the Precious Cross upon his head, the triple singing of *The voice of the Lord upon the waters*, censing with candle-bearer, and the solemn prayer of St. Sophronius of Jerusalem (*"Great art Thou, O Lord..."*); Doskovsky notes that the later prayer *"O Transubstantial Trinity"* is absent from ancient Trebniks.
[^186]: The fasting discipline and liturgical rearrangement when the Eve of Theophany falls on a Saturday or Sunday: fasting is relaxed from strict xerophagy to cooked food with oil; the Royal Hours are anticipated on Friday without a Liturgy; the Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom is celebrated on the Eve in the morning, followed in the afternoon by solemn Vespers and the Great Blessing of Water; and the Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great is deferred to the day of the feast itself (January 6).
[^187]: The Great Feast of Holy Theophany (January 6): celebrated at 4:00 AM with Great Compline and Litiya; at the Divine Liturgy, the baptismal troparion *As many of you as have been baptized into Christ* (*Елицы во Христа крестистеся*) replaces the Trisagion; the Liturgy is celebrated early due to the labor of the vigil, and all fasts are broken, meat being permitted even if the feast falls on a Wednesday or Friday.
[^188]: The Synaxis of the Holy Prophet, Forerunner and Baptist John (January 7) and the post-Theophany Introit: the celebrant chants the special festal Antiphon response *"Save us, O Son of God, Who wast baptized in the Jordan by John, who sing to Thee: Alleluia"* (*Спаси ны, Сыне Божій, во Іорданѣ крестивыйся отъ Іоанна, поющія Ти: Аллилуіа*), retained at every Liturgy until the Apodosis of Theophany on January 14.
[^189]: Coordination of the Saturday after Theophany with the Synaxis of the Baptist (when January 7 falls on Saturday): prioritizing Ephesians §233 and Matthew §7 for the Saturday after Theophany, followed by Acts §33 and John §3 for the Forerunner, with both communions chanted.
[^190]: The integration of the post-Theophany season with the Pre-Lenten Triodion via the Paschal Boundary Keys (*Ключи граничные*): determining the precise number of Sundays after Theophany before the Septuagesima cycle commences, with the immutable rubrical decree that the Sunday of Zacchaeus (Luke §94) must invariably immediately precede the Sunday of the Publican and Pharisee.
[^191]: Concurrence of the Feast of the Three Holy Hierarchs (January 30) with Meatfare Saturday (*Суббота мясопустная*): the festal office of Sts. Basil, Gregory, and John Chrysostom is transferred to Friday, while the ancestral Commemoration of all Reposed Orthodox Christians occupies Saturday; if the temple is dedicated to the Three Hierarchs, the commemoration of the reposed is anticipated to the preceding Saturday.
[^192]: Concurrence of the Meeting of the Lord (Hypapante, February 2) with the Pre-Lenten Triodion: regulating the combination of festal propers with the Sunday of the Publican and Pharisee, Prodigal Son, Meatfare, or Cheesefare; on Cheesefare Wednesday and Friday, three great prostrations are made at the conclusion of each service, yet the Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom is celebrated; if falling on Clean Monday, the feast is anticipated on Cheesefare Sunday.
[^193]: The movable liturgical boundaries of the First and Second Finding of the Precious Head of the Baptist (February 24): roaming from Meatfare Wednesday to Tuesday of the 4th Week of Great Lent; if coinciding with Meatfare Saturday, it is transferred to Meatfare Friday; if falling on Clean Monday or any weekday of the first week of Lent, it is observed on Cheesefare Sunday or the First Saturday of Great Lent (St. Theodore the Recruit).
[^194]: Concurrence of the Finding of the Head with Monday of the 2nd, 3rd, or 4th Week of Great Lent: celebrated on Sunday evening with Great Vespers, the Great Prokeimenon, closing of the Royal Doors, three great prostrations, and the Typika chanted without a Liturgy, concluding with the bells striking for Fasting Vespers.
[^195]: Concurrence of the Finding of the Head with Tuesday or Thursday of Great Lent: celebrated without prostrations at Vespers; Great Compline without prostrations and omitting *Lord of hosts, be with us*; Doskovsky notes that ancient Greek Typika omit Old Testament Paremias and the Polyeleos on fast days.
[^196]: Concurrence of the Finding of the Head with Wednesday or Friday of Great Lent: celebrated with the Divine Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts, an Entrance with the Gospel, the solemn singing of *Let my prayer be set forth* (*Да исправится молитва моя*) accompanied by three great prostrations, and the commemoration of the Baptist at the dismissal.
[^197]: The Feast of the Holy Forty Martyrs of Sebaste (March 9): observed invariably according to the rubrical pattern established for the Finding of the Head; if it coincides with Great and Holy Thursday of the Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete (5th Week of Lent), the entire office of the Forty Martyrs is transferred forward to Tuesday of that same week.
[^198]: Concurrence of the Holy Forty Martyrs of Sebaste (March 9) with the Saturday of the Akathist (5th Saturday of Great Lent): their full festal office is transferred to the 5th Sunday of Great Lent (St. Mary of Egypt); furthermore, when the Forefeast of the Annunciation (March 24) falls on Thursday of the 5th Week of Lent (the day of the Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete), the Forefeast stichera and canon are sung beforehand on Wednesday in the daily cycle, while the Menaion saint is transferred to Compline.
[^199]: The rubrics governing the Feast of the Annunciation of the Most Holy Theotokos (March 25) when coinciding with the 3rd (Veneration of the Cross), 4th (St. John Climacus), or 5th (St. Mary of Egypt) Sunday of Great Lent: on Saturday afternoon Little Vespers is chanted according to the Triodion, followed at night by Great Vespers with Litiya and an All-Night Vigil uniting the Sunday Resurrectional and festal propers.
[^200]: Liturgical order of the Divine Liturgy when the Annunciation coincides with a Lenten Sunday: the Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great is celebrated without alteration; the festal heirmos of Ode 9, *As of an animate ark* (*Ꙗ҆́кѡ ѡ҆дꙋшевле́ннꙋ*), replaces the megalynarion *In thee rejoices* (*О тебѣ̀ ра́дꙋется*); on the 3rd Sunday of Lent (Veneration of the Cross), the hymn *Thy Cross do we adore* (*Крестꙋ̀ Твое́мꙋ*) replaces the Trisagion.
[^201]: Annunciation occurring on fasting weekdays (Tuesday through Friday) of the 4th, 5th, or 6th Week of Great Lent: if falling on Wednesday or Friday, Vespers on the Eve is celebrated without a Kathisma, including three Old Testament readings (Genesis 28:10–17, Ezekiel 43:27–44:4, and Proverbs 9:1–11), concluding with *Let my prayer be set forth*, three great prostrations, and the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts; if falling on Tuesday or Thursday (without a Presanctified Liturgy on the Eve), Great Compline with Litiya and Vigil prayers is sung before sunrise, followed by Matins and the Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom celebrated in conjunction with Vespers.
[^202]: Annunciation occurring on Saturday of the 3rd, 4th, or 5th Week of Great Lent: Friday evening Vespers is celebrated with the Liturgy of the Presanctified Gifts with five festal Old Testament readings; on Saturday before sunrise Great Compline is sung with Litiya; Matins is celebrated entirely for the feast with the Great Doxology; and the Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom is celebrated in the morning without Vespers.
[^203]: The rare concurrence of the Annunciation with Thursday of the 5th Week of Great Lent (the Great Canon): the monumental Great Canon of St. Andrew of Crete is transferred forward to Tuesday of the 5th Week, where it is combined with Tuesday's Triodion, while the Lucan Triodion of Thursday is chanted alongside the propers of the Annunciation.
[^204]: The Annunciation coinciding with Great and Holy Friday (*Kyrio-Paraskeue* / Kyrioparaskeue): on Great Thursday evening the Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great is celebrated with Vespers; Matins of the Holy Passions (the Twelve Passion Gospels) commences at the 1st hour of the night (7:00 PM or 1:00 AM depending on local monastic custom); at *God is the Lord* the festal troparion is sung three times, followed by *When the glorious disciples*; between the 7th and 8th Passion Gospels, the festal Gradual, Prokeimenon, and Gospel of the Annunciation are inserted.
[^205]: The celebration of the Divine Liturgy on Annunciation Great Friday: at the 8th hour of the day (2:00 PM), Great Friday Vespers is sung, at which the priest prepares the Holy Gifts at the Prothesis, followed by an Entrance with the Gospel, five Old Testament readings, and the Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom—constituting the sole instance in the Byzantine rite where a Eucharistic Liturgy is celebrated on Great and Holy Friday.
[^206]: The monumental concurrence of the Annunciation on Holy Pascha (*Kyriopascha*): on Great Saturday evening the Divine Liturgy of St. Basil the Great includes one Paschal and five Annunciation Paremias; early Paschal Matins includes the festal troparion and the Annunciation Gospel after Ode 6; at the Divine Liturgy of St. John Chrysostom, the Paschal Gospel (John §1) is chanted dialogically by the celebrant and deacon, followed immediately by the Annunciation Gospel (Luke §3) proclaimed by a single deacon without bell chiming.
[^207]: The feast of the Holy Great-Martyr George (April 23) across the movable Paschal cycle: roaming from Great Friday to Thursday of the 5th Week after Pascha; when coinciding with Great Friday, Great Saturday, or Pascha itself, the entire office of the Great-Martyr is transferred to Bright Monday and sung jointly with the Paschal daily office.
[^208]: The Commemoration of the Holy Fathers of the Six Ecumenical Councils (July 16): celebrated on the Sunday following July 16 (the feast of the Hieromartyr Athenogenes); if July 16 itself falls on a Sunday, the office of the Fathers is sung on that day, while the coinciding Menaion saint is transferred to Compline.
[^209]: The Procession of the Precious Wood of the Honorable and Life-Giving Cross and the Seven Maccabee Martyrs (August 1): marking the inception of the two-week Dormition Fast; before Vespers the priest enters the skevophylakion (sacristy), places the Precious Cross surrounded with fragrant basil upon a paten at the Table of Oblation (Prothesis), and elevates it to the Holy Table after Vespers.
[^210]: Ceremonial of the Lesser Blessing of Water (*Малое водоосвященіе*) on August 1: after the Great Doxology at Matins, the priest in full sacerdotal vestments elevates the Precious Cross upon his head, processes via the north door to the decorated analogion, censes the Cross, and performs the sanctification of water in the church or at exterior fountains, followed by the solemn veneration of the Cross (*Thy Cross do we adore, O Master*) and aspersion of the faithful, church, and cemetery; concluding with the evening return of the Cross to the Holy Table.
[^211]: The Translation of the Icon Not-Made-by-Hands of our Lord Jesus Christ (the Holy Mandylion or Ubrys) and St. Diomedes (August 16): celebrated within the Afterfeast of the Dormition of the Theotokos; when coinciding with Sunday, the combined office follows the typikon model of St. Demetrius and the Earthquake (October 26).
[^212]: The strict fast of the Beheading of the Holy Prophet, Forerunner and Baptist John (August 29), followed by the Title Page of Part II of the Typikon: encompassing the complete liturgical cycle of the Lenten and Paschal Triodion (*Тріодіонъ Постный и Цвѣтный*) from the Sunday of the Publican and Pharisee through Pentecost unto the Sunday of All Saints.
