## Document I: Letter of the Ruthenian Metropolitan Province of Halych to the Holy Apostolic See
### Humbly Requesting and Awaiting Permission to Convoke and Celebrate a Synod

**Most Holy Father!**

Ever since the Ruthenian people embraced holy union with the Holy Apostolic See, which was happily accomplished in the year 1595, they have celebrated in the course of 125 years only one synod, and that a provincial one in the year 1720, in the city of Zamość [^1]. That from that time onward no other synod was celebrated was due to many impeding circumstances, above all the political partition of the realm, namely the Kingdom of Poland, of which the Ruthenian principality constituted no small part. Through that partition the dioceses were likewise severed from one another and annexed to new states, namely to Russia and Austria [^2].

The Churches of the Catholic Ruthenians who stood under the dominion of Russia experienced indeed at first a semblance of certain tolerance from the rulers of Russia; nevertheless even then the Ruthenian Churches were not granted a genuine opportunity freely to develop their spiritual welfare. With time, however, even that apparent liberty was completely taken away; what is more, through the employment of diverse artifices and intrigues on the part of the schismatics, the Ruthenian Church in the aforementioned state was not only unable to celebrate any synod, but, alas! led by the most unhappy fate, fell into ruin; for, having been forcibly dragged into lamentable apostasy and compelled to draw and drink foul water from the cesspool of schism, she lost the vital power of the spirit, and, having abandoned Peter, who by eternal decree from Christ the Lord was established as the lawful Pastor of the flock of His sheep, she perished miserably.

As regards the other part of the Ruthenian Church, which Divine Providence was pleased to entrust to the solicitude of the Rulers of Austria, she, by the grace of God down to this day, ever listens to the voice of the Supreme Pastor, and follows Him whithersoever He leads, and flourishes, nourished by the wholesome food provided unto her by Him; yet notwithstanding this, neither was she in a position to celebrate synods. For the two Ruthenian dioceses situated in Hungary, on account of territorial distance, possess no hierarchical union with the Metropolitanate of Halych, but are subject to the Primate of Hungary, on which account from almost immemorial times they have had no connection with the Galician Ruthenians, especially since the state was divided, as it were, into two parts, namely into the Austrian and the Hungarian. The third diocese, that of Križevci (of the Holy Cross), situated in Croatia, which employs in its sacred ministrations the very same rite and liturgical language as the Ruthenians, is likewise not joined to the Metropolitanate of Halych, but is subject to the Archbishop of Croatia and Slavonia of the Latin rite [^3]; so that when Your Holiness happily assumed the governance of the universal Church, only two Catholic Ruthenian dioceses remained in connection with each other, and these were not in a position to hold a provincial synod.

When, however, the Most Merciful God was pleased to choose Your Holiness, Most Blessed Father, as the Pastor of His Flock, and in this manner commanded You to shine upon the horizon of His Church as a heavenly luminary, whose beneficent rays have illuminated us also, although situated upon the farthest frontiers; it is only now, when through the wise foresight of Your Holiness our Ruthenian Hierarchy has been augmented by one Bishopric, that the possibility of celebrating a synod opens before us. On the other hand, the necessity of a synod is likewise manifested on account of the diversity in the holy rites, which, by reason of the accession of a new Diocese, becomes even greater. Wherefore we, the three Hierarchs of the Dioceses of the Galician Province, having taken counsel among ourselves—although the great difficulties that must yet be overcome with respect to the expenses that the celebration of such a synod requires are not unknown to us—nevertheless, placing our hope in God, have resolved to celebrate a Provincial Synod, which is to take place in the following year in Lviv.

Before the same is canonically announced and happily accomplished, We have deemed it our duty to have recourse to that Heavenly Light which is unceasingly illuminated by the light of the Divine Spirit, the Comforter—to the Infallible Pastor teaching from the Chair of Peter, unto You, Most Holy Father, and to implore the Apostolic Blessing, which I, the humbly subscribed Metropolitan, for the Bishops, the Clergy, and the faithful Ruthenian people devoted unto You, prostrate at the feet of Your Holiness, together with my Brother Bishops, with the greatest reverence, most fervently implore.

*In Lviv, on the 5th day of December, 1888.*

Of Your Holiness the most humble, most devoted, and most obedient servant:

**SYLVESTER SEMBRATOVYCH**  
*Metropolitan of Halych, Ruthenian Archbishop of Lviv.*

## Document II: Letter of the Metropolitan of the Ruthenian Province of Halych to Cardinal Simeoni
### Prefect of the Sacred Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith, Regarding the Ruthenian Provincial Synod to Be Celebrated in Lviv

**Your Eminence!**

To the highly valued letter of Your Eminence of January 18 of the past year, No. 21, I did not wish immediately to reply, because I deemed it necessary first to confer personally with both of my fellow Bishops, which could not take place earlier than after the conclusion of the sessions of the Provincial Diet, at which I, as Deputy Marshal, was obliged constantly to be present, and at times even twice a day. Having thus discharged that duty of mine, we traveled with the Right Reverend Bishop of Stanyslaviv to Przemyśl, in order to confer with the Bishop of that city, the Right Reverend Stupnytsky. On the 28th day of January of this year, having communicated to him the letter of Your Eminence and held a consultation regarding the reply that ought to be given, we agreed that in general nothing would stand in the way—on the contrary, it would be advantageous and honorable for the Synod—if an Apostolic Delegate were present and presided over it; yet so that the speech in the Synod, both in assemblies and in sessions, should be Ruthenian, not excluding Latin in the event that anyone should feel competent properly to express himself therein [^4].

The reasons for such a resolution are precisely these two:  
First: the small number of those who might possess sufficient practice to express themselves in Latin;  
Second: to prevent any possible complaint from any quarter, as though there were not full liberty in the synodal determinations precisely because the Latin language was not sufficiently understood, or because it was not possible adequately to express oneself in the Latin language when it was impermissible to express oneself in Ruthenian.

In the event that Your Eminence should be pleased to accept that resolution of ours, which we submit to Your wise judgment, nothing further would remain than to assure Your Eminence that one of us Bishops would explain to the President representing the Holy See the motions and arguments of the members constituting the Synod, whose customary speech would be Ruthenian, adhering to the principle that the text would be written down both in Latin and in Ruthenian. And finally, as regards the person of the Apostolic Delegate, we doubt not that the Holy See will be pleased to send us one who will give no occasion unto the Ruthenians to suspect any political tendency unfavorable to their ecclesiastical and national affairs.

Relying upon all that the Holy See shall be pleased to ordain, we have the honor to inform Your Eminence that we have resolved to hold the Synod in the month of September or October, and this for the reason that we may have sufficient time to prepare the subject matters, which are so diverse and so important, and to examine the labors prepared by the various commissions, so that they may be mutually communicated and definitively resolved.

Awaiting the esteemed commands of Your Eminence, wishing every blessing from the Lord, and thanking You for Your untiring solicitude for the good of this portion of the Lord's vineyard entrusted especially unto me, I approach with reverence to kiss the sacred hands of Your Eminence and declare myself with the deepest veneration of my heart,

*In Lviv, on the 13th day of February, 1889.*

Of Your Eminence the most humble and most devoted servant:

**SYLVESTER SEMBRATOVYCH**  
*Metropolitan of Halych, Ruthenian Archbishop of Lviv.*

*To His Eminence the Most Reverend Father Giovanni Cardinal Simeoni, Prefect of the S. Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith for the Oriental Rite, etc., in Rome.*

## Document III: Letter of Cardinal Giovanni Simeoni to the Ruthenian Metropolitan of Halych
### Informing Him of the Blessing of the Holy Father and the Appointment of Archbishop Agostino Ciasca

*Rome, the 10th day of August, 1891.*

**Your Grace, Most Reverend Father!**

Your Grace informed me by your valued letter of February 13, 1889, that you had agreed with your colleagues that the presence and presidency of an Apostolic Delegate would not be an obstacle to the Provincial Synod, but on the contrary would be advantageous and bring honor, and expressed the desire that the Holy See would be pleased to send a person who would give no occasion unto the Ruthenians to suspect any political tendency unfavorable to their ecclesiastical and national affairs.

Now it rejoices me that I can inform you that the Holy Father received this resolution with pleasure, and was pleased to appoint to the honorable office of President of the same Synod Monsignor, the Most Reverend Father Agostino Ciasca of the Order of Augustinians, Archbishop of Larissa [^5]. The said Prelate has for a long time been a Consultor of this Sacred Congregation, unto which he has rendered important services, both in the capacity of President of the Papal Interpreters of the same Congregation for Oriental languages, in which he is exceedingly experienced, and in commissions of great weight which were repeatedly entrusted unto him, and which he discharged with great knowledge and prudence. For these and other illustrious services of his, the Holy Father elevated him to the important position of Prefect of the Archives of the Holy See. It is pleasing to me, therefore, to assure you that he will respond completely to the confidence which the Holy Father has reposed in him, and will fulfill his office to the satisfaction of the Ruthenian Episcopate.

The aforementioned Most Reverend Father intends to depart at the beginning of the following month of September, in order to be on site at a suitable time for the purpose of conferring with Your Grace regarding the mission that has been entrusted unto him. He will take with him one priest as his private Secretary, and one servant. Since the Sacred Congregation assumes upon itself the expenses necessary for their maintenance, I therefore request you to inform me whether you will be in a position to procure for them suitable lodgings in the local Seminary, or in some other building.

It is likewise pleasing to me to inform you that the Holy Father gladly imparts the requested blessing upon the opening of the Synod and upon the happy progress of the synodal labors. Finally, I trust that by the opening of the Synod the new Bishop of Stanyslaviv will already have been appointed, and for the accomplishment of this His Eminence the Cardinal Secretary of State is making preparations. Furthermore, I pray the Lord that He may grant every blessing.

Of Your Grace the most sincerely devoted servant:

**GIOVANNI CARDINAL SIMEONI, Prefect**  
**ANDREA, Archbishop of Achrida, Secretary**

*To the Most Reverend and Honored Sylvester Sembratovych, Ruthenian Archbishop in Lviv.*

## Document IV: Announcement of the Convocation of the Ruthenian Provincial Synod
### Solemn Pastoral Letter of Metropolitan Sylvester Sembratovych

**SYLVESTER SEMBRATOVYCH,**  
*By the Grace of God and the Favor of the Holy Apostolic See, Metropolitan of Halych, Archbishop of Lviv, Bishop of Kamianets-Podilskyi, Privy Councillor of His Imperial and Royal Apostolic Majesty, Member of the House of Lords of the Austrian Imperial Parliament, Vice-Marshal and Member of the Diet of the Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria with the Grand Duchy of Cracow, Doctor of Sacred Theology, etc.*

*To the Most Reverend and God-beloved Ruthenian Bishops of this our Galician Province united with the Holy Apostolic Roman See, to the Most Reverend Chapters, Metropolitan in Lviv and Episcopal in Przemyśl and Stanyslaviv, and to all the Reverend and Venerable Secular and Religious Clergy of this our Province:*  
**Peace in the Lord and Our Metropolitan Blessing!**

**Beloved in Christ!**

### Theological Foundation
The highest good of man, unto which he is bound to strive with all his powers, consists in the attainment of the ultimate end destined for him by God. Above this there is nothing greater and nothing more important: *"For one thing is necessary,"* saith the Savior unto Martha, the sister of Mary (Luke 10:42): the salvation of one's soul. And indeed: *"For what shall it profit a man,"* saith the same Divine Savior of ours in another place, *"if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul? Or what shall a man give in exchange for his soul?"* (Mark 8:36–37).

And when the entire human race through the sin of our first parents turned away from God, wandered from the right way, and fell into the abyss of eternal perdition, God, in His ineffable love for mankind, had mercy upon the unhappy condition of man, that most illustrious creation, the work of His all-powerful hands, and in a wondrous manner surpassing all human comprehension, prepared anew salvation for him, and that through the great mystery of the Incarnation of His Only-Begotten Son. For, prizing highly the salvation of man, He so loved him that He hesitated not to surrender even His own Son for him: *"For God so loved the world, as to give His Only-Begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in Him should not perish, but may have life everlasting,"* declareth the same Savior Himself (John 3:16). And He delivered Him over to toils, poverty, dishonor, suffering, and torments, even unto death upon the Cross. O truly! great is the mercy of God, great is His love toward the human race!

And how highly the Son of God Himself valued the salvation of human souls is demonstrated from this, that He, as St. Paul the Apostle saith to the Philippians (2:6–8): *"Who being in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God: but emptied Himself, taking the form of a servant, being made in the likeness of men, and in habit found as a man; He humbled Himself, becoming obedient unto death, even to the death of the Cross."* For the obtaining of eternal salvation for us, He willingly shed His most precious blood and gave His life: *"I lay down My life for the sheep... No man taketh it away from Me: but I lay it down of Myself,"* saith the same Only-Begotten Son of God the Father (John 10:17, 18).

And after His glorious Resurrection, when He was about to ascend unto His Heavenly Father, He manifested that great love of His for His sheep and solicitude for their eternal salvation also in this, that, as the great Doctor of the Church St. John Chrysostom observeth: passing over the other Apostles, He committed the pasturing of His sheep unto that one among them who was the most eminent and who loved Him more than the rest, and consequently loved His sheep also (Vol. 87 and *On the Priesthood*, Book 2, ch. 1): *"Jesus saith to Simon Peter: Simon, son of John, lovest thou Me more than these?... Feed My sheep... Feed My lambs"* (John 21:15–18).

The election of the Apostles, the establishment of St. Peter as Supreme Pastor, the institution of the Holy Mystery of the Priesthood, the commission given to the Apostles and their successors to preach, baptize, and administer the Holy Mysteries, is a clear witness of the fervent love and solicitude of Christ for men, and a luminous proof of how highly He valued the eternal salvation of their souls: *"Going into the world, preach the Gospel to every creature. He that believeth and is baptized shall be saved"* (Mark 16:15).

### Apostolic Succession & Synodal Tradition
Now Christ instituted the Church of the New Testament, and willed that this same Church, founded by Him for the purpose of preparing eternal salvation for men, should not be bound to one single place or to one single nation, as was the church of the Old Testament, but that she should spread throughout the whole world and encompass all nations; wherefore also that power of governance which He bequeathed unto the Bishops in order further to carry on the work of salvation, He did not restrict to the single person of Peter and his successors (as was the case in the Old Testament, when the power of governance was tied to the person of Aaron and his successors), but distributed the same likewise among the other Apostles and their successors the Bishops; and they, together with St. Peter and under the primacy of St. Peter and his Successors, care, both of themselves and with the assistance of the lower ministers, the priests, called by them, for the salvation of souls; and thus they cooperate with God Himself in that work which, among all divine works, is the most sublime work of God.

And although each individual pastor of souls is bound himself to answer for the sheep committed unto him, nevertheless, because this work is of exceeding great weight—so that, as St. John Chrysostom affirmeth, Christ would not have hesitated to undergo death for the salvation of even a single soul—therefore all chief Pastors endeavor to summon unto their counsel and assistance other lower fellow laborers also, who in spiritual matters are more experienced and above all solicitous for the salvation of souls, so that by every possible means they may secure their salvation, and lest at any time the Lord should require their blood at their hands. And if in matters of greater weight, even temporal ones, whether public or private—especially when it concerneth the preservation of the integrity of the state or the health of the body—men are accustomed to call into common counsel those who are most skilled in art, wisdom, or statecraft; far more just is it that such consultations should take place where the question concerneth the attainment of the highest good and the averting of the greatest evil for man, and what is more, for all mankind.

Common consultations of this kind among churchmen assembled in one place, termed Councils or Synods, have been held, beginning from the Apostles of Christ themselves, very frequently in the Christian Church, and have always been regarded as one of the most suitable means for acquiring the necessary knowledge for the proper and successful guidance of souls unto eternal salvation, and for gaining skill in steering the ecclesiastical ship, so as to conduct those committed to their charge amidst the perilous waves of the agitated sea of this world unto the tranquil haven of heaven.

### Historical Succession of Ruthenian Synods
And thus the Most Holy Ecumenical Pontiffs, the Popes of Rome, as Supreme Pastors of the entire Church of Christ, in the course of nineteen centuries have held more or less nineteen Councils, called Ecumenical Councils. We have likewise a great number of assemblies held by the presiding Hierarchs of Provinces, which are called Provincial Councils or Synods, and a multitude of assemblies celebrated by individual Bishops of dioceses, which are called Diocesan or Eparchial Synods. And our Ruthenian Church also, following the example of the same Catholic Church—from which she received the fruitful branch that, engrafted upon her healthy trunk, immediately took root and grew into a great fruitful tree, bearing many fruits of faith and splendid virtues pleasing unto God—animated by that same spirit of zeal for the salvation of souls, in order to manifest her vitality, celebrated many Provincial Councils, of which the most notable were the following:
* That of Volodymyr, called also of Kyiv, convoked by Metropolitan Cyril in the year 1274 [^6];
* That of Kobryn, held after the conclusion of the Union of the Ruthenian Bishops with the Holy Apostolic See of Rome, under the leadership of Metropolitan Rutsky of blessed memory in the year 1626, confirmed by Pope Urban VIII of blessed memory [^7];
* And the last one, appointed for Lviv, but on account of the plague celebrated in the city of Zamość under Metropolitan Leo Kishka, under the presidency of the Apostolic Nuncio, Archbishop Girolamo Grimaldi, in the year 1720, confirmed by His Holiness Pope Benedict XIII in the year 1724.

Yet although Holy Catholic Church, seeing and recognizing the great benefits that Councils customarily bring, commandeth by decree of the Council of Trent that the same be celebrated in every ecclesiastical province at least every three years (Sess. 24, ch. 2 *de Reformatione*); and according to the prescription of our Council of Kobryn, at least every fourth year (see Kulczyński, *Specimen Ecclesiae Ruthenicae*); nevertheless in our Ruthenian Church 171 years have already elapsed since the last Synod of Zamość, in which time not a single Provincial Synod has been celebrated, and that on account of circumstances, partly political and partly religious, unfavorable to the well-being of the Church of our Ruthenian people. Namely: on account of the hostile persecution of holy Union with the Catholic Church, there was no Provincial Synod in the Province of Kyiv, and that persecution, alas! had as its consequence the destruction of the holy Union, at least externally, in the Russian realm. As for our Galician Province, the chief cause for the non-celebration of Synods was especially the lack of the requisite number of eparchial Bishops.

### Imperial Patronage & Creation of Stanyslaviv
Now, however, since on the one hand our Ruthenian Church in the Galician Province, remaining under the paternal sceptre of the magnanimous and pious successor of the devout Rudolf of Habsburg, His Majesty our God-preserved Emperor Franz Joseph I, the most illustrious Protector of the holy Catholic faith, has recovered full freedom to profess her holy faith and cherish her native Ruthenian rite, as also autonomous governance; and since on the other hand the Great Pope Leo XIII, that universal Hierarch who loveth our Ruthenian people and is exceedingly solicitous for the welfare of our Ruthenian Church and of our rite, in agreement with and after magnanimous and generous endowment by the same most gracious Emperor gloriously reigning over us, ordained the creation of the Diocese of Stanyslaviv and was pleased to establish a third Bishop in our Province, whereby every hindrance to the celebration of Synods was removed:

Therefore We, whom it pleased God according to His unsearchable counsels to choose as Ruler of the Metropolitan Archprimary Diocese of Lviv, and thereby to place upon the heights for the watch of this ecclesiastical Province—conscious in the full measure of that great calling of ours, deeply convinced of the importance of that task which God was pleased to lay upon Us, though His least servant, and conscious likewise that of the proper fulfillment of that obligation an account will one day especially be required of Us—having frequently and diligently meditated upon all that is set forth above, resolved to make known unto the Holy Apostolic See our conceived intention and mature counsel to celebrate a Provincial Synod in our Archcathedral church, and to submit the same in humility for approval.

And having received the most favorable approval of that intention of ours, as also the approval of the Program of the Ordo to be observed in the celebration of the Synod, by letter issued by His Eminence the Cardinal Prefect of the Sacred Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith for the Oriental Rites, Giovanni Simeoni, under date of July 4 of the current year, No. 337/14: We hasten unto you with that joyful tidings, and by this our present Encyclical We announce the Provincial Synod to be celebrated in Lviv; and appointing the first solemn and public session thereof for the 27th (15th) day of September of the current year, that is, on the 14th Sunday after the Descent of the Holy Spirit, which is at the same time the Sunday after the Exaltation of the Precious Cross—before which day there will take place also the 1st Preparatory General Congregation on Thursday morning, September 24 (12)—We invite in the Lord, by our Metropolitan authority, unto that same Synod:

### Formal Synodal Summons
Above all, you, the Most Reverend Bishops and spiritual Rulers of the Ruthenian Eparchies of Przemyśl and Stanyslaviv, as our fellow laborers in pastoring Christ's flock in this Province, **with decisive voice** (*голосъ рѣшаючий* / *votum deliberativum*) [^8];

The Most Reverend and Very Reverend Chapters: our Metropolitan Chapter of Lviv and Episcopal Chapters of Przemyśl and Stanyslaviv; the Very Reverend honorary Canons of the Metropolitan Consistory; the Very Reverend Father Protohegumen with the Reverend Hegumens of the Monasteries of the Order of Saint Basil the Great situated within the territory of our Archeparchy of Lviv; the Most Distinguished Reverend Priest-Professors of the University of Lviv of our Ruthenian rite; Doctors of Sacred Theology belonging to the Clergy of the Archeparchy of Lviv; the Most Reverend Superiors of our Seminaries, General of Lviv and Central of Vienna; the Very Reverend Fathers Deans and Administrators of Deaneries of our Archeparchy of Lviv; and those of the Venerable Clergy of our Archeparchy who shall receive a summons from Us by special letters: **with consultative voice** (*голосъ совѣтуючий* / *votum consultivum*).

Finally, following the custom of our preceding Synods, We invite also the Highly Honored Senior of our praiseworthy Ruthenian Stauropegion Institute, the Most Illustrious Doctor and Professor of the University of Lviv, Isidore Sharanevych, as **witness and hearer** (*свѣдокъ и слушатель* / *auditor et testis*) of this our Provincial Synod [^9].

We implore you, at the same time, Most Reverend Brethren in Christ, Bishops and spiritual Rulers of the Dioceses of Przemyśl and Stanyslaviv, that you be pleased to summon unto the same our Provincial Synod on your part—besides your Most Reverend Episcopal Chapters, likewise invited by Us through this our present Metropolitan Encyclical—the Very Reverend Fathers Deans, the Reverend Fathers Hegumens of Monasteries situated within the territory of your Dioceses, and other most distinguished and experienced churchmen according to your pleasure; so that We, assembled in Christ and with Christ in our midst, with the luminous and conscientious counsel of those our fellow laborers, as spiritual Rulers of this Province, may be able to decide and enact at this our Synod that which may serve unto the honor and greater praise of the One God in the Trinity, unto the glory of our Holy Catholic Church, unto the elevation and progress of our native Ruthenian rite, and unto the welfare, benefit, and salvation of our people committed by God unto Us.

### Mandatory Prayer Rule & Bell-Ringing Rubrics
Yet remembering withal that *"every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights"* (James 1:17), and that we cannot even think anything saving without the grace of the Holy Spirit: We implore you, Most Reverend Brethren, and beseech you that you yourselves in your prayers offer unto God humble petitions for the good success of this our Synod, as also that you be pleased to call upon your subject Very Reverend Eparchial Clergy and all the faithful diocesans committed unto your solicitude, to join in common prayer for the intention of the good success of this our Synod; even as I, by this my present Encyclical, ordain the same in my Metropolitan Archeparchy, calling in the Lord upon the Very Reverend Archeparchial Clergy:

That, beginning from the first nearest Sunday or from the first Feast Day after the reception of this Encyclical, until the conclusion of this our Provincial Synod, for the intention of imploring for Us divine light and grace for our common consultations and our determinations in this Synod, they recite on every Sunday and every Feast Day at the Divine Liturgy after the Ambo Prayer together with their faithful parishioners—whom they shall previously instruct in their sermons concerning the importance of the Synod—for the above-mentioned intention:
* **3 times the *Our Father***;
* adding **3 times *Rejoice, Mother of God, Virgin***, to invoke the intercession of the Most Holy and Immaculately Conceived Virgin Mary, the Mother of God, unto whose Protection (*Pokrov*) We commit this our Provincial Synod;
* and **1 time the *Creed*** (*Вѣрую*).

Moreover, We ordain that in all churches, at least parish ones, on the 14th, 15th, and 16th Sundays after the Descent of the Holy Spirit, on which Sundays the solemn sessions of our Synod are to take place, as also on Tuesday, October 1 (13), on the Feast of the Protection of the Most Holy Virgin Mother of God, at the conclusion of our Synod, the bells be rung for the duration of approximately fifteen minutes, and in the churches of Lviv for half an hour.

We append also unto this Encyclical the Program of the Ordo of the Synod to be celebrated, one copy for each Parish.

### Papal Confirmation & Appointment of Archbishop Ciasca
**Most Reverend Bishops, Very Reverend Fathers, and Brethren in Christ!**

At this last moment, as We were concluding the printing of our Encyclical, We received that joyful communication which, by means of a dispatch of His Eminence Giovanni Cardinal Simeoni, Prefect of the S. Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith, under date of August 10 of the current year, No. 368, was communicated unto Us: that His Holiness Pope Leo XIII was graciously pleased to impart, for the inception and prosperous conduct and success of the synodal labors, His Apostolic Blessing; and at the same time, to confer greater honor and importance upon this our Synod, to appoint as His Delegate and President:

His Excellency the Most Reverend **Agostino Ciasca**, titular Archbishop of Larissa, Prefect of the Vatican Archives, Member and Consultor of the Sacred Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith.

Hastening therefore to share this tidings with you, let us render for this honor, glory, and thanksgiving unto the Lord, and let our hearts be filled with unquenchable gratitude toward His Holiness Pope Leo XIII.

May God prosper Us! May the Queen of the Heavens protect Us!

*The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God the Father, and the communion of the Holy Spirit be with you all, Brethren! Amen.*

*In Lviv, on the feast of the Holy Great Prince Vladimir, Equal-to-the-Apostles, July 15 (27), 1891.*

**SYLVESTER,**  
*Metropolitan of Halych, Archbishop of Lviv.*

## Document V: Ordo to Be Observed in the Convocation and Celebration of the Synod
### Directorium of Actions and Ceremonies in the Month of September, 1891

#### Section I: Reports to the Holy See
a) Letters to Rome, in which permission is requested for the convocation and celebration of the Synod, as also the Apostolic Blessing and indulgences for the faithful, from His Holiness, through His Eminence the Cardinal Prefect of the Sacred Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith for the Oriental Rites.  
b) Submission of the program to His Eminence the Cardinal Prefect regarding the order and method in which the Ruthenian Provincial Synod in Lviv is to be celebrated; especially the submission of the Ceremonial which the Ruthenian Bishops have resolved to observe, whether in preparatory assemblies, or in public or solemn sessions, as also at the inception and conclusion of the Synod.

#### Section II: Actions Pertaining to Synodal Convocation
Having obtained permission and blessing from the Holy Apostolic See for the celebration of the Synod, there was established and approved by common consent of the Bishops of the Galician Ruthenian Province, at a meeting of the Bishops of the same Province held in Lviv on the 18th day of February of the year 1891, the following rule (program) to be observed at the celebration of the Synod:

**1) The Metropolitan shall announce:**  
A pastoral proclamation announcing the Provincial Synod, addressed to:
* The Most Reverend and Honored Brother Bishops;
* The Most Distinguished and Very Reverend Chapters: Metropolitan and Episcopal of Przemyśl and Stanyslaviv;
* Archimandrites and Hegumens of Monasteries of the Order of St. Basil the Great;
* Other ecclesiastical dignitaries who, whether by right or custom, are bound to be present;
* And also to the Honored Senior of the renowned ecclesiastical-civic Stauropegion Institute.

#### Preparatory General Congregation

In the chapel of the Metropolitan Palace before the Holy Table (*Prestol*), upon which the emblem of the Cross must stand in the midst of six candles and the Book of the Holy Gospels, there shall be prepared a table covered with green cloth for the seating of the members summoned to the Synod [^10]. ### Instruction
#### For Officials at the Provincial Synod, Issued by the Most Reverend Metropolitan of Lviv, 1891

**a) Duties of the Synodal Judges.**  
The duties of the Synodal Judges in the present times comprise the following points:  
1) It shall belong to the Synodal Judges to examine the credentials of proxies and to demand from participants of the Synod the letters on the basis of which they have been summoned as theologians to the present Synod.  
2) They shall verify the excuses both of those who were duly summoned to the Synod, but being detained by a just impediment, did not appear, and of those who, on account of some unforeseen occurrence, shall submit a written petition to the President of this Court requesting to be dismissed in the Lord before the conclusion of the Synod. — Having received permission to depart, they shall return to the President of the Court the outline of synodal subjects delivered unto them.  
3) Likewise, to resolve doubts concerning the right of attendance or precedence and other difficulties arising among members of the Synod, and that, as Benedict XIV says: "by summary process" (*въ короткôй дорозѣ* / *via summaria*).

4) The Judges shall watch over the preservation of secrecy and with discretion admonish those who fail to observe it.  
5) It shall be the duty of the President to ensure that the proceedings of the Judges are recorded in a separate book, commonly called the "Protocol," and that upon the conclusion of the Synod a report of the proceedings be presented unto the Metropolitan.

**b) Duties of the Promoters of the Synod.**  
1) It shall be the duty of the Promoters to insist in a proper manner that the agenda for the General Congregations and particular Sessions be prepared and well ordered in due time.  
2) It belongs unto them to see to it that whatever pertains to the form of synodal proceedings, especially that employed at public Sessions, be carried out with the observance both of the order of the Divine Liturgy and the custom of ecclesiastical tradition.  
3) They shall fulfill ceremonial duties by turns at Congregations and Sessions, and they shall likewise read aloud, alternately with the Secretaries of the Synod, the contents of the Decrees to be promulgated at the Sessions.  
4) It shall also be their duty to collect both the minutes of synodal proceedings recorded by the individual Secretaries and those copies of decrees which, as authentic instruments, contain the promulgated synodal matter.  
5) Finally, upon the conclusion of the Synod, they shall submit all records of proceedings together with a corresponding report unto the Metropolitan.

**c) Duties of the Secretaries of the Synod.**  
1) It shall be the duty of the Secretaries to ensure that at least one day before public Sessions the announcement of the Session to be held be posted upon the doors both of the Metropolitan Church and of the city church under the title of the Dormition of the Most Holy Virgin Mary.  
2) They shall promulgate in turn the Decrees belonging to the program of the Synod, and read aloud by turns with the Promoters and others appointed from among the Notaries or otherwise assigned unto such reading, the matters to be announced at the Congregations and Sessions.

**d) Duties of the Notaries of the Synod.**  
1) It shall be the duty of the Notaries to draw up the minutes of proceedings that shall be transacted at the individual sessions of particular Congregations. These minutes, having been read aloud if the President of the Congregation so directs, they shall present unto the President in due time for signature and safekeeping.  
2) Whatever the President of the Congregation shall direct concerning the manner of conducting proceedings, they shall make known unto the other members of that Congregation.  
3) To present unto the President the excuses of those who on one or two occasions could not attend a particular Session on account of a just impediment.  
4) Upon the conclusion of the proceedings to be transacted in particular Congregations, they shall collect the lithographed manuscripts of the synodal subject matter, copies of which are distributed at the first particular Session to individual members; and upon the conclusion of the Synod they shall present them unto the Promoters.  
5) That Notary who, in addition, shall hold the office of Notary of Synodal Acts, shall likewise prepare the protocol of proceedings transacted at General Congregations and public Sessions, and shall publish the contents of those protocols at the end of the Congregations and Sessions. These protocols he shall present unto the Metropolitan for signature, and, once signed, deliver them unto the Promoters for safekeeping.

**e) Duties of the Masters of Ceremonies and their Director.**  
It shall be the duty of the Masters of Ceremonies to oversee that at General Congregations and public celebrations everything is conducted in accordance with the rules to be observed in the celebration of the Synod.

*Given in Lviv, on the date as in the proclamation of the Synod, 1891.*

**Sylvester Sembratovych, m. p.**  
*Archbishop-Metropolitan.*

### 1. Decree on the Opening of the Synod
In the name of the Holy, Consubstantial, Life-Creating, and Undivided Trinity, Father, and Son, and Holy Spirit.  
We, Sylvester Sembratovych, by the grace of God and of the Apostolic See Metropolitan of Halych, Archbishop of Lviv, Bishop of Kamianets; unto the praise and glory of Almighty God; in honor of the Most Blessed and Immaculate Ever-Virgin Mary Theotokos; of St. Joseph, Spouse of the same Queen of Heaven; of St. Michael the Archangel; of our Holy Apostles Cyril and Methodius; of the Holy Hieromartyr Josaphat; of the holy and glorious God-crowned Ruthenian princes Volodymyr and Olha; of the holy Great-Martyr George and of all the Saints; as well as unto the glory of Holy Mother Catholic Church and the increase of holy Union and communion with the Holy Apostolic See; by the counsel and agreement of the Most Reverend and Right Reverend Provincial Bishops, — decree and determine that on the 15th (27th N.S.) day of September, on the Sunday after the Exaltation of the Precious Cross, the Provincial Synod shall begin, which by Our Metropolitan authority We have announced, proclaimed, and promulgated.  
May the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies and God of all comfort [^13] look down to our aid, and grant that we may sense the presence in our midst of the same Jesus Christ, His Only-Begotten Son, in Whose most holy Name we have assembled; and may the Holy Spirit, proceeding from the Father and the Son, enlighten our mind with the light of His grace and inflame our hearts. Amen.

### 2. Decree on the Making of the Profession of Faith
In the name of the Holy, Consubstantial, Life-Creating, and Undivided Trinity, etc.  
We, Sylvester Sembratovych, by the grace of God... etc. *(as above).*  
Whereas faith, without which it is impossible to please God, is the beginning and foundation of salvation; and whereas it is likewise one of the first and principal duties of Our Episcopal solicitude vigilantly to strive that the Catholic faith, held by the Holy Roman Church, may ever flourish unblemished and whole in this province: We deem that this Provincial Synod ought to begin with a unanimous profession of the faith which We believe in Our heart. Following therein the instructions and ordinances of the Holy Apostolic Roman See and the decrees issued for the faithful of the Oriental rite, as well as the enactments of our last Ruthenian Provincial Synod celebrated in the city of Zamość in the year 1720: We decree that We, the Most Reverend Bishops, and others seated in this Synod, shall openly and publicly make profession of faith according to the form prescribed by Pope Urban VIII of blessed memory for the faithful of the Oriental rite, and that according to the Slavonic text diligently reviewed and corrected here by the Metropolitan Ordinariate, and approved by the Sacred Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith by letter of June 21, 1890, No. 846/14.

### 3. Decree on the Manner of Life during the Time of the Synod
In the name of the Holy, Consubstantial, Life-Creating, and Undivided Trinity... etc.  
We, Sylvester Sembratovych... Metropolitan... etc. *(as above).*  
Whereas We are conscious that Our powers are insufficient for so momentous an obligation of the Metropolitan; and conscious further that such is the infirmity of human nature that every sufficiency, of whatever kind it be, proceeds from the Father of lights: We, in celebrating this Provincial Synod, relying not upon Our own powers, but depending upon the assistance and help of the One God, Whose servants We are, implore Him with all Our heart in humility that He may sustain Us, govern Us, and incline Our mind toward that which is best and most salvific for Our province. We beseech the Most Reverend Bishops, Our Brethren, who have assembled with Us in our Lord Jesus Christ and the grace of the Holy Spirit, that all the resources of piety and the full abundance of their episcopal...

...virtues with which they are endowed, they might unceasingly direct unto the spiritual benefit of the province. Let them likewise strive, especially by salvific counsels and holy prayers, to assist the synodal work which We, by the cooperation of the Holy Spirit, have begun this day. The Honorable Dignitaries, Canons, and all Priests who have assembled with Us, and all other Ecclesiastical Men We admonish and adjure by the mercy of God, that, as at all times they shine before layfolk by the light of their virtues, so especially during the celebration of this Synod they conduct themselves in a manner pleasing unto God, mindful of their office, and live holily, as befitteth those who are called unto the service of the Lord; likewise that by the most holy Sacrifice of the Divine Liturgy they devoutly assist the work of this Synod, and with pure prayers implore God, the Giver of all good things, to grant unto Our minds the light of His grace, that We may adopt the best counsels and decree salvific enactments. — All and each of the citizens and inhabitants of the city of Lviv, beloved unto Us in Christ, We admonish and summon in the Lord, that, as always, so especially during this Synod, they elevate their spirit unto zeal for Christian virtues, that they entirely abstain from evil, turn away from carnal lusts, apply themselves unto prayer, feed Christ the Son of God in the poor, confess their sins, fortify themselves with the Bread of Angels, and daily with devout prayers implore the God of mercies, that We, in celebrating this Synod and in every pastoral undertaking, may serve the glory of God and the salvation of souls with reverent solicitude [^14].

### 4. Decree on the Non-Infringement of the Rights of Others
In the name of the Holy, Consubstantial Trinity... etc. We, S. S., Metropolitan, etc. *(as above),* decree and declare that if it should happen by chance that anyone, not possessing the right, were present in this Synod, or, possessing the right, were absent; or that anyone should not sit in the seat belonging unto him, or, without observing the order, should take the floor, or perform any other actions during the Synod: no one may infer therefrom that anyone's rights acquired outside the Synod are thereby infringed, just as similarly none of them acquires any new right and nothing is taken away from anyone...

...of his rights or possession, but everything remains valid and inviolable in the very same state in which it previously stood before this Provincial Synod commenced.

### 5. Decree That It Is Not Permitted to Withdraw from the Synod
We, S. S., Metropolitan, etc. *(as above).* We implore and admonish the Most Reverend Bishops who are obliged to sit in this Provincial Synod that, until the same be concluded and until it be dismissed according to custom, they depart not from the city of Lviv without just cause. If, however, any Bishop, for a verified cause approved by Us, should receive permission from Us to depart, he is bound to leave a suitable proxy, who shall accept in his name and that of his Church the decisions and decrees of the Synod and subscribe the same, as is the custom. Furthermore, all others sitting together with Us in the Synod We admonish and command that they do not depart before the conclusion of the Synod without a cause acknowledged by the Judges of Excuses and approved by Us [^15].

## First Public and Solemn Session
*At the appointed day and hour, all belonging to the Synod shall appear in the Metropolitan Palace; while all others of the clergy and people who are able to attend the solemnity, as well as the processions from both the city and neighboring parish churches, shall wait on the square between the Church and the palace until the Masters of Ceremonies of the Synod give the signal for the procession to the Metropolitan Church to begin.*

*At the signal given by the Masters of Ceremonies and upon the tolling of all the bells of the city, the procession shall set out toward the Metropolitan Church in the following order:*  
*The Cross of the Archeparchial Cathedral Church shall be borne by one of the senior members of the brotherhood of the same Church.*  
*The Cantors of the Archeparchial Cathedral Church.*  
*The suburban parish processions with their Crosses and banners, arriving with their pastors.*  
*The processions of the city parishes, namely: of the Holy Apostles Peter and Paul, of St. Paraskeva, of the Dormition of the Most Holy Virgin Mary, and then of the Archeparchial Cathedral of the Holy Great-Martyr George with their brotherhoods, cross, and banners, under the leadership of their pastors or assistant clergy.*  
*The Cross of the Metropolitan Chapter between two candle-bearers.*  
*The clergy of the Metropolitan Church.*  
*The Master of Ceremonies with the ministrants for the Hierarchical Divine Liturgy.*  
*The Metropolitan Cross borne by a Chapter Canon between two candle-bearers.*  
*Priests invited to the Synod.*  
*Deans who hold no specific office in the Synod.*  
*Theologians and Canonists of the Synod.*  
*Notaries and Secretaries of the Synod.*  
*Synodal Promoters.*  
*Procurators or proxies of the Episcopal Chapters and Canons of those Chapters.*  
*The Metropolitan Chapter.*  
*The Provincial Bishops with their chaplains and assistants.*  
*The Metropolitan and the Most Reverend Apostolic Delegate with their assistants.*  
*Note: The priests, concelebrants, canons, and Most Reverend Bishops with the Metropolitan shall be vested as is customary at similar ecclesiastical solemnities when a Hierarchical Divine Liturgy is to be celebrated. —*  
*Having entered the Church, the clerics and other priests who have arrived for the solemnity shall take places as convenience affords; the other concelebrants at the Divine Liturgy and Officials of the Synod shall take their assigned places in the choir proper, or presbytery; the Most Reverend Provincial Bishops shall take their places upon the seats prepared opposite the Metropolitan throne.*

*Note: This order shall be observed at all public Sessions that shall be celebrated, with this distinction only, that when one of the Provincial Bishops shall celebrate the Divine Liturgy, the seat for him shall be prepared opposite the Metropolitan throne.*  
*The Most Reverend Metropolitan, vested in all hierarchical vestments and the pallium, shall begin the solemn Divine Liturgy for the invocation of the assistance of the Holy Spirit, as found in our Ruthenian Sluzhebniks. After the reading of the Gospel, either the officiating Metropolitan himself or another person designated shall deliver a spiritual discourse unto the assembled faithful.*  
*If by chance not all the priests assembled for the Synod should be able to celebrate the Divine Liturgy on this day or concelebrate with the Metropolitan, they shall at least receive Holy Communion from the hands of the Metropolitan. Upon the conclusion of the Divine Liturgy, all who took part in the procession shall proceed to the place in the Church prepared for the sessions of the Synod, where they shall perform the devotions and prayers according to the rule indicated above for the preparatory General Congregation.*  
*Then all non-members shall depart, and only those who have been summoned to the Synod shall remain. The church doors — if it so pleases the Fathers — shall be closed, and the session shall begin with a short synodal address by the Metropolitan on the origin, utility, and importance of provincial synods.*  
*Following the address, the Promoters of the Synod shall move the opening of the Synod, more or less in these words:*  
"Right Reverend Father in Christ! Whereas we see that all who by law and custom ought to be present at this Synod have here assembled in the Lord: I, N. N., named and appointed by Your Grace through Metropolitan authority as Promoter of this Synod, move and humbly pray that Your Grace deign to issue the command to open this Synod and order the promulgation of the decree on its opening."  
*The Metropolitan answers:* "We command that Our Decree on the opening of the Synod be read." *He then hands the Decree unto the first Secretary to be read aloud from the ambo or some other prominent place.*  
*The first Secretary of the Synod receives the Decree from the Metropolitan, whose ring he kisses (as he likewise does whenever he receives anything from the hand of the Metropolitan), and reads it aloud in a clear voice...*

*...from the assigned place. — After the reading of the decree, which the Bishops hear seated, but the rest standing, the second Secretary shall approach with one of the Notaries individually unto each of the Provincial Bishops and inquire concerning their opinion, saying:* "Will Your Grace deign to declare whether you accept the decree on the opening of the Synod just read?" *Upon the reply of both:* "I accept," *the Secretary returns to the Metropolitan, and presenting unto him the reply of the Bishops, shall say:* "The Most Reverend Bishops accept the Decree." *Whereupon the Metropolitan announces:*  
"With the agreement of the Most Reverend Bishops, We decree and hereby declare the opening of the Synod, which has indeed taken place."

"Your Grace! The Decree on the manner of life during the Synod and the Decree on non-departure from the Synod; wherefore I, N. N., Promoter of this Synod, move and humbly pray Your Grace that those decrees be read aloud and promulgated."  
*The Metropolitan commands the reading of the decrees in the words:* "This We decree and command."

Are those decrees that have been read pleasing unto you?" *The reply of the Bishops:* "They are pleasing," *he presents unto the Metropolitan, who says:* "The decrees are pleasing unto the Most Reverend Fathers the Bishops; wherefore thanksgiving is due unto God."

"Your Grace! Whereas for the conduct of the Synod's affairs it is necessary to establish Judges who should have the duty to hear and adjudicate complaints, excuses, and misunderstandings, should any arise, and submit them unto the Most Reverend Bishops for decision: I, N. N., Promoter of this Provincial Synod, move and pray that Your Grace appoint such Judges and order the decree on their appointment to be promulgated."  
*The Metropolitan answers:* "This We decree and command." *The first Secretary of the Synod receives the decree from the Metropolitan, and reads it aloud in the manner indicated above.*  
*Finally, the Promoter moves the appointment of the second Session in the words:* "Your Grace! At provincial synods it was always the custom that at the first session the day and place of the second session was appointed. Wherefore I move that the same practice be observed now in this Synod."  
*The Metropolitan answers:* "It is proper to inquire of the Most Reverend Fathers the Bishops concerning their opinion." *The Secretary of the Synod, approaching the Most Reverend Bishops, asks in the words:* "Most Reverend Fathers the Bishops! Is it pleasing unto you that the next session be..."

*...held on the N. day, on the N. Sunday of the month of N., yet so that the designated term may in General Congregation be freely shortened or extended, as shall appear profitable for the affairs of the Synod?" N., Promoter of this Synod, remind and request you, Reverend Notaries appointed for this Synod, that according to your duty you record carefully all who were present at this first Session, and prepare an accurate and trustworthy report of everything that was deliberated and transacted."

*Then shall follow the conclusion with prayers according to the form and manner established above at the preceding Congregation.*

## General Congregation II
*On the appointed day and hour the General Congregation shall take place (the first after the preparatory one, and the second in general sequence), and since almost all the priests receive lodging and sustenance in the complex of the General Seminary of Lviv, it shall be held in the Seminary Church, where the other General Congregations shall likewise take place.*  
*The order of proceedings of this second Congregation, as well as of the other General Congregations, shall be almost identical with that indicated above for the preparatory assembly. Namely:*  
*All duly summoned to the Synod, in clerical dress, shall take their assigned places and, under the leadership of the Metropolitan, perform the prayers prescribed above for the opening of an assembly.*  
*Then follows a short address by the Metropolitan, as for instance: concerning the diligence to be observed in comprehensive deliberations upon the decrees of the Synod, and the methodology of conducting debates.*  
*Following the address, the Metropolitan shall command, according to the customary form described above, the announcement of the names of those chosen for the three synodal sections or Commissions, each of which shall have as its president a Bishop, or, in case of any impediment, one of the senior Canons or dignitaries. These Commissions the Metropolitan shall establish and designate their members with the agreement of the Bishops.*

*Three such Commissions shall be established, and their task shall be properly to deliberate and prepare everything that is subsequently to be decided publicly. Namely:*  
*Commission I shall consist of a president (one of the Bishops), a vice-president, and 8 or 10 members or consultors, as well as one Notary.*  
*Commission II, whose president shall be the second of the Most Reverend Bishops, along with a vice-president, 8 or 10 members or consultors, and one Notary.*  
*Commission III, whose president shall be one of the dignitaries of the Metropolitan Chapter, and the other members as stated for the first two.*  
*To each of these chosen Commissions are assigned the points of inquiry and subjects for examination. Then it shall be announced to all members of the Commissions that meetings of the Commissions during the Synod shall take place daily, before and after noon, with the exception of those days on which General Congregations and public solemn Sessions are to be held. Furthermore, it shall belong to the Bishops presiding over the individual Sections or Commissions to determine not only the day and hour for each meeting, but also the entire order in which the synodal subject matter is to be discussed. The meetings of the Commissions shall be held in the buildings of the General Seminary of Lviv, where a separate hall shall be prepared for each section, in which the members of that section shall occupy themselves with the subject matter pertaining to the Synod.*  
*The meetings shall begin with the following prayers:*  
*President:* "Blessed is our God, always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages." *The members answer:* "Amen." *Then all recite or sing:* "Heavenly King..." *as above;* "Theotokos and Virgin, rejoice..." *and the rest as above. — President:* "Seat of Wisdom." *Members:* "Pray for us;" *and the sign of the cross:* "In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen."  
*Then the debate shall begin upon the points of doctrine or assigned subject matter according to the order and method indicated by the president.*  
*The subjects discussed in these particular assemblies, together with the observations they deem proper to make, shall be delivered unto the Metropolitan, who shall present them to the General Congregations as he himself shall announce, and in which all Synodal members are to sit, so that they may be submitted there to the judgment and approval of all participants.*

*The subjects examined by the General Congregations shall be weighed once again by the Bishops with the Metropolitan, and finally by a majority of their votes they shall definitively resolve what is to be established and decreed; these decisions the Metropolitan shall subsequently order to be read aloud and promulgated at the public Sessions.*  
*Here it is also to be observed that during the deliberations both of particular and of General Congregations, it must lie upon the heart of the members of the Synod to weigh everything diligently and maturely; and if anything should appear doubtful unto them, or such as ought to be amended, added, or omitted, each shall be free, in proper turn, to express his opinion, stating therewith the reason for the desired amendment. But in order that it may be known with certainty and precision what opinion each has expressed, each member shall submit his proposals together with the arguments supporting them in brief written form to the President of the Congregation or to the Notary performing the duties of Secretary, which shall subsequently be annexed unto the Acts.*  
*Upon the conclusion of the proceedings of each Congregation, the protocol of proceedings drawn up by the Synodal Notary shall be read aloud.*  
*At the conclusion, the order of thanksgiving shall be performed after each Congregation: after a General Congregation in the manner indicated above for the first preparatory General Congregation; and after a particular assembly with the prayer:* "Beneath Thy protection..." *and the rest, as above, and the recitation once of:* "Our Father..."

## Congregation III in Order (General Congregation II)
*On the designated day and hour all members of the Synod shall assemble in the manner prescribed above in the Church of the General Seminary. The opening of this Congregation shall take place, as in the preceding ones, with the customary prayers, and thereafter the points of doctrine of Titulus I, etc., shall be presented for discussion. The Promoters of the Synod shall read aloud in order one title after another, and the debate shall begin. Here shall also be read whatever was observed in the particular Sessions or Congregations. Upon the conclusion of the reading and debate, when, to the inquiry of the Promoters whether the public solemn Session already announced for the nearest day is actually to take place, an affirmative reply is given, this third General Congregation shall conclude with the customary prayers. Following this Congregation follows:*

## Second Public and Solemn Session
*At the appointed day and hour, according to the ceremonial indicated for the first Session, the Metropolitan with the Most Reverend Bishops, the Metropolitan Chapter, and all who are obliged to be present at the Sessions of the Synod, shall proceed in procession from the Metropolitan Palace to the Archeparchial Cathedral Church. The solemn synodal Divine Liturgy shall be celebrated by one of the Provincial Bishops. Thereafter all proceed to the place of the Sessions, and after the performance of the prayers and the celebration of the holy rite in due order, one of the Most Reverend Bishops shall deliver a short homily. Following the homily, the Promoter shall move the promulgation of the Decrees, more or less in these words:*  
"Your Grace! Whereas after renewed deliberations of the Congregations and mature reflection of the Most Reverend Bishops, with the help of God it has now come to pass that further decrees and statutes could be adopted by the common agreement of the same Most Reverend Fathers: I, N. N., named Promoter of this Synod, most humbly move that Your Grace deign to resolve and command that the said decrees be now proclaimed and promulgated in this Synod." *The Most Reverend Metropolitan shall answer:* "This We decree and command."  
*The Secretary of the Synod, having taken the decrees, steps forward to the designated place, whence in a raised voice he reads them aloud. After their reading, the Secretary with a Notary approaches the individual Most Reverend Bishops and asks:* "Most Reverend Father! Are all these enactments pleasing unto Your Grace?" *Having received the reply:* "They are pleasing," *the Secretary presents the reply unto the Metropolitan, who says:* "The enactments are pleasing unto all the Most Reverend Fathers the Bishops; wherefore thanksgiving is due unto God." *All answer:* "Thanks be to God."  
*Then the second Promoter shall move the appointment of the third Session, which shall likewise be done as described for the first Session. Finally, the Promoter shall demand that the acts of this Session be drawn up; and everything is to be conducted according to that same method and rite as at the first Session.*  
*As regards the other Congregations, whether particular or general, as also concerning the solemn and public Sessions, all those Congregations and Sessions are to be conducted in the manner, order, and pattern indicated above, with this difference only: that the solemn Divine Liturgies at the second and third Sessions shall be celebrated by turns by the Bishops of the province in the presence of the Metropolitan, and likewise the synodal discourses at those Divine Liturgies shall be delivered by the Most Reverend Fathers the Bishops.*

*At the first Session the Metropolitan himself shall deliver the discourse, and the concluding blessing at all public Sessions shall likewise be imparted by the Metropolitan himself.*

## Final Session and Conclusion of the Synod
*On the designated day and hour, the Metropolitan, the Most Reverend Bishops, and all who were present at the procession of the first Session, shall proceed according to that same rite and order as on that occasion to the Metropolitan Church, and there everything shall be conducted in the manner already indicated. When it therefore appears that there remains nothing further to be transacted in the Synod, the Promoter of the Synod shall move the election of Synodal Witnesses, more or less in these words:*  
"Your Grace! Ancient custom observed at provincial councils requires that at least two Synodal Witnesses be elected and appointed for each Eparchy. Wherefore I, N. N., appointed Promoter of this Synod, move and most humbly pray that Your Grace deign to resolve upon the promulgation of the decree concerning the Synodal Witnesses to be elected and those chosen" [^16]. *The Metropolitan shall say:* "This We decree and command."  
*Whereupon the Secretary receives the decree of nomination of the Witnesses and proclaims the same in these words:*  
"S. S., Metropolitan... etc. Whereas custom in the celebration of provincial synods requires that for each Eparchy of this Province two churchmen, venerable in age and morals, prudent, and loving blameless life and discipline, should be chosen and constituted as Synodal Witnesses, whose duty it is diligently to see to it that the statutes and enactments issued by this Synod, when promulgated after receiving Apostolic approval, be everywhere introduced throughout the province; and besides this, it belongs unto them to report sincerely and according to the truth unto the Metropolitan of the Province—either before a future Synod, should this be required, or subsequently in the assembled Synod—concerning everything that, from the conclusion of this Synod until the nearest provincial Synod, should run contrary to the order of the Church and due discipline, and concerning all defects or abuses, whether among the clergy or among the faithful of their Eparchy, of which they may have learned and which ought to be eliminated through a Synod..."

"...have represented the truth. Wherefore, adhering unto this custom, We have resolved to choose and appoint two Synodal Witnesses for each Diocese [^17]. We therefore name as Synodal Witnesses:  
1) For Our Archeparchy: a) N. N., b) N. N.  
2) For the Eparchy of Przemyśl: a) N. N., b) N. N.  
3) For the Eparchy of Stanyslaviv: a) N. N., b) N. N.  
Those among them who are present shall be summoned unto the Most Reverend Metropolitan and take an oath before him upon the Holy Gospel in the following manner:  
'I, N. N., appointed by the Most Reverend Metropolitan as Synodal Witness for the Eparchy of N., promise, vow, and swear from henceforth and for the future, that concerning everything that in whatever way I shall hear and learn with certainty that might be desirable for the implementation of the Decrees of this Synod, when they shall have been lawfully promulgated; as also whatever from the conclusion of this Synod until the nearest provincial Synod should run contrary to good ecclesiastical order and wholesome discipline; as well as concerning all defects and abuses, whether among the clergy or among the people of my Eparchy, of which I might become aware and which ought to be eliminated through a Synod: I shall report conscientiously and truthfully unto the Metropolitan, either before a future Synod, should this be required of me, or subsequently in the assembled Synod. So help me God and this His holy Gospel' [^18].  
As regards the other appointed Witnesses who may not be present, the Most Reverend Metropolitan shall command that they take that same oath before their own Bishop, and that the Bishops subsequently notify the Metropolitan thereof.  
Thereafter the Promoter shall request the signing of the Synod in these words:  
'Your Grace! Whereas after the promulgation of the Decrees which with the help of God have been established in this Synod, and which have been accepted by the Most Reverend Fathers, it yet remains that the Very Reverend Fathers sign the same before the closing of the Synod: I, Promoter of the same Synod, move and pray that Your Grace deign to direct the signing of the Decrees' [^19]."

*The Most Reverend Metropolitan shall declare:* "This We decree and command." *Whereupon the Secretary receives the said Decree and reads it aloud, namely:*  
"S. S., Metropolitan... etc.  
When We, by the help of God, have reached the point where everything that it was pleasing to deliberate, ordain, and decree in this Synod has now been accomplished and concluded: We implore and admonish with brotherly love the Very Reverend Fathers who are participants of this Synod, that after Us, each one individually in his place and order, approach the High Altar (*Velykyi Prestol*) and subscribe that which in this Provincial Synod has been enacted and determined."  
*After the reading of the Decree, all the Very Reverend Fathers approach in order unto the Altar, upon whose mensa on the northern side—that is, on the left facing the Altar—there shall lie one or more copies of all the Decrees collected together, upon which first the Metropolitan, and then the other Most Reverend Bishops and remaining Synodal members, shall sign in the manner indicated in the Synod of Zamość, namely: Christian name, surname, and title.*  
*Likewise the Bishops of the Province, as was done in the Synod of Zamość: the signature of Christian name and surname with their own title, and nothing more.*  
*Upon the conclusion of the signings, the Promoter shall move the announcement of the next Synod, saying:* "Your Grace! At provincial synods it was the custom, at the conclusion of the final Session, likewise to appoint and announce the time of the subsequent Synod. Wherefore I, Promoter of this Synod, move and pray that Your Grace, according to this custom, deign even now to determine and designate the time for celebrating the next provincial Synod, and order the Decree on the date of the future Synod to be promulgated." *The Most Reverend Metropolitan shall answer:* "This We decree and command."  
*And the Secretary shall read aloud the Decree of the following tenor:*  
"S. S.... etc. Following the ancient custom of provincial synods, according to which at the conclusion of the final Session the time of the nearest subsequent Synod is designated and appointed: with the agreement of Our Most Reverend Provincial Bishops, We decree and determine that the next subsequent Provincial Synod shall take place in the year 1896; yet so that this date may be freely shortened or extended, as the circumstances in Our Province shall require for the growth and welfare of the Church."  
*Finally, the Promoter moves the closing of the Synod in the words:*

## 1. Conclusion of the Conciliar Ceremonies

### Promoter's Motion & Suffrage of Dissolution

"Your Grace! Whereas, on the one hand, all that it pleased the Fathers to deliberate and establish at this Synod hath, by the help of God, been happily accomplished; and, on the other hand, pastoral solicitude summoneth the Most Reverend Bishops present at this Synod back to the flock entrusted unto Them: I, the Promoter of this Synod, do make motion and most humbly pray that Your Grace may deign to bring this Synod to a close, and to promulgate the Decree on the Conclusion of the Synod."  
*The Metropolitan shall say:* "This we decree and command, if it so please the Most Reverend Fathers" [^20].  
*The Secretary asketh the Most Reverend Fathers Bishops:* "Most Reverend Fathers Hierarchs! Doth it please you now to conclude this Synod unto the glory of Almighty God?"  
*Having received the answer:* "It pleaseth us," *and reported the same to the Metropolitan, the latter shall say:* "It pleaseth all the Fathers; wherefore we decree the conclusion of this Synod, and hereby do conclude it."  

### Decree on the Conclusion of the Synod

*The Secretary, having received the Decree on the Conclusion of the Synod from the Most Reverend Metropolitan, shall read it and announce it as followeth:*  
"S. S. . . . Metropolitan, etc.  
Whereas all that this Provincial Synod was to deliberate upon, what it was to establish and decide, hath by the help of God already been accomplished and brought to an end, and nothing further remaineth whereon deliberation should be held; and moreover, pastoral solicitude summoneth the Most Reverend Fathers Bishops present at this Synod back to their flock: with the assent of the same Most Reverend Fathers Bishops, we ordain and decree that this Synod be concluded, and by this very act we do conclude it. Thanks be to God!" [^21].  

### Admonition to Notaries & Descent to the Altar

*After the promulgation of the Decree, the Promoter summoneth the Notaries with these words:* "I, the Promoter of this Synod, do admonish and pray you, Venerable Notaries of this Synod, that you diligently record all who were present at this final Session, and draw up an accurate and authentic report of all that was treated and enacted at this Synod."  
*The First Notary answereth:* "I shall draw it up."  
*After all this, the Most Reverend Metropolitan shall deliver a brief concluding address to the conciliar members.*  
*At the conclusion of the address, the Most Reverend Metropolitan, vested in all sacred vestments, the Bishops in mantiyas and mitres, and the Chapter Canons in epitrachelia and phelonia, shall descend to the lowest step of the Altar (*Prestol*),*

### Eucharistic Exposition & The Slavonic Supplications

*and here all, having bent their knees, shall bow down with a profound inclination of the head before the Most Holy Mystery of the Eucharist, removed from the Tabernacle (*Kyvot*) by the Deacon and exposed upon the Altar. After the thrice-repeated incensation of the Most Holy Mystery by the Metropolitan, the same Metropolitan together with the assisting Priests shall chant the hymn used in the Ruthenian Churches, the so-called Supplication (*suplikatsia* / *умилительну пѣснь*) in the Slavonic tongue:*  
1) "Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, have mercy on us" *(thrice); and the people, chanting, repeat the same three times.*  
2) "From pestilence, famine, fire, and war, deliver us, O Lord" *(thrice); and the people thrice.*  
3) "From sudden and unprovided death, preserve us, O Lord" *(thrice); and the people three times.*  
4) "We sinners pray Thee, O God, hear us, O Lord." *And the people the same.*  
5) "We sinners pray Thee, O God, forgive us, O Lord." — *And the people the same.*  
6) "We sinners pray Thee, O God, have mercy on us, O Lord." — *And the people the same.*  
7) "O Jesus, O Jesus, O Jesus, Son of the Living God, have mercy upon us." *And the people the same.*  
8) "O Mary, O Mary, O Mary, Mother of the Son of God Most High, intercede for us." *And the people.*  

### The Te Deum & Triple Eucharistic Blessing

*Upon the conclusion of these prayers and the thrice-repeated incensation of the Most Holy Gifts, the Metropolitan shall intone the hymn:* "We praise Thee, O God" (*Te Deum laudamus*), *and then the choir and people standing alternately with the clergy shall chant the remaining verses of the hymn up to the words:* "Save Thy people, O Lord." *Before these words, the Metropolitan, having incensed the Most Holy Gifts three times, approacheth the Altar, and holding in his hands the Ostensorium (*Daronosytsia*) containing the Most Holy Gifts, facing the people, proclaimeth:* "Save Thy people, O Lord!" *once and a second time, and these same words are chanted by all assisting with the people; then the Metropolitan proclaimeth again:* "Save Thy people, O Lord, and bless Thine inheritance," *and chanting these words, blesseth the people toward the three sides, while the people, together with the clergy and choir, chant the remaining verses of the Ambrosian hymn unto the end.*  
*After this, having incensed the Most Holy Gifts three times, They are reposed into the Tabernacle (*Kyvot*).*  
*The choir shall begin to chant:* "Blessed be the name of the Lord from now and forever" *(thrice).*

### Solemn Dismissal, Polychronion, & Kiss of Peace

*The Metropolitan, facing the faithful in mitre and with pastoral staff, shall impart the customary blessing to the people and those present; and if he hath most graciously obtained from the Holy Father the faculty to impart the Papal Blessing, he shall then give the Papal Blessing with plenary indulgences according to the form prescribed for such a blessing.*  
*Then the Deacon proclaimeth:* "Wisdom!" *Choir:* "More honorable than the Cherubim, and more glorious beyond compare than the Seraphim, without defilement Thou gavest birth to God the Word: true Mother of God, Thee do we magnify."  
*Metropolitan:* "Glory be to Thee, O Christ God, our hope, glory be to Thee." *Choir:* "Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages. Amen. Lord, have mercy" *(twice).* "Master, bless."  
*The Metropolitan, standing facing the people and holding the holy Cross in his right hand, proclaimeth the Dismissal (*Otpost*):*  
"May Christ our true God, through the prayers of His most pure Mother; by the power of the precious and life-giving Cross; of the holy Archistrategos Michael; of the holy Joseph the Betrothed; of the holy, glorious, and all-praised Apostles Peter and Paul; of the holy Andrew the First-Called, and of the other holy Apostles; of the holy Apostles of the Slavs Cyril and Methodius; of the holy Hieromartyr Josaphat; of the holy, glorious, God-fearing Ruthenian Princes Vladimir and Olga; of our venerable Fathers Anthony and Theodosius of the Caves; of the holy and righteous Ancestors of God Joachim and Anna; and of the holy Great-Martyr, Victorious Wonderworker George, whose temple this is; and of all the Saints: have mercy † and save † you †, for He is good and the Lover of mankind." —  
*Choir:* "Amen."  
*After these prayers, the Metropolitan intoneth the Polychronion (*Mnohaya Lita*):* "To the Most Holy Ecumenical Hierarch Leo, Pope of Rome: Many years!"  
*Clergy, choir, and people:* "Many years!"  
*Metropolitan:* "To our right-believing and God-preserved Emperor Franz Joseph: Many years!"  
*Choir:* "Many years!"  
*The First Bishop:* "To our Most Reverend Archbishop and Metropolitan, the Lord Sylvester: Many years!"  
*Choir:* "Many years!"  
*Metropolitan:* "To the God-beloved Bishops: Julian, Bishop of Przemyśl, and Julian, Bishop of Stanyslaviv: Many years!"  
*Choir:* "Many years!"  
*The Second Bishop:* "To the entire Catholic Episcopate: Many years!"  
*Choir:* "Many years!"

*The same:* "To the Very Reverend Canons of all our three Chapters: Many years!"  
*Choir:* "Many years!"  
*Then the same:* "To the entire Catholic Priesthood: Many years!"  
*Choir:* "Many years!"  
*The same:* "To all orthodox Christians: Many years!"  
*Choir:* "Many years!"  
*After this, the Metropolitan, having laid aside the sacred vestments, vesteth in the mantiya, and giving the Kiss of Peace, kisseth the senior Bishop on the left shoulder, saying:* "Christ is among us," *who, kissing the shoulder of the Metropolitan in turn, answereth:* "He is and shall be"; *and he approacheth the other Bishop and giveth him the Kiss of Peace in the same manner, who thereafter approacheth the first Canon and greeteth him with peace and kissing with the aforesaid words, and so it proceedeth down unto the last Priest, repeating the same greeting and response.*  
*When all have greeted one another in this manner, they shall forthwith proceed in procession to the Metropolitan Palace, and having bidden farewell to the Metropolitan, shall depart to their homes in peace.*

### Mandate on Latin Translation & Propaganda Submission

The Acts and Decrees of the Synod, not only recorded in the original text, that is, in the vernacular Ruthenian language, but also well and faithfully translated into the Latin tongue, shall be dispatched as speedily as possible to the Holy Apostolic See, through His Eminence the Cardinal Prefect of the Sacred Congregation of Propaganda, for examination and approval.  
And finally, when they shall have been approved, they shall be promulgated and observed.

**B. V. V. S. Pr. D. M. Ch.**  
*(To God Almighty in the Holy Trinity, and to the Ever-Virgin Mary, Honor.)*

## 2. Part VI: Epistolary Exchange with the Apostolic See

### VI.

#### Synodal Letter of the Ruthenian Episcopate to Pope Leo XIII
*Letter of the Ruthenian Episcopate of the Province of Galicia, Wherein the Same Reporteth to the Holy Father the Completion of the Synod and Humbly Prayeth for the Approval of the Decrees.*

**Most Holy Father!**

We hasten to report to Your Holiness that the work which we, having assembled for the benefit and good of our Church, undertook, hath been happily accomplished with the help of Almighty God and the Most Blessed Virgin Mary. For unto You, Most Holy Father, and unto Your goodwill toward our Ruthenian people, is it to be attributed that this Provincial Synod was celebrated for the growth of the Catholic faith, for the closest bond of holy Union with the Roman Church, for the ordering of the morals of the clergy, for the proper regulation of priestly life, and for the fitting establishment of our discipline. This Synod, mindful of those truths which You, Most Holy Father, in Your exalted Constitutions and Encyclicals have set forth as testimonies of heavenly doctrine, and which illumine the whole universe like the brightest light, accepteth and revereth the same, and hath resolved to append them unto its own Acts. We profess that all which the Hierarchs and the entire Priesthood of this ecclesiastical Province desired, hath come to pass through the fatherly love of Your Holiness toward us, since it was regarded as exceedingly desirable that some prelate from among the Roman Hierarchy should preside over this our assembly, who, representing the person of the Roman Pontiff Himself, should exercise the office of Apostolic Delegate.  
Receive, Most Holy Father, the grateful sentiments of our soul, that, gifted with piercing discernment of mind, You selected for that office the Most Reverend Agostino Ciasca, Archbishop of Larissa, a man so illustrious, distinguished in learning, and exceedingly skilled in the knowledge of Eastern rites. He, by his prudence, patience, moderation conjoined with resolve, and other endowments so greatly necessary for such an office, distinguished himself especially in the General Congregations, so that he not only guided divergent and differing opinions to the attainment of truth, but also won the minds of those present unto himself and carried them away in admiration. And indeed his wisdom shone forth most brilliantly in his collaboration both in drafting and in properly arranging all that relateth...

...unto the establishment of the Minor Seminary, unto the fitting education of alumni in the Major Seminary, unto the proper ordering of the life of the clergy of the New Testament, and unto the statutes to be observed in conducting ecclesiastical courts. He spoke effectively for the propagation of the veneration of the Virgin Mother of God, whose praises and magnificats we have drawn anew, Most Holy Father, from Your most recent Encyclical of the 22nd day of September last (*Octobri Mense*, September 22, 1891). No small labor and effort did he also contribute together with the Bishops both in examining the diverse proposals of the Commissions, in preparing the agenda of the General Congregations, and in collating the Latin text with the Ruthenian translation. Wherefore the outcome of the work was such that everything proceeded in this Synod fittingly and orderly, amid the unanimous and perfect concord of the Fathers.  
Wherefore, prostrating ourselves at the feet of Your Holiness, we humbly pray that You, Most Holy Father, may accept the celebration of this Synod as the brightest pledge of our honor, obedience, devotion, and love toward You, our most loving Father and most gracious Vicar, Protector, and Defender of the Catholic Ruthenians.  
Deign, Most Holy Father, to command that the Acts of this Synod, as soon as they are transmitted to Rome, be examined and provided with Apostolic confirmation.  
Humbly prostrate at the feet of Your Holiness, we implore the Apostolic Blessing.

At Lviv, the 8th day of October, 1891.

**Sylvester Sembratovych,**  
Metropolitan of Halych, Archbishop of Lviv.  
**Julian Pelesz,**  
Bishop of Przemyśl.  
**Julian Kuylivsky,**  
Bishop of Stanyslaviv.

#### Apostolic Letter of Pope Leo XIII to the Ruthenian Hierarchy
*To Sylvester Sembratovych, Archbishop of Lviv, — Julian Pelesz, Bishop of Przemyśl, — Julian Kuylivsky, Bishop of Stanyslaviv — of the Ruthenians.*

### Leo, Pope XIII

**Venerable Brethren!**

We cannot sufficiently express in words the singular joy which seized Us when We learned that you, Venerable Brethren, for the increase of the Catholic faith and to make firmer the bond with the Roman Church, outside of which there is no salvation, under the leadership of Our Delegate, have happily celebrated the First Synod of Lviv. For from it We have known that you with provident zeal have striven to provide appropriate remedies whereby the errors introduced by the hardships of time and the deceit of men might be removed; that the priests committed to your care might manifest holiness of life; that candidates called to the lot of the Lord's service might in the seminaries be trained in the best manner for the proper fulfillment of the duties belonging to the holy order of the priesthood; that certain and secure rules of liturgy and discipline might be established; that the obedience due to sacred pastors might be strengthened; that fraternal love and concord might endure; and that the people might faithfully apply themselves to the duties of Catholic profession and piety.  
Therefore, having rendered thanks unto the Heavenly Chief of Pastors, Who assisted you with His aid that you might provide for the common good of your flock, We deemed it the duty of Our supreme Pastorate to bestow upon you sincere and well-deserved praise for what you have accomplished in religious zeal, and We earnestly exhort you that in this holy undertaking you remain steadfast and continue to labor for the cause of God and of the Church.  
Having this in view in the future, you will multiply your merits before God and contribute to the increase of the true happiness of the entire Ruthenian Nation, most dear unto Us.  
We beseech Almighty God, the Giver of all good things, with Our whole heart, that He may graciously incline unto the desires expressed by you, and We most lovingly impart the requested Apostolic Blessing unto you, Venerable Brethren, and unto the entire Ruthenian people.  
Given at Rome, at St. Peter's, the 20th day of May, in the year 1895, the eighteenth of Our Pontificate.

## 3. Part VII: Acts of the Congregations and Sessions

### VII.

### Acts of the Congregations and Sessions

#### General Congregation I

On the 24th day of September, at 9 o'clock before noon, there assembled in the chapel of the Metropolitan Palace:  
The Most Reverend Fathers:  
Agostino Ciasca, Archbishop of Larissa, President of the Synod;  
Sylvester Sembratovych, Metropolitan, etc.;  
Julian Pelesz, Bishop of Przemyśl;  
Julian Kuylivsky, Bishop of Stanyslaviv;  
and all the other Fathers.  
The Most Reverend Metropolitan celebrated the Divine Liturgy in the Archcathedral Church of St. George the Great-Martyr, and at its conclusion celebrated in his chapel prayers with the invocation of the Holy Spirit, greeted the assembly with a brief address, and set forth the purpose and benefit of the Synod.  
At the conclusion of the address, His Excellency the President of the Synod and Apostolic Delegate Father Agostino Ciasca, upon the counsel of the Bishops, summoned the Metropolitan, the Bishops, all Priests present, and the Senior of the Stauropegion Institute to make the promise of observing secrecy. — Then all the Priests upon their priestly conscience gave the promise of observing secrecy, and the Senior upon his dignity and honor.  
The Apostolic Delegate and Synodal President commanded through the Consistorial Chancellor that the decrees of election and nomination of the officials of the Synod be promulgated, namely:  
1. Judges of Excuses and Complaints:  
President: The Most Reverend Bishop of Przemyśl, Dr. Julian Pelesz.  
Members: The Very Reverend Mykhailo Sinhalewych, Archpresbyter of the Metropolitan Chapter.  
The Very Reverend Venedykt Lytynsky, Archdeacon of the Chapter of Przemyśl.  
The Very Reverend Ivan Lytvynovych, Custos of the Chapter of Stanyslaviv.  
The Very Reverend Andrei Biletsky, Scholastic of the Metropolitan Chapter.

### Conciliar Address of Apostolic Delegate Ciasca

**Most Reverend and Very Reverend Fathers!**

In the grievous misfortunes of our times, which press upon the Church of Jesus Christ from all sides, nothing so greatly comforteth and uplifteth the spirit of our Most Holy Father Pope Leo XIII as the union of Pastors in faith and charity with the Supreme Governor of the Church and their endeavor actively to provide for the integrity of morals and the benefit of the faithful. Wherefore ye will readily understand, Very Reverend Fathers, how pleasing unto the same Most Holy Chief Pastor was the tidings of your desire to celebrate this Synod in this renowned Metropolitanate of Lviv, imbued with the principles of the true faith by the most holy teacher and Apostle to the Slavs Methodius, and distinguished by an episcopal see from the very beginning. In order to give proof unto all of the joy which arose in His heart from the above-mentioned tidings, and of the solicitude with which He careth for all Churches, and most especially for this your own, the same Supreme Pontiff hath deigned to entrust unto my humility, not only that I should represent His Person in this your glorious Assembly for the elevation of the dignity and splendor of this Synod, but also that I should declare the sentiments of His paternal benevolence and singular love toward the renowned Church of the Ruthenians, and testify anew that your Churches occupy the greatest and most important part of His cares, and that He desireth nothing more or more earnestly than that He might provide what is necessary for their benefit and prosperity [^22].  
Finally, His Holiness, convinced that very great benefits shall come forth from this Synod, bestoweth upon you all assembled in the Lord, and upon your strivings and labors, and upon the entire Ruthenian people, a special blessing from the heart; and may this blessing be for you a renewed, clear proof of His benevolence toward you, and may it bring unto you, who desire to cultivate the fruitfulness of the Lord's field, an abundance of heavenly gifts.  
This mandate of the Supreme Pontiff I willingly fulfill and with joy announce unto you, and at the same time I pray and beseech Your Graces that ye look upon the supreme dignity of Him Who commanded me to fulfill this mission among you, Most Illustrious Sacred Pastors, and not upon the weakness of the legate. Unto me indeed, on account of that reverence and affection with which I have been imbued from my earliest youth for the rite and the Churches of the East, among...

...which the Ruthenian Church is exceedingly distinguished both by the number of the faithful and the excellence of her men, it is pleasing to be present at these your assemblies, in which, by God's help and protection, ye strive with redoubled diligence to preserve inviolate the integrity of this Church and to uphold her welfare. Truly a magnificent counsel! And if from the beginning of matters it is permissible to foretell their end, I doubt not that on the foundation of the constitutions and decrees of this Synod, your Church shall from day to day flourish more and more, diffuse the splendor of faith and ecclesiastical discipline, and attain her ancient glory and pristine beauty. — That this may proceed happily, having invoked the aid of the Most Holy Virgin Mary, Mother of God, immaculately conceived, and of the Holy Archangel Michael, heavenly patron, of Cyril and Methodius, Apostles to the Slavic race, of the Hieromartyr Josaphat, and of Vladimir and Olga, former Ruthenian princes: let us beseech God with fervent prayers that He may graciously favor our intentions, direct our counsels, assist the work begun, and crown its end.

**Venerable Brother!**

### Formula of the Profession of Faith
*(Forma Fidei Orthodoxae)*

In the Name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.  
I, Sylvester Sembratovych, Metropolitan of Halych, Archbishop of Lviv, Bishop of Kamianets, etc., believe with a firm faith and profess each and every thing contained in the Symbol of Faith which the Holy Roman Church maketh use of, that is to say:  
I believe in one God, the Father Almighty, Maker of heaven and earth, and of all things visible and invisible. And in one Lord Jesus Christ, the only-begotten Son of God, begotten of the Father before all ages. God of God, Light of Light, true God of true God, begotten, not made, consubstantial with the Father, by Whom all things were made. Who for us men and for our salvation came down from heaven, and was incarnate by the Holy Spirit of the Virgin Mary, and became man. He was crucified also for us...

...under Pontius Pilate, and suffered, and was buried. And He rose again on the third day according to the Scriptures. And He ascended into heaven, and sitteth at the right hand of the Father. And He shall come again with glory to judge the living and the dead, Whose kingdom shall have no end. And in the Holy Spirit, the Lord and Giver of life, Who proceedeth from the Father and the Son; Who with the Father and the Son together is worshipped and glorified, Who spake by the Prophets. And in one, holy, catholic, and apostolic Church. I confess one baptism for the remission of sins. And I look for the resurrection of the dead, and the life of the world to come. Amen.  
I also venerate and accept the Ecumenical Councils, as followeth, that is:  
The First Nicene; and I confess that which was defined therein against Arius, of condemned memory: that the Lord Jesus Christ is the Son of God, begotten of the Father, only-begotten, that is, begotten of the substance of the Father, not made, consubstantial with the Father; and those impious assertions were rightly condemned at the same Council: that there was a time when He was not, or that the Son of God is changeable or mutable.  
The First of Constantinople, the Second in order; and I confess that which was defined therein against Macedonius, of condemned memory: that the Holy Spirit is not a servant, but the Lord; not a creature, but God; and hath one Godhead with the Father and the Son.  
The First of Ephesus, the Third in order; and I confess that which was defined therein against Nestorius, of condemned memory: that from the Godhead and the manhood, by an ineffable and incomprehensible union in the one Person of the Son of God, one Jesus Christ was constituted for us, and for this reason the Most Blessed Virgin is truly the Mother of God (*Theotokos*).  
The Council of Chalcedon, the Fourth in order; and I confess that which was defined therein against Eutyches and Dioscorus, both of condemned memory: that the one and the same Son of God, our Lord Jesus Christ, is perfect in Godhead and perfect in manhood, true God and true man of a rational soul and body, consubstantial with the Father according to the Godhead, and the same consubstantial with us according to the manhood, like unto us in all things, sin apart; begotten before the ages indeed of the Father according to the Godhead, and in the latter days the same for us and for our salvation of Mary the Virgin Mother of God according to the manhood, one and the same Christ the only-begotten Son, acknowledged in two natures without confusion, without change, without division, without separation; the distinction of natures being in no way taken away...

...by the union, but rather the property of each nature being preserved and concurring into one Person and one Hypostasis; not parted or divided into two persons, but one and the same Son and Only-begotten, God the Word, the Lord Jesus Christ. Likewise, that the Godhead of the same our Lord Jesus Christ, according to which He is consubstantial with the Father and the Holy Spirit, is impassible and immortal: that He was crucified and died only according to the flesh, as was likewise defined at the said Council, and in the Epistle of Saint Leo, Pope of Rome, through whose mouth the Fathers at the same Council proclaimed that the blessed Apostle Peter had spoken; by which definition is condemned the impious heresy of those who added to the Trisagion handed down by the Angels and chanted at the aforesaid Council of Chalcedon: "Holy God, Holy Mighty, Holy Immortal, have mercy on us," the clause: "Who wast crucified for us"; and thus asserted that the divine nature of the three Persons was passible and mortal.  
The Second Council of Constantinople, the Fifth in order, wherein the definition of the aforesaid Council of Chalcedon was renewed.  
The Third Council of Constantinople, the Sixth in order; and I confess that which was defined therein against the Monothelites: that in one and the same our Lord Jesus Christ there are two natural wills and two natural operations, without division, without change, without separation, without confusion; and that His human will is not contrary, but subject to His divine and almighty will.  
The Second Nicene Council, the Seventh in order; and I confess that which was defined therein against the Iconoclasts: that the Icons of Christ, of the Virgin Mother of God, and of the other Saints are to be possessed and retained, and that due honor and veneration are to be rendered unto them.  
The Fourth Council of Constantinople, the Eighth in order; and I confess that Photius was justly condemned therein, and Holy Patriarch Ignatius restored.  
I likewise venerate and accept all other Ecumenical Councils lawfully held and confirmed by the authority of the Roman Pontiff; and especially the Council of Florence, and I confess those things which were defined therein, that is: that the Holy Spirit is eternally from the Father and the Son, and hath His essence and His subsistent (hypostatic) being from the Father together with the Son, and from both eternally as from one principle and by a single spiration. Likewise, that the phrase "and the Son" (*Filioque*) was lawfully and reasonably added to the Symbol for the sake of clarifying the truth and out of imminent necessity. Likewise, that in unleavened or leavened wheat bread the Body of Christ...

...is truly consecrated, and that priests are bound to consecrate the Body of the Lord in either of these, that is, each according to the custom of his Church, whether Western or Eastern. — Likewise, that if those who are truly penitent depart this life in the charity of God before they have made satisfaction by worthy fruits of penance for sins committed and omitted, their souls are cleansed after death by purgatorial pains, and that, for the lightening of such pains, the suffragals of the living faithful are profitable unto them, that is to say, the sacrifices of the Divine Liturgy, prayers, alms, and other pious works, which are customarily performed by the faithful for other faithful according to the ordinances of the Church; but that the souls of those who, after receiving baptism, have incurred no defilement of sin at all, and also those which, after contracting the defilement of sin, whether in their bodies or after leaving them, have been cleansed, are straightway received into heaven and clearly behold God Himself, one in Trinity, as He is, yet one more perfectly than another according to the diversity of their merits. But the souls of those who die in actual mortal sin, or in original sin alone, straightway descend into hell, to be punished, however, with unequal pains. Likewise, that the Holy Apostolic See and the Roman Pontiff hold primacy over the whole world, and that the Roman Pontiff himself is the successor of blessed Peter, Chief of the Apostles, and the true Vicar of Christ, and the Head of the whole Church, and the Father and Teacher of all Christians, and that unto him in blessed Peter was delivered by our Lord Jesus Christ full power to pasture, rule, and govern the universal Church, as is contained in the acts of the ecumenical councils and in the holy canons. — Likewise, that the legal ceremonies of the Old Testament or the Mosaic law, the sacred sacrifices and mysteries, upon the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, have ceased and cannot be observed after the promulgation of the Gospel without sin. Likewise also, that the distinction of clean and unclean meats of the Old Law pertaineth to ceremonies which have passed away with the arrival of the Gospel. — Likewise, that the apostolic prohibition against things offered to idols, and blood, and things strangled, was suited to that time, that the occasion of discord between Jews and Gentiles might be taken away; which apostolic prohibition having lost its cause, its operation hath also ceased.  
Likewise I venerate and accept the Council of Trent; and I confess those things which were defined and declared therein. Especially that in the Divine Liturgy there is offered unto God a true, proper, and propitiatory sacrifice for the living and the dead; and in the Most Holy...

...Mystery of the Eucharist (according to the faith which hath always been in the Church of God) there are contained truly, really, and substantially the Body and Blood, together with the Soul and Divinity of our Lord Jesus Christ, and therefore the whole Christ; and that there is made a conversion of the whole substance of bread into the Body, and of the whole substance of wine into the Blood, which conversion the Catholic Church most aptly calleth transubstantiation, and that under each species, and under each separate part of each species when divided, the whole Christ is contained. Likewise, that there are seven Sacraments of the New Law instituted by Christ our Lord for the salvation of the human race, although not all are necessary for every individual, namely: Baptism, Chrismation (*Myroponamazaniye*), the Eucharist, Penance, Anointing of the Sick (*Eleosvyashcheniye*), Holy Orders, and Matrimony; and that they confer grace: and that of these, Baptism, Chrismation, and Holy Orders cannot be repeated. Likewise, that Baptism is necessary for salvation: and therefore, when danger of death threateneth, it ought to be conferred forthwith without any delay; and when conferred by anyone, at any time, with the proper matter, form, and intention, it is valid.  
Likewise, that the bond of the Sacrament of Matrimony is indissoluble; and even if, on account of adultery, heresy, or other causes, separation of bed and cohabitation may take place between spouses, yet it is not lawful for them to contract another marriage.  
Likewise, that Apostolic and Ecclesiastical traditions are to be received and venerated. Likewise, that the power of Indulgences was left by Christ in the Church, and that their use is most salutary to Christian people. Likewise, what was defined at the aforesaid Council of Trent concerning original sin, concerning justification, and concerning the canon and interpretation of the sacred books of the Old and New Testaments, I accept and confess.  
Likewise I venerate and accept the Ecumenical Vatican Council, and all things handed down, defined, and declared thereby, and especially concerning the primacy of the Roman Pontiff and his infallible magisterium, I most firmly accept and confess.  
Likewise I accept and profess all other things which the Holy Roman Church accepteth and professeth; and at the same time all things contrary, and whatever schisms and heresies have been condemned and anathematized by the same Church, I likewise condemn and anathematize.  
Moreover, unto the Roman Pontiff, successor of blessed Peter, Chief of the Apostles, and Vicar of Jesus Christ, I promise and swear true obedience.

This faith of the Catholic Church, outside of which no one can be saved, which I at this present time freely profess and truly hold, the same whole and inviolate, even unto the last breath of life, most constantly, with God's help, to retain and confess, and to take care, as far as in me lieth, that it be held, taught, and preached by those subject to me, or by those whose care shall belong unto me in my office: I, the same Sylvester Sembratovych, Metropolitan of Halych, Archbishop of Lviv, Bishop of Kamianets, do promise, vow, and swear.  
So help me God, and this Holy Divine Gospel.

### General Congregation II & Session II

#### General Congregation II
*Held in the Seminary Church on September 29, 1891.*  
On the 29th day of September, 1891, on the Feast of Saint Michael the Archistrategos, after the Latin Rite, upon the celebration of a Low Mass (read Divine Liturgy) by the Most Reverend Lord Bishop of Przemyśl, all the Synodal Fathers assembled in the Seminary Church under the presidency of His Excellency the Apostolic Delegate, Father Agostino Ciasca, Archbishop of Larissa.
After prayers, His Excellency the Metropolitan delivered an address, wherein he set forth the utility of synods for the Church and the mode of proceeding in synodal deliberations and discussions.  
The Most Reverend President directed the Secretaries to read the proposals discussed in the Commissions, namely:  
**Titulus I. On Faith.**  
Ch. 1. On the profession of faith.  
Ch. 2. On the Catholic faith.  
Ch. 3. On indifferentism.  
Ch. 4. On communion in sacred things with heretics and schismatics.  
Ch. 5. On the censorship of books.  
Ch. 6. On the sect of Freemasons and other condemned and prohibited sects.  
Ch. 7. On the preaching of the Word of God.  
Ch. 8. On the catechism.  
**Titulus II. On the Mysteries (Sacraments).**  
Ch. 1. On Baptism.  
Ch. 2. On Chrismation.  
Ch. 3. On the Eucharist.  
Ch. 4. On Penance.  
Ch. 5. On the Anointing of the Sick (Euchelaion).  
Ch. 6. On the Priesthood (Holy Orders).  
Ch. 7. On Matrimony.  
**Titulus IV. On the Public Worship of God.**  
Ch. 1. On the Sacrifice of the Divine Liturgy.  
Ch. 2. On the intention and stipends for Divine Liturgies.  
Ch. 3. On the Canonical Hours and other Services.  
Ch. 4. On the celebration of extraordinary Rites.  
Ch. 5. On church chant.  
Ch. 6. On feast days.  
Ch. 7. On the veneration of the Saints.  
Ch. 8. On holy processions.  
Ch. 9. On domestic worship of God.  
**Titulus VI. On Churches Dedicated to Divine Worship.**  
Ch. 1. On the building of churches.  
Ch. 2. On the interior arrangement of churches.  
Ch. 3. On church vessels and furnishings.  
Ch. 4. On the colors of church vestments.

**Titulus VII. On the Ecclesiastical Hierarchy.**  
Ch. 1. On the Roman Pontiff.  
Ch. 2. On the Metropolitan.  
Ch. 3. On the Bishops.  
Ch. 4. On the Chapters.  
Ch. 5. On the Deans.  
Ch. 6. On the Pastors.  
Ch. 7. On the Co-adjutors.  
Regarding these proposals, freedom of speech was granted to each; and after due examination of all things, all unanimously agreed upon the doctrine set forth.  
Thereupon the President, having taken counsel of the Bishops, ordered that these decrees be promulgated in the Second Session.  
With thanksgiving and prayers the General Congregation was concluded.  
Oleksiy Toronsky,  
Synodal Protonotary.

After prayers, the Promoters moved the promulgation of the statutes (decrees).  
His Excellency the Apostolic Delegate and Synodal President decided and ordered the promulgation and announcement of the statutes; and the Synodal Secretaries, having received the statutes, read them through:  
**Titulus I. On Faith.**  
Ch. 1. On the profession of faith.  
Ch. 2. On the Catholic faith.  
Ch. 3. On indifferentism.

Ch. 1. On Baptism.  
Ch. 2. On Chrismation.

Ch. 4. On Penance.  
Ch. 5. On the Anointing of the Sick (Euchelaion).  
Ch. 6. On the Priesthood.  
Ch. 7. On Matrimony.  
**Titulus IV. On the Public Worship of God.**

Ch. 3. On the Canonical Hours and other Services.

The Promoters then moved the announcement of the Third Session. His Excellency the President, with the assent of the Most Reverend Metropolitan and the Bishops, determined that the Third Session should be held on the 4th day of October; and the Secretary announced the decree convoking the Third Session.
The Promoters requested that the Acts of this Synod be drawn up.  
The First Notary of the Synod answered that he would draw them up. With thanksgiving and prayers this Second Session was concluded.  
Oleksiy Toronsky,  
Protonotary of the Synod.

### General Congregation III & Session III

#### General Congregation III
After the Read Divine Liturgy celebrated by the Most Reverend Julian Kuylivsky, Bishop of Stanyslaviv, all the Fathers of the Synod assembled; and after prayers and supplications were offered, discussions were held under the presidency of His Excellency the Apostolic Delegate and Synodal President upon the following Titles:  
**Titulus III. On Blessings and Consecrations (Sacramentals).**  
**Titulus IX. On the Priests of the New Testament.**  
**Titulus X. On Monks.**  
**Titulus XI. On Fasts.**  
**Titulus XII. On Prayers for the Departed.**  
Ch. 1. On the celebration of the Divine Liturgy.  
Ch. 2. On stipends (studies).  
When all had agreed upon the aforementioned Titles, the Most Reverend President ordered that these decisions be promulgated in the Third Session.  
With prayers this Third General Congregation was concluded.  
Oleksiy Toronsky,  
Protonotary of the Synod.

#### Session III
On Sunday, the 4th day of October, 1891, after the celebration of the solemn procession as at the First Session, and then after the celebration of the Chanted Divine Liturgy by the Most Reverend Lord Julian Kuylivsky, Bishop of Stanyslaviv, the Synodal Fathers assembled in the Metropolitan Church.

After the offering of prayers and supplications, the Synodal Promoters moved that the decisions read in the Third General Congregation be promulgated. Having received the order from His Excellency the Apostolic Delegate and President of the Synod, the Synodal Secretaries read:  
**Titulus III. On Blessings and Consecrations (Sacramentals).**  
**Titulus IX. On the Priests of the New Testament.**  
Ch. 1. On the dignity of Priests of the New Testament.  
Ch. 2. On the blamelessness of priestly life.  
Ch. 3. On the studies of Priests.  
**Titulus X. On Monks.**  
**Titulus XI. On Fasts.**  
**Titulus XII. On Prayers for the Departed.**  
Ch. 1. On the Divine Liturgy for the departed and the Parastas.  
Ch. 2. On church funerals and cemeteries.  
With the assent of the Bishops, His Excellency the President ordered that all this be inscribed among the Acts of the Synod.  
The Fourth and Final Session is announced for the 8th day of October, 1891.  
The Promoters move that the Acts of this Synod be drawn up.  
The First Notary answereth that he shall draw them up.  
Oleksiy Toronsky, m.p.

### General Congregation IV & Solemn Closing Session IV

#### General Congregation IV
After the reading of the Divine Liturgy by the Very Reverend Father Mykhail Singalewych, Archpriest of the Metropolitan Chapter, the Fathers of the Synod assembled in the Seminary Church on the 6th day of October, and there, under the presidency of His Excellency the Metropolitan in the absence of His Excellency the President of the Synod and with his assent, after the offering of supplications, discussions were held upon the titles: On Seminaries, On Synods, On the shortening of the Horologion (Office). With prayers and supplications this Congregation was concluded.  
Oleksiy Toronsky,  
Protonotary of the Synod.

#### Session IV and Final
On the 8th day of October, 1891, after the celebration of the procession and after the celebration of the Chanted Divine Liturgy by His Excellency the Metropolitan with the Most Reverend Bishops, the Fathers of the Synod assembled in the Metropolitan Church; and after supplications, the remaining Synodal statutes were read, namely:  
**Titulus VIII. On Seminaries.**  
Ch. 1. On Minor Seminaries.  
Ch. 2. On Major Seminaries.  
**Titulus XIII. On Ecclesiastical Courts.**  
**Titulus XIV. On Synods.**  
**Titulus XV. On Church Property.**  
There followed the nomination and announcement of the Synodal Witnesses.  
For the Archeparchy of Lviv:  
1. Andriy Biletsky; 2. Isidore Dolnytsky.  
For the Eparchy of Przemyśl:  
1. Mykola Rozdilsky; 2. Ivan Kopystiansky.  
For the Eparchy of Stanyslaviv:  
1. Vasyl Fatsiyevych; 2. Celestyn Kostetsky.  
The said Witnesses took the following oath upon the Holy Gospel:  
"I, N. N., Synodal Witness appointed by the Most Reverend Metropolitan for the Eparchy of N., do promise, vow, and swear from now and for the future, that whatever I shall in any way hear or certainly ascertain that would be desirable for the execution of the decrees of this Synod, when they shall be lawfully promulgated, as also whatever from the conclusion of this Synod unto the next Provincial Synod should be contrary to the good order of the Church and sound discipline, as well as all defects and abuses both among the clergy and among the people of my Eparchy, which I shall learn of and which ought to be removed through a Synod, I shall conscientiously and truthfully report to the Metropolitan even before the future Synod, if it be required of me, or thereafter in the assembled Synod, so help me God and this Holy Gospel."  
There followed the subscription of the decrees of the Synod. After the reading of the decree on subscription, all the Very Reverend Fathers, led by the Delegate in order, approached the high altar and signed the decrees.  
Upon the conclusion of the subscriptions, the Secretary read the decree announcing that the next Provincial Synod is to be celebrated in the year 1896.

With the assent of the Most Reverend Metropolitan and the Most Reverend Bishops, the Apostolic Delegate and President of the Synod, His Excellency the Archbishop of Larissa, Father Agostino Ciasca, decided that the Synod should be brought to a close, and ordered the Decree on the Conclusion of the Synod to be read. The Promoters summon the Notaries, that the Acts of this Session be drawn up. The First Notary answereth that he shall draw them up.  
After all this, His Excellency the Apostolic Delegate, in a brief address, expressed joy that the Synod had been happily brought to completion, extolled the labors expended by His Excellency the Metropolitan, the Most Reverend Bishops, and the other Fathers in framing the decrees, and praised their unanimity and mutual love. Then he gave thanks to Almighty God, Who for the attainment of so great an end deigned to grant His help. He doubted not that the Bishops would apply all diligence that these decisions should enter into life, when they shall have been examined and approved by the Holy See. In conclusion, he bade them farewell.  
Thereto His Excellency the Metropolitan likewise responded in a brief and graceful address, affirming the complete and sincere subjection of the Ruthenian Church to the Holy Apostolic See and the Roman Pontiff, and at the same time thanked him that he had deigned to appoint his Delegate as President of this Synod.  
Finally, prayers were celebrated as customary, and with polychronions the Synod was concluded.  
Oleksiy Toronsky,  
Synodal Protonotary.

### The Four Synodal Postulata & Apostolic Delegate's Verification

Moreover, the Fathers of the Synod expressed further the following desires (petitions / postulata), namely:  
1. That the Metropolitan Church of the Ruthenians be elevated to the Primatial dignity.  
2. That the Decree of Concordia of the year 1863 be equally observed also by all Latins.  
3. That there be in Rome a Procurator of the Ruthenian Church, distinguished with some dignity.  
4. That the Ruthenian Church in the future be called "Ruthenian-Catholic."  
Oleksiy Toronsky,  
Synodal Protonotary.

I, the undersigned, do certify that all and singular things contained in the Acts and Decrees of this Synod were accomplished according to the rules established by the sacred canons and approved by customs; and in particular I do certify that the above-cited Profession of Faith was made into my hands by the Most Reverend and Most Worthy Sylvester Sembratovych, Metropolitan of Halych, Archbishop of Lviv, and Bishop of Kamianets; by the Bishops: of Przemyśl and of Stanyslaviv; as also by the others whose names are written above.  
+ F. Agostino,  
Archbishop of Larissa, Apostolic Delegate,  
President of the Synod.  
+ Seal.

### Notes

[^1]: The Provincial Synod of Zamość (1720) was convoked under Metropolitan Leo Kishka (Kiszka) and presided over by the Apostolic Nuncio Archbishop Girolamo Grimaldi. It established comprehensive canonical, liturgical, and disciplinary legislation for the Ruthenian Greek Catholic Church, confirmed by Pope Benedict XIII in the Apostolic Constitution *Apostolicus Praedecessor* on December 15, 1724. A span of 171 years elapsed between the Synod of Zamość and the convocation of the 1891 Lviv Provincial Synod.
[^2]: Following the three Partitions of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth (1772, 1793, 1795), the Ruthenian eparchies were geopolitically divided. Those falling under the Russian Empire (Kyiv, Polotsk, Lutsk, Brest) were systematically suppressed and forcibly liquidated by Tsars Catherine II, Nicholas I (the 1839 Synod of Polotsk), and Alexander II (the 1875 liquidation of the Eparchy of Chełm). Conversely, the eparchies annexed by the Habsburg Monarchy (the Archeparchy of Lviv and Eparchy of Przemyśl) were preserved and granted imperial protection under Austrian law.
[^3]: The Greek Catholic Eparchy of Križevci (lat. *Dioecesis Crisiensis*, croat. *Križevačka eparhija*) was erected by Pope Pius VI in 1777 to encompass Byzantine-Ruthenian rite faithful across Croatia, Slavonia, and Vojvodina. Although sharing identical liturgical books, liturgical Church Slavonic, and Ruthenian chant traditions with the Galician eparchies, it was canon-juridically suzerain to the Latin Metropolitan Province of Zagreb rather than the Metropolitanate of Halych.
[^4]: The question of synodal language was of paramount canonical and pastoral delicacy. Standard Roman curial practice expected Latin to serve as the exclusive statutory language of Catholic councils. Metropolitan Sembratovych and the Galician episcopate successfully argued that relatively few parish clergy and deanery delegates commanded fluent spoken Latin, and requiring Latin would effectively stifle genuine debate. The Holy See accommodated this pastoral reality by permitting the Ruthenian language (*rus'ka besida*) for oral deliberations and the drafting of decrees, provided that a certified Latin translation was prepared for Rome's review and confirmation.
[^5]: Agostino Ciasca, O.S.A. (1835–1902), titular Archbishop of Larissa, was a distinguished scholar of Semitic languages, Oriental epigraphy, and Patristics. He served as Prefect of the Vatican Secret Archives (1891–1892), Secretary of the Sacred Congregation *de Propaganda Fide pro negotiis Ritus Orientalis*, and was later created Cardinal by Pope Leo XIII in 1899. His appointment as Apostolic Delegate and President of the Synod granted the assembly direct Roman authority and curial protection.
[^6]: Metropolitan Cyril II (1242–1281) convoked the Council of Volodymyr in Volhynia in 1274 to implement canonical restorations and ecclesiastical reforms following the devastation of the Mongol invasion, formally receiving the Serbian *Kormchaia Kniga* (Nomocanon) of St. Sava into the canonical law of Kyivan Rus'. Cf. Bishop Julian Pelesz, *Geschichte der Union der ruthenischen Kirche mit Rom von den ältesten Zeiten bis auf die Gegenwart*, 2 vols. (Würzburg/Vienna, 1878–1881).
[^7]: The Council of Kobryn (1626) was convoked under Metropolitan Josyf Veliamyn Rutsky (1574–1637) to reform monastic disciplines, consolidate the Order of Saint Basil the Great, and address pastoral order following the martyrdom of St. Josaphat Kuntsevych (1623). Its decrees were submitted to and approved by Pope Urban VIII. Cf. Ignatius Kulczyński, O.S.B.M., *Specimen Ecclesiae Ruthenicae* (Rome, 1733).
[^8]: Under universal canonical jurisprudence (Council of Trent, Sess. 24, c. 2 *de Reformatione*; 1917 CIC c. 286; CCEO c. 102), a decisive vote (*votum deliberativum / голосъ рѣшаючий*) is an exercise of legislative jurisdiction belonging solely to the diocesan and eparchial bishops of the province. A consultative vote (*votum consultivum / голосъ совѣтуючий*) belongs to chapter canons, protohegumens, seminary superiors, and deanery delegates, who contribute to debate, committee drafting, and synodal consultation, but do not possess legislative suffrage over the final decrees.
[^9]: Dr. Isidore Sharanevych (Iwan Szaraniewicz, 1829–1901) was an eminent Galician historian, archaeologist, Senior Professor of History at the University of Lviv, and Senior of the Stauropegion Institute of Lviv. The Stauropegion Institute, stemming from the historic 16th-century Dormition Brotherhood, enjoyed an autonomous canonical status. In recognition of this ancient dignity, its lay Senior was invited as an official synodal witness and auditor (*auditor et testis*).
[^10]: In traditional Byzantine-Ruthenian synodal protocol, a large table covered in green baize cloth (*sukno*) is prepared before the sanctuary altar or in the episcopal hall, symbolizing life, spiritual renewal, and the presence of the Holy Spirit. Upon this table are placed the Crucifix between six lit wax candles and the Holy Gospel Book, upon which all synodal participants make their profession of faith and take their solemn oaths. The bishops are vested in solemn choir dress: the episcopal *mantiya* (flowing monastic mantle with red and white ribbons / *istochniki*), small *omophorion*, *mitre*, pectoral cross (*enkolpion*) over the omophorion, and pastoral staff (*zhezl*).
[^11]: The First Vatican Council (1869–1870), *Constitutio Dogmatica Prima de Ecclesia Christi* (*Pastor Aeternus*), and its preliminary procedural regulations (c. 27), strictly mandated confidentiality concerning conciliar drafts, schema distributions, and internal oral votes to protect free theological deliberation from external political interference and secular press misrepresentation until official papal confirmation and promulgation. The synodal oath of secrecy (*secretum synodale*) was likewise standard canonical practice across post-Tridentine provincial councils.
[^12]: The Provincial Council of Orvieto (celebrated under the Archdiocese of Florence / Etruria in Central Italy), tit. 23, governing the exact wording of the obligation of secrecy imposed upon non-episcopal consultors, religious superiors, and lay auditors until acts and decrees receive recognitio from the Holy See.
[^13]: 2 Corinthians 1:3: "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies and God of all comfort" (Septuagint / Vulgate: *Benedictus Deus et Pater Domini nostri Jesu Christi, Pater misericordiarum, et Deus totius consolationis*).
[^14]: The Provincial Council of Vienna (1885), celebrated under Cardinal Archbishop Cölestin Joseph Ganglbauer, Tit. I *De ratione vitae tempore concilii ducenda*, which enjoined intensified communal prayer, reception of the Holy Mysteries, fasting, and works of charity upon the faithful and clergy during the celebration of provincial assemblies to invoke divine assistance upon the synodal deliberations.
[^15]: The Provincial Council of Vienna (1885), Tit. I, cap. 3, confirming ancient canonical jurisprudence (Council of Trent, Sess. 24, c. 2 *de Reformatione*) forbidding any prelate or synodal member who has taken his seat from departing from the council prior to its solemn closure without legitimate cause formally examined by the Court of Excuses (*Judices Excusationum*) and approved by the Metropolitan.
[^16]: The Provincial Council of Cologne (1859), celebrated under Cardinal Archbishop Johannes von Geissel, Tit. VII *De Testibus Synodalibus*, reviving the ancient canonical office of synodal witnesses (*testes synodales*) established in the medieval Church and reaffirmed by Pope Benedict XIV (*De Synodo Dioecesana*, lib. II, cap. 5). The synodal witnesses were upright clerics appointed for each diocese to oversee the promulgation and uniform enforcement of conciliar decrees and to report disciplinary abuses to the Metropolitan prior to the next provincial synod.
[^17]: The Provincial Council of Cologne (1859), Tit. VII, prescribing the statutory constitution of two synodal witnesses per diocese and detailing the procedure for their nomination and confirmation before the close of the council.
[^18]: The Provincial Council of Cologne (1859), Tit. VII, formula of the solemn oath (*juramentum testium synodalium*) taken upon the Holy Gospels by the appointed synodal witnesses, binding them to investigate and faithfully report infractions of conciliar statutes and moral discipline to the Metropolitan without partiality or human respect.
[^19]: The Provincial Council of Cologne (1859), Tit. VIII *De subscriptione et clausura Concilii*, setting forth the canonical protocol whereby all voting members and consultative participants subscribe the conciliar acts at the solemn closing session prior to the formal recess of the assembly.
[^20]: The Provincial Council of Cologne (1859), celebrated under Cardinal Johannes von Geissel, Titulus VIII *De subscriptione et clausura Concilii*, establishing the formal canonical procedure whereby the presiding Metropolitan queries the assembled conciliar fathers for their definitive consent (*placet*) prior to promulgating the decree of conciliar dissolution.
[^21]: Ibid., Provincial Council of Cologne (1859), Titulus VIII, setting forth the solemn statutory decree of conclusion and closure of the provincial council (*Decretum de clausura Concilii*), followed by the solemn thanksgiving (*Deo gratias*) and the formal charging of the conciliar notaries to compile and authenticate the synodal acts.
[^22]: Pope Leo XIII, Encyclical Letter *Grande Munus* (September 30, 1880), *Acta Sanctae Sedis* 13 (1880), pp. 145–153. In this seminal apostolic encyclical, Pope Leo XIII extended the liturgical feast and veneration of Saints Cyril and Methodius, the Apostles to the Slavs, to the universal Church. The Pontiff extolled the historical planting of the Catholic faith among the Slavic peoples, underscored the paternal solicitude of the Apostolic See toward Eastern Christendom, and affirmed the venerable antiquity, liturgical dignity, and canonical integrity of the Byzantine-Slavonic patrimony within Catholic communion.
