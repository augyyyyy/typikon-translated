The Greeks likewise take the Sunday concerning Zacchaeus immediately before the Triodion, yet only in four cases, that is, if between the Sunday after the Enlightenment and the Triodion there be either one Sunday, or two, or three, or four; and if there be one Sunday, in it they take the Sunday which is the 32nd among the Slavs (the 15th of Luke among the Greeks) concerning Zacchaeus; if there be two Sundays, they take first the 29th Sunday (the 12th of Luke among the Greeks) concerning the Ten Lepers, then the 32nd concerning Zacchaeus; if there be three Sundays, they take the 29th, the 32nd, and the 17th of Matthew, which is concerning the Canaanite woman; and if four, they take the 29th, the 30th, the 31st concerning the Blind Man, and the 17th of Matthew. (Thus have the *Evangelistaria* of the Greeks; thus also Leo Allatius in Assemani, *Calend. Univ. Eccl.*, Tom. VI, p. 516).
