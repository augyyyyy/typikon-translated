[^559]: The Akathist Order, that is, the Non-sitting [Hymn], is chanted in memory of the thrice-repeated deliverance of Constantinople from the invasion of the heathen, and this by the intercession of the Great Mother of God, whose precious Robe, brought from Jerusalem and placed in the Blachernae Church by Saint Pulcheria, and her icon under the name Hodegetria, painted according to the opinion of the Greeks by Saint Luke, they carried around the City, calling upon her aid. The first invasion was under the Emperor Heraclius (reigned 610 to 641) by the Persian army under the command of Satrap Sarbaros, and likewise by the Scythian army under the command of the Khagan. It was defeated partly by a small number of Christian Warriors who went out against the adversaries from the Church of the Most Holy Theotokos; and partly by a violent tempest at sea, which destroyed them with their ships. The second invasion was 36 years later, namely under Constantine Pogonatus (668–685), and ended partly by pestilence among the soldiers, and partly by a tempest at sea, when they were returning home. The third was under Leo the Isaurian (717–741), and ended by a tempest at sea, and famine, and the division of their forces against the Bulgars, by whom they were crushed. (*Synaxarion* of this Saturday, concerning which also Nilles, *Kalendarium Manuale*, Vol. 2, p. 152).
