### Instruction Concerning the Saturdays and Sundays Between the Nativity and Theophany

Between the Nativity and Theophany there may be:
1. One Saturday, and one Sunday.
2. Two Saturdays, and one Sunday.
3. One Saturday, and two Sundays.
4. Two Saturdays, and two Sundays.

This difference regarding the number of Saturdays and Sundays depends on the day of the week on which the Nativity of Christ occurs, as is evident from the following Table.

If the Nativity of Christ occurs on Sunday, or Monday, then between the Nativity and Theophany there is one Saturday, and one Sunday, as thou shalt see in the 1st and 2nd transverse row of weekly letters.
If the Nativity of Christ occurs on Tuesday, then there are two Saturdays, and one Sunday, as thou shalt see in the 3rd row.
If the Nativity of Christ is on Wednesday, Thursday, or Friday, then there are two Saturdays, and two Sundays, as thou seest in the 4th, 5th, and 6th rows.
If the Nativity of Christ is on Saturday, then there is one Saturday, and two Sundays, as thou seest in the 7th row.

Now therefore, if thou desirest to know how many Saturdays and Sundays there will be in a given year between the Nativity and Theophany, thou needest first of all to know on which day of the week the Nativity of Christ occurs, then proceeding from that day of the week in a transverse line to the right even unto Theophany, thou shalt find the number of Saturdays and Sundays between the Nativity and Theophany. Regarding the Typikon for these Saturdays and Sundays, for the case when there are two Saturdays and two Sundays (as in the 4th, 5th, and 6th rows) there will be no difficulty whatever, just as there will be no difficulty in the case when there are either two Saturdays, or two Sundays (as in the 3rd and 7th rows): for these two Saturdays and two Sundays have their own fixed Typikon; but the entire difficulty is confined to the case of only a single Saturday or Sunday (as in the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, and 7th rows). For this case, therefore, two questions are posed:
1. Which Saturday or Sunday of the two is then to be taken? That after the Nativity, or that before Theophany?

---

[Leaf p210 / Page 208]

2. What is to be done with the other Saturday or Sunday, which then has no place?

To the 1st question we answer: that if this single Saturday or single Sunday is found within the Feast of the Nativity, that is, within the limits of December, then the Saturday or Sunday after the Nativity is taken; but if outside the Feast of the Nativity, that is, within the limits of January, then the Saturday or Sunday before Theophany is taken.

Regarding the 2nd question, that is, what is to be done with that Saturday or Sunday which sometimes has no place: we answer that the Greek books provide no Typikon whatever concerning this, and for this reason they would have to be omitted; nevertheless the Slavs do not wish to omit anything, and provide for this case the following Typika.

### Typikon for the Saturdays and Sundays Between the Nativity of Christ and Theophany

#### Saturday
A Saturday, if between the Nativity and Theophany there be only one, then this may occur either within or outside the Feast of the Nativity of Christ: within the Feast, namely on December 30 or 31, as in the 1st and 2nd rows; outside the Feast, namely on January 1, as in the 7th row. When, therefore, it occurs within the Feast, then the Apostle and Gospel will be first for the Saturday after the Nativity, then for the Saturday before Theophany; but the Prokimenon, Alleluia, and Communion Hymn on December 30 will be only for the Saturday after the Nativity; while on December 31, that is, on the Apodosis of the Feast, it will be only of the Feast. When, however, it occurs outside the Feast,

---

[Leaf p211 / Page 209]

as in the 7th row, that is, on January 1, then the Apostle and Gospel will be first for the Circumcision, then for the Saturday before Theophany; but the Prokimenon, Alleluia, and Communion Hymn only for the Circumcision, because the Saturday does not have these. And for the Saturday after the Nativity, the Apostle and Gospel are taken on the Apodosis of the Feast, December 31; but the Prokimenon, Alleluia, and Communion Hymn only of the Feast.

#### Sunday
A Sunday, if between the Nativity and Theophany there be only one, then, similarly as also the Saturday, it occurs either within the Feast, that is, on December 30 or 31, as in the 3rd and 2nd rows, or outside the Feast, that is, on January 1 with the Circumcision, as in the 1st row. And if, therefore, it occurs within the Feast, then the Sunday after the Nativity is taken on December 30 or 31, according to the Typikon set forth here on pages 204 and 205; while for the Sunday before Theophany, it is taken on January 1 in the first place, then of the Circumcision, and all the rest according to the Typikon that is to be set forth for January 1 when it occurs on Sunday.
