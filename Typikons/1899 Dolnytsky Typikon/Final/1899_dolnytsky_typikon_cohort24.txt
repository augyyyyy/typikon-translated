the Holy Mysteries from the top of the Tabernacle (*Skynia*), and having placed them upon the Iliton, steps back a little to the side; and the Hierarch (or presiding Priest), having censed the Holy Mysteries thrice according to custom, and put on the Omophorion, standing up and ascending the steps, and having bowed down, receives the Holy Mysteries from the hands of the Deacon, turns toward the people, and when he begins to sing the Sticheron **"Jesus is risen from the tomb"** (*Воскре́съ І҆исꙋ́съ ѿ гро́ба*), while all the bells chime joyfully, the Procession around the church takes place, in all respects as was depicted on Great Friday at Vespers, here on page 445, except for the Plashchanytsia, which here, by order of Cardinal Sembratovych of blessed memory, is not to be carried, because it does not agree with the joy of the Resurrection; rather, it is fitting to carry the Icon of the Resurrection, as the Triodion has it at the beginning of Paschal Matins during the Procession to the church doors. And when the Superior begins to sing the Sticheron **"Jesus is risen from the tomb"**, the Choirs and the people follow in singing the same, and they sing it, repeating it until the Procession is completed, that is, a threefold, or at least a single circuit around the church, which when it is completed, all enter into the church, while the banner-bearers and icon-bearers remain before the church doors to avoid commotion during the second Procession; while in the church the cross-bearer with two candle-bearers, the Brotherhood, the Choirs, and the Clergy arrange themselves for the Procession; and when the sacred ministers have entered into the Sanctuary, the Superior ascends the Altar, and having turned toward the people, blesses them with the Holy Mysteries, saying in a gentle voice: **"Save, O God, Thy people, and bless Thine inheritance"** (*Спасѝ, Бо́же, лю́ди Твоя̀, и҆ благословѝ достоя́нїе Твоѐ*); and having set down the Holy Mysteries upon the Altar, he descends before the steps, bends his knees, and having laid aside the Omophorion, censes the Holy Mysteries thrice according to custom; and the Deacon, having approached the Holy Mysteries and bowed deeply, removes them with the lunette from the Monstrance (*Darookazalnytsia*), and places them into the Tabernacle (*Kivot*),

---

[Leaf p462 / Page 460]

and the Tabernacle (*Kivot*) into the Ark (*Skynia*), and having bowed down, closes it. And where there is no Monstrance (*Darookazalnytsia*), the Holy Mysteries are exposed with the Tabernacle (*Kivot*), and with it are closed in the Ark (*Skynia*).

*Note.* This Procession with the Holy Mysteries before Paschal Matins is a later adornment of our Churches, borrowed neither from the Greeks nor from the Romans, but from the Latin Churches of these regions, and an added magnificence to our Paschal Matins; nor is it set down in our Typika.

## Paschal Matins

1. **Procession Before the Church Doors.** As all the people go out from the church, the sacred ministers likewise go out after them, and the Procession takes place thus: when the sacred ministers have taken their stand, in all their bright sacred vestments, before the steps of the Holy Table, the Superior takes the Cross, and the Deacon the Censer; and when all have bowed equally and deeply toward the Holy Mysteries and turned toward the west, the Superior begins to sing the Sticheron **"Thy Resurrection, O Christ Savior"** (*Воскресе́нїе Твоѐ, Христѐ Спа́се*); and while all follow in singing the same, they go out from the Sanctuary, the Priests indeed through the Royal Doors, and the other sacred ministers through the Deacon's Doors into the church, and while all the bells are ringing, they go even unto the great Church Doors; and having gone outside the church, they stand orderly, facing the church doors, which are immediately closed.[^619] And inside the church there are placed upon the

---

[Leaf p463 / Page 461]

Holy Table, first of all the Icon of the Resurrection of Christ, usually upon the Artos, and other venerable Icons, and Reliquaries, and the most precious Gospels, or whatever else of sacred things, all these according to the number of Priests who intend to give them to the people to kiss at the end of Matins; while outside before the church doors the beginning of Matins takes place thus.[^620]

2. **Beginning of Matins.** Immediately upon the closing of the church doors, the presiding Priest, holding in his left hand the Cross, and in his right the Censer, and censing the church doors,[^621] proclaims with a loud voice: **"Glory to the holy..."** (*Сла́ва ст҃ѣ́й*), and the rest.

---

[Leaf p464 / Page 462]

and when the Choirs have answered: **"Amen"**, having handed over the Censer, he sings with a loud voice the Troparion **"Christ is risen"**, then all sing the same; and a second time the Priest sings the same, and all the same; and a third time the Priest sings the same, and all the same; then the Priest in a clear voice proclaims the Verses, while all after each verse chant the same Troparion once.[^622] Finally the Priest, having sung in a higher voice the same Troparion only up to **"trampling down death by death"** inclusively, opens the doors, while all conclude: **"and upon those in the tombs bestowing life"**; and while all the bells are rung again, all enter into the church.[^623] But the Morning Prayers at

---

[Leaf p465 / Page 463]

Paschal Matins throughout all of Bright Week are not said, because these are linked to the Six Psalms, and since the latter here give way, the Prayers also give way. If the Prayers were to be said, the Typikon would have had to indicate where to say them, for during the Verses **"Let God arise"**, which occupy the place of the Six Psalms, they cannot be said, for at that time the Typikon commands the Priest to pronounce the verses, and not to add the Prayers besides.

3. **Entrance into the Church.** The Entrance of the Procession into the church is made in the reverse order from that in which it went out from the church, that is, first the Superior enters, then the other Priests and sacred ministers, and the entire Clergy, then the Chanters, the Brotherhood, the banner-bearers and icon-bearers, the cross-bearer, and all the people. And the Priests indeed enter into the Sanctuary and approach the Holy Table, while the Hierarch, if there be one, ascends his Throne; and the Deacon takes his stand at his place before the Royal Doors, that he may sing the Ektenia from there.

4. After the entrance into the church, the first Deacon says the Great Ektenia **"In peace let us pray to the Lord"** (*Ми́ромъ Го́споду помоли́мся*), standing at the accustomed place, and immediately we sing the Canon.

5. **The Paschal Canon:** Heirmoi 4 times (in the Greek 2 times), and Troparia for 12. (Today Heirmoi once, and Troparia with the Heirmos for 4). Katavasia after each Ode, the Heirmos of the Canon, by both Choirs together

---

[Leaf p466 / Page 464]

once. The Priest sings the Heirmoi, and the Choirs sing the Troparia and Katavasia. Refrain to the Troparia of the Canon: **"Christ is risen from the dead"** (*Христо́съ воскре́се и҆зъ ме́ртвыхъ*); at the penultimate, **"Glory"** (*Сла́ва*), and at the last, **"Both now"** (*И҆ ны́нѣ*), according to custom.

At the beginning of the Canon, after the singing of the Heirmos, the Priest censes all things according to custom.[^624]

The Little Ektenia after each Ode is said by the Deacons standing at their accustomed place before the Royal Doors, with their proper Exclamations sung in turn by the Priests in the holy Sanctuary.[^625]

After the 3rd Ode, the Hypakoe; after the 6th, the Kontakion and Ikos, and **"Having beheld the Resurrection of Christ"** (*Воскресе́нїе Христо́во ви́дѣвше*) thrice, and **"Jesus is risen from the tomb"** (*Воскре́съ І҆исꙋ́съ ѿ гро́ба*) once (according to others, thrice); at the 9th Ode, at **"The Theotokos and Mother of Light"** (*Богоро́дицу и҆ Ма́терь Свѣ́та*) the customary censing takes place, and instead of **"More honorable"** (*Честнѣ́йшꙋю*) we sing **"The Angel cried"** (*А҆́нгелъ вопїя́ше*) and **"Shine, shine"** (*Свѣти́ся, свѣти́ся*); after the 9th Ode, the Exaposteilarion of Pascha thrice.

6. **At the Praises**, 8 Stichera: 4 Resurrectional of the Octoechos, Tone 1, with the customary refrains from the Praises, and 4 of Pascha, with their proper refrains; then **"Glory... Both now..."** of Pascha, and **"Christ is risen"** thrice, then many times until the kiss is completed.

---

[Leaf p467 / Page 465]

7. **The Kiss of Peace.** This in the Typika is appointed at **"Christ is risen"** at the conclusion of the last Sticheron of Pascha; nevertheless, today we do not kiss one another, as the Sticheron has it, saying: **"and let us embrace one another"**, but only the Venerable Cross, the Gospel, and the Holy Icons held by the Priests, who greet each of those approaching with the words: **"Christ is risen"**, and receive the response: **"Truly He is risen"**. Furthermore, we begin the kiss not after the Paschal Stichera, but at their beginning at the Verse **"Let God arise"**, because of the many people approaching for the kiss. When very many approach, the kiss takes place even during the Canon. And this kiss according to our custom takes place thus: if there be only one Priest, he, standing in the Royal Doors, gives the Cross from there to the people to kiss, saying to each: **"Christ is risen"**, and the one kissing answers: **"Truly He is risen"**. If there be more Priests, first the Superior, having gone out with the Cross in his hand from the Royal Doors, stands before the Iconostasis on the right side, that is, by the Icon of the Savior, looking toward the north; and the second Priest, having gone out after him from the Royal Doors with the Gospel, approaches him, and the Superior gives him the Cross to kiss, saying: **"Christ is risen"**, and he having kissed it gives him the Gospel to kiss, answering: **"Truly He is risen"**, and takes his stand at his left hand; then the third Priest, having gone out with the Icon of the Resurrection of Christ, approaches the first, who gives him the Cross to kiss, saying: **"Christ is risen"**, and he having kissed it gives him the Icon of the Resurrection to kiss, answering: **"Truly He is risen"**; and he approaches the second Priest, who gives him the Gospel to kiss, saying: **"Christ is risen"**, and he having kissed the Gospel gives him the Icon of the Resurrection to kiss, answering: **"Truly He is risen"**, and

---

[Leaf p468 / Page 466]

takes his stand at the left hand of the second. And the fourth Priest, having gone out with the Icon of the Theotokos, approaches the first, then the second and the third, each of whom gives him his sacred object to kiss, saying: **"Christ is risen"**, and he having kissed it gives each of them the Icon of the Theotokos to kiss, answering: **"Truly He is risen"**; and in this manner do also the other Priests, as many as there be. In Cathedral Churches the Hierarch, sitting upon his Throne by the Icon of the Savior, gives the Cross to kiss, and likewise the Choir-clerics, each giving his sacred image to kiss, sitting upon the benches. When the kiss has been completed among the Priests, then the other sacred ministers and all the people approach one by one to each Priest according to rank, beginning from the first, and kiss his sacred emblem, answering: **"Truly He is risen"**.[^626] After the kiss there was sometimes read the Catechetical Homily of Chrysostom, which is in the Flowery Triodion.

8. **Ektenias:** **"Have mercy on us, O God"** (*Поми́луй на́съ, Бо́же*) and **"Let us complete"** (*И҆спо́лнимъ*), by the Deacons standing at the accustomed place.

9. **Great Dismissal**, proper, at which in place of **"More honorable"** we say **"Shine, shine"**, and in place of **"Glory... Both now..."** we say **"Christ is risen"** once. The Priest having taken his stand with the Cross in the Holy Doors, the Deacon (if there be none, the Priest himself) says: **"Wisdom!"** Choir (or second Deacon, if there be one):[^627] **"Bless!"**

Priest: **"He Who is blessed and most glorified, Christ our God, always, now and forever, and unto the ages of ages."**

Choir: **"Amen. Confirm, O God, the Catholic faith, unto the ages of ages."**

Priest: **"Most Holy Theotokos, save us!"**

Choir: **"Shine, shine, O New Jerusalem... at the resurrection of thy Son."**

Priest: **"Glory to Thee, O Christ God, our hope, glory to Thee!"**

Choir: **"Christ is risen from the dead... bestowing life. Lord, have mercy"** (thrice), **"Lord, bless!"**

Priest: Dismissal with the Cross: **"May Christ, Who is risen from the dead, trampling down death by death, and upon those in the tombs bestowing life, our true God, through the prayers of His most pure Mother, of the holy, glorious, and all-praised Apostles, of our venerable and God-bearing Fathers, and of all the Saints, have mercy on us and save us, for He is good and the Lover of mankind."**

Then elevating the Cross, he says: **"Christ is risen!"** thrice, and we each time answer: **"Truly

### Note Concerning the Doors of the Iconostasis
"Be it known also this, that the Doors of the Royal Altar, both the great ones and the small ones at the sides, throughout all the churches during all of Bright Week we do not close, even at the time of Communion" (*Typikon of our Pochaiv Triodion* and *Russian Typika*).

## Paschal Hours
For each of the four Hours, likewise also for Compline and the Midnight Office, throughout all of Bright Week we say the brief Office set down in the Triodion, and in our Horologia and Prayer Books at Paschal Matins.

*Note.* If the Hours are read together, then we say **"Blessed is our God"** only at the first, while the rest we begin from **"Christ is risen"** thrice.

## At the Divine Liturgy
All things according to the general Typikon of a Feast of the Lord; only:

1. In place of **"O Heavenly King"** at the Proskomide and at the Liturgy, and wherever it may occur, we say **"Christ is risen"** thrice, even unto the Ascension; while from the Ascension unto the

---

[Leaf p471 / Page 469]

Descent [of the Holy Spirit], in place of **"O Heavenly King"** we say nothing.[^629]

2. Concerning **"Christ is risen"** it should be known that after **"Glory to Thee, O Christ God"**, before the Dismissal of the Proskomide and Liturgy, in place of **"Glory... Both now..."**, we say **"Christ is risen"** once, **"Lord, have mercy"** thrice, and **"Lord, bless"**.

After **"Blessed is the Kingdom"** we sing **"Christ is risen"** thrice; that is, the Priest once, and both Choirs once each; (although our Triodion says: the Priest thrice, and the Choirs the same thrice each); and where there is only one Choir, the Priest once and the Choir once, then half by the Priest, and half (from **"and upon those in the tombs"**) by the Choir.

Likewise also after **"With fear of God"**, in place of **"Blessed is He that cometh"**, and in place of **"We have seen the true Light"**, **"Christ is risen"** once; while in place of **"Let our mouths be filled"** and **"Blessed be the name of the Lord"**, **"Christ is risen"** thrice.[^630] Our people usually say **"Christ

---

[Leaf p472 / Page 470]

is risen"** also in place of **"Be Thou exalted above the heavens, O God"**; nevertheless, this impairs the memorial of the Mystery of Christ's Ascension, which was originally instituted at this place of the Liturgy. Our Priests also say **"Christ is risen"** (only these two words) after **"With fear of God"**, and after **"Save, O God"**, and after **"Always, now and forever"**; nevertheless, in no Typikon do we find this.

3. In place of the Trisagion we sing **"All who have been baptized into Christ"** (*Є҆ли́цы во Хрїста̀ крести́стеся*).

4. At the Reading of the Gospel, after each verse they strike all the bells.[^631]

"If the Liturgy is celebrated concelebratedly" (say our Sluzhebnyks), "then this Gospel is read by all the Priests and Deacons by verses, as the Superior shall arrange." And concerning this we have a detailed Typikon in our Triodia and the Russian ones. Nevertheless, to read one and the same Gospel by all the Priests and Deacons is a superfluous invention of the Graeco-Slavs, which today among us has a place only when the Gospel is to be

---

[Leaf p473 / Page 471]

read in various languages, as takes place in our Cathedral Churches, wherein the Hierarch reads the same in the Greek tongue from the Holy Doors, then the Deacon in Slavonic from the Ambo, and at the end the Choir-clerics and Priests in various liturgical languages, positioned throughout the church. In Russia the Hierarch reads in Slavonic, the other Priests in Hebrew, Greek, and Latin, in which the Title of the Cross was written, and then in other modern languages (Muralt, *Epistle 14*, p. 142). The Greeks likewise read the Gospel according to modern custom in various languages, yet not at the Liturgy, but at Vespers (the Gospel which is read at Vespers of this day), as takes place in the Patriarchate of Constantinople.[^632]

5. In place of **"It is truly right"**, according to the new Typikon we sing the Heirmos **"Shine, shine"**, with the refrain **"The Angel cried"**, as the Triodion and Constantinople Typikon have it, and not also with the Troparia of the 9th Ode of the Canon, as our Sluzhebnyks have, because these Troparia are not concerning the Most Holy Theotokos, but concerning Christ's Resurrection.

6. [7 in original] Before the Dismissal, in place of **"More honorable"** we say **"Shine, shine"**, and in place of **"Glory... Both now..."**: **"Christ is risen"** once; the Dismissal itself with the Cross, and the rest that follow after the Dismissal with the elevation of the Cross, without change as indicated at Paschal Matins.

7. **Blessing of the Artos.** After the Ambo Prayer[^633] takes place the blessing of the Artos, for which thou hast a prayer in the Sluzhebnik after the Liturgy of Pascha; and at the end of the prayer, the sprinkling of the Artos with holy water. And this

Artos with the Feast, that is, with the Icon of the Resurrection, after the Dismissal of the Liturgy is carried out from the Sanctuary where it was blessed onto the Tetrapod, where it remains even unto Bright Saturday, and is kissed by the faithful entering the church. On Bright Saturday at the Liturgy after the Ambo Prayer, the Priest having gone out from the Holy Doors reads over it the Prayer for the Breaking thereof, set down in our Sluzhebnik only in the order of Bright Saturday; and thereafter having been taken from the Tetrapod, it is broken in the Sacristy, and on the morrow, that is, on Thomas Sunday after the Ambo Prayer, it is distributed to the faithful by the Priest standing in the Holy Doors.[^634]

## General Typika from Thomas Sunday unto the Ascension
The divine services from Thomas Sunday even unto the Ascension could be compressed into a general Rule within a Feast of the Lord. But since each Sunday, beginning from Thomas Sunday, has a distinct Title and a distinct Office extending to the other weekdays of the same week, and is as it were another Feast within the Feast of Pascha, for this reason in place of the Paschal Office there enters the Office of the Sunday, likewise also on weekdays, together with certain parts of the Resurrection Office of the Octoechos, except for the eight-day Feast of Mid-Pentecost, to which on weekdays the Sunday Office yields. The general rules of this time are the following:

1. At the beginning of every office, in place of **"O Heavenly King"** we sing **"Christ is risen"** thrice;[^640] and at the conclusion of every divine service, according to our custom, **"Christ is risen"** thrice; then at the conclusion we add: **"And unto us

---

[Leaf p480 / Page 478]

**He hath granted eternal life: we worship His three-day resurrection"**;[^641] and before the Dismissal, in place of **"More honorable"** we sing **"Shine, shine"**.[^642]

2. **At Vespers:** On Sunday evening for Monday an Entrance does not take place, as takes place on the Sundays of Great Lent (for here there is no Great Prokimenon, and the Triodia appoint the Ektenia **"Have mercy on us, O God"** at the conclusion of Vespers), except on Thomas Sunday, which, as a Feast of the Lord, has also a Great Prokimenon. For the rest, in the provinces on all Sunday evenings they make the Entrance even without a Feast.

3. **At Compline:** From this day we begin to sing the Canons to the Theotokos. After **"It is truly right"** we say throughout the whole week the Kontakion of the Sunday; only during the octave of the Feast of Mid-Pentecost, the Kontakion of Mid-Pentecost.

4. **The Midnight Office** we sing according to custom; only after the 1st Trisagion on the Feast of Mid-Pentecost we say the Troparion of Mid-Pentecost, and at the Apodosis of Pascha the Sunday Resurrection Troparion with its Theotokion; and after the 2nd Trisagion within Mid-Pentecost we say the Kontakion of Mid-Pentecost, but outside Mid-Pentecost the Kontakion of Pascha; while the prayers **"Remember, O Lord"** we do not say.

5. **Matins** we begin from **"Glory to the holy..."**, and immediately **"Christ is risen"** thrice, then **"Glory to God in the highest"**, and the rest; and before Psalm 50 we say **"Having beheld the Resurrection"**\n
