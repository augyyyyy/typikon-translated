### On Thursday After the Sunday of All Saints
#### Feast of the Most Holy Eucharist
**Transferred to the 2nd Sunday After the Descent.**

This Feast is celebrated with an Octave (*osmodnéviye*), from the Thursday after the Sunday of All Saints unto the following Thursday, on which it also has its Apodosis. But since according to our custom it is transferred from Thursday to the following Sunday, on this account the Synod of Lviv commands that its Service be sung on Sunday, with a Vigil, unalterably as it is set down in the Triodion on the Thursday of the Feast itself, according to the General Typikon of a First-Class Feast of the Lord, without the Resurrectional [hymns]; and on the days preceding and following this Sunday to sing it according to the General Typikon Inside a Feast of the Lord. ### Typikon I
#### Concerning the Exposition of the Holy Mysteries at the Liturgy

The Priest, on one of the days preceding the Feast, consecrates at the Liturgy two Lambs; and from the one he communicates, but the other, placing it in the Pyx (*Kivot*), he preserves until the day of the Feast. All these things he performs according to the Typikon written for Great Thursday, here on page 435, in the Note. When the day of the Feast itself arrives, after the conclusion of the Proskomide, immediately before the beginning of the Divine Liturgy, the Deacons (or the Priest himself) having censed all things according to custom, there takes place the Exposition

---

[Leaf p508 / Page 506]

of the Holy Mysteries, thus: The ranking Deacon (the one at the right hand of the Priest), having laid aside the Gospel Book to the right side of the Holy Table, and having opened the Tabernacle (*Skíniya*), and having bowed profoundly toward the Holy Mysteries, brings forth the Pyx (*Kivot*) from the Tabernacle, all having bended their knees, and places it upon the folded Iliton; and if there is an Ostensorium (*Daroukazálnitsya* / Monstrance), having opened the Pyx, and having taken the Lamb out of it, not touching it with the fingers, but with the forceps in which it is held, places the Lamb into the receptacle (*rovéts*) of the Ostensorium, and having closed it well, and having placed the Pyx back into the Tabernacle, and having bowed toward it, and having closed the Tabernacle, sets the Ostensorium upon the Iliton, turned with its front side toward the people, and steps aside a little to the right. And the Priest, having placed incense into the Censer, and having blessed it, saying: **"Blessed is our God"** (*Благослове́нъ Бо́гъ на́шъ*), censes the Holy Mysteries with both hands thrice, bowing his head after each censing; and having thus taken upon himself the humeral veil (*Omofórets*) with the assistance of the second Deacon, and having approached the Holy Table, and having bowed profoundly, takes, with the assistance of the first Deacon, with both hands through the humeral veil the Ostensorium, and having turned its back side toward himself, turns by his right toward the people, and sings slowly and devoutly the beginning of the first stanza: **"O Saving Victim"** (*Ѡ҆ спаси́тельная Же́ртво*); all the people following with the remainder of that stanza. And again the Priest [sings] the beginning of the second stanza: **"To the One God in Trinity"** (*Є҆ди́номꙋ въ Тро́йци Бо́гꙋ*); while all follow with the rest, he blesses the people with Them crosswise, completing a circle from the south to the north side; and having thus turned by the north side toward the Holy Table, he sets the Ostensorium upon the Iliton, turning it, with the Deacon assisting, with its front side toward the people; then having stepped aside a little to the left, he descends before the step of the Holy Table in such a way that his back is not turned toward the Holy Mysteries, bends his knees, and having laid aside the humeral veil, and having censed again

---

[Leaf p509 / Page 507]

the Holy Mysteries thrice, as before, waits there kneeling until the Deacon, having approached the middle of the Holy Table, and having bowed profoundly, sets the Holy Mysteries atop the Tabernacle. This having been done, the Priest, having ascended the steps, begins the Liturgy from **"Blessed is our God"** (*Благослове́нъ Бо́гъ на́шъ*), **"O Heavenly King"** (*Царю̀ небе́сный*), and the rest. During the Liturgy, the Sacred Ministers never turn their backs toward the Holy Mysteries, as our Sluzhebniks command, saying: *"When the Priest, according to the order of the rites, is to turn his face to the people, he ought not to turn from the middle of the sanctuary, lest he turn his back to the Body of Christ, but having made a profound bow, and having stepped somewhat toward the left side, from this side he turns to the people, and having turned back to the sanctuary, he makes a profound bow to the Body of Christ; and where the Priest is to make a full circle, as at 'Always, now and ever' (*Всегда̀, ны́нѣ и҆ при́снѡ*), when he turns with the Holy Mysteries, at that time he does not complete a full circle in turning, lest he turn his back to the Body of Christ."*[^666] We add also this: that when the Priest, or the Deacon, goes forth before the Iconostasis for the censing, he is not to cense from the middle, nor turn his back toward the Holy Mysteries, but to stand somewhat to the side, the Priest indeed from the side of the Theotokos, and the Deacons, one from the Savior's side, the other from the Theotokos' side.

2. After the cleansing of the Chalice, the Priest does not place the Gospel Book upon the Iliton, since on the Holy Table it will be necessary after the Prayer behind the Ambo to set the Holy Mysteries.
3. If there are no Deacons, the Priest himself performs all these things.

### Typikon II
#### Concerning the Procession with the Holy Mysteries After the Prayer Behind the Ambo

After the conclusion of the Prayer behind the Ambo, the Sacred Ministers having stood before the step of the Holy Table, the first Deacon, having ascended the step, and having bowed profoundly, takes down the Holy Mysteries from atop the Tabernacle, and having set Them upon the folded Iliton, steps aside a little to his own side. And all having bended their knees, the Priest, having censed the Holy Mysteries thrice, and having taken upon himself the humeral veil, ascends the step, and having taken hold of the Holy Mysteries with both hands, with the Deacon assisting, and having turned to the people, as at the beginning of the Liturgy, and having intoned the beginning of the Troparion: **"Bread Supernatural"** (*Хлѣ́бъ преесте́ственный*), or some other devout hymn, goes forth by the Holy Doors into the church, two Deacons preceding with Censers; and having entered beneath the Canopy (*Baldakhin*), advances unto the doors of the church; and with the bells chiming, the Procession proceeds from the church unto the first Altar, the Choirs and the people following singing the Troparion, or something else. And the order of the Procession is as follows: First of all goes the Cross-bearer between two Taper-bearers, then the Banner-bearers and Icon-bearers, then four Maidens carrying icons, after them the Church Brotherhood, two by two, with candles, the Choirs, and all the Clergy two by two with candles, according to the order of their dignity, the Priests nearest in Phelonia, the others in Epitrakhelia, and last of all the celebrant Priest with the Holy Mysteries, two Deacons preceding and unceasingly censing from the side, and Taper-bearers surrounding the Canopy. When

# Part V
## Typikon Concerning Temples

The Temple Typika (*Ustavy Khramoviyi*) in use among us are of a twofold kind: some are borrowed from the local Western Churches, which do not enter into the original fabric of our Divine Services, but have been attached to it; and others are innate to our Divine Services. The typika borrowed from the Western Churches relate to the Holy Mysteries of the Eucharist, which on the day of the Patronal Feast are exposed upon the Holy Table, and with Them a Procession takes place around the church; and concerning these it behooves us first to set forth the Typikon, and afterwards concerning the rest.

### Chapter I
#### Concerning the Exposition of the Holy Mysteries Upon the Holy Table

The Priest, on one of the days preceding the Feast, consecrates at the Liturgy two Lambs, and of the one he communicates, but the other, having placed it into the Pyx (*Kivot*), he preserves until the day of the Feast. All these things he performs according to the Typikon written for Great Thursday, here on page 435. When the feast-day itself arrives, after the conclusion of the Proskomide, immediately before the beginning of the Liturgy, the Deacons (or the Priest himself) having censed all things

---

[Leaf p515 / Page 513]

according to custom, the Exposition of the Holy Mysteries takes place unalterably as was indicated on the Feast of the Eucharist, here on page 512.
After the Prayer behind the Ambo, the Holy Mysteries are hidden in the Tabernacle, and before the beginning of Vespers are exposed again; for it is not fitting to leave the Holy Mysteries without adorers, and without a sufficient number of candles, of which according to the Synod of Lviv there are to be at least six of wax.[^667]

### § 2. Concerning the Procession with the Holy Mysteries
#### At Vespers

Before the beginning of Vespers, the Priest vested in all the priestly vestments, and the Deacon in all the deacon's vestments, having come before the steps of the Holy Table, open the Holy Doors; and the first Deacon, having laid aside the Gospel Book to the right, takes the Holy Mysteries from the Tabernacle, and sets Them upon the Iliton; and the Priest having censed Them thrice, in all things as at the beginning of the Liturgy, he sets Them atop the Tabernacle upon the folded Iliton, and having placed the Gospel Book again in the middle of the Holy Table, exclaims: **"Bless, Master"** (*Благословѝ, Влады́ко*); and the Priest begins: **"Blessed is our God"** (*Благослове́нъ Бо́гъ на́шъ*), standing with the Deacons by the Holy Table; and Vespers is sung with the Entrance, the Priest stepping away from the Holy Table only at the Entrance, and the Deacons also at the Ektenia, and after the Prokeimenon for the Sermon, if this is ever to take place, the Holy Mysteries being veiled by the Tabernacle.
Before the Dismissal of Vespers, all having bended their knees, the Deacon, having taken the Sacred Gospel Book from the middle of the Holy Table, and having set it on the right side thereof, takes down

### Chapter II
## Temple Typika
### Entering into the Fabric of Our Divine Services

Such Typika are either general or proper. The general apply to many cases, the proper only to certain ones. We set forth first the general Typika, and then the proper ones.

### § 1. General Typika[^669]

1. On a Patronal Feast, even if it be of a Saint of the lowest rank, an All-Night Vigil must be celebrated. Thus do we perform, and thus has the *Tserkovne Oko* in Chapter 4 concerning Temples.[^670]
2. The Feast of the Patron Saint of a Temple, even if of the lowest rank, takes precedence even over the Feast of a Saint with a Vigil, first in that if it occurs on a Sunday, it excludes at Matins before the Canon the Prokeimenon, Gospel, and Resurrectional Sticheron, unalterably as does a Feast of the Theotokos, and likewise transfers the Gospel Sticheron from the Praises to the end of Matins. Besides this, if it occurs

---

[Leaf p519 / Page 517]

on a weekday, it excludes at the Liturgy the Epistle and Gospel of the day, which are then read on the day preceding the Feast. Finally, the Patronal Feast of a Saint has its own Apodosis, which takes place on the very day of the Feast at Vespers toward the following day, and this with an Entrance, like a Feast of the Lord. There will be therefore at this Apodosis at **"Lord, I have cried"** (*Го́споди воззва́хъ*), 6 Stichera: 3 to the Temple [Saint], and 3 to the Saint of the day; **"Glory"** (*Сла́ва*) to the Temple; **"Both now"** (*И҆ ны́нѣ*), Theotokion from the Resurrectional ones (Dogmatikon) according to the Tone of the Doxastikon (*Naslávnyk*). If the Temple is dedicated to the Lord, or to the Theotokos, then both **"Glory... Both now..."** (*Сла́ва, и҆ ны́нѣ*) of the Temple. On Friday evening toward Saturday, we always sing the Dogmatikon of the departing Tone. — After the Prokeimenon: **"Let us all say"** (*Рцѣ́мъ всѝ*). At the Aposticha, [Stichera] of the Temple with its refrains; **"Glory"** (*Сла́ва*) of the Temple; **"Both now"** (*И҆ ны́нѣ*), Theotokion from the Resurrectional ones according to the Tone of the Doxastikon. If the Temple is dedicated to the Lord or to the Theotokos, then **"Both now"** (*И҆ ны́нѣ*) of the Temple. At the conclusion, Troparion of the Temple; **"Glory"** (*Сла́ва*), Troparion of the Saint of the day; **"Both now"** (*И҆ ны́нѣ*), Theotokion from the Resurrectional ones according to the Tone of the Doxastikon. If the Temple is dedicated to the Lord, or to the Theotokos, then Troparion of the Saint of the day; **"Glory... Both now..."** (*Сла́ва, и҆ ны́нѣ*), of the Temple. Great Dismissal with commemoration of the Temple, and of the Saint of the day. With this Vespers the Service of the Patron Saint of the Temple is concluded; however, the Patron Saint of the Temple has no Apodosis if on the morrow there occurs a Sunday, or a Saint with Polyeleos.
3. the Praises, **"Glory"** (*Сла́ва*) to the Temple, but the Gospel Sticheron after the Dismissal of Matins.[^672]
4. On the very day of the Feast after the Prayer behind the Ambo, commonly in our lands there takes place the Blessing of Waters outside the church, whither the Procession goes, the Cross-bearer preceding, and the Censer-bearer, and the Deacon with the Gospel Book, and the Priest with the Hand Cross. And the church servers ought to carry thither also the Incense Boat (*Korablets*) with incense, and the Aspergillum (*Kropylo*) with the Aspergil-bucket (*Kropilnitsya*), and a vial (*stklyanitsya*), and a small towel (*obrusets*) for wiping the Cross upon its immersion into the water, and the Priest's hands. After the Dismissal of the Liturgy there takes place the Polychronion (*Mnoholitstviye*) to the Ecumenical Pontiff, and to the Emperor, and to the Metropolitan, and to the local Hierarch, and to all the Sacred Ministers present, and to the people of the Parish.
5. On the morrow of the Feast, according to the local custom, the Liturgy is sung for the Departed of the Parish, with a Parastas, at which it is not fitting to take anything of the Feast, but all things only for the Repose.
6. The Feast of the Patron Saint of a Temple is commonly transferred among us to the following Sunday; but in Cathedral Churches it is celebrated on its own day.
