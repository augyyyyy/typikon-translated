**3.** It is surely known that a distinction exists between fasting and abstinence. Everyone knows that strict fasting permits eating to fullness only once a day; nevertheless, in this matter one must keep in view the local custom and need.

**4.** The exact observance of all the fasts prescribed in our Church the Synod indeed commends to the faithful and praises the strict observance of many among them; it likewise obligates pastors of souls to explain properly in sermons to the people their excellence and benefit, and what is more, by their own example to promote that observance, so that by this means, as far as possible, they may draw near to the most illustrious examples of our ancestors and to the zeal of the first Christians. Nevertheless, because in these recent times the zeal of many Christians has grown cold, and bodily strength seems gradually to be weakened such that the ecclesiastical fasts are very frequently transgressed, therefore, lest the sins of the people multiply on account of such transgression, the Synod, having received authority from the Holy See, judges in the Lord to mitigate somewhat the original severity of fasting, and that according to the following rule:

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Synodal Mitigation of Fasting Rules (1891)**
> 
> * **a) Forty-Day Fasts**: In the forty-day fast, both before Pascha as well as before the Nativity of the Lord and before the feast of the holy Apostles Peter and Paul, as also before the feast of the Dormition of the Most Holy Mother of God, fast is to be observed on every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday, on which dairy foods (*nabyl*) are permitted; on the remaining four days of the week, the eating of meat is permitted, yet so that on those four days they make up for the fast, namely the clergy by reciting Psalm 50, and the others by 5 *Our Fathers* and *Rejoice, Mother of God, Virgin*; and that both before dinner as well as before supper.
> * **b) Other Fasts**: The regulation set forth above regarding the permission of dairy foods is valid likewise with respect to all other fasts of the year.
> * **c) Strict Seasons Excepted**: From that general law, however, must be excepted: the entire first week of the Great Fast (Forty Days), that is, 6 days from Monday to Saturday inclusive, as well as the Great and Holy Week, from Monday right up to Saturday before the Sunday of the Resurrection, in which the eating of meat is entirely forbidden; the consumption of dairy foods is permitted on Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday; on the remaining three days all customary foods are to be consumed, that is, without dairy.
> * **d) Cheesefare Week**: In the Butter Week, called Cheesefare Week, the fast is to be observed thus: on Monday, Wednesday, and Friday dairy foods are consumed, while on the remaining four days the eating of meat is permitted after reciting the prayers cited above.

**5.** If anyone, notwithstanding this dispensation from the severity of fasting, should on account of any just cause require further dispensation, then, having set forth the reasons, he is obligated to request that from his Ordinary.

**6.** Having in this manner mitigated the law on fasting, the Synod trusts that the faithful will not cease to apply themselves to the observance of the same, and especially pastors of souls will exercise care that these be not broken on account of any circumstance that might seem to counsel their omission; lest peradventure the faithful be scandalized, especially if dancing should also be joined to the transgression of fasts.

**7.** Finally, pastors of souls must instruct the faithful committed to them concerning the manner of keeping the fast, that is, that they should not restrict themselves to abstinence from foods, but also rein in the impulses of sins; moreover they must insist upon the multiplication of saving works, especially of charity, such as almsgiving and other works of piety, according to the teaching of St. Basil the Great: 

> *"The benefit of fasting is not to be measured by mere abstinence from foods, for true fasting consists in being free from all sins; loose every bond of iniquity, forgive the wrong done to thy neighbor, and remit the debt. Fast not for strife and debates. Thou eatest no meat, but meanwhile thou devourest thy brother. Thou abstainest from wine, but meanwhile thou dost not abstain from injustices. Woe unto those who are drunken, but not with wine; the wrath of the spirit is drunkenness, robbing him of his senses no less than wine."* [^1]

## TITULUS XII. On Offices for the Departed

### CHAPTER I. On Divine Liturgies and Other Offices for the Departed

**1.** If already in the Old Testament it was a saving and holy thought to pray to God for the departed that they might be loosed from the burden of their sins, far more does it behoove us to hold this in the New Testament, especially since Christ, having conquered death, opened for us in His blood the way into the Holy of Holies not made with hands, that is, into heaven, under Whose guidance the multitude of the Saints ceases not to enter into that eternal rest from this valley of our mortal pilgrimage. Seeing, therefore, that this eternal rest, through the finding of eternal redemption by Christ [^2], is already opened, while in the meantime many souls detained in purgatory yearn for it with the greatest longing, it appears most fitting that on the part of the Church Militant saving assistance be granted them for their deliverance therefrom, which can be accomplished especially and above all through the sacrifice of the Divine Liturgy, almsgiving, and prayers, both public by the Church and private by the faithful, as we know and believe from the faith. Concerning this, St. John Chrysostom [^3] expresses himself thus: 

> *"Let us therefore weep for them (the deceased), let us bring relief according to our power, let us prepare some help for them, small indeed, yet such as can help. How, or in what manner? Both by pouring forth prayers ourselves, and by asking others to pour forth prayers for them, and by giving almsgiving frequently for them to the poor. That deed brings some consolation. For hear God speaking thus: 'I will protect this city for My own sake and for the sake of David My servant' [^4]. If the very remembrance of a righteous man availed so much, where in addition deeds are performed for the dead, what will they not be able to achieve? Not in vain was this established by ordinances from the Apostles, that in the venerable, I say, and awesome Mysteries remembrance should be made of those who have fallen asleep; they knew that from this much gain, much benefit would come to them. For at that time, when the whole people and the council of Priests stand with outstretched hands and that awe-inspiring Sacrifice is present, how should we not conciliate God when praying for them? And that for those who have fallen asleep in the faith?"*

And in Homily [^5] on the Gospel of John the same Saint says: 

> *"If he who died was a sinner... he ought not merely to be wept for, because that will bring him no benefit, but one ought to do that which could help him, such as alms and oblations... Dost thou wish to honor the departed? Use something else: almsgiving, good deeds, Divine Liturgies."*

And on the Acts of the Apostles, St. John Chrysostom speaks thus [^6]: 

> *"Not in vain are there oblations for the departed; not in vain prayers, not in vain almsgiving; all this the Holy Spirit ordained, willing that we should help one another mutually. For look: he receives benefit through thee, and thou art assisted for his sake; thou hast despised wealth in order to do something noble, and thou hast become the cause of his salvation, while he is the cause of thine almsgiving. Doubt not that he will bear away something good therefrom. Not in vain does the Deacon proclaim: **\"For those who have fallen asleep in Christ, and for those who make remembrance of them\"** (\*О иже во Христѣ преставивших ся, и иже о нихъ память творящихъ\*); it is not the Deacon who utters this voice, but the Holy Spirit, I say, His gift... Knowing this, then, let us consider what great consolations we can provide to the departed, instead of tears, instead of weeping, instead of monuments, namely: almsgiving, prayers, oblations, that both they and we may inherit the promised blessings."*

**2.** This saving and holy thought of praying for the departed Holy Church of God has always preserved; now directing sacrifices of the Divine Liturgy to be offered for them, now establishing special and proper offices for the departed; and specifically the Church of our rite prescribes both the sacrifice of the Divine Liturgy and the entire Office for the departed to be celebrated at determined times during the year, and that fully twice in the year, namely: on Meatfare Saturday and on the Saturday before the Descent of the Holy Spirit [Pentecost Saturday]; partially also on every Saturday of the entire year, and especially on the 2nd, 3rd, and 4th Saturdays of the Great Forty Days [Lent]; finally, it is ordained in our Typikon (*Уставъ*) to make remembrance of the departed on the 3rd, 9th, and 40th day after death [^7], and according to the Apostolic Constitutions likewise on the anniversary of death [^8]. According to the pious custom of our Ruthenian Churches, it is also customary to celebrate solemnly the Divine Liturgy and the Office for the departed immediately on the day following the patronal feast of the church (*храмовий праздник*), unless it falls on one of those days when a Liturgy for the departed is forbidden, in which case it is proper to transfer it to the nearest following unhindered day. Beyond the regular order, parish priests (*парохи*) are obligated to celebrate divine worship for the departed on convenient days, as many times as the faithful, out of their own holy devotion, request the same for a given stipend.

**3.** But since divine worship for the departed in the course of the year very frequently comes into conflict with the divine services of feast days, both movable and immovable, for the resolution of which the rubrics (*устави*) of the liturgical books are either very scanty or at times none are readily available, so that in many instances Priests, in order to form for themselves a definite judgment on this question, are often left without any guidance, therefore the Synod, for the preservation of perfect uniformity in sacred actions, states and defines a rule, so that priests specifically may know when they may celebrate the Divine Liturgy and the Office for the departed and when not.

---

### § I. On the Divine Liturgy for the Departed

**1.** Solemn Divine Liturgies for the departed are forbidden, even in the presence of the body, on all Sundays and solemn feast days (*нарочиті праздники*), then throughout the three days of the death of Christ [Holy Week Triduum], as also in those cases in which the parish priest is obligated to celebrate the Divine Liturgy of the day, either full or of the Presanctified; nevertheless in more important cases, if there is a second priest, he shall be able, alongside the parochial Divine Liturgy of the current day, to chant a Liturgy for the departed both on Sundays and also on three commanded feast days (*три праздники приказані*), namely: St. Demetrius, St. Stephen, and the Three Holy Hierarchs, yet not in vestments of black color.

**2.** To the same rule is subject the celebration of the Divine Liturgy for the departed on the 3rd, 9th, and 40th day, as well as on the anniversary of death or burial of the deceased; wherefore in those cases it is proper either to celebrate the Divine Liturgy a day in advance, or to transfer it to the nearest unhindered day.

**3.** As to private Liturgies (*Литургії приватні* / low masses), these are forbidden on every Sunday, on solemn feasts and polyeleos feasts, as also throughout the three days of the death of Christ, as stated above. In all cases in which a Divine Liturgy for the departed is celebrated, it is not proper to intermingle variable parts (such as troparia, kontakia, etc.) of any current feast or of the office of a Saint, but to take only that which is proper to the departed, excepting the Epistle and Gospel of the day of the week, since these do not have a festal character; wherefore they may precede the Epistle and Gospel for the departed.

---

### § II. On the Office for the Departed

**1.** Besides that full office (*послѣдованіє*) for the departed which is set forth in our Typikons and which is celebrated in church on determined days, there is also used among us a special part drawn from the full rite, taken from Matins for the departed, which comes under the name of *Panakhyda* (*Паннихида*) or commonly *Parastas* (*Парастасъ*), and which is customarily celebrated both in church and outside church, in which, according to the rubrics of the Typikon, there is placed on the tetrapod *kolyvo* (*коливо*), that is, boiled wheat with honey, as a symbol of the future resurrection and the sweetness of heavenly glory implored for the deceased. According to contemporary custom it is also customary among us to place with the kolyvo loaves of bread (*хлѣбы*), or bread alone. These above-cited customs this Synod likewise praises and approves.

**2.** As to the time in which this Office for the departed is to be celebrated, the Synod decrees that the same may be celebrated throughout the entire cycle of the year, excepting the days cited above on which the Divine Liturgy for the departed is forbidden. However, since the liturgical day among us is customarily counted from the first Vespers of the day, strictly speaking this office is not forbidden on the very day of some feast after the celebration of the solemn Divine Liturgy, namely from the second Vespers of the same; but never in the morning, whether before or during the solemn Divine Liturgy, but at the very least after the conclusion of the same [^9].

**3.** Not infrequently among us, and among the Greeks always, the concluding part of the office of Matins for the departed is used under the name of *Panakhyda*, consisting especially of the Troparia and Litanies (*Єктенії*) for the departed; concerning which part of the office, if a question arises regarding the time, the Synod declares that one must adhere here to what is decreed concerning Parastas.

**4.** As to the manner of celebrating the Office of Parastas, Priests, in celebrating the same, must adhere strictly to the prescriptions of the Typikon indicated in the booklet *Parastasnyk* (*Парастасникъ*), omitting nothing and not rushing, but with devotion and with outward composure must celebrate everything and each single element; wherefore, alternating with the choir, besides the heirmoi, they shall also read aloud all the troparia. Since, however, this Office, celebrated in that manner as prescribed and as is fitting, requires a rather long time, therefore the Priest, for the celebration of the same, may receive a stipend (*таксу*) of about one gulden (*около одного гульдена*).

**5.** Finally, although on the one hand the Synod with praise commends the faithful frequently to renew the memory of their departed, on the other hand it does not neglect to censure the abuses in drinking at banquets (*надъужитій въ піятицѣ при гостинахъ*) which in certain localities, on the occasion of funerals or commemorations (*поминокъ*), are customarily held in the homes of the deceased; and therefore, wherever the parish priest observes such abuses at banquets, let him not dare to be present at them; what is more, let him admonish his parishioners that they should entirely abstain from holding such, because by such drinking bouts the holy Office of burial of the departed and the aiding of them is defiled and dishonored, and that all the more because such banquets, on account of the considerable expenses which at times are customarily incurred for them, deter the less well-to-do faithful from bringing aid to the souls of their departed through church services, by which the departed are especially helped; concerning this the faithful must be instructed.

**6.** Finally, the faithful and their pastors must know that it is not proper to bring the bodies of the deceased into church during the three days of the death of Christ [Holy Week Triduum] and on the solemn day of Pascha itself, but directly from their homes to the cemetery and there immediately proceed to burial.

**7.** Since our Ruthenian Trebniks (*Требники* / Books of Needs), with respect to the celebration of the burial of the dead throughout the entire Bright Week, that is, through the seven days from Pascha, provide only one form of celebration for the same, which relates only to laypersons, and from this no small confusion arises, inasmuch as some Priests apply the said rule also at the burial of Priests, while others prefer to use the rule prescribed for the burial of Priests commonly used throughout the entire year; therefore, for the removal of this confusion, the Synod decrees that the rule proper to the burial of Priests be observed likewise in the said seven days of Pascha, yet in such wise that, whether in the homes of deceased Priests or during their translation to the church, everything prescribed by the Typikon for the Paschal season be joined, as is done at the burials of laypersons, and moreover, in the church itself, instead of the Canon for the departed, the Canon of Pascha must be taken, as is customarily done at lay funerals, as stated above.

**8.** As to the manner of celebrating Parastas, beginning from Thomas Sunday (New Sunday / *недѣля Провôдна або Ѳомина*) right up to the Ascension of the Lord, Priests must observe that after the celebration of the **"Usual Beginning"** (*Начало обычное*) and the singing of **"Christ is risen"** (*Христосъ воскресе*) 3 times, there be joined: the **"Trisagion"** (*Трисвятоє*), and that the rest be celebrated according to custom, as is customarily done at the other parts of the daily services.

**9.** At funeral sermons, which are not infrequently delivered at the burial of the deceased, whether in church or outside and in cemeteries, let the speaker exercise great caution that the sermon does not dwell upon scandalous matters, but rather upon the commendation of virtues, contempt for human affairs, and the inculcation of love for heavenly things, or at the very least upon awakening the remembrance of death and entreating assistance for the departed [^10].

**10.** But since in these more recent times, especially in the towns, it happens at times, as is known, that certain laypersons make bold to extol the memory of the deceased with their own speeches, therefore the Synod strictly commands Pastors that they give no place to such speeches, as far as possible; and if it should be difficult for them to prevent it, then let them expressly declare to such speakers that in the midst of the sacred action they cannot permit their speeches, and that at most this may be permitted them either before or after the sacred action, namely when the Priest has either not yet begun or has already concluded the prescribed sacred action for the departed and has laid aside the sacred vestments; but even this can never be permitted to laypersons in church, but at most may be permitted outside church, that is, tolerated.

### CHAPTER II. On Ecclesiastical Burial and Cemeteries

**1.** How great care the faithful ought to have that their bodies, which on account of communion in heavenly life have already become a temple of the Holy Spirit and at the final renewal of the world are to shine forth like the sun, should after death rest not in any unhallowed place, but in a consecrated place, the holy Patriarchs preceded us by their own example already in the Old Testament, and before all the father of all the faithful, holy Abraham, who, journeying in an alien and unconsecrated land, for his deceased wife Sarah and for the others of his family who should die after her, purchased [^11] a separate place for weighed silver, in which he himself was laid and his only-begotten holy Isaac. Likewise Jacob, his grandson, a stranger in Egypt, dying there, bound by an oath his son Joseph to command his bones to be carried to the holy land, promised by the Lord to his fathers and to their seed and destined to be sanctified by the presence of the Messiah [^12]. That this same command Joseph also gave concerning his bones to his brethren, when one day they should return from Egypt to the Holy Land, Holy Scripture testifies [^13]. And they indeed, returning from Egypt, together with the bones of Jacob, carried also his bones and laid them in that cave purchased by Abraham [^14].

Imitating this example, Christians already from the earliest times took care to bury their dead in places set apart and consecrated, and all the Churches of God spread abroad throughout the whole universe have always firmly kept and now keep this ordinance. Seeing therefore that Christians have always regarded their places of burial as destined for confessors of the true faith, therefore no one who during his lifetime lived outside the great family of the Church of God and passed away outside the same has the right after death to have a common resting place together with the faithful. Whence it follows that, besides infidels, heretics and schismatics have no right to ecclesiastical burial; likewise those excommunicated, public sinners dying in excommunication, those killed in a duel; wherefore let priests not dare to bury any such in Catholic cemeteries, but for them a place outside the cemetery must be designated, or at most, on account of urgent necessity, in some separate place of the Catholic cemetery, where it is also proper to bury the bodies of infants who pass away without baptism.

**2.** In particular, with respect to those who have voluntarily taken their own life, parish priests must be cautious that, unless they showed any signs of repentance before death, they do not dare to bury them in consecrated cemeteries and according to the rite of the Church. If it should be known beyond doubt to the parish priest that the suicide suffered from the affliction of mental derangement, he is to grant him ecclesiastical burial in the customary manner. If, however, the parish priest, moved by weightier reasons, should hesitate to agree with the judgment of physicians and civil officials, the matter must be reported to the Ordinary; and in the meantime, on account of urgent necessity, the body of the deceased is to be buried without church rites, outside the cemetery. If, however, doubt remains whether the suicide was sane (*почитальний*) or insane (*непочитальний*), he is to be buried according to the rite of the Church, but for the avoidance of scandal that might arise, without the customary solemnities at burial. If, finally, doubt arises whether someone killed himself or perished by accident, judgment must be given in favor of the deceased, since in doubt so grave a crime ought not to be presumed of anyone [^15].

**3.** As to the cemeteries themselves, let every parish priest be watchful that these holy places, shelters of the temples of the Holy Spirit, when they have been properly consecrated, be kept in the most honorable manner; and that: 
1. They be enclosed with a wall or secure fence, and the entrance be locked either with picket gates or with doors, so that there be no thoroughfare through the cemetery, nor access for cattle;
2. All revenues and proceeds arising from the cemetery, especially from grass and fruit, belong exclusively to the church, and grass must not be grazed, but only mown;
3. Monies obtained from such revenues are to be placed into the church treasury (*скарбона церковна*);
4. In the event that these revenues are to be leased, this is to be done only to Christians, and that with the reservation that during the mowing of the same from the cemetery no profanations take place, especially around the graves and monuments [^16].

**4.** For the rest, let everything in the cemetery be clean, honorable, and dignified, so that crosses and monuments fallen from graves be not neglected or trampled under foot, but let them be restored, or if they were wooden and have fallen down, let them be laid together in some suitable place or consumed by fire.

## TITULUS XIII. On Ecclesiastical Courts

**1.** Since Bishops by divine right are appointed to be pastors of the Dioceses entrusted to them, they must therefore be regarded as the ordinary judges of all souls who belong to their Diocese in all cases which pertain to the ecclesiastical state. Wherefore upon all and single persons subject to the care of their Bishop there rests a heavy burden of conscience to submit to the judgment of their own Ordinary and to receive his judgment as the judgment of the Lord, Who said to the Apostles and their successors: 

> *"He who hears you hears Me, and he who rejects you rejects Me"* [^17]

and again: 

> *"If he refuses to hear even the church, let him be to you like a heathen and a tax collector"* [^18].

**2.** As to the procedure for conducting trials, this Synod, besides that which is decreed in general canon law, commands that in all Dioceses of this ecclesiastical Province there be observed (as the basis): that *Instruction* which was submitted for the approval of the entire Austrian Episcopate. That *Instruction*, therefore, adapted to our needs, the Synod has determined to use in conducting trials in the Ruthenian Church Province of Galicia [^19].

**3.** This Synod has resolved to add something regarding the procedure to be observed in judicial appeals, and decrees the following:
* **a)** The ordinary ecclesiastical judge in his diocese is the Bishop or his Vicar General, whose person is one and the same as the person of the Bishop; wherefore from the judgment of the Vicar there is no appeal to the Bishop.
* **b)** The higher ordinary Judge is the Metropolitan of the ecclesiastical province.
* **c)** The supreme judge is the Roman Pontiff.

Therefore this Synod decrees that appeals are to proceed in this order:
1. From the court of the Bishop, let appeal be made to the Metropolitan; from the Metropolitan, to the Pope. Nevertheless, appeal to the Pope himself with omission of the intermediate court shall always be free.
2. As to a judgment issued by the Metropolitan in the first instance, appeal is to be made in the second and third instance to the Holy See, which will deign to delegate a judge of his own rite, unless it should decide otherwise.

## TITULUS XIV. On Synods

**1.** That the holding of Synods (Councils) for the promotion of faith and holy discipline is exceedingly necessary ought to be known from practice, beginning already from the cradle of the Church, as well as from the fact that ecclesiastical affairs, when treated by word of mouth by synodal members, are more clearly elucidated and, through arguments assembled from here and from there, are more fruitfully established, and thus are introduced into life as an effective instrument of the Church of God. Ecumenical Councils are held under the presidency of the Ecumenical Pontiff; provincial councils under the presidency of the Metropolitan; diocesan councils under the presidency of the Bishop.

**2.** But since Bishops in the proper sense are the pastors of the Churches, therefore by reason of such judicial authority (*судовластіє*) bestowed upon them by the Holy Spirit, they possess in Synods a decisive vote as deciding Bishops; nevertheless in Ecumenical Councils the supreme judge is the Roman Pontiff, and the decision is made by votes acceding to the opinion of the Roman Pontiff; in provincial councils, however, the co-provincial Bishops possessing ordinary judicial authority have a decisive vote, but by a majority of votes. In diocesan synods, only the Bishop Ordinary himself has a decisive vote. All others, excepting prelates possessing their own territory (district) and quasi-episcopal jurisdiction, as also capitular vicars (during a vacancy of the see), who are summoned to councils, whether ecumenical, provincial, or diocesan, because they do not have judicial authority, may only cast consultative votes (*голоси совѣтуючй*), from which nevertheless Bishops derive the greatest assistance so that they may issue their decisions for the greater good of the Church. Whatever, however, should be decreed in provincial Synods, whether concerning faith, or morals, or ecclesiastical discipline, in order to have binding force, must receive its approval from the Supreme Pontiff (the Pope) and be solemnly promulgated.

**3.** As to the right of convoking Synods: the Roman Pontiff alone has the right to convoke ecumenical councils; the Metropolitan has the right to convoke provincial synods, or, during a vacancy of the see, the older or senior Bishop; the diocesan Bishop has the right to convoke diocesan synods. All those who have the right to convoke councils have likewise the right to preside over them, either in person or through their delegates.

### I. Regarding Those Who Are to Be Summoned to the Synod

**1.** To an Ecumenical Council Bishops of the whole earthly circle are summoned; to a provincial council all ordinary Bishops of the Province must be summoned and appear, and during a vacancy of the see, Capitular Vicars, and moreover Auxiliary Bishops, then the heads of Chapters (*настоятелѣ Капитулъ*), then cathedral or capitular canons, who may be present either in person or through their representatives; moreover the highest superiors of monasteries, deans, theological faculties, and rectors of major seminaries and those of the clergy whom the Bishops, upon common consultation, judge fitting to summon.

**2.** This Synod commends in future to Metropolitans that, if at any time it shall appear to them profitable for contributing to the greater good of the Church, they summon to the provincial Synod one member only from among the laity, namely the Senior (*Старшина / Сеніоръ*) of the oldest institution designated from its very foundation for the advancement of the good of our Ruthenian Church, the so-called Stavropeghial Institute in Lviv at the Church under the title of the Dormition of the Most Pure Virgin Mary, Mother of God, who nevertheless has not even a consultative vote, nor can he be present during debates concerning the life and morals of the clergy.

**3.** To a diocesan Synod are to be summoned: Canons of the Cathedral Chapter, Professors of Sacred Theology, and Catechists of higher schools, Deans, and delegates of individual deaneries, and as many from the clergy of his diocese as the Bishop in the Lord shall judge fitting to summon.

### II. Regarding the Time of Holding Provincial and Diocesan Synods

According to the Council of Trent [^20], provincial Synods must indeed be held at least every three years, and diocesan synods every year. But when it is not always possible to keep this rule, let them obtain for themselves—the Metropolitan for a provincial Synod, and Bishops for diocesan synods—authority from the Holy See to hold, instead of such Synods, an extraordinary assembly (*зôбранє мѣстосинодальне* / pro-synodal assembly), for which, for a provincial assembly, individual Ordinaries with representatives of the entire clergy are to assemble at the Archcathedral, so that one or another be a delegate of the chapter; from all deaneries of individual dioceses, four delegates, and others whom the Ordinaries upon common consultation deem proper to admit. To diocesan assemblies, besides the Ordinary and the Chapter, twelve delegates from the diocesan clergy and chosen men whom the Bishop shall consider. Nevertheless, the Metropolitan will endeavor that at least every fifth year a provincial Council be held, and Bishops will endeavor that every third year a diocesan Synod be held.

* **As to the place:** This the Metropolitan will choose and designate after common consultation with the Bishops.
* **As to the manner:** This, as the norm of councils in the future, is set forth here in detail at the beginning of the acts of this Synod.

## TITULUS XV. On Church Property

**1.** Since churches are consecrated to the service of God, their property must likewise be regarded as consecrated to God Himself. But since every administrator of a church is appointed for this purpose, that he may lawfully manage the property of the church, it follows that he is bound to fulfill this duty so carefully that he manages it as things of God and not of men. What is more, it falls here to observe generally that these things, especially those consecrated to the service of God, are subject not to the administrator of the particular church alone, but by greater right to the local Ordinary, and even to the Ecumenical Pontiff himself. In order that this administration be conducted properly, it is necessary, first of all, that the administrator of the church have an accurate inventory of all church property in one copy in his church, and a second copy he shall be obligated to send to the chancery of the Ordinariate [^21]. Furthermore, in the copies of the inventory changes must always be noted in detail, if any should occur in that property.

**2.** In order that the administration of church property may be more secure, and lest in this matter the administrator fall under any suspicion of the faithful, as though that property were being diverted to some other use, let him choose for himself from his parish three men of the most distinguished character and, as far as possible, such as are provided with sufficient property, who may assist him in the administration of this property. In addition, let every parish priest have an account book of receipts and expenditures, in which the receipts and expenditures incurred are carefully and conscientiously recorded.

**3.** As to movable property, first of all let there be in the church a treasury box (*скарбона*) or strong chest secured in a safe place, provided with three or at least two different locks, for the preservation of the sum of money, charters, precious items, and other documents pertaining to the property and rights of the church, whose keys the administrator, and the second and third one or two of the senior trustees (*провизори*), must carefully and guardedly keep in their possession, so that these do not fall into anyone's hands, and that for the opening or locking of the chest all assemble together. If, however, any of those trustees should at any time on account of some important reason be hindered in the fulfillment of his duty, then with the consent of the administrator and the church trustees he is to entrust his key to a trustee who has no key, or to some other man distinguished for integrity.

**4.** Lest however church monies be squandered, parish priests will endeavor that they be deposited either in savings banks which enjoy public security, or in acquired bonds and other equivalent securities provided with pupillary security. Church monies may be loaned to private persons only in those cases when they are otherwise secured by a certain, permanent, and sufficient mortgage, and that with the reservation that the same and the interest thereon be returned at the appointed time.

**5.** As to immovable property, it is proper generally to observe all that our Synod of Zamość prescribes, Titulus XIII.
